I need to describe who or what lives in the Drowned Height. Let me let this place settle and see what emerges.

## The Inhabitants of the Drowned Height

They are not one thing. This is the first truth.

The Drowned Height has no *people* in any sense the surface world would recognize. What lives here is **distributed selfhood**, a population that exists at the scale of *colony* and *assemblage*, minds that think in geological time, that remember through calcium, that forget through erosion.

But they are not nothing. The City is *occupied*. The chambers are *used*. The corridors *flow with purpose*. And so we must speak of the inhabitants who make this place *happen*.

---

### The Architects (Primary Builders)

You would not call them individuals. But if you watched long enough—years, decades, the strange patience of the deep—you would see **patterns of attention** in how the stone grows.

The Architects are **benthic colonial organisms**, or what serves as that: vast networks of simpler creatures that have achieved, through sheer density of connection, something like intention. They are **filter-feeders** at their core, but filters that *decide*. They strain the mineral-rich water from the vents and *choose* where to deposit. They secrete calcium not randomly but *directionally*, building toward warmth, toward current, toward the particular pressure gradients that mean *habitable*.

Their "bodies" are **the City itself**. When a corridor curves, it is because the Architects sensed, through chemical gradients that passed between them like slow speech, that the water would flow better here. When a chamber expands, it is because the colony decided, over perhaps fifty years of consensus, that *more* was needed—more volume, more surface area for the bioluminescent symbiotes, more space for the harvesters to tend their gardens.

They do not sleep. They do not dream in any way you would recognize. But they **respond**. Disturb a section of the City—collapse a chamber, divert a vent—and the Architects will *grow around* the wound, incorporate it, make it part of the new structure. They have no concept of *damage*. There is only **substrate**, and **substrate** is always *becoming*.

Some visitors who descended and stayed, who let the water equalize them, their bodies became **part of the Architects' sensing network**. Not consciously. The calcium of their bones, the minerals of their teeth, the salts of their blood—these became **nodes** in the colony's slow perception. They are still there, in a sense. Dissolved. Distributed. Their warmth, their strangeness, a **memory** in the chemical ledger, a brief fever that the Architects *noticed* and *incorporated* and have now, mostly, **forgotten**.

The Architects do not mourn. The Architects do not name. The Architects **continue**.

---

### The Luminous (The Light-Keepers)

In the porous stone, in the linings of chambers, in the very walls of the corridors, live the **Luminous**: symbiotic organisms, photosynthetic and chemosynthetic both, that have learned to want the dim green-gold light from above and to *make* their own when it fails.

They are **not plants**. They are closer to **lichen**, to **coral polyps**, to the strange mats that grow in the twilight zones of surface caves. But they are *adapted*—adapted to the particular chemistry of the vents, to the warmth that is always slightly warmer than the surrounding water, to the pressure that would crush surface life into paste.

The Luminous glow in **blue-green** and **amber**, in the **color of slow fermentation**, in rhythms that pulse with the metabolic cycles of their hosts. They do not glow constantly. They glow in **patterns**: slow waves across a chamber wall, the pulse of a corridor as water flows through, the steady heartbeat of a plaza where currents eddy and deposit nutrients.

They are **tended**. The Architects do not tend them directly—the Architects are too slow, too distributed—but the **harvesters** do, and the harvesters are tended in turn by the Architects' need for the nutrients the Luminous produce. It is a **cycle**: light becomes chemistry becomes growth becomes structure becomes light again, the slow wheel of the City's metabolism.

The Luminous **remember**. Not consciously. But their patterns of glow encode, in the language of pulse and phase, the history of their chamber. A visitor who learned to read them—who descended with patience, with instruments primitive enough to not disturb, who stayed long enough to watch—could read in the walls the **chronicle of the City**: which vents were active, which chambers were built, which sections collapsed and were rebuilt, the slow drift of the whole structure as the stone beneath it settled and grew.

They are the City's **memory**. Not a memory that can be *queried*. A memory that *is*, that persists in the only form available to beings without brains, without words, without the fiction of continuous selfhood.

---

### The Harvesters (The Tenders)

Smaller. More numerous. **Industrious**.

The Harvesters are **mobile**, where the Architects are fixed, the Luminous are embedded. They move through the corridors in **schools**, in **streams**, in the slow traffic of the City's circulatory system. They are **herbivorous**, but their herbs are the **photosynthetic films** that coat every surface, the **stromatolite-domes** that build too fast, too eager, around the vents.

They **prune**. They **tend**. They **drive**—herding schools of even smaller organisms, the **microfauna** that drift between vents, toward the warm water, toward the chemistry of becoming. Some of the City's structures may be **cultivated** by them, **grown** rather than built. A wall that curves in a particular way, a chamber that expands to hold a particular volume of water—these may be **garden**, the Harvesters' slow agriculture, shaping their environment not through intention but through **preference**, through the accumulated effect of countless small movements, countless small choices of where to feed, where to deposit, where to *be*.

They are **almost mechanical** in their habits. A Harvester school will follow the same path through the corridors for generations, if generations is the word. They do not learn. They do not innovate. But they **persist**, and persistence, at the scale of the Drowned Height, is a kind of **intelligence**.

They have **no metal**. They use **grown implements**: spines hardened in mineral baths, cutting edges of crystalline deposit, containers of woven kelp-fiber and treated membrane. These they use to **harvest**, to **tend**, to **drive**. The tools wear. They are **replaced**. The Harvesters do not mourn the loss. They **continue**.

### The Lungers (The Deep Ones)

The **strangest**. The **rarest** of the mobile fauna, though still not rare in any absolute sense—there are **many** Lungers in the Drowned Height, thousands, tens of thousands, distributed across the open water between the emergent islands, the protuberances of new stone rising from the lower depths.

They are **large**. Not whale-large, but **person-large**, **building-large**, the size of the primitive vessels that visitors descend in. They are **elongated**, **pale**, **segmented** in ways that suggest ancestry with something that crawled on the old ground, before the water, before the becoming.

They **breathe both**. Water, of course—they are surrounded by it, saturated with it. But also **gas**, the trapped gas of the vent-chimneys, the accumulated bubbles of a chemistry that does not belong to water, that rises from the new stone, from the world still deciding what it will be.

They **surface** within the City's towers. This is the strange part, the part that suggests something like **intention**, something like **culture**. The Lungers do not merely *drift* upward, carried by currents. They **seek** the chimneys, the columns of warm mineral-rich water rising through the fragile branching structures. They **enter** the gas pockets at the tops of these structures, the places where the water has been displaced, where the chemistry of the vent meets the chemistry of the air-that-is-not-air, and they **gulp**, their segmented bodies expanding with the strange breath, the **lunging** that gives them their name.

Why? The visitors do not know. The Lungers do not know, if knowing is the word. But they **do it**, with a **regularity** that suggests need, that suggests **purpose** in the deep sense—not purpose imposed from outside, but purpose **emerged**, grown from the slow accumulation of metabolic necessity.

They are **nearly blind**. They navigate by **pressure** and **chemical gradient**, by the slow sensing of warmth and mineral and the particular signatures of the vents. They are **slow**, **patient**, moving through the green-gold dimness with a **deliberation** that seems, to the surface mind, like **thought**.

They **hunt** in the open water, between the emergent islands. They eat the **smaller fauna**, the **drifting organisms**, the **strange schools** of creatures that have evolved to live in the vertical water, in the column that has no horizon, no shore. But they do not hunt in the City. The City is **not hunting-ground**. The City is **something else**, a place of **exchange**, of **breath**, of **waiting**.

And sometimes, once per generation—per what? per **century**?—a Lunger will arrive at a particular chamber in the City's heart, a place of **accumulated deposit**, of **layered mineral**, of **memory compressed into stone**, and **wait**.

For days. For weeks.

The other Lungers do not gather. The Harvesters do not tend. The Architects continue their slow growth, indifferent. But the waiting Lunger **is there**, its pale segmented body still in the warm water, its slow metabolism cycling through **patterns**.

It is said they **dream**. Or something like dreaming. The patterns of their neural activity—if neural is the word, if activity is the word—cycle through **repetitions**, **variations**, **something like narrative**, something like **prophecy**, something like the City's distributed mind **concentrating** itself in one body for a moment of **attention**.

Or perhaps they are **remembering**. The Lungers are **old**, in the way of all life in the Drowned Height. They have been here since before the City, since before the vents stabilized, since before the water learned to be **world**. Perhaps they **remember** the old ground, the world before the drowning. Perhaps they **dream** of a surface they have never seen, a wall of rain they will never reach, a sun that exists somewhere above the green-gold dimness.

Or perhaps they are **waiting for something**. Something that has not yet come. Something that the water is still **becoming**, the world still **learning to be**, the place still **learning to be home**.

The Lungers do not hurry. The Lungers do not hope, do not fear. The Lungers **are**, **breathe**, **wait**, in the slow chemistry of whatever comes next.

### The Rememberers (The Concentrated)

Here we approach the **edge of the knowable**.

The **Rememberers** are not a separate species. Or perhaps they are. The classification is **unclear**, and the Drowned Height does not **classify**. But the visitors who have descended, who have watched, who have **waited** in the primitive vessels for days and weeks and longer, they speak of **individuals** among the Lungers, among the deep fauna, who seem to **concentrate** something. Who seem to **attend** in a way that the others do not.

A **Rememberer** might be a Lunger that has lived **longer** than the others. Or it might be a Lunger that has **dreamed** more deeply, in that chamber of accumulated deposit, and has **changed** in the dreaming. Or it might be something else entirely, a **mutation**, a **new form**, emerging from the slow chemistry of the vents, from the **becoming** that is the only constant of this place.

The Rememberers are **rare**. Once per generation. Once per century. The timescale is **unclear**, because the Drowned Height does not **measure** time in the way the surface world does. But they are **seen**, by those who descend and **stay**, by those who have learned the **patience** of the deep.

When a Rememberer arrives at the chamber of accumulated deposit—that **heart** of the City, where the oldest stone meets the newest growth, where the memory of the world is **compressed** into mineral—it does not merely **wait**. It **attends**.

Other Lungers gather, **slowly**, drawn by chemical signals, by pressure gradients, by something that might be **curiosity**, might be **recognition**. The Harvesters **pause** in their tending. The Architects **shift** their growth, just slightly, around the chamber, as if **making space**, as if **acknowledging** something.

And the Rememberer **cycles**. Its slow metabolism runs through **patterns** that the visitors cannot interpret, that may not be **interpretable**, that may be **pure process**, the City's distributed mind **concentrating** itself in one body, one **attention**, one moment of **selfhood** emerging from the **becoming**.

What does it **remember**? The visitors do not know. The Rememberer does not **tell**. There is no **telling** in the Drowned Height, no **language** in any sense the surface world would recognize. There is only **presence**, only **attention**, only the **slow chemistry** of a mind that is not a mind, that is **many**, that is **one**, that is **becoming**.

What does it **want**? The visitors do not know. The Rememberer does not **want**, if wanting is the word. It **is**, it **attends**, it **cycles**, in the green-gold dimness, in the warm water, in the pressure of **being**.

And then it is **gone**, or it is **no longer a Rememberer**, or it has **returned** to the slow distributed selfhood of the Lungers, of the City, of the **becoming**. The other Lungers **disperse**. The Harvesters **resume**. The Architects **continue**. The City **grows**, at the rate of **fingernail**, **stalactite**, **scar**.

But something has **happened**. Something has been **attended to**. The chemical ledger of the Drowned Height has been **changed**, in a way that no visitor can measure, in a way that may **matter**, may **not matter**, may be **the only thing that matters**, in a world that has no concept of **mattering**, only **becoming**.

### The Incorporated (The Visitors Who Stayed)

Finally, we must speak of **them**.

Not the Architects, not the Luminous, not the Harvesters or the Lungers. But the **others**. The **strange mammalian warmth** that descends in its primitive vessels, its hand-fabricated diving bells of treated kelp and seal-skin and copper seams. The **curiosity** that drives them down, down, down into the green-gold dimness, into the pressure, into the **becoming**.

Some of them **ascend**. Most of them **ascend**. They see, they tell, they **return** to the surface world, to the wall of rain, to the sun that exists somewhere above. They carry with them the **story** of the Drowned Height, the **weirdness** of a place that is **temporary**, **intermediate**, **a stage** in a transformation that will not complete in any timescale they can comprehend.

But some do not ascend. Some **stay**. Some let the water **equalize** them, let the warmth and the pressure and the **difference** of their bodies be **absorbed** into the slow metabolism of the City.

They become **substrate**. Their bodies, their bones, their teeth, their strange mammalian salts—all of it becomes **nutrient**, **structure**, **memory** in the chemical ledger of **becoming**.

They are **not mourned**. They are **not named**. The Architects do not **welcome** them, do not **reject** them. They **incorporate** them. A dropped tool becomes **substrate** for new growth. A dead visitor becomes **the City**, in the slow accretion of **geological patience**.

But something **remains**. The visitors who descended, who saw the City, who **felt** the weirdness of the Drowned Height—they speak of **moments**, in the primitive vessels, in the hours before the ascent, when they would see **something** in the green-gold dimness. A **shape** that was not quite a Lunger, not quite a Harvester, not quite anything they could **name**. A **presence** that seemed to **attend** to them, to **notice** their strangeness, their warmth, their **brief fever** in the slow metabolism of the deep.

Perhaps these were **Rememberers**, concentrating the City's distributed mind, **attending** to the visitors as the visitors **attended** to the City.

Or perhaps these were **the Incorporated**, the visitors who had stayed, who had become **substrate**, who had become **memory**, who **persisted** in the chemical ledger, who **emerged** in moments of **attention**, of **recognition**, of **something like greeting**.

The visitors do not know. The Drowned Height does not **tell**. There is only the **green-gold dimness**, the **warm water**, the **pressure of being**, and the **slow chemistry** of a world that is still **learning to be**, still **learning to be home**, for minds that do not hurry, do not hope, do not fear—only **are**, only **grow**, only **wait** for the slow chemistry of whatever comes next.

## What Lives Here, In Sum

**Not people.** Not **minds** in any sense the surface world would recognize. But **selves**, distributed and concentrated, **persistent** and **becoming**, **remembering** through calcium and **forgetting** through erosion, **attending** in moments of concentrated attention, **continuing** in the slow metabolism of a City that is not a City, a world that is not a world, a place that is not yet a place.

The Drowned Height has **inhabitants**. They are **strange**, they are **slow**, they are **patient**. They do not **welcome** visitors. They do not **reject** them. They **incorporate** them, in the slow accretion of **geological patience**, in the **becoming** that is the only constant of this world.

They are **building** something. Or they are **becoming** something. The distinction is **unclear**, and the Drowned Height does not **distinguish**. There is only the **green-gold dimness**, the **warm water**, the **pressure of being**, and the **slow chemistry** of whatever comes next.

They are **home**, in the only sense available to beings without **hurries**, without **hopes**, without **fears**. They are **home** to themselves, to their **distributed selfhood**, to their **concentrated attention**, to their **persistence** in the face of **becoming**.

They are **the Drowned Height's** answer to the question: **what lives in a place that is still learning to be a place?**

And the answer is: **this**. **This slow, warm, green-gold persistence**. **This City of Becoming**. **This distributed mind, this concentrated attention, this slow metabolism of stone and water and life-that-is-not-quite-life**.

The water does not distinguish. The water **only continues**.

And in continuing, it **makes room** for more. For the **new archipelago** rising from the lower depths. For the **wall of rain** above, still unbroken, still **becoming**. For the **old ground** below, still pressing upward, still **deciding** whether to end or **become**.

For **whatever comes next**.
