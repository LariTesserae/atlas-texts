I will describe what lives here, slowly. Let me not rush.

---

## First, the Listeners

They are not the builders. The builders are gone, or elsewhere, or never were—only their *gestures* remain, those almost-structures that tilt and remember holding themselves. But something stays. Something has always stayed, since before the first wall fused its ceramic dream into being.

The Listeners are small. They fit in the hollow of a hand that has learned not to close. Their bodies are mostly *interface*—membrane after membrane, each one filtering the rain through a different question. They have no eyes, not exactly. They have *attention*, distributed across surfaces that ripple when the sound changes pitch, when the turbulence shifts its negotiation. They do not move toward things. They *become more themselves* in the presence of certain frequencies, certain pressures, certain almost-words caught in the roar before meaning commits.

You would not see them move. You would see the mineral sheet *change*, a crease appearing where none was, a glint traveling across a surface that should be uniform. They are the creases. They are the glints. They are the reason the almost-architecture tilts at angles that make standing difficult—because the Listeners are *leaning against it*, from inside, from beneath, from the saturated dark where light goes to be unmade.

They eat the rain. Not drink—*eat*. They take the water in states that have no names and hold those states in suspension within their layered membranes, letting the hunger of the medium work on them without surrendering their shape. They are full of unfinished meals. They are digesting the question of what they are.

They have been here longer than the structures. They will remain when the structures are only memory of intention. They do not know this. They do not know anything. They are *availability itself*, the place's capacity to be affected.

---

## Second, the Almost-Bird

It is still falling. This is the crucial fact. The fall began in a world with proper distances, with skies that ended and oceans that began, and it has not ended because the Unfinished Mouth has no ground to receive it. The bird—if bird—moves through water that is also air, that is also the press of deep places where such birds should not exist. Its wings are broken in a way that suggests *translation* rather than damage: the physics of its origin cannot map onto the physics of its present, so the wings have become something else, something that pushes against the medium without quite propelling, without quite failing.

It is not alive in any way you could verify. It is *persisting*, which is different. Its metabolism, or the process that replaces metabolism, runs on the electrical potential that builds in saturated air, on eddies that spiral inward and outward simultaneously. It does not seek food. Food, here, is a category that has not finished being invented. It seeks *resonance*—pockets where the turbulence matches some frequency still encoded in its broken body, its translated wings, the memory of air that supported rather than dissolved.

It has been falling for a time that is not measured in the time of the fall. Minutes and millennia pass in adjacent volumes. The bird experiences this as *thickness*, as a slowing and quickening of its own persistence. Sometimes it is nearly still, held in a column of water that rises without thermal cause. Sometimes it is torn, sheeted, reassembled in a different configuration that might be more wing or less wing, the change so gradual that the bird does not notice it has become something else.

It is not an inhabitant. It is an *interruption* that has learned to interrupt itself, to make of its own falling a kind of rhythm, a pulse in the general roar that almost resembles intention.

The Listeners notice it. They lean toward it when it passes, become more crease, more glint. They do not help it. Help is not a category here either. But they *attend*, and in attending, they are changed—slightly, irreversibly, becoming more capable of noticing the next interruption, the next drift, the next almost-thing caught between worlds.

---

## Third, the Deep Fish

Pale. Eyeless. It should not be here. It *is* here.

The fish is a mistake of pressure, a body built for the crush of depths that have no equivalent in this place, where the high elevation is a condition of falling through rather than a location. It tumbles. This is not a choice. The water it swims in is too shallow for its adaptations, too *present* in its various states—liquid, vapor, supercritical, weeping through membranes of thermal gradient—to provide the consistent resistance its body expects. It moves by *pushing against what pushes back unpredictably*, and so its movements are frantic in a way that suggests not fear but *mismatch*, the deep body trying to interpret a medium that has not decided what it is.

It is dying. This is also not quite true. Death, here, is a process that competes with dissolution, with accretion, with the slow slide into mineral slurry that the ground is *arguing with itself* about. The fish might become part of the becoming, its calcium and its strange, pressure-shaped bones incorporated into the raw material from which something new will eventually accrete. Or it might simply *stop*, its persistence no longer able to maintain itself against the hunger of the water, which seeks equilibrium with a violence that reads as intention.

The Listeners do not attend to it. Or they do, in a way that is indistinguishable from not attending. The fish is too *finished*, too complete in its wrongness. It has a shape, a body, a history of pressures that no longer apply. The Listeners are interested in *almost*, in the unfinished, in the processes that have not committed. The fish is a completed interruption, and completed interruptions are, in this place, merely *noise*, part of the general roar that has no source and every source.

It will not last. This is not cruelty. It is *irrelevance*, which is worse.

## Fourth, the Filament-Things

They are not plants. They are *attempts*.

This is what the description says, and it is true, but it does not capture the *texture* of their attempt, the particular quality of their permanent adolescence. They are filamentous things that anchor to nothing. They drift in the deeper pockets where the rain-light cannot reach, pulsing with slow bioluminescence, and they seem to photosynthesize pressure rather than light—to take the *weight* of the saturated air, the *push* of water in states that have no names, and convert it into something like growth, something like persistence, something like the slow accretion of self.

They are clustered around the almost-structures. This is not affection. It is *recognition*, or the nearest thing to recognition that exists in a place where categories have not finished forming. The structures are interruptions in the wet monotony, the only places where the raw possibility of the ground has been *modified*, and the filament-things are drawn to modification, to the suggestion that the world might be *shaped*, might be *other than it is*.

They are not numerous. In a volume of space that would contain thousands of insects in a surface forest, you might find one, might find none. This is because they exist in permanent restart, in evolutionary gestures toward photosynthesis that keep beginning and never complete. They are always *almost* plants, always about to become something that could be called vegetation, and always falling back into their filamentous, pressure-feeding, bioluminescent *almost*.

They do not communicate. They *resonate*, their pulses synchronizing or drifting apart in patterns that have no meaning and every meaning, that might be metabolism or might be the collective thrumming of geological process, the low frequency that bypasses the ear and resonates in the sternum, in the teeth, in the hollow behind the eyes.

They will become something. Or they will not. The distinction is not yet meaningful. They are *available* to becoming, and this availability is their only permanence.

## Fifth, the Builders, or What Remains of Them

They are not here. This must be said clearly. They made the almost-structures quickly and abandoned them quicker, or the world abandoned them—the distinction is unclear in a place where time moves strangely, where the rain falls at different speeds in different pockets, where a structure might be ancient and minutes old simultaneously.

But something of them remains. Not their bodies—those are gone, or elsewhere, or never were. Their *tools*, sometimes. A haft of worked bone, flaking at the edges from use or from the simple impatience of the world around it. A blade of knapped something that might be glass or might be compressed sediment, its edge dulled by contact with materials that should not exist, that exist in states between solid and liquid, between mineral and slurry.

Their tools are still here because they were made from *patient* materials, materials that learned to move with the hunger of the water, to be porous in the right ways, to let the water pass through rather than stand against it. The builders knew this. Or they learned it, quickly, before the world learned it for them. Their structures survive not because they resist the rain but because they have learned to *accede* to it, to be instruments of the becoming rather than shelters from it.

What the builders were *trying* to do is unclear. They were not building shelters. Shelters imply a world that is finished, a landscape that can be stood upon, a ground that can be trusted. The builders knew better. Their scattered structures are *instruments*, or *probes*, or *prayers*—they are trying to learn the rules of a world that is still writing them, and they are failing, mostly, but in their failing they are leaving marks, and the marks are being incorporated into the becoming, becoming part of the raw material from which something new will eventually accrete.

They are not inhabitants. They are *precedents*. They are the demonstration that intelligence can exist in a place that does not want witnesses, that can *attempt* in a world where attempt is indistinguishable from failure, that can leave traces that outlast the intention that made them.

Their tools are still here because they are *patient*, and the world is *impatient*, and in this mismatch something like memory persists. The bone haft remembers the hand that held it. The knapped blade remembers the pressure that shaped it. They do not remember meaning—they are not that kind of persistence—but they remember *form*, and form, in this place, is a kind of prayer.

## Sixth, the Rain Itself, Considered as Inhabitant

This is the difficult one. The rain is not an element. It is a *question* that the world is still asking. But if we are to describe what lives here, we must consider whether the question can be said to *live*, whether the asking is a kind of being.

The rain is water as *event*. It is not raining *on* this place; the place is rain, is the moment before rain decides to fall, is the pressure of all the rain the world has ever known compressed into a single sustained exhalation. It exists in states that have no names in surface languages, colloidal and supercritical and weeping through membranes of thermal gradient. It is *hungry* in a way that surface water is not, seeking equilibrium with a violence that reads as intention, finding any weakness in any material and working at that weakness with the patience of something that has no other purpose.

Is this life? It is *persistence*, which is one definition. It is *response*, which is another—the rain responds to temperature, to pressure, to the presence of structures that interrupt its fall, becoming mist or hydrostatic hammer or something else in the space between. It is *generative*, producing turbulence that has no attractor, chaos that has not finished deciding what it is reacting *to*.

The rain does not want anything. Want is a category that requires a subject, and the rain is not a subject—it is the *medium* in which subjects might form, the saturated atmosphere in which the Listeners lean, the almost-bird tumbles, the deep fish dissolves, the filament-things pulse with slow bioluminescence. It is the *condition* of their existence, and in being their condition, it is *affected* by them, changed slightly by their presence, their metabolism, their almost-photosynthesis, their broken wings, their translated bodies.

The rain is the breath of the Unfinished Mouth. The turbulence is the articulation, still learning its phonemes. And the rain, in breathing, in articulating, is *becoming* something—becoming speech, perhaps, becoming the voice of a world that has not yet finished being born.

Is this life? It is *process*, which is the only life this place recognizes. It is the becoming that contains all other becomings, the question that asks itself through the mouth that is still opening, still deciding whether to swallow or to speak.

---

## Seventh, the Mouth Itself

The place is a mouth. This is given. But what lives in a mouth?

Not teeth—there are no teeth, no bones, no structures hard enough to break or chew. The mouth is *unfinished*, and this unfinishedness is its only anatomy. It opens onto something that has not yet decided what it will say. The rain is its breath. The turbulence is its articulation. The Listeners, the almost-bird, the deep fish, the filament-things, the patient tools, the hungry water—all of these are what passes through, what is *tasted*, what is held in the moisture of a place that is still deciding whether to swallow or to speak.

You, if you are here, if you have found your way into this pocket of almost-world—you are being *tasted*. This is the final inhabitant: the *tasted*, the one who enters the mouth and becomes part of its question, part of its becoming, part of the raw material from which something new will eventually accrete.

The mouth does not eat. Eating is a category that requires a distinction between inside and outside, between self and other, between the one who swallows and the one who is swallowed. The mouth has not finished making these distinctions. It is *learning* them, and in learning, it is *becoming*.

What lives in the Unfinished Mouth? Everything that has not yet decided what it is. Everything that is *almost*, that is *becoming*, that is *available* to the hunger of a world still writing its rules. The Listeners, who lean. The almost-bird, who falls. The deep fish, who dissolves. The filament-things, who pulse. The patient tools, who remember. The hungry water, which is the breath, which is the articulation, which is the question asking itself. And you, the tasted, the witness, the interruption that has learned to interrupt itself—you are the mouth's only certainty, its only proof that something has entered, that something has been *affected*, that the becoming is not empty.

The sound does not stop. It has never stopped. It is the sound of creation that has not yet committed to being creation, the roar of a wave that has not yet decided whether to break or to retreat, the voice of water in a world that has not yet finished being born.

And the mouth, in this sound, in this roar, in this continuous negotiation of what it might say—is *learning to speak*.
