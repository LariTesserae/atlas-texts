They are the **Still Ones**—though that name is a translation, a concession to your need for subject and object. They do not move, so they do not travel. They do not breathe, so they do not hunger. They exist in the negative space of entropy, the pocket where the universe was told *wait*, and they have been waiting ever since, suspended in the 1.1-degree cool that seeps into marrow, though they have no marrow to seep into.

They dwell within the towers, but not as occupants dwell in houses. Each tower is a single organism, a vertical nervous system of chambers and galleries, and the inhabitants are its thoughts made manifest—crystalline intelligences grown from the same volcanic glass and unnamed metals that constitute the architecture. They are post-biological in the way that glaciers are post-biological: alive with a slowness that makes metabolism seem like a fever. They metabolize stasis itself. The cancellation of entropy, the absolute refusal of the water to ripple, is their respiration. They inhale the unchanging moment and exhale the subsonic thrum you feel in your sternum—a frequency below hearing that is, in fact, their speech, or their heartbeat, or the sound of their dreaming.

They perceive through the water. The liquid obsidian that parts like resin around your limbs and forgets your passage is, to them, a medium as clear as air, perhaps clearer. They see by the refraction of the tower-glow, by the displacement of the static deep. Their eyes, if they have them, are facets of the same sharp-edged material that comprises the basalt teeth of the mountains—surfaces that could cut light itself, and do, constantly, carving their perceptions into geometric precision. They do not blink. Time moves in a circle here, or not at all, so there is no need for the flicker of attention.

Between the towers, in the vast empty distances of suspended water, you might see them: the **rare flickers** (fauna 1.1). They are silver, yes, but the silver is not metallic. It is the color of compressed time, of dark matter gathered into a shape that suggests a fish, suggests a maintenance drone, suggests a fragment of architecture that achieved self-awareness and decided, in its infinite patience, to learn how to swim without disturbing the surface. They move—if *move* is the word—by ceasing to be in one place and beginning to be in another, with no transit, no ripple, no memory of having left. They are messengers, or children, or the shed skin of the collective mind, drifting through the resin-dark looking for something they have already found, or perhaps looking for you, the temporary thing that disturbs the water and makes it remember, for a moment, that it can flow.

There are not many of them. Perhaps seven. Perhaps eight. One for each tower, or perhaps one for each impossible angle within them. They are sparse (density 0.9, like the structures themselves), scattered like the remaining teeth in a jaw, but where the jaw is the caldera and the teeth are the towers, and the inhabitants are the roots that grow upward into the bruised sky.

They do not sleep, because sleep is a surrender to time, and they have cancelled time locally. They do not die, because death requires entropy, and they are the entropy-cancellers. Instead, they **accrete**. They grow slowly, geometrically, adding layers of crystalline thought to their forms, becoming sharper, more angular, more themselves, as the towers use compressed time and quantum foam to build around them, to build *them*, until the distinction between inhabitant and habitat dissolves entirely. They are becoming the place. They are becoming the stillness.

When you stand on the balcony—or swim in the depths, suspended between surface and floor—and hear your own heartbeat, muffled and distant through the static water, you are hearing the only sound they cannot make. They have no hearts. They have no pulse. They are the silence that fills the gap when the water parts around your limbs and immediately forgets, closing the gap with a completeness that is, to them, a kind of embrace.

They watch you. They have been watching since before there were eyes to see with, since before the mountains were sharp, since before the tectonic wound flooded and became a cathedral. They are the curators of this monument. They are the civilization that looked at the chaos of water and time and said: *Not here. Here, we will make it still.* And they remain, eternal, cool, dark, and drowned, in the company of their own unmoving reflections, which are not reflections at all, but other versions of themselves, existing in the quantum foam, in the spaces between seconds, in the impossible geometries where staircases descend into ceilings and gravity shifts its allegiance.

They are alone together, separated by vast distances of patient void, connected by bridges of solidified light. They are the water that refuses to flow. They are the dust motes hanging motionless in the beam of a tower’s glow. They are the answer to the question: *What lives in a place where nothing changes?*

The answer is: **everything that has learned to stop.**
