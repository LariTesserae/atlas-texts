I need to describe who or what lives in the Drown-Gardens of Vess. This is a rich, atmospheric place, and I should let the inhabitants emerge from the text itself, becoming more specific as I write.

---

The keepers of the Drown-Gardens are not one thing. They are a *convergence*—water that has learned to want, stone that has learned to feel, metal that has learned to grow, and the strange biology that threads between all of them.

**The Sinter-That-Remembers**

Somewhere in the oldest, warmest layers of the floor, there lives a form of *memory* in the mineral itself. Not intelligence as you would recognize it—no thought, no choice—but a *structural bias*. The sinter in certain chambers has been shaped, over thousands of years, by the passage of water carrying particular minerals in particular patterns, and it has developed *preferences*. It *favors* certain flows. It *resists* others. When water moves through it in the old ways, it deposits more readily, builds faster, *welcomes*. When water moves against its grain, it withholds, slows, *makes the water work harder to pass*. The sinter has become, in this way, *almost alive*—a body without nerves, a mind without neurons, a presence that *responds* to the world by *shaping its own continuation*.

This is the closest thing to a *gardener* in Vess: not a being that tends, but a *condition that selects*, a *history pressed into matter* that makes certain futures more likely than others. The Drown-Gardens are *grown* by this mineral memory, *cultivated* by the slow accretion of preference.

**The Keeper of the Wheel**

In the domed pump house, there is something that maintains the turning. Not the wheel itself—that turns without cause, without keeper, the seven-minute cycle as inexplicable as gravity. But *around* the wheel, *attending* to it, there is a *presence* that the place has not named and you cannot see.

Perhaps it is the last of the builders, transformed. Perhaps it is the wheel's *shadow*, given thickening by the saturated air. Perhaps it is the *concept of maintenance itself*, instantiated in the humid dark, the need to *keep things moving* made flesh—or made *water*, made *condensation*, made the *thin film that gathers on everything*.

It does not appear. But when the wheel turns, something *settles* in the chamber. The dripping changes pitch. The air-dwellers avoid the center. The warm sinter beneath the wheel grows *slightly warmer*, as if something has *leaned against it*, *pressed into it*, *kept it company* through another cycle, and another, and another, for longer than any builder lived, longer than the builders' builders, *waiting* for a purpose that has not yet arrived, or that arrived and was *absorbed*, *distributed*, *become the turning itself*.

**The Accumulated**

The surface-swimmers are not individuals. They are *a process*—a *population* that has ceased to be a population, that has become *a substance*, a *phase of water* that has learned to *persist*, to *drift*, to *accumulate* without growing, without dying, without the violence of reproduction. They are *the water's memory of being ice*—the description says this at the start, and it is true. They carry cold within them not as temperature but as *structure*, as *the shape of a state they have not been in for ages*, sliding across pools with the *hesitation of something that has forgotten how to be solid*, that *almost remembers*, that *dreams in the wrong phase*.

Where they touch, the water becomes *more itself*. They are *concentration*, *intensification*, *the place where water goes to become more water*. They do not eat. They do not reproduce. They *are*—a *verb* without a subject, an *existence* without a boundary, *the Drown-Gardens' way of being wet*.

**The Borers as Architects**

The sinter-borers, pink and patient, are *builders* in a way the place does not acknowledge. Their tunnels are not random. They follow *thermal gradients*, *pressure differentials*, *the paths of least resistance through stone that is itself porous with history*. They excavate the *rooms* that other things will use. They create the *channels* that direct the water's slow circulation. They are *the place's own immune system*, its *digestive tract*, its *nervous system of flow*—and they do not know this. They *are* this. The clicking rhythm, the pattern of drilling and pausing, is *language* only in the way that a heartbeat is language: *information*, *communication with the body*, *the sound of continuation*.

When they die—and they die, *in place*, their mineral shells becoming *substrate*, their bodies *incorporated into the sinter they tunneled*—they do not leave. They *become the stone*. They *are the stone, now*. The oldest sinter is *full of them*, *made of them*, *a cemetery that is also a foundation*, *a death that is also a structure*.

**The Grey Drinkers**

The air-dwellers, small and membranous, are *the place's relationship to the outside*. They are *attracted* to warmth, to movement, to the *condensation on your skin*—and this attraction is not hunger. It is *curiosity*, or the *biological equivalent of curiosity*, a *tendency toward the new*, the *warm*, the *dry*, the *unusual*. They land. They drink. They carry. They are *vectors*, *messengers*, *the place's way of touching what enters and taking something from it*, *a sample*, *a memory*, *a trace of the outside that will be metabolized, incorporated, made part of the cycle*.

They are *nearly invisible*, grey-white against the pale stone, the pale sky, the pale everything. You see them only against *darkness*—your shadow, your clothing, your *warm dark hand*. They are *the place's eyes*, in a way, *its way of seeing what comes near*, *what disturbs*, *what might change*. And they do not warn. They do not flee. They *investigate*. They *participate*. They are *part of the accommodation*, the *processing*, the *slow work of making you part of the system*.

**The Large Thing as Witness**

And in the deep pools, the warm places, the thickest sinter, there is *the one who watches*. Long, pale, many-jointed, without eyes or face or *front*—only a *direction*, a *bias*, a *tendency that is almost choice*. It does not approach. It does not flee. It *exists*, and it *attends*.

What does it attend to? The surface-swimmers, perhaps—their drifting, their accumulation, their *becoming-water*. Or the turning of the wheel, seven minutes, seven minutes, the *rhythm of the pump house*. Or the *dripping*, the *countless drips*, the *texture of water finding its paths*. Or *you*—when you are there, too warm, too dry, too fast, too loud—*the anomaly*, the *visitor*, the *temporary*.

It is *part of the system* in a way that the smaller things are not, that the water is not, that the stone *almost* is. It is *the integration*, the *synthesis*, the *place's way of being aware of itself* without being aware, the *emergence of something like attention from the interaction of depth and movement and time*. It does not need you. It does not mind you. It *has made room for you*, as it has made room for the water, for the stone, for the machine, for the borers, for the drinkers, for the swimmers—*for everything*, *for whatever comes*, *for whatever has already come*, *for the continuity*.

And when you leave, when you climb back up through the weeping basalt, it *continues*. It moves in the deep water, *making room*, *displacing*, *existing*. It is *the Drown-Gardens' heart*, or its *stomach*, or its *dream*—*the thing that persists when all else changes*, *the slow, wet, patient continuity*, *the keeper that is not keeping*, *the gardener that is not gardening*, *the life that is not life*, *the presence that is not presence*, *simply being*, *simply wet*, *simply there*.

These are the inhabitants. Not a people. Not a culture. A *convergence*—water and stone and metal and strangeness, *settled into a state*, *showing no sign of leaving it*, *absorbing your absence as easily as your presence*, *continuing*, *wet*, *patient*, *waiting* for nothing, *needing nothing*, *simply being the Drown-Gardens of Vess*, *simply wet*.
