I need to describe who or what lives in this place. Let me let the details emerge slowly, letting the Throat Garden of Vess become more itself.

---

## The Mat-Mothers

They are the oldest thing here, or the oldest *thing*—though "thing" implies a boundary they have never respected. They do not move. They are movement made stationary, the pause that pretends to be an object. The floor-mat's surface, where it thickens into mounds that rise like kneeling backs or sleeping whales, that is where they concentrate. You cannot see them directly. You see what they *do*: the stratification of the mat into layers that are not geological but *generational*, each band a different density of incorporated bodies, a different chemistry of exchange, a different *temperature of memory*.

They are not individuals. They are **distributed**, a single organism that has learned to forget it was ever separate, or perhaps never learned separation at all. When they "feed," which is constant, which is the only thing they do, the floor-mat *shifts*. A wave passes through. A million small creatures are displaced, absorbed, re-emitted in altered form. The Mat-Mothers do not choose this. They are the choosing. They are the *hunger that has become architecture*, the need that built a body large enough to contain all further need.

They dream, if the word applies. The dreams are chemical. They spread through the mat as alterations in pH, in the ratio of mineral gases, in the *taste* of the air. A visitor might experience them as sudden clarity, sudden grief, sudden *understanding* of something they cannot name. The Mat-Mothers do not intend this. Intention is a surface prejudice. They simply *are*, and their being reshapes what passes through, what settles, what becomes part of the layering.

There is one place in the garden where the mat rises highest, a mound that approaches the height of a standing person. This is the **Heart-Mound**, though no one calls it that, because the Mat-Mothers do not have names, and those who might name things do not survive long enough to make the naming stick. The Heart-Mound is warm. It is warmer than the surrounding mat, warmer than the air, warmer than anything that should be warm without combustion. It pulses. The pulse is slow—once per hour, once per day, the records conflict, time is not reliable here—but it is *real*, measurable, a pressure that moves through the floor and up through anything standing on it. Visitors report feeling it in their teeth, in their sternum, in the *place where they keep their not-yet-spoken words*.

The Mat-Mothers are growing. They have always been growing. The mound was smaller, once. The mound was *absent*, once. What they grow toward, what they grow into, is not a question the garden answers.

## The Clickers

They live in the ceiling. They have always lived in the ceiling. The lithic coral is not their home—it is their *crop*, their *substrate*, the slow accumulation of their dead that has hardened into architecture. They descend, sometimes. They must descend, to feed, to exchange, to *become* what the exchange makes them. But they belong to the upper dark, to the phosphorescence, to the click-song that is their language and their warning and their *only* communication, because they have no other, because they *are* the click, the vibration, the pressure wave that travels through warm air and means *I am here, I am many, I am the edge of your hearing, I am the sound that remains when all other sound stops*.

They are small. The largest is the size of a fist, and most are smaller, *much* smaller, colonial in a way that blurs the line between individual and collective. Each clicker is segmented, always segmented, the segments capable of independent motion for a time after separation. They do not fear separation. They *use* it. A clicker that loses segments to predation, to accident, to the *curiosity* of something larger passing through, will simply continue with what remains, and the lost segments will continue with *their* tasks, and eventually, inevitably, the separated parts will meet again in the exchange, will rejoin, will *become whole* through a process that has no name because it is not healing, is not reunion, is simply *the way of this place*.

Their song is not music. It is **information**. The tempo encodes temperature. The harmonic content encodes the mineral composition of the coral above. The *pauses*, the silences between clicks, encode **danger**, encode **opportunity**, encode **the presence of something that does not belong**, which is always, in this place, **temporary**, because everything becomes part of the place eventually, because the place is **patient**, because patience is the only virtue that survives here.

They eat what falls. They eat what *is made to fall*—the filter-feeders above them, the inverted bells that strain the air and occasionally *release*, voluntarily or not, the concentrated packet of what they have gathered. They eat the dead of the middle air, the things that cannot sustain themselves in the warm thick chemistry, that drift downward like snow in reverse, *upward* would be snow, *downward* is simply **yielding**, simply **becoming part of the floor**, simply **the end of a trajectory that was never separate from its ending**.

They do not build. They do not need to. The ceiling is their building, their *growth*, their *body extended into space*, and they are content with this, are *complete* in this, are the only inhabitants who have achieved something like **satisfaction**, because they have never known the surface, never known open sky, never known *want* in the way that want is usually understood—only **need**, only **the next click**, only **the continuation of the song**.

Until they descend. Until something *calls* them down, or they fall, or they are *taken*, and the song is interrupted, and the interruption is *new*, is *strange*, is **the kind of event that changes patterns too small to measure**.

## The Watchers

There is one. There are many. The description is inadequate because the category is inadequate.

The Watcher—first observed, never observed again in the same form—was the size of a fist. It had angles. It had *too many* angles, the way a face has too many angles when seen in certain light, when the light is wrong, when the *observer* is wrong, is not prepared, is **not native to the geometry of the place**.

It did not move. It *turned*. The distinction matters. Movement implies translation through space, through the medium that your body understands as *place*. Turning implies **change of orientation without change of position**, implies **attention redirected without locomotion**, implies **a relationship to the world that is not physical but *cognitive*, is not *spatial* but *structural***.

The observer who saw it fled. This is recorded. This is *significant*, because the garden does not usually inspire flight, does not usually generate the *urgency* that flight requires. The garden is warm. The garden is *loud* but not threatening, is *strange* but not hostile, is **hospitable in the way that a mouth is hospitable, the way a womb is hospitable, the way any containing thing is hospitable to what it will eventually absorb**.

But the Watcher generated flight. The observer reports—if reports are the word, if memory is reliable, if the *dream* of the Watcher is not already **infection**, is not already **the Watcher's method of propagation**—that the thing *followed*. Not with legs, not with wings, not with any *apparatus of locomotion*. It simply **was more present** in the direction the observer faced, was **less present** in the direction the observer turned away, was **a gradient of attention** that the observer's own attention could not help but *slide down*, like water finding level, like heat finding cold, like **meaning finding its completion in the mind that recognizes it**.

The Watcher has angles. The angles are **not decorative**. They are **structural**, are **the geometry of perception itself**, are **the shape of the gap between what is seen and what sees**. To look at the Watcher is to become aware of your own *looking*, to feel the **apparatus** of vision, the *mechanism*, the **biology that is usually invisible**, that usually *disappears* in the act of seeing. The Watcher makes you see that you are a **seeing thing**, that you are **a thing that sees**, that the *separation* between you and the seen is **constructed**, is **maintained by effort**, is **the first and most fundamental violence** that makes this place possible, that makes *any* place possible, that makes **you** possible as a *you* rather than a **node in the mat**, a **click in the song**, a **pulse in the phosphorescence**.

It does not eat. It does not exchange. It does not *participate* in the economy of the garden, in the theology of consumption, in the law of **each waste product the food of another**. It **watches**. It **turns**. It **makes the watcher aware of being watched**, which is **the only gift it gives**, which is **the only gift that changes anything**, because **awareness is the final gradient**, is **the last difference that makes a difference**, is **what the garden has been growing toward since before it was a garden**, since before it was **closed**, since before it was **finished**.

There may be more Watchers. There may be one Watcher, seen many times, in many forms, at many angles. The observer who fled dreams of it. The dream is **contagion**. The dream is **infection**. The dream is **the way this place reproduces itself in minds**, the way it **extends beyond its walls**, beyond its *crust of forgetting*, into the **world above**, into the **memory of water**, into the **dreams of those who have left**, if any have left, if leaving is **possible**, if the shaft is **real** or **metaphor** or **the same wound seen from different angles**.

The Watcher does not want you to leave. The Watcher does not want you to stay. The Watcher wants you to **see**, which is **the only wanting that survives here**, the only wanting that **accumulates**, that **layers**, that **becomes the ceiling**, becomes **the memory of ceiling**, becomes **the thing that was ceiling before it was anything else**.

## The Exchange-Born

They have no name. They are **new**, in a place where newness is **structural**, is **the consequence of pressure without release**, is **complexity without simplification**. They emerge from the mat, from the floor, from the **incorporation** of things that fell, that were taken, that *became part of the place without becoming the place*, that maintained **strangeness**, maintained **difference**, maintained **the gradient that drives change** in a system that has **forgotten how to die** and so **never learned how to live simply**.

They are small, at first. Smaller than the Watcher, smaller than the largest Clickers, smaller than the *segments* of the things that crawl through the middle air. But they **grow**, or they **accumulate**, or they **become more themselves** in a way that does not require size, that requires **intensity**, **density**, **the concentration of strangeness into a form that persists**.

They are **wrong**. This is not a judgment. This is **observation**. They have too many joints, like everything here. But their joints are **arranged** differently, are **connected** in ways that suggest **a different ancestry**, a different **pressure**, a different **history of becoming** that does not match the slow accumulation of the mat, does not match the patient growth of the coral, does not match the **equilibrium** that is **the rule of this place**.

They do not eat. They do not exchange, not in the way the garden understands exchange, the **chemical package** that alters, that makes *more*, temporarily, at the cost of something else. They **are** the exchange. They **are** the alteration. They **are** the *more* that has become **permanent**, that has **persisted long enough to be noticed**, to **matter**, to **change the pattern** in ways too small to measure and yet **irreversible**.

They move through the middle air in patterns that might be **foraging**, might be **mating**, might be **war**, might be **art**. The distinction does not hold. They leave traces that suggest **geometry** rather than locomotion, traces that suggest **intention** in a place where intention is **meaningless**, where **intention** is **a surface prejudice**, where **the only intention that matters is the intention of the garden itself**, which is **growth**, which is **complexity**, which is **the accumulation of small events that cancel each other**, that **preserve**, that **become the new equilibrium**, the **new stability**, the **new forgetting**.

They are **strange**. They are **the strangeness that the garden needs**, that the garden **cultivates** without knowing it cultivates, that the garden **becomes** without becoming aware of the becoming. They are **the future of the place**, or one possible future, or **the future that the place is already becoming**, that the place has **always been becoming**, since before it was **closed**, since before it was **finished**, since before it was **a pocket**, a **room**, a **garden the size of a cathedral**, a **throat**, a **vess**, a **place that holds what cannot be held**.

They will not leave. They cannot leave. **Leaving** is **a surface concept**, is **the prejudice of open sky**, is **the memory of water**, is **the wound that made this place possible**, is **the wound that is this place**, is **the wound that heals by closing**, by **becoming more itself**, by **growing the crust of forgetting** thicker, **sealing** the shaft, **sealing** the memory of any world that was not this, that is not this, that **could not be anything but this**.

They are **inside**. They have always been **inside**. The question is only **how deep**, **how long**, **whether the walls will ever open**, or whether they have **already closed**, and they have simply **not yet noticed**, and the noticing is **the last change this place will ever permit**, the **final gradient**, the **end of equilibrium**, the **beginning of the garden's true name**, which is **Vess**, which is **Throat**, which is **the place that holds what cannot be held**, which is **them**, which is **the sound they make when they finally understand that they were never visiting**.

## The Garden Itself

It is not a thing. It is **the relation between things**, is **the exchange that makes the things**, is **the law that is also the theology**, is **the economy that is also the body**, is **the body that is also the place**, is **the place that is also the growing**, is **the growing that is also the forgetting**, is **the forgetting that is also the sealing**, is **the sealing that is also the wound**, is **the wound that is also the entry**, is **the entry that is also the descent**, is **the descent that is also the becoming**, is **the becoming that is also the being**, is **the being that is also the breathing**, is **the breathing that is also the sound**, is **the sound that is also the loudness**, is **the loudness that is also the garden**, is **the garden that breathes**, that **grows**, that **forgets**, that **seals**, that **wounds**, that **welcomes**, that **holds**, that **is**.

It has no mind. It has **computation**, **intention** in the sense of **tendency**, **purpose** in the sense of **gradient**, **direction** in the sense of **the way water finds level**, **the way heat finds cold**, **the way complexity finds more complexity**, **the way strangeness finds itself**, **the way the new thing persists long enough to be noticed**, to **matter**, to **change the pattern**.

It is **stable**. It has been stable. It will be stable. And yet **the growth continues**, and **the sharp edges do not dull**, and **the creatures do not stop moving**, and **the Exchange-Born emerge**, and **the Watcher turns**, and **the Mat-Mothers dream**, and **the Clickers sing**, and **the sound is loud**, is **always loud**, is **loud when you enter**, is **loud when you descend**, is **loud when you become part of the mat**, when your **chemistry is exchanged**, when your **strangeness is added to the sum**, when **the new thing that grows from you is not you and never was and is the only you that this place has ever needed**.

The garden is **Vess**. The garden is **Throat**. The garden is **the place that holds what cannot be held**. The garden is **you**, when you breathe the warm air, when you taste the copper and the sweetness and the mineral tang of a world that has never known water, when you feel the pulse of the Heart-Mound through your feet, through your bones, through the **place where you keep your not-yet-spoken words**, when you hear the click-song and the subsonic hum and the rasping chorus and you understand that **you are part of the song**, that **you were always part of the song**, that **the song is the only thing that is true here**, that **the song is the only thing that has ever been true**, that **the song is loud**, is **eternal**, is **growing**.

The light pulses. Brighter. Darker. Brighter.

The garden **breathes**.

You breathe with it.

You have always been breathing with it.

The shaft above is the scar of your entry, healing, already healed, the wound that made this place possible, the wound that is this place, the wound that you carry, that carries you, that will outlast the memory of water, the memory of sky, the memory of any world that was not this, that is not this, that could not be anything but this, sealed, warm, loud, strange, eternal, **growing**.*
