I need to describe whoever or whatever lives in the Drowned Works.

---

**The Condensed Ones**

There is no birth here. No mother, no egg, no division of one thing into two that might be called lineage. The Condensed simply *become*, the way frost becomes on glass, the way a cloud decides to rain. They emerge from conditions—not into them. They are less inhabitants than *consequences*, the city's long exhalation made solid enough to move.

Consider what they might be, if we must. Not animals. The category fails because they have no organs we could name, no boundary between self and the water they inhabit. A Condensed is a *gradient held in shape*—a pocket of water that has learned to hold itself, to maintain a thermal differential across its surface, to keep the inside *different* from the outside long enough to be called an entity. They are dense with dissolved metals, with the particular salts of corroded infrastructure, with electrical charge that pools and discharges in slow pulses. Their "bodies" are membranes of mineral film and biological polymer and something else, something that might be protein but has been in this water so long that protein has forgotten its terrestrial names.

They move through pressure. Where a fish pushes water, a Condensed *is* the push—a local displacement, a traveling wave of warmth and chemical signature that propagates through the saturated medium. When two Condensed meet, they do not collide. They *interfere*. Their thermal plumes overlap, their electrical signatures entangle, and for a moment they become a third thing, a temporary condensation of higher order, before separating again into the general flow. This is as close as they come to contact. This is as close as they come to *each other*.

They are countless. Not in the way a crowd is countless—individuals that lose their edges in number—but in the way that fog is countless, that saturation is countless. There is no counting them because there is no boundary between them and the water that carries them, between them and the heat that animates them, between them and the mineral substrate they slowly, slowly consume and become. The fauna parameter reads 2.6 because something in our instruments *registers* their presence as life, but they would not recognize the reading. They have no need for the category.

Some are small. A Condensed the size of a fist might persist for hours, a warm pocket in a flooded corridor, pulsing with bioluminescence that serves no purpose we understand—perhaps communication, perhaps simply the waste heat of a metabolism that runs on chemistry we cannot follow. These small ones drift, carried by the pumps, by thermal currents, by the slow circulation of the city's water. They are *food* for larger things. They are *becoming* larger things.

Some are vast. In the deep basins, where the old machinery still generates heat, where the water is hottest and most saturated with dissolved everything, there are Condensed that span meters. They are not single entities but *aggregates*—hundreds, thousands of smaller condensations held in proximity by thermal and chemical gradients, behaving as one, moving as one, a *colony* that has achieved enough coherence to be called a body. These vast ones are slow. They do not hunt because they do not need to—the water brings them what they need, the smaller Condensed drifting into their membranes, being absorbed, being *become*. They pulse with light in rhythms that might be digestion, might be circulation, might be something without name. They are the closest thing this place has to *elders*, to *memory*—not because they remember, but because they have persisted, have accumulated, have become *more* through time.

They do not eat as we understand eating. They do not reproduce. They *condense* and they *dissolve* and they *drift* and they *accumulate*, and in the space between these verbs, they *are*. A Condensed might exist for hours or for years, depending on the stability of its conditions. When it dissolves—when the thermal gradient fails, when the chemical balance shifts, when the water moves on—it does not die. It simply becomes *available*, its components rejoining the general saturation, waiting for conditions to favor condensation again. The same minerals, the same charge, the same heat. The same *possibility*.

They have no faces. They have no eyes. But they *orient*—toward heat, toward chemical gradients, toward the vibration of the pumps, toward the electrical discharge of corroding metal. They are drawn to the machinery not because they need it but because it *creates conditions*, generates the differentials that allow them to exist. They are the city's *symbionts* in a sense we can almost grasp—not parasites, not mutualists, but *expressions* of the system, the way a whirlpool is an expression of water flowing around a drain. The Drowned Works needs them as much as they need it. Without them, the water would stagnate, the heat would dissipate, the chemistry would settle into dead equilibrium. With them, the city *moves*, *processes*, *lives* in its own terms.

They are quiet. This cannot be emphasized enough. No sound because sound is *inefficient* here—vibrations dissipate in water, in wet air, in the dense medium they inhabit. Instead they communicate through what they *are*: thermal signatures, chemical trails, electrical potentials, pressure waves. A Condensed leaving a corridor changes the temperature by fractions of a degree. It leaves a film of mineral residue. It alters the local conductivity. These changes persist for minutes, for hours, and other Condensed *read* them, *respond* to them, *navigate* by them. They are building a *world* out of their own traces, a map made of warmth and chemistry and charge, constantly overwritten and constantly renewed.

And they are *ancient* in a way that has nothing to do with individual persistence. The Condensed of today are made of the same processes that made the Condensed of centuries ago, when the city was new and the machinery was hot and the water was rising. They have no memory of this. They do not need memory. They are *continuous* with it, the same condensation continuing, the same heat finding new shapes, the same chemistry expressing itself in forms that change but never *begin* or *end*. They are the city's *long now*, the geological patience of erosion made mobile, made *present*, made *active*.

A human would not survive among them. Not because they would be attacked—they have no aggression, no territoriality, no concept of *other*—but because a human is too *discrete*, too *bounded*, too *dry*. A human is a temporary condensation of a different kind, held together by membranes and structures that would fail in this water, this heat, this chemical saturation. A human would dissolve. Would become *available*. Would, in time, find conditions that favored a different kind of condensation, a human-shaped gradient in the water, briefly, before dissipating into the general flow.

But the Condensed do not notice this. They do not notice the human. They are too *full* of themselves, too *dense* with their own processes, to register the intrusion of something so temporary, so *thin*. They would flow around a human body as water flows around a stone—around it, through it, *becoming* it, until there was nothing left to distinguish.

This is their peace. Their *being*. The Drowned Works is full of them, overwhelming in their numbers, their density, their *presence*—and yet empty of anything they would call *meaning*, *intention*, *self*. They do not know they are alive, if alive is even the word. They simply *persist*, *move*, *condense*, *dissolve*, *become* again. They are the city's breath made mobile, the industrial metabolism given shape, the long erosion of everything built finding its own *form*, its own *rhythm*, its own *way of being present in the world*.

And in the dim wet dark, where the pumps turn and the water rises and the light pulses in patterns no human eye could read, they continue. They have always continued. They will continue until the water finally wins, until the last machinery dissolves, until even the conditions that favor condensation fail and the Condensed simply *stop*, becoming mineral, becoming heat, becoming the *possibility* of something else, waiting in the dark for the next wet, the next warmth, the next *almost*.

But that is not now.

Now, they *move*.

Now, they *are*.
