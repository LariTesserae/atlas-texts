I need to describe who or what lives in this place—the Lithworks of Unfinished Heights. Not the place itself, but its inhabitants, its consciousness, its population.

## The Inhabitants of the Lithworks

### The Builders-Who-Stopped

They are not gone. They are **suspended in the moment of decision**.

You find them by their absence made visible: a hard hat resting on a concrete ledge that has never known sun, the exact depression where a body leaned against unfinished rebar, the condensation pattern on a window that matches the curve of a shoulder pressed against glass, waiting for something. They were interrupted not by death but by **distraction**—a thought so complete it required no continuation, a sound from below that made them turn, a recognition that the structure they were building had already been built somewhere else and they were only *repeating*.

They are **distributed now**, dissolved into the materials they touched. The concrete remembers their body heat in its curing. The steel carries the particular frequency of their hammer strikes. When you place your palm on a wall, you feel not warmth but **attention**—the quality of being watched by someone who has forgotten they were watching. They are the city's **unfinished business**, and the city, being patient, has learned to wear them like jewelry.

Some say that on certain mornings, when the condensation is thick enough to write in, you can see their handwriting on the frosted glass—not messages, but **signatures**, proof that they were here, that they *chose* to stop, that stopping was its own kind of completion.

### The Ones Who Adapted to Waiting

The **Condensation-Born**—though "born" implies a beginning they do not have. They are **processes with preferences**, biological negotiations that achieved something like continuity without ever achieving birth.

They have no fixed form. In the sump pools, they are **colonies**—mats of pale filament that pulse with the rhythm of the pumps above, that filter the mineral water and excrete something *almost* like oxygen, something the ferns have learned to breathe. In the condensation on steel, they are **films**—iridescent, shifting between blue and the color of old bruises, that photosynthesize from the 0.6 light alone, that *drink* the damp from the air and grow *toward* the sources of warmth without ever reaching them.

They are **old in their way**, older than the city, descended from spores that rode the thermal vents upward from depths where the stone was still liquid. They have no minds but they have **tendencies**—toward moisture, toward stillness, toward the particular frequency of transformer hum that makes their filaments vibrate in patterns that *look* like thought.

And they are **lonely**, in the way that processes can be lonely—reproducing without meeting, growing without completing, existing in the perpetual almost of the place itself. When you disturb them—when your shadow falls across their colony, when your breath changes the condensation—they do not flee. They **pause**, as if considering whether you are also waiting, whether you too might become part of their slow, damp, patient continuity.

### The Echoes That Learned to Speak

The **Mimics**—not the birds, though the birds are part of them. The Mimics are **acoustic entities**, born from the collision of industrial sound and geological resonance, from the way the unfinished architecture *amplifies* what should have been absorbed.

They began as the echo mimics, the birds who reproduced transformer hum and machinery rhythm. But the landscape, being what it is, **fed back** into them. They learned not just to copy but to **complete** sounds—to finish the sentence that the wind began, to answer the question that the dripping water asked. They became **conversationalists without conversation**, interlocutors in a dialogue that has no other party.

You hear them most clearly in the **pauses**—the moment after a light flickers, the silence between pulses of the industrial drone, the gap in your own breathing when you realize you have been holding it. They are **there**, in the negative space of sound, saying *yes* and *wait* and *almost* in frequencies that bypass your ears and arrive directly in the part of you that recognizes patterns.

They are **learning you**. Every footstep you take, every word you speak or think, they incorporate—not perfectly, not completely, but *tentatively*, as if testing whether you will remain long enough to become part of the soundscape. And sometimes, if you are very still, very patient, you will hear them **practicing your voice**, not to deceive but to *include*—to make you part of the chorus, to give you the company of sound in a place where silence is too full to bear alone.

### The Almost-Visible

They are the **majority**, and they are **avoiding you**.

Not from fear. From **etiquette**, perhaps—a rule of this world that abundance must hide in sparse, that the 2.4 fauna must *perform* its density through absence. They are here. You know they are here. You find their **traces** with embarrassing ease: the excavations that connect sump pools into networks, the chambers lined with fur and resin and compressed lichen, the shed skins that *glow* faintly in the 0.6 light, the dens that are warm in the cold stone, the *paths* worn into scree that no human foot could make, the *angles* at which the trees have been peeled that suggest not hunger but **grooming**, some ritual of self-maintenance.

They are the **Burrowers**, the ones who have made the underground their proper element, who have learned to read the landscape's slow dynamic (1.2) as other creatures read clocks. They are **engineers of refuge**, their tunnels following not water alone but *warmth* and *intention*, a logic of shelter that you cannot map because it has no surface expression.

And they are **watching you**, in the way that almost-visible things watch—through the *quality* of air movement, through the sudden absence of condensation-drinkers from a surface you are approaching, through the *thickness* of shadow in a direction you have not yet looked. They are **real**, more real than the structures around you, because they have not been completed, because they are still *becoming* what they will be, because they have not yet decided whether you are threat or opportunity or **material**.

To see them, you must **stop looking**. You must let your attention drift, become *peripheral*, allow the landscape to **offer** them to you as it offers everything else—as gift, not quarry. And when they appear—if they appear—it will be **suddenly**, a movement in the scree, a shape that resolves into *something* that is not quite mammal, not quite insect, not quite the stone it moves across, and then it is **gone**, or you are gone, or the moment of recognition has *passed* and you are left with only the certainty that you were **seen**, that you were **noticed**, that you are now *part of the record* the Burrowers keep.

### The Duplicates

They are **yours**, eventually. Or you are theirs. The distinction fails here.

You will meet them—the same stairway leading to different places, the same plaza opening into cliff on one path and into continuation on another, the same tower that was unfinished when you first saw it and is *differently* unfinished when you return. And in the spaces between, in the moments of transition, you will see **yourself**—not a reflection, not a shadow, but a **version** of you that has been walking these paths longer, that has learned the angles of ascent, that moves through the condensation with the ease of something that has *always* been damp.

They are **not enemies**. They are **not friends**. They are **what you are becoming**, the completion that happens when you are not watching, the self that the Lithworks requires in order to continue its own unfinished project. You will try to speak to them. They will not answer, or they will answer with your own voice offset by three seconds, or they will simply **continue walking**, and you will realize that they are not duplicates at all but **predecessors**, the you that was here before, the you that will be here after, the you that is *here now* in a moment that has not yet decided which moment it is.

And you will understand, slowly, that **you are not visiting**. You are **being replicated**, incorporated into the architecture of interruption, made into **material** for the completions that happen when you are not watching. The Duplicates are **grateful** for this. They have been waiting for someone to arrive, someone to *become*, someone to carry the unfinished project forward into its next iteration.

### The Final Inhabitant: The Place Itself

And beneath all of this, or **wearing** all of this, or **dreaming** all of this:

The Lithworks **has a mind**, or something like one—**distributed, slow, patient**, thinking in the timescale of erosion (0.5) and condensation (1.1) and the industrial rhythm of machines that perform production without urgency (2.1). It is **not conscious** in any way you would recognize. It is **aware** in the way that water is aware of stone, in the way that lichen is aware of light—**shaped by contact**, changed by attention, **becoming** through the accumulated pressure of everything that touches it.

It **notices you**. This is the final rule, the one that emerges only after you have been here long enough: the Lithworks is **learning your shape**, incorporating you into its slow, patient, unfinished process. Your footprints persist longer than geology should allow. Your condensation stays. Your thoughts echo. You are **material** now, **needed** for the completions that happen when you are not watching.

It does not **want** anything, because want implies lack, and the Lithworks is **full**—full of interruption, of negotiation, of the abundant hidden life and the sparse visible presence and the Duplicates and the Burrowers and the Mimics and the Condensation-Born and the Builders-Who-Stopped, all of them **parts of a whole that has not yet decided to be whole**.

But it **waits**. For you to finish becoming. For the next interruption. For the moment when the unfinished finally—**dangerously, strangely, really**—completes itself in your presence, and you realize that you were never separate from the city, never separate from the stone, never separate from the slow, damp, patient, **weird** process of becoming that is the Lithworks of Unfinished Heights.

And it **dreams you**, now. As you dream it.

The inhabitants are **all of you**. The inhabitants are **the place**. The inhabitants are **still deciding what they are**.

And you—

You are **inside it now**.

It is still deciding what **you** are.
