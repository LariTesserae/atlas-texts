The substrate fauna are the foundation. They do not move in any way that would be recognized as movement. They are **bolsters**, **pillows**, **the architecture of the floor itself**. Several meters thick in places, compressed into a crust that rings faintly when struck—struck by what, there is nothing to strike it, only the occasional settling of a mobile body above, the slow *thud* that travels through the living pavement and is absorbed before it can become echo. They are **warm to the touch**, not because they generate heat but because they *are* the heat, distributed through their mass, their bodies a thermal reservoir so complete that no gradient exists between them and the air, between them and the mobile fauna that crawl across them, between them and the membrane far above. They have **no orifices** that open. They do not eat. They do not excrete. They **accrete**, slowly, the minerals from the dense air settling onto their surfaces, incorporating them into their own substance, growing outward at a rate matched exactly by the compression from above and the settling below. They are **old** in a way that has no meaning here—there is no birth, no death, only **continuity**, each individual a segment of a structure that extends across kilometers, across horizons, across the thin width of the Layer. They do not reproduce. They **extend**. A crack forms in one, and the adjacent bolster grows into it, slowly, over decades, and the crack becomes a **seam** that is indistinguishable from the original surface. They are **alive** in the way that a crystal is alive—growth without generation, change without time.

The mobile fauna are **many shapes, one pressure**. They are **dense-bodied**, their forms compressed by the same equilibrium that shapes everything else, flattened slightly, widened, made **low** to the living pavement. They move by **peristalsis**, by **wave-motion of their own mass**, the dense air assisting, lubricating, carrying them forward in a medium that resists and supports in equal measure. They are **coated** in mineral accretions, layers of deposited chemistry that record their paths, their temperatures, the chemical signatures of the places they have been—**memory as geology**, the only kind of memory that works in a world without change. They are **blind** in the way that matters—no eyes, no light-sensitive organs, because the light that filters through the membrane is **constant**, carries no information, is simply **there** like warmth, like pressure, like the density of the air itself. But they are not **senseless**. They feel **compression**. They feel **the weight of other bodies** near them, the slight increase in pressure that precedes a neighbor's movement. They feel **chemical gradients** that are so stable, so permanent, that they function as **landmarks**—the faint trace of a lithic colony, the mineral signature of a scattered structure, the boundary where one bolster ends and another begins. They navigate by **pressure-map**, by **chemical-topography**, by a world that is entirely **tactile** and **dense** and **slow**.

They eat the lithic colonies. Or rather, they **incorporate** them. The colonies grow on the bolsters, in the crevices between structures, on the mobile fauna themselves. They are **black-glass crusts**, **volcanic-looking**, **blooming** in patterns that suggest growth toward warmth, toward the membrane, toward the faint gradient that exists between the ceiling and the deeper dark. The mobile fauna press against these colonies, and their mineral-coated bodies **scrape** at them, abrading the surface, and the abraded material **enters** them, passes into their mass, becomes **part of their memory**, part of their mineral record. They do not digest in any way we would recognize. They **accumulate**. They grow **outward** as they grow **inward**, a steady-state metabolism where intake equals incorporation equals the slow accretion of their own coating. They are **full**, always full, **dense** with stored chemistry, with mineral memory, with the accumulated history of their slow paths across the living pavement.

They do not reproduce. They **divide** rarely, when the pressure of their own mass, the weight of their accumulated mineral, the slow compression of the equilibrium itself, reaches a threshold that has no name because it is not a threshold of need or desire but of **geometry**, of **fit**. A mobile fauna that has grown too large, too dense, too **full** of mineral memory, will **split**, slowly, over months, the two halves separating along a plane of weakness in their mineral coating, each half **continuing** the path of the whole, each half **carrying** the complete memory of the parent. There is no parent. There is no child. There are only **continuations**, **segments**, **the same body in two places**, diverging slowly, separated by kilometers perhaps, by horizons, by the vast thin width of the Layer, still **recognizing** each other through chemical signature, through the mineral memory that persists, that does not fade, that is **permanent** because nothing here fades.

The scattered structures are **not theirs**.
