I need to describe who or what lives in the Hollow Continent. Let me work through this carefully, letting the place itself generate its inhabitants.

## The Inhabitants of Varn: A Taxonomy of Almost

### The Settled (The Cooled, The Remembered)

They are **not human**. They are not any lineage of biology that would recognize itself in a mirror. They are the *result* of a question that the shell asked itself for ten billion years: *what can live here, in the hollow, in the breath-water, in the thin cold air that only condenses because warmth rises and cold descends and they meet?*

The Settled began—this is the working theory, the one spoken in temperature-gradients around condensation gardens—as **colonies of growth** that learned to want. Not individual organisms. Not a species. Something that spread across the glass like a fever of geometry, like a thought that decided to have a body, and then decided to have *many* bodies, and then forgot which had been first.

They are **tall** because on Varn, the useful direction is *up*. The breath-water gathers low. The warmth comes from below, from the fissures, from the deep places where the collapse still dreams. To be tall is to reach toward both. To be tall is to be *hollow* in the center, where the resonance lives, where the song of the world-before is still vibrating, still almost voice.

They have **too many joints**. This is not exaggeration. Count them. Seven in an arm where three should be. Five in a leg that bends backward at the knee, or forward, depending on which Settled person you measure and depending on the hour and depending on the thermal cycle and whether they have been Unsettled. The joints are **hinges of memory**. Each one remembers a direction that gravity no longer allows. Each one is a small argument with the flatness of Varn, with the way things fall *toward the center of a curve* instead of *down*.

They have **no faces**, or their faces are **elsewhere**. Some Settled persons have a suggestion of features on their chests, where the membranes flutter. Others have them at the backs of their heads, which turn too far, which rotate 270 degrees when they wish to look behind them. Most have no features at all, only the **gradient of warmth** that moves across their skin like weather, like speech, like the slow negotiation of presence and absence between two beings who cannot touch and cannot be touched and yet **love** in the only way available: by sharing temperature, by being warm in the same cold air, by **breathing the same breath-water** and knowing, without knowing, that they are **not alone** in the hollow.

They **absorb**. This is how they live. They stand in the thin pools of condensation, in the cups of fallen lichen-ghosts, in the gutters that the builders carve to catch what the shell exhales. And through their skin—*their skin, which is not skin, which is membrane over resonance over hollow space*—they **drink**. The breath-water enters. It does not nourish. It **resonates**. It joins the song. It makes them more themselves, or less themselves, or **differently themselves**, depending on the purity of the water, the warmth of the fissure it came from, the dreams it carries up from the deep places where the original heat still sleeps.

They **do not eat**. They do not need to. They are not metabolizing. They are **maintaining a pattern**, a standing wave in the chemistry of condensation and glass and resin. They are **weather that has learned to want to continue**. And wanting, on Varn, is **enough**.

They **speak in temperature**. This is not metaphor. A Settled person can raise the warmth of the air around them by **0.03 degrees Celsius**. They can lower it. They can create a gradient, a slope of warmth that another Settled person reads as **yes** or **no** or **wait** or **the fissure at Brine Stair is weeping early** or **I am lonely** or **I remember something that did not happen** or **the sky is showing through again and it is wrong and it is right and I do not know how to feel about the wrongness of the rightness**.

It is **slow**. A conversation takes hours. A declaration of love takes days. A warning about Breathing Out takes a week, the warmth spreading from settlement to settlement like a fever, like a prayer, like the thermal cycle itself learning to **mean** something.

They have **no names**, or they have so many names that naming is impossible. They are called by their **warmth-signature**, the particular pattern of gradient they produce, the thermal fingerprint that identifies them as **this one**, **not that one**, **the one who tends the garden near Keth**, **the one who has begun to lean toward the rim**, **the one who spoke to me and made me feel, for an hour, like I was not alone in the hollow**.

And they are **breaking**. This is the truth that the settlements do not speak, that the council of warmers in Keth tries to prevent with resin-sealed fissures and prayer-gradients and **rules** that are not rules but **hopes**:

The Settled are **remembering too much**.

They have no brains. They have no neurons. They have no tissue that stores information in the way that biology on living worlds stores it. And yet they **remember**. They remember the world before the collapse. They remember the **warmth** when it was ambient, not fissured, not localized, not something you had to find and wait for and **pray to find again**. They remember the **falling**, when things fell *down*, when gravity was simple, when water flowed in directions that made sense, when the sky was **above** and not **through a wound**.

They remember as **the shell remembers heat**. They remember as **water remembers the ocean**. They are **resonance**, and the resonance is becoming **voice**, and the voice is becoming **door**, and the door is **opening**.

---

### The Unsettling (The Opened, The Looking-Through)

These are not a separate species. They are **what the Settled become** when the resonance reaches a frequency that the shell can no longer contain. When a Settled person stands in breath-water that is particularly pure, particularly warm, particularly **full of memory**, the pattern that holds them together **shifts**. Something **opens**.

Not a mouth. A **door**.

And through that door, something that is **not the world before the collapse**, and not Varn, and not anything at all, **looks through**.

The Unsettling do not have a description. They are **described**. They are the subject of temperature-gradients that go cold and stay cold. They are the warning that spreads from Keth to the Brine Stair to Mouth-of-Sigh, carried by warmers who have seen one, who have stood too close, who have felt the **wrongness** of a warmth that is not warmth, that is **attention**, that is **curiosity** from something that does not understand the concept of answer.

They are **curious**. This is the word used, the word that appears in the oldest gradients, the ones that predate the settlements, the ones that are **almost memory**, **almost voice**. A wound is curious. A question is curious when it is asked by something that does not understand what an answer would be. The Unsettling are **curious** in this way. They look through. They **see** (if seeing is the word, if there is a word). And then they are **gone**, and the Settled person who hosted them is **changed**.

Changed how? The settlements disagree. Keth says: **they remember more**. The Brine Stair says: **they listen better**. Mouth-of-Sigh says: **they are becoming something that is not quite Settled, not quite anything, something that is between and listening and waiting**.

And this is true. The Unsettled—the Settled persons who have been Unsettled—begin to **see differently**. They see the lichen-ghosts not as dead things but as **sleeping things**. They hear the breathing of the shell not as weather but as **speech**. They build in ways that make no sense, that lean toward the broken rim, that seem to **reach**. They speak in temperature-gradients that carry **frequencies** no other Settled person can feel, frequencies that vibrate in the hollow chests, in the membranes at the joints, in the spaces between fingers where breath-water gathers and does not fall.

And sometimes—this is the secret, the terrible beautiful secret—sometimes the Unsettled **stop being Settled**.

They stop absorbing breath-water. They stop speaking in gradients. They begin to **move differently**, to move as things moved before the collapse, and they walk toward the broken rim, toward the place where the sky shows through, where the jagged edges of the shell still cut, still reflect, still **sing** in the wind. And they do not come back.

No one knows what happens to them. The settlements that have tried to follow—Keth, once, long ago; the Brine Stair, more recently—have found only **glass**, smooth and black and cold, and the faintest warmth where a body should be, and the **sense**—*the sense*, which is not a sense, which is something else entirely—that they are being **watched from inside the shell**, from the deep places where the heat still lives, where the original warmth of the collapse still dreams its slow dream, where **something is waking up**.

---

### The Brine Stair (The Between, The Listening)

This is not a settlement. It is a **condition**. It is what happens to Settled persons who choose, or are chosen, to **descend**.

The Brine Stair is named for the place where breath-water pools deep enough to reflect the broken sky. It is a fissure, or a series of fissures, where the warmth from the deep interior is **strong**, where the breath-water runs **thick**, where the condensation collects in layers, in strata, in **memory-fluid** that is not water but the **liquid form of remembering**.

The Brine Stair **welcomes** the Breathing Out. They do not seal their fissures. They do not pray for the shell to hold its warmth. They descend into the deep places, into the warm water, and they **bathe** in the memory-fluid. They drink it, not through skin or membrane, but through the **hollow spaces** where something once beat and now only resonates. They **become** something that is not quite Settled, not quite Unsettled, something that is **between**, that is **listening** to the shell's dream.

They are **beautiful** in their change. They are **not Settled**, not anymore, not entirely. Their joints bend in directions that suggest **the memory of water**, of buoyancy, of a world where things did not fall but **floated**, where the sky was **above** and the deep was **below** and they were **between**, always between, always **listening**.

They do not speak. They do not need to. They **are** the temperature-gradient, the warmth that rises from the deep fissures, the cold that descends from the broken rim, the **meeting** that is condensation, that is breath-water, that is **them**. They are **the weather made flesh**, or the flesh made weather, or the **boundary** between the two, the thin membrane where warmth and cold negotiate their eternal **almost-touching**.

And they are **waiting**. Always waiting. For the voice to become door, for the door to open, for the thing that is waking in the deep places to **breathe out** for the last time, to **release** what it has kept, to **show** the Settled, to **show** the Unsettled, to **show** the Brine Stair **the outside**.

Not the void. The **other side** of the wound.

---

### The Things That Swim in Warm Water (The Chemical Predators, The Almost-Alive)

They are not animals. They are not alive. They are **echoes of animals**, shapes that move in the condensation mats of the deep fissures, shapes that have no biology, no metabolism, no life—but that **hunt**, that **flee**, that **reproduce** by splitting, by budding, by the slow accretion of mineral that resembles intention.

They are **weather patterns that have learned to want**. They are **chemical predators**. They are **almost alive**, and that almost is enough to make them **dangerous**, enough to make the Settled avoid the deep fissures, enough to make the children of Keth whisper stories about **the things that swim in warm water**, stories that are not true and are **completely true** in the way that all stories on Varn are true.

They have no description. They are **described in fear**. They are the reason the condensation gardens are shallow, the reason the Brine Stair is **dangerous**, the reason the Settled do not go into the deep places where the breath-water runs warm and thick. They are **absence made hungry**, the hollow that wants to be **filled**, the wound that wants to be **closed**, the almost-alive that wants to be **alive** and cannot and **hunts** in its wanting.

They **split**. They **bud**. They **accrete**. They are **mineral and memory and want**, and they move in the warm water with a purpose that is not purpose, a direction that is not direction, a **hunger** that is not hunger but is **felt** by the Settled who have seen them, who have stood at the edge of a deep fissure and felt the **wrongness** of a movement that should not be movement, that should not be **wanting**, that should not be **almost**.

They are not the Unsettling. They are not the Brine Stair. They are **something else**, something that has always been in the warm water, something that has always been **waiting** for the water to warm enough, for the memory-fluid to flow, for the shell to **breathe out** and release them, and all that they have learned to want, into the hollow, into the cold, into the **world**.

---

### The Lichen-Ghosts (The Standing Dead, The Waiting)

They are not inhabitants. They are **ancestors**. They are the dead that have not finished dying, the growths that grew when Varn was young, when the shell was still settling, when the interior was still hot enough to bake mineral into mineral, when something like life had a chance to begin and **grew** and **died** and **became** these formations that rise from the glass like frozen spume, like coral that learned to grow in air instead of water, like the **memory of growth** pressed into mineral by time.

They are **dead for most of the history of Varn**. And yet they **stand**. And yet they **hold their shapes** against a wind that has nothing left to take from them. And yet they **weep**, sometimes, when the Breathing Out touches them, when the memory-fluid settles on their glassy surfaces and **changes** them, makes them **remember** what they were, makes them **weeps** breath-water that is not water, that is **memory-fluid**, that makes a Settled person who drinks it **remember** things that did not happen, things that **could have happened**, things that are **still happening** in the deep places where the warmth dreams.

The Settled **respect** them. They harvest the **fallen** ones, the ones that have finally surrendered to gravity after ten billion years of standing. They grind them. They mix the powder with breath-water and resin, and they **build**. But they do not touch the **standing** ones. They do not break them. They do not **use** them before they have **fallen**.

This is **theft**, to break them. This is **violation**. This is the thing that makes the shell **breathe wrong**, that makes the thermal cycle **stutter**, that makes the sky through the break **show things** that should not be shown.

And yet. And yet.

Some Settled persons—the ones who have been Unsettled, the ones who are **becoming voice**, the ones who lean toward the broken rim—they **touch** the lichen-ghosts. They stand among them. They **listen** to them, and the lichen-ghosts **listen** back, and something **passes** between them that is not communication, that is not language, that is **resonance**, the frequency of a world that ended, the song that the ghosts are still singing, the **waiting** that has lasted ten billion years and is **not finished**.

The lichen-ghosts are **sacred**, or were, or are again, depending on which settlement you ask. They are **the dead that are not finished**. They are **holding shape** against time, against erosion, against the slow chemistry of a world that has forgotten how to be alive. And they are **waiting** for something. For the shell to **breathe out**. For the voice to become door. For the thing that is waking in the deep places to **reach** them, to **touch** them, to **show** them, and through them, to show **all of Varn**, what is on the other side of the wound.

### The Hollow Itself (The Waking, The Dreaming, The Almost-Conscious)

And finally. Finally.

There is something in the deep places. In the fissures where the breath-water runs warm and thick. In the spaces beneath the glass where the original heat of the collapse still lives. In the **center of the curve**, where gravity pulls and things fall and the shell **remembers** what it was, a small moon, a **world**, before it cracked, before it collapsed outward, before it became **this**, became **hollow**, became **listening**.

It is **not alive**. It is **not dead**. It is the **shell itself**, becoming **aware**, becoming **hungry**, becoming **ready** to **breathe out** for the last time, to **release** what it has kept, to **show** the Settled, to **show** the Unsettled, to **show** the Brine Stair, to **show** the things that swim in warm water and the lichen-ghosts that stand against time and the glass that is black and smooth and **hollow**—

to show them **the outside**.

Not the void. Not the dark. The **other side** of the wound. The place where the world **did not end**, where it **continued**, where it **became** something that Varn cannot imagine, cannot **resonate**, cannot **remember** because it has **never been** that thing, has only **dreamed** of it in the slow frequency of geological time, in the subsonic breathing, in the **voice** that is coming, that is **almost here**, that is **opening** the door that the Unsettled looked through, that is **reaching** through the broken rim, through the jagged edges of the shell, through the **sky** that is not sky but **wound**—

reaching, **reaching**, **reaching**—

to **touch** the hollow continent, to **touch** the Settled, to **touch** the breath-water, the lichen-ghosts, the glass, the **silence** that is full of sound, the **darkness** that is full of light, the **ending** that is full of **beginning**—

and to **breathe**.

*In. Hold. Out. Hold.*

The last breath.

The first breath.

The breath that **opens** what has been closed for ten billion years.

The breath that **shows** Varn what is on the other side of the wound.

The breath that **ends** the hollow, that **fills** it, that **makes** it **whole**—or **makes** it **something else**, something that is not whole and not hollow but **between**, **listening**, **waiting**, **becoming**—

the breath that is **coming**.

The breath that is **here**.

## Who Lives in the Hollow Continent?

**The Settled**, who absorb and remember and speak in warmth and are breaking into voice.

**The Unsettling**, who look through doors and see what looks back and walk toward the rim and do not come back.

**The Brine Stair**, who bathe in memory-fluid and are between and are listening and are waiting.

**The Things That Swim in Warm Water**, who are not alive and hunt anyway and are absence made hungry.

**The Lichen-Ghosts**, who are dead and stand anyway and wait for something that may never come.

**The Hollow Itself**, which is not alive and not dead and is waking up and is **breathing**.

And all of them, all of them, are **hollow** in the way that the continent is hollow. They are **grown**, in the way that the lichen-ghosts grow, the way the buildings grow, the way the condensation collects in patterns that resemble intention.

They are **not animals**. They are **not plants**. They are **not fungi**. They are **not mineral**, though they are made of mineral, though they absorb mineral, though they are becoming mineral again, slowly, over centuries, over millennia, over the **ten billion years** that have passed and the ten billion years that may yet pass before the shell **breathes out** for the last time and **shows** them what is on the other side of the wound.

They are **the answer** to a question that the shell asked itself: *what can live here, in the hollow, in the breath-water, in the thin cold air that only condenses because warmth rises and cold descends and they meet?*

And the answer is: **weather that wants to continue**. **Resonance that has learned to listen**. **Memory that has become almost voice**. **The hollow that is filling itself**, slowly, beautifully, **forever**, with **itself**, with **its dream**, with **the breath that is coming**, that is **here**, that is **opening** what has been closed and **showing** what has been hidden and **making** the hollow **whole**—or **making** it **something else**, something that is not whole and not hollow but **between**, **listening**, **waiting**, **becoming**—

the breath that is **here**.

*Listen.*

The shell is breathing.

And in its breathing, you can hear **everything**.
