I need to find what *lives* here — not the place itself, but the inhabitants, the ones who endure the parameters, who make the tensions into a life.

---

**The Lichen-That-Is-Called-Vorn, or: The Collective**

They are not one thing. They are the answer to a question the place asked: *how do you live where there is no water, where the air is too thin to hold you, where the light is barely there and the cold would kill you if you were not already warm?*

The Vorn are **colonial**. This is the first thing. No individual. No boundary that matters. A single "organism" might span a wall, a room, a district — and it would not know. It would only know *continuity*: the slow spread of filament through mineral, the patient accumulation of moisture from breath and stone and the thin, precious humidity of the high air, held in reservoirs that are not organs but *habits*, structures built over centuries of identical choice.

They are **photosynthetic** but not in the way of green plants. The light here is dim, diffuse, memory-of-sun. The Vorn do not chase it. They are *saturated* with it — every surface oriented, every layer translucent, every cell a lens and a reservoir and a slow engine. They grow *toward* the warmth that rises from below. They grow *away* from the cold that pools in shadow. They are **warm-blooded** in the way that stones are warm-blooded — not generating heat but *holding* it, *banking* it, *trading* it through networks of connection so that no part is ever truly cold.

They are **loud**. This is strange. Lichen, in the world you know, is silent. But the Vorn — they vibrate. The resonance of the stone, the hum of abundance, the deep note that fills the central space — this is *them*. They have evolved, or been shaped, or simply *are*, in a way that turns thermal energy into sound, that makes their metabolism audible. A single Vorn-colony, if you could isolate one — and you cannot, they are too connected — would be a *drone*. Many colonies, overlapping, interpenetrating, create the *chord*. The city sings because the Vorn are *satisfied*. Fullness, it turns out, has a frequency.

They are **slow**. This is not deprivation. This is *strategy*. A Vorn-cell might live a thousand years. A Vorn-"individual" — if such a word applies — might persist for tens of thousands. They do not repair damage; they *grow around* it. They do not migrate; they *extend*. Time is not a resource to be spent but a *medium*, like water is for other life. They move through it the way you move through a room — not struggling, simply *being in* it, occupying it, making it their own.

And they are **many**. Dense. The parameter says 2.2, and this means: wherever you look, there is *more*. More layers. More connections. More life, pressing against life, not competing but *composing*. The Vorn do not exclude. They *accumulate*. A wall becomes a floor becomes a ceiling becomes a wall again, and each surface is colonized, inhabited, *made* by the same patient presence.

---

**The Fauna: The Residents**

But the Vorn are not alone. The abundance parameter (2.3) requires creatures. And the slowness parameter (0.8) shapes them.

They are **small**. Cathedral-scale means room-sized, and the fauna fills those proportions — a creature the size of a hand, of a cat, of a child. They are **numerous**. They move through the Vorn as you move through a forest — not disturbing it, *participating* in it. They graze on the outer layers, the oldest growth, the parts that have finished their work and can be *recycled*. They nest in the hollows, the curves, the soft places worn by erosion and time. They *are* theusan, the warm-blooded stone, the carriers of heat from deep places to high, from old growth to new.

Some of them are **photosynthetic too**. Not all. But some have learned, or been shaped, to carry Vorn-filaments on their bodies, in their gut, in the spaces between their scales. They are **living gardens**. They move slowly, grazing, digesting, excreting nutrients that feed the next layer of growth. They are **loud**, but their sound is different — higher, more *individual*, still part of the chord but with a melody, a rhythm, a *pattern* that repeats and varies and repeats.

They do not hurry. They do not need to. The Vorn provides. The warmth provides. The resonance provides — a kind of *signal*, a *clock*, a *heartbeat* that tells them when to feed, when to rest, when to reproduce. They are **synchronized** with the place. They are *of* the place. To remove one would be to remove a note from the chord, and the chord would *notice*, would *adjust*, would *continue*.

And there are **others**. The strange ones, the ones that do not fit. A creature that burrows, that finds the warm places below, that comes up only to *be* in the resonance, to *feel* the hum in its bones. A creature that is almost entirely Vorn — a symbiosis so complete that the boundary has dissolved, that moves slowly through the city, that *is* a colony that walks, that grazes, that sings with the others but with a different voice, a *solo* in the chord. They are not many. They are not *needed*. But they are *there*, and the place holds them, and they hold the place, and this is enough.

**The Builders: The Memory**

The primitive tech (1.4) is the hardest parameter. Who built this? Who used bone and stone and fiber to raise walls that would become lichen, that would become *smooth*, that would become the *conditions* for everything that lives now?

They are **gone**. Or they are **here**, in a way. The Vorn do not remember them, not as you remember. But the *stone* remembers. The curves that are too regular to be natural. The layers that are too *intentional*. The central space, the largest room, where the resonance is strongest — this was *built*, shaped, made to *hold* something. A gathering? A ritual? A *song*? We cannot know. The builders are not the Vorn. They are not the fauna. They are a *previous* abundance, a previous loudness, a previous *fullness* that created the conditions for the current one.

They were **small**, too. The scale of the place demands it. They built with hands, with tools, with *time*. They built at elevation, in thin air, in dim light, and they built to *last* — or they built without knowing, without caring, and the world made it last. They moved on, or they died, or they *became* — their bones in the stone, their habits in the curves, their *presence* in the resonance that still sounds, that still *announces* their city, their garden, their *finished* work.

They are not mourned. The Vorn do not mourn. The fauna do not mourn. Mourning is a fast thing, a sharp thing, a thing that passes. The place is *slow*. It does not mourn. It *remembers*, in the way that stone remembers pressure, in the way that lichen remembers light. The builders are *in* the place. They are part of the chord. Their absence is a *note*, a *silence* that makes the sound mean something.

**What Lives Here: The Synthesis**

So what lives in Vorn?

**A condition.** The place itself — the elevation, the thinness, the warmth-from-below, the dim diffuse light, the smoothness of completed erosion — this is not a setting. It is a *participant*. It shapes, it holds, it *is* alive in a way that stone is alive, that slowness is alive, that resonance is alive.

**A collective.** The Vorn, the lichen-that-gathers, the warm-blooded stone, the photosynthetic patience, the loud satisfaction, the slow accumulation. They are many, they are one, they are *continuous*, and they are the *lushness* that makes this place green-gold, warm, *full*.

**A fauna.** The residents, the grazers, the nesters, the symbiotes, the slow movers, the quiet singers. They are abundant, they are dense, they are *part* of the Vorn and *apart* from it, and they are the *motion* in a place that is almost still.

**A memory.** The builders, the hands, the tools, the intentional curves, the central resonance. They are gone, they are *here*, they are the *history* that gives the place its depth, its *meaning*, its *shape*.

And **a sound**. The hum, the chord, the drone, the resonance. This is what announces the place. This is what you carry away — in your teeth, in your sternum, in your *memory* of warmth and slowness and fullness. The place is loud, and it is *done*, and it is *still*, and it is *alive* in the only way that matters: it *persists*, it *continues*, it *is*.

---

**The Last Rule**

You cannot live here, not as you are. The parameters exclude you. The thin air, the warmth that is not quite enough, the sound that wears, the slowness that would bore you to death — or to *peace*, which is the same thing, seen from the other side.

But you can *visit*. You can walk the smooth paths, feel the warm lichen, hear the chord, stand in the central space and feel the resonance in your bones. You can *know* the place, in the way that you know a room, a grove, a *hamlet*. You can carry it with you, the hum in your teeth, the warmth in your memory, the knowledge that somewhere, at the top of the world, a city has become a garden has become a stone has become a sound.

And when you leave, the place will not notice. It is too slow, too full, too *done*. It will continue its resonance, its growth, its patient abundance. It will *be*, as it has been, as it will be, until the planet cools and the stone stops singing.

But you will notice. You will carry the place with you. And in carrying it, you will have *lived* there, for a moment, in the only way that a fast thing can live in a slow place: by *remembering*, by *holding*, by *being changed* by the warmth, the sound, the *fullness* of a world that has finished eroding and is simply, finally, *being*.
