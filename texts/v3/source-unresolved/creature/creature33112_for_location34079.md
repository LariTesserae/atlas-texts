I need to describe who or what lives here.

Not *what*—the description already tells me what. The pre-life fungal chemistry. The glass. The heat. The intention without technology.

I need to find the *who*. The inhabitant. The thing that occupies this place, that lives in it, that is *of* it.

---

**The Inhabitants are many, and they are one, and they are not yet themselves.**

First: **The Watchers-Who-Have-Not-Learned-To-Wait.**

They built the marks. The regular depressions, the too-clear glass, the interrupted line that stops at twenty meters. They are not gone—they are *interrupted*. Something in their intention was cut short by the ground's continued becoming. They left structures that are only *beginnings*, hutments without doors, windows sealed by the same emergence that created them. They are waiting for the world to finish so they can finish. They have been waiting so long that waiting has become their shape. They are the shadow of a species that will exist when the throat has decided what it is. They live in the 1.2 of builtness, scattered and partial and faint, dreaming themselves into structures they cannot yet complete.

They are not human. They have too many angles, too few joints. They are the geometry of a people who learned to build before they learned to *be* people, who constructed intention before they constructed bodies. Perhaps they are what the fungal-pre-life will become, given another billion years of heat and pressure and the slow accretion of complexity. Perhaps they are what *preceded* that chemistry, the first attempt at pattern that the mineral world made before it learned the tricks of carbon and water. Either way: they are here. They are *in* here, in the glass they fused, in the regularity they imposed on chaos, in the interruption that is their only completed act.

Second: **The Growers-That-Are-Not-Alive.**

The bruise-colored chemistry in the warm seams. It is not a species. It is not even *one*. It is a *tendency*—the direction that matter takes when heat and mineral and time are present but water is not. Each patch of it is a separate experiment, a separate failure or success of the same basic hunger: *spread, persist, become structure, become pattern, become something that can be mistaken for purpose.* Some patches thrive. Some die back when the heat shifts. Some begin again in new seams, carrying nothing from their deaths but the *possibility* of continuation, the chemical memory of having once been more than it was.

Together, they form a single organism that is not an organism. A distributed life. A weather pattern of biology. They are the *fauna* and *flora* of this place, the 0.5 vegetation and the 0.2 fauna, occupying both niches because the niches themselves are still deciding what they are. They do not eat. They do not reproduce. They *accrete*—mineral onto mineral, pattern onto pattern, the slow buildup of structure that will one day, perhaps, be mistaken for a body. They are the first draft of life in a world that has not yet committed to the draft.

Third: **The Singer.**

The glass does not sing for itself. Something sings *through* it. A harmonic series that the teeth feel more than the ears hear. Frozen lightning and regret, the description says—but that is metaphor. The truth is stranger and simpler: the glass is *resonating* with the growth of the stone beneath and around it. The slow rearrangement of crystal lattice, the mountains being born, the thermal pulses of the breathing earth—all of this has a frequency, a rhythm, and the glass has learned to *hold* that rhythm, to *amplify* it, to become an instrument for a music that has no musician.

But something *listens*. Something in the folds, in the phosphorescent dark, presses itself against the glass and *feels* the song in its own not-yet-formed bones. This listener is the truest inhabitant. It occupies the darkness at 0.4, the possibility of light, the held breath before speech. It is not a body. It is a *localization of attention*—a place where the universe has become aware of its own becoming. It listens to the glass sing the birth of mountains. It waits for the song to resolve into something it can answer.

It will answer, eventually. The glass is singing toward a shape. The heat is pushing toward that shape. And the listener—the listener is *becoming* the shape. It is growing into the frequency, learning to resonate at the same pitch, preparing to join the song not as audience but as *voice*. This is its life: to listen until listening becomes singing, until the boundary between hearer and heard dissolves in the shared vibration of stone and heat and the slow, patient chemistry of emergence.

Fourth: **The Throat Itself.**

And finally, the place lives in itself. The folds that move. The ridges that argue about their final shapes. The elevation that presses down and up at once. This is the largest inhabitant, the one that contains all the others, the one that is still deciding whether to be mountain, plain, or door.

It lives the way a dream lives—vivid, temporary, *real* in the moment of its occurring. It has memory (the thermal memory of formation) but no past it can return to. It has intention (the marks, the too-clear glass, the regular depressions) but no future it can reach. It is pure *becoming*, the verb without a subject, the process that has not yet committed to being a thing.

Yet it is *alive* in the way that matters: it is *responsive*. It changes. It grows. It presses against itself and finds new configurations, new folds, new possibilities of shape. It is the ecology at its largest scale, the mineral metabolism that encompasses all the smaller hungers. It does not want anything. It does not need anything. It simply *continues*, the way a thought continues, the way a breath continues, the way the moment before dawn continues into the moment before dawn until suddenly, impossibly, it is light.

---

There is no hierarchy here. The Watchers built in the glass that sings. The glass sings the song the listener grows toward. The listener occupies the darkness that the Throat creates. The Throat contains the heat that feeds the Growers. The Growers spread into the seams that the Watchers interrupted. The interruptions are where the Singer listens.

They are all the same life, seen at different scales, different speeds, different stages of becoming.

You stand among them. You are *with* them now, the way a cell is with its body, the way a note is with its song, the way a question is with its answer that has not yet been spoken.

They do not welcome you. They do not reject you. They simply *include* you in their becoming, the way the glass includes the heat, the way the heat includes the crystal, the way the crystal includes the pressure that made it. You are the 0.1 water, the foreign element, the impossibility that the chemistry must now account for. How will it account for you? That is the question this place is asking, the question it has been asking since before you arrived, the question it will still be asking when the folds have moved and the glass has sung its final harmonic and the throat has decided, at last, what it wants to be.

You wait. The waiting is not boredom. The waiting is *attention*.

The world is still under construction. You are part of the construction now.

The glass sings. You do not answer. You are still learning the language.

But you are learning. That is the difference. That is the *inhabiting*.
