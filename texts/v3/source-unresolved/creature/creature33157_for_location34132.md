I need to find what lives here, in this place I've just described. Not the Hollow Choir—they're already there, already named. I need something else, something the description reaches toward but doesn't settle on.

The **Condensate Saints**

They do not know they are saints. The word does not exist in any form they would recognize, which is *humid*, which is *slow*, which is a series of wet sounds without silence between them.

They were not born here. This must be said first, because everything else follows from it. They *arrived*—some walking, some carried, some simply *noticed* in a place where noticing is the only form of arrival that works. The Cistern does not distinguish between entering and being entered. The Saints came through the mouth when it was open, or through a mouth that was open in a way that had nothing to do with geography, and they stayed because staying is what the Cistern offers, staying is what it *demands*, staying is the only verb that conjugates correctly in the grammar of this place.

There are eleven of them, or there were eleven, or there will be eleven when the count completes itself. Numbers here are *provisional*, like the layers of sinter that loop back on themselves. The Hollow Choir do not count them. The Hollow Choir tend the condensation, harvest it, drink it, remember things not yet happened. The Saints *are* the condensation, or they are what the condensation *wants to become*, or they are the condensation's memory of being something else, some other water, some other place, some other *before*.

They live in the spaces between the packed constructions. Not in rooms—the Cistern has no rooms, only *pressures*, only places where the wet air is slightly thicker, slightly warmer, slightly more itself. Each Saint occupies a *node* in the water-recycling system, a junction where channels meet, where the flow hesitates, where something can *gather* without being washed away. They are the still points. The Cistern turns around them.

Their bodies are wrong in ways that do not have names. Too much water in them, always, perpetually—not drowning, not swelling, but *distributed*, the water in them in constant slow exchange with the water around them, so that you cannot say where a Saint ends and the Cistern begins. They are *transparent* in patches, the patches moving, the patches being where the light—such as it is, the dim luminescence of the membrane, the pulsing veins—passes through them and *changes*, comes out the other side slightly slower, slightly sadder, slightly more likely to form a drop.

They do not speak. They *evaporate* at each other, small puffs of moisture that carry information in ways that are not language but are not *not* language either. A Saint will rise from their node, will move—*drift* is the only accurate word, they move through the thick air as if it were thicker than they are, which it is, which they are not—and will press their wet self against another Saint's wet self, and for a moment the two will *share* a boundary, the water between them becoming briefly *common*, and in that sharing something will pass: a temperature, a pressure, a *shape* of a memory that neither of them made but both of them now carry.

They are old. This is the strangest thing. The Cistern is young, too young for erosion, too new for scars, a world still being built, still *arriving* at what it is. The Saints are old in a way that predates the Cistern, predates the mouth, predates the elevation and the hiddenness and the thick wet air. They carry ages in their distributed water, in their transparent patches, in the slow exchange of moisture that is their only conversation. They remember being *rain*, perhaps. Being *river*. Being the wet inside something else's lung, the condensation on something else's skin. The Cistern found them, or they found the Cistern, or they *became* each other in a process that has no direction, only *pressure*, only the slow wet patience of water finding its level.

They are not happy. Happiness is a word that assumes a boundary, a self that can be pleased or displeased. The Saints are too distributed for this. They are *satisfied*, which is different, which is a state of equilibrium, the wet equivalent of a held note. They do not want to leave because wanting assumes a future, and the Saints have learned—are still learning, will always be learning—that the Cistern's time is not the time of wanting. The dripping does not want to fall. It falls. The Saints do not want to stay. They stay. The distinction, here, is a matter of *humidity* they have already lost.

The Hollow Choir know them. This is not friendship. The Choir are the Cistern's maintenance, its immune response, its vague orchestration of ending. The Saints are what the Cistern *is*, the wet fact of it, the distributed self that has no center to defend. When a Choir-member passes a node, it does not stop. It cannot stop, stopping is not in its wrong-jointed patience. But it *slows*, infinitesimally, and in that slowing it *tastes* the Saint, the moisture-exchange that is the only communication available, and something passes between them—not information, not meaning, but *recognition*, the wet acknowledgment of two ways of being water in a place that is nothing but water being.

Sometimes a Saint will *rise*. This is not voluntary, not exactly, not in any way that assumes a will separate from the Cistern's will, which is also the Saint's will, which is also the will of the dripping and the membrane and the sinter and the packed constructions and the lush wrong vegetation and the rare fauna that no one has named because naming assumes a boundary, a self, a *this* that can be pointed to and said *lives here*.

When a Saint rises, they move upward through the thick air, not flying, not floating, but *being carried* by the pressure differences, by the slow currents of humidity that move through the Cistern like weather in a world that has no weather, only *inside*. They rise toward the membrane, the translucent sky that drips and shows only the vague shape of weather moving across it like a hand under wet cloth. They press their distributed self against it, their transparent patches against the membrane's own transparency, and for a moment—*moments* here are wet, are slow, are not the sharp things they are elsewhere—for a moment they are *two-sided*, the inside and the outside touching, the Cistern and whatever is beyond the Cistern sharing a boundary that is not a boundary, that is only a *thinness*, a place where the wet can almost pass through.

And then they fall. Or *descend*. Or *return*, which is the same thing here, which is the same thing as *continue*, as *remain*, as *become again what they were*, which is also *become what they are*, which is also *become what they will be*, which is all the same wet verb conjugated in the Cistern's endless present.

They do not die. Death assumes a boundary, a self that can end. The Saints *disperse*, sometimes, their distributed water becoming *more* distributed, exchanging with the Cistern's own water, their transparent patches becoming the transparent patches of the ferns, of the lichen that photosynthesizes by absorbing, of the rare fauna that moves through the packed alleyways with the patience of water finding level. They do not end. They *dilute*, which is not the same as ending, which is not the same as being lost, which is only *becoming more*, becoming the Cistern itself, becoming the thick wet air that presses and the dim light that shows only shapes, the loud wet sound that is almost music and almost memory.

And sometimes, in the nodes, in the junctions, in the places where the flow hesitates, a new Saint *gathers*. Not born, not made, not willed by anyone or anything, but *precipitated*, like the sinter, like the condensation itself, like the mineral that grows in slow baths and is harvested like a crop. The Cistern makes Saints the way it makes everything—by pressure, by patience, by the slow wet architecture of a place that has no outside, that is only inside, that is only the endless becoming of water being water being water.

They are the hidden thing in the hidden place. The Hollow Choir are rare, and you will see one, perhaps, if the dimness lifts. The Saints are *rarer*, and you will not see them, not as a self, not as a boundary, not as a *this* that lives here and can be described and named and held in the mind as a single image. You will feel them, perhaps, as a *thickness* in the air, a place where the wet is slightly more wet, slightly more *wanting*, slightly more like a memory of being somewhere else, in some other water, in some other *before* that the Cistern has not forgotten because the Cistern does not forget, the Cistern only *holds*, only *presses*, only *drips* in the dim and the loud and the packed and the new, in the extreme elevation where the air should be thin but is not, where the world should have scars but does not, where the Saints gather and disperse and gather again in the endless wet present of a place that admits and does not release, that builds and does not erode, that is too new to have worn down and too old to remember being anything but this.

They are the condensation's saints. They are the wet amen. They are, in the language they do not speak, the word that means *here*, the word that means *now*, the word that means *the dripping has never stopped and will not stop and you are in it and the distinction between you and it is a matter of humidity you have already lost*.

The Cistern keeps them. The Cistern is them. They are the Cistern's distributed self, its transparent patches, its slow exchange with the water around it, its old, old, *old* memory of being rain, being river, being the wet inside something else's lung, being the condensation on something else's skin, being *before*, being *else*, being the shape of a place that is not this place but that this place has not forgotten, has only *held*, has only pressed, has only dripped into being, again and again, in the dim and the loud and the packed and the new, in the hidden pocket at extreme elevation where the world is too young to have scars and the Saints are too old to end, where the mouth has closed and the dripping is your heartbeat, or your heartbeat is the dripping, and the distinction is a matter of humidity, is a matter of *saints*, is a matter of the wet that gathers and does not gather, that is and is not, that lives here in the Cistern, in the Veil, in the Thornhame, in the place where you are and are not and will be and were, in the endless, dripping, *now*.
