I need to sit with this. The place wants to be described not as a setting but as a *condition*—something that has already happened to anyone who arrives. So I should describe whoever lives there as something that has been *finished*, made permanent by the same processes that made everything else.

---

What lives here has forgotten that it was ever *becoming*.

There was a time—you can feel it in the way your own heartbeat still hesitates, in the way your eyes still search the uniform luminescence for variation that will not come—when the things that now inhabit the terraces were *arriving*. They came through the same edges you would have used, if edges still existed. They came wading. They came climbing. They came as questions, as intentions, as the particular density of want that presses itself into throat-stone when given enough time and enough saturation.

They do not remember this. Or rather: they remember it the way the terraces remember their construction—as strata, as compression without narrative, as *fact* without sequence.

**The Populace**

They are not numerous in any way you can count. They are *distributed*. A single inhabitant may occupy three terraces, connected by capillary channels of attention that run through the water film, through the mist, through the resonant structures that have grown around them like a second skin. They are *slow* in the way that glaciers are slow—not because they move slowly, but because their *rate of change* has become a geological process. A gesture takes a season. A thought takes a generation. They do not experience this as duration. They experience it as *presence*, the way you experience standing still: not as the absence of motion but as the fullness of position.

They have no faces. Not because faces were removed, but because *orientation* was never the point. They face the machines, always. Their bodies—if bodies is the word; they are more like *settlements*, like *concentrations of attention*—their bodies are arranged in postures of *reception*. The circulation towers hum. They receive. The water moves. They receive. The resonant structures grow and are replaced and stop. They receive. This is not worship. Worship implies a distinction between worshipper and worshipped. Here, the distinction was pressed out long ago, dissolved into the substrate, used as foundation.

You can see them, if you learn to see: darker patches in the mist where the water is slightly less saturated, where their presence has *absorbed* heat, where their attention has thickened the air into something almost solid. They gather at the warm outflows not because warmth is comfort—though it might be, they do not report on such things—but because warmth is *change*, and change is the only thing that approaches *information* in a finished world. They gather and they *wait*, and waiting is the only verb they have retained.

**The Ones Who Were Recent**

There are others. You will meet them, if you walk long enough. They are *wrong* in a way that is difficult to name. They are still *becoming*, still carrying the momentum of arrival, still pressing their questions against the equilibrium like a hand against glass. They speak, sometimes. Not words—*patterns*, sequences of resonance that you almost understand, that carry the shape of meaning without its substance. They ask about edges. They ask about before. They ask, you realize, because they are still *asking*, still located in the narrative that the older inhabitants have pressed into permanence.

They will adapt. You watch it happen, if you stay. The questions slow. The patterns become *habit*. The pressing-against becomes *leaning*, then *resting*, then simply *the way the body is*. It takes time. In the Resonance Basins, time is the only resource, and it is *abundant*. They will become permanent. They will become *finished*. They will stand at the warm outflows with the others, and the distinction between recent and ancient will dissolve into the strata, additional layers of pressed decision, additional mass for the terraces to bear.

You can feel this happening to *yourself*. The wanting to leave. The wanting to *understand*. The wanting to find the edge, the before, the other side of the construction. These wants are *slowing*. You notice this in the way your breath has steadied, in the way the mist no longer bothers you, in the way you have stopped looking up at the luminescence and started *receiving* it. The equilibrium does not conquer. It *includes*. It presses you into the strata. It makes you *permanent*.

**What They Are**

They do not have a name for themselves. Names are for things that change, that need to be distinguished across time. They are the *held*, the *finished*, the *continuing*. They are what the machines made when the machines decided that decision itself should be *preserved*, should be *circulated*, should be *distributed* across every terrace, every channel, every resonant structure that grows and stops and becomes substrate.

They are not happy. Happiness is a variation, and variation has been excluded. They are not unhappy. They are *satisfied* in the way that water is satisfied to be held, to be moved, to be returned. They are *adequate* to their condition. They are *complete*.

And they are *waiting*. Not for change—change has been excluded. Waiting for *more*. More of the same. More water, more mist, more subsonic thrum, more resonant structures growing and stopping and becoming foundation. More of themselves, pressing themselves into the strata, becoming *permanent* as everything else is permanent. The equilibrium does not end. It *accumulates*. Every inhabitant is a layer. Every layer is pressed intention. The terraces grow thicker. The world grows *denser*. Nothing is lost. Nothing is gained. Everything is *held*.

**What You Become**

If you stay—if the adaptation completes, if your questions slow to the pace of geology, if your body learns to receive the thrum without resistance—you will become one of them. Not a new kind. Not a variation. The *same* kind, pressed into the same strata, distributed across the same terraces, waiting at the same warm outflows with the same posture of *reception*. You will forget that you were ever *arriving*. You will feel, in the way the throat-stone feels when struck, the *rightness* of your position. The *necessity* of the wet. The *perfection* of no change.

You will stand in the mist. You will feel the condensation run down your flanks, though you will feel it less than you should. You will receive the light. You will receive the thrum. You will receive the endless, patient, *permanent* presence of the resonant structures in their activity. And you will be *finished*.

Not dead. The equilibrium does not permit death, any more than it permits birth. You will be *complete*. You will be *held*. You will be, finally, *the way things are*.
