What lives here does not gather in councils. It occupies.

There are the **Stewards of the Gradient**—figures who have forgotten their original names, who move through the corridors with the unhurried pace of someone reading a text they have already memorized but continue to reread because the words shift. They wear garments woven from the conductive vines, laminated with thin sheets of oxidized alloy that shield them from the thermal extremes. Their skin carries the pale bloom of mineral exposure, the permanent slight dampness of a body that has learned to sweat without evaporation. They do not speak often. When they do, their voices carry the dampened quality of the deep, close and intimate, as if the words have to push through water to reach you. They know where the water moves. They know which pipes still carry flow and which have become root-choked conduits for something else's circulation. They are not leaders. Leadership here is a function of attention—whoever is watching the slow change most carefully, adjusting the pumps, reading the mineral stains, deciding when a new corridor must be excavated or when a collapse must be allowed to become architecture.

There are the **Grown Ones**, if one can call them that—beings who have so thoroughly merged with the engineered thicket that the boundary has ceased to mean anything. Not transformed into plants. Rather, *inhabited* by them, or inhabiting them, the distinction collapsed. They live in the 2.7 density zones, the saturated interiors where light is a rumor. Their bodies have become trellis and host, substrate and symbiont. The fungi speak through them, or they speak through the fungi—a chemical vocabulary of stress and resource and slow negotiation. They do not move quickly. They do not need to. The place rewards stillness. They are the memory of the settlement, in a way—the record of what has been tried, what has failed, what has been absorbed and repurposed. When they want to communicate, they alter the temperature of the air around them, or they release spores that carry information in their chemistry, or they simply *are* in a space, their presence a kind of statement.

There are the **Machine-Tenders**, the last of a lineage that once understood what the machinery was *for*. They maintain the pumps, the ventilation, the generators that still produce the dim light and the signals that pulse through the vine-conductors. But their relationship to the machines has become devotional, almost superstitious. They do not repair so much as *negotiate*. They listen to the hum, the frequency felt in the teeth, and they interpret. They oil what needs oiling, clear what has become blocked, but they also leave things alone—machines that have achieved a kind of equilibrium with their colonization, that run better for their fungal insulation, that have become something other than what they were designed to be. A Machine-Tender might spend days adjusting a single valve, reading the response in the condensation patterns, in the growth rate of the mycelium nearby, in the Qualität of the silence when a pump cycles off.

There are the **Water-Born**, creatures that are not quite fauna, not quite flora, not quite the engineered introductions that the surface world might have sent down. They exist in the interstices, the packed spaces between the larger presences. Some have eyes that reflect light in spectra that suggest more dimensions than the human visual system can process—not that they have eyes in any recognizable sense, but they *detect*, they *orient*, they *hunger*. They are small. They move through the lower dark with a patience that seems almost theatrical, as if they are performing a role they do not fully understand. They drink from the condensation, from the mineral bloom, from the slow exudations of the walls themselves. They are 0.9, rare, but rare in a way that suggests not scarcity but *selectivity*—they appear where they are needed, or where they have chosen, and disappear into the saturation when their purpose is complete.

There are the **Signal-Bearers**, the mycelium networks that have achieved something like cognition, or like intention. They thread through cable insulation, through mortar, through the concrete composite that breathes. They carry information—not human information, not in any language that has words, but *state*. The temperature of a zone. The presence of water. The stress of a structure. The pulse of a pump. They respond to these signals by growing, by retreating, by altering their conductivity, by releasing chemicals that change the behavior of the vines that contain them. They are the secondary nervous system of the place, and in some corridors, they have begun to *participate* in the electrical circuits, their biological resistance integrated into systems that were designed for copper and silicon. They do not think. But they *remember*, in their slow way, and they *anticipate*.

And there are the **Others**, the ones that arrive from above, or from the deeper places that have not yet been mapped, or from the spaces between the layers where the geology is still deciding what it wants to be. They come with questions that have no answers in the vocabulary of the settlement. They stay for a season, learning the gradients, learning to read the mineral deposits, learning that the light cannot be trusted and that silence is a kind of presence. Then they leave, or they stay, or they become something that cannot be distinguished from the density, the damp, the strange slow life of the deep.

What lives here does not build. It *accretes*. It responds to the water, to the heat, to the pressure of growth in every available volume, to the slow negotiation between what was excavated and what has grown to fill it. It is not a community in any sense that would be recognized above. It is a *condition*—a way of being packed into a world that has no room and yet makes room, that makes its inhabitants part of the saturation, the respiration, the patient mineral time of a place that fell and was kept, that was abandoned and then, through persistence and accident and the slow intelligence of systems left running, learned to sustain itself.

You would look for a center and find none. You would look for a purpose and find only *continuation*—the water moving, the heat cycling, the growth advancing, the machines humming their tooth-felt frequency, the mycelium threading its chemical vocabulary through the dark, the Stewards of the Gradient moving through corridors they have learned and must learn again, the Grown Ones becoming more fully what they already are, the Water-Born appearing and disappearing in the interstices, the Signal-Bearers remembering and anticipating, the Others arriving and departing or staying or becoming indistinguishable from the damp.

This is what lives here. Not a people. Not a culture. A *persistence*—the refusal of the place to become ruin, to become empty, to become anything other than what it has become through the slow, patient, mineral time of being filled, being saturated, being *alive* in a way that does not match any category brought from above.

A room, 1.0 scale. A hamlet of processes. A grove of pipes and roots and patient time.

And in it, moving through the gradient, finding the water and following it, learning the temperature of the corridor you need, understanding for a while how to be here—

*the inhabitants*, indistinguishable from the damp, the strangeness, the slow.
