What lives here cannot be called flora or fauna in any taxonomy that remembers the sun. They are the End-Stage Biota, organisms that have outlived photosynthesis, that metabolize the heat of the mantle and the slow rot of stone. They are patient to the point of geology, and they occupy this cathedral not as inhabitants but as *continuations*—the chamber is their exhalation, their shed skin, their collective dreaming.

**The Carpet-Dwellers** form the first skin of the world. The fungal mats—those tapestry-sized growths of pale, pulpy flesh—are not individual organisms but colonial super-entities, thick as quilts, warm to the touch, pulsing with a peristaltic rhythm slower than a heartbeat. They breathe. You can feel it if you press your palm to their surface: a systolic expansion, a diastolic collapse, the gentle tide of chemosynthetic respiration drawing minerals from the thermal vents below. They do not green; they glow with a faint, internal phosphorescence (2.5) that registers not as light but as a warmth against the back of your eyelids, a bioluminescence turned inward, illuminating only the dark blood of their own tissues. Between them grow the columnar things—towers of soft, fleshy crystal that expand and contract like the lungs of some vast, sleeping beast, filtering the warm, iron-heavy air through membranes that taste of copper and predate chemistry.

**The Drifting Sediments** are the megafauna. They are amphibious in a medium thicker than water—the air itself, swimming through it like living silt. Some possess too many joints: segmented things like centipedes made of translucent cartilage and mineral deposits, each articulation clicking with a sound too low to hear but felt in the teeth. Others have too few—seamless, boneless tubes of gelatinous matter that flow across the fungal carpets with a sluggish, deliberate grace, leaving trails that shimmer with residual heat. They are islands (1.3), each occupying its own territory of several square meters, spaced across the empty nave like dark continents in a black sea. They do not cast shadows—there is no light to cast them with—but they displace the darkness (0.0), appearing as mobile holes in the void, shapes of *slightly-less-absolute-black* that move with the patience of erosion. When they pass, the air thickens behind them, as if the void itself recoils from their absence.

**The Vertical Ones** exploit the water that climbs (0.9). Where the stone sweats and the microscopic rivulets defy gravity, rising upward in subtle, wrong geometries, these creatures have evolved to live in the ascending currents. They are filter-feeders, translucent sacks anchored to the walls by root-like tendrils of calcified tissue, their bodies perpetually bathed in the warm, mineral-laden flow that moves against the natural order. They are sessile, ancient, and vast—some have grown for millennia, their bodies distended with the slow accumulation of iron and older things, their surfaces encrusted with the same crystalline structures that punctuate the chamber.

**The Resonant Ones** are the singers. They are the creatures that emit the low, sternum-vibrating tones (1.7) that merge with the chamber’s ambient roar. They possess too many joints—hundreds of articulated segments that they can vibrate independently, creating harmonics that match the frequency of the rock itself. They are the loudest life here, their song a constant drone that seems to come from their bones, or perhaps from the stone they inhabit. They move rarely, and when they do, it is across the fungal mats with a grinding, tectonic slowness (1.1), their many legs leaving the faint indentations (1.3)—patterns that look like writing, or like the trails of something that moves only once per decade. They are the archivists of the place, their passage marking time in the soft flesh of the world.

**The Crystal-Tenders** are the smallest and strangest. They live within the technology (2.6)—those crystalline stalagmites that pulse with a slow, warm light, that register not as illumination but as a thickening of the air, a change in pressure. These creatures are microscopic, or nearly so: colonies of chemosynthetic bacteria encased in the lattice of the ancient machines, metabolizing the waste heat of the geometry-holders, cleaning the surfaces of the crystal with cilia that brush against the smooth, worn stone. They are the symbiotes of the old civilization, the last caretakers, ensuring that the structures which hold the chamber’s shape—through means that resemble gravity but are not—continue to function across epochs. When the crystals pulse, the tenders pulse with them, their biochemistry synchronized to the ancient rhythm.

**The Center-Dweller** is a matter of debate, for the center of the chamber is a void (0.2), a pooling of absolute darkness (0.0) so thick it has weight, pressing against the skin. Most life avoids it; the fungal carpets thin at the perimeter, and the drifting creatures maintain a respectful distance, as if the darkness there is a predator. But occasionally, in the deepest dark, something moves—a shape of slightly-less-black that is larger than the others, that does not drift but *hangs*, suspended in the viscous absence. It is warm (2.1), impossibly so, a pocket of heat in the cold void. It does not eat, does not breathe in any way that can be observed, does not make the resonant tones of the others. It simply *is*, a hole in the world where the world has forgotten itself, perhaps the oldest thing here, perhaps the chamber’s dream of itself, waiting for the erosion to finally claim the walls and let the hollow collapse into the mantle below.

They are all connected by the water (1.5), by the drone (1.7), by the slow, loud song of existence in the dark. They do not rush. They migrate across the chamber over the span of years. They metabolize time itself, drawing energy from the thermal vents, from the friction of stone against stone, from the decay of the ancient technology. They are the life of the end of time, clinging to the surfaces of the world while the center remains a void, warm and wet and hollow, humming forever in a room the size of a cathedral, at the top of the world.
