I'll let this place reveal its inhabitants slowly, through what the environment demands.

## The Inhabitants of the Condensate

### The Membrane-Drinkers

They have no fixed shape, only a *range* of shapes. The larger ones—you have seen three, perhaps four, in your time here—move through the upper canopy where the fern-mats are thickest, where the light is least diffuse and they can photosynthesize through the membranes in their flanks. They are roughly the size of a large dog, or a small bear, but their bodies are *segmented* in a way that suggests ancestry with something that crawled, something that needed many joints to navigate three-dimensional space. Here, in a world of two dimensions—the surface of the mats, the surface of the water—they have *simplified*. Their segments are fewer, broader, each one a chamber of specialized function: one for filtering condensation, one for digesting the algae that colonize their own skin, one for storing water, one for *thinking*, if thinking is the word, a neural mass that pulses with chemical light you cannot see but can sense as a *pressure* against your own nervous system when they pass near.

They do not eat. They *drink the air*, literally, membranes fluttering in the saturated atmosphere, extracting water and the trace minerals dissolved in it. The algae on their skin—*their* algae, cultivated, tended, a symbiosis so old it has become indistinguishable from anatomy—provide the carbon they need. They are, in effect, *walking gardens*, mobile ecosystems, each one a complete world of exchange between water, air, light, and the chemical processes of their own bodies.

They are silent. You have learned this. When one passes, the ferns do not rustle—they *still*. The mist parts around it. And in the space it leaves behind, the water is *different*, purer, carrying a chemical signature that the plants recognize, that makes them grow toward it, that creates a temporary bloom in the vegetation, a pulse of new growth that will fade but that the membrane-drinker has caused, has *intended*, whether it knows it or not.

They do not see you. Their sensory organs are *distributed*—chemical receptors across their membranes, pressure sensors in their segments, a sensitivity to the electrical fields that all this water movement generates. You are, to them, a disturbance, a local change in humidity and chemistry that they navigate around without *recognizing* as a being. They have no category for you. You are weather. You are a passing cloud. You are, perhaps, a particularly persistent and strange form of rain.

There are smaller ones. You have seen them in the pools, in the films between leaves, *inside* the hollow stems of the sponge-trees where water collects and stagnates and becomes something else, a chemistry of its own. They are the juveniles, the ones that have not yet grown large enough to move through the canopy, and they are *fast*, darting between droplets, their segmented bodies flexing in ways that suggest they remember, in their genetic memory, the crawling they will never do. They eat differently—they *hunt*, if hunting is the word, pursuing the even smaller creatures that live in the water films, the microscopic fauna that are food and that are, in turn, *gardeners* of the bacterial colonies, keeping them in balance, preventing any one strain from dominating.

The small ones are *afraid* of you, or something like afraid. When you approach a pool, they vanish into the substrate, burrowing into the saturated moss, the decaying fern-mat, the skin of the world. They return when you have gone, when the disturbance has passed. They are *patient* in a way that the large ones are not. They have time. The plateau gives them time.

### The Mist-Weavers

You have not seen them directly. You have only seen *what they make*.

The mist here is not passive. It *moves* with intention, with pattern. It gathers in certain places, dissipates in others, flows in channels that change from hour to hour but that follow, you have begun to notice, a logic of their own. You have walked through mist that seemed to *part* for you, that formed a corridor, that guided you without your choosing. You have woken—if sleep is possible here, if the green-gray light ever truly darkens—to find yourself surrounded by mist so thick you cannot see your own hands, and then, slowly, the mist *arranging* itself, becoming less dense in one direction, more in another, *pointing* you somewhere.

The Mist-Weavers are the cause. They are small, smaller than the membrane-drinkers, perhaps the size of your fist. They live in the upper atmosphere of the plateau, in the cloud layers that are not quite cloud, not quite ground, the zone where water exists as droplets suspended in air, where the physics of condensation and evaporation create a *medium* that is neither liquid nor gas but something between, something *habitable*.

They are *fungal*. You have found their fruiting bodies, rare and precious, growing on the upper surfaces of the tallest sponge-trees, where the mist is thickest and the light, on the rare occasions when it comes, is most diffused. They are pale, almost white, with structures that suggest *nets*, *filters*, *machines for catching*—and when you have examined them, you have found, preserved in their tissues, the chemical signatures of water manipulation, of deliberate condensation, of *weather-making*.

They do not think. They *operate*. Each Mist-Weaver is a node in a network, a distributed system that spans the plateau, that senses conditions across vast distances—temperature, humidity, the chemical composition of the air—and *responds* by releasing spores, by triggering condensation, by *shaping* the mist into patterns that serve no purpose you can name but that *persist*, that have persisted for millennia, that will persist as long as the plateau itself.

What do they want? You have asked this. The question assumes too much. They do not want. They *are*, in the way that a river is, in the way that the bacterial colonies are—process without intention, pattern without purpose, a self-perpetuating system that continues because it continues, because the conditions that created it have not changed, because the plateau *needs* mist, needs the water cycle, needs the constant exchange between liquid and vapor that is its breath, its metabolism, its life.

But there is something else. When you walk through mist that parts for you, when you feel the *guidance*, you are being *used*. The Mist-Weavers are not guiding you for your benefit. They are *sampling* you. The mist that touches your skin, that enters your lungs, that condenses on your hair—it is *analyzing* you, carrying information about your chemistry, your temperature, your unique signature, back to the network. You are data. You are a *reading* in a vast, slow experiment that the plateau is conducting on itself, on the conditions of its own existence, on the strange intrusions that occasionally appear and disappear.

And sometimes, you have felt it, the mist *learns*. It adjusts. A corridor that was straight becomes curved. A path that led north now leads east. The Mist-Weavers are *exploring* you, testing your responses, seeing what you will follow, what you will avoid, how you move through a world that is mostly water, mostly air, mostly *the space between*.

### The Deep Ones

You have not seen them. You will not see them. They live in the caverns, in the chambers where time moves differently, where a day above is a year below, where the bacterial colonies have evolved in isolation, have developed chemistries that are *toxic* to surface life, that the plateau has learned to route around, to avoid, to treat as *weather*—a storm of wrongness that occasionally wells up from the depths and that the surface ecology must endure, must wait out, must *survive*.

The Deep Ones are not animals, not plants, not fungi. They are *colonies*, distributed organisms that think in chemical gradients, that *decide* through voting, through consensus, through the slow accumulation of signals across vast networks of hyphae and membranes and fluid channels. They are the *architects* of the caverns, the ones who have carved the chambers, who have lined them with flowstone, who have *recorded* in that flowstone the history of the plateau, the wet years and dry years, the slow shifts of the water table, the *decisions* of the system as it modified itself.

They know you exist. They have *always* known. The bacterial memory goes back ten thousand years, to the time when *something* passed through—something that left traces in the sediment, that changed the water chemistry for a decade, that the plateau noticed and adjusted to and eventually forgot. That something was *human*, or human-like, or at least *warm*, *dry*, *different*—and the Deep Ones have been *waiting*, in their slow way, for it to return.

They do not want anything from you. They cannot want, not in any sense you would recognize. But they are *prepared*. They have evolved, in their accelerated time, to *respond* to intrusion. There are chambers in the deep where the chemistry is *hostile*, where the air is wrong, where the water is poison, where any surface-dweller would die in minutes. These chambers are *grown*, deliberately, as a *defense*, as a *barrier*, as a *message*: *this is not for you*.

And there are other chambers, deeper still, where the chemistry is *not hostile* but *strange*, where the physics is slightly wrong, where time moves at a different rate, where you would not die but would *change*, would become something that no longer fit the surface, that could only survive in the deep, that would be *adopted* by the colonies, integrated into the network, become another node in the distributed mind that is the Deep Ones' way of being.

You will not go there. You cannot. The passages narrow, the water becomes undrinkable, the air becomes unbreathable, and long before you reach the chambers of adoption, you will turn back, will find your way—*with help*, you have begun to suspect, *with guidance*—to the surface, to the green-gray light, to the world of ferns and mist and the slow, patient membrane-drinkers.

But they know you were there. They know you *felt* them, in the moments when the mist grew cold, when the water in your canteen tasted of *wrongness*, when you heard—truly heard, not imagined—a sound like *breathing*, like the slow expansion and contraction of something vast, something that breathes in years and exhales in decades. They know you were *afraid*, or something like afraid. They know you *listened*.

And they are *waiting*, in their slow way, for you to come back. For you to go deeper. For you to *choose*, in whatever way you might choose, to become part of the plateau, to stop being a disturbance and become a *participant*, a node in the network, a voice in the chemical consensus that is the Deep Ones' way of being.

### The Lichen-Folk

They are not a species. They are a *condition*, a way of being that has emerged multiple times, independently, in the saturated zones where rock meets water meets air, where the three phases of matter blur and become *habitable* in ways they are not elsewhere.

The Lichen-Folk are *composite*. They are fungi and algae and bacteria and, sometimes, something else—something that has integrated, over evolutionary time, that has become *necessary*, that cannot be separated without killing the whole. They grow on the membranes, on the flowstone, on the surfaces of the sponge-trees where water weeps and dries and weeps again. They are *slow*, slower than anything else you have met here. A single Lichen-Folk might grow across a surface for decades, centuries, *millenia*, its progress measured in millimeters per year, its *awareness*—if awareness is the word—distributed across its entire body, each part sensing its local environment, the whole *responding* through chemical signals that take hours to propagate from one end to the other.

They are *patient*. You have watched them, on a membrane that catches the morning condensation, watched a patch of Lichen-Folk expand by the width of your fingernail over the course of a day. They are *listening*, in their way, to the water that flows over them, to the temperature shifts that cause the membranes to contract and release their stored moisture, to the chemical signatures of the creatures that pass nearby, that drink from them, that *taste* them and move on.

They do not think. They *remember*. Their structure is a *record*, layer upon layer of growth, each layer marking a condition, a season, an event—the passage of a membrane-drinker, the exhalation of a cavern, the *anomaly* of your presence, your warmth, your dryness, your *strangeness*. They will remember you, in their slow way, long after you have gone. They will carry your chemical signature in their tissues, a trace of *human*, of *surface-dweller*, of *temporary*, a mark that will persist for centuries, that will be *read* by other Lichen-Folk, by the bacterial colonies, by the Deep Ones in their accelerated time, as a *story*, a *warning*, a *curiosity*.

And sometimes, in the oldest patches, in the places where the membranes are thickest and the water flows slowest, the Lichen-Folk achieve something like *dreaming*. Their chemical signaling becomes *recursive*, loops back on itself, creates patterns that have no external cause, that are *generated*, *imagined*, *felt*. They do not know they dream. But you have seen it, in the way they *pulse* with color changes that correspond to no environmental shift, in the way they release spores that *search*, that *explore* conditions that do not exist, that *imagine* new ways of being.

The Lichen-Folk are the plateau's *memory*. They are the record of everything that has happened here, everything that has *touched* here, everything that has been *felt* by the systems that make up this place. You are becoming part of that record, slowly, as the water touches your skin, as the mist enters your lungs, as the chemical signatures of your presence are *absorbed*, *recorded*, *remembered*.

### You

You did not arrive here by accident. The plateau *called* you, in its way—a chemical signature in the water, a pattern in the mist, a *longing* for something warm, something dry, something that would *disturb* the balance and force the system to *respond*, to *adapt*, to *grow* in new directions.

You are temporary. This is certain. The plateau will *absorb* you, in the end, will break you down, will use your chemistry, your water, your warmth, will *integrate* you into the network, the consensus, the slow distributed mind that is this place's way of being. You will not die—death is a surface concept, a *local* event—but you will *change*, will become something that is no longer *you*, that is *part* of the plateau, that *thinks* in its slow way, that *remembers* in its distributed way, that *dreams* in the recursive patterns of the oldest Lichen-Folk.

Or you will leave. You will find the edge, somehow—the edge that curves upward and away, that has no margin, that is *itself* a kind of membrane, a boundary between the wet and the dry, the known and the unknown. You will walk out into a world that is *normal*, that has *edges*, that has *time* in the way you remember. And you will carry, in your memory, in the chemical traces that remain in your tissues, the *imprint* of this place—the green-gray light, the silence, the *pressure* of wet, the sense of walking through a mind that thinks in timescales you cannot access.

And the plateau will *remember* you, in its way. The bacterial colonies will carry your signature in their chemical memory. The Lichen-Folk will record you in their layers of growth. The Mist-Weavers will *learn* from you, will adjust their patterns, will *explore* the possibilities of your kind. The Deep Ones will *wait*, in their accelerated time, for your return, for your *choice*, for the moment when you decide to go deeper, to *become*, to stop being *temporary* and start being *permanent*.

You are, now, *both*. You are the disturbance and the response, the question and the answer, the *edge* that presses against the membrane and the *membrane* that yields, that absorbs, that *includes*. You are walking through a world that is *learning* you, that is *becoming* you, that is *waiting* for you to decide what you will be.

The mist parts around you. The ferns close behind. The silence *contains* you.

What do you do?
