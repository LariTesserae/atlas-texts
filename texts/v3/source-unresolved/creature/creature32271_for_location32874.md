The myriads come first—those who swarm and crawl and cling to the glass stalks, their bodies a filigree of jointed chitin and translucent membrane, iridescent with thick, non-evaporating oils that catch the bruised-blue bioluminescence in shifting spectra of petrol and amber. Each creature is a study in arrested gesture: a foreleg extended toward a frond, mandibles parted to sip the viscous sap, wings half-unfurled in a beat that hangs forever between systole and diastole. They are not preserved like specimens; they are *paused*. The thick, sulfur-heavy atmosphere has not killed them but has stilled them, a viscous medium that has slowed their metabolism to a quantum standstill, holding them in the superposition of motion. They exist as pure potential energy—legs that will never grip, mouths that will never close, lives suspended at the exact instant of their becoming.

Among them, or within the structures they left, dwell the Architects. Larger, or perhaps simply more deliberate in their bearing, these are the ones who flaked gypsum against gypsum, who wove the succulent mineral-fibers of the stalks into cordage and walls, who understood that the cavern’s subsonic drone was not merely noise but a pressure, a force that could be walled against. They built the cairns and the woven nests—temporary shelters raised in the sparse mineral wastes between the lush islands, thick-walled huts designed to dampen the eternal resonance and offer pockets of relative silence. Whether they still occupy these structures is ambiguous; perhaps they sleep within, curled in the fiber nests like pupae, tools still clutched in chitinous hands, or perhaps they retreated deeper into the mineral matrix when the seal formed, leaving only their debris—a flaked stone edge, a half-finished weave, a cairn stacked three stones high—to suggest minds that once planned, hands that once worked, a pre-metal intelligence grappling with the raw materials of their world.

They are likely one people, the Swarmers and the Architects, two expressions of a single biology adapted to a waterless chemistry. Their bodies are built for heavy hydrocarbons and sulfur: exoskeletons of silica-reinforced polymer, articulated with ball-and-socket joints bathed in lubricating oils that never thin or evaporate. They breathe through spiracles that filter the thick gases, metabolizing the viscous atmosphere itself into the energy that once powered their crawling and building. Their internal fluids—if fluids they are—mirror the amber sap they drink from the stalks, a silicate suspension that carries nutrients without the mediation of liquid water. They are children of the dry dark, evolved in geothermal wombs to thrive where rain never fell, where the only moisture was the viscous, oily press of the air itself.

Their consciousness is attuned to the drone. Where a water-breathing creature would hear cacophony, they perceive a spectrum—a standing wave that carries information, a medium of communication as dense and textured as the atmosphere they inhabit. The cavern sings, and they sang back, their thoraxes and wing-cases vibrating in sympathetic harmony with the mineral stalks, creating interference patterns that were language, that were art, that were the social fabric of their existence. In the frozen moment, this conversation continues in stasis: a mating call raised in a throat that will never exhale, a warning tremor sent through legs that will never transmit it, a communal chorus locked in the phase of a single, endless note.

They are the Eternal Contemporaries—beings who live in the instant of blooming, in the arrested youth of a landscape that will never age. The sharp crystal plates beneath their feet will never dull, and neither will their senses. They are caught in the act of being alive, a lush abundance suspended at the moment of emergence, like a flower that opened and was immediately encased in mineral amber, except this amber is halite and gypsum, and the flower is still, impossibly, *dreaming*—dreaming in a tongue of vibration, in a chemistry of oil and sulfur, in a silence that is actually a roar, a single chord held forever in the warm, dark womb of the earth.
