The inhabitants of the Throat-Pocket do not breathe, do not walk, and do not die. They are the **Packed** and the **Scattered**, a population suspended in the exact posture of a held breath, existing in the narrow moment between life and preservation.

Beneath your boots lives the **Copper-Green Congregation**—the metallic moss. These are not plants in any familiar sense, but lithophytes: silicate-carbon hybrids that metabolize sulfur and iron in place of sunlight and water. They form a mattress of such density that the word *packed* becomes literal, a fully-occupied volume where filament presses against filament with no intervening void. Each strand is hard, crystalline, utterly desiccated, yet cool to the touch, drawing heat from your palm with the efficiency of a heat sink. They prickle like frozen steel wool, a carpet of desiccated filaments that never grows but remains, in its arrested state, perpetually lush. They are the floor-dwellers, the base layer of the pocket’s biology, a jungle-floor that is not soil but compressed, mineral-saturated tissue.

Rising from this mattress are the **Glass-Stalked Towers**—translucent columns of silica fiber that function as thermal engines rather than photosynthetic leaves. They do not catch sunlight; they harvest the gradient between the anhydrous mineral pan and the thin, pewter-colored air, converting temperature differentials into the chemical stasis that keeps them upright. Their surfaces bead with condensation that is not water but liquid sulfur dioxide, gleaming amber in the dim, blue-grey twilight. They catch what photons filter down through the mineral dust and refract them into a faint, internal glow, so the towers appear to smolder from within. They stand in a packed forest of glass, their transparency revealing the tungsten vines that braid around them—heavy, inert drapers of braided metal that drape over the towers like funeral wreaths, adding their weight to the saturated density, their inertness a kind of vegetal patience made mineral.

From the polygonal plates of the mineral crust erupt the **Shelf-Beings**—fungal brackets in rust-red and ochre, growing in packed layers, shelf upon shelf, like geological accretions that have learned to metabolize. They are the decomposers who have forgotten how to decompose, their forms frozen in the moment of eruption, their colors locked in permanent rust-bloom. They crowd the spaces between the towers and the moss, filling every cubic centimeter of the basin with their ochre and crimson geometry, so that the ground itself seems to be rising in shelves, a packed topography of biological ambition stalled mid-gesture.

But the vegetation does not exist alone. Scattered through this thicket—**scattered** but unmistakably **industrial**—are the **Stilled Ones**, the machines. A drilling rig stands frozen, its bit half-buried in the gypsum crust, cables draped like dead vines across the metallic moss. A processing hut, no larger than a garden shed, squats among the glass-stalks, its corrugated walls weathered to a matte, rust-bloom patina that matches the fungal brackets, as if the biology has begun to claim it. Conveyor belts of uncorroded alloy snake through the thicket in loops that serve no purpose now, their rollers locked, their paths overgrown but not obscured. Pipes pierce the mineral pan, venting nothing, humming instead—these are the veins of the place, carrying not fluid but vibration, connecting the dormant reactors to the resonant geometry of the caldera. The machinery is embedded within the biology, grown around and through; the moss carpets the hut’s foundation, the tungsten vines drape over the conveyor frames, the fungal shelves erupt from the rig’s base. They have reached a stalemate, the industry and the alien botany, neither consuming the other but existing in a frozen embrace.

And then there is the **Resonance**, the third inhabitant, the one that occupies the air itself. It is a 50-hertz drone, a standing wave generated by the caldera’s geometry and the high-altitude wind that scours the rim but never enters the bowl—or perhaps it is the harmonic of the dormant industrial cores, reactors that are not off but *stilled*, vibrating at a frequency that suggests power without motion. It is loud, a physical pressure that fills the sternum, rattles the teeth, and vibrates in the hollow of the throat. It is time-invariant; there is no gust, no lull, no change in pitch. It is the voice of the place, the only thing that happens in the Throat-Pocket, a constant roar that makes conversation impossible and fills the biological silence left by the absence of animals. No birds wheel in the thin air, no insects scrape against the glass-stalks, no microfauna stir in the mineral dust. The fauna register is empty, leaving only this acoustic presence to inhabit the space between the packed vegetation and the frozen machines.

Together, these form the population: the lithophyte colonies in their copper-green, glass, tungsten, and rust-red profusion; the industrial remnants in their arrested motion; and the hum that binds them. They exist under the rule of **frozen equilibrium**. Nothing grows, though the moss looks lush and the towers stand tall; nothing decays, though the machines look weathered and the hut’s walls bloom with rust; nothing moves, though the sound suggests vibration and the stalks gleam with amber beads. A leaf does not fall. A gear does not turn. The erosion that shaped the rock happened in a time before, and now the forms are fixed, settled into their own state, in balance.

To be among them is to be inside a held breath. The inhabitants stare back with vegetal patience, their metallic moss prickling your palms, their glass-stalks glowing with internal fire, their fungal shelves stacked in silent layers, while the scattered rigs and huts stand as monuments to a mechanized ambition that stopped, mid-motion, at the edge of the sky. They are the denizens of the pocket-scale world, locked in a stasis that mimics death but is instead a perfect, unchanging stasis—a verdant desolation where the only life is the strange vegetation, the frozen machines, and the sound that never changes, filling the intimate room at the top of the world with its eternal, resonant drone.
