Someone lives here. No—*something else* lives here, first, and the someone came later, or perhaps always was here, dreaming in the dark.

There is a woman. She is not young and not old; she has that quality of people who stopped tracking time when they stopped expecting to be found. She descends the stair at the hour when the light above turns to the particular green of a bruise healing, when the city's hum becomes a pressure rather than a sound. She carries a lantern that throws light *sideways*, so she never fully sees what she passes, only what the light grazes—moss, a frog, the sheen of water, the rust where rebar shows through concrete like a wound showing through skin.

She does not live in one place in the stair. She lives in *transitions*—the landing where the temperature drops three degrees, the corner where two walls meet at the angle gravity disapproved of, the sunken room where water has pooled so long it has become *address*, a place with its own ecology, its own weather. She knows which steps crumble and which hold. She has named them. She does not speak the names aloud.

She keeps a room. Not a room with walls—the stair has few of those—but a *volume of air* she has made hers through attention. In it: a mattress on a pallet, elevated from the damp. A stove that burns Sterno, its flame the color of the bioluminescence she has begun to see in her peripheral vision, in the dark she has learned to prefer. Shelves of jars containing things she will not describe—pickled, preserved, *waiting*. A mirror, small, that she does not look into directly. A notebook in which she records the drip. Not the count—the *quality*. The drip that falls on stone. The drip that falls on moss. The drip that falls on nothing and is still, somehow, *heard*.

She is not fleeing. This is the crucial thing. She is not hiding. She has made a *choice* that looks like surrender from above, from the places where choices are made in daylight, with witnesses, with consequences that can be calculated. Here, the choice is made in the body. In the particular itch of damp cotton. In the warmth that presses against the damp like a hand against a feverish forehead. In the *relief* of descending, of being enclosed, of finding the scale that fits a single life.

She has a relationship with the fox. This is not friendship. The fox—she has begun to call it *citizen*, though she does not know if this is name or title—appears at the landing where the etiolated leaves grow toward light from below. It does not approach. It watches her with the flat assessment of something that has also chosen this place, that has also stopped expecting to be found. Sometimes she leaves food. The fox takes it, or doesn't. Neither event is *meaningful*. They have reached an understanding that requires no exchange.

She dreams. The dreams are of the stair *finished*—not completed, but *resolved*. Of concrete cured, sealed, holding its shape against the hill. Of water running in pipes instead of weeping through mortar. Of light entering from above, the honest light, the light that reveals rather than transforms. She wakes from these dreams with her face wet—condensation, or something else—and does not know if she is grieving or grateful for the dream's release.

She is being changed. This is visible. Her skin has taken on the quality of the place: *porous*, *responsive*, *storing* what it touches. She sweats differently now. Her hair has begun to grow in patterns she did not choose, following the moisture, the warmth, the *logic* of the stair. She no longer cuts it. She has begun, in the dim light, to see her own glow—faint, on her hands, on her face, the particular pale of things that live in water-light. She is not alarmed. She is *noticing*.

The city above continues. She hears it, sometimes—footsteps on the stair above hers, the creak of a door opening onto a world she has left, or been left by, or never entered. She does not go up. She has tried, once, or twice. The light at the top is *too much*. The sky is *wrong*. The green-gold afternoon, filtered through leaves that grew *toward* the light from below, produces in her a vertigo that is not fear but *recognition*—she recognizes what she has become, and cannot bear to confirm it from the outside.

She is waiting. Not for rescue. Not for the stair to be finished, or demolished, or *claimed* by the city that doesn't know it's built on a wound. She is waiting for the *next stage*—the one the erosion parameter calls *emerging, pre-formation*. The stage where the concrete, still young, still *wanting* to be something, learns its own impermanence. The stage where the tree splits the beam completely, where the moss becomes *soil*, where the room she has made by attention becomes a room made by *process*, by the collision of damp and warmth and the endless pressure of life finding its way into everything.

She will be there. She is already there, in the way that the stair is already a garden with architectural memories, already a ravine with the shape of human ambition visible in its walls. She descends further, now, than she did when she arrived. She has found doors that open onto rooms with no doors. She has learned to breathe the water-light, to *become* it, the way the frog in the standing water has become it—brown, tiny, *loud* when she chooses to be, silent otherwise, *present*.

The stair is not empty. It was never empty. It is *full*—of the woman, of the fox-citizen, of the millipedes in their procession, of the tree gripping its beam, of the fungus glowing blue in the corner where water has pooled and never left. Full of the city continuing above, indifferent, its hum a pressure in the ears of everything below. Full of the *argument* between built and growing, damp and warm, hidden scale and urban impulse—an argument that has no resolution, only *continuation*, only the endless, patient, *abundant* pressure of life finding its way into everything, including the woman who descends, who waits, who is becoming, in the dim light, the particular blue of shadow in a corner where water has pooled and never left.
