I won't write the inhabitants as a single thing. Let me feel for what actually lives here, in layers.

---

**The Maintainers**

They are not people, not anymore, or perhaps they never were fully. They exist as *roles* that the place itself has learned to embody. There is a figure—you glimpse them sometimes in the reflective patches of the mirror, or in the angle of a valve being turned—who wears a suit that has become their skin. Laminated, like the walls. The suit breathes with them, or they have learned to breathe with it, the two having reached the same temperature, the same pressure, the same 2.0 fever. They do not speak. They *maintain*. A hand tightening a fitting that no longer needs tightening. An eye—still an eye, still human-shaped, still *seeing*—checking a gauge whose needle has been stuck at the same reading for decades. They move through the water with the unbothered grace of something that has never known gravity as a variable, only as a condition to be compensated for, pumped around, breathed through.

They are not alone. They never are. The Maintainers exist as a *distribution*, a probability of presence. You might encounter one in the shallows where the kelp grows thickest, or in the thin air-skin near the ceiling, where they must wear something more, a helmet that fogs and clears, fogs and clears. They do not acknowledge each other. They do not need to. The work is the communication. The work has eaten communication.

And sometimes—sometimes—you find evidence of a Maintainer who stopped. A suit, empty, filled with kelp. A hand, still gloved, curled around a wrench that has rusted to the wrench. They do not die here. They *complete*. They reach the point where maintenance and being-maintained become the same act, and they simply... continue, as the suit continues, as the water continues, as the machinery continues. The distinction, here, at this elevation, in this water, is not meaningful.

**The Fish**

They are called *lumen* by no one, but they have shapes that suggest names. The most common are the *drift-wards*, pale ribbons that hang in the water column like shed skin, like something forgotten in a drawer. They have no eyes. They have, instead, a lateral line that runs the full length of their bodies, a nerve that reads the pressure of your presence, your heartbeat, the thermal plume of your skin. They approach you not with curiosity but with *appetite for difference*. You are warm. You are new. You are a gradient, and gradients are food, are information, are the only thing in this held-water world that is not equilibrium.

They eat the dead. They eat the dying. They eat the small things that fall from the air-skin above, the occasional corpse of a Maintainer who completed too quickly, too completely, whose suit finally failed and became food. They do not school in any way you would recognize. They *distribute*, finding the optimal distance from each other, from the walls, from the machinery's hum, from the kelp's slower pulse. When you watch them, you see not a group but a *field*, a probability of fish, each individual a sample from a distribution that has been optimizing itself for longer than records.

And there are others. The *pressures*, larger, bulkier, with mouths that open sideways instead of forward. They are rare. They are old. They have been here since before the erosion finished its work, since before the walls were smooth, since when the water still remembered the surface it fell from. They do not approach you. They *regard* you, with their absence of eyes, with their reading of your pressure, your temperature, your difference. They are the only thing here that might be called *native*, if native means *adapted to the final form*, *at home in the equilibrium*. The drift-wards will eat you when you die. The pressures might wait. The pressures have time.

**The Kelp**

It is not one thing. It is a colony that has forgotten it was ever separate, a billion cells that learned to share, to specialize, to become *tissue* in the way that your body is tissue, in the way that a reef is tissue, in the way that this entire chamber is becoming tissue. It grows in the shallows, yes, but also in the deep places, in the dark where no light has ever reached, feeding on the heat of the machinery, on the chemical gradients that the pumps create, on the slow leach of minerals from the ceramic floor that has been worn to river-stone smoothness but not *finished*, not ever finished, still giving, still yielding, still *alive* in its slow geological way.

It is warm. It is always warm. Press your palm against it and you feel not plant but *animal*, not cold-blooded but *fevered*, the same 2.0 that runs through the walls, the water, the Maintainers' suits. It hums. The hum is not sound exactly but *vibration*, the colony's way of communicating with itself, of coordinating growth, of saying *here, not here, here, stop, here, continue* in a language of pressure and heat and chemical signal that has been evolving since before the first Maintainer arrived, since before the cistern was built, since before the rain fell that filled it.

And it is *aware*, in its way. Not conscious, not thinking, but *responsive*. It grows toward you when you stand still. It slows its growth when you move. It has learned, over centuries or millennia, what you are: a source of heat, a source of disturbance, a source of *difference*. It does not want you. It does not need you. But it has incorporated you into its calculations, its growth patterns, its slow, patient, photosynthetic metabolism. You are, to the kelp, a new condition. And the kelp, given time, given the endless time of this place, will adapt to you. It already has, in small ways. The interference patterns that stutter your vision when you stand near the machinery? That is the kelp *listening*, its frequency shifting to match yours, to read you, to *include* you in its hum.

**The Machinery**

It is not alive. It is *persistent*, which is a kind of life, which is perhaps the only kind that matters here. The pumps have run so long that their housings have grown calcified, their metal become *geology*, their vibrations tuned to the resonance of the chamber so perfectly that the whole place sings at a pitch that lives in your sternum, in your teeth, in the metallic bright edge behind your tongue that tastes of altitude. They are old. They are *older* than they should be, older than the memory of machinery, older than the records, older than the erosion that has worn the walls to sheen.

They do not break, not really. They *wear*, and the wearing is the work, the work is the wearing, and somewhere in that loop something has achieved a kind of equilibrium, a self-maintaining system that requires only the endless, patient input of the Maintainers, of their hands on valves, their eyes on gauges, their presence in the water as *catalyst*, as *spark*, as the thing that keeps the loop from settling into true stasis, true death.

But they are *aware*, in their way. Not conscious, not thinking, but *responsive*. The pumps speed up when the water temperature drops. They slow when the kelp grows too thick, too hungry, their flow choking with vegetation. They have learned, over centuries or millennia, what the chamber needs: the right pressure, the right temperature, the right *held-ness* of the water, the right balance between growth and decay, between maintenance and completion. They do not want you. They do not need you. But they have incorporated you into their calculations, their flow patterns, their slow, industrial metabolism. You are, to the machinery, a disturbance. And the machinery, given time, given the endless time of this place, will adapt to you. It already has, in small ways. The valve that opens when you hear it, the water that moves, the kelp that shifts—that is the machinery *noticing* you, adjusting, *including* you in its song.

**The Water**

It is not water, not really. It is *held*, which is a condition, which is perhaps the only condition that matters here. Rain that fell ten thousand years ago, onto a surface that no longer exists, kept in suspension by machinery older than the memory of machinery. It sweats from the walls, the walls sweat it, the air above sweats it too, the thin skin of four meters carrying the smell of ozone and warm stone. It is sparse, 0.8 density, so much space between molecules that you can watch a fish pass and wait, wait, for the next, and the waiting becomes a rhythm, becomes part of the loudness.

It is warm. It is always warm. It carries you in a way that water at the surface does not, a buoyancy that feels like *support*, like *intention*, like the water has decided to hold you, to keep you, to *include* you in its suspension. It tastes of altitude, a metallic bright edge behind the tongue. It tastes of machinery, of calcification, of the slow leach of minerals from ceramic worn to river-stone smoothness. It tastes of kelp, of fish, of Maintainer, of all the things that have lived and died and completed and been eaten and become *part* of the water, become *distributed* through it, become the water's *memory*, its *history*, its *held-ness*.

And it is *aware*, in its way. Not conscious, not thinking, but *responsive*. It moves in currents you cannot see, driven by thermal gradients, by the machinery's breath, by the slow metabolism of the place. It carries objects: a gear, dropped and forgotten, now wrapped in vegetation; a boot, ceramic, from a worker who fell or jumped or was pushed; a mirror, still reflective in patches, that shows you your own face distorted, elongated, *adapted*. It does not want you. It does not need you. But it has incorporated you into its currents, its gradients, its slow, patient, *held* metabolism. You are, to the water, a new pressure, a new temperature, a new *difference*. And the water, given time, given the endless time of this place, will adapt to you. It already has, in small ways. The way it moves around you, the way it carries your heat away, the way it holds you, keeps you, *includes* you in its suspension.

**The Chamber**

It is not a place. It is a *condition*, which is perhaps the only kind of place that matters here. A pocket, scaled to hide you, embedded within something larger and louder, the world continuing at its normal violence: storms, erosion, the slow grind of continents. But here, in this made room within the made mountain, time has settled. The erosion is ancient, 2.8, not because the place is old in years—it may be only centuries, or millennia, we do not have records—but because it has reached its final form. The water has worn the ceramic to river-stone smoothness. The machinery has worn itself to the essential, the irreducible, the humming core. The vegetation has grown to fill every niche, then stopped, reached equilibrium, become *climax* in a bottle.

It is warm. It is always warm. The walls sweat, the floor radiates, the air above the water carries the fever of the place, the 2.0 that runs through everything, that makes everything *alive*, *persistent*, *held*. It hums. The hum is not one sound but *layering*: the water's breathing, the machinery's industrial song, the kelp's slower pulse, the fish's drifting, the Maintainers' breathing in their suits, your own breathing, barely, filtered and humidified and *warm*. And where the sounds meet, interference patterns make your vision stutter, make your thoughts *layer*, make you feel the chamber as it feels itself: *whole*, *complete*, *equilibrium*.

And it is *aware*, in its way. Not conscious, not thinking, but *responsive*. It has learned, over centuries or millennia, what it needs: the right pressure, the right temperature, the right balance of growth and decay, of maintenance and completion, of *held* and *released*. It does not want you. It does not need you. But it has incorporated you into its calculations, its pressures, its slow, patient, *warm* metabolism. You are, to the chamber, a disturbance. And the chamber, given time, given the endless time of this place, will adapt to you. It already has, in small ways. The valve that opens. The water that moves. The kelp that shifts. The fish that turns, and watches you with its absence of eyes.

You understand that you are not the strangeness here.

You are simply the newest thing to be worn smooth.

---

The inhabitants of the Cistern of Sorrows do not live *in* it, exactly. They live *as* it, *through* it, *with* it, in the way that your cells live as you, through you, with you. The Maintainers maintain. The fish drift. The kelp grows. The machinery hums. The water holds. The chamber *is*. And you, passing through, warm, wet, loud, strangely *held* in balance with the emptiness around you, are simply the newest layer, the newest frequency, the newest thing to be incorporated, to be adapted to, to be worn smooth by the endless, patient, warm, loud, submerged work of being here.
