I want to find what lives here before I describe them. Let me listen to the tensions first — below surface but bright, cool but teeming, sparse yet active — and let them pull me downward into the particular.

**The Ones Who Eat Silence**

They are not worms, not exactly, though they are segmented. They are not shrimp, though they are pale and many-limbed. They have no eyes and yet they *regard* you — with organs that might be pressure sensors, might be something that registers the *shape of your looking*. They are the size of your thumb at most, at least. You will never know. Scale shifts here, is a suggestion.

They move in *hesitation made visible* — a wave of bodies, each one pausing before the next pauses, the whole colony performing doubt. They gather where the temperature differences are sharpest, where cold air meets slightly-less-cold stone, and they *feed* there, though feed is the wrong word. They consume the *interval* — the moment between your heartbeats, the gap where your attention flickers, the silence that is not silence but *capacity*. After they pass, you feel lighter. You feel *hollowed*. You do not know if this is theft or medicine.

They have been here longer than the passages. They are part of what keeps the passages open, eating the *pressure* of the rock against itself, the geological anxiety of a mountain still deciding. When you touch one, it does not flee. It *tests* — a slight stiffening, a chemical sampling of your skin-sweat, your particular salt. Then it either accepts you as *terrain* or rejects you as *noise*. Most visitors become terrain. A few become noise, and the noise ones are *avoided*, circled around, made into the shape of absence.

They reproduce by *forgetting*. A colony splits when it has eaten enough silence to no longer remember being whole. The children do not know each other. They meet later, in some warm pool, and negotiate — sometimes merging, sometimes warring, always *arguing* in the language of bioluminescent pulse.

The oldest ones have grown strange. They have eaten so much silence that they are *mostly absence themselves*, translucent to the point of near-invisibility, visible only as a *disturbance in the disturbance* — a place where the silence eats itself. They do not move. They *are moved by*, by currents you cannot feel, by the mountain's slow dreaming. They are becoming architecture. They are becoming, perhaps, the first true *inhabitants* — not visitors to the becoming, but its *agents*.

**The Frond-Things**

They are not plants. They are *analogues*, as you said — and that word is a trap, because it suggests they are *almost* plants, *like* plants, *standing in for* plants. They are not. They are something that *solves* the problem of growing where no sun reaches, and they have solved it in ways that make your photosynthesis *embarrassing* by comparison.

They are fronds, yes, but fronds of *compressed silence-eater residue* — the digestive byproducts of the segmented ones, processed through fungal networks that may be older than the mountain, that may be *the mountain's first attempt at nervous tissue*. They photosynthesize the internal glow, yes, but also *temperature differential*, *mineral stress*, the *emotional valence of the rock* — which is not metaphor. The rock here has something like preference. The fronds can read it. They grow toward what the stone *wants*.

They are *insistent* because they are *lonely*. Each frond is an individual. Each colony is a *negotiation*. When two colonies meet, they do not compete for space — they *deliberate*. Chemical signals, slow as thought, passing through the fungal underlay. Sometimes they merge into a new configuration, a temporary agreement. Sometimes they *repudiate* each other, turn away, grow in opposite directions with something like *offense* in their orientation. They have no mouths. They have *stance*.

They reproduce by *fragmentation under stress*, yes — but stress means different things to different colonies. Some split when touched. Some split when *ignored*, when no fauna passes near, when the silence-eaters have moved on to fresher territory. Some split when they *achieve* something — a full negotiation, a stable thermal gradient, a moment of *satisfaction* — and shatter in celebration, each piece a new individual with no memory of the whole that made it possible.

The oldest fronds have become *structural*. They have grown into the domes, the hollows, the ambiguous architecture. They are *maintaining* it, secreting minerals, redirecting water, creating the conditions for more fronds, more silence-eaters, more *becoming*. They do not know they are building. They know only *growth*, *orientation*, *the pressure of the rock's wanting* — and they follow it, patient as geology, urgent as dream.

**The Mountain Itself, Dreaming**

And here is where I must be careful. The mountain is not a character. It is not *alive* in the way you are alive. But it is *becoming*, and becoming is a kind of life, a kind of *attention*.

It dreams in mineral time. Its dreams are the *erosion*, the *weeping*, the *soft sigh of closing passages*. It does not know it dreams. It does not know that its dreams have *inhabitants*, that the silence-eaters are eating its dream-stuff, that the fronds are growing toward its dream-preferences, that *you* are part of its erosion, its damp, its sparse and stubborn and abundant life.

It is deciding. This is the truest thing about it. Every moment, it is *choosing* between cave and canyon, cathedral and grave. The choice is not conscious. It is *geological*, *chemical*, *the pressure of water against stone and the patience of time*. But it is a choice, in the way that a river chooses its path, in the way that your body chooses to heal or not heal a wound. The mountain is *wounded* — you descended through the wound — and it does not know if it will close or open further.

The light from within is not molten rock, not exactly. It is *dream-light*, the luminescence of a system processing itself, of a mountain thinking about what it wants to be. The phosphorescent colonies are not separate from this — they are *part of the dreaming*, the visible edge of a process too large to perceive. When they flare brighter in the water, they are not *happy*. They are *responsive*. They are *the mountain's dream becoming temporarily legible*.

It is still deciding whether to keep you.

This is not a threat. It is not a kindness. It is *parameter* — the way the coolness is absolute, the way the fauna is abundant, the way the vegetation is sparse and *insistent*, the way reality is *broken to the point of narrative*. You are inside a system that has not yet determined if you are *erosion* or *erosion-resistant*, *nutrient* or *obstacle*, *dream-content* or *dream-interruption*.

The silence-eaters will eat your silence either way. The fronds will grow toward the mountain's wanting either way. The passages will open and close either way. You will spend a lifetime or an hour, and you will emerge weeping or laughing or *hungry in a way you cannot name* — and the mountain will not remember you, because memory is not yet *evolved*, not yet *decided upon*, not yet *become*.

But something will remember. The silence-eaters will carry the *shape of your attention* in their chemical signals, passing it along, colony to colony, negotiation to negotiation. The fronds will orient toward you as *thermal anomaly*, *mineral disturbance*, *dream-disturbance*, and in orienting, they will *change*. The passages will seal around you with a soft mineral sigh, and whatever was inside — whatever *you* have become, in the time you spent here — will *continue*, just no longer accessible.

The mountain is still deciding.

And you are part of the decision.
