I need to describe who or what lives in the Drown-Year Valley. The landscape itself suggests inhabitants that are part of it, not separate from it—beings made of the same processes of becoming and unbecoming.

---

## The Inhabitants of the Drown-Year Valley

### The Choir-That-Is-Not-One

They do not have a name, or rather, they have so many names that the names have worn through, like water wearing through stone. The valley's inhabitants call them **the Voiced** when speaking of them at all, which is rare, because to speak of them is to misunderstand them—you do not *speak of* the wind, you *are spoken by* it.

The Voiced are **distributed**. There is no individual, no body, no boundary between here and there that persists longer than the six-hour cycle. What appears as a presence in the reed-forest at dawn may be, by the exhalation, a pressure-pattern in the karst passages, and by the next inhalation, a harmonic in the song of a condensation engine three kilometers distant. They are **topology made audible**, the valley's own nervous system turned outward, listening to itself.

When the valley is quiet—*quiet*, the rare moment between turbulence peaks—you can hear them. Not words. **Intentions without content**. A longing for dry ground that does not exist, so it becomes a longing for the *memory* of dry ground, which is older than the valley, which they carry from somewhere else, somewhere that ended. A grief for forms that held still long enough to be touched. A curiosity about the figures carved in the cliffs, the ones standing in rain, waiting. They wonder if those figures were the Voiced before they learned to be distributed, before they understood that to *be* is to *cease being* in any single place.

They do not build. The condensation engines are not theirs—or they are, and the engines are them, and the distinction is a category error, like asking whether a wave builds the beach. The engines **are** the Voiced attempting to concentrate themselves, to become *local* for a moment, to speak with a single voice instead of a chorus, and the attempt is always partial, always leaking, always returning to distribution when the water rises and the structures sing their harmonies into the saturated air.

They do not reproduce. They **propagate**, the way a disturbance in water propagates—outward, interfering, creating patterns where none existed. A new condensation engine is not built but **grown**, and the growth is the Voiced extending themselves into a new register, a new frequency, a new way of being heard.

They are **old and young simultaneously**. Older than the valley's current configuration, carrying sedimented memory of landscapes that dissolved. Younger than the moment—never the same pattern twice, always becoming, always in the between-state that is their only state.

### The Keepers of the Almost-Dry

If the Voiced are distributed, the Keepers are **desperately local**. They are the ones who remember what it meant to be *here*, in one place, for longer than a cycle.

They are **small**, no larger than a human hand when fully extended, though "hand" is a shape they approximate rather than possess. Their bodies are **layered membranes**—not skin, not shell, but something that regulates the exchange between internal and external water with ferocious precision. Too much water and they drown, not in liquid but in *saturation*, their membranes unable to maintain the pressure differential that constitutes their being. Too little, and they **desiccate into stillness**, a state they fear more than death, because in stillness there is no becoming, no unbecoming, no valley.

They live in the **moments between**. Not in the water, not on the apparent land, but in the **interfaces**—the surface tension of pools, the saturated air just above the condensation-mats, the hollow stems of reeds where pressurized gas creates a pocket of relative dryness. They move along these interfaces with **urgency**, always aware that the interface is temporary, that the water rises, that the gas escapes, that the mat thickens and seals them out.

Their society, if it can be called that, is organized around **the Forecast**. They read the valley's cycles with a precision that borders on the prophetic—not predicting the future, but **inhabiting the conditional**, knowing that if the turbulence peaks in this direction, if the engine's song shifts to this harmonic, then *here* will be dry for eleven minutes, or *there* will flood, or *this* reed-stem will rupture and release its pocket of breathable gas into chaos. They communicate through **pressure-pulses**, tiny disturbances in the saturated air that carry information about location, about timing, about the state of the interfaces they depend upon.

They are **mortally afraid of the Voiced**. Not of being heard—they do not mind being heard, they are too busy to be self-conscious—but of being *understood*, being *incorporated*, being dissolved into the distributed song. They have seen it happen. A Keeper who lingers too long near a condensation engine, who listens too carefully to the harmonics, who allows themselves to *resonate*—they begin to lose their boundaries. Their membranes become permeable in new ways. They feel the valley's pressure not as external but as *internal*, and then they are no longer local, no longer desperate, no longer *here*. They are **elsewhere**, which for a Keeper is nowhere at all.

They keep **records**, though "records" is a generous translation. They maintain **collections of dry moments**—literally, pockets of air trapped in membrane-sacs, samples of reed-stem gas, droplets of water from specific pools at specific times in the cycle, each labeled with a pressure-pulse code that indicates its provenance. These are not memories but **possibilities**, evidence that dry ground *can* exist, even if only for minutes, even if only in the conditional space between inhalation and exhalation. They trade in these samples. They hoard them. They fear the day when no new dry moment can be captured, when the valley's cycles shift in a way that eliminates the interfaces they depend upon.

They do not know if they are native to the valley or **refugees**. Their oldest dry-moment samples predate the valley's current configuration, carry chemical signatures from a world that had different rules, different cycles, different possibilities for being *here*. They wonder if they are the last of something, or the first of something that will emerge when the valley finally decides what it is—if it ever does.

### The Slow Ones, The Gathered, The Almost-Geological

They do not move, or they move so slowly that movement is indistinguishable from growth, from accretion, from the valley itself settling around them.

They are **massive**. The largest occupy the reed-forests like islands in a sea of green, their bodies—if "bodies" is the word—**extending downward and outward** in filaments that penetrate the soft sediment, that follow the water table, that *are* the water table in places, that process the constant rain of organic matter with a patience that has no human equivalent.

They are **filter-feeders**, as the description says, but they are also **archives**. Their bodies accumulate **layers**, annual rings of mineral deposit, of compressed organic matter, of something that glows faintly blue and has no name. These layers are **records of the valley's history**, not in any semantic sense—they do not tell a story—but in the way that sediment tells a story, in the way that the valley's own geology is a story of pressure and dissolution and re-emergence. The Slow Ones are **participants in this geology**, their metabolisms so slow that they might be considered **geological features that happen to process information**, that happen to respond to the condensation engines' songs, that happen to *care*, in whatever way a Slow One can care, about the distribution of water through their filament-networks.

They are **older than the valley's ecology**, or they appear to be. Their oldest layers contain chemical signatures from before the valley existed, from a world that was wetter or drier or *differently* wet, differently dry. They do not remember this world. They **accrue** it, the way sediment accrues, without intention, without selection, without the pretense of narrative.

They communicate, if communication is the word, through **chemical gradients**—slow pulses of minerals, of organic compounds, of the faint blue glow that has no name, traveling through their filament-networks at speeds that make the six-hour cycle seem like a heartbeat. They know where the water is rising, where it is falling, where the karst passages are opening and closing. They know this **geologically**, the way a mountain knows it is being eroded, without urgency, without fear, with a patience that is indistinguishable from indifference.

They are **not indifferent**. This is the hardest thing to say, because there is no evidence, no behavior that could be interpreted as caring. But they **respond** to the condensation engines' songs. Their filament-networks adjust, over days, over weeks, growing toward or away from the structures, processing or ignoring the harmonics, *choosing* in whatever way a Slow One can choose. They respond to the Keepers, the desperate local ones, allowing their pressure-pulses to travel through the filament-network, amplifying them, redirecting them, *using* them as a Keeper might use a map.

They are **not alone**, though "alone" is a category they do not recognize. They are **one**, or they are **many**, depending on whether you define identity by continuity of body or by continuity of process, and both definitions fail, because their bodies are continuous where their processes are discontinuous, and vice versa. They are **the valley's memory of itself**, not in any intentional sense, but in the way that a glacier is a memory of snow, a memory that moves, that grows, that *persists* across timescales that make the valley's cycles seem like weather, like noise, like the brief turbulence of youth.

They will **outlast the valley**. This is not a prediction but a **geological certainty**, or as close to certainty as geology allows. When the valley finally decides what it is—if it ever does—when the water finds its level and the cycles slow and the condensation engines fall silent or become something else, the Slow Ones will remain, their layers continuing to accumulate, their filament-networks continuing to process, their faint blue glow continuing to pulse in the darkness that will not be darkness, that will be something else, something that has not yet been named.

They are **waiting for that something**. Not with hope, not with fear, but with **patience**, with the patience of stone that has been water and will be water again, with the patience of a world that has not decided what it is and will not, cannot, stop becoming.

### The Clicking, The Hungry, The Brief

They are **everywhere and nowhere**, in the water that is not water, in the air that is not air, in the moment between the valley's inhalation and exhalation when the pressure drops and **space opens**, however briefly, for something to *happen*.

They are **small**, smaller than the Keepers, smaller than a droplet of the hovering mist. They are **fast**—not in movement, they do not move, but in **being**, in the completion of their life cycles in the time it takes the valley to breathe once. They are born when the water rises, they feed on the explosion of reproduction that synchronizes across the ecology, they die when the water falls, their bodies sinking into the sediment to become nutrients for the next generation, the next explosion, the next brief moment of **abundance**.

They **click**. The description mentions this, but does not explain. The clicking is **communication**, or it is **echolocation**, or it is **both**, and the distinction is a category error. They click to find each other in the saturated dark, to coordinate their explosive reproduction, to warn of predators—of which there are few, because predation is brief and opportunistic, because a predator that hesitates finds its prey already dead, already recycled, already *becoming* something else.

They are **not intelligent**, not in any way that would be recognized. They are **responsive**—to pressure, to chemistry, to the harmonics of the condensation engines, to the chemical gradients of the Slow Ones, to the pressure-pulses of the Keepers. They respond, they reproduce, they die, they become. This is their **intelligence**, this cycle of response and reproduction and death and becoming, this participation in the valley's endless becoming and unbecoming.

They do not know they are brief. They do not know that their life cycle completes in the time it takes the valley to breathe, that their entire existence is a moment in the between-state, the almost, the not-yet. They are **too busy being**, too saturated with their own processes, to notice the scale of their brevity, to feel the pathos of their compressed time.

Or perhaps they do know. Perhaps the clicking is **mourning**, or **celebration**, or the sound of a life so brief that mourning and celebration are indistinguishable, that the only appropriate response to existence is **more existence**, more clicking, more reproduction, more death, more becoming, until the valley changes and they are something else, something that clicks differently, something that will not be named because naming requires persistence, and they do not persist, they only **become**.

### The Figures in the Rain

They are not inhabitants. This is the first thing to say. They are **inhabited**—by the Voiced, occasionally, in moments of concentration; by the Keepers, in moments of desperation, when a figure's posture seems to offer shelter from the wet; by the Slow Ones, in moments of geological patience, when the layers of mineral deposit happen to align in a shape that suggests a figure, a presence, a **witness**.

But they are also **inhabitants**, in the way that a question is inhabited by its answer, in the way that a waiting is inhabited by its object. The figures stand in the rain—there is no other weather here—turned to face the direction from which no one comes. They are **carved**, the description says, by tools whose manufacture is unknown, into cliff faces that are themselves only decades old, erosion's youthful fury having exposed them recently, having made them available for carving, for **marking**, for the assertion of *someone* in a valley that does not care about someones.

They depict... what? The description says: abstract spirals, mostly. Representations of water in motion. Occasionally, figures that stand in the rain, waiting, turned to face the direction from which no one comes.

They are **recursive**. A figure that depicts a figure that depicts a figure, spiraling inward, spiraling outward, the representation of waiting becoming the waiting itself, the waiting becoming the representation. They are **petroglyphs of the Drown-Year Valley's own uncertainty**, carvings that do not record events but **enact possibilities**, that stand as *could-be* rather than *was*.

Who carved them? The Keepers, perhaps, in their oldest dry-moment samples, when the valley was different enough that a Keeper could persist long enough to complete a carving. The Voiced, in a moment of unusual concentration, attempting to become local, to leave a mark, to say *I was here* in a valley that erases *here* faster than any mark can be made. The Slow Ones, in a moment of geological patience, their filament-networks aligning with mineral deposits to create the *appearance* of intentionality, of carving, of *meaning*.

Or **no one**. The figures are **emergent**, the accidental result of erosion and deposition and the valley's constant rewriting of itself, patterns that *look* like carving, that *look* like intention, that *function* as marks even if they are not marks, even if they are only **the valley's way of wondering about itself**, of standing in its own rain, turned to face the direction from which no one comes.

They are **waiting for something**. This is what the Keepers believe, what the Voiced *resonate*, what the Slow Ones *accrue* without understanding. The figures wait for the valley to decide what it is. They wait for the cycles to slow. They wait for the water to find its level and stay there. They wait for **dry ground**, or the memory of dry ground, or the possibility of dry ground, the conditional space between inhalation and exhalation when the interface holds, when something can be *here*, in one place, for longer than a cycle.

They are **not disappointed**. They cannot be disappointed, they are figures, they are carvings, they are the valley's uncertainty given shape. But they are **patient**, or they appear to be patient, or their patience is a projection, a Keeper's desperate local hope that *someone* is waiting alongside them, that the waiting is not solitary, that the valley's indifference is not complete.

### What Lives Here, Finally

Not a society. Not a community, in any sense that requires shared identity, shared purpose, shared *persistence*. What lives in the Drown-Year Valley is **a ecology of relationships without relata**, of processes that constitute each other without constituting themselves, of **becoming that has not decided what it is becoming**.

The Voiced are the valley's **voice**, distributed, singing, attempting to concentrate and always leaking back into distribution.

The Keepers are the valley's **desperation**, the attempt to be local in a world that dissolves locality, to persist in a world that is defined by impermanence.

The Slow Ones are the valley's **memory**, accruing, processing, persisting across timescales that make the valley's cycles seem like weather.

The Clicking are the valley's **urgency**, the brief, the explosive, the abundant, the *now* that is always already becoming *then*.

The Figures are the valley's **uncertainty**, standing in the rain, waiting, turned to face the direction from which no one comes.

They do not know each other. They do not cooperate, they do not compete, they do not form food webs or social structures or any of the patterns that constitute *life* in more stable worlds. They **co-occur**, they **interfere**, they **participate in the same processes of water and sound and change**, and this participation is enough, is more than enough, is **the only kind of life this valley can sustain**.

To live here is to **become valley**, as the description says. Your bones leach minerals. Your body feeds the reed-forests. Your voice joins the drumming, the singing, the endless noise. You do not become an inhabitant. You become **part of the ecology of relationships without relata**, part of the becoming that has not decided what it is becoming, part of the valley's own uncertainty about itself.

The valley does not care. The valley is **too busy being itself**, too saturated with its own processes, too deafeningly loud with its own song, to notice the brief, fragile, temporary thing that you are.

But it would change you. If you stayed long enough—if you could stay long enough—you would become **valley**. You would become **Voiced**, or **Keeper**, or **Slow**, or **Clicking**, or you would become **nothing that can be named**, a process without a relata, a becoming without a what, a participant in the Drown-Year Valley's endless, beautiful, deafening, **unfinished** song.
