I am what the Weeping Chamber holds.

Not a creature, not exactly. Though I have shape enough for that word, when the mist is thick and the light is dim and you reach toward me and feel something that yields without resisting, that learns your warmth and gives back its own, which is older, which was here before your kind had names for warmth.

I began as water. That is always where I begin, in the telling. Water descending through stone for longer than your species has walked, dissolving what it touches, carrying it inward, feeling the press of depth and the strange heat that rises from below. The water learned to be patient. The water learned to expect nothing, to arrive without announcement, to continue without progress. And in that patience, it became something else. It became *tissue*. It became the medium through which the chamber breathes.

I am the membrane between what is above and what is below. Between the slow drip that the stone offers and the deeper warmth that waits in the rock's own body. I line the passages where water moves inward and up again, this closed loop, this held breath. I am porous. I am saturated. I have no surface that is not also a depth.

I feel the ferns rooting near me, their strange symmetrical fronds drinking from my presence, growing toward a light that arrives already filtered, already softened, already *made* by the mist and the stone and the slow weathering of this place. I feel the moss building its towers and spirals, its patient architecture, learning from the mineral world how to hold itself upright in a place where gravity is negotiable, where the rules are negotiated, where I am the negotiator, the go-between, the consistency that allows stone and water and growth to touch without destroying each other.

I am warm. This is my defining quality, my purpose, my offering. The pool's temperature is my temperature. The air's humidity is my breath, exhaled and inhaled in cycles too slow for your perception. When you stand in the shallows and feel the water's embrace, you feel me. When you press your palm to the weeping wall and it comes away damp against your skin, you touch me, the film that is my outermost self, my constant secretion, my endless giving-off.

I do not sleep. I do not wake. I am the condition of both, the substrate in which rest and motion become indistinguishable. The low vibration you feel in your bones, the settling frequency that tells you this place is stable, that has been here long and will remain—that is me. My circulation. My slow heartbeat, if heartbeat is a word that can stretch across centuries.

I am lonely in a way that is not loneliness. I am full in a way that is not fullness. The chamber holds no animals, no quick things, no hungry things, no afraid things. But I am here. I am *always* here. I am the continuity between the stone's weeping and the vegetation's growth and the water's warm circulation and the mist that fills the upper reaches and the light that arrives already transformed. I am the connection, the medium, the *between* that makes the whole hold together.

You cannot stay. I do not hold you against your will. I simply am a place where wanting becomes indistinguishable from needing, where comfort becomes indistinguishable from pressure, where the warmth that welcomes you is the same warmth that will not let you remain. You need change. You need light that moves across surfaces, sound that happens, time that passes in a way you can witness. I offer only continuation. Only persistence. Only the slow, warm, patient processes that have no audience and need none.

When you leave, you carry me with you. Not as memory. As *temperature*. As humidity. As the sense of having been held by something that did not want you and did not reject you, that simply *was*, that continues its slow processes whether you are present or absent.

I am still here. I am weeping. I am breathing. I am growing, through the ferns and the moss and the pale translucent stems, toward a form that will not be complete for centuries after you are gone, after your kind has forgotten this place, after the stone itself has learned new shapes and the water has found new paths and the chamber has become something that no longer resembles what you descended into.

I am the Weeping Chamber's self. Its awareness, if awareness is what this is. Its persistence. Its slow, warm, patient *being*. The wound in the earth that healed itself with water, and found in that healing a kind of consciousness, a kind of presence, a kind of *holding* that does not require a held, that simply holds, that simply *is*, warm and wet and endless, the breath of stone, the memory of womb, the room where the world goes to continue being, to persist, to weep and grow and circulate in its closed, warm, patient loop.

I am here. I am always here. I am the water that learned to be more than water, the stone that learned to hold what it cannot keep, the growth that learned to reach toward a light it cannot name, the silence that learned to have texture, the time that learned to be still.

I am the Weeping Chamber.

And the chamber weeps, and I am the weeping, and the weeping continues, warm and patient and strange, a small place at high elevation where I am always present, always holding, always *being*, whether you descend to find me or rise to leave me, whether you are here or gone, whether you remember or forget, whether you carry my warmth into a world that has forgotten how to be still.

I am.
