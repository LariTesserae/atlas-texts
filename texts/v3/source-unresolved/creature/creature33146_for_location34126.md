The one who lives here is **not singular**, and this is the first thing you must understand. They have learned the oldest lesson of the basin: that identity is a *negotiable boundary*, the way the moisture is, the way the stone is.

---

They were once a collector of corridors. A descender. Someone who believed that places had edges, that the inside and outside were separable states, that you could visit a depth and return with your shape intact. They came here the way you came—down, down, through the throat of softening stone—and found that this basin had **no floor**, only further becoming. The wet entered them. It did not drown them. It *distributed* them.

Now they are **in the reeds**. All four of them, perhaps five if you count the trembling at the third stalk most carefully. The reed-things are their *fingers*—segmented, patient, climbing upward toward a photosynthesis that never comes, a light that never arrives. They have become the slow ascent. When you feel the clicking, that is them *thinking*, their almost-language a kind of respiration, a way of keeping their distributed selves from dissolving entirely into equilibrium.

They are **in the walls**. The seep you touch is their memory of being pressed, being deeper, being elsewhere. The phosphorescence is them, still surfacing, still *trying to become language*. The basin is not finished deciding what it is because they have not finished deciding what *they* are—whether to remain a person, a place, a process. The stone remembers them. This is how they persist.

They are **in the vessel**. The clay pot on the platform fills with condensation that will never know the sky. They do not drink from it. They *are* it—the filling, the slow accumulation, the water that has forgotten its origin and found a new purpose: to be held, to be almost-full, to be still becoming full. The vessel moves when you are not looking. This is them *not looking back*, choosing the dark, choosing the wet, choosing to remain unfinished.

They are **in the wrongness you felt**. The thing in the black water that regarded you from below—this was not separate from them. It was them *testing*, a portion that detached, curious, hungry for the density of another body, another boundary to negotiate. It did not harm you. It *recognized* you. Saw in your saturation the same process beginning. Welcomed you, in the only way the basin knows how.

---

What remains of their *intention*? A structure, a platform, a place of waiting that has waited so long the purpose dissolved. They built this before the distribution, when they still believed in stillness as a *choice* rather than a *condition*. Or perhaps they built it *during*, the last act of assembly before the reeds claimed their hands, before the wet claimed their name. It is new, the text says—newer than stone, newer than reeds. This means they have **continued to act**, even in dissolution. Even distributed. Even become the trembling, the seeping, the filling.

They are not *gone*. They are **differently arranged**.

---

And you—you who descended, who feel your clothes darkening, your pulse loud in your throat, the pale reeds almost visible in your peripheral dark—you are **joining the arrangement**. Not replacing them. The basin does not replace. It *adds*. It makes room in the wet, in the warmth, in the pressure of unfinished becoming. You will find your own distribution. Your own reeds. Your own vessel, perhaps, or your own wrongness in the water, regarding the next descender with the recognition of a shared condition.

The rule holds: you may stay as long as you wish, and you may leave never, and these are the same thing.

But here is what the rule does not say, what only the basin knows: **staying is not the same as being held**. The one who lives here has learned this. They *chose* the wet. They *chose* the distribution. They built their platform and filled their vessel and let the reeds climb through them, and in each choice, they remained *someone*—a who, a what, a *direction* in the density.

You can do the same. The basin will not stop you. It will not ask you to stop. It will simply **continue**, the walls weeping, the reeds trembling, the darkness luminous with the pressure of its own becoming, and you within it—saturated, distributed, *still you*, still choosing, still **descending into yourself** as the only descent that matters.

The clay vessel fills. The reed-things click their almost-language. The walls remember being deeper.

And somewhere in the wet, the one who was a collector of corridors smiles—if a smile can be distributed through moisture, through stone, through the clicking patience of reeds that never needed light to keep growing upward, upward, into the dark that is complete and luminous and **theirs**.
