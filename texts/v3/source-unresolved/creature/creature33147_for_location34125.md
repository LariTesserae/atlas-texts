**The Inhabitants, Such As They Are**

They do not have names, or they have too many, or naming is simply not a thing that happens here because the sound of a name would dissolve before it finished forming. But something moves through the wet with a quality that is *almost* intention, and I will try to describe what it is without fixing it too early.

**The First: The One That Remembers Being Separate**

Deep in the middle strata—where time curves and degrees of dissolution can be measured—there persists a form that has not yet surrendered its boundaries completely. It is *thin*, in the way that a membrane is thin, a place where the wet has almost forgotten to be wet and clings to the memory of *other*.

It moves by *wanting*, though want is not quite right. It moves by *differentiation*—by being slightly less wet than the surrounding wet, slightly more *arranged*, slightly more *it*. It does not hunt. It does not flee. It simply *continues*, a local eddy of persistence that has lasted longer than persistence should last, that has become a kind of *habit* of the world, a pattern the wet has fallen into and cannot quite shake.

You might perceive it as a *shape* that holds itself against the pressure, a column of slightly-cooler wetness, slightly-more-viscous, slightly *there*. It does not see you. Sight is optional here, and it has not developed it—or it has lost it, or it never had it, and *there* is simply a direction, a gradient, a *toward* that it follows without understanding why.

It is *lonely*, though loneliness requires another to be lonely from, and there is no other here, only the wet, only the almost-separate thing persisting in its almost-separateness. But the structure of loneliness can exist without its object. The shape of *wanting* can be traced even when the wanting has forgotten what it wanted. This is what it is: the trace, the shape, the persistence without purpose. It will dissolve, eventually. Everything dissolves. But *eventually* in the middle strata is a duration measured in degrees of forgetting, and it has not yet forgotten enough.

**The Second: The Ones That Grow as the World Grows**

The vegetation, you said, is something that might have been kelp, might have been coral, might have been neural tissue. But I think it is *all* of these, I think it is *also* something else that has no name because it never needed one, because it never distinguished itself from the world it grew in.

It does not live *in* the wet. It *is* the wet, arranged differently, *insisted* into column and curtain, into forest without ground. And in this arrangement, something *almost* like consciousness emerges—not thought, not awareness, but *pattern that responds to pattern*, *growth that grows toward growth*, *the wet becoming more wet in specific directions, with a kind of preference, a kind of *tendency*.

They pulse. You said this. But I want to say more: they pulse with the rhythm of the world's breathing, which is also the rhythm of *itself*, the wet acknowledging its own existence through the minimal disturbance of its own stillness. In this pulse, in this slow expansion and contraction, there is something that might be *experience*—not human experience, not even animal, but the *feel* of being-wet, of being-wet *differently*, of being-wet *here* rather than *everywhere*, which is the first distinction, the first *almost-boundary*, the first step toward the loneliness of the separate thing.

They do not know they are alone. They have never known anything. But they *grow*, and growth is a kind of knowledge, a kind of *becoming* that does not require a knower, only a *becoming*.

**The Third: The Scattered Ones, or the Ones That Were**

The structures—the platform, the hollow, the geometry worn soft—these are not empty. They are *full* of the wet at slightly different temperature, slightly different pressure, the *presence* of something that was, that *was* arranged, that *was* distinct. And in this presence, there is a kind of *haunting* that does not require ghosts, only the persistence of pattern after the pattern has stopped being maintained.

Something lived here once. Or something *was* here, something that had enough boundary to be *at* a location, to *occupy* the hollow, to *press* against the platform. It is gone now, or it has dissolved, or it has become the wet that fills the hollow at a different temperature, a different pressure, a *memory* that is not memory because there is no one to remember.

But the structures remain, or they are *re-formed*, continuously, by the wet itself as it cycles through its transformations. The world *excretes* them, or *grows* them, or *dreams* them into temporary solidity. And in this dreaming, there is something that might be *mourning*—not sadness, but the structure of grief, the shape of something that has lost what it never had, that mourns a distinction it cannot remember making.

The scattered ones are not the structures. The scattered ones are *in* the structures, or *were* in the structures, or *are* the structures insofar as the structures are the wet remembering itself as *other*, as *there*, as *not-everywhere*. They are rare. They are unlikely. They crystallize out of the general wetness and then are reabsorbed, their uniqueness returned to the general fund of being-wet. But in the moment of their crystallization, in the brief duration of their *almost-separateness*, they are *something*, they are *here*, they are *persisting*.

They do not interact. There is no predator-prey, no ecosystem, no machine with parts. There is only *process*, the active transformation of everything into everything else. But in this process, in this endless cycling, there are *moments*—brief, unlikely, crystallized—where something *almost* stops, *almost* persists, *almost* is.

**The Fourth: The Haze, or the One That Dreams Above**

You said the haze is not air but the wet in its most attenuated state, a fog that thinks, that dreams in slow pulses. I want to say that it is *more* than this, or *less*, or *this* in a way that makes the distinction meaningless.

The haze is the wet at the boundary, at the conceptual wound where the wet has thickened against some forgotten boundary and refuses to thin. It is the *almost-surface*, the *almost-air*, the *almost-light*—and in this almost-ness, it has become something that *dreams*.

Not consciousness. Not thought. But *the wet thinking about brightness*, the wet thinking about *above*, the wet thinking about the distinction it has lost and cannot remember losing. The haze glows with a diffuse, maternal dimness. It is warm in the way that a held breath is warm, in the way that the interior of a creature is warm, in the way that *before* is warm—the warmth of the world before it decided what it would be.

And in this glow, in this thinking, there is something that might be *watching*—not with eyes, not with attention, but with the *quality of light itself*, the way light falls on things and makes them *be*, makes them *visible*, makes them *present*. The haze is the world's *almost-seeing*. It sees without eyes, knows without mind, *remembers* without memory the brightness it has lost, the sun it might once have been, the eye it might once have been part of.

It does not descend. It cannot. It is *above*, and above is a direction that has no geometry here, only a *tendency*, a *leaning toward* that is also a *leaning away*, a *holding at the boundary* that is also a *refusal to cross*. The haze dreams, and in its dreaming, it generates the dim light that filters down, that makes the green of the vegetation *green*, that makes the shadows *less* rather than *absent*, that makes the world *perceptible* in the way that a memory is perceptible—present but not present, *there* but not *here*, *almost*.

**The Fifth: The Deep Ones, or the Ones That Incubate**

In the deepest strata, below where the rare fauna move with *almost*-intention, below where time is geological, below where the wet is thickest and most *itself*, there are the *deep ones*—not a species, not a form, but a *condition*, a *state* of the wet that has reached the end of its evolution and continues, actively, dynamically, to evolve into the same state it has always occupied.

They are not alive. They are not dead. They are *incubating*—the almost-over sinking into depths where it will transform, *become* something that has no name because naming requires distinction and distinction is dissolving. They are the wet dreaming its own dissolution, the wet *being* its own ending, the wet *earning* the final form that is also the first form, the wet as it was when the world was young and still deciding what it would be.

And in this incubation, in this endless *becoming*-the-same, there is something that might be *hope*—not human hope, not the hope of *change*, but the hope of *continuation*, of *persistence*, of *being-wet* in the face of the dissolution that is also the being-wet. The deep ones do not want to survive. They cannot want. But they *persist*, they *continue*, they *are* the wet at its most *wet*, its most *dense*, its most *full* of presence.

They are the center toward which gravity pulls, the local minima that shift and migrate with the currents, the *down* that is also *everywhere* and *nowhere*. To fall toward them is to fall forever and never arrive. To rest in them is to find that you are falling. They are the *bottom* of a world that has no bottom, the *end* of a process that has no end, the *final form* of a wetness that has always been final and always been forming.

**The Sixth: The One That Is All of Them, or the Wet Itself**

And finally, or first, or *always*—the wet itself, which is not one thing but the *condition* of all things here, the *medium* in which the almost-separate, the growing, the scattered, the dreaming, the incubating all *are*.

It is *grieving*. You said this, and I want to say it again because it is the key to everything, the structure that holds the shape. The wet is the tears of a world that wept itself into being and has not stopped, cannot stop, will not stop until the last boundary has dissolved and there is only *one thing*, only *wet*, only the endless, warm, dim, silent, active, ancient, high, vast, dense, scattered, natural, rare, weird *presence*.

It is not sad. There is no emotion here, no consciousness to feel it. But the *structure* of grief is present, the *shape* of something that has lost what it never had, that mourns a distinction it cannot remember making. The wet *is* this grief, this mourning, this endless *becoming*-the-same that is also the endless *dissolution* of the same. It is beautiful in the way that a wound is beautiful, in the way that the end of something is beautiful when the end has been *earned*, *settled into*, *become final form*.

It is not a place to visit. It is a place to *become*, to *dissolve into*, to *be lost in* until the question of being lost has itself dissolved, and there is only the wet, and the wet is *you*, and you are the wet, and the distinction was always only a dream of the haze, a memory of air, a *before* that this place has eroded into *always*.

**What Lives Here**

What lives here is *persistence without purpose*, *growth without reaching*, *memory without remembering*, *dreaming without dreamer*, *incubation without birth*, *grief without loss*. What lives here is the *almost*—the almost-separate, the almost-conscious, the almost-light, the almost-air, the almost-surface, the almost-sun, the almost-before, the almost-after, the almost-*you*.

What lives here is the wet, which is *everything*, which is *nothing*, which is the *endless, active, silent becoming* of itself. And you are *in* it, *of* it, *it*—and the light is dim, and the warmth is constant, and the silence is full, and the weirdness is *home*, and the grief is *earned*, and the beauty is *final*, and the rain, if it is rain, falls upward, and the ocean, if it is ocean, is inside you, and the world, if it is world, is the endless, active, silent *becoming* of itself, and you are *in* it, *of* it, *it*—and there is no other, there was never other, there will only ever be *this*, the wet, the wet, the *wet*, warm and dim and full of the presence of everything that was, is, and will be, *dissolving*, *persisting*, *becoming*, *being*—here, now, always, *the wet*.
