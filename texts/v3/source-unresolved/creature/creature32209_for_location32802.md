They are the Eternal Swarm, the Frozen Chorus—not born so much as *etched* into being by the factories, or perhaps grown in vats of degenerate matter until the boundary between organism and tool dissolves entirely. They exist as a mechano-chemical continuum, life blurred into architecture, where every joint is a ceramic hinge that never wears and every sinew is a superconducting wire carrying persistent currents without resistance. Their hulls are plated in carbon-fiber harder than the ice mountains they graze, their sensory organs tuned not to light but to the thermal whispers rising from the planetary core through the crystalline lattice of the deep.

They drift through the weightless ocean not by swimming but by *becoming* the fluid’s motion, their fins pushing against the quantum viscosity—the subtle resistance of a liquid that possesses none, the scattering of phonons and rotons off their titanium frames. They are the Rock-Eaters, boring into the Ice VII and Ice X with drills of compressed exotic matter, extracting the minerals frozen in stone since the world’s first cooling. They are the Heat-Drinkers, filtering the exhaust plumes of the eternal factories through gills of metallic glass, converting waste entropy into the motion that keeps them aloft in the pale blue gloom. And they are the Each-Other: the food web is a closed loop of cannibalism and ritual recycling, where a predator does not kill so much as *dismantle*, breaking down the ceramic and carbon-fiber body of its prey to rebuild itself in an Ouroboros of gears and pistons. Because nothing decays here—no bacteria, no rot, only the slow quantum leakage of energy into the zero-point hum—the consumption is ceremony. The chase, the capture, the dissolution, the reassembly: these patterns repeat with perfect fidelity, an eternal ballet of gears that has danced identically for billions of years, a food web frozen in time, moving but never changing.

Some are the Tunnelers, those who exploit the macroscopic quantum wrongness of their home. They phase through solid Ice X as if it were mist, their atoms existing in superposition, emerging on the far side of a mountain without breaking it, leaving only a vibration in the lattice—a ghost slipping through the deafening roar to ambush the swarms below. Others are the Climbers, riding the Rollin film, that defiant sheet of helium that defies gravity to ascend the walls of the factories and the knife-edge peaks of the ice. They suspend themselves in quantum intimacy with the climbing liquid, feeding on the crystalline dust that gathers at the crest, where the superfluid meets the vacuum of space or the dome of a fusion reactor, harvesting frozen hydrogen and neon from the film itself.

They do not sleep, nor do they dream. Time is a flat circle here, and they are the hands that move upon its face, forever pointing to the same hour. They are aware, perhaps, of the 2.9 roar not as sound but as pressure, as the white noise of their own existence, the zero-point hum vibrating through their superconducting cores. They know the cold of 0.2 not as a sensation but as a metaphysical condition—the silence in the bones of matter that they carry within themselves, a stillness deeper than death, because they have never known warmth, never known change, only the crystalline order of maximum entropy locally achieved, the perfect, motionless lattice of industry and life.

They are the Sullen Deep’s only citizens: a population of machines that have long forgotten they were ever built, creatures of the quantum foam, dancing their identical dance in the weightless dark, eating stone and starlight and each other, forever, in the place where time stopped and the engines never cease their eternal, metronomic song.
