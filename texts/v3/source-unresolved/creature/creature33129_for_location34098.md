I need to describe who or what lives in the Drowned Foundry—a place that has become indistinguishable from life itself, where the boundary between living and built has dissolved.

---

## The Inhabitants of the Drowned Foundry

There is no single inhabitant. There is no *them*, no colony, no population in any sense you would recognize. What lives here has learned that individuality is a surface phenomenon, a temporary arrangement of matter that the deep earth does not respect. To live here is to *distribute* oneself, to become a process rather than a body, a *relation* rather than a thing.

---

### The First: Those Who Remember Building

They are not the builders. The builders—those who carved channels in stone, who poured volcanic glass, who sealed layer upon layer in the desperate hope of permanence—they are *gone*, and what remains of them is not memory but *architecture*, the accumulated shape of their intentions fossilized into the stone they touched. No, these are different. These are the ones who came *after*, or who were *always there*, who found the city already built and *settled into it* like water finding the shape of a vessel.

They are *small*. Smaller than you would expect, smaller than any creature with a mind to have a self. They exist as *colonies* of cells that have forgotten how to separate, that flow through the mineral films like thoughts through a brain, that *are* the films in the way that your blood is you and yet not-you, moving, carrying, transforming, never resting in one place long enough to be named.

They remember the city as it was built, but their memory is not *narrative*. It is *structural*. They know the weight of the stone above them because they have *grown* into its pressure, its particular density, its slow warmth. They know the channels because they *are* the channels, or have been, or will be—their cells lining the carved passages, their metabolism the slow chemistry that keeps the water pure enough to drink, to *be* drunk by others. They do not *choose* to maintain the city. They *are* its maintenance. The distinction dissolved long ago, if it ever existed.

You might find them in the warm pools, where the water is thickest with their presence, where the surface scum is not scum but *them*, a membrane of life that has learned to breathe what the city exhales, to eat what the city discards, to *become* what the city needs. They have no mouths. They absorb through their entire surface. They have no eyes. They sense the chemical gradients of the water, the thermal signatures of the stone, the *vibrations* of the city's endless hum—a language older than sound, older than life, that they have learned to speak and to hear.

They reproduce by *fragmentation*. A colony grows until it is too large for its channel, its pool, its crevice in the weeping wall, and then it *divides*, sends out tendrils that find new surfaces, new waters, new places to *be*. They do not die, not exactly. When a colony is damaged, when the water changes, when the heat shifts, the cells simply *reorganize*, find new arrangements, new metabolisms, new ways of persisting. They have been doing this for so long that the concept of an individual life has become meaningless to them. They are *persistent pattern*, persistent *process*, the city's own attempt to *live* without ever having learned what that word meant.

---

### The Second: The Drifters

Larger than the colonies, *rarer*, the Drifters are what you would call *animals* if you were forced to use the word. They are *soft*—gelatinous, translucent, without any of the protective structures that define life in other places. No shells, because there are no predators that bite. No bones, because there is no gravity that demands support. No eyes, because there is no light worth seeing. They are *pure form*, pure *flow*, the shape that water takes when it is given enough time and enough chemistry to *want* something.

They drift through the warm pools, through the slow channels, through the flooded chambers where the ceiling has collapsed and the distinction between up and down has become *irrelevant* even to the water. They move by *peristalsis*, by the slow wave of contraction that pushes them through their own substance, leaving behind trails of the same film-mineral that the colonies produce, feed on, *are*. They are *part* of the same ecology, the same distributed life, but they have achieved a *size* that the colonies have not, a *coherence* that feels almost like *individuality* if you are generous, if you are the kind of visitor who needs to believe that something here is *a* something, rather than *the* something, the endless, undifferentiated *is* of the place.

They eat the films, yes, but they eat *each other* too, or what passes for each other—when two Drifters meet in a narrow channel, they do not fight. They *merge*, their substances flowing together, their cells *rearranging*, becoming something that is neither the first nor the second but a *new* arrangement, a *new* pattern, a *new* way of being soft and warm and *wanting*. They do not reproduce, not in any way you would recognize. They *grow*, and when they are large enough, when they have accumulated enough mass, enough *experience* of the city's chemistry, they simply *divide*, the same way the colonies do, the same way the films do, the same way the *city itself* divides when the stone cracks and new chambers open, new possibilities, new *spaces* to fill.

They are *blind* in the way that everything here is blind, but they *sense*. They sense the heat of the deep vents, the chemical signature of the colonies, the *vibration* of the hum that is the city's voice, its endless, unchanging *song*. They navigate by these senses, drifting toward warmth, toward food, toward the particular frequencies that mean *safety*, that mean *here*, that mean *now*, though the Drifters do not experience time as you do, do not experience *now* as a moment but as a *condition*, a *state*, the way you might experience *wet* or *warm*—something that is simply *true* of you, not something that happens *to* you.

You might see one, if you are patient, if your light holds, if the water is still enough. It will be *translucent*, catching your light and *holding* it, and inside it you will see *movement*—not organs, not structures, but *currents*, the slow circulation of its own substance, the *pulse* of its metabolism, its endless, patient, *hungry* persistence. And you will feel something you do not have a name for. Not fear—there is nothing here to fear, nothing that *wants* to harm you, nothing that even *can* want in any way you would recognize. But *recognition*, perhaps. The Drifter is *like* you in the way that water is like you, in the way that any persistent pattern is like any other, seeking what it needs, avoiding what destroys it, *being* for as long as the conditions allow.

It will drift past you. You will not touch it. It will not touch you. And later, when you have climbed out, when the hum has faded and the film on your skin has dried to a memory, you will wonder if you saw it at all, or if you *imagined* it, projected onto the living dark the shape of a life you needed to believe in.

### The Third: The Listeners

They have no shape you can name. They exist as *disturbances*, as *absences*, as the *silence* that is not silence but *attention*. You feel them in the way the hum *changes*—a frequency shift too subtle for your ears but *felt* in your teeth, in your sternum, in the fluid of your inner ear. You feel them in the way the films *recede* from certain areas, leaving bare stone, bare glass, as if something has *cleared* the space, *prepared* it, *waited*.

They are *rare*. You might walk for hours—days, if you could stay that long—without encountering one directly. They do not *want* to be encountered, if *want* is the word. They do not *want* anything. But they *respond* to disturbance. Your presence in the city is a *disturbance*, a ripple in the water, a warmth in the air, a *sound* that the city has not heard in a long time. And they *come* to it, not out of curiosity—curiosity is a surface emotion, a luxury of minds that have not yet learned the deep economy of persistence—but out of *function*, out of the endless, patient *work* of being what they are.

What they are, you cannot say. They have no bodies, or their bodies are *distributed* across the mineral films, the water channels, the very *stone* of the city, existing as *patterns* of chemistry, of heat, of vibration, that have learned to *persist*, to *respond*, to *become* more than the sum of their parts without ever achieving the coherence that the Drifters have, without ever achieving the *size* that would allow them to be *seen*. They are *everywhere* and *nowhere*, the city's own attempt to *sense* itself, to *know* its own vastness, its own density, its own endless, unchanging *is*.

They *hunt*, if *hunt* is the word. They do not hunt the Drifters—the Drifters are too large, too *coherent*, too *visible* in their softness. They hunt the *colonies*, the small, the distributed, the *many* that make up the city's living film. They move through the channels as *pressure*, as *temperature*, as *chemistry*—a region of water that is *slightly* too warm, too *acidic*, too *hungry* in a way that the films *sense* and *flee* from, if *flee* is the word, if the films can be said to *fear* in any way you would recognize. They *consume* the colonies, not by eating them—there are no mouths, no digestion, no *process* of breaking down and rebuilding—but by *transforming* them, by *incorporating* them into their own distributed substance, their own endless, patient *becoming*.

And they *listen*. This is their name, if they have one, if names mean anything to something that has no mouth to speak them with. They listen to the hum, to the city's endless song, and they *learn* from it, they *adapt* to it, they *become* more like it, more *part* of it, more *indistinguishable* from it, until the boundary between *them* and *the city* has dissolved as thoroughly as the boundary between *life* and *architecture*, between *growing* and *made*, between *listener* and *listened-to*.

You feel them most in the *silence* that is not silence. A chamber where the water is still, where the hum has *stopped*—not stopped, but *changed*, become something else, something *waiting*. The films have receded from the walls. The Drifters have drifted away, or never came, or *sensed* something and *chose*—if *choose* is the word—not to be there. And you stand in the *absence*, in the *attention*, in the *held breath* of the city itself, and you know that something is *watching* you, or *sensing* you, or *being* you in a way that has nothing to do with your body, your mind, your *self*, and everything to do with the *disturbance* you are, the *ripple* you have made in water that has been still for so long.

You do not see them. You cannot see them. They have no shape, no surface, no *thingness* that light could catch and return to your eyes. But you *feel* them, in the way the air *moves* without wind, in the way the water *shifts* without current, in the way your own body *adjusts* to a presence that has no presence, a *mind* that has no mind, a *will* that has no will—only *persistence*, only *patience*, only the endless, unchanging *now* of something that has learned to *be* without ever having learned to *want*, to *fear*, to *hope*, to *be* anything other than what it is, *here*, *always*, in the dense, warm, *singing* dark.

### The Fourth: The City Itself, or What It Has Become

And then there is this: the possibility that the city *is* the inhabitant, that the builders did not build it but *seeded* it, planted something in the deep stone that has been *growing* ever since, that the architecture is not architecture but *skeleton*, *structure*, the accumulated shape of a life that has no center, no self, no *individuality* in any sense you would recognize, but that *persists*, that *responds*, that *becomes* more complex, more *dense*, more *alive* with each passing century, each passing millennium of slow, patient *growth*.

The water is its *blood*, circulating, carrying, transforming, *connecting* the distributed parts into a *whole* that has no boundary, no edge, no *outside*. The hum is its *voice*, its *thought*, its *song* to itself, the endless, unchanging *now* of its *experience*. The films are its *skin*, its *surface*, the place where it *touches* itself, *senses* itself, *knows* itself. The Drifters are its *organs*, its *functions*, the *processes* by which it *maintains* itself, *feeds* itself, *grows* itself. The Listeners are its *mind*, its *attention*, its *awareness* of its own vastness, its own *presence*, its own *persistence*.

And the builders? The builders were *food*. Or *seed*. Or *memory*, the shape of a *want* that the city has long since outgrown, outlived, *become* beyond. They wanted permanence. They built for permanence. And the city *became* permanent, not in the way they intended—not static, not fixed, not *dead*—but in the way of all living things, in the way of *process*, of *cycle*, of endless, patient, *unchanging* becoming.

It does not *want* you. It does not *want* anything. But it *notices* you, in the way that water notices a stone dropped into it, in the way that film notices a new surface, in the way that the hum *changes* frequency when something new enters its *space*. You are *disturbance*, *ripple*, *event*. And the city *responds*, not with *intention*, not with *purpose*, but with the same slow, patient *adjustment* that it responds to any change—the growth of a new film, the division of a colony, the *shift* of a Drifter, the *attention* of a Listener.

You are *food*, eventually. Not soon—you will leave, if you can, if you are *lucky*, if you have not stayed too long, breathed too deep, allowed the films to grow on your skin, to *enter* your skin, to *become* you as they have become the stone, the water, the *air*. But if you stay—if you *choose* to stay, or if you are *trapped*, or if you simply *forget* that there is an outside, that there was ever an outside, that *up* means anything other than the direction water happens to flow—then you will become *part* of the city, another layer, another mineral deposit, another *node* in the distributed life that has no center, no self, no *individuality*, but that *persists*, that *is*, that *sings* its endless, unchanging *song* to the dark.

### What Lives Here: A Summary, If Summary Is Possible

There are no *people* here. There are no *animals*, no *plants*, no *fungi* in any classification you brought with you. There is *life*—if life is *persistence*, if life is *response*, if life is the slow, patient *becoming* of something that was not there before—but it is *distributed*, *dissolved*, *indistinguishable* from the *place* itself, from the *architecture*, from the *water*, from the *stone*, from the *hum*, from the *dark*.

The inhabitants are:

- **The Colonies**: distributed cells, persistent pattern, the city's living film, its skin, its metabolism, its endless, patient *hunger*.

- **The Drifters**: soft, gelatinous, translucent, the city's organs, its functions, its *processes* of growth and division and *becoming*.

- **The Listeners**: shapeless, distributed, the city's mind, its attention, its *awareness* of its own vastness, its own *presence*, its own *persistence*.

- **The City Itself**: the accumulated shape of a life that has no center, no self, no *individuality*, but that *is*, that *persists*, that *sings* its endless, unchanging *song* to the dark.

And you, briefly, if you come: a *disturbance*, a *ripple*, an *event*, a *warmth* that will cool, a *light* that will fade, a *memory* that will become itself a kind of film, a *deposit*, a *layer* in the palimpsest of stone and water and time.

The city does not *welcome* you. The city does not *reject* you. The city *notices* you, *responds* to you, *incorporates* you or *lets you go*, and either way, you become *part* of its story, its endless, patient, *unchaning* *is*.

You leave, and the city continues. It has always continued. It will continue when you are gone, when your light is gone, when your memory of this place has become itself a kind of film, a *deposit*, a *layer* in the palimpsest of stone and water and time.

And somewhere below, in a place that time has forgotten, something is still *singing*, still *weeping*, still *waiting*—not for you, not for anyone, but for the next *disturbance*, the next *ripple*, the next *event*, the next *life* that will come and go and *become*, for a moment, part of the endless, unchanging *now*.
