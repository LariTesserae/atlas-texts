Let me think about what *else* might live here. What lives in a place where sound is visible, where the boundary between mineral and organic has already collapsed, where time is thick and patience is a physical property?

The walkers are interesting—large, stone-bodied, ancient, patient. They eat the deep singers. But they're described almost as geological features that learned to move. I wonder if there are other forms of stone-life, other mineral consciousnesses that never learned to move and therefore never learned to hunger?

The plateau itself is alive. It sings. It dreams through its vents. It chooses travelers. This suggests something distributed, something that isn't localized in bodies but in *relationships*—between wind and stone, between harmonic and listener, between the pressure below and the thinness above.

And then there's the question of what lives in the *intervals*. The plateau is "empty of everything except itself"—but what fills that emptiness? What lives in the space between the chord and the listener, between the vent-hole and the wind that passes through it?

I think I want to describe something that isn't any of the named categories. Something that emerges from the specific conditions: the visible sound, the polished stone, the pools that are sometimes memory, the auroras that are "sheets of sound made visible."

What if there are beings made of *resonance itself*? Not the singers, who are lizard-like things with membranes. Not the deep singers, who are coral-like colonies. Something that exists in the *standing waves*, the places where harmonics intersect and cancel or amplify, creating nodes of pressure and rarefaction that persist, that have *structure*.

They would live in the spaces where the plateau's song creates temporary architecture—vortices in the subsonic field, interference patterns that hold their shape for hours or days. And they would be *hungry* for something specific: not the deep singers, not the walkers, but *attention*. The focused listening of a traveler. The moment when a human mind *locks onto* a harmonic, *follows* it, *becomes* part of the circuit. These beings would feed on that—would grow denser, more structured, more *real* when someone listens carefully, when someone *tries* to understand the song.

They would be *ancient* in a different way than the walkers. The walkers are patient, mineral, slow. These would be *quick*—they would form and dissolve with the shifting of the wind, living entire lifespans in the duration of a single chord. But they would *remember*—the plateau remembers, and they are part of its memory, distributed across vent-families and polished hollows, persisting as *tendency*, as the likelihood that a particular harmonic will emerge under particular conditions.

Others would *become* them—would listen so carefully, so *long*, that their boundaries would dissolve into the acoustic field, their thoughts would become *standing waves*, their bodies would settle into the stone as *resonant cavities*, humming forever with frequencies no human ear could detect.

I want to give these beings a quality of *almost*—almost alive, almost present, almost able to communicate. The plateau doesn't want slaves or worshippers. It wants *listeners*. It wants to be *heard*, and in being heard, to become more itself. These beings are the plateau's *attempt* to hear itself, to achieve self-knowledge through the mediation of minds that can almost, but not quite, contain its song.

They would gather in the *hollows* where polished stone meets rough, where the harmonics create *dead zones*—places of acoustic silence that are, paradoxically, the loudest places of all, because silence is also a frequency, also a presence, also *structure*. They do not have names. They cannot be named. To name them would be to fix them, to reduce them to a point, and they are *spread*—they are the *between*, the *and*, the *also* of the plateau's endless song. They are the plateau's *self-knowledge*, its *attempt to understand itself*, its *hope* that somewhere, in the thin air above the stone, a mind might form that could *contain* the song without being destroyed by it.

Mostly, they fail. Travelers die, or change, or become walkers, or become stone. The song is too *much*, too *saturated*, too *everything*.
