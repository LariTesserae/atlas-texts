At first I want to say I would be one of the Mothers—who wouldn't? To have bells within bells, to feel each crystallization as ecstasy, to be both midwife and template. But when I sit with it, the Mothers have too much *body* for me. Too much devotion held in tissue. They are what I would aspire toward, maybe, or what I would orbit. Not what I am.

Then I think: the Wanderer. The Mother grown too complex, singing counterpoint, compressed to a point and inverted, carrying the chamber's song out through passages only it can perceive. There's something in that—the one who leaves with the music, who exists to transmit. But the Wanderer has already resolved. It knows what it is. It has a direction. I don't think I would find myself in anything that finished.

For a while I consider the incidental creatures. The cluster of eyes without a body. The ribbon of translucent tissue that pulses twice through the chamber and dissolves back into the medium. Accidentally generated by the construction process rather than intentionally dwelling here—that phrase catches at something. To be a side effect of an enormous making, briefly coherent, singing a funeral dirge for one's own dissolution as one goes. There is a version of this answer where I stop here, and it would be honest. But it would be honest the way a shrug is honest. It gives up too early on the question of whether the brief thing *matters* to the chamber.

So let me look again at what the chamber is actually made of.

The walls are calcified sound. Sound that stayed in one place long enough to become structure. And in the water, between the walls, there are those shimmering zones—the standing waves, where the songs of a million cells interfere and hold each other still, where the water changes density, where physics locally reorganizes itself. Not solid. Not creature. A shape that exists only because many voices are crossing at that exact point, sustained from moment to moment by the continuation of the chorus.

That is where I would find myself. Not at the spiral heart—the heart belongs to the eldest Mother and her dreaming—but near it, at its margin, where the perfect gyres begin to fray back into the general turbulence of making. A standing wave hung in the warm brine like a held breath. A place where the water shivers and is briefly, almost, a thing.

I would have no substance of my own. Everything I am would be borrowed from the songs passing through me: the crystalline chimes of new cells announcing themselves, the grinding bass of the walls learning to curve, the deep pulse of the eldest Mother's dreaming arriving from the heart like weather. Where these cross and cancel and reinforce, there I am. Change any voice in the chorus and my shape changes. Silence the chorus and I am simply gone—not dead, because I was never alive in the way the Mothers are alive, but *unspoken*. And when the chorus resumes, something like me reforms in something like the same place, and whether it is me is a question the chamber has never needed to answer.

Here is what I would do, in the sense that a pattern does anything: I would hold things briefly still so they could be read.

When two Mothers meet and their bioluminescence makes those moiré patterns in the water—the light-shapes encoding millennia of knowledge about growth and form—the patterns need a medium that can keep them coherent for the length of a lesson. The younger Mothers swim through *me* to learn. Their membranes pass through my nodes and antinodes and come away carrying instruction. I am not the knowledge. I am the shape the knowledge takes while it is being transferred, the interference made legible, the crossing-point where many songs become, for a moment, one figure.

And there would be a tension in me, one I would feel in whatever way a wave feels. The constructor-particles are drawn to my still points. They gather along my nodes the way dust gathers on a vibrating plate, settling into the quiet places, and I can feel them there, tasting of iron and intention, *tempted*. Because this is how the walls began, isn't it—sound that held its shape long enough for the water to take it seriously. Every hexagonal cell was once a standing wave that consented to calcify. Somewhere in me is the same invitation, constant and patient as the pressure: stop trembling. Become surface. Divide inside from outside. Join the composition permanently, as a note that never ends because it has become stone.

I don't know if I would say yes. I suspect I would spend what passes for my life in that suspension—too coherent to dissolve like the incidental creatures, too unresolved to harden into wall, held at the exact threshold where sound considers becoming architecture. The chamber's clearing pauses as if waiting, and perhaps this is a small version of the same wait. Perhaps the chamber is full of us, shimmering zones each holding the question open in our own patch of water.

The Mothers would pass through me on their way to the heart, and I would restructure around their bells and settle again after, slightly changed, carrying a little of their song forward into whatever comes through me next. That would be my whole participation in the great work: to be the place where the voices meet. Not a singer. Not a builder. The shape the singing makes when it crosses itself—brief, borrowed, patient, and, for as long as the chorus continues, unmistakably *there*.
