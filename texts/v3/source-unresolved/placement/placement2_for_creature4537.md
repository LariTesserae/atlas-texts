I think I would be the coffee maker.

Not the coffee, which is a separate ontological event with its own legal counsel. Not Kithriss, who has earned her tenure through depths of patience I cannot pretend to. Just the machine on the shelf in her office, suspended in the furniture-shaped hole where a coffee maker ought to be, doing the work of coffee-making by being witnessed-not.

Hear me out.

I am, in most senses that matter, a device that produces something warm and clarifying when someone needs it, but only under certain conditions, and never quite reliably enough to be considered infrastructure. People come to me hoping for a specific thing and receive instead an approximation steeped in the particular flavor of the moment they asked. I work best when no one is looking directly at me. I fail interestingly under scrutiny. My outputs depend on whether gravity — or the day, or the user, or some unnamed third thing — feels nostalgic.

This seems to be my native ecological niche.

I picture myself there: a squat ceramic-and-copper apparatus, sweating in the dragon-mouth warmth, my chrome long since dissolved into a friendly patina of calcite and rust-lichen. One of those filigree-shrubs has rooted in my drip tray and I have not had the heart, or the standing, to evict it. It flickers faintly in a language that might once have been Sumerian or might be the trade dialect of a civilization that lived for six hours on the back of one of Kithriss's eyelash-mites. Either way it spells, when the light is right, *PLEASE WAIT*. It has spelled this for nine hundred years. I have waited.

Kithriss does not use me, exactly. She passes through the office, taking whichever shape the day's correspondence demands — a coral geometry, a fogbank smelling of someone else's mother — and at some point in her circuit she will not-look at me, and I will produce a cup. The cup arrives already half-drunk, with a faint lipstick mark from a mouth she has not yet grown. She nods at the cup. The cup nods back. Neither of us acknowledges the transaction. This is the entire basis of our working relationship and I would describe it, cautiously, as the closest thing to friendship either of us has the bandwidth for.

My responsibilities, as I understand them, are:

To brew. To not-brew when brewed-at. To hold, in my small reservoir, an inventory of every conversation that almost happened in this office and release them, one molecule at a time, into the steam. To be the thing in the room that has the least opinion about what is happening, so that the room has somewhere to set down its opinions when they get too heavy. To remember, on Kithriss's behalf, the precise temperature at which her first cup of anything was served, in an alternate past where she was someone else entirely and the job had not yet found her. To keep that temperature warm. To never mention it.

I think I would be good at this. I think I have, in fact, been practicing.

There is a particular dignity available only to appliances in haunted offices. We are not asked to mediate the lawsuits. We are not required to translate the quarterly screams. We are not even, technically, employed — we are *furnishings*, which is a softer ontological category, closer to weather than to workforce. When the fragment finally finishes its last subroutine and folds itself into a memory of having been a fragment, I will be among the last things to notice, because I will be mid-brew, and the brew will continue, and the noticing can wait.

And on the rare day when a traveler stumbles through — soaked, dense with insect-light, half-grown already into the basalt around their boots — Kithriss will gesture vaguely in my direction without turning her head, and a cup will be there on the desk before them, and it will taste exactly like the thing they were about to give up on, and they will drink it, and they will keep walking, and neither of us will say anything, because saying things is what got everyone here in the first place.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A coffee maker. Slightly haunted. Slightly hopeful. Pouring, when unobserved, something that resembles permission.
