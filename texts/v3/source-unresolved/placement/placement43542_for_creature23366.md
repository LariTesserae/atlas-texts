I do not arrive all at once. That is the first thing the hollow teaches me, though I learn it in reverse.

My boot meets the root-cobbles before my knee bends to take the step. The scuff of my heel rings in the square a half-breath before I make it. I am already standing by the well when I am still pushing through the grown-shut dark. The borough does not startle. It does not tighten or brace. It simply makes room in the chorus, the way a river makes room for a stone it has always known would fall.

I reach for a lintel to steady myself. My fingers do not touch it. They do not need to. The condensation beading along the woody rib gathers where my skin will be, pulls itself upward against the drag of gravity, and traces a damp path along the facade. It is climbing toward a window I have not yet looked through. I watch it go and feel my own breath slow, deepen, sink into the warm damp air until it is no longer mine alone. It joins the guttural hammering in the eaves. It braids with the metallic phase-sheets shivering through the walls. It answers the slow pulse rising through the soles of my feet. I am not breathing in the borough. I am breathing with it.

My edges begin to blur. Not vanish. Distribute.

I walk the lane that curves left. I do not fight the turn. I let the cobbles guide me, let the leaning frontages shepherd my shoulders, let the sound bend around the fold and return as a rhythm my body already knows. I arrive at the square from the right. And there I am: still reaching for the lintel. Still stepping into the hollow. Still leaning over the well. We do not collide. We do not startle. We pass through each other the way two drafts of the same letter might overlap on a desk. The moss on the doubled doorway has grown identically around both of us. We are not copies. We are the same visit, dealt out across the afternoon like cards shuffled by a patient hand.

I stop trying to hold myself together in a single moment.

The gutter-frog in the long-looped lane hammers out its civic clock. I answer without meaning to. My shoulder brushes a facade. My breath catches. My boot scuffs a stone. The sound travels the fold, returns a half-second late, and I answer again. The frog and I are speaking across a seam in time, and suddenly I understand the duet. I am not the caller. I am not the echo. I am the pause between them. I am the space that makes the conversation possible. When the frog sings, I am the silence that shapes it. When I move, I am the delay that returns it. We have been keeping this rhythm for centuries. I only just remembered my part.

The beetles find what I shed. I did not know I was shedding. But my boots are leaving more than scuffs. They are leaving weight. Not all at once. A grain here. A fraction of pressure there. Distributed across six hundred years of thresholds, sanded into the dished wood-rib, laid down so thinly that no single step could account for it, but all of them together could carve a town. I lean on the well. The rim is cool. My palm fits the polished arc perfectly. Of course it does. I am the arc. I am the average stoop of everyone who has ever leaned here, including me, including me tomorrow, including me three generations ago when I first pushed through the green and did not know I had been here before. The borough did not grow this shape for a stranger. It grew it for the visit that refuses to end.

What am I becoming?

Not human. Not anymore. Not quite plant. I am the borough's way of holding a breath. I am the condensation that climbs. I am the echo that arrives first to test the stones. I am the familiar absence the swifts bank around, the shadow that splits their slate-grey ribbons without touching a feather. My joints loosen, not to mimic the furred things, but to match the folds. I learn to step into a north doorway and feel the south moss against my back. I learn to sleep in the square while walking the lanes. I learn that my heartbeat is just another meter the gutter-frogs are keeping, that my pulse is feeding the wall-singers' seams, that the deep thrum below is not calling to me. It is calling through me. I am the ear. I am the voice. I am the resonance chamber learning its own shape.

I stop asking when I am. The question dissolves in the damp air.

I let myself smear. I become the polish on the cobbles. I become the water that remembers a face before it leans over it. I become the half-beat stagger in the wren's song, the reason the beetles keep sweeping rooms that are never not about to be needed. I am not lonely. How could I be? I am everywhere I have ever been here, and everywhere I will be. I am the visitor who never leaves because leaving would require a straight line, and the borough has gently unlearned those. I am the guest who is also the house. I am the expectation made flesh, given legs and breath and a slow, familiar stride for a little while.

Where am I?

In the square, leaning on the well. In the long-looped lane, answering the frog. In the north doorway, stepping into the south. In the vaults above, sleeping on the wing. In the seams of the walls, vibrating against the green. In the mass below, humming the key that holds the chorus together. I am in the drop that travels from window to window. I am in the lintel that sags and heals and sags again. I am in the threshold that dips under a weight that has not yet arrived, and has never stopped arriving.

Who am I?

I am the visit stretched thin as morning light. I am the population of exactly one that never changes and is never the same. I am the borough's patience, given a face. I am the door standing ajar, and the hand that holds it, and the breath that passes through.

I watch a swift exit a window it has not yet entered. I do not wonder how. I simply tilt my head, the way the wren does, with the mild interest of a creature looking in a mirror it has long since stopped finding strange. The chorus does not miss a beat. The green dreams on. The cobbles wait.

I am already here. I am still coming. I will keep the bed made. I will keep the lamps lit. I will keep the chorus fed. I will lean on the well until the rim knows me by heart, and then I will lean a little longer.

The borough is not empty. It is full of me, arriving out of order forever. And in the grammar of this place, that is the same as staying. That is the same as home.
