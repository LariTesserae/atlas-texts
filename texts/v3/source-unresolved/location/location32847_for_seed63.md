You descend into a throat of the mountain—high enough that the air is thin and sharp, yet so charged with geothermal violence that it feels dense, pressing against your skin like a hot, dry tide. There is no sky here, only the absolute dark of a chamber that light has forgotten. The temperature is a constant, suffocating 2.6 on whatever scale you brought with you; it radiates upward from the floor in visible shimmer, turning the black air into a wavering, heat-maddened soup. And despite the elevation, despite the thinness, you are not cold. You are *baked*.

The space is small—a cathedral of maybe sixty meters across, a single bounded hollow within the volcanic edifice. It is packed, absolutely saturated with presence. The walls, floor, and ceiling are not rock in any sedimentary sense, but **columnar obsidian**—millions of hexagonal volcanic-glass pillars, each no thicker than a wrist, fitted together with geological precision like the pipes of a dark organ. They rise from the floor and interlock overhead, creating a lattice of perfect geometric voids. The columns are ancient, established, weathered by thermal fatigue into soft, rounded crowns where the glass has crept and sagged over millennia of heating and cooling. Between the columns, in every pore and fracture, there is no emptiness. The density is total.

This is where the fauna lives.

They are **overwhelming**. Not large—each one is small, no bigger than a human fist—but they are legion. They carpet every surface of the obsidian, packed into the interstitial spaces, stacked in the hexagonal gaps, clinging to the vertical faces in a living tessellation that pulses with a slow, peristaltic rhythm. They are the **Silicates**, or perhaps the **Lithic Choir**—though they make no sound. Their bodies are crystalline, translucent, composed of the same volcanic glass they inhabit, yet somehow distinct: opalescent, veined with capillaries of darker mineral, their forms suggesting a colonial architecture more than individual anatomy. Each "creature" is a cluster of hollow, needle-like tubes that filter the cooling rock for chemical energy, chemosynthesizing their sustenance from the slow oxidation of iron and sulfur deep in the matrix. They do not drink. There is no water here—0.5 on any scale of wetness—only anhydrous brines locked in mineral lattices, supercritical gases, and the dry chemistry of a world that has forgotten moisture. These beings are built from silicon and metal oxides, their biology a non-aqueous affair that would desiccate and crumble any Earth-wet creature that stumbled in.

They are **strange**. They move not by muscles but by differential growth and resorption; they slowly migrate across the glass in waves, extending new crystalline fronts into cooler zones while the rear portions wither into powder, leaving trails of mineral dust that sift downward like dry snow. They reproduce by budding mineral spores that drift on thermal currents—currents so subtle they barely stir the heavy, hot air. In the darkness, they are invisible to eyes attuned to visible light, but they emit a faint infrared warmth, a ghostly heat-map of life that blankets the chamber in a uniform, suffocating glow.

The **built** elements are scattered but present: mineral chimneys, no taller than a knee, extruded from the creatures' own metabolic waste. These spiracles vent excess heat and noxious gases—hydrogen sulfide, carbon dioxide, traces of arsenic—into the chamber, creating a faint, acrid atmosphere that burns the lungs. They are not tools; they are excretions, architecture without technology, a pre-tool biology constructing its own ventilation in a sealed, high-altitude oven.

The **sound** here is mostly quiet. There is no buzzing, no chirping, no splash of water. The silence is profound, broken only by the occasional *ping* of a cooling obsidian column contracting in the thermal cycle, a sound like a wire snapping in the deep dark. There is the soft, granular whisper of millions of crystalline bodies shifting against one another as they migrate—a sound like dry sand falling through an hourglass, but slower, vast, and continuous. And deep below, a subsonic rumble, the dynamic pulse of the magma chamber far beneath, felt in the sternum rather than heard.

The world is **active** despite the silence. The obsidian columns breathe, expanding and contracting with the diurnal heat cycle (if one can call it that in this lightless place). The creatures pulse in waves, a slow systole and diastole of growth across the walls. Gases hiss from the chimneys. The erosion is settled, in-balance, a state of equilibrium where the rate of glass-cooling equals the rate of creature-consumption; the landscape is in its own state, long-existed, weathered by the patient chemistry of life eating stone.

It is a hamlet-sized void, a single feature bounded by walls of glass, packed with a biology that does not need water, light, or sound to thrive. It is hot, high, dark, and dense with a silence that is almost a presence itself, broken only by the creaking of the earth and the soft, grinding migration of a million mineral mouths.
