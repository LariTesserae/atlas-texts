You stand on the **Saturated Shelf**—a plateau so high the horizon curls upward like the lip of a cup, the air thin enough to taste of copper and ozone, yet impossibly damp. The temperature hovers at that cruel, cool edge where moisture clings to everything, a perpetual ten degrees that never warms, never freezes. Here, water does not fall from the sky; it *exhales* from the stone.

Not dramatic seepage, but a constant, mineral-heavy sweat that beads on every surface, every crack, every ruin. It is a landscape in a state of permanent, slow weather—erosion and accretion in balance, the mineral world dreaming itself into new configurations.

You are alone. There are no animals, no plants, no people. Only the wet stone, the bright wrong light, the humming silence, and the scattered, ancient marks of those who once stood here, looked up at the fraying sky, and built their small shelters against a wind that never stops.
