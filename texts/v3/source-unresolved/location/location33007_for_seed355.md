The heat arrives before anything else—a wet, pressing weight that soaks through clothing and skin alike, pooling in the hollows of your body. It is thermal oppression without illumination, a sauna at midnight. You stand—or rather, wade—in water that reaches your chest, warm as fresh milk, stagnant, the color of weak tea left to sour for centuries. It does not lap against your legs; it *breathes*, rising and falling in slow, tidal rhythms that have nothing to do with any moon, driven instead by the pulse of heat from the bedrock below.

Above, the sky is not visible. A ceiling of mist hangs perpetually, three meters overhead, condensing on the undersides of pale, architectural forms that loom from the flood. These are the buildings. They are not constructed but *grown*, or perhaps eroded into being—vast, smooth, bone-white formations that rise from the water like the calcified vertebrae of something immense and drowned. They have existed here long enough to reach a terrible equilibrium (erosion 2.1), their surfaces worn glassy by the patient chemistry of the warm water, rounded at every edge, pitted with cavities that serve as doorways, windows, and courtyards. The city (built 2.3) is a sculpture of negative space, carved by dissolution rather than addition, a metropolis that the water has whittled from the living rock over epochs.

There is no technology here (tech 0.4). No metal gleams in the dark. No tools litter the plazas. The architecture is biological in its logic but mineral in its substance—calcium carbonate, perhaps, or silicate soaked until it becomes plastic, then lithified into permanence. Staircases spiral upward into the mist, but they are not steps; they are the natural terraces of the stone, worn smooth by the slow traffic of water and time. Bridges arc between towers, but they are natural arches, the last remnants of stone roofs that collapsed centuries ago, leaving only the lintels to span the canals.

The layout is urban in its regularity, yet sparse (density 0.9), with vast, water-filled voids between the structures. Districts extend into the murk, open and regional in scale (1.5), a valley-filling settlement visible only in fragments through the humid dark. You can see the silhouettes of distant towers, other plazas, fading into the charcoal distance. The spaces between them are not empty; they are full of mist and the slow, convective movement of the flood.

Vegetation is sparse (0.9). A few pale, leafless shrubs cling to the higher terraces, their branches like bleached coral or the ossified nerves of something that once tried to grow here. They do not photosynthesize; they are mineral accretions that mimic plant form, or the last, desperate experiments of a biology that has otherwise surrendered to the stone. There is no fauna (0.2). No fish break the surface. No insects hum in the humid dark. The city is a necropolis of silence, broken only by the sound of water dripping from the ceilings of caverns, the creak of thermal expansion in the rock, and the low, subsonic hum of the earth itself (sound 0.6, mostly quiet).

But the world is active (dynamic 2.0). The water is not stagnant; it circulates in slow, laminar currents, hot at the bottom, cooler at the surface, driving a constant exchange of minerals and heat. The mist above is alive with condensation and evaporation, a self-contained weather system. The stone is dissolving, molecule by molecule, feeding the water, which deposits it elsewhere in new, provisional configurations. The city is slowly, imperceptibly, rebuilding itself while it rots.

And then there is the wrongness (weirdness 2.8). Reality is thin here, worn smooth like the architecture.

The water reflects not the darkness above, but a different city—one of sun-bleached stone, dry and bright, filled with movement and the echo of voices. The reflection does not match your actions. When you raise your hand, the reflection raises a different hand, or a hand that belongs to someone standing behind you, though no one is there.

Gravity pools in the plazas. In the center of the main square, the water is heavier; you sink, struggling against a density that feels like syrup. At the edges, it is lighter; you float, suspended, unable to swim downward, your feet dangling in mid-water, touching nothing.

The architecture is non-Euclidean. You walk down a staircase for ten minutes, counting forty-seven steps, and emerge at a terrace that is visibly higher than where you began. Corridors loop back on themselves but deposit you in different districts, the walls shifting when unobserved. The mist forms shapes—faces, hands, fragments of architecture—that watch you with hollow, patient eyes before dissolving into vapor.

The darkness is not empty. It is a substance, a pressure, filled with the echoes of footsteps that were never taken, the murmur of conversations in languages that have not been spoken. The heat is not uniform; it comes in waves, pockets of scalding air that burn the lungs, alternating with drafts of preternatural cold that make the water steam and hiss where it meets your skin.

Time moves unevenly. A minute in one courtyard is an hour in another, or perhaps no time at all. The erosion is not slow here; in certain alcoves, it is a visible process, the stone visibly softening, flowing like warm wax under your fingertips, then hardening again into a new shape when you look away.

There are no animals, but the city dreams. The mist carries images of what this place was—or what it will be—or what it is in a parallel moment, overlapping like double exposures. The water remembers. It holds the topographical shapes of swimmers who drowned here millennia ago, and their outlines sometimes surface, not as ghosts but as ridges in the water that part around your legs, solid and cold, before sinking back into the depths.

This is a place where the distinction between built and natural has dissolved into the warm, dark flood, where water is both architect and sole inhabitant, where heat exists without light, and where the silence is not empty but full of the geometry of absence. It is a flooded, dreaming metropolis, slowly eating itself and rebuilding in the same breath, a landscape in perfect, terrible balance with its own dissolution.
