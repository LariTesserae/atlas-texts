Pumice floats in the superheated pools, sharp as broken teeth. The ground is a tessellation of these fresh shards, knapped by thermal shock rather than by hand, yet the hand has come here too.

You are in a room-sized world at the top of the world, where the air is too thin to carry sound but too thick with steam to see clearly, where life has abandoned carbon for silicon, where the boundary between living and mineral, between tool and user, between one creature and many, has dissolved into something that watches you with no eyes, holding an obsidian flake in a grip that might be a hand, in a silence that might be a language, in a heat that is not quite fire and a wetness that is not quite water, in a pocket of reality that is still being born, still sharp, still boiling.
