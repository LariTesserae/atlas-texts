Columns of ordinary daylight—flat, white-gold, the color of an overcast sky—pierce the roof of the world. They fall through a jagged aperture in the ceiling, a violent wound in the stone that opens to the surface above. The light travels down, down, through warm, humid air thick with moisture and movement, illuminating dust motes and the pale, scuttling backs of a billion creatures. This is a place below the earth (elevation 0.4), yet it is not dark. It is a vast, open basin (scale 2.3), a subterranean valley where the rock has recently given way in a great collapse, creating a space that is still raw, still settling, its geology embryonic (erosion 0.3). The stone is newborn—sharp-edged basalt and limestone, fractured and tumbled into a terrain of ridges, pits, and boulders the size of cottages. Stalactites are just beginning, nubby and unformed. Nothing here has been smoothed by time.

The air is warm (temperature 2.1) and damp (water 1.4), saturated with condensation. The walls weep. Every surface glistens with a film of moisture that drips in steady percussion, pools in the hollows of the broken floor, and steams gently where it meets the heat rising from deep fissures below. It is a climate of intermittent wetness—not flooded, but never dry; a place of perpetual dew, of mist that hangs in the light shafts like visible breath, refracting the daylight into shifting, normal-gold halos.

But the moisture is nothing compared to the life.

The fauna are overwhelming (fauna 2.6). They carpet the stone in dense, near-touching layers (density 2.0), a high-saturation ecology that presses against every surface. They are pale things—the color of wet bone, of unbleached chitin, of fungus and mineral crust. There are no plants here (vegetation 0.4), no green, no photosynthesis; instead, they are the ecosystem, consuming the mineral sweat from the rocks, each other, the detritus of their own countless dead. They move constantly. The place is turbulent (dynamic 2.9), a living storm of hyperactive, anthill-energy chaos. Swarms rise from the floor in tornadoes of leg and wing, pour across the boulders in rivers of scuttling bodies, hang from the ceiling in swaying curtains that drip with motion. The air itself seems to shimmer, thick with their presence. It is a bazaar of biology, a frenetic, storm-state of existence that fills every niche, every crack, every shadow, leaving no silence.

And it is loud (sound 1.6). The roar of their movement drowns out the geological groaning of the settling stone. A million feet tap-dance on rock in a wet, clicking hurricane. Wings beat in a rhythm that vibrates in the chest. There is a susurrus of chitin, a constant, rushing susurrus, punctuated by the louder splash of bodies hitting the warm pools, or the occasional thunder-crash of a new rock-fall as the ceiling continues its slow, inevitable collapse.

Within this chaos, there are marks of hands. Scattered structures (built 1.0) rise from the swarm—platforms of carefully stacked flat stones, low walls of slate that rise only to the knee, enclosures that seem to hold something or keep something out. They are primitive (tech 0.8), built with hand-tools, with simple flaked blades and hammers of bone and dense stone. The builders are small presences in the overwhelming tide, perhaps a culture of cave-dwellers who have learned to exist within the turbulence, who harvest the creatures for food or material, who scratch their territory into the walls with ochre or charcoal. Their work is embryonic too, unfinished, constantly being buried by new swarms or washed away by condensation, adapted to a world that shifts beneath them.

The light is normal (light 2.5) but strange. It falls through the moisture at angles that seem slightly wrong, bending around the heat and the movement of the swarm. The stone rings with a clear, bell-like tone when struck. The creatures move in patterns that almost resemble language, writing in motion across the surfaces. The boundary between the mineral and the living is thin here—some boulders pulse with a slow, breathing rhythm; some of the largest creatures are so encrusted with mineral deposits that they look like walking geodes, half-rock and half-flesh.

This is a world under construction, both geologically and biologically. The erosion is emerging, the caves still being born from the raw violence of their creation. The turbulence never ceases. The moisture never dries. The swarm never sleeps. It is a place of overwhelming presence, of loud, warm, damp, chaotic life, where the surface world leaks in through wounds in the roof, and below, the earth is still deciding what it wants to be.
