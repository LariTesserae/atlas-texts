The Plain of Settled Snow

You are below the surface, and have been for a long time. The world here is liquid—not merely wet, but submerged, drowned, a place where the boundary between water and world has dissolved. This is a vast, regional basin (scale 2.0) lying at moderate depth, where the pressure is constant, ancient, and absolute. The elevation is low, far beneath the waves, in a realm where the water column above is so thick with history that it has become a kind of weather.

The floor is flat. Not flat like a table, but flat like a memory worn smooth—eroded to its final form over eons (erosion 2.8). It is a plain of compacted ooze and lithified sediment, a surface so thoroughly aged that all sharpness has been leached away by time and current. This is the end of the world’s violence, the settled-into-final-form state where mountains have become dust and dust has become stone, only to be worn down again into dust. The terrain offers no purchase, no variation, no shelter. It extends past the immediate horizon, a multi-feature landscape of absolute sameness, broken only by the occasional outcropping of primordial rock that has resisted the flattening, a knuckle of stone rising from the silt like a memory of height.

Above this floor hangs a sky of water—but this is not clear water. The medium is saturated, packed to its limit with suspended matter (density 2.9). It is a snow that never melts, a perpetual fall of mineral and organic particulate that has been churning since before the first life. The water is cold (temperature 0.5), a chill that penetrates to the bone of the stone, keeping everything in a state of suspended animation, near-frozen, viscous, slow.

There is no light (light 0.4). The darkness is absolute, a pressure of blackness that has nothing to do with night. In this darkness, the dense particulate snow glows faintly with its own stored phosphorescence, or perhaps it is simply the absence of light made visible by the density of the medium. You cannot see your hand in front of your face, but you can feel the snow against your skin—millions of tiny grains pressing, drifting, swirling, a tactile blizzard.

And it moves. Despite the ancient stillness of the floor, the water is turbulent (dynamic 2.8). It is a storm-state, a chaotic churning of the saturated medium, eddies and vortices spinning in viscous, slow-motion fury. The snow falls upward and sideways, caught in thermal currents that should not exist in such cold, in pressure differentials that scream of geological violence. It is a maelstrom without impact, a tempest of particulate matter that would deafen you if it could make a sound.

For here is the central paradox: there is silence (sound 0.4). The density of the water, thick as cold soup, absorbs all vibration. The turbulence is visual only, a silent churning of the saturated medium. Even the grinding of stone against stone, the movement of the rare creatures, produces no echo, no ripple of sound. The world is mute. You would hear your own heartbeat, if you had ears that could transmit the vibration through this medium, but the silence is total, a quiet so complete it feels like a physical weight pressing against your eardrums.

Vegetation is sparse (vegetation 0.7). In patches, where ancient cold seeps weep minerals from the primordial rock, there are thin mats of chemosynthetic bacteria—films of grey and faint white that pulse with chemical luminescence. They are the only "plants" here, and they are sparse, scattered across the vast plain like lichen on a tombstone, surviving on the chemistry of the deep.

Fauna is rare (fauna 1.3). You might drift for hours through the silent snow-storm without seeing a living thing, surrounded only by the packed density of falling sediment. Then, perhaps, a shape materializes out of the particulate fog: a pale, eyeless creature, translucent, moving with a jerking, primitive grace through the viscous water. It is a tool-user (tech 0.8), though its technology is indistinguishable from geology at first glance. It carries a stone—a flake of the ancient floor, worn smooth by eons of current but sharpened by deliberate striking against another stone. It uses this primitive hand-tool to crack the calcium shells of the sparse mollusks that graze on the bacterial mats. There are no structures (built 0.2), no burrows, no nests, no made artifacts beyond these scattered implements. Only the tools, left behind when the creature dies and sinks to the flat floor, or carried in translucent appendages, evidence of minds working with the only materials available: stone, bone, and the precipitated calcium of the snow itself.

These creatures are the inheritors of a world that is ending. The erosion has reached its oldest-stable-state; the floor is flat, the mountains are gone, the world is settling into its terminal form. Yet the water above churns with a hyperactive, restless energy, as if the world cannot decide whether to be still or to move. The snow falls forever. The cold persists. The darkness is absolute.

It is a landscape of saturated silence, packed with the debris of deep time, where the only technology is the stone in the appendage of a rare, drifting ghost, and the only sound is the silence of a world that has worn itself smooth and is now simply waiting, turbulent and mute, beneath the weight of its own ancient, liquid sky.
