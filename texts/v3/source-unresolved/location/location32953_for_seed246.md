The place is a **basin** — a single, bounded hollow set high in the old mountains, where the air is thin and the cold bites at 0.3 on your scale. It is not a valley that opens to the world; it is a room with walls. The surrounding ridges, softened by 2.8 units of erosion, have settled into their final, primordial form: near-flattened, deeply aged, the stone worn smooth as river glass but for the slow creep of frost. The basin floor is a vast, empty plain of that same ancient rock, sparsely carpeted with vegetation that clings to the damp.

Water exists here not as river or rain, but as **condensation** — a persistent, intermittent wetness. The elevation traps moisture; at night it settles as dew and frost, by day it beads on stone and moss in the thin, sharp light. There are pockets of standing water in the hollows, ice-rimed and still, reflecting the sky in fractured mirrors.

In the center of this emptiness stands the **City** — a dense, hamlet-sized cluster of structures that qualify as urban (1.5) by their organization and layering, yet possess no technology (0.2). They are grown, not built. Towering, hollow constructs of calcified mycelium and chitin, honeycombed and cathedral-tall, rising from the moss in a tight grove. They are empty (0.4) — vast, near-void chambers inside, with no furnishings, no tools, no sign of the hands (or hyphae) that raised them. The architecture is biological, layered, accreted over centuries by processes now autonomous, spiraling upward at angles that defy the logic of masonry.

The vegetation (1.5) is lush in the cold damp: thick reindeer moss, silver-grey lichens, and bioluminescent fungi that pulse with faint blue-green light when the sun dips behind the basin walls. The towers themselves are still alive, or were recently enough — they weep calcium-rich moisture from their pores, feeding the moss that blankets their bases.

The **sound** (2.4) is overwhelming, a constant presence. The wind — funneled through passes in the surrounding rim — enters the basin and finds the hollow towers. It sings through them, not as a single note but as a loud, complex drone, a chord of aeolian pipes the size of redwoods. The sound is physical; it vibrates in your sternum, fills the hollow chambers, and returns to you delayed, as if the space itself is breathing. Between gusts, you hear the **drip** of condensation from the tower eaves, the slow **creak** of moss expanding in the damp, and the distant, tectonic **groan** of the ancient stone settling deeper into the earth.

**Light** (2.1) is normal in quality but thin in substance — high-altitude sharpness, long shadows, a clarity that makes the emptiness feel more absolute. The **weirdness** (1.7) lives in the geometry and the acoustics. The towers lean toward a north that no compass can find. Their interiors are wrong — too large, with spaces that swallow sound and hold it, releasing it seconds later in distorted whispers. The wind does not howl randomly; it plays patterns, almost-language, repeating phrases that feel like memory.

**Fauna** (1.0) is rare. You might encounter, in the deepest twilight of the tower hollows, a pale, eyeless creature the size of a loaf of bread, grazing the phosphorescent moss with movements of geological patience. Otherwise, the city is empty of animal presence — a place of structure, wind, and wet stone.

The world here is **dynamic** (2.1) in a paradoxical way: everything is simultaneously growing and decaying. The fungal towers accrete new layers, thickening by millimeters each century, while the wind erodes their surfaces grain by grain. The moss spreads across the basin floor, inch by inch, reclaiming the stone. At night, the condensation freezes into lace; by morning, it crashes to the ground in small, tinkling avalanches. The city is still being built, and still being unbuilt, in the same slow breath.
