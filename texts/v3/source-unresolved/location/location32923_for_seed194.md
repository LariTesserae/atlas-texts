In the dark, the first thing is the sound. It is not silence; it is a pressure. A low, tectonic thrumming that seems to emanate from the walls themselves, from the deep hollows where the architecture breathes. The frequency is wrong—too slow to be a heartbeat, too steady to be wind, too loud to be stone. It is the resonance of growth, amplified through chambers that were once carved by hands and are now being reclaimed by something older.

You are in a room. Not a cave—the geometry is too deliberate, too urban. The walls curve overhead in a vault that vanishes into absolute blackness (light 0.0), a darkness so complete it has weight, pressing against the eyes like cold water. The air is thin, sparse, almost absent (density 0.1). It does not circulate. It hangs, a cool (temperature 0.8), viscous medium that carries the drone with terrible, bone-shaking clarity. To breathe is to inhale a memory of atmosphere—dry, mineral, faintly metallic.

The walls are plant. But not wood, not anything that drinks. These are lithovores, chemosynthetic giants that draw sustenance from the bedrock itself through non-aqueous chemistry (water 0.3), secreting acids that dissolve stone and reconstituting it as lignin fused with silica. The surface is smooth, glassy, worn to a polish by erosion (erosion 2.9) that has no water to aid it—instead, the slow, grinding expansion of the vegetation itself has planed down the centuries, millimeter by millimeter, until the boundary between the grown and the built has dissolved entirely. It feels like touching a river stone that has been in the current for the age of the world, but colder, and faintly, impossibly fibrous, like corduroy made of granite.

Embedded in this vegetal matrix are the remnants of a settlement (built 2.2, tech 0.7). Primitive tools: flakes of struck flint, hafts of bone, crude cordage now mineralized into the wall itself. They built here, layering their constructions into the hollows of these massive organisms, or perhaps the organisms grew around their buildings, swallowing them whole. Now it is one thing. The erosion has flattened everything to a single, continuous surface (elevation 1.4), yet you can feel the ghost of infrastructure: a niche worn flush, a step smoothed to a ramp, the suggestion of a doorway half-digested by the expanding plant-tissue. It is urban in its complexity, a pocket-scale (scale 0.5) chamber within a vast necropolis of similar rooms, but here, in this intimate hollow, there is only emptiness—vast space between the presences of wall and floor, the silence broken only by the drone.

The vegetation (vegetation 2.8) is total, but not lush. There is no green, no softness. These are architectural plants, filling the space with their mass, their hollows, their slow, geological metabolism (dynamic 1.4). A column might widen by the thickness of a fingernail in a century. But they grow, and as they grow, the chambers within them shift, flex, and resonate. The sound (sound 2.0) is their voice—loud, constant, a chord that never resolves, seeming at moments to form patterns, almost language, almost counting, though no mind guides it. It is strange (weirdness 1.6): the way the resonance seems to watch you, the way the smooth walls reflect a sound that has no source, the way the darkness feels textured, as if the blackness itself is a substance being slowly, silently consumed.

There are no animals (fauna 0.2). Or rather, so few that they might be a geological anomaly—a single, sessile thing perhaps, half-plant and half-animal, lost in the deep dark beyond the chamber, adding its infinitesimal pulse to the great thrum. You are alone with the builders' ghosts and the plants' patient, mineral hunger.

The rules of this place are absolute:
- Light is a myth. Eyes are vestigial. You navigate by the pressure of the drone against your skin.
- Water is a poison or a forgotten dream. The chemistry here is dry, sulfuric, slow.
- Time is a texture. You can feel it wearing down the flint tools, feel it in the creak of the lignin as it expands around the ancient stone.
- The city is a garden; the garden is a tomb; the tomb is still growing.

And the sound continues, louder now, as the walls contract around you, imperceptibly, millimeter by millennium. A heartbeat of wood and stone, in the dark, forever.
