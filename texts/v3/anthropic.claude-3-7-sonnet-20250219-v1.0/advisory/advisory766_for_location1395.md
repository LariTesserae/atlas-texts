# Preparation Guide for The Clockwork Reliquary

## Essential Preparations

### Physical Protection
- Wear thin, tightly-woven clothing that won't snag on delicate mechanisms
- Avoid loose garments, dangling accessories, or anything that could catch in gears
- Bring heat-resistant gloves (the constant mechanical operation maintains a warm ambient temperature)
- Select footwear with non-conductive rubber soles to prevent electrical grounding through the floor plates
- Consider a thin dust mask to protect from iron filing inhalation

### Health Precautions
- Receive mercury exposure training and bring a portable mercury vapor detector
- Pack activated charcoal tablets to counteract mild mercury poisoning symptoms
- Include antihistamines in case of allergic reaction to the fungal spores
- Bring eye protection against metal filings and tiny mechanical debris
- Consider a small oxygen supplement as the air may be thin in higher chambers

### Scientific Equipment
- Chronometer with multiple settings to track both Earth time and observe the Reliquary's timekeeping
- Non-magnetic tools (brass or titanium) for emergency repairs to your equipment
- Electrical insulation meter to detect current variations in the floor plates
- High-sensitivity microphone to capture the subtle acoustic patterns
- Small non-ferrous containers for collecting samples (if permitted)
- Specialized camera with variable light settings for the dim environment

## Environmental Adaptation

### Sensory Adjustment
- Practice moving slowly and deliberately to match the Reliquary's rhythm
- Train yourself to notice minute movements and subtle patterns
- Prepare for the disorienting effect of time distortion by practicing meditation techniques
- Acclimate to low-light conditions beforehand (your eyes will need to adjust to the dim bioluminescence)
- Condition yourself to function in near-silence for extended periods

### Mental Preparation
- Study mechanical systems to better appreciate and understand your surroundings
- Practice patience—the Reliquary operates on a seventeen-minute cycle, and rushing will only disturb its balance
- Mentally prepare for the uncanny sensation of being observed by the more complex mechanical fauna
- Research timekeeping philosophies to better contemplate the purpose of the cathedral
- Consider the philosophical implications of artificial evolution before arrival

## Cultural and Ethical Considerations

### Interaction Guidelines
- Observe the "mechanical prime directive": do not interfere with any functioning system
- Move with extreme care to avoid disrupting the mechanical fauna's activities
- If approached by the larger constructs (especially the brass owls), remain still and non-threatening
- Never attempt to collect mercury or disrupt the flow of lubricants
- Consider that you are entering a potentially sentient ecosystem—act with appropriate respect

### Communication Attempts
- Bring a small mechanical music box as a potential communication tool
- Study horological symbolism to recognize possible messages in the mechanisms
- Learn to identify patterns in gear movements that might indicate communication
- Practice sketching mechanisms quickly to record any meaningful configurations

## Practical Matters

### Survival Essentials
- Pack concentrated nutrients—there is nothing edible in the Reliquary
- Bring sealed water containers (the mercury "waters" are highly toxic)
- Include a small portable battery pack (the electrical currents in the floor are not compatible with human technology)
- Carry a detailed map-making kit to document your path through the chambers
- Bring a climbing harness for navigating between vertical levels

### Navigation
- Compass will be unreliable due to electromagnetic fields—rely on mechanical methods
- Mark your path using temporary chalk marks that will dissipate over time
- Study the seventeen-minute cycle to time your movements between gear shifts
- Listen for rhythm changes that might indicate transitions between chambers
- Look for patterns in the copper wire trees that might serve as navigational markers

### Time Management
- Plan explorations in seventeen-minute increments to align with the gear movements
- Set a strict schedule for your visit—time distortion could cause you to stay longer than intended
- Allow buffer time between chamber explorations for rest and observation
- Schedule regular checks of Earth time against Reliquary time to maintain temporal awareness

## Emergency Preparations

### Exit Strategy
- Memorize at least three potential routes back to your entry point
- Establish time checkpoints to prevent becoming lost in the Reliquary's temporal distortions
- Arrange signal patterns with any companions to regroup if separated
- Consider bringing a thin cord to trace your path if the environment permits

### Threat Response
- If the larger mechanical fauna become aggressive, retreat slowly without turning your back
- In case of gear shifts or mechanical changes, find stable ground and wait for the cycle to complete
- If exposed to mercury, immediately use detoxification protocols and exit to cleaner air
- Should the entire mechanism accelerate unexpectedly, seek higher ground on the wire trees

## Final Thoughts

The Clockwork Reliquary is not merely a location but an experience in mechanical harmony. Your visit will be most successful if you adapt to its rhythms rather than imposing your own. Remember that you are entering a system that has evolved beyond its original design—it deserves both your caution and your reverence. The cathedral does not accommodate human needs, so your preparation must be thorough and your mindset flexible. 

Above all, approach with curiosity rather than the desire to extract or alter. The greatest danger is not to your physical safety but to the delicate balance of this extraordinary mechanical ecosystem. Move at the pace of the great mechanism itself—one tooth every seventeen minutes—and you may be permitted to witness the profound mystery of purpose that drives this clockwork world.
