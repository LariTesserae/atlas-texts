# Preparing for Eyrie Mercantile

## Essential Physical Preparation

**Altitude Acclimation**: At over 12,000 feet, altitude sickness is a serious concern. Spend at least 3-5 days gradually ascending to this elevation. If possible, stay at 8,000 feet for two days, then 10,000 feet for another two days before reaching Eyrie Mercantile. Bring acetazolamide (Diamox) to help with acclimatization and prevent altitude sickness symptoms.

**Respiratory Support**: The thin air will be your most immediate challenge. Practice deep breathing techniques before your journey. Consider bringing supplemental oxygen in small portable canisters for emergencies, especially if you have any respiratory conditions.

**Physical Conditioning**: Train for both cardiovascular endurance and lower body strength at least 2-3 months before your journey. Focus on stair climbing, hiking with a pack, and exercises that strengthen your ankles and knees. You'll be navigating endless stairs, steep inclines, and uneven terrain.

**Vestibular Training**: The swaying rope bridges and cable cars can cause severe disorientation. Practice balance exercises and consider taking medications for motion sickness (meclizine or scopolamine patches).

## Clothing & Gear

**Layered Clothing**: Temperature variations will be extreme. Pack merino wool base layers that wick moisture, insulating mid-layers of down or synthetic fill, and an outer shell that's both windproof and waterproof. Ensure all layers can be easily added or removed.

**Specialized Footwear**: Invest in mountaineering boots with excellent ankle support, aggressive treads, and waterproofing. Ensure they're broken in thoroughly before your trip. Bring crampons for icy conditions and gaiters to prevent debris from entering your boots.

**Hand Protection**: Wind-resistant gloves with gripping surfaces are essential, as you'll often need to hold onto railings, ropes, and cables in high winds.

**Eye Protection**: Bring wraparound goggles with UV protection for wind, debris, and the occasional intense sunlight when clouds part. Include clear lenses for foggy or nighttime conditions.

**Respiration Mask**: A breathable mask with filtration capabilities will help with both the industrial haze from lower tiers and the occasional dust from rockslides.

**Water Management System**: Bring a multi-stage water filtration system and purification tablets as backup. Include a collapsible water container to collect morning condensation or rainfall.

**Anchoring Equipment**: A personal safety harness with carabiners and short tethers will prove invaluable during high winds or when navigating the more precarious parts of the settlement.

**Illumination**: Multiple light sources are essential—headlamp, handheld light, and chemical light sticks as emergency backups. The deep gloom requires more powerful illumination than you might expect.

## Health Considerations

**Hydration Strategy**: Despite the moisture in the air, dehydration occurs rapidly at high altitudes. Drink at least 3-4 liters of water daily, adding electrolyte supplements to maintain proper balance.

**Skin Protection**: The combination of wind, low humidity, and UV exposure (even through clouds) makes skin protection crucial. Use high-SPF, wind-resistant balms for exposed skin and lips.

**Sleep Aids**: Altitude often disrupts sleep patterns. Consider natural sleep aids like melatonin or valerian root, as well as a high-quality sleeping pad and warm sleeping bag rated for alpine conditions.

**Emergency Medical Kit**: Beyond standard first aid, include medications for altitude sickness, respiratory infections, and trauma. Learn how to recognize and initially treat hypothermia, frostbite, and altitude-related conditions.

## Cultural & Practical Preparation

**Currency & Trade**: Bring tradable goods rather than relying solely on currency. Small, high-value items like spices, specialized tools, or compact medical supplies can serve as alternative currency. Study the current exchange rates and trading practices before arrival.

**Language Preparation**: Learn key phrases in at least two or three of the most common languages used in the marketplaces. Focus on terminology related to trading, directions, emergency situations, and basic needs.

**Mechanical Literacy**: Familiarize yourself with basic mechanical systems, particularly steam and hydraulic mechanisms. Understanding how the city's infrastructure functions could prove invaluable during emergencies.

**Local Customs**: Research the social protocols for different tiers of the city—customs may vary significantly between the upper and lower levels. Learn appropriate greetings and the expected etiquette when entering homes or businesses.

**Weather Interpretation**: Study the patterns of cloud movement specific to mountain regions. Learn to recognize signs of impending storms or dangerous wind conditions. The locals' claim of "understanding" the wind isn't merely poetic—there are subtle cues in sound and air pressure that can provide crucial warnings.

## Special Considerations

**Mental Preparation**: The constant movement of the city—from swaying bridges to buildings on hydraulic stilts—combined with the precarious setting can trigger anxiety or vertigo. Practice mindfulness techniques and bring comfort items to create psychological stability.

**Wildlife Awareness**: Develop a basic understanding of the behavior patterns of mountain goats and the large ravens. The "cliff mice" are generally harmless but protect your belongings from their curious investigation. Never attempt to touch or feed any wildlife.

**Temporal Planning**: Understand that travel times between city sections can vary dramatically based on weather conditions. Always buffer your schedule with extra time, and never attempt to cross between city sections when storms are approaching.

**Emergency Retreat Plan**: Map out multiple evacuation routes before arrival. Identify shelters or more stable structures where you could wait out severe weather or geological events. Establish communication protocols with someone outside Eyrie Mercantile who can coordinate help if needed.

**Ethical Considerations**: Determine your stance on local industrial practices before arrival. Some visitors find themselves uncomfortable with certain mining or manufacturing methods used in the lower tiers. Decide in advance how you'll navigate potentially complex ethical situations.

By preparing thoroughly for these unique challenges, you'll be better positioned to appreciate the remarkable engineering, cultural diversity, and stark beauty of Eyrie Mercantile—a human settlement that exemplifies our species' determination to thrive in even the most improbable environments.
