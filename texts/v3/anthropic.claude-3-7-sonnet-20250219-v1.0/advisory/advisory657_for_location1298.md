# Preparation Guide for The City of Wax

## Essential Preparations

### Climate Considerations
Pack for a dry, warm environment similar to desert regions. The City of Wax exists in what appears to be an arid basin where water is scarce:
- Lightweight, loose-fitting clothing in natural fabrics (linen or cotton)
- Light-colored garments that reflect rather than absorb heat
- Wide-brimmed hat or head covering for sun protection
- Comfortable shoes for walking, though consider going barefoot within city limits as locals do
- Light layers for cooler mornings and evenings
- Sun protection (sunscreen, sunglasses)

### Water Management
Water scarcity is a defining feature of the city:
- Bring a personal water filtration system
- Carry multiple water containers (preferably not plastic, as clay or metal would better align with local materials)
- Electrolyte tablets or powder to prevent dehydration
- Be prepared to conserve water rigorously; learn water-saving hygiene practices

### Communication Tools
Given the sound-dampening properties of wax:
- Small handheld chalkboards or tablets for written communication when needed
- Consider learning basic sign language for silent communication
- A quiet musical instrument might be appreciated (flute, small stringed instrument) as music seems valued in intimate settings

## Cultural Sensitivity

### Etiquette
- Practice speaking softly; the city maintains a perpetual hush
- Move deliberately and unhurriedly; rushing is culturally inappropriate
- Respect the morning gathering ritual at cisterns—observe from a distance initially
- Ask permission before touching or photographing wax structures
- Prepare for a different time perception; the city operates on natural rhythms, not clock time

### Appropriate Gifts
If meeting locals, consider bringing:
- Seeds for drought-resistant plants (if allowed through interdimensional customs)
- Small amounts of rare spices or herbs
- Simple wooden or bone tools that might be useful for wax-working
- Nothing plastic or synthetic, which would clash dramatically with the organic aesthetic

## Specialized Equipment

### Documentation
- Cameras with manual settings for the challenging amber light conditions
- Non-invasive light sources that won't damage or alter the wax (no hot lights)
- Sketchbooks and pencils for observations—less intrusive than photography
- Audio recording equipment for capturing the unique acoustic properties

### Scientific Instruments
For those with research interests:
- Thermometer to monitor ambient temperature and wax surface temperatures
- Small hygrometer to measure humidity levels
- Material sampling kit (if permission is granted) to study wax composition
- Foldable microscope for examining wax structures and local flora/fauna

### Safety Equipment
- Compact first aid kit focusing on treatments for:
  * Minor burns (cool compresses, burn gel)
  * Cuts from potential wax edges
  * Dehydration remedies
- Small fire extinguisher (specialized for wax fires, which are different from ordinary fires)
- Collapsible walking stick to test stability of wax structures before putting full weight on them

## Cultural Immersion

### Learning Opportunities
- Research wax-working techniques before arrival
- Study methods of water conservation in desert communities
- Learn about architectural principles that might explain how wax structures maintain integrity
- Familiarize yourself with slow-living philosophies to better appreciate the city's pace

### Participation
- Request permission to apprentice briefly with different artisan groups:
  * Repairers who patch and reinforce structures
  * Artists who create textural designs
  * If extremely fortunate, observe the revered Shapers
- Offer to help with communal water collection in mornings (after observing protocols)
- Ask to assist with tending the precious plants in the city's niches

## Practical Considerations

### Accommodation
- Expect to stay in wax chambers that may be translucent
- Bring minimal privacy screens if needed
- Pack a sleeping mat that distributes weight evenly to avoid damaging wax floors
- Consider a sleeping bag liner rather than full sleeping bag due to the warm climate

### Food and Cooking
- Bring dry foods that require minimal water for preparation
- Portable cooking equipment that generates minimal heat (to avoid affecting nearby wax)
- Spices to share with locals (cultural exchange)
- Sealed containers for any food—to avoid attracting the rare desert animals that sometimes enter the city

### Waste Management
- Carry all waste out with you or learn local disposal methods
- Bring biodegradable soap for personal hygiene
- Pack a portable waste compactor to minimize what you must carry out

## Etiquette for Specific Locations

### The Comb
- Request permission before entering inhabited hexagonal chambers
- Keep light sources low-intensity to avoid overpowering the natural glow through translucent walls
- Remove shoes before entering private dwellings
- Bring a small gift if invited into someone's home

### The Hall of Memory
- Approach as you would a sacred site
- Do not touch the historical tableaux
- Spend time observing how the oldest scenes have transformed
- If sketching or photographing, do so unobtrusively

### Outskirts
- Respect those who have chosen more solitary living arrangements
- Maintain extra distance from workshops that might contain special materials or processes
- Watch for partially eroded structures and avoid climbing or touching unstable areas

## Final Recommendations

- Plan to stay at least several days to adjust to the city's tempo
- Begin and end each day with quiet reflection, aligning yourself with the unhurried rhythm
- Consider bringing a journal to record sensory experiences that technology cannot capture
- Most importantly, prepare your mindset: The City of Wax teaches patience, impermanence, and the beauty of gradual transformation. To truly visit this place means temporarily abandoning modern urgency and embracing a different relationship with time.
