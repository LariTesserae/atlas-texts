# Preparation Guide for Visiting the Submersible Gardens of Eld

## Essential Preparations

### Physical Conditioning
Before attempting to visit the Submersible Gardens of Eld, undertake at least three months of preparation focusing on:
- Flexibility training, especially for prolonged crouching and navigating low ceilings
- Swimming proficiency and breath control exercises
- Balance training for walking on the slightly springy floor surfaces
- Sensory adaptation exercises in low-light environments

### Mental Preparation
- Practice meditation daily to prepare for the altered sense of time
- Study botanical classification systems to help catalog the unique flora
- Learn basic sketch-journaling techniques to document observations
- Develop comfort with enclosed spaces through gradual exposure

## Equipment and Clothing

### Attire
- Lightweight, breathable natural fiber clothing (cotton, hemp, linen)
- Multiple layers that can be removed as you acclimate to the humidity
- No synthetic materials—they can disrupt the delicate ecological balance
- Soft-soled shoes with good grip but minimal tread pattern to avoid damaging the floor
- Waterproof foot wrappings as an alternative (preferred by experienced visitors)
- Head covering to protect from constant ceiling drips

### Essential Equipment
- Handcrafted wooden or plant-based containers for collecting water samples
- Bamboo or wooden notepad with graphite pencils (no ink that could spill)
- Manual wind-up timepiece (no electronics)
- Natural fiber bags for collecting fallen specimens (with garden tender permission only)
- Small mirror for redirecting bioluminescent light when needed
- Hand-carved wooden combs to help groom away clinging moss or spores

### Lighting
- Avoid bringing artificial lights if possible
- If necessary, use only amber-colored light with minimal brightness
- Beeswax candles in protective glass as emergency backup only

## Health and Safety

### Immunizations and Medical Preparation
- Standard tetanus booster
- Anti-fungal oral medication course before and during visit
- Waterborne parasite preventatives
- Natural antihistamines (nettle tea, quercetin) for potential allergic reactions

### Safety Precautions
- Carry a natural whistle carved from hardwood for emergencies
- Memorize the geometric patterns of major water channels for navigation
- Learn to identify the gardeners' hand signals for basic communication
- Practice techniques for clearing eyes and ears of water without contaminating surroundings

## Cultural Protocols

### Approaching the Gardeners
- Never initiate verbal communication—wait for them to speak first
- Learn basic mimicry of their plant-tending gestures as a sign of respect
- Bring small offerings of rare surface-world seeds (only non-invasive species)
- When acknowledged, bow slightly with palms facing upward to show empty hands

### Etiquette Within the Gardens
- Never cut, pluck or damage any living matter without explicit permission
- Step only on established paths where the floor has hardened
- Avoid disrupting water flow in the irrigation channels
- When crossing paths with gardeners, stop and yield the right of way
- Breathe shallowly in flowering chambers to avoid disturbing pollen patterns

## Sensory Adaptation

### Sight
- Spend two weeks prior to your visit adapting to low-light conditions
- Practice identifying shapes by bioluminescent light
- Train yourself to notice subtle color shifts in low illumination

### Sound
- Practice listening for minute sounds in humid environments
- Learn to distinguish between water droplet patterns
- Cultivate "peripheral hearing"—awareness of distant sounds while focusing elsewhere

### Smell and Taste
- Detox from strong scents and flavors one month before visiting
- Avoid perfumes, colognes, and scented products for at least two weeks prior
- Practice identifying plant materials by scent alone
- Bring only the blandest foods to avoid introducing competing aromas

### Touch
- Develop sensitivity in fingertips through texture discrimination exercises
- Practice walking barefoot on various natural surfaces
- Train yourself to detect subtle air current changes

## Navigation and Timekeeping

### Orientation Methods
- Learn to navigate by bioluminescent patterns in the ceiling vegetation
- Study the geometric principles underlying the irrigation canal layouts
- Note that major chambers often have distinctive flora arrangements that serve as landmarks
- Practice mental mapping techniques to remember complex pathways

### Temporal Awareness
- Abandon conventional timekeeping
- Learn to track time by observing the opening and closing cycles of bioluminescent flora
- Note the movement patterns of ceiling-dwelling beetles as rough hourly indicators
- Listen for the timing of the resonant hum in larger chambers, which follows regular cycles

## Return Adaptation

- Plan for a gradual readjustment period after leaving the gardens
- Expect temporary sensitivity to bright light and loud sounds
- Allow time for your body's circadian rhythm to reset
- Journal about your experience immediately while sensory memories are fresh
- Perform a thorough self-check for any clinging spores or microscopic hitchhikers

Remember that the Submersible Gardens of Eld represent an ancient and delicate equilibrium. As a visitor, your primary responsibility is observation with minimal intervention. Those who approach with respect and preparation may be granted deeper access to this living monument of botanical architecture—perhaps even learning some of the gardeners' techniques, passed down through countless generations since time immemorial.
