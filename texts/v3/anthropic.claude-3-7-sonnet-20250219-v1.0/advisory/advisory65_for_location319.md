# Preparation Guide for Visiting the Levelsea

## Essential Gear

**Footwear & Lower Body Protection:**
- Waterproof boots extending at least to mid-calf, preferably knee-high
- Consider bog shoes or specially designed platforms to distribute your weight on the moss mat
- Gaiters to seal the gap between boots and pants
- Waterproof pants or overalls; the environment is perpetually damp

**Clothing Layers:**
- Moisture-wicking base layer (wool or synthetic, not cotton)
- Insulating mid-layer despite the season (the cold here is persistent and penetrating)
- Waterproof and breathable outer shell
- Gloves that balance dexterity with waterproofing
- Head covering that protects ears from the cold

**Temporal Protection:**
- Chronometric stabilizer if available in your timeline (compact wristband models preferred)
- Failing that, bring analog rather than digital timepieces
- Mechanical watches in waterproof cases
- Consider bringing multiple chronometers to track temporal discrepancies

**Navigation Tools:**
- Conventional compasses behave erratically; bring three and triangulate
- Avoid GPS and electronic navigation tools near the larger machines
- Physical maps laminated or in waterproof cases
- Bright colored marking stakes or biodegradable flags

**Scientific & Observation Equipment:**
- Field journal with waterproof pages
- Mechanical camera (avoiding electronic components when possible)
- Glass vials for specimen collection
- Extendable probe/walking stick to test mat stability
- Small mirror on telescoping handle to look under the mat safely

**Sustenance & Medical:**
- Water purification tablets or filter (despite surrounding water, none is safe to drink)
- High-calorie, compact food (your body will burn energy combating the cold)
- Anti-nausea medication (temporal anomalies often induce motion sickness)
- Antihistamines (unusual plant life may trigger allergic reactions)
- Basic first aid including treatment for minor burns (machine encounters)

## Physical Preparation

- Acclimatize to cold, damp conditions for several weeks before visiting
- Practice walking on unstable surfaces to develop balance
- Strengthen core muscles to maintain stability on the undulating mat
- Learn basic bog survival techniques including how to extract yourself if partially submerged
- Practice meditation or other focusing techniques to maintain mental equilibrium during temporal disruptions

## Movement Techniques

- Walk with a wide stance, distributing weight
- Place feet deliberately and slowly, avoiding sudden movements
- Use your walking stick to test surface integrity before stepping
- When the mat undulates, bend knees and flow with the motion rather than fighting it
- Never run, even in emergency situations; the substrate cannot support rapid movement
- If you feel the mat thinning beneath you, immediately but gently redistribute your weight and back away

## Machine Interactions

- Maintain minimum 10-meter distance from smaller machines
- For larger installations, observe the boundary where temporal effects become noticeable (watch for distorted movement of insects or plants)
- Never touch any part of the machines, regardless of how benign they appear
- If you observe steam venting, immediately move perpendicular to the wind direction
- Should you encounter parts that appear damaged or non-functional, do not attempt repairs
- Note that brass and copper components may not be what they seem; some are biological mimics

## Temporal Anomaly Management

- If you experience time slippage, remain still until normal flow resumes
- Should you encounter your own temporal echo, avoid physical contact
- Document any anomalies immediately while memory remains fresh
- If caught in a persistent anomaly, focus on a single object and count breaths until synchronization returns
- In severe cases, move away from the area by following your entry path precisely
- Remember that sensory experiences may occur out of order; what you hear may be from minutes past or future

## Biological Hazards

- Do not collect or consume any plant material regardless of apparent edibility
- Avoid contact with the sundews and pitcher plants; their digestive enzymes affect human skin
- The bioluminescent frogs secrete a mild hallucinogen through their skin
- Insect swarms are generally harmless but disorienting; bring a head net
- Submerged fauna occasionally breach the mat; if you feel movement below, move away calmly

## Mental Preparation

- Accept that conventional physics may not apply consistently
- Prepare for altered perception of time and potential memory discontinuities
- Consider bringing a companion to provide objective observation
- Establish a grounding ritual (counting, physical object manipulation) for temporal disorientation
- Set strict exploration time limits and adhere to them regardless of perceived time passage

## Duration Advisory

- First visits should be limited to 3 hours objective time
- Wear two watches—one sealed in a metal container as control, one exposed
- Upon exit, compare the times to understand your temporal exposure
- Allow 24 hours of recovery time for each hour spent in the Levelsea
- Prolonged exposure has been linked to chronic temporal dysphoria

The Levelsea demands respect rather than conquest. You are visiting a place that exists partially outside conventional time and physical laws. Your goal should be observation and safe return, not mastery or acquisition. The machines have their purpose, incomprehensible as it may be to us. We are merely momentary witnesses to processes that span beyond human timescales.
