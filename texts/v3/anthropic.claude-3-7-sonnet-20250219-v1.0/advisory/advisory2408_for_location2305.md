# Preparation Guide for Visiting the Weavers' Cisterns

## Essential Preparations

### Physical Conditioning
- Begin humidity acclimation exercises 2-3 weeks before your visit; daily steam room sessions of increasing duration will help
- Practice walking on uneven surfaces to prepare for the undulating tile floors
- Train your balance for navigating slippery surfaces
- Develop your night vision through exercises in low-light environments, as lighting will be limited and ambient

### Mental Preparation
- Study meditation techniques focusing on patience and slow observation
- Practice sensory exercises emphasizing touch and smell identification
- Prepare yourself for altered time perception; the Cisterns operate on a slower rhythm that can be disorienting

## Required Equipment

### Clothing
- Breathable, quick-drying natural fabrics (cotton, linen, hemp) that won't degrade in constant humidity
- Layer with thin, wicking undergarments
- Avoid synthetic materials which become uncomfortable in high humidity
- Footwear: Non-marking soft rubber soles with excellent grip; consider traditional rope-soled espadrilles or specialized climbing shoes
- Bring a breathable head covering to protect from continuous moisture dripping

### Essential Tools
- Waterproof document case for your traveler's passport and permissions
- Non-electric illumination: beeswax candles or oil lamps (no flashlights—their harsh light damages the delicate ecosystem)
- Copper drinking vessel (glass or ceramic alternatives acceptable; avoid plastics and metals other than copper)
- Small copper gathering bowl for water collection
- Natural fiber cloths for wiping condensation from your skin and equipment
- Cork-stoppered vials for water samples (if permitted by the caretakers)

### Sensory Aids
- Natural beeswax ear plugs to enhance the subtle acoustic properties of the chambers
- Mineral-oil free eye drops for comfort in the mineral-rich steam
- Unscented skin balm (beeswax and olive oil based) to protect against mineral deposits

## Cultural Protocols

### Approach Rituals
- Upon arrival, wait at the threshold until your body begins to perspire—this is considered proper "introduction" to the space
- Bring a small offering of purified water from your home region, to be poured into the entrance channel
- Learn the basic water-greeting gestures: cupped hands held forward, then touching moisture to forehead and heart

### Interaction Guidelines
- Never interrupt water flow patterns; observe flow directions before moving
- Walk only on the darkest colored tiles; lighter tiles may indicate fragile sections
- Speak in whispers or remain silent; sound affects water vibration patterns
- If you encounter caretakers, make yourself smaller by crouching and wait to be acknowledged
- Never touch the clay tablets or attempt to clean mineral deposits from any surface

### Observation Practices
- Spend your first hour simply sitting and acclimating before exploring
- Use the copper plates' reflections as navigation guides
- Note how different chambers affect your physical sensation; some areas may induce calm while others stimulate awareness
- When examining cistern cells, never cast shadows across their surfaces

## Health Considerations

### Medical Preparations
- Consult a respiratory specialist about humid environment precautions
- Get tested for copper sensitivity or allergies to mineral compounds
- Receive fungal preventative treatment for feet and body folds
- Consider bronchodilators if you have any respiratory concerns

### Survival Needs
- Bring water purification tablets compatible with high mineral content
- Pack dried fruit with high electrolyte content (apricots, dates)
- Include emergency salt tablets to replace what you'll lose through continuous sweating
- Bring small amounts of activated charcoal in case of adverse reaction to mineral water

### Danger Signs
- If water channels suddenly empty or overflow, exit immediately
- If the ambient humming intensifies to uncomfortable levels, move to a different chamber
- Watch for changes in plant color; yellowing indicates potential toxin release
- If copper surfaces darken rapidly, air quality may be deteriorating

## Temporal Considerations

- Plan for visits of at least three days to properly attune to the cistern's rhythm
- Schedule regular "outside time" every 6 hours to reset your time perception
- Keep a water clock rather than mechanical timepiece; track time by drops, not minutes
- Allow final day for physical readjustment before traveling elsewhere

## Post-Visit Protocol

- Gradually reacclimatize to normal humidity levels
- Document your sensory experiences immediately while memories are fresh
- Check skin folds and respiratory passages for unusual mineral deposits
- Consider fasting for 24 hours to clear any accumulated trace elements

Remember that the Weavers' Cisterns operate on principles of slow intention and water reverence. Your presence there is both an intrusion and an offering—approach with equal parts curiosity and humility, and you may be permitted to glimpse the water alchemy that has sustained this remarkable system for generations.
