# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Secondaries (City of Apparatus)

## Essential Physical Preparations

**Clothing & Attire:**
* Pack moisture-wicking, quick-dry fabrics - the perpetual mist will gradually saturate everything
* Bring non-slip footwear with soft soles - hard soles create unwanted noise and may damage sensitive surfaces
* Avoid metal accessories or decorative elements that might interfere with the city's ambient energy fields
* Include a lightweight hooded jacket that can protect against occasional water droplets without impeding movement
* Choose muted colors (whites, pale blues, grays) to harmonize with the environment - bright colors appear jarring and may attract unwanted attention from the geometric fauna

**Specialized Equipment:**
* Compass alternatives: Traditional compasses are unreliable; bring a personal inertial tracking device
* Moisture protection for all electronics - waterproof cases are mandatory
* Light-filtering glasses to manage the omnidirectional glow (polarized lenses with adjustable tint)
* Thin-soled shoes that allow you to sense subtle vibrations in the floor - these can provide navigational cues
* Sound amplification device (discreet earpiece) to help navigate in the sound-absorbing environment
* Marking system (temporary adhesive dots or dissolvable chalk) to track paths through shifting architecture
* Timepiece that can maintain multiple synchronized clocks to track temporal inconsistencies between regions

## Navigation Strategies

**Mapping Techniques:**
* Abandon traditional cartographic approaches - the city defies Euclidean mapping
* Use relative positioning rather than absolute coordinates
* Document junction patterns and architectural motifs rather than distances
* Note the flow patterns of the geometric fauna - they follow consistent if incomprehensible routes
* Track water channel configurations - they reorganize predictably and can serve as navigation references
* Maintain awareness of your elevation relative to visible "horizon lines" within the structure

**Movement Protocols:**
* Walk deliberately but never hurriedly - rushing seems to increase the likelihood of architectural shifts
* When faced with a newly appeared wall or changed passageway, backtrack three junctions before attempting a new route
* If disoriented, follow the water channels downstream - they generally lead to larger gathering spaces
* Never attempt to force entry through resistance - doors that don't open easily aren't meant to be used at that moment
* Rest periodically in areas where crystalline flora are clustered - these zones tend to remain stable longer

## Physiological Considerations

**Sensory Adaptation:**
* Practice peripheral vision exercises before arrival - much of the city's activity happens just outside direct focus
* Prepare for potential inner ear disturbances caused by spaces that visually expand beyond their physical dimensions
* The absence of shadows can cause visual fatigue - plan regular rest periods with eyes closed
* The omnidirectional light may disrupt circadian rhythms - bring melatonin supplements or a sleep mask
* Practice mindfulness techniques to manage the cognitive dissonance of impossible architecture

**Sustenance:**
* Bring all required food and water - nothing in the environment is safely consumable
* The ambient energy of the city can create a false sense of nourishment - maintain regular eating schedule regardless of hunger signals
* Store water in non-reactive containers (glass or specific ceramics) - some plastics degrade in the city's energy field
* Avoid eating in areas where geometric fauna congregate - they appear interested in unfamiliar organic processes

## Psychological Preparation

**Mental Frameworks:**
* Study non-Euclidean geometry and optical illusions to prepare your mind for spatial inconsistencies
* Practice dissociating visual perception from physical orientation - your eyes will frequently contradict your vestibular system
* Develop comfort with liminal spaces and architectural transitions through meditation exercises
* Keep a detailed journal - writing helps anchor personal continuity when environment shifts subtly
* Establish a routine of reality-checking exercises to maintain psychological grounding

**Interaction Guidelines:**
* Observe the Secondaries from a respectful distance before attempting communication
* Mirror their deliberate pace and measured movements
* Communicate in simple, concrete concepts - abstract ideas translate poorly
* Respect their silences - pauses in conversation may last minutes and are considered part of the dialogue
* Never interrupt the movement patterns of the geometric fauna - their paths serve functions you cannot perceive

## Ethical Considerations

* The city itself may be sentient in ways difficult to comprehend - treat all surfaces with care
* Do not collect or remove any materials, especially the crystalline flora
* Avoid disrupting water flow in the channels - they serve as the city's circulatory system
* Minimize sound production - the silence is functional rather than aesthetic
* Document observations without intervention - you are witnessing processes beyond your understanding

## Departure Protocol

* Return to your entry point by retracing the water channels in reverse
* Ensure you carry nothing from the city - check thoroughly for water droplets or microscopic crystalline particles
* Undergo a period of sensory recalibration before returning to conventional reality
* Allow 3-7 days for readjustment to normal spatial and temporal perception
* Record all observations promptly - memories of the City of Apparatus tend to realign with conventional physics over time

Remember: The City of Apparatus doesn't accommodate human visitors; rather, humans temporarily align themselves with its reality. Your presence is neither welcomed nor rejected - you exist as a temporary perturbation in systems too vast to register your significance. This indifference is your greatest protection and your greatest challenge.
