# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Cog Garden

## Essential Preparations

### Physical Protection
- Wear thick leather gloves and sturdy boots with steel toes. The mechanical flora may have sharp edges that can easily slice unprotected skin.
- Bring layered clothing made of tough, tear-resistant fabric. The constant temperature means you won't need thermal protection, but protection from the mechanical elements is crucial.
- Consider wearing safety goggles with side shields to protect your eyes from any small flying metal fragments or crystal shards.
- A thin helmet or reinforced cap is advisable, as the space is confined and some mechanical plants may hang at head height.

### Auditory Considerations
- The mechanical symphony will be overwhelming. Bring high-quality earplugs or noise-canceling headphones that can reduce volume while still allowing you to hear.
- Consider specialized audio filters that can dampen mechanical frequencies while preserving your ability to communicate with travel companions.
- Your ears may still ring for days afterward. Accept this as part of the experience.

### Light and Navigation
- Bring a headlamp with adjustable brightness. While the bioluminescent mechanisms provide ambient light, you'll want directed illumination to examine details.
- A small mirror on an extendable pole will help you see around corners and into crevices without disturbing the delicate mechanical ecosystem.
- Bring a compass, though be prepared for it to malfunction—the massive amount of metal and possibly electromagnetic fields may render it useless.

### Tools and Equipment
- Pack a set of watchmaker's tools for emergencies. If you accidentally damage a component, basic repairs might be possible.
- Bring a notebook with metal pages and an etching tool. Paper will likely deteriorate in this environment, and you'll want to document what you see.
- A small magnet on a string can be useful for testing materials and potentially detecting hidden passages.
- Include several small vials of fine machine oil. If you find yourself stuck or need to traverse a seized mechanism, a drop of oil might create a path.

## Mental and Metaphysical Preparation

### Perceptual Adjustment
- Practice meditation techniques focusing on rhythmic sounds. This will help you acclimate to the overwhelming mechanical symphony.
- Before your visit, spend time in clock shops or mechanical museums, training yourself to find patterns in mechanical noise.
- Prepare for possible time perception distortion. The looping nature of The Cog Garden may cause disorientation about how long you've been inside.

### Interdimensional Awareness
- The obsidian walls create infinite reflections. Practice maintaining your sense of direction when surrounded by mirrors.
- The space between worlds can affect your sense of self. Carry a small personal object to ground your identity.
- Be prepared for the cognitive dissonance of a space larger inside than outside. Practice visualizing non-Euclidean geometry.

## Exploration Strategy

### Movement Techniques
- Move slowly and deliberately. Quick movements risk disturbing the precision of the mechanical ecosystem.
- Step only on areas that appear designed for weight-bearing. The metal flora may be more fragile than they appear.
- Develop a "light touch" approach—observe without manipulation whenever possible.
- If you must touch components, use the "three points of contact" rule: always maintain three stable points while moving the fourth limb.

### Observation Protocol
- Document the patterns of gear movement. They may contain encoded information or reveal the underlying logic of the garden.
- Pay attention to recurring sequences. The time loops may not be identical, and variations could be significant.
- Listen for changes in the mechanical symphony—rhythmic anomalies might indicate special areas or functions.

### The Thirteen-Locked Door
- Do not attempt to force the locks. The door likely serves a vital function in the garden's equilibrium.
- Document the lock mechanisms carefully. The keys may exist in other dimensions or realms.
- Consider the possibility that the locks are not meant to be opened sequentially but simultaneously.

## Ethical Considerations

- This ecosystem exists in perfect balance. Your presence is already an intervention—minimize your impact.
- Resist the urge to take "souvenirs." Even a small gear may be crucial to the garden's functioning.
- If you find yourself compulsively wanting to "fix" or "improve" mechanisms, recognize this as your human bias and abstain.
- Consider leaving small offerings of high-quality metal or oil near the entranceway as a gesture of respect.

## Departure Preparation

- Before leaving, check your clothing and equipment for any hitchhiking mechanical components that may have attached themselves.
- Document your exit path carefully. The reflective walls may make returning to your entry point challenging.
- Be prepared for temporal disorientation upon returning to linear time. Keep a clear record of when you entered.
- Allow several days of decompression afterward. The sensory experience of The Cog Garden can affect your perception of ordinary environments.

Remember that The Cog Garden neither needs nor acknowledges your presence. You are witnessing a system of perfect mechanical harmony that has functioned without observers for eons. Your role is that of the respectful witness, not the explorer or conqueror. Move through this space with reverence, and you may be permitted to leave with understanding.
