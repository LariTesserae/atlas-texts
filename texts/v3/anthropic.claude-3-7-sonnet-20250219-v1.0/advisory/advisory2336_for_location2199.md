# Preparation Guide: Visiting the Submerged Borough

## Essential Preparation

### Mental Readiness
Before anything else, prepare yourself mentally. The Submerged Borough exists in a liminal space - physically beneath the modern world but also suspended between past and present. You'll be entering a place of profound quiet and isolation where time moves differently. Claustrophobia, disorientation, and a sense of melancholy are common reactions. Meditation practice beforehand may help you adjust to the contemplative atmosphere.

### Physical Conditioning
* Train in swimming and treading water for extended periods
* Practice controlled breathing exercises (you'll need to navigate flooded passages)
* Build upper body strength for climbing and maneuvering through tight spaces
* Acclimate to cold water exposure gradually before your trip

## Equipment Essentials

### Clothing
* Full wetsuit (5mm neoprene recommended) - water temperatures remain consistently cold
* Neoprene hood, gloves, and boots
* Thin, flexible waterproof layers for drier sections
* Moisture-wicking undergarments
* Thermal socks and compression wear to maintain circulation

### Light Sources
* Primary waterproof headlamp with adjustable brightness (avoid harsh white light which damages the delicate ecosystem)
* Backup waterproof handheld light with red/amber filter options
* Several chemical light sticks (gentle illumination that won't disturb the environment)
* Waterproof matches in sealed container (emergency only)

### Navigation & Safety
* Waterproof compass (electronic devices may malfunction in the damp conditions)
* Chalk markers or biodegradable tape for marking your path
* 100ft of lightweight nylon rope
* Emergency whistle (sound carries distinctively through the chambers)
* Waterproof first aid kit with hypothermia treatments
* Small oxygen tank with regulator for emergencies
* Personal flotation device designed for cave exploration

### Specialized Equipment
* Dry bag for essential items (multiple smaller bags recommended over one large one)
* Small collapsible boat or inflatable raft for resting and equipment storage
* Extendable probe stick for testing water depth and stability of submerged structures
* Compact underwater camera with low-light capabilities
* Waterproof notebook and pencil for observations
* Small mesh bags for collecting water samples

## Navigation Tips

The Submerged Borough has no standard maps, and its layout shifts subtly with changing water levels. Learn to navigate by:

* Memorizing distinctive architectural features
* Noting patterns in the ceiling formations
* Tracking air flow directions
* Observing the behavior of resident creatures (bats often know the quickest routes to the surface)
* Using the faint sounds from the city above as orientation points

Create your own mapping system, but be prepared to adapt when passages that were open one day may be submerged the next.

## Ecological Awareness

The ecosystem of the Submerged Borough is extraordinarily fragile. Observe these practices:

* Move slowly to minimize water disturbance
* Never touch or collect the unique flora and fauna
* Avoid introducing foreign substances into the water
* Keep voice levels low to prevent disturbing bat colonies
* Do not attempt to feed or interact with the larger amphibious creatures
* Leave no trace - carry out everything you bring in

## Cultural Considerations

While the original inhabitants are long gone, treat the space with reverence:

* Don't remove artifacts, even those that appear mundane
* Avoid disturbing the structural integrity of buildings
* Consider leaving a small offering (traditionally a silver coin) at entry points
* If you encounter markings or symbols on walls, do not photograph them directly

## Timing Your Visit

* The optimal visiting periods are during moderate rainfall seasons when water levels are stable
* Avoid visiting after heavy storms when water levels rise unpredictably
* The "twilight period" (when city lights above are just coming on) provides the most distinctive illumination through the grates and fissures
* Plan for shorter initial visits (2-3 hours) to acclimate before attempting longer explorations

## Entry and Exit Protocols

There are several known access points to the Submerged Borough:

* The Forgotten Cistern (requires official permission and specialized equipment)
* The Washerman's Descent (accessible only during low water periods)
* The Mercantile Tunnel (most reliable but requires navigation through narrow passages)

Always:
* Register your entry plan with a trusted contact above ground
* Establish clear return times and emergency protocols
* Enter and exit with a minimum of two companions
* Carry multiple means of marking your exit path

## Post-Visit Care

After visiting the Submerged Borough:

* Thoroughly clean all equipment to prevent transferring the unique microbiology elsewhere
* Document your observations while memories are fresh
* Allow time for psychological decompression - many visitors report vivid dreams and a lingering sense of displacement
* Soak in a warm bath with epsom salts to draw out the mineral-rich water that may have absorbed into your skin

## Final Considerations

The Submerged Borough exists in a delicate balance between preservation and decay. Each visitor inevitably disrupts this balance in small ways. Consider whether your visit is necessary, and approach with humility. Many who have spent time there describe it not as exploration but as visitation - you are a guest in a place that continues its slow transformation whether humans witness it or not.
