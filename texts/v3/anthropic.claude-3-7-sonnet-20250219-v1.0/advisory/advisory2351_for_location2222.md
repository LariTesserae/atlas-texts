# Preparation Guide for the Crystalline Undercity of Harkosh

## Essential Preparation Overview

Visiting Harkosh requires meticulous preparation as it presents a unique combination of environmental hazards: confined spaces, crystalline structures that can lacerate, cold temperatures, thin air, potential toxins, and biological systems utterly foreign to human physiology. This guide will help you prepare for safe interaction with this crystallizing world.

## Environmental Conditions

### Temperature Management
- Pack insulated, flexible clothing that can withstand near-freezing temperatures without limiting mobility
- Bring battery-powered heating elements for gloves and boots, as traditional fire is inadvisable in the oxygen-poor environment
- Include multiple layers rather than one thick layer to adjust to temperature fluctuations
- A thermal emergency blanket could save your life if you become trapped or immobilized

### Respiratory Considerations
- The air is thin and likely contains mineral particulates and fungal spores
- Bring a filtration mask with replaceable HEPA filters and activated carbon
- Consider a small oxygen supplement system with enough capacity for your stay plus 72 emergency hours
- Practice controlled breathing techniques to maximize oxygen efficiency

### Illumination
- While the silicate veins provide minimal phosphorescent light, human eyes require additional illumination
- Bring multiple light sources: headlamp (keeps hands free), handheld light, and chemical light sticks
- Choose lights with red settings to minimize disruption to the bioluminescent ecosystem
- Solar chargers are useless underground; bring multiple battery backups

## Specialized Equipment

### Navigation Tools
- Traditional compasses may malfunction due to mineral deposits; bring an inertial navigation system
- Map-making supplies are essential as the environment constantly changes
- Biodegradable markers to create temporary trail indicators
- Long-lasting glow markers for emergency exit paths

### Crystalline Environment Protection
- Knee and elbow pads, reinforced with cut-resistant material
- Puncture-resistant gloves that maintain dexterity
- Safety goggles with side protection against crystal shards
- Lightweight helmet with face shield option

### Communication
- Sound-amplifying devices to overcome the sound-dampening properties
- Visual communication tools like light signals and flags
- Note that standard radio equipment may have severely limited range due to crystal interference

### Sampling and Research Tools
- Non-invasive testing equipment (spectral analyzers, microscopes)
- Sterilized collection containers for any samples (though collection should be minimal)
- pH testing strips for the silicate solutions
- Camera with macro lens capability and adjustable light settings for documentation

## Biological Considerations

### Food and Water
- Absolutely no local consumption of any kind
- Bring all required water plus 50% emergency reserve
- Consider nutrient-dense, low-waste food options
- Purification tablets as backup if emergency water collection becomes necessary

### Biological Protection
- Full-spectrum antimicrobial agents to clean any equipment that contacts the environment
- Sealed containers for waste management; everything must leave with you
- First aid kit supplemented with treatments for potential chemical exposure and puncture wounds
- Barrier creams to protect exposed skin from potential fungal contact

### Medical Preparations
- Pre-visit allergy testing recommended to determine sensitivity to fungal compounds
- Anti-inflammatory medications for potential respiratory irritation
- Antihistamines for possible allergic reactions
- Sterile wound closure materials (fungal contamination would be dangerous)

## Cultural and Ethical Guidelines

### Interaction Protocol
- Observe but do not disturb the symbiotic fungal-mineral colonies
- Move slowly to minimize disruption to crystallization processes
- Avoid touching or breaking any crystal formations; damage may be irreparable
- Document any changes to aid future visitors

### Ethical Considerations
- This is likely an extremely fragile ecosystem in development
- Follow "minimal trace" protocols – even stricter than "leave no trace"
- Do not attempt to collect specimens of the inhabitants
- Avoid introducing foreign biological material (skin cells, hair, etc.)

## Practical Travel Logistics

### Movement Techniques
- Practice confined space navigation before your visit
- Learn to assess structural stability of crystal formations
- Master slow, deliberate movement patterns to avoid breakage
- Bring compact tools for careful widening of passages if absolutely necessary

### Time Management
- The city operates on geological timescales; plan for gradual exploration
- Schedule regular rest periods to avoid fatigue-induced accidents
- Allow double the expected time for any planned activity
- Consider that what you see may change even during your visit

### Emergency Procedures
- Establish clear extraction protocols before entry
- Identify emergency shelter locations throughout your journey
- Create a response plan for tunnel collapse scenarios
- Develop signaling methods that work in sound-dampening environments

## Final Preparation Steps

1. Undergo physical conditioning focusing on flexibility and controlled movement
2. Acclimate to cold environments gradually before your journey
3. Practice using all equipment while wearing gloves and protective gear
4. Mentally prepare for an environment where human intuition about structural stability may not apply
5. Consider bringing a small offering of inert crystalline material from your world as a gesture of respect

Remember that Harkosh represents an unprecedented form of proto-civilization. Your visit may be the first human contact with this emerging crystal-fungal symbiotic society. The responsibility to preserve its integrity rests entirely with you.
