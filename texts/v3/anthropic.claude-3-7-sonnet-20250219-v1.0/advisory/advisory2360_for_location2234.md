# Preparation Guide for Multiverse Travelers: The Nascent Hollows

## Essential Overview
You're preparing to visit an actively forming underground cave system in its geological infancy. This is not a stable environment—it's a dynamic, dangerous realm where the very rock is being sculpted before your eyes. The darkness is near-absolute, the noise deafening, and the terrain treacherous and constantly changing.

## Physical Preparation
- **Fitness level:** High. You'll need to climb, crawl, wade, and possibly swim through unstable terrain.
- **Training:** Basic spelunking techniques, rock climbing, and swimming are essential. Practice moving on slippery surfaces.
- **Acclimation:** Spend time in dark environments before your visit to adjust your vision and comfort level with darkness.

## Essential Equipment

### Protection & Safety
- **Helmet:** High-quality with attached LED headlamp and backup light sources.
- **Full body coverage:** Waterproof coveralls or caving suit to protect against constant moisture and abrasions.
- **Knee and elbow pads:** For crawling on rough surfaces.
- **Gloves:** Waterproof with good grip.
- **Hearing protection:** Earplugs or noise-canceling earbuds to mitigate the constant geological cacophony.
- **Respiratory protection:** Light mask to help with the heavy moisture in the air.
- **Life line/safety rope:** Always remain tethered to prevent getting lost.
- **Emergency beacon:** Waterproof with geological signal penetration capabilities.
- **First aid kit:** Waterproofed, with emphasis on trauma supplies, splints, and wound closure.
- **Hard-shell case:** For delicate equipment protection against rock falls.

### Navigation & Illumination
- **Multiple light sources:** Primary headlamp plus at least three backups.
- **Extra batteries:** Waterproofed and accessible.
- **Mapping tools:** Waterproof paper, pencils, compass (though be aware magnetic disturbances may occur).
- **Marking system:** Biodegradable tape or chalk for marking paths.
- **3D mapping device:** If available in your timeline, to track constantly changing passages.

### Water & Nutrition
- **Water purification:** Despite abundant water, it may contain harmful minerals or microorganisms.
- **High-calorie rations:** Sealed against moisture, providing 3-4 days' worth minimum.
- **Electrolyte supplements:** The humid environment will deplete your body's minerals.

### Clothing
- **Moisture-wicking base layer**
- **Insulating mid-layer:** Despite humidity, temperatures remain cool.
- **Waterproof outer layer**
- **Multiple pairs of socks:** Moisture control is critical for foot health.
- **Caving boots:** With excellent grip for slippery surfaces.

### Tools
- **Multi-tool with geological attachments:** For testing rock stability.
- **Collapsible walking staff:** To test ground stability before stepping.
- **Pitons and climbing accessories:** For vertical passages.
- **Waterproof bags:** For all equipment and supplies.

## Psychological Preparation
- **Darkness adaptation:** Practice spending extended periods in complete darkness.
- **Noise conditioning:** The constant geological sounds can be psychologically taxing; prepare with audio simulations.
- **Claustrophobia mitigation:** If you have any tendency toward claustrophobia, this environment will severely challenge you.
- **Meditation techniques:** Learn methods to remain calm during inevitable moments of fear or disorientation.

## Cultural Considerations
The primitive human artifacts suggest early human or human-like presence. Approach with anthropological respect:
- Document but don't disturb arrangements of stones, figurines, or collection vessels.
- Learn to recognize basic ritual patterns so you can avoid inadvertently disrupting them.
- Bring small, biodegradable offerings (seeds, wooden tokens) if you wish to acknowledge these sites.

## Movement Techniques
- **Three-point contact rule:** Always maintain three points of contact with stable surfaces.
- **Weight testing:** Never put full weight on any surface without testing it first.
- **Regular reorientation:** The constant changes make it essential to frequently confirm your position and exit route.
- **Sound awareness:** Learn to distinguish between "normal" geological sounds and imminent danger sounds (sharp cracks preceding rock falls).

## Specific Hazards & Mitigations

### Flash Floods
- Monitor water levels constantly.
- Note escape routes to higher ground.
- Be aware that even small increases in water flow can signal major floods coming from deeper in the system.

### Rock Falls
- Avoid touching or leaning on vertical surfaces when possible.
- Listen for warning sounds—cracks, pops, or grinding noises.
- Move quickly but carefully away from areas where dust or small pebbles are falling from above.

### Disorientation
- Mark your path obsessively.
- Create a mental map and verbally rehearse your route periodically.
- If lost, remain in place and use your emergency beacon rather than wandering.

### Hypothermia
- Despite the seemingly mild temperatures, constant wetness can rapidly cool your body.
- Change into dry layers whenever possible.
- Carry chemical heat packs for emergencies.

## Final Notes
This environment exemplifies the phrase "nothing is permanent." The map you create today will be obsolete tomorrow. The passage you used to enter may collapse or flood. The humble fungi and primitive life forms you encounter represent the first colonizers of a world still being born.

Approach with reverence, caution, and the knowledge that you are witnessing geological processes that normally take place over timescales beyond human perception. You are a guest in the womb of the earth itself.
