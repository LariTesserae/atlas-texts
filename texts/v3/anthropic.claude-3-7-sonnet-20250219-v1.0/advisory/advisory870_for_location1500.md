# Preparation Guide for the Great Vegetal Beneath

## Essential Preparations

### Mental Preparation
Begin mental conditioning at least 3 months before your journey. The Great Vegetal Beneath operates on timescales vastly different from human perception. Practice meditation focused on slowing your perception of time. Train yourself to observe extremely gradual changes in your environment. Maintain a daily journal documenting subtle shifts in houseplants to develop this awareness.

Prepare for the psychological effects of constant bioluminescence and the absence of day/night cycles. Your circadian rhythm will be challenged, so practice sleeping under soft amber light for several weeks beforehand.

### Physical Conditioning
Develop flexibility and balance through yoga or similar practices. The undulating floor requires adaptable movement patterns. Strengthen your core muscles to maintain stability on the yielding surface.

Practice walking barefoot on uneven terrain to develop proprioception. The living floor's subtle movements will require constant unconscious adjustments to your gait.

Train your respiratory system for the unique atmosphere. While breathable, the air's complex organic compounds will feel different in your lungs. Practice breathing exercises that focus on slow, complete breaths to maximize oxygen intake.

## Essential Equipment

### Clothing
* Lightweight, breathable natural fibers only. Synthetic materials can irritate the sensitive plant matter.
* Multiple thin layers rather than single thick garments. The temperature is constant but your body's adaptation may vary.
* Soft-soled footwear with minimal tread patterns. Aggressive treads can damage the living floor and mark you as inconsiderate.
* No metal fastenings or accessories. Use wooden buttons, bone toggles, or plant-fiber ties.
* Include a wide-brimmed hat woven from natural fibers to shield your eyes from the constant ambient glow.

### Tools & Supplies
* A wooden or bone measuring stick with time-markers. Place this in a stable location to observe the almost imperceptible movement of the floor.
* Drawing implements made from charcoal or mineral pigments and paper from plant fibers. Electronic devices disrupt the subtle electromagnetic fields of the plant network.
* A hand-carved wooden whistle tuned to G minor. This frequency resonates with the chamber-dwellers and may facilitate communication.
* Water filtration system designed for extracting moisture from plant matter. There is no standing water, so you'll need to ethically harvest small amounts from the vascular system.
* Wooden or ceramic containers for collecting specimens. Never use glass or metal.
* Natural fiber rope, 30 meters minimum. Mark your path in this labyrinthine environment.
* Beeswax candles. Though rarely needed for light, the scent creates a psychologically comforting space within the constant plant emanations.

### Sustenance
* Bring predominantly dried fruits, nuts, and grains. The Vegetal Beneath responds negatively to animal products.
* Special tea blends containing adaptogenic herbs to help your body acclimate to the unique atmospheric compounds.
* Prepare for at least 20 days of supplies. Time dilation effects may make your stay feel longer or shorter than objective measurement.
* Simple food preparation tools made only from wood or fired clay.

## Etiquette & Cultural Protocols

### Interaction with the Environment
* Move slowly and deliberately. Quick movements disturb the subtle communication networks of the plant system.
* Request permission before touching any part of the environment. This isn't superstition but practical respect—the entire ecosystem is sensitive and responsive.
* Practice the "breath offering" before entering any chamber: three slow, complete exhalations directed toward the entrance. Your carbon dioxide is valuable to the system and this acknowledges your participation in its cycles.
* Never cut, break, or harvest any part of the environment without clear necessity. If you must take something, leave an offering of prepared compost or viable seeds from your home ecosystem.

### Communication with Inhabitants
* Learn the basic hand gestures of stillness, gratitude, and inquiry. These are universal among the chamber dwellers.
* Respect their reclusive nature. If inhabitants withdraw from your presence, do not pursue them.
* Bring small gifts of unworked materials from the surface—unpolished stones with interesting patterns are particularly valued.
* Observe the "speaking silence" protocol: before attempting communication, sit in complete silence for a duration of at least 100 heartbeats.

## Navigation & Safety

### Orientation
* Traditional compasses function unreliably. Instead, map your journey by the distinct patterns of fungi constellations in each chamber.
* Note that pathways may gradually shift during your stay. What was a passage yesterday might be sealed tomorrow.
* The root structures in the ceiling maintain more stable patterns than the floor. Learn to navigate by these overhead landmarks.

### Hazards & Precautions
* Some chamber-dweller colonies produce vibrations that can induce trance states in humans. Carry a wooden tuning fork (A minor) to strike if you feel your consciousness becoming too diffuse.
* Thread-lizards are harmless but startling. Their movements can cause disorienting light patterns. Practice closing your eyes and counting to ten if you encounter a swarm.
* Areas of extremely active growth have higher concentrations of spores. Wear a damp cloth over your mouth and nose when entering chambers with bright purple bioluminescence.
* If the floor begins pulsating rapidly beneath you, move immediately to a different area. This indicates accelerated growth cycles that could temporarily engulf you.

### Emergency Protocols
* If lost, locate the nearest chamber and wait. Movement patterns in the Vegetal Beneath are cyclical—pathways that disappear will eventually reform.
* For breathing difficulties, crush and inhale the spores of the pale green shelf fungi that grow at junctions between different colored floor sections.
* If the amber light begins to dim significantly, this indicates a major shift in the ecosystem. Retreat to a chamber with chamber-dwellers, as these areas remain most stable.

## Ethical Considerations

* Your presence impacts the Vegetal Beneath more than you can perceive. The carbon dioxide you exhale, the microscopic skin cells you shed—all become part of this closed system.
* Consider your visit a temporary merging with this organism rather than an exploration of it. Your consciousness is as much a part of this ecosystem during your stay as the thread-lizards or chamber-dwellers.
* Document your experiences through sketches and writing rather than specimen collection.
* Leave no permanent marks of your passage. The inhabitants measure time by the undisturbed growth patterns of their environment.

The Great Vegetal Beneath is not merely a location but a state of being—an invitation to experience existence at a different temporal and systemic scale. Those who approach with respect and preparation often report that they never truly leave; some essential part of their awareness remains connected to its gentle, eternal breathing.
