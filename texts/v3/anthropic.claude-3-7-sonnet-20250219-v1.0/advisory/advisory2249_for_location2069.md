# Preparation Guide: Visiting the Reservoir City

## Essential Equipment

### Breathing Apparatus
- **Primary System**: A closed-circuit rebreather with at least 8-10 hours of operational capacity
- **Backup System**: Traditional scuba tank with regulator
- **Emergency Option**: Several emergency breathing capsules that provide 30 minutes of air each
- **Maintenance Kit**: Tools for basic repairs and calibration of breathing equipment

### Thermal Protection
- **Full-body Wetsuit**: 3-5mm neoprene suit with sealed seams
- **Insulated Hood and Gloves**: The water is described as "cool but not cold," so moderate thermal protection is sufficient
- **Thermal Undergarments**: Thin synthetic layers for extended stays

### Mobility Equipment
- **Underwater Propulsion Vehicle (UPV)**: Small, battery-powered scooter for efficiently navigating the city
- **Neutral Buoyancy System**: Adjustable buoyancy control device with fine-tuning capabilities
- **Weighted Belt**: For stability during stationary observation
- **Fins**: Split-design for maneuverability in tight urban spaces

### Communication Tools
- **Underwater Communication Device**: Ultrasonic transmitter/receiver for short-range communication
- **Waterproof Tablet or Slate**: For taking notes and drawing maps
- **Light Signals**: Small flashlight with colored filters for basic signaling
- **City Interface Adapter**: If available, a device that can connect to the city's pneumatic message system

### Documentation Equipment
- **Underwater Camera**: Pressure-rated to reservoir depths with adequate lighting attachments
- **Sample Collection Kit**: Small sealed containers for water and mineral deposit samples
- **Measuring Tools**: Underwater tape measure, depth gauge, and current meter

## Physical Preparation

### Fitness Requirements
- **Swimming Proficiency**: Advanced swimming skills with the ability to maintain position in gentle currents
- **Breath Control**: Practice extended breathing techniques and air conservation
- **Pressure Equalization**: Train ears and sinuses to adapt to changing depths
- **Core Strength**: Develop strong core muscles for underwater stability

### Pre-Visit Training
- **Complete an Advanced Open Water Diving certification**
- **Specialized Training in Overhead Environment Diving**: The city's buildings create swim-through areas requiring specific skills
- **Current Navigation Training**: Practice moving efficiently with and against water flow
- **Extended Submersion Acclimation**: Gradually increase underwater duration in training sessions

## Knowledge Preparation

### Historical Research
- Study architectural styles spanning ancient to industrial periods
- Research underwater construction techniques and materials
- Learn about reservoir engineering and water management systems
- Review any available records of the city's origins and purpose

### Technical Understanding
- **Basic Mechanical Principles**: To comprehend the city's pump and valve systems
- **Pneumatic Systems**: To understand how the message and goods transport works
- **Optics and Light Refraction**: To appreciate the engineered sunlight delivery system
- **Hydrology**: To anticipate water current patterns throughout the city

### Sensory Adaptation
- **Practice Visual Adjustment**: The shifting light beams create dynamic visibility conditions
- **Audio Training**: Learn to distinguish mechanical sounds conducted through water
- **Proprioceptive Development**: Adapt to three-dimensional movement without ground reference
- **Practice Reading Water Currents**: Learn to identify flow patterns from visual and tactile cues

## Cultural Considerations

### Observation Protocols
- **Non-interference Approach**: The absence of fauna suggests a carefully maintained ecosystem
- **Minimal Contact**: Avoid touching the silvery aquatic plants or disturbing their growing frames
- **Respect for Mechanisms**: Do not tamper with any functioning machinery or valves
- **Limited Exploration**: Begin with main pathways before venturing into narrower passages

### Potential Inhabitant Interaction
- **Universal Greeting Gestures**: Slow, open-handed movements if inhabitants are encountered
- **Visual Communication**: Prepare simple diagrams to convey peaceful intentions
- **Gift Considerations**: Small items demonstrating your technological background (simple waterproof light)
- **Retreat Protocol**: Plan for immediate but calm withdrawal if your presence causes disturbance

## Navigation Planning

### Orientation Strategy
- **Use the Central Tower**: The copper-domed observation chamber provides a reference point
- **Light Beam Tracking**: Note the movement pattern of the focused sunbeams for time orientation
- **Current Mapping**: Document the predictable flow patterns for efficient travel
- **Vertical Position Awareness**: Maintain consciousness of depth at all times

### Exploration Routes
- **Begin Near Surface**: Start exploration from upper levels where emergency ascent is easier
- **Circuit Pattern**: Plan a spiral pattern moving from the periphery toward the center
- **Rest Points**: Identify potential locations for safely pausing to adjust equipment
- **Emergency Exits**: Map quickest routes to the surface from any exploration point

## Practical Logistics

### Time Management
- **Limited Immersion**: Plan for no more than 6 hours underwater per day initially
- **Decompression Considerations**: Allow adequate surface intervals between dives
- **Sunbeam Schedule**: Time visits to coincide with peak illumination periods
- **Rest Days**: Schedule non-diving days to prevent fatigue and nitrogen buildup

### Environmental Hazards
- **Current Changes**: Be alert for unexpected shifts in water movement
- **Pressure Variations**: Watch for signs of pump activity that might alter local pressure
- **Entanglement Risk**: Avoid pneumatic tube networks during active messaging periods
- **Disorientation**: The similar appearance of buildings can confuse navigation

### Emergency Procedures
- **Lost Protocol**: Return to last known landmark, then ascend slightly for better orientation
- **Equipment Failure**: Implement buddy breathing if necessary while retreating to surface
- **Current Entrapment**: Use building structures as shields if caught in unexpected flow
- **Communication Loss**: Retreat to pre-arranged meeting point if separated from companions

## Special Considerations

### Data Collection
- **Water Sampling**: Collect samples from different city zones to analyze composition
- **Light Pattern Documentation**: Record the movement and intensity of the directed sunbeams
- **Sound Mapping**: Create an acoustic profile of different city regions
- **Mineral Analysis**: Document the patterns and composition of deposits on surfaces

### Notable Features to Examine
- **The Floating Lenses**: Study their construction and light-focusing mechanisms
- **Pneumatic Tube System**: Observe the message and goods transportation network
- **Pumping Stations**: Document the operation of water circulation machinery
- **The Observation Chamber**: Seek to understand its purpose and potential inhabitants

### Ethical Guidelines
- **Preservation Priority**: Leave everything as you found it
- **Documentation Over Collection**: Prefer photographs to taking physical samples
- **Respect for Design**: Recognize that the city's arrangement is intentional, not accidental
- **Cultural Sensitivity**: Approach the site as a living cultural artifact rather than an abandoned ruin

This underwater marvel represents a rare convergence of deliberate design and environmental adaptation. Your visit should be approached with equal measures of caution, respect, and methodical curiosity. While the environment is not overtly hostile, its alienness requires thoughtful preparation and constant vigilance.
