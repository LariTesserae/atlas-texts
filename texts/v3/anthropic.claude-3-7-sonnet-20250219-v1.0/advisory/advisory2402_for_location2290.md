# Preparation Guide for Visiting the Eversong Mycelia

## Essential Preparations

### Protection Equipment
- **Full-body impermeable suit** with sealed seams and reinforced composites resistant to fungal penetration
- **Self-contained breathing apparatus** with minimum 24-hour air supply and triple-redundant filtration systems
- **Helmet with integrated HUD** displaying environmental readings, mycotoxin levels, and structural integrity of your suit
- **Anti-fungal coating** for all equipment and supplies; these must be regularly reapplied during your expedition

### Lighting
- **Multiple light sources** - primary headlamp, backup headlamp, handheld lights, and emergency chemical lights
- **Red-spectrum filters** for all lights to minimize disruption to the bioluminescent patterns (critical for observation)
- **UV light source** to detect invisible spore accumulations on your gear

### Navigation & Communication
- **Inertial mapping system** as conventional GPS won't function underground
- **Physical markers** (biodegradable) to mark your path through the network
- **Tethering system** with mechanical line trackers to prevent getting lost
- **Surface relay beacons** to maintain communication with your support team
- **Emergency transponder beacon** with at least 30 days of battery life

### Scientific Equipment
- **Multi-spectrum camera** to capture the bioluminescent signaling
- **Air sampling devices** for analysis of the chemical compounds
- **Vibration monitors** to record and possibly interpret the network's "language"
- **Remote sampling tools** that minimize direct contact with the mycelia

## Medical Considerations

### Pre-Expedition
- **Complete immunization** against known fungal infections
- **Lung capacity assessment** and respiratory fitness evaluation
- **Psychological screening** for claustrophobia and isolation tolerance
- **Establishment of biological baseline** for post-expedition comparison

### Medical Kit
- **Broad-spectrum anti-fungal medications** in injectable and oral forms
- **Emergency cauterization tools** if fungal penetration of the skin occurs
- **Respiratory support medication** in case of spore inhalation
- **Anti-inflammatory drugs** specifically formulated for mycotoxin reactions
- **Topical barriers** to apply if suit integrity is compromised

## Training Requirements

- **Minimum 100 hours in sensory deprivation** to adapt to the darkness
- **Chemical sensing training** to identify dangerous shifts in the mycelia's emissions
- **Slow movement techniques** to minimize disturbing the network
- **Emergency extraction procedures** including self-rescue methods
- **Recognition training** for identifying aggressive growth patterns in the mycelia

## Psychological Preparation

- **Meditation practice** to maintain focus during periods of sensory alteration
- **Sound deprivation adaptation** to function effectively in near-silence
- **Visual processing exercises** to make maximum use of limited bioluminescent light
- **Isolation resilience training** to withstand the absence of animal life
- **Decision-making drills** for high-stress, low-information scenarios

## Expedition Planning

### Timing
- Plan for **short excursions** (4-6 hours maximum) before returning to a secure base camp
- Schedule **dark adaptation periods** before each venture into the deeper network
- Allow for **decontamination time** (minimum 2 hours) after each expedition segment

### Movement
- **Slow, deliberate motions** are essential; rapid movements may trigger defensive responses
- **Step only on stone surfaces** whenever possible, avoiding direct contact with mycelia
- **Establish safe rooms** at regular intervals for suit integrity checks and air supply changes
- **Plan rest periods** in areas with minimal mycelial density

### Observation Protocols
- **Document bioluminescent patterns** methodically, noting any changes in response to your presence
- **Record sound at regular intervals** to capture the subtle acoustic properties
- **Sample air without disturbing growth** using passive collection methods
- **Observe before touching** - many structures may be more sensitive than they appear

## Ethical Considerations

- **Minimize disturbance** to this unique ecosystem
- **Take nothing but data, leave nothing but footprints** - and even those should be minimal
- **Consider the possibility of distributed intelligence** and treat the network with appropriate respect
- **Document rather than collect** whenever possible; samples should only be taken when absolutely necessary

## Emergency Procedures

### Contamination Response
- **Immediate isolation** if your protective equipment is compromised
- **Chemical neutralization** of any exposed areas followed by sealing
- **Emergency extraction** if signs of mycelial attachment to skin or equipment occur

### Structural Collapse
- **Carry inflatable supports** to create safe passage through unstable areas
- **Know collapse warning signs** - increased clicking sounds, sudden spore releases, or rapid mycelial withdrawal can indicate imminent structural changes

### Getting Lost
- **Implement buddy system** and regular communication checks
- **Use biodegradable trail markers** with phosphorescent properties
- **Carry emergency shelter components** for if you must wait for rescue

## Final Thoughts

The Eversong Mycelia isn't merely a location—it's potentially a single vast organism with distributed processing capabilities far beyond our understanding. Your visit should be approached with the humility of entering another being's body. The network appears to absorb and learn from what it encounters. You are not just an observer here; you are being observed, analyzed, and potentially incorporated into its processes.

Remember that nothing in your training can fully prepare you for the alien nature of this environment. Be adaptable, respect the system you're entering, and always prioritize caution over curiosity. The mycelia has existed without human intervention for millennia—your presence is the anomaly, not the network itself.
