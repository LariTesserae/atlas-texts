# Preparation Guide for the High Scalds

## Essential Physical Preparations

### Footwear & Movement
- Bring specialized climbing boots with graphene-enhanced soles designed for extreme grip on wet glass surfaces
- Pack crampons with obsidian-safe spikes for particularly treacherous areas
- Carry telescoping hiking poles with rounded, non-damaging tips to test surfaces before stepping
- Practice balance exercises extensively before visiting—the combination of slick surfaces and subtle gravitational anomalies demands exceptional equilibrium

### Clothing
- Dress in breathable, moisture-wicking synthetic layers that won't retain the constant wetness
- Your outer layer should be a hydrophobic membrane suit treated with extremophile-resistant coating
- Include a lightweight helmet with attached face shield to protect against unexpected geyser eruptions
- Wear thin, waterproof gloves with enhanced grip patterns—you'll need tactile sensitivity while maintaining protection

### Protection & Safety
- Apply specialized ear protection that filters dangerous frequencies while allowing normal hearing
- Bring goggles with polarized lenses to reduce eyestrain from the shadowless light
- Use a respirator with mineral-filtering capabilities to prevent lung irritation from the metallic air
- Apply dental guards to prevent tooth damage from the numbing mineral content in the atmosphere
- Pack chemical heat/cold packs for medical emergencies, as conventional temperature regulation may behave unpredictably

## Navigation & Orientation

- Traditional compasses and GPS will malfunction; bring a non-magnetic stellar navigation system
- Mark your path with temporary biodegradable holographic markers that can be seen from multiple angles
- Maintain a physical logbook with sketches of landmarks—digital records may corrupt in the reality fluctuations
- Create a "buddy system" of three people minimum, remaining within visual contact at all times
- Establish regular reality-checks: each member verbally confirms identical environmental observations

## Psychological Preparation

- Train in sensory deprivation chambers to acclimate to the absence of normal light/shadow relationships
- Practice meditation techniques specifically focused on acoustic disorientation
- Keep a vocal journal—speaking your observations aloud helps anchor your perception against the reality shifts
- Establish a strict schedule of rest periods—the constant light and lack of natural day/night cycles will disrupt circadian rhythms
- Undergo pre-trip counseling to build resilience against the cognitive dissonance of shifting landscapes

## Scientific Equipment

- Bring mechanical, not electronic, timekeeping devices
- Pack simple analog measurement tools with no electronic components
- Include manual recording equipment—paper notebooks treated with hydrophobic coating and graphite pencils
- Carry glass vials with mechanical seals for specimen collection
- Bring a simple mechanical camera with specialized film that can capture the unique light properties

## Water & Sustenance

- Do NOT consume any water from the landscape despite its abundance—it contains consciousness-altering minerals
- Bring water purifiers that specifically filter metallic and exotic compounds
- Pack calorie-dense, mentally-stabilizing foods—the cognitive dissonance of the environment burns extraordinary mental energy
- Include electrolyte supplements specifically designed to counteract the mineral absorption your body will experience through skin contact with the atmosphere

## Interaction Guidelines

- Never merge any part of your body with the translucent organisms—they appear to have mutagenic properties
- Avoid making loud, sustained sounds that might trigger unpredictable acoustic feedback loops
- Maintain physical contact with at least one personal item from your home reality at all times as an anchor
- If you experience reality shifts, focus on the tactile sensation of a familiar object until perception stabilizes
- When collecting samples, use only non-metallic tools to avoid triggering unexpected reactions

## Emergency Protocols

- If separated from your group, remain stationary and create a rhythm-based sound pattern—moving will make you harder to find as the landscape shifts
- In case of acoustic disorientation, use bone conduction earplugs to ground yourself in your own voice
- If you encounter sudden reality instability, close one eye and focus on a single point until the distortion passes
- Establish predetermined meeting points using geological features that appear most stable and consistent
- Carry signal mirrors that work in the diffuse light—they're more reliable than electronic signals

## Departure Preparation

- Plan for a decompression period after visiting—many travelers report persistent auditory hallucinations for weeks following exposure
- Schedule post-visit medical screening for mineral accumulation in tissues
- Quarantine all items brought back for at least 30 days to ensure they stabilize to home reality physics
- Document your experience immediately upon return while the memory remains unfiltered by normal reality constraints

Remember that the High Scalds exist in a state of ontological flux. No preparation can be complete, and adaptation will be your greatest asset. The place itself seems to respond to consciousness—approach with humble curiosity rather than the expectation of mastery, and you may find the landscape itself becomes more navigable.
