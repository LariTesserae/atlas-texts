# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Seethe-Below

## Essential Warning

Before considering a journey to The Seethe-Below, understand that this location represents an extreme hazard to your psychological and potentially physical integrity. This is not a tourist destination but a liminal space where reality operates by different rules. Many visitors never return, not due to physical barriers but because they become psychologically integrated with the environment. Proceed only with the utmost caution.

## Physical Preparations

### Gear Requirements:

1. **Light sources** - Bring multiple independent light sources that don't rely on the same power mechanism:
   - Chemical light sticks (at least 20)
   - Hand-cranked LED flashlight
   - Standard battery-powered headlamp
   - Avoid UV or high-intensity lights which may disrupt the ecosystem

2. **Protective clothing**:
   - Full-body coverage with mycological hazard suit if available
   - At minimum, wear tightly woven natural fabrics that cover all skin
   - Sealed boots and nitrile gloves to prevent direct contact with fungi or mycelium
   - Face mask with HEPA filtration to avoid inhaling spores
   - Goggles that seal completely around the eyes

3. **Tether system**:
   - Physical rope anchored outside the grotto entrance
   - Mark the rope with directional indicators every meter
   - Attach the rope to a full-body harness, not just held in hand

4. **Temporal anchoring**:
   - Analog watch with second hand
   - Mechanical metronome to maintain regular time perception
   - Journal and pencil to record observations and maintain your narrative

5. **Mental safeguards**:
   - Audio recorder playing a continuous loop of your name and personal details
   - Small object of personal significance to ground your identity
   - Photographs of loved ones sealed in waterproof container

## Cognitive Preparations

1. **Practice grounding exercises** daily for at least two weeks before your visit:
   - Sensory awareness techniques
   - Reality-testing methods
   - Meditation focused on self-identity
   
2. **Establish memory anchors** - Create specific memory triggers that remind you of your identity outside The Seethe-Below

3. **Study dissociative disorders** to recognize early symptoms in yourself

4. **Preset visitation parameters**:
   - Maximum stay duration (recommend under 30 minutes for first visit)
   - Specific exit triggers that will compel you to leave immediately
   - Questions to periodically ask yourself to test cognitive coherence

## Procedural Guidelines

### Upon Approach:
- Leave detailed plans with a trusted contact
- Establish check-in times and emergency protocols
- Set multiple alarms on different devices to signal departure time
- Have a companion remain outside the grotto, monitoring your rope

### During Exploration:
- Maintain constant verbal narration of observations
- Test reality frequently by attempting simple mathematics
- Do not make direct eye contact with the central pool
- Avoid touching any biological structures
- If droplets land on you, immediately wipe them away
- Keep moving to prevent "settling" into the environment
- Touch your tether regularly to maintain connection to the outside

### Warning Signs Requiring Immediate Exit:
- Losing track of time
- Feeling that your thoughts are visible or external to you
- Sensation of merging with surroundings
- Hearing "voices" in the silence
- Seeing your reflection act independently
- Finding unusual growth on your skin or clothing
- Feeling compelled to remain or "join" the ecosystem

### After Exiting:
- Immediately document your experience
- Have someone verify your identity and cognitive function
- Quarantine all equipment used inside
- Monitor for unusual thoughts, dreams, or physical symptoms
- Dispose of clothing if possible, or at minimum wash thoroughly

## Special Considerations

### Temporal Anomalies:
Time perception may be severely distorted. What feels like minutes may be hours or vice versa. Set multiple time-keeping methods and alarms.

### Identity Dissolution:
The boundary between self and environment is particularly fragile here. Regularly affirm your name, purpose, and intention to return home.

### Information Hazards:
The patterns within fungi and the "language" of the silence may contain information hazards that restructure thought patterns. Avoid trying to "decode" or understand these patterns.

### Psychological Aftermath:
Many visitors experience lingering effects:
- Dreams of returning
- Perception of subtle patterns in everyday objects
- A sense of "incompleteness" or being "called back"

Arrange for post-visit psychological support regardless of how unaffected you believe yourself to be.

## Final Caution

The most dangerous aspect of The Seethe-Below is not its physical hazards but its allure. Many who enter find it increasingly difficult to remember why they should leave. The ecosystem there is ancient and patient, and has been incorporating visitors for thousands of years. Your greatest protection is a firm commitment to your separate identity and temporary presence before you enter.

Consider carefully if this journey is necessary. The knowledge gained may come at the cost of who you are.
