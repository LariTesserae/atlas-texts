# Preparation Guide for The Aqualevation

## Essential Preparations

### Physical Conditioning
* Undergo pressure acclimation therapy for at least 3 weeks prior to departure
* Complete a hyperbaric chamber training course (minimum 40 hours)
* Learn slow-breathing techniques to manage the psychological effects of enclosed spaces
* Build cardiovascular endurance - the verticality of the settlement requires comfortable navigation of stairs and ladders

### Documentation and Clearances
* Obtain deep-sea research clearance (Form DS-7892)
* Complete psychological evaluation for extended isolation environments
* Secure temporary resident permit from the Aqualevation Administrative Council
* Medical certification indicating fitness for high-pressure environments
* Sign liability waivers for experimental biological exposure

### Communication Training
* Learn basic light-signal semaphore used within the settlement
* Memorize essential hand gestures for emergency protocols
* Download the latest Aqualevation interface compatibility package to your neural assistant
* Practice subvocalization techniques for the sound-sensitive environment

## What to Pack

### Clothing
* Lightweight, moisture-wicking garments (cotton will never dry in the humidity)
* Shoes with non-slip soles for condensation-slick surfaces
* Compact, easy-drying towels (minimum 3)
* No dark clothing - it becomes nearly invisible in the dim, misty environment
* Luminescent threading accessories for personal visibility

### Personal Equipment
* Anti-fog eyewear treatment
* Digital pressure gauge (personal monitoring is mandatory)
* Waterproof cases for all electronics
* Bio-luminescent location marker (in case of dome section power failure)
* Personal air quality monitor
* Moisture-resistant note-taking equipment
* Red-spectrum headlamp (to avoid disrupting creatures' light sensitivity)
* Multiple backup power cells (charging opportunities are strictly rationed)

### Medical Supplies
* Decompression sickness medication
* Inner ear pressure stabilizers
* Anti-fungal treatments (humidity promotes growth)
* Electrolyte supplements (perspiration is constant in the environment)
* Sleep aids compatible with pressure fluctuation
* Anti-anxiety medication (optional but recommended for first-time visitors)

## Cultural Considerations

### Etiquette
* Observe "light silence" during research hours (minimize use of personal illumination)
* Never tap or knock on observation glass - vibrations disturb both creatures and sensitive equipment
* Speak in hushed tones at all times; shouting is considered both dangerous and deeply offensive
* Respect the "wet paths/dry paths" system - never drip on designated dry areas
* Always yield to researchers in bioluminescent lab coats
* The Core Column is approached only after formal invitation

### Social Structure
* Understand the hierarchy signified by living quarter elevation (higher = higher status)
* Temporary visitors are expected to participate in community maintenance rotations
* Mealtimes are communal and mandatory - private eating is considered antisocial
* The "midnight gathering" around the Core Column is ceremonial rather than scientific - participation is expected but observation protocols apply

## Safety Protocols

### Emergency Procedures
* Memorize the location of pressure suits stationed at 50-meter intervals
* Learn the five-tone emergency signal that indicates dome compromise
* Know your assigned emergency pod location (based on your visitor designation)
* Practice underwater breathing techniques even if you don't expect to need them
* In case of power loss, follow the bioluminescent path markers to designated safe zones

### Health Warnings
* Report any persistent ear pain immediately - it may indicate pressure regulation issues
* Watch for signs of "depth rapture" - a psychological state where inhabitants become obsessively fixated on deep-sea creatures
* Monitor sleep patterns - the constant twilight disrupts circadian rhythms
* Limit exposure to the Core Column's amber light to 30 minutes daily
* Be aware of the early symptoms of hydrostatic psychosis (time perception distortion, fascination with water pressure, whispering to the creatures)

## Psychological Preparation

* Accept that communication with the surface will be limited and delayed
* Prepare for the disorientation of perpetual twilight - there is no day/night cycle
* Understand that time perception alterations are normal and expected
* Bring meaningful personal items to maintain connection with the surface world
* Consider keeping a journal to anchor your perceptions
* Be prepared for dreams of drowning or breathing water - they affect 87% of visitors

## Departure Preparation

* Schedule a minimum three-day decompression and readjustment period after your visit
* Understand that surface light may be painful for 1-2 weeks after extended stays
* Arrange for psychological debriefing to process the isolation experience
* Preserve any samples or observations according to strict containment protocols
* Be prepared for a sense of "pressure phantom" - the psychological feeling that water pressure remains even after returning to the surface

Remember: The Aqualevation exists in delicate balance with forces that could destroy it instantly. Your presence there is both a privilege and a risk. Prepare accordingly, move thoughtfully, and resist the temptation to stay longer than your designated time. The deep has its own gravity - psychological as well as physical - and not everyone who surrenders to it returns unchanged.
