# Visitor's Guide to the Anhydrobe Enclosure

## Essential Preparation

### Physical Conditioning
Begin hydration training at least 3 months before your visit. Your body needs to adapt to functioning with less ambient moisture than anywhere on Earth. Start by gradually reducing your daily water consumption while maintaining health markers. Practice breathing exercises designed for moisture conservation. Visit dry saunas regularly to acclimate your skin and respiratory system.

### Medical Screening
Undergo a thorough medical examination focused on:
- Kidney function
- Respiratory capacity
- Skin condition assessment
- Natural moisture production levels

Individuals with eczema, chronic dry eye, respiratory conditions, or kidney disorders may be advised against visiting.

### Required Implants/Modifications
Temporary subcutaneous hydration implants are mandatory for all visitors. These slow-release moisture reservoirs will supplement your body's needs during your stay. The outpatient procedure is performed 2-3 weeks before travel to allow for healing.

## Essential Equipment

### Moisture Retention Suit (MRS)
You'll be issued a personal MRS upon arrival, but be fitted for one beforehand. The suit:
- Collects and recycles 98% of bodily moisture
- Regulates body temperature
- Monitors hydration levels
- Includes emergency moisture reserves
- Features joint seals that must be properly maintained

Practice wearing a training version for at least 48 consecutive hours before your journey to adapt to the sensation.

### Hydration Supplements
Bring specialized solid-state hydration compounds. These mineral blocks release moisture when metabolized and are compatible with the Anhydrobe biochemical systems. Standard liquid water is STRICTLY prohibited as it disrupts the carefully maintained anhydrous environment.

### Eye Protection
Special non-evaporative lubricant eye drops and sealed goggles are essential. The intense artificial lighting combined with extremely low humidity will damage unprotected eyes within hours.

### Communication Device
Standard electronics may malfunction due to static buildup in the dry environment. Acquire an Anhydrobe-compatible communication device with anti-static coating.

## Cultural Preparation

### Resource Consciousness
Understand that in Anhydrobe culture, moisture is currency. Study their resource exchange systems and moisture-conservation etiquette. Actions considered harmless elsewhere—like excessive talking (which releases moisture through breath) or unnecessary movement (causing perspiration)—can be seen as wasteful or even offensive.

### Language Modifications
Learn basic Anhydrobe terminology. Their vocabulary has evolved to include dozens of words describing states of dryness, mineral compositions, and moisture efficiency that have no Earth equivalents.

### Movement Protocols
Practice the efficient movement patterns used within the enclosure. Space is at a premium, and the workers have developed specific ways of navigating the tight corridors that minimize energy expenditure and potential collisions.

## Psychological Preparation

### Sensory Adaptation
The constant machinery noise, intense lighting, and unusual smells can be overwhelming. Practice meditation techniques for sensory filtering.

### Claustrophobia Management
The dense, warren-like environment challenges even those without claustrophobic tendencies. Virtual reality simulations of the enclosure are available for acclimatization.

### Circadian Adjustment
Prepare for constant bright light conditions. The enclosure does not maintain Earth-standard day/night cycles. Use light-blocking sleep masks for two weeks prior to departure, worn during increasingly random intervals to adapt to the enclosure's work-rest cycles.

## Safety Precautions

### Emergency Procedures
Memorize emergency protocols for:
- Moisture suit failures
- Dehydration response
- Dome breach scenarios
- Mineral dust inhalation

### Contamination Prevention
You must be absolutely free of loose water when entering. This includes:
- No liquid personal care products
- Special solid-state alternatives for medicine
- Thorough dehydration chambers before entering the main enclosure

### Return Adaptation
Schedule post-visit rehabilitation. Reintroducing your body to normal humidity levels must be done gradually. Many visitors experience "reverse dehydration sickness" upon returning to water-rich environments.

## Cultural Etiquette

### Gift-Giving Protocol
If bringing gifts for hosts, only offer approved anhydrous items. Traditional Earth gifts like food or plants are inappropriate unless specially engineered for the environment.

### Work Observation
Request permission before observing any industrial processes. Many extraction techniques are proprietary, and workers may be protective of their specialized knowledge.

### Resource Contribution
Visitors are expected to contribute to the enclosure's resource cycle. This often involves donating collected bodily moisture above subsistence levels to community reservoirs.

Remember that the Anhydrobe Enclosure represents one of humanity's most extreme adaptations. Visitors are rare, and your respect for their unique lifestyle and environment will be crucial for a successful visit. The experience will fundamentally change your relationship with moisture and human adaptability.
