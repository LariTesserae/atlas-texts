# Preparation Guide for the Gathering Pool

## Essential Preparations

### Clothing and Footwear
- Wear waterproof boots that rise at least to mid-calf; the ground everywhere is saturated
- Choose clothing made from natural, muted fibers (cotton, wool, linen) in subdued colors that mimic the environment (greens, grays, browns)
- Dress in lightweight layers to manage the consistently cool, damp microclimate
- Avoid synthetic materials which may emit odors offensive to the amphibious inhabitants
- Bring a wide-brimmed hat that won't cast deep shadows on your face

### Observational Equipment
- A small notebook made of water-resistant paper with graphite pencils (no ink that could run or spill)
- A timepiece for tracking the rhythmic gatherings of the creatures
- A small hand mirror for viewing underneath rock ledges without disturbing habitats
- Small binoculars or a magnifying glass for detailed observation from a distance
- No photography equipment—electronic devices seem to disturb the subtle electrical fields some creatures emit

## Approach Protocol

The Gathering Pool must be approached with deliberate slowness. Begin your approach at dawn when activity is minimal. Walk with measured, consistent steps that create predictable vibrations. The creatures respond negatively to erratic movements but seem to accommodate steady rhythms integrated into their environment.

Stay behind the low stone walls for at least one hour before entering the depression. This time allows the inhabitants to recognize your presence and incorporate you into their awareness. When you do enter, follow existing pathways between the basins, never stepping over the carved channels that connect them.

## Behavioral Guidelines

### Do:
- Move slowly with deliberate, fluid motions
- Maintain a low center of gravity; crouch rather than stand fully upright
- Breathe deeply and audibly—your respiration becomes part of the acoustic environment
- If invited by the creatures' behavior, gently trail fingertips in peripheral pools (never the central pool)
- Observe the pattern of creature movements before navigating the space

### Don't:
- Make sudden movements or loud sounds
- Attempt to capture, touch, or relocate any creatures
- Block established pathways between basins
- Introduce any foreign substances to the water
- Rearrange stones or alter the constructed elements in any way
- Stay after dusk, when the more sensitive nocturnal species emerge

## Health and Safety Considerations

The mineral-sweet scent contains mild psychoactive compounds that gradually enhance pattern recognition. This effect is temporary and harmless if exposure is limited to 4-6 hours.

Some amphibians may secrete compounds through their skin that cause mild hallucinations if touched directly. These are not typically dangerous but can disorient visitors unfamiliar with the effects. Bring a small vial of vinegar to neutralize any accidental skin contact.

The water itself contains microorganisms unknown elsewhere. While not inherently harmful, they can cause digestive distress if ingested. Carry your own drinking water and avoid eating while at the Pool.

## Cultural Context

The Gathering Pool exists in a liminal space between human and non-human intentionality. The original human builders likely created the basic structure, but generations of amphibious inhabitants have adapted it to their own purposes.

When you encounter the crab-like creatures altering the channels, sit quietly and observe without interference. Their modifications are part of an ongoing adaptive process that has continued for centuries.

The clay figures in the niches represent a rudimentary collaborative language between humans and the Pool's inhabitants. You may notice tiny impressions in the clay that could only have been made by the creatures themselves.

## Offerings and Respect

If you wish to leave something behind, bring only objects that will fully dissolve in water within a day: small amounts of clay, unfired and uncolored; crushed eggshells; or a few drops of spring water from another location. Place these in one of the outermost basins at the moment when the chorus of sounds reaches its highest intensity—you'll recognize this moment when it comes.

## Departure

Leave as deliberately as you arrived. Move gradually toward the periphery over the course of an hour. Before departing completely, turn once to observe the Pool in its entirety, committing the pattern to memory. Many visitors report that the exact configuration of basins and channels appears different in recollection than it did in person—this is normal and part of the Pool's nature.

Your footprints will disappear within hours of your departure, absorbed by the constantly shifting moisture of the ground. This is how the Pool maintains itself—by integrating, adapting, and continuing its slow, purposeful evolution.
