# Preparation Guide for The Draining

## Essential Equipment

- **Waterproof footwear**: Military-grade waterproof boots with excellent grip for navigating slippery metal surfaces. Pack multiple pairs as they will never fully dry.
- **Moisture-resistant clothing**: Multiple layers of synthetic quick-dry fabrics. Avoid cotton at all costs as it will never dry in this environment. Gore-Tex or equivalent outer layers are essential.
- **Heat-retention gear**: Despite not being brutally cold, the constant chill combined with omnipresent moisture will drain your body heat quickly. Bring thermal undergarments and a lightweight insulated jacket.
- **Head protection**: A durable helmet with attached headlamp. The uneven bulkheads and hanging mechanical appendages make head injuries common.
- **Rope and climbing gear**: To navigate flooded sections and potentially scale vertical shafts.
- **Water purification**: While water is everywhere, it may contain industrial contaminants or biological hazards from the pale fungi and adapted plant life.

## Specialized Tools

- **Pressure gauge**: To detect imminent flooding events by monitoring air pressure changes.
- **Sound analyzer**: To "read" the different mechanical rhythms of the pump stations and detect dangerous deviations.
- **Metal detector/floor density scanner**: To identify thin flooring that might collapse under weight.
- **Communication equipment**: Radio signals struggle in this environment; bring acoustic communicators that utilize the metal structure to carry sound over distances.
- **Mapping tools**: GPS is useless here. Bring traditional compasses, inclinometers, and devices to mark your path.
- **Chemical test strips**: To identify dangerous chemical buildups from centuries of industrial processes.

## Survival Skills

- **Learn to "read" the machine complex**: Before visiting, study records of the rhythmic patterns of the pump stations. Local guides say each major section has its own "heartbeat."
- **Develop situational awareness**: Train yourself to constantly scan ceilings for leaking pipes and listen for changes in the mechanical chorus.
- **Practice heat conservation techniques**: The constant moisture and chill can induce hypothermia even at seemingly moderate temperatures.
- **Study hydrology**: Understanding how water moves through the complex will help predict flooding.
- **Learn industrial archaeology**: Recognizing the original purpose of different mechanisms can help you predict their behavior.

## Health Precautions

- **Respiratory protection**: The constant moisture, metal particulates, and potential fungal spores necessitate filtration masks.
- **Skin protection**: The mineral-rich water may contain irritants; keep skin covered.
- **Anti-fungal medications**: The unique fungi may cause unusual infections.
- **Ear protection**: The constant mechanical noise can cause hearing damage over extended exposure, but must allow you to still detect subtle changes in the machinery sounds.
- **Immunizations**: Update tetanus and any available immunizations against waterborne pathogens.
- **UV light therapy**: Pack a small UV lamp to counter the effects of extended time without natural light.

## Mental Preparation

- **Sensory adaptation training**: The constant dripping sounds, mechanical hums, and perpetual twilight require mental adjustment.
- **Claustrophobia management**: Despite the vastness, the low ceilings and pervasive mist can trigger claustrophobic responses.
- **Temporal disorientation training**: The lack of natural light cycles will disrupt your circadian rhythm; practice maintaining time awareness without environmental cues.
- **Isolation protocols**: Communication with the surface may be impossible for extended periods.

## Cultural Considerations

- **Respect local knowledge**: The people who make their living in The Draining have developed specialized knowledge and customs. Their apparent superstitions often have practical foundations.
- **Learn the trade signals**: Residents have developed a language of taps and knocks that carry through the metal infrastructure.
- **Understand territorial boundaries**: Different groups claim different sections; crossing boundaries without permission can be dangerous.
- **Bring valuable trade items**: Materials from the surface are precious to permanent residents—especially items that resist the omnipresent moisture.

## Final Advice

The Draining is neither wholly artificial nor natural anymore. It has evolved into its own ecosystem over three centuries. Don't try to understand it solely through engineering or biology—it's become a hybrid reality with its own rules. Most importantly, never ignore the warnings of those who have adapted to life here; what seems like mysticism to you is often hard-earned survival knowledge translated through generations of isolation.
