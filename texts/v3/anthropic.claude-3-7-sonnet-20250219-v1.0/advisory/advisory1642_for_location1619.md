# Visitor's Guide to The Droning Catacombs

## Essential Preparation

Before venturing into the Droning Catacombs, understand that you're entering a self-contained ecosystem that exists in a delicate state of equilibrium. Your presence is an intrusion, so prepare to minimize your impact.

### Clothing & Personal Items:
- Wear minimal, lightweight, breathable clothing made of natural fibers. The persistent warmth and humidity make heavy clothing uncomfortable.
- Bring a change of clothes in waterproof packaging. You will be perpetually damp.
- Avoid cotton, which stays wet and promotes chafing. Opt for quick-drying synthetics or merino wool.
- Footwear should be soft-soled to minimize damage to the fungal floor mats. Something like aqua shoes or minimalist trail runners.
- No jewelry or accessories that might snag on the root systems.
- Waterproof bag for any electronics or items that must stay dry.
- Headlamp with a red light setting to minimize disruption to the ecosystem.

### Communication:
- The overwhelming drone makes verbal communication nearly impossible. Bring small whiteboards or notebooks with pencils.
- Alternatively, learn basic sign language with your traveling companions.
- Flashlights can be used for rudimentary signaling.

### Navigation:
- Standard compasses are unreliable due to the unusual properties of the root system.
- Bring biodegradable string or chalk to mark your path.
- Digital mapping equipment seems to malfunction in unpredictable ways; analog is more reliable.

### Duration Planning:
- Time perception becomes distorted in the Catacombs. Wear an analog watch.
- Plan shorter expeditions initially until you understand how your body responds to this environment.

## Health & Safety

### Respiratory Considerations:
- The golden spores released by the fungal floor are not immediately toxic but can cause respiratory sensitivity with prolonged exposure.
- Bring a lightweight mask or respirator for areas where spore density seems higher.
- If you have asthma or respiratory conditions, consult a specialist before visiting.

### Hydration & Nutrition:
- Despite the omnipresent moisture, drinking the water directly is not recommended without purification.
- Bring a portable water filter capable of filtering particulates and potential biological contaminants.
- Pack high-energy, compact food that requires no cooking. The environment isn't conducive to food preparation.
- Protein bars, dried fruits, and nuts are good options.

### Psychological Preparation:
- The constant drone can cause auditory fatigue and disorientation after 3-4 hours.
- Practice meditation techniques before your visit to help maintain focus.
- The visual sameness of chambers can trigger spatial disorientation. Be prepared for this.
- Bring earplugs or noise-canceling earbuds if you need occasional respite from the sound.

### First Aid:
- Waterproof first aid kit with emphasis on treating fungal infections, rashes, and minor injuries.
- Anti-fungal cream is essential, as any small cut can quickly develop fungal growth.
- Include antihistamines for potential allergic reactions to spores.

## Scientific Equipment (if applicable)

- pH testing strips for water and surface sampling
- Small specimen containers (take minimally and only if necessary)
- Non-invasive temperature and humidity monitors
- Audio recording equipment with noise filtering capabilities
- Macro photography equipment with non-disruptive lighting

## Ethical Guidelines

1. **Take nothing but images, leave nothing but footprints**: The ecosystem appears to exist in perfect equilibrium. Removing samples could disrupt this balance.

2. **Respect the structures**: The crude platforms and enclosures may have cultural significance to unknown entities. Do not disturb them.

3. **Sound discipline**: While communication is difficult, resist the urge to shout or make loud noises to overcome the drone. This may disrupt the insect populations.

4. **Step carefully**: The fungal floor mats are living organisms. Try to distribute your weight and avoid repetitive treading in the same areas.

5. **Limited light use**: The bioluminescent fungi provide adequate lighting for movement. Use your headlamp sparingly and at low settings.

## Special Considerations

### Time Distortion:
Visitors consistently report that time feels "stretched" in the Catacombs. What feels like an hour might be three, or vice versa. Set timers to remind yourself to check your watch and maintain awareness of how long you've been inside.

### Sensory Adaptation:
After approximately 6-8 hours in the Catacombs, some visitors report a strange adaptation where the drone becomes almost inaudible to them. This state, while peaceful, can be dangerous as it often coincides with a desire to remain. Establish a buddy system to monitor each other for signs of unusual attachment to the environment.

### Emergency Exit:
If you experience:
- Difficulty breathing
- Persistent disorientation
- Hearing loss
- Unusual fascination with or desire to merge with the walls
- Loss of time exceeding 3 hours
- Skin rashes or fungal growth on your body

Immediately begin making your way back to the entrance. The Catacombs have been known to "accept" visitors who stay too long, incorporating them into the living walls in ways not fully understood.

Remember that the Droning Catacombs exist in a state of perpetual birth and stasis simultaneously. You are witnessing something precious and paradoxical. Move through with respect, curiosity, and caution.
