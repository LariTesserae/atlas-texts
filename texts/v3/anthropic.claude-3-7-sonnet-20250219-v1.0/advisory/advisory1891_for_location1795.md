# Preparation Guide for Visiting Vaulthome

## Essential Preparations

### Mental and Sensory Adjustment
Before visiting Vaulthome, you must prepare for sensory disorientation. Your eyes will need time to adjust to perpetual twilight—begin reducing your exposure to bright light two weeks before travel. Practice functioning in dim conditions and train yourself to notice subtle variations in low light. Your hearing will be less useful than normal, while your sense of smell will become crucial. Practice identifying different scents with your eyes closed, as olfactory communication will be essential for interaction.

### Physical Conditioning
The humid environment can be taxing for those unaccustomed to it. Begin humidity training by spending time in steam rooms or saunas, gradually increasing duration. Your lungs need to adapt to the moisture-saturated air. Practice slow, measured breathing techniques to prevent hyperventilation in the dense atmosphere.

### Clothing and Personal Items
Pack only natural fiber clothing—absolutely no synthetic materials. The living architecture reacts negatively to artificial substances. Bring lightweight, loose-fitting garments made of cotton, linen, or silk that can absorb moisture without becoming uncomfortable. Multiple layers are recommended as temperature regulation can be challenging.

Avoid bringing any metal items, including jewelry, watches, or tools. The wall-vine will contract from metal, potentially causing structural disturbances or isolating you from hosts. Replace essential items with wooden, ceramic, or bone alternatives.

## Communication Tools

### Scent Vocabulary
Invest in learning basic scent communication. Purchase or create a set of natural essence oils (pine, moss, various fungi, flowers) to practice recognizing subtle differences. Local guides recommend at least recognizing 20 basic scent "phrases" before arrival.

### Gesture Dictionary
Obtain and memorize the fundamental gestures used in Vaulthome. Practice performing them with minimal movement—the gestures are subtle, not theatrical. A good visitor can communicate needs like hunger, thirst, rest, and gratitude through appropriate hand movements.

### Light Signals
Bring several small containers of bioluminescent moss (available from specialized botanical suppliers). These serve as personal light sources and can be used for emergency communication when gesture and scent are insufficient.

## Health Precautions

### Respiratory Support
The spore-dense atmosphere presents challenges to visitors with respiratory sensitivities. Consult a physician about natural anti-fungal nasal applications and bring lungwort tea sachets to help your breathing adjust. Non-medical cotton filters that can be discreetly placed in nostrils are recommended during sleep cycles.

### Skin Protection
The constant moisture can lead to fungal skin infections. Pack natural anti-fungal preparations containing tea tree oil, garlic extract, or neem. Apply these preventatively to skin folds and feet daily.

### Vision Support
Your eyes will strain in the dim conditions. Bring preservative-free lubricating eye drops made from natural ingredients. Some visitors report success with supplements containing bilberry extract to improve night vision when taken for several weeks before visiting.

## Practical Considerations

### Navigation Tools
Traditional compasses malfunction due to the magnetic properties of certain minerals in the chamber walls. Instead, bring string for marking paths and natural chalk made from crushed shells for temporary markings.

### Time-Keeping
Abandon solar-based time. The settlement operates on biological rhythms different from surface day/night cycles. Bring a simple hourglass or water clock made of glass or wood to maintain your own schedule if necessary.

### Food and Water
While visitors are typically offered fungal cuisine, those with sensitive digestion should bring simple preserved foods like dried fruits and nuts in wooden containers. Water is abundant but tastes strongly mineral. Bring natural flavor essences (mint, fruit extracts) if this might bother you.

## Cultural Etiquette

### Approach to Architecture
Never touch the living walls without explicit invitation. The wall-vine is sensitive to unfamiliar biochemistry. When permitted to touch structures, do so with an open palm, fingers slightly spread, and maintain gentle, consistent pressure.

### Interaction with Residents
Initial meetings should be conducted with downcast eyes and slow movements. Vaulthome residents are uncomfortable with direct gaze and quick gestures. Wait for them to establish communication terms before attempting conversation.

### Gift-Giving Protocol
Bringing gifts is appreciated but complicated. Appropriate offerings include unpolished wooden objects, interesting seeds (provided they've been checked for compatibility with the ecosystem), or naturally dyed textiles. Never bring preserved food, metal, or anything containing synthetic materials.

## Emergency Preparations

### Disorientation Response
The environment can cause spatial disorientation. If lost, find the nearest light-moss patch and remain there. The living architecture will detect your presence, and guides will locate you through the wall-vine's subtle communications.

### Allergic Reactions
Some visitors develop sensitivities to the unique fungal species. Bring natural antihistamines like nettle tea and quercetin supplements. In severe reactions, breathing through a damp cloth soaked in saline solution can provide temporary relief.

### Exit Strategy
Always maintain awareness of the main entrance path. The chamber's continuous growth can alter passages. Memorize the specific texture and luminosity patterns of the primary access tunnel, as visual landmarks may shift during your stay.

## Specialized Gear

### Documentation Tools
If you wish to record your experiences, bring wooden-framed charcoal sticks and paper made from cotton or hemp. Electronic devices fail quickly in the humidity, and the electromagnetic fields they produce distress the living architecture.

### Light Sources
Beyond the provided bioluminescent moss, bring naturally derived light sources like beeswax candles in glass holders. These are acceptable in private pods during rest periods but should never be used in common areas.

### Footwear
The floor surface varies between soft, spongy moss and occasionally slippery fungal patches. Bring lightweight shoes made of natural rubber or woven plant fibers with excellent grip. They should be washable, as they'll need daily cleaning to prevent transporting spores between different garden sections.

## Final Advice

Vaulthome requires visitors to surrender certain comforts and expectations. The settlement operates as part of a living system that has its own intelligence and rhythm. Those who approach with humility and adaptability will be welcomed into an extraordinary symbiotic community. Those who resist the necessary adjustments often find themselves subtly encouraged to leave through increasingly uncomfortable environmental responses.

Remember that you are entering not just a settlement but an organism. Your presence will be felt by the entirety of Vaulthome. Move through it with respect, breathe with its rhythm, and you'll experience one of the most profound examples of human adaptation to an extraordinary ecological niche.
