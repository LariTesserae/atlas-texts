# Preparation Guide for The Suspended Garden

## Essential Preparations

**Physical Conditioning:**
* Practice deep yogic breathing for at least 2 weeks prior to your visit. The Garden's rhythmic atmosphere will attempt to synchronize with your respiratory patterns.
* Train your balance on unstable surfaces—the responsive moss will subtly alter your proprioception.
* Acclimate to varying humidity levels; your skin will be engaged in constant moisture exchange.

**Mental Preparation:**
* Meditate daily, focusing particularly on experiencing time non-linearly. The Garden's temporal distortions can be disorienting for those who perceive time as strictly linear.
* Practice maintaining focus while peripheral phenomena change—use mandala meditation techniques.
* Familiarize yourself with the feeling of gentle mental probing. The Garden's awareness will attempt to establish connection.

## What to Bring

**Clothing:**
* Natural fibers only—synthetic materials create dissonance in the Garden's harmonic field.
* Loose, flowing garments that allow for air circulation.
* No shoes—direct skin contact with the moss is essential for establishing equilibrium.
* No metal jewelry—it can disrupt the bioelectric fields and cause plant distress.

**Equipment:**
* A wooden or clay vessel for collecting water samples (never metal or plastic).
* A natural reed flute or wooden whistle—musical responses enhance communication.
* A small quantity of honey or tree sap as an offering.
* A wooden sketchbook and graphite pencil for documentation—electronic devices may malfunction.

**Do Not Bring:**
* Digital timepieces—they will fail and potentially disrupt your temporal adjustment.
* Recording devices—they cannot capture the inter-dimensional aspects and may break your perceptual immersion.
* Cutting tools—any harm to the Garden's tissues will trigger defense mechanisms.

## Behavioral Protocols

**Upon Entry:**
* Stand still for seven full breath cycles, allowing the Garden to sense your biorhythms.
* Place both palms against the living wall briefly—this establishes you as visitor rather than threat.
* Speak your name clearly but softly—the Garden will begin learning your voice pattern.

**During Your Visit:**
* Move slowly and deliberately—rapid movements create disruptive energy patterns.
* When disoriented by water flow patterns, focus on your breathing rather than trying to visually track the impossible trajectories.
* If you feel the Garden's intelligence attempting contact (sensed as a warming pressure behind your eyes), remain calm and open your thoughts.
* Periodically hum a single tone—this helps stabilize your presence within the acoustic field.

**Communication:**
* The Garden understands intention more than language—focus on clear mental imagery.
* When you hear your voice translated into plant-language, repeat those sounds back—this establishes dialogue.
* If the amber light pulses synchronize with your heartbeat, you've been accepted as temporary symbiont.

## Health Considerations

**Physical Risks:**
* The spores are generally benign but may cause mild respiratory sensitivity—practice breath filtering techniques.
* The moss's moisture exchange can lead to slight dehydration—drink pure water before entering.
* Temporal dissonance may cause minor nausea—bring ginger root to chew if needed.

**Psychological Preparation:**
* Some visitors experience expanded consciousness that persists after leaving—arrange for 24 hours of quiet integration time afterward.
* The sensation of being observed is genuine—practice comfort with benign surveillance.
* The Garden may share knowledge through direct impression—this can overwhelm unprepared minds.

## Departure Protocol

* Signal your intent to leave by placing your forehead against the floor moss for several moments.
* Walk clockwise three times around the chamber before seeking the exit.
* Express gratitude through visualization of nurturing light emanating from your chest.
* Do not attempt to take samples unless explicitly invited through a pod detaching and following you.

## After Your Visit

* Document your experience immediately through drawing or writing—memories of inter-dimensional aspects fade quickly.
* Expect altered dream patterns for 3-7 nights as your mind processes the experience.
* You may notice enhanced sensitivity to plant life for several weeks—this is normal integration.
* If you find yourself unconsciously humming tones from the Garden, record them—they often contain encoded information.

Remember: The Suspended Garden exists in a state of perpetual invitation and judgment. It has welcomed countless visitors over centuries, but truly opens its deeper knowledge only to those who approach with reverence and perceptual flexibility. The Garden doesn't just observe you—it reads your timeline, past and future. Be authentic in your curiosity and respect.
