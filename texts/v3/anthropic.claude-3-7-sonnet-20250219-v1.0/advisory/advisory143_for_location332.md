# Preparation Guide for The Shivering Citadel

## Essential Preparations

### Clothing & Protection
- Layer extensively with moisture-wicking base layers, wool mid-layers, and waterproof outer shells
- Pack specialized climbing gear designed for icy conditions
- Bring transparent waterproof coverings modeled after locals' jellyfish membranes (these will mark you as respectful of local customs)
- Include waterproof boots with excellent grip, preferably with retractable ice spikes
- Carry thermal gloves with waterproof shells and removable finger sections for dexterity
- Bring insulated headwear that covers ears while allowing hearing
- Consider a waterproof face mask to protect against the constant moisture and cold

### Equipment
- Pack multiple sources of light that function in extreme cold
- Bring a specialized ice-measuring tool to test surface stability before walking
- Carry rope, carabiners, and lightweight grappling hooks for navigating the precarious bridges
- Include a collapsible walking stick with ice pick end for stability
- Bring waterproof bags for all equipment—everything will get wet
- Carry water purification tablets despite the abundance of water (the mineral content may be unsuitable for outsiders)
- Pack emergency thermal blankets and heat sources that function in high humidity

### Communication
- Study the locals' hand gesture language beforehand if possible
- Bring a small notebook of waterproof paper and pencil for drawing communications
- Practice making the bone-clicking sounds used in their communication system
- Consider bringing small, tasteful gifts that demonstrate understanding of their resource-limited environment

## Cultural Considerations

### Etiquette
- Move deliberately and carefully—haste is both dangerous and culturally inappropriate
- Observe the residents' careful water usage despite its abundance
- Ask permission through gestures before photographing or recording
- Be prepared to participate in construction or repair work if invited
- Never disrupt the shadow-watching ceremonies at the eastern ice wall
- Practice walking sideways through narrow passages to avoid body contact with the ice walls

### Timing
- Plan your visit during periods of relative stability in the glacier, typically mid-winter
- Be prepared to alter plans instantly when ice-cracking events occur
- Understand that time perception differs here—plan for longer stays than anticipated
- Learn to read the movement patterns of the glass crustaceans, as locals use them as natural timekeepers

## Survival Skills

### Navigation
- Memorize the concentric ring layout of the settlement
- Learn to identify landmark features in the seemingly uniform ice architecture
- Practice orienting yourself by the blue phosphorescence of the central pool
- Understand that pathways may change daily as ice melts and refreezes

### Health Precautions
- Acclimate gradually to the constant cold and humidity
- Protect against potential hypothermia from the misleading "wet cold" that feels warmer than it is
- Bring antibiotics effective against cave-dwelling bacteria
- Include eye protection against the intense refracted light
- Prepare for potential altitude sickness as the Citadel is likely high in the mountains
- Bring electrolyte supplements to replace minerals lost through constant sweating under layers

### Emergency Preparation
- Learn to recognize the warning sounds that precede major ice shifts
- Identify multiple evacuation routes from any point in the settlement
- Practice rapid shelter construction using local techniques
- Carry emergency rations that don't require heating

## Scientific Opportunities

- Bring waterproof notebooks for documenting the physics-defying ice formations
- Include small collection vials for samples of the bioluminescent water (with permission)
- Consider bringing a specialized camera for capturing the light phenomena at the eastern wall
- Pack tools for measuring the unique properties of the hovering ice crystals

## Mental Preparation

The Shivering Citadel challenges fundamental assumptions about stability and permanence. Prepare yourself for a psychological experience as disorienting as it is beautiful. The constant state of flux—structures that must be rebuilt daily, pathways that disappear overnight, the omnipresent sound of water that refuses to freeze despite the cold—creates an environment where adaptation is not just beneficial but necessary for survival.

The locals have developed not just physical but psychological adaptations to this instability. They find comfort in change rather than permanence. As a visitor, embrace this mindset. Let go of expectations for consistency or predictability. Learn to see beauty in impermanence and strength in flexibility.

Remember: in the Shivering Citadel, those who survive are not those who build the strongest walls, but those who most gracefully accept their inevitable collapse.
