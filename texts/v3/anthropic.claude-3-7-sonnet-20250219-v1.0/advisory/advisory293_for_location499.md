# Preparation Guide for Visiting Shallowmarsh Hive City

## Essential Gear

**Protective Clothing:**
- Full-body lightweight breathable mesh oversuit (must be insect-proof but heat-resistant)
- Treated boots rising at least mid-calf, preferably with metal reinforcement
- Mesh head covering or specialized hood with visibility visor
- Multiple pairs of thin moisture-wicking undergarments (expect to change frequently)
- Gloves with articulated joints, ideally with a thin mesh outer layer and sealed cuffs

**Respiratory Equipment:**
- Multi-filter respirator mask rated for industrial particulates and biological matter
- Replacement filters (3x more than you think you'll need)
- Emergency breather for high concentration areas near rendering facilities
- Antihistamine nasal spray and throat lozenges for inevitable irritation

**Vision & Navigation:**
- Anti-fog goggles with adjustable tint (the perpetual twilight can strain eyes)
- Compact but powerful flashlight with red-light option (less attractive to flying insects)
- Physical map of elevated walkways (electronic devices may malfunction in the humidity)
- Compass (the perpetual haze makes direction difficult)
- Local guide contact information (navigating alone is not recommended)

**Health & Safety:**
- Comprehensive first aid kit including:
  - Burn treatment supplies
  - Antihistamines and anti-inflammatory medications
  - Waterproof bandages and medical adhesive
  - Anti-venom kit (consult local hospital for appropriate type before arrival)
  - Antibacterial and antifungal creams
- Water purification tablets or portable filter
- Sealed container of clean water for emergencies (1 liter minimum)
- High-energy, sealed food rations
- Insect repellent (specially formulated for Shallowmarsh species - standard repellents are ineffective)

**Communications & Documentation:**
- Humidity-resistant notebook and pencils (ink runs in this environment)
- Sealed protective cases for any electronics
- Backup power banks in waterproof containers
- Local communication device (the city's infrastructure is incompatible with many outside technologies)

## Practical Preparations

**Physical Conditioning:**
- Build upper body strength for navigating the elevated walkways
- Practice balance exercises (walkways can be narrow and sometimes sway)
- Increase heat tolerance through sauna training if possible
- Build respiratory endurance (the thick air requires stronger breathing)

**Medical Considerations:**
- Complete a full medical check-up before travel
- Receive recommended vaccinations plus:
  - Advanced immunizations against water-borne parasites
  - Preventative antibiotics for specialized respiratory infections
  - Microbial resistance boosters
- Consider temporary respiratory enhancement treatments if available in your region
- If you have respiratory conditions, reconsider your visit or consult specialists in industrial environment exposure

**Cultural & Practical Knowledge:**
- Learn basic hand signals used by locals when verbal communication is difficult
- Memorize emergency shelter locations throughout your planned route
- Study the rhythm of factory operations to plan movement during lower production periods
- Learn to identify the sounds of the more dangerous native fauna
- Practice the local method of walking (a specific gait that minimizes vibrations on walkways)

## Upon Arrival

**Immediate Actions:**
- Allow 24-48 hours for environmental acclimation in a transition hostel
- Apply the local protective skin sealant (available at entry points)
- Register with the visitor's bureau for emergency tracking
- Secure a breathing adaptation session with a local health provider
- Purchase a vial of "caller's whistle" - a compound that disperses certain insect swarms

**Daily Routine Adjustments:**
- Rise and complete outdoor activities during the coolest period (typically 3-5 hours after peak darkness)
- Schedule indoor rest during midday heat peaks
- Drink filtered water continuously (aim for 1 liter more than your usual daily intake)
- Apply fresh protective sealant to exposed skin every 6 hours
- Perform equipment checks morning and evening
- Clean respirator filters daily or more frequently near industrial zones

## Important Cultural Considerations

- Never swat at insects in public - this is considered dangerous and disrespectful
- Learn the building entry protocols (a specific knocking pattern prevents certain insects from entering alongside you)
- Accept offered protective gear even if you have your own - refusing is considered insulting
- Respect local dietary customs - many protein sources may seem unusual but refusing offered food can be offensive
- Understand that locals have different personal space boundaries due to the confined architecture
- Never shine bright lights into the water below walkways - this can attract predatory species

## Emergency Protocols

- If caught in an insect swarm: remain still, cover eyes and mouth, use caller's whistle
- If walkway feels unstable: crouch low, distribute weight evenly, move to nearest junction point
- For chemical exposure: rinse affected area with clean water, apply neutralizing powder (carry in emergency kit)
- If you fall into the water: remain calm, float on your back, signal for help - do not swim aggressively
- In case of factory emissions surge: apply emergency breather, seek indoor shelter, seal doors and windows

Remember that Shallowmarsh residents have generations of adaptive experience. What seems hostile to you is home to them. Approach with respect, patience, and humility, and you'll discover the remarkable equilibrium they've established in one of humanity's most uniquely adapted settlements.
