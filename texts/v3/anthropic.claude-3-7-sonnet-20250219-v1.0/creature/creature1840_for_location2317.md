# The Custodians of the Foundry Below

The primary inhabitants of the Foundry Below call themselves the Mendics, though they have little need for names among themselves. They evolved from the maintenance crews who chose to stay when the original builders departed or perished millennia ago.

Standing nearly seven feet tall, Mendics possess elongated limbs and torsos that taper to narrow waists. Their skin has a translucent quality—not quite albino but bearing a pearlescent sheen that subtly refracts the omnipresent blue-white light. Beneath this skin, a network of bioluminescent filaments pulses in patterns that correspond to their emotional and physiological states. These patterns serve as their primary form of communication.

The Mendics' most striking feature is their modified respiratory system. Having adapted to the Foundry's perfectly calibrated atmosphere, they breathe only once every few minutes—a deep, resonant inhalation that expands their ribcages dramatically, followed by a gradual release that powers their internal biological processes. This breathing cycle creates a monastery-like rhythm throughout the Foundry's chambers as groups of Mendics unconsciously synchronize their respiration.

Their eyes are enormous and entirely black, evolved to function perfectly in both the brilliant illumination of the "day" cycle and the subtle bioluminescence of "night." These eyes can perceive spectra far beyond human vision, allowing them to "read" the microscopic patterns etched into every surface of the Foundry—maintenance instructions left by the original builders, now a sacred text to the Mendics.

Most distinctive is their symbiotic relationship with the Foundry itself. At birth, each Mendic receives implants—part mechanical, part grown tissue—that interface directly with their nervous systems. Their right arms terminate not in hands but in modular appendages that can reconfigure to interface with any of the Foundry's countless maintenance ports. When connected, Mendics enter a trance state, their consciousness temporarily merging with the Foundry's systems.

They organize themselves into Circuits—groups of twelve to twenty individuals specialized in maintaining particular systems. The Pipe Circuit tends to water reclamation, the Light Circuit cultivates the luminous fungi, and so on. Each Circuit develops distinct physical adaptations over time; members of the Depth Circuit who maintain the geothermal systems have skin that's heat-resistant and eyes adapted to darkness.

The Mendics are not alone. They cultivate and direct the smaller beings of the Foundry:

The Argent Climbers—the silver-furred monkey-spider hybrids—serve as their assistants, bred for their dexterity and problem-solving abilities. Climbers possess a rudimentary collective intelligence, communicating through subtle shifts in their fur's reflectivity.

The Lumin Beetles are semi-sentient living diagnostic tools, their glowing patterns shifting to indicate system malfunctions.

Most mysterious are the ancient Archivists—beings that might once have been human but are now floating columns of preserved brain tissue suspended in crystalline cylinders mounted on spider-like mechanical legs. They rarely interact with the Mendics directly, instead traveling to the deepest reaches of the Foundry to commune with its oldest systems, returning with cryptic instructions that the Mendics interpret through generations of tradition.

The Mendics reproduce rarely and deliberately, creating new members only when needed to maintain their numbers. Young Mendics spend their first decade in the Nursery Galleries, where the moss grows thickest and the chiming flowers are most abundant. Here they learn to synchronize their breathing with the Foundry's rhythms and develop their initial connection to its systems.

They possess no concept of individuality as humans understand it. Each Mendic sees themselves as a specialized cell within the greater organism of the Foundry. They have no ambition beyond perfect maintenance, no desire except the continued functioning of their world-spanning home. Their greatest fear—rarely spoken of but present in their oldest songs (performed as patterns of bioluminescence and carefully timed breaths)—is that the Foundry has a purpose they've forgotten, a greater function they no longer serve.

When rare human explorers encounter the Mendics, they find them neither hostile nor particularly welcoming. The Mendics view outsiders with detached curiosity, sometimes offering them small mechanical tokens—perfectly crafted objects whose purpose no one can determine. These gifts may be tests, invitations, or warnings—the Mendics never explain, and explorers who accept them often find themselves dreaming of the Foundry's rhythms long after they've returned to the surface world.
