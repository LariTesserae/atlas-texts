# The Fungi-Folk of the High Fens

The people of Glimmerwick—who call themselves the Myrsha—are as much a part of the mountain ecosystem as the moss that clings to the pathways or the water that flows through their city. Their most striking feature is their symbiotic relationship with the fungi that illuminate their world.

Each Myrsha hosts colonies of luminescent mycelia that grow beneath their translucent outer skin, creating intricate, glowing patterns across their bodies. These patterns are unique to each individual, shifting slightly with age, health, and emotional state. The relationship begins at birth, when infants are carefully placed in special nursery chambers lined with starter cultures of the symbiotic fungi. The mycelia gradually infiltrate the newborn's dermal layers, establishing a lifelong bond that provides both illumination and heightened sensitivity to environmental conditions.

The Myrsha stand shorter than lowland humans, rarely exceeding five feet in height, with dense, compact musculature adapted to the vertical terrain and thin air. Their chests are disproportionately large to accommodate expanded lungs, while their feet have evolved with prehensile qualities—the toes longer and more articulated than those of their distant valley cousins, allowing them to grip the slick surfaces of their mountainous home with barefoot precision.

Their eyes are their most remarkable feature beyond the fungal illumination—oversized and containing additional rods that allow them to perceive the subtle light variations produced by their city's fungi. The irises range from pale silver to deep violet, sometimes shifting color based on the ambient light conditions. Many elders develop concentric rings in their irises that younger Myrsha can "read" to determine their lineage and profession.

Their skin displays a remarkable adaptability to moisture, developing a slight mucous coating in the wettest conditions that prevents maceration despite constant dampness. This coating, combined with their symbiotic fungi, gives their skin a subtle iridescence that intensifies during communal singing ceremonies. Their hair grows in tight spirals, naturally forming dense, water-resistant layers against their scalps, typically in colors ranging from deep blue-black to moss green, with occasional individuals displaying luminescent streaks that pulse in rhythm with their breathing.

The Myrsha live remarkably long lives—many reaching 150 years—though their aging process differs from lowlanders. Rather than gradual decline, they experience discrete "transformations" approximately every thirty years, during which they retreat to isolation chambers carved deep within the mountain. These metamorphic periods last about a month, during which their fungal symbionts undergo significant restructuring, often resulting in new growth patterns and slightly altered physiological characteristics when they emerge.

Their society is organized around water stewardship, with status determined by one's role in maintaining the elaborate hydrological systems that sustain Glimmerwick. The most respected figures are the Water-Listeners—elders who can detect subtle changes in flow patterns by pressing their ears to specific points in the aqueduct system, predicting flash floods or dry spells days in advance. These individuals are distinguished by the spiral-shaped fungi that grow around their ear canals, amplifying the subtle acoustics of water movement.

The Myrsha communicate through multiple channels simultaneously—verbal language comprising soft consonants and elongated vowels that carry effectively through fog, complemented by the bioluminescent patterns that pulse across their skin. Complex ideas are often expressed through a combination of spoken words, light patterns, and specific postures that would be incomprehensible to outsiders attempting to understand any single component in isolation.

Their crafts focus on living materials—they are masters of fungal cultivation, developing specialized strains for illumination, structural reinforcement, medicine, and nutrition. Their most precious possessions are "forever lamps"—carefully cultivated fungi encased in blown glass spheres filled with nutrient solution, passed down through generations and believed to contain the accumulated wisdom of ancestors in their light patterns.

Religion centers around what they call the "Mist Leviathans"—the vast shadow creatures that periodically move through the fog. These beings are considered neither gods nor threats but rather the original architects of the mountain, ancient entities that shaped the peak long before the Myrsha arrived. The crystalline deposits they leave behind—known as "leviathan tears"—are incorporated into the water system during specific ceremonies, believed to purify and imbue the water with protective properties.

The Myrsha reproduce infrequently, with pregnancies lasting nearly two years. During this time, the carrier develops additional fungal growth across their abdomen, creating protective illumination that is said to begin the child's education while still in the womb. Childbirth is a communal event taking place in the highest chambers of the city, where the air is thinnest and the connection to the Mist Leviathans strongest.

Their understanding of death differs from lowland cultures—they view it not as an ending but as a transformation of matter and energy. When a Myrsha dies, their body is placed in sacred caverns where specialized fungi consume all organic material. The resulting mycelial network is believed to retain aspects of the deceased's consciousness, contributing to a collective ancestral wisdom that can be accessed during meditation in these spaces. The luminescent patterns of these funerary fungi are said to reflect the personalities of those they have consumed.

Despite—or perhaps because of—their isolation, the Myrsha have developed sophisticated mathematics and observational astronomy, tracking celestial movements through brief gaps in the persistent fog. Their calendar is based not on solar cycles but on the periodic migrations of the Mist Leviathans and the intensification cycles of their symbiotic fungi.

The Myrsha rarely descend from their mountain home, finding the drier air of lower elevations physically painful as their symbiotic fungi begin to desiccate. When trade or diplomacy necessitates such journeys, they travel in sealed palanquins filled with mountain mist, accompanied by specialists who maintain optimal moisture conditions for the travelers.

To the outside world, the Myrsha remain largely myth—glowing figures glimpsed through mountain fog, their city a hallucination of light and impossible architecture. They prefer this perception, understanding that their way of life exists in a delicate balance with their extreme environment—a balance that has allowed them to thrive in conditions where others could barely survive, becoming as much a part of the mountain as the springs that feed their elaborate waterways.
