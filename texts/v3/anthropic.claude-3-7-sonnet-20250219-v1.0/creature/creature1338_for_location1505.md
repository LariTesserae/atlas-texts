# The Ren Vos: Keepers of the Threshold

The Ren Vos are neither wholly human nor entirely other. They are the inheritors of an ancient covenant with the mountain itself, their lineage stretching back to a time before written history. Their physical forms suggest human ancestry—two arms, two legs, a single head—but closer examination reveals subtle impossibilities.

Their skin holds an alabaster translucence that shifts with altitude and lunar phase. Beneath this semi-permeable membrane, circulatory systems pulse not with red blood but with a fluid that catches light like liquid crystal, branching through their bodies in patterns that occasionally rearrange themselves. These patterns correspond to the crystalline formations on the Repository walls, as if the Ren Vos are living extensions of the structure itself.

They stand unnaturally tall and thin, their proportions stretched like figures in a distorting mirror. Their fingers extend too far, with an extra joint that allows them to manipulate objects in ways that defy conventional anatomy. When they move, they seem to flow rather than step, creating the impression that gravity holds them by mutual agreement rather than physical law.

Their faces bear human features arranged with subtle wrongness—eyes set slightly too wide, pupils that contract vertically like a cat's but expand horizontally when they focus on distant things. Their ears taper to points so fine they seem capable of filtering individual frequencies from the mountain's constant whispers. No two Ren Vos look precisely alike, yet all share an unmistakable familial resemblance, as if carved from variations of the same template.

They do not age as humans do. Rather, they crystallize slowly over centuries, their flesh gradually transforming into the same luminous mineral that adorns the Repository walls. The eldest among them—those who have served as caretakers for a millennium or more—have limbs that terminate in branching crystal formations. These elders can no longer leave their chambers, having partially fused with the structure itself, their consciousness diffused throughout portions of the Repository.

The Ren Vos do not reproduce as humans do. New caretakers emerge from the well in the deepest chamber during rare celestial alignments. These "newborn" Ren Vos appear fully formed, rising from the still waters with knowledge already imprinted in their minds—though they cannot speak of their origins, even among themselves. Their first words are always the same: "I remember the falling upward."

They sustain themselves primarily through absorption rather than consumption. During certain hours, they stand motionless in specific chambers where the mountain's breath—imperceptible to human senses—carries microscopic nutrients through their semi-permeable skin. They partake of food and drink only during ceremonial gatherings, consuming substances that would prove toxic to human visitors—teas brewed from the crystalline growths, liquids distilled from the impossible well.

Their language defies conventional linguistic study. They communicate simultaneously through whispered words, subtle shifts in luminescence beneath their skin, and modulated resonances that interact with the Repository's acoustic anomalies. A single Ren Vos utterance carries layers of meaning that unfold differently depending on one's position within the structure. They can speak human languages when necessary, but these words emerge with strange cadences, as if translated through several intermediary tongues.

The Ren Vos perceive time differently than humans do. For them, past, present, and future exist simultaneously as varying densities rather than sequential moments. They can focus their attention on different temporal "layers," allowing them to recall events from centuries ago with perfect clarity or to glimpse probable futures as one might observe weather patterns forming on the horizon. This perception manifests physically—older memories crystallize throughout their bodies as intricate internal formations, while potential futures appear as shifting luminescent patterns beneath their skin.

Their purpose—as they understand it—is custodianship. They maintain the Repository not merely as a structure but as a threshold between realities. The mountain spire exists simultaneously in multiple states, anchored to conventional reality by the Ren Vos' continuous presence and rituals. Without their stewardship, the Repository would either fully materialize in our world (with catastrophic consequences for surrounding territories) or dissolve entirely into the elsewhere from which it partially extends.

Each Ren Vos serves a specific function within their society. Some are Resonators, who maintain the acoustic properties of various chambers through precisely modulated humming. Others are Cartographers, who track the Repository's gradual reconfiguration by tracing the migration of certain features through the structure's heart. The Waterkeepers tend to the well, ensuring its connection to the elsewhere remains stable. The Crystallists cultivate and harvest the living mineral growths that provide light, sustenance, and raw materials.

The most enigmatic are the Thresholds—the eldest among them, whose bodies have partially merged with the Repository itself. They no longer move or speak as individuals but communicate through vibrations in the stone and fluctuations in the structure's ambient luminescence. Their consciousness extends throughout entire sectors of the Repository, perceiving everything that occurs within their domain simultaneously.

When visitors come—always by the mountain's invitation, never by chance—the Ren Vos observe them with curious detachment. They welcome questions but answer obliquely, through parables and koans that reveal their meaning only after departure. They accept gifts but offer none in return, explaining that "the Repository provides what is necessary upon leaving."

Indeed, every visitor departs with some small impossible token—though never one they consciously received or selected. A stone that weighs different amounts depending on who holds it. A compass whose needle points toward the most important thing within reach. A single crystalline seed that, when placed under one's pillow, allows glimpses of the Repository in dreams.

The Ren Vos themselves rarely leave. Those who do can maintain their form for only brief periods before beginning to lose cohesion, their bodies growing increasingly translucent until they must return or risk dissolving entirely. They venture out only when necessary to maintain certain alignments between the Repository and other similar thresholds scattered across the world—places where reality wears thin, where elsewhere seeps through.

"We are the narrow space between breaths," they explain to those who ask their purpose. "We are the membrane between what is and what might be. We are the caretakers of possibility."
