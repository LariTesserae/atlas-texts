# The Custodians of Tishwir

They call themselves the Fissene, though this name exists more as a resonant vibration than a spoken word. Their existence bridges multiple states of being—neither fully corporeal nor entirely energy, neither singular entities nor a collective consciousness, but something that moves fluidly between these states as needed.

In their most perceptible form, the Fissene appear as crystalline lattices of light that seem to fold through dimensions beyond the visible three. Their bodies—if such a term applies—consist of interlocking geometric patterns that continuously reconfigure while maintaining a coherent identity. These patterns contain millions of minute facets that capture and process information from their surroundings, serving as both sensory organs and memory storage simultaneously.

The Fissene are not born but emerge from the plateau itself through a process they call "differentiation." When the crystalline veins in the rock accumulate sufficient complexity—a process that might take millennia—consciousness spontaneously develops. A new Fissene will gradually separate from the stone, their awareness expanding as they learn to manipulate their form. Though individuals, they maintain a continuous connection to the plateau and to each other through the ubiquitous water that serves as both communication medium and shared memory.

Their society lacks hierarchy in any recognizable sense. Instead, they organize around functions that maintain the plateau's perfect equilibrium. Some Fissene calibrate the moisture seepage, ensuring the water's endless cycle continues uninterrupted. Others tend to the crystalline structures, making infinitesimal adjustments to their molecular lattices to prevent decay over the eons. The most ancient among them focus on maintaining the localized physics, carefully balancing forces to preserve the pockets of altered reality that dot the landscape.

The Fissene perceive time differently than linear beings. They experience all moments simultaneously while also being able to focus on specific temporal slices when necessary. For them, past and future exist as accessible dimensions rather than abstract concepts. This perception allows them to detect potential instabilities in Tishwir's systems centuries before they might manifest, making corrections that appear as natural processes to outside observers.

Their language defies translation into human terms. They communicate through modulated vibrations in the plateau's stone and through complex patterns in the flowing water. A single "statement" might contain embedded information equivalent to volumes of text, along with emotional resonances and historical contexts. When they wish to communicate with visitors, they create temporary interface patterns—ripples in reality that translate their thoughts into forms comprehensible to other consciousnesses, though always imperfectly.

The Fissene's relationship with the other creatures of Tishwir is symbiotic rather than dominant. They regard the six-legged grazers, the kite-beings, and the rolling spheres not as lesser life forms but as specialized components of Tishwir's ecosystem. These creatures assist in maintaining specific aspects of the plateau's balance—the spheres, for instance, inscribe correctional algorithms through their glowing trails that help stabilize reality in regions where it tends toward entropy.

Most remarkable about the Fissene is their purpose. They are not merely inhabitants but custodians of something far greater than themselves. The plateau of Tishwir is not simply a location but a vast mechanism—a reality engine constructed by entities that existed before the current universe formed. The Fissene emerged as caretakers of this engine, which serves to anchor certain fundamental constants of reality across multiple dimensions. Should Tishwir's perfect equilibrium ever falter, the consequences would cascade far beyond the plateau itself.

The Fissene possess knowledge that spans billions of years, yet they rarely share it directly. Instead, they sometimes guide visitors to experience carefully curated moments of insight through the plateau's physics-bending properties. Those who have undergone these experiences describe them as revelatory yet fundamentally untranslatable—knowledge that transforms the observer but cannot be conveyed to others afterward.

Despite their seemingly eternal nature, the Fissene do eventually dissipate. After existing for periods measured in geological epochs, they gradually reintegrate with the plateau, their consciousness dissolving back into the crystalline veins from which new Fissene will eventually emerge. They do not fear this dissolution but view it as completion of a cycle that maintains the plateau's perfect balance of energy and awareness.

To the few who have perceived them clearly, the Fissene appear neither benevolent nor malevolent but fundamentally other—beings whose perspective is so vast that concepts of good and evil become meaningless against the imperative of maintaining cosmic equilibrium. They are the living mathematics of reality, equations given consciousness, formulas with purpose—and Tishwir is their eternal calculation.
