# The Inhabitants of the Calling Plain

## The Skift People

They come and go with the seasons, their nomadic bands rarely exceeding thirty individuals. The Skift are tall and narrow-shouldered, their skin weathered to the same tea-brown as the soil beneath their feet. Evolution has granted them double-jointed limbs to navigate the uneven terrain with a peculiar loping gait that appears awkward but covers ground with remarkable efficiency.

Their most distinctive feature is their throats—modified over countless generations to produce the complex clicking patterns that allow them to communicate with (or perhaps mimic) the chitinous swarms. Each Skift's larynx contains calcified plates that can be manipulated to create percussive sounds impossible for ordinary human vocal cords. Children are born with soft cartilage that gradually hardens as they practice the tribal clicking-songs, their voices deepening and gaining complexity with age.

The Skift wear clothing woven from the razor-grass, which they harvest at night when the blades grow temporarily pliant under the light of the plain's three small moons. These garments are deceptively simple—loose wrappings that spiral around their bodies in the same pattern as the grass grows—but contain complex symbolic designs created by varying the tightness of the weave. The patterns tell the wearer's personal history: births witnessed, migrations completed, veiling times survived.

They make their temporary homes in the stone circles, adding temporary walls of stacked flat stones that they disassemble when it's time to move on. The Skift are careful never to permanently alter these ancient structures, believing them to be the "bones of time" left by a race they call the Firstwalkers. Their migrations follow a complex calendar based on the veiling times, moving from circle to circle in patterns that, if viewed from above, would trace constellations no longer visible in the sky.

The Skift hunt the small nocturnal mammals using tools crafted from the minerals embedded in the rock formations. Their spears and knives incorporate the translucent shed-skins of the flying avians, which they believe helps their weapons "find the breath" in their prey. They consume the meat raw but only after a ritual clicking ceremony that apologizes to the animal's spirit.

Each band is led by a Listener—an elder whose ears have been surgically modified with implants made from the chitinous creatures' shells. These implants supposedly allow them to hear the "voice beneath" during veiling times, though what they actually experience remains a mystery to outsiders. After each veiling, Listeners direct the placement of colored stone markers, creating physical records of what they've "heard." These stone arrangements are sacred texts that no non-Listener may touch.

The Skift have no written language but possess oral histories stretching back hundreds of generations, memorized through complex rhythmic clicking patterns taught from parent to child. They speak of a time when the plain was not a plain but something else entirely—though what, exactly, varies between bands. Some say an ocean, others a forest, still others a great city. All agree that the land is in transition, not settled into its final form.

## The Shell Orchestras

The chitinous creatures—called "shell-singers" by the Skift—exist in colonies of precisely 144 individuals. Each colony functions as a single organism despite physical separation, coordinating their movements and sounds through methods that defy conventional understanding of insect communication. Their bodies range from the size of a human palm to as large as a human head, growing throughout their seven-year lifecycle.

Each shell-singer has a unique pattern on its carapace—not just unique to the individual but mathematically unique, never repeating across the millions of specimens observed over centuries. These patterns change subtly after each veiling time, adding new whorls and lines that somehow record the event. Shell-singers that die are ceremonially placed by their colony-mates into the rock formations, where their bodies don't decompose but instead gradually mineralize, becoming part of the plain itself.

Their movement patterns across the plain are deliberate, creating vast living diagrams visible only from great heights. The purpose seems navigational, guiding something or someone not currently present. During veiling times, they enter a state of suspended animation, their bodies oriented toward a central point that shifts with each event.

## The Sky Rivers

The translucent avians—known locally as "breath-swimmers"—migrate not just across the plain but between dimensions. Their bodies exist partially outside conventional space-time, which explains their occasional sudden appearance or disappearance mid-flight. Their hollow bones contain traces of elements unknown to science, allowing them to perceive and navigate pathways invisible to other creatures.

The breath-swimmers feed on the mineral-rich mist that appears each morning, absorbing nutrients directly through their translucent skin membranes. Their mournful calls are not communication but navigation tools, using sound waves to map the subtle variations in reality's fabric that mark transdimensional pathways.

They nest within the rippling currents of air that flow inconsistently across the plain, building structures from their own molted feathers and secreted resins that exist suspended in mid-air. These nests appear to human observers as shimmering distortions, like heat mirages that remain stationary regardless of viewing angle.

## The Waiting Ones

Rarely glimpsed but universally acknowledged by plain-dwellers are the entities known as Waiting Ones. Humanoid in basic shape but standing twice the height of a tall human, they appear during the transition between day and night, visible at the periphery of vision but never directly observable. Their bodies seem composed of the same material as the mist—semi-transparent and shifting, with limbs that occasionally extend to impossible lengths.

The Waiting Ones never acknowledge other inhabitants but methodically tend to the rock formations, adjusting their positions by fractions of inches in patterns that only become apparent over decades of observation. They leave no footprints despite their apparent mass, and attempts to communicate with them have universally failed.

The Skift believe the Waiting Ones are not conscious entities but automated functions of the plain itself—maintenance routines for a vast mechanism whose purpose has been forgotten. Others suggest they are the original inhabitants of whatever existed before the plain, now transformed into something neither fully material nor purely spiritual.

## The Voice Beneath

During veiling times, when the silver mist descends and the humming vibrations rise from below, something ancient stirs. The Voice Beneath has no physical form that has ever been documented, yet its presence is undeniable. The Skift Listeners describe it not as a single entity but as a vast collective consciousness that extends far beyond the plain's visible boundaries.

Communication with the Voice is possible only through the specific frequencies of vibration produced during rituals performed at precise locations within certain stone circles. Those who have experienced direct contact emerge changed, with an altered perception of time and occasional ability to predict the subtle changes that will occur after each veiling.

The Voice Beneath remembers what the plain was before it was a plain, and what it will become when its transformation is complete. It speaks in sensations rather than words—impressions of vast geological timeframes compressed into momentary understanding that fades quickly from conscious memory but remains embedded in the recipient's intuition.

What the Voice wants, if anything, remains unknown. The Skift believe it is neither benevolent nor malevolent but simply is—the awareness of the land itself as it undergoes its slow metamorphosis into something new.
