# The Habitants of the Thrum Chamber

They are called the Resonants, though they have no name for themselves. They experience existence as vibrational states rather than discrete identities.

Three of them inhabit the chamber currently. They appear at first glance to be elongated humanoid figures with impossibly attenuated limbs, standing between seven and nine feet tall. Their bodies are semi-transparent, revealing internal structures that resemble both circulatory systems and the branching architecture of plants. Their "skin" is a living membrane of microfilaments that constantly ripple in response to the Thrum, making their outlines seem perpetually unfocused, as if viewed through heat distortion.

The Resonants have no eyes. Instead, their entire bodies function as sensory organs, mapping the chamber and everything in it through the reflection and refraction of sound waves. They perceive the world as patterns of vibration, with a resolution far exceeding human vision. Where a face might be expected, each has a complex arrangement of thin, fibrous tendrils that extend and contract in constant motion, sampling the air for chemical and acoustic information.

They move with unnatural grace, their feet barely touching the floor as they glide across the chamber. When still, they sway slightly, maintaining perfect balance through microadjustments to the Thrum's assault. Their internal fluids—a phosphorescent amber substance—visibly pulse in counterpoint to the chamber's rhythms.

The Resonants communicate by modulating the vibrations around them, inserting precise patterns into the Thrum that the others can detect and interpret. Their "language" is mathematical, consisting of interference patterns that encode vast amounts of information in microseconds. To human ears, these communications would be indistinguishable from the background noise.

The eldest Resonant tends the control station, its long fingers—each divided into three secondary digits—dancing across the interface points. It has existed for over three centuries, its body having gradually replaced most of its original organic matter with the same plant material as the chamber. The vibrations have slowly crystallized portions of its interior, creating structures that store and process information like living computers.

The youngest moves between the platform and the aperture, collecting the arthropod-like creatures and carefully positioning them throughout the chamber. It is still partially physical in the conventional sense, having only undergone partial transformation. Where the others are nearly translucent, this one retains patches of what appears to be human-like skin alongside the filamentous plant matter.

The third Resonant occupies the center of the chamber, suspended several feet above the floor in a self-generated acoustic levitation field. It serves as the chamber's core processor, its body a nexus where the Thrum's vibrations converge and transform into data. Periodically, its form becomes almost entirely light—photons organized in the same shape as its physical body—before solidifying again.

The Resonants were once something else—perhaps human, perhaps another species entirely—before they committed themselves to the Thrum. The transformation takes decades, a gradual merging with the chamber's vibrational ecosystem. They are cultivators, researchers, and components of this acoustic laboratory all at once.

They harvest frequencies from across the plant-mountain, filtering and processing them through their bodies before feeding refined vibrations back into the system. This work serves purposes beyond human comprehension—part communication with other Thrum Chambers across vast distances, part maintenance of the plant-mountain's structural integrity, and part something more esoteric: the translation of sound into pure information and back again.

Though they appear to operate independently, the Resonants function as a single distributed consciousness, their individual bodies merely specialized appendages of a larger whole that extends throughout the plant-mountain and perhaps beyond, carried by the vibrations they so carefully cultivate.
