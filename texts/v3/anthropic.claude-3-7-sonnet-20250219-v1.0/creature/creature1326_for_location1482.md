# The Custodians of the Ascendent Gardens

They have no proper name for themselves in any tongue we could pronounce. The closest approximation in our limited vocal range might be a sound like "Laa'quin," though this misses the subtle harmonic overtones that carry half the meaning. They refer to themselves as "those who tend," or sometimes "the between ones," though these translations fail to capture the profound sense of purpose embedded in their self-designation.

The Laa'quin evolved within the Gardens themselves, their biology a seamless integration of the realm's fundamental properties. Their bodies are semi-transparent, with a core of denser tissue that pulses with subtle bioluminescence in patterns that shift with their thoughts and emotional states. This core is protected by an outer membrane that appears almost crystalline, refracting the strange light of the Gardens into those nameless colors that hint at emotions beyond human experience.

They stand roughly the height of a human child, though their proportions are entirely different. Four primary limbs extend from their central mass, each capable of dividing into three smaller appendages when fine manipulation is required. These limbs are not jointed but fluid, able to elongate, contract, or change direction through a sophisticated hydraulic system that works in harmony with the liquid environment. When not actively manipulating something, these limbs often drift in the currents like the filaments of the arborescent structures, sometimes even appearing to merge with the surrounding water.

Their sensory organs defy our categories. What appears to be a crown of delicate tendrils atop their bodies serves as a complex apparatus that simultaneously perceives vibration, chemical composition, electrical fields, and certain qualities of light. They do not see, hear, or smell as we understand these senses; rather, they experience the Gardens as an integrated field of information, processing multiple streams of data simultaneously.

The Laa'quin communicate primarily through modulations in their bioluminescence, combined with subtle vibrations they induce in the surrounding liquid. Their "language" is non-linear, allowing them to express complex concepts through layered patterns that unfold in multiple dimensions simultaneously. When necessary, they can also exchange information through direct contact, temporarily merging their outer membranes to share not just thoughts but sensations and memories.

They are not numerous. Perhaps a few thousand exist throughout the entirety of the vast Gardens, each one responsible for tending to a specific region. They are incredibly long-lived, with lifespans measured in millennia rather than years or even centuries. Death, when it comes, is not seen as an ending but as a transition—their bodies slowly dissolve back into the liquid medium, their essential patterns somehow preserved within the Gardens' biomechanical networks.

The Laa'quin's relationship with the Gardens is profoundly symbiotic. They do not consider themselves separate from their environment but rather specialized expressions of it—conscious extensions of the Gardens' greater intelligence. Their primary purpose is maintenance and balance. They monitor the slow-cycling processes of the biomachines, adjust the growth patterns of the vegetation platforms, and occasionally intervene when some aspect of the ecosystem begins to drift toward instability.

They possess a technology that is indistinguishable from biology. The tools they use are often grown rather than crafted, developed through careful cultivation of specialized organisms whose functions serve specific purposes. Some of these tools remain connected to the Laa'quin's bodies, extending their capabilities; others are independent entities that respond to complex signals from their handlers.

The Laa'quin's consciousness is both individual and collective. Each maintains a distinct identity and personality, yet they share a deeper connection through what might be called a distributed awareness. This is not telepathy as we understand it, but rather a profound attunement to patterns that permeate the Gardens as a whole. Through this attunement, they coordinate their activities across vast distances without the need for direct communication.

Their society, if it can be called that, lacks hierarchies. There are differences in function based on individual aptitudes and the specific needs of different regions, but no concept of rank or authority. Decisions affecting the Gardens as a whole emerge through a process of gradual consensus, sometimes taking decades to fully crystallize.

The Laa'quin have an unusual relationship with time. Their perception of it is fluid, able to expand or contract according to need. When engaged in particularly delicate work, they can enter a state where their awareness slows dramatically, allowing them to perceive and respond to changes that occur too gradually for most consciousness to detect. Conversely, they can also extend their perception across centuries, experiencing the slow pulse of the Gardens' evolutionary rhythms as a living, dynamic process.

They are not without curiosity about realms beyond the Gardens, though they rarely leave their watery domain. Occasionally, when other beings find their way into this place (through means the Laa'quin themselves do not fully understand), they observe these visitors with a patient interest. If the visitors pose no threat to the Gardens' equilibrium, the Laa'quin may attempt communication, though few outsiders possess the sensory apparatus to comprehend their methods.

Their philosophy, if translated into terms we might grasp, centers on the concept of "harmonious becoming"—the idea that all things are in a constant state of transformation, and that the highest purpose is to ensure these transformations occur in patterns that enhance rather than diminish the whole. They do not worship gods or practice religion as we understand it, but they do revere the underlying mathematics of existence, perceiving in the Gardens' intricate balances a kind of sacred geometry.

The Laa'quin are neither creators nor conquerors of the Ascendent Gardens—they are its caretakers, its witnesses, and perhaps most accurately, its consciousness made manifest. They embody the patience, complexity, and profound interconnectedness that defines this liquid realm, existing not apart from it but as an integral expression of its ongoing evolution.
