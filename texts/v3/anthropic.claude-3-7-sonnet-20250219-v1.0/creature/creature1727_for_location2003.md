# The Denizens of the Underhalls

The people of Merovere are not quite human anymore, though they began as such. Centuries of adaptation have transformed them into a subspecies uniquely suited to their aquatic realm. Their skin has a slight translucence, with visible blue-green veins that trace patterns just beneath the surface, almost resembling the bioluminescent algae that lights their world. Their eyes are large and dark, adapted for the perpetual twilight, with secondary translucent eyelids that protect against the water's constant touch.

The Meroverians are lean and long-limbed, their fingers and toes webbed, though not completely—they maintain the dexterity needed for their intricate craftwork. Their chests are expansive, housing modified lungs and specialized gill-like structures that developed through generations of alchemical treatments and careful breeding. Small ridges of cartilage along their necks conceal these breathing apparatuses, visible only when they fully extend during deep exhalations.

Their hair grows thick and water-resistant, often worn in elaborate braids woven with polished stones, carved bone beads, or living bioluminescent organisms that serve as personal illumination. Many adorn themselves with intricate patterns etched into their skin using dyes derived from deep-sea creatures, creating flowing designs that seem to move with the water around them.

The eldest of Merovere command the greatest respect. These ancient ones, some over 150 years old, move with deliberate slowness, conserving their energy like all resources in this challenging environment. Their skin bears a patina of age that resembles weathered stone, and they often incorporate small living coral formations into their flesh—a symbiotic relationship that strengthens both parties.

The Tenders are the gardeners and caretakers of the settlement's living systems. Their fingers permanently stained with the pigments of the plants they nurture, they move through the gardens with a dancer's grace, using specialized tools of carved whale bone to trim, shape, and guide the growth of their charges. They wear garments woven from the finest filaments of cultivated seaweed, embedded with tiny bioluminescent organisms that respond to their touch, glowing brighter as they work.

The Shapers are the artisans and architects who expand and maintain the physical structures of Merovere. Their muscles are dense from generations of working the stone, and their skin is calloused from handling abrasives. They use tools inherited through family lines, some dating back centuries, each one customized to fit the hand of its current owner. When they work, they enter a meditative state, moving in perfect rhythm with the water's subtle currents.

The Listeners form the settlement's memory and communication hub. Their eardrums have evolved particular sensitivity to underwater vibrations, allowing them to detect subtleties in the sound-carrying water that others cannot perceive. They maintain the sounding stones—massive resonant crystals positioned at key junctions throughout the settlement—and have developed a percussion language of remarkable complexity. Their social status is marked by necklaces of tiny stones that click together as they move, creating personal signatures of sound.

The Wayfarers are the rare few who venture beyond the settlement boundaries. Their bodies are more heavily modified than most, with thicker skin resistant to temperature changes and eyes that can adjust to the absolute darkness of the ocean depths. They collect resources and intelligence from the wider underwater world, and bear ritual scarifications that record their journeys and encounters with the deeper ocean's dangers.

The Breathless are a small monastic order who have undergone the most extreme adaptations, capable of surviving the crushing pressures of the abyssal plains. Nearly translucent, with enlarged hearts and specialized blood that carries extraordinary amounts of oxygen, they make pilgrimages to the ocean floor where they commune with entities that predate human existence. When they return, they speak rarely but are consulted on matters of greatest importance.

Meroverian children are born in special birthing chambers where the water is kept warmer and enriched with nutrients. Their first years are spent in communal nurseries, where they develop the swimming skills and water breathing reflexes necessary for survival. By age seven, they begin apprenticeships, their bodies gradually adapting to specific roles through carefully administered alchemical treatments and specialized exercises.

Merovere also hosts a small population of surface-dweller diplomats and scholars who have undergone temporary adaptations to survive underwater. They are easily identified by their awkward movements and reliance on external breathing apparatuses. Though respected for the connections they maintain with the upper world, there is always a sense that they are merely visitors who cannot truly comprehend the Meroverian way of life.

The settlement's equilibrium also depends on its relationship with certain non-human intelligences. The most visible are the Drift Minds—massive jellyfish-like entities with neural networks comparable to mammalian brains. These creatures float through the larger chambers during dim seasons, their tendrils sensing and processing information about the settlement's condition. They communicate through patterns of light and electrical impulses that only the Listeners can fully interpret.

Finally, dwelling in the deepest excavated chamber of Merovere is the entity known simply as the Ancient. Neither fully plant nor animal, this sprawling colonial organism has existed since the settlement's founding, its consciousness distributed through a network of pulsing nodules embedded in the chamber walls. It grows at an imperceptible pace, adding new neural tissue only once every few decades. The Ancient rarely communicates directly, but its presence permeates Meroverian culture—a living embodiment of their highest virtue: persistence against the weight of oceans and time.
