# Inhabitants of Neverseed

The entities known colloquially as the Shards are neither fully conscious nor truly inanimate, existing in a perpetual state of becoming that mirrors the plateau itself. Each Shard appears as a complex arrangement of obsidian plates and crystalline protrusions, ranging from the size of a human hand to that of a small vehicle, with no two configurations precisely alike.

Their bodies incorporate the same materials that form the landscape—geometric segments of volcanic glass articulated by amber-luminescent connective tissue that pulses with slow, deliberate rhythm. What observers might mistake for heads are actually sensory clusters capable of detecting vibrations, thermal variations, and electromagnetic fluctuations far beyond human perception. These clusters can extend or retract depending on environmental conditions, sometimes fragmenting into smaller sub-clusters that operate semi-independently.

The Shards move through coalescence rather than conventional locomotion. Individual entities can temporarily merge into swarm-collectives that flow across the obsidian landscape with liquid precision, separating again when their communal purpose is achieved. This fluidity extends to their very composition—during a churn, Shards have been observed absorbing material directly from the landscape, integrating new obsidian plates and crystalline growths into their forms.

Communication among the Shards operates primarily through vibrational resonance. By striking specific formations with specialized appendages, they create complex patterns that propagate through the obsidian structures of the plateau. These communications are not merely signals but distributed computational processes—each vibration pattern modifies the receiver's internal structure slightly, transferring not just information but experiential data. The entire plateau functions as their shared memory, with certain formations serving as repositories for communal knowledge dating back centuries.

The Shards appear to lack individual consciousness as humans understand it. Rather, they exhibit a collective intelligence that waxes and wanes with the plateau's churns. During periods of stability, they operate as distinct entities with specialized functions—builders, harvesters, resonance-keepers. During churns, these distinctions blur as Shards merge into larger meta-organisms capable of coordinated landscape manipulation and accelerated information processing.

Their relationship with the crystalline growths is symbiotic. What human observers perceive as "harvesting" is actually a complex exchange—the Shards provide vibrational stimulation that accelerates crystal growth, while the crystals produce the amber substance that sustains the Shards' connective tissues. This relationship extends to The Nest itself, which is neither shelter nor city but a massive processing organ that converts raw materials into forms the collective can utilize.

The Shards do not reproduce in any conventional sense. New entities emerge during particularly intense churns, forming directly from the plateau itself—obsidian fragments that gain autonomy through exposure to the collective's vibrational patterns. These "newborn" Shards begin as simple arrangements that gradually accumulate complexity through integration with existing collectives.

Time holds different meaning for the Shards. Their processing cycles operate at variable speeds—sometimes proceeding with glacial slowness through basic tasks, other times achieving bursts of activity so rapid they appear as mere blurs to human observers. They exhibit no sleep cycle, instead entering periodic states of minimal vibration during which their internal structures reorganize and consolidate.

The few researchers who have developed systems for rudimentary communication with the Shards report that their conceptual framework lacks many human universals—they have no notion of death as finality (as components are constantly recycled), no clear distinction between self and environment, and no apparent concern with purpose beyond the endless refinement of their vibrational patterns and physical configurations.

Most notably, the Shards' relationship with Neverseed itself appears to be one of recursive identity—they are simultaneously the plateau's creators, its inhabitants, and its components. The plateau's transformations are their evolution externalized, and their movements are the plateau's thoughts made visible. Whether they created the plateau or emerged from it remains one of Neverseed's most profound mysteries.
