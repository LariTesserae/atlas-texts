# Inhabitants of the Scalding Vault

The Archivists are the true denizens of this impossible space. They stand no taller than children, their bodies seemingly formed from the same obsidian as the floor, though more fluid in composition. Their surfaces constantly shift between solid and semi-molten states, reflecting the chamber's relentless light in prismatic patterns. Where faces should be, they have only shallow depressions with five small openings arranged in a pentagon—not for breathing or eating, but for sensing the minute vibrations that permeate the vault.

They move deliberately across the chamber floor, leaving momentary footprints of cooling glass that reabsorb into the stone within minutes. The Archivists' limbs extend and retract as needed, sometimes elongating to reach high along the water-membrane, other times splitting into delicate filaments to manipulate the small artifacts atop the spiral columns.

They are not a numerous species—perhaps twelve exist within the vault at any time. Each bears a unique pattern of luminescent veins that pulse with the same crimson hue as the algae colonies. These patterns are not merely decorative but constitute a form of identity and communication; when two Archivists pass near each other, their veins pulse in alternating rhythms, exchanging information through light rather than sound.

The Archivists tend to the spiral columns with ritualistic precision. Each dawn (as measured by the changing intensity of light filtering through the water-membrane), they rearrange the bone and shell artifacts according to patterns that correspond to the movements of the fish-swarms outside. This is their primary purpose—to record and replicate the geometric configurations of the luminous fish, preserving each pattern within their arrangement of artifacts.

They coexist with the pale amphibious creatures, which they seem to regard as both tools and companions. The Archivists sometimes gently lift these eyeless beings and place them near specific algae colonies, using their bioluminescent responses to calibrate some unseen measurement. In return, they feed the creatures minuscule fragments of their own obsidian bodies, which the amphibians consume with evident satisfaction.

The Archivists never attempt to breach the water-membrane. Yet they are clearly aware of the world beyond, often pressing their sensing depressions against the boundary, remaining motionless for hours as if listening to the ocean. Their bodies vibrate subtly in response to distant undersea events—earthquakes, migrating whale pods, the movement of currents—cataloging these occurrences through minute adjustments to the spiral columns.

They are ancient, though not ageless. When an Archivist reaches the end of its existence—approximately every century—it moves to the chamber's center and slowly solidifies completely. Its body becomes a new column in the spiral arrangement, its knowledge preserved in the very structure of the stone. A new Archivist then emerges from the obsidian floor, inheriting the patterns and memories of its predecessors through means not fully understood.

The Archivists did not create the Scalding Vault—they are its caretakers, born from the interaction of volcanic heat, ocean pressure, and time. They maintain the impossible equilibrium of this place through their continuous documentation and adjustment. The water-membrane itself responds to their touch, subtly reinforcing wherever they press against it, as if acknowledging their role as guardians of the boundary.

Humans who discover this place often feel the Archivists' attention immediately—a sudden stillness in their methodical movements, a collective turning of their featureless forms. They do not communicate directly but will sometimes create small stone replicas of the visitors, placing them carefully among their artifacts as if cataloging a new species for study. These figurines remain long after the visitors depart, gradually changing position within the spiral arrangement as the Archivists incorporate the memory of these encounters into their endless documentation of the threshold between worlds.
