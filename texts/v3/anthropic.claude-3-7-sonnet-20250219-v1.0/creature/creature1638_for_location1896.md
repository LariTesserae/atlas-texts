# The People of the Ascending Gardens

The people who make their home in the Ascending Gardens of Tarnish call themselves the Shapers, though they rarely need to say this aloud. Their identity is expressed in their daily work: the patient guidance of stone and root, water and light.

They are tall and slender, with proportions that suggest adaptation to the thin mountain air. Their skin tones range from warm amber to cool umber, often bearing natural markings resembling the lichen patterns that adorn their buildings—not tattoos, but hereditary features that appear during adolescence. Their eyes are large and widely spaced, with unusual double irises that contract independently in different light conditions, allowing them to work comfortably both in the bright glare of high-altitude sun and in the dim glow of their evening chambers.

The Shapers move with a characteristic fluidity that outsiders initially mistake for slowness. In truth, they are conserving energy and maintaining constant awareness of their surroundings. Every motion serves multiple purposes—a hand gesture simultaneously communicates with neighbors, tests the air's humidity, and aligns with the Gardens' broader rhythms. Their gestures form a rich language that incorporates the movement of water, light, and growing things into its grammar.

Most distinctive are their hands—slightly elongated fingers with an extra joint, and highly sensitive fingertips that can detect minute variations in texture, temperature, and vibration. These hands are their primary tools, capable of manipulating the crystalline growth medium in the chambers with microscopic precision. When they work the growth chambers, their fingers emit subtle biochemical signals that influence the developing structures, literally extending their nervous systems into the architecture they create.

Shapers are born with an innate sensitivity to the flows of energy through their environment. Children begin training this sense before they can walk, learning to perceive the subtle currents of water beneath stone, the tension in a growing stem, the potential energy in a seed. This perception becomes so fundamental to their experience that they perceive the Gardens as a constantly shifting energy pattern as much as a physical place.

They live in family units of three to seven individuals, though their concept of family extends beyond blood relation to include those who share complementary sensitivities—"resonant kin" whose abilities harmonize to enable their collaborative work. These family groups inhabit chambers that they have personally shaped, spaces that evolve as they do, with walls that gradually adjust to their movement patterns and needs.

The Shapers measure their lives not in years but in "bloomings"—the cycles of growth and integration that mark both their personal development and the evolution of the Gardens. A young Shaper experiences their First Blooming around age twelve, when they first successfully guide growth in a communal chamber. The most revered elders have lived through seven or eight Bloomings, their bodies becoming increasingly integrated with the Gardens' systems as they age.

Diet consists primarily of the specialized plants they cultivate—dense root vegetables, protein-rich fungi, and fruits that store sunlight energy with unusual efficiency. They eat communally, sharing food from spiral-shaped platters that continuously rotate, ensuring equal distribution. Their digestive systems have adapted to extract maximum nutrition from minimal intake, another adaptation to their resource-conscious lifestyle.

Leadership among the Shapers is fluid and contextual. Those with particular sensitivities take the lead when their abilities are most needed. The closest thing to formal governance is the Council of Resonance—seven elders who have demonstrated the ability to perceive and maintain harmony between all the Gardens' systems. These elders rarely issue direct instructions; instead, they adjust subtle aspects of the community's rhythms, like gardeners gently training vines.

The Shapers maintain an oral history tradition centered around what they call "pattern stories"—narratives encoded in the embroidery of their clothing, in the flow paths of their water systems, and in the growth structures themselves. To "read" these stories requires both visual perception and touch, running sensitive fingers along surfaces to detect variations imperceptible to ordinary senses.

Their relationship with technology is unlike anything in conventional human development. They have no metal tools beyond simple copper implements, no written language as outsiders would recognize it, and nothing resembling machinery. Yet they manipulate matter at nearly molecular levels through their biocrystalline growth techniques. Their technology is fundamentally biological and collaborative, requiring not just technical skill but empathic connection with the materials.

The Shapers believe that consciousness exists in all complex systems—not just in animals but in plants, in waterways, in weather patterns, and especially in their Gardens as a whole. Their spiritual practices focus on maintaining respectful communication with these various forms of awareness. They do not worship gods but seek to understand and align with what they call "the patterns that connect," the underlying organizational principles of existence.

They have a complex relationship with the world beyond their valley. They neither seek isolation nor actively engage with outside societies. Visitors are greeted with courteous curiosity rather than suspicion, but few outsiders have the patience to adapt to the Shapers' deliberate rhythms or to learn their subtle forms of communication. Some anthropologists who have studied them believe the Shapers represent not a remnant of ancient human culture but a glimpse of one possible human future—a people who have evolved beyond technological domination of nature into a symbiotic relationship with their environment.

As evening falls across the Ascending Gardens, the Shapers gather in spiral formations around the bioluminescent pools. Their shadows merge with the structures around them as they perform the Evening Alignment—a practice that is part meditation, part communal decision-making, and part maintenance ritual for the entire settlement. Their movements ripple outward, influencing water flow, plant growth, and crystal formation throughout the night. By morning, the Gardens will have shifted subtly, continuing their endless becoming under the careful guidance of these extraordinary stewards.
