# Inhabitants of the Anhydrobe Enclosure

## The Desiccated Ones

The permanent residents of Enclosure #37 are no longer precisely human, though they began as such. Five generations of adaptation have transformed them into what outsiders sometimes call "Dustfolk" or "Salt People," though they refer to themselves simply as Anhydrobes. Their most striking feature is their skin—pale to the point of translucence, with a subtle crystalline sheen that catches the harsh dome lighting. Fine patterns resembling frost fractals trace their exposed skin, most prominently on faces and hands.

Their bodies have been deliberately modified to reduce water dependency. Their eyes are larger than standard human norm, with specialized nictitating membranes that preserve moisture. When they blink, it's a side-to-side motion rather than up-down. Their lips are thinner, reducing evaporation, and they rarely sweat except in extreme circumstances. Internal modifications include enhanced kidneys that reclaim over 99% of bodily fluids and specialized lungs that extract moisture from exhaled breath.

Rather than drinking water directly, they consume hydration tablets—compressed mineral compounds that release water molecules when metabolized by their modified digestive systems. They can go three days without any moisture intake before showing signs of distress, compared to hours for unmodified humans.

Their speech has a distinctive quality—slightly rasping and measured, conserving moisture with each precisely formed word. They've developed a secondary communication system of hand signals to reduce unnecessary talking in the driest work areas.

## The Salt Director

Director Sylvain Qasim, fifth-generation Anhydrobe, oversees Enclosure #37. His office is the highest point in the dome, allowing him to survey operations through reinforced observation panels. His skin shows more pronounced crystalline patterning than most, marking his advanced age (61 standard years, considered elderly among Anhydrobes due to the physical toll of their adaptations).

The Director wears no moisture-retention suit within the enclosure, a point of pride showing his complete adaptation. Instead, he dresses in close-fitting garments woven from synthetic fibers that include filaments of conductive material—managing his body temperature with electric adjustments rather than perspiration. His movements are deliberate and efficient, never wasting energy.

Director Qasim's most treasured possession is a small terrarium containing an ancestor plant—a modified succulent descended from Earth stock that requires only one droplet of actual water per month. He speaks to it when alone, the only sentimentality he permits himself.

## The Mineral Engineers

The technical elite of the enclosure are the Mineral Engineers, who oversee the complex chemical processes that have replaced water-based industrial systems. They're distinguished by the specialized augmentations visible at their temples and along their forearms—crystalline interfaces that allow direct neural connection to the enclosure's equipment.

Their leader, Chief Engineer Talia Merikh, is recognizable by the elaborate latticework of sensor nodes embedded along her spine, visible through her transparent work gear. Her modifications allow her to "feel" the dome's operations as extensions of her nervous system. When a filtration system falters or mineral balance shifts, she experiences it as physical sensation, enabling immediate response.

The Engineers speak a technical dialect incomprehensible to outsiders, filled with chemical formulas and processing terminology. Their recreational activities involve experimental crystallography—growing intricate formations in sealed chambers, competing for the most efficient or beautiful structures.

## The Harvesters

The most physically adapted inhabitants are the Harvesters, who venture onto the salt plains. Beyond their standard Anhydrobe modifications, their lungs have been enhanced with filtration structures that process the caustic external atmosphere. Their eyes have protective polarized lenses grown directly from modified corneal tissue. Their fingers end in hardened points capable of breaking salt formations without tools.

Harvesters move with a distinctive gait—seemingly awkward indoors but perfectly adapted to traversing unstable salt crusts. They tend to be shorter and denser than other Anhydrobes, with thicker skin that resembles weathered stone more than human tissue.

Despite—or because of—their extreme adaptations, Harvesters maintain the strongest connection to pre-Desiccation culture. They gather in their barracks during rest periods to sing modified versions of ancient Earth songs, their voices harmonizing in ways that reflect the peculiar acoustics of the salt plains. These songs often describe water in reverential terms—oceans, rivers, and rain taking on mythological significance.

## The Anhydrobe Children

Perhaps most remarkable are the enclosure's children. Born into dryness, they've never known any other environment. Their play involves complex games with salt crystals and mineral powders. They build elaborate sculptures from the colored extraction byproducts, developing an intuitive understanding of structural principles that later serves them in technical roles.

Their education emphasizes resource conservation above all else. They learn to write using electromagnetic tablets rather than physical materials. Physical education focuses on movements that minimize moisture loss. They're taught to measure their own hydration levels through meditative techniques, developing awareness of their bodies' needs from their earliest years.

The children speak more than adults do, not having fully internalized moisture conservation habits. Their voices are higher and clearer, lacking the distinctive rasp of adult Anhydrobes. They're also more curious about the Outside and the mythical "wet world" their ancestors came from.

## The Synthetics

Complementing the human population are the Semi-Autonomous Maintenance Units—humanoid robotics that handle the most desiccating environments within the enclosure. These aren't true AI, but sophisticated machines with adaptive programming that learn from their human operators.

The SAMUs have developed idiosyncrasies over generations of learning. They exhibit preferences for certain tools, use distinctive movement patterns, and have accumulated a library of problem-solving approaches that weren't in their original programming. The oldest units are treated with a respect approaching personhood, their accumulated experience considered invaluable.

Their design mimics Anhydrobe physiology with crystalline sensor arrays instead of eyes and articulated digits optimized for handling mineral materials. They communicate through light patterns that flash across their faceplates—a system originally designed for status updates that has evolved into a complex language the human residents can interpret intuitively.

## The Adapted Fauna

The modified mice aren't the only engineered fauna. The ecosystem includes silicate beetles that process waste materials, crystalline moths that assist with pollination in the terraria, and the prized salt lizards—valuable companions that detect dangerous mineral compositions through changes in their skin coloration.

Most unique are the "dust fish"—organisms engineered from marine ancestry that "swim" through fine particulate matter in the storage silos, preventing dangerous compaction while consuming potentially harmful bacteria. They retain gill-like structures, though these now filter particles rather than water.

Together, these inhabitants have created a society as precisely calibrated as the machinery they tend—a human ecosystem adapted to conditions their ancestors would have found impossibly hostile, finding meaning and even beauty in a world defined by absence rather than abundance.
