# The Dwellers of Hollow Thunder

The Hollow Thunder belongs to the Ekkos, though they would never claim to own it. They are creatures of perfect symmetry—no larger than a human hand when fully grown—with bodies that seem to shift between solid and translucent depending on how the light catches them. Their skin has the quality of living opal, refracting the limited light into subtle rainbows that ripple across their surface with each movement.

The Ekkos have no eyes as we understand them. Instead, their entire bodies function as sensory organs, particularly attuned to vibration. The hollow's acoustic properties are not accidental—generations of Ekkos have subtly shaped this chamber over centuries, carefully cultivating the resonant frequencies that sustain their community. The deep thrumming that humans feel through their feet is, to the Ekkos, a complex language, a constantly evolving conversation between the mountain, the water, and themselves.

Their society is structured around seven distinct forms, each specialized for different roles. The Collectors are smallest, with delicate finger-like appendages that gather specific minerals from the water. Shapers use secretions to modify the flow patterns of water, directing it to nourish their cultivated moss gardens or to create new acoustic chambers. Harmonizers, distinguished by their elongated resonating chambers, produce the tones that maintain the hollow's vibrational balance. Tenders oversee the relationships with the hollow's other creatures, particularly the iridescent beetles, which the Ekkos have developed a complex symbiotic relationship with over thousands of generations.

The concentric circles of pebbles are not merely decorative—they form a calendar, library, and communication device. The Ekkos rearrange these stones in patterns that record seasonal changes, astronomical events, and community histories. When placed in precise configurations and activated by the right water flow, these stone arrangements can produce specific resonances that stimulate community activities from breeding cycles to collective meditation.

Ekkos don't reproduce individually but collectively. During rare alignments when light enters the hollow at precisely the right angle, the entire community gathers in the deepest pool. There, they synchronize their vibrations until their bodies partially dissolve and intermingle. From this shared solution, new Ekkos gradually coalesce, embodying the collective memory and characteristics of the community.

They perceive time differently than most creatures. An Ekko can live for centuries, spending decades in contemplative stillness that outsiders might mistake for dormancy. During these periods, they commune deeply with the mountain itself, their consciousness diffusing through the stone in ways that blur the boundaries between individual and environment.

The Ekkos maintain careful balance in their ecosystem. They cultivate the electric blue and violet flowers, which produce compounds that regulate the hollow's microbial balance. They orchestrate controlled population fluctuations among the insects and amphibians to maintain equilibrium. The hand-sized dragonflies serve as their messengers to similar communities in other hollows scattered throughout the mountain range.

Visitors rarely notice the Ekkos themselves, instead experiencing only the effects of their presence—that sense of orchestrated harmony, the feeling that the chaos of sound and movement follows some underlying pattern just beyond human comprehension. Those few who do glimpse an Ekko often mistake them for tricks of light on water, or perhaps unusual formations of the cavern's minerals.

The Ekkos are neither alarmed nor particularly interested in human visitors. They've witnessed many species come and go over their long existence. They simply incorporate the new vibrations into their ongoing symphony, adding another layer to the complex resonance that is both their voice and their home.
