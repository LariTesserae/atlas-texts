# The Scaffold-Weavers

The Scaffold-Weavers are never seen, yet their presence permeates every fiber of this suspended realm. They move within the interstitial spaces of perception—visible only through the corner of one's eye, or in the moment between wakefulness and sleep.

These beings are neither fully corporeal nor entirely spectral. Their bodies, when glimpsed fleetingly, appear as elongated silhouettes composed of interwoven strands that mimic the scaffold's own structure. Limbs extend and contract like telescoping bamboo, allowing them to navigate the lattice with silent efficiency. Their forms suggest humanity but deviate in crucial ways—too many joints in their arms, fingers that split into delicate manipulators, torsos that can compress to pass through narrow gaps in the framework.

The Weavers communicate through vibrations transmitted through the scaffold itself. Their language consists of precise taps, stretches, and tensions applied to the structure—conversations conducted through the medium of the world itself. To the unacquainted ear, these communications are indistinguishable from the natural creaks and groans of the lattice.

Their society operates in cycles rather than hierarchies. Each Weaver shifts roles according to patterns dictated by the subtle changes in the amber light. Some gather the special fibers from cultivated lichens, others ferment adhesives in the hanging pottery, while still others extend the scaffold according to designs passed down through generations of structural vibrations.

The Scaffold-Weavers possess extraordinary patience. A single section of lattice might take decades to complete, each strut placed according to calculations that account for air currents, weight distribution, and the mysterious aesthetic principles they hold sacred. They experience time differently than most beings, perceiving the scaffold's gradual expansion as a form of meditation rather than labor.

Their relationship with the void below is complex. The Weavers revere it not as an emptiness to be feared but as the source of the warm currents that sustain their world. Their creation myths speak of ancestors who once lived in the depths but ascended to build a realm closer to the ambient light. They maintain ceremonial openings in the scaffold—perfectly geometric gaps through which offerings of woven figures are periodically lowered.

The Scaffold-Weavers consume very little. Their primary sustenance comes from the specialized succulents they cultivate, extracting a nutrient-rich sap that they store in those spiral-etched vessels. They supplement this diet with microscopic organisms they harvest from the air currents rising from below, filtering them through elaborate woven nets.

Their lifecycle remains largely mysterious. Young Weavers are rarely glimpsed, though occasionally new, smaller figures join the perpetual construction effort. The elders, distinguished by the complex patterning of their woven forms, eventually choose to descend through the ceremonial openings into the void below—whether this represents death or transformation remains unknown.

The Scaffold-Weavers harbor no hostility toward visitors, but neither do they seek interaction. They recede further into the boundaries of perception when directly pursued, yet may leave small woven tokens or newly constructed platforms near those who respect their space and rhythms. To earn their trust requires adopting their timescale—the willingness to sit motionless for days, attuning oneself to the subtle language of the creaking scaffold.

In their own understanding, they are not building a world. They are growing one, strand by careful strand, into the infinity above the void.
