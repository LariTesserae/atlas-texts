# The Basket-Tenders

The primary inhabitants of the Undershelves are the translucent amphibians—called Vividians by those few outsiders who have named them, though they have their own form of identification, a subtle biochemical signature unique to each individual that other Vividians can sense with receptors along their flanks.

The smallest Vividians (fingertip-sized) are the newly-emerged, born from gelatinous egg sacs carefully positioned where three water channels intersect. These tiny beings begin work immediately upon hatching, their translucent bodies showing complex internal structures that pulse with amber bioluminescence. Their skin is permeable, constantly absorbing moisture from the environment, giving them a glistening appearance. Their six legs end in delicate filaments that can splice into the woven reeds for repairs or split into tool-like appendages for more detailed work.

As they grow, Vividians develop specialized organs visible through their clear bodies—not just for respiration and digestion, but mysterious structures that appear to sense the subtle rhythms of the water drops. Their heads widen and flatten, with compound eyes that perceive spectra invisible to humans, allowing them to see structural weaknesses in the baskets before they form.

The eldest and largest Vividians (forearm-length) are the Pattern-Keepers. Their bodies are segmented like shrimp but flexible like salamanders, with elongated cranial chambers housing complex neural structures. They move deliberately across the baskets, sometimes remaining motionless for days, their internal light pulsing in sequences that communicate with younger workers. When they move, others clear paths for them. Their touch on damaged sections of basketry appears almost ceremonial, as they trace patterns that younger workers then implement.

All Vividians secrete a clear fluid from glands beneath their throats—this substance not only waterproofs the reeds but adds structural integrity. They apply it methodically, weaving it into the baskets in patterns that match the water channels, creating an integrated system that appears to serve purposes beyond mere structural maintenance.

The creatures operate in perfect coordination without apparent communication. They respond to disturbances with neither aggression nor fear, simply adjusting their activities to restore equilibrium. When a visitor steps onto a basket, nearby Vividians pause momentarily, their internal lights pulsing rapidly as they assess the disruption, then continue their work with subtle alterations that accommodate the new weight distribution.

Occasionally, in the largest cathedral-spaces, the Pattern-Keepers gather in concentric circles. They position themselves with mathematical precision, their bodies forming living mandalas across multiple basket levels. During these gatherings, their bioluminescence synchronizes into complex, shifting patterns that illuminate the space with unprecedented brightness. These events last precisely 13 hours, after which the Pattern-Keepers disperse to different sectors of the Undershelves.

The relationship between the Vividians and their woven world appears symbiotic yet transcends typical ecological relationships. They are not merely inhabitants but components of the system itself. The moisture they absorb through their skin is processed and released into the air, contributing to the constant humidity. Their bodies contain specialized organs that filter the water, removing impurities and maintaining its perfect circulation through the reed channels.

Most curiously, when they die—which appears to happen rarely and perhaps voluntarily—Pattern-Keepers position themselves at specific junctions where multiple baskets connect. Their bodies gradually lose opacity until they appear to be made entirely of light. Then they dissolve, their substance absorbed directly into the basket structure, strengthening junctions and adding new patterns to the weave. In these locations, the luminescent moss grows more vibrantly, suggesting that something of the Vividian's essence remains.

Whether the Vividians created the Undershelves or were created by them remains unknown. Their activities suggest both maintenance and ongoing creation—as if the entire realm is both finished and eternally in process, each basket simultaneously ancient artifact and living tissue. The Vividians themselves offer no explanation, continuing their work with the same deliberate care whether observed or not, maintaining their woven world in timeless equilibrium.
