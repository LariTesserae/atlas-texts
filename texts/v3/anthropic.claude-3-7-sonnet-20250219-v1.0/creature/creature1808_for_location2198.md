# The Custodian of the Well

The Well of Embers is tended by a being that calls itself Ashgant, though this name is seldom spoken aloud. Ashgant's form is difficult to comprehend at first glance—a figure composed of countless interlocking geometric shapes that seem to fold in on themselves, creating an entity that stands roughly the height of a human but possesses no fixed dimensions.

Ashgant's body gleams with the same copper-black luster as the well itself, as if cut from the same ancient stone. Its surface is crisscrossed with fine lines that glow with a subdued amber light, pulsing in slow, deliberate rhythms that match the well's exhalations. These lines form patterns similar to those etched in the mineral dust surrounding the well, suggesting some deeper connection between keeper and kept.

Ashgant moves with remarkable deliberateness—each motion precise and economical. It can remain motionless for days at a time, becoming so still that visitors might mistake it for another obelisk until it shifts position. When it does move, it seems to glide across the mineral plain without disturbing the dust patterns, leaving no footprints despite its apparent weight and solidity.

The Custodian communicates rarely and enigmatically. It does not speak in words but in impressions—sensations of warmth or coolness that form thoughts in the mind of whoever it addresses. These communications carry emotional tones that suggest Ashgant possesses a profound patience born from existing across timescales incomprehensible to most beings. When it chooses to communicate, the nearby Scintillae gather around it, their blue glow intensifying as if amplifying its silent voice.

Ashgant's purpose seems to be maintenance of the delicate balance within the Well of Embers. At irregular intervals, it approaches the well and extends a limb that unfolds into increasingly complex geometries before dipping into the darkness below. During these moments, the humming of the obelisks synchronizes into a single tone, and the air becomes charged with potential. After such interactions, the well's exhalations often change in subtle ways—sometimes becoming warmer, sometimes carrying new mineral scents.

The Custodian appears to have a particular relationship with the Scintillae. It collects the microscopic etchings they leave behind, harvesting them with geometric appendages that sweep just above the dust. These patterns are then carried to one of the seven obelisks where they're pressed against the shifting surface, seeming to upload information. In return, Ashgant occasionally releases small crystalline structures that the Scintillae eagerly consume, their bodies glowing more intensely afterward.

Visitors who remain respectful may eventually witness Ashgant's most remarkable behavior. During rare astronomical alignments when certain stars appear directly above the well, the Custodian performs what can only be described as a ritual. It positions itself at the well's edge and reconfigures its form into a complex arrangement that casts precisely shaped shadows across the circuit-like patterns in the dust. As this happens, the well's exhalations take on color—wisps of blue, amber, and violet that rise and dance before dissipating. The Scintillae gather in concentric circles, pulsing in unison.

In the presence of Ashgant, time feels different—stretched and compressed simultaneously. Some visitors report experiencing memories that couldn't possibly be their own—glimpses of the mineral plain when it was a vast sea, or when great structures stood where now only dust remains. Others describe a sensation of being gently examined, as if Ashgant is collecting their experiences just as it collects the etchings of the Scintillae.

Ashgant seems neither to welcome nor discourage visitors. It simply acknowledges their presence with the same patient attention it gives to every aspect of its domain. Those who approach with respect are permitted to observe; those who disturb the patterns or threaten the well find themselves gently but firmly guided away by invisible forces that emanate from the obelisks.

The Custodian of the Well of Embers may be the last of its kind, or perhaps the first of something new. Either way, it continues its quiet work, maintaining the rhythms of a place that operates on a timescale where centuries pass like moments, preserving whatever ancient purpose the well serves in the greater order of things.
