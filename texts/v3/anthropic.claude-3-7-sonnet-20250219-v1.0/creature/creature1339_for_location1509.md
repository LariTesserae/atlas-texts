# The Inhabitants of Lacuna

The beings who call the Suspended Metropolis home defy conventional categorization as thoroughly as their environment defies physics. They are known collectively as the Lacunae, though this simple term masks their diverse nature.

Most numerous are the Transitionals—humanoid figures whose bodies exist in a permanent state of flux between matter and potentiality. Their skin shifts like liquid mercury, occasionally revealing glimpses of crystalline skeletal structures beneath. Their features are suggestion rather than definition; eyes that might be light sources or windows to elsewhere, mouths that reconfigure when speaking different phonemes. They don't exactly walk but flow through space, leaving faint luminescent trails that dissipate moments later.

The Transitionals organize their society around Probability Guilds, collectives that specialize in different methods of reality manipulation. The most prestigious is the Architectural Guild, whose members can extend their consciousness into unformed districts, their thoughts becoming blueprint-seeds for new structures. They are recognizable by the geometric patterns that constantly orbit their heads like living diadems, each shape representing a mastered theorem of space-folding.

Less numerous but more striking are the Constants—beings who appear jarringly solid and unchanging amid Lacuna's fluidity. Their bodies remain perfectly stable, a paradoxical anchor in the shifting metropolis. This immutability comes at tremendous metabolic cost; they must consume vast quantities of "raw possibility" harvested from the city's edges. Constants are valued as navigators and anchors during reality storms, their unwavering presence creating pockets of stability. Their eyes reflect nothing, as if their gaze absorbs rather than receives light.

Living in the industrial sectors are the Composite beings—entities that began as conventional lifeforms but have gradually incorporated machinery, crystalline growths, and fragments of semi-formed matter into their bodies. Some are barely recognizable as once-human, having become walking collages of organic tissue, mechanical components, and partially-manifested possibility. They speak in harmonics that machinery can understand, their words carrying executable code that machines respond to instinctively.

The most enigmatic inhabitants are the Lacunae Proper—beings that appear to be absences rather than presences. They manifest as person-shaped voids moving through the city, visible only because they displace the fluctuating reality around them. The Lacunae Proper are thought to be the original inhabitants, entities that predate the metropolis itself. They communicate through thought-impressions rather than language, leaving faint emotional residues in places they've recently occupied. When they gather in numbers, reality grows exceptionally thin, sometimes revealing glimpses of whatever lies beyond Lacuna's dimensional bubble.

Children born in Lacuna develop unique adaptations from birth. Known as the Nascent, these young ones can perceive quantum probabilities as visible auras, their minds naturally interpreting the underlying math of reality as intuitive patterns. They play games incomprehensible to newcomers, involving the manipulation of probability fields and the creation of temporary pocket dimensions. As they mature, most Nascent lose this perception, though some retain it and become the city's most skilled reality engineers.

The phase beetles and drift whales are not merely fauna but possess forms of intelligence unique to Lacuna. The beetles organize into vast colonial intelligences, their phase-shifting synchronized to process information across multiple dimensions simultaneously. Residents have learned to interpret their swarming patterns as predictive models of upcoming reality fluctuations.

The drift whales, meanwhile, are believed to be conscious manifestations of the dimensional pocket itself—aspects of Lacuna that have developed self-awareness. When they vocalize, their calls temporarily solidify reality around them, creating safe harbors during dimensional storms. Some Transitionals have developed methods of communication with these entities, entering trance states where their minds can briefly interface with the whales' alien consciousness.

Newcomers to Lacuna gradually transform, their biology and consciousness adapting to the city's peculiar physics. This metamorphosis begins with subtle perceptual shifts—seeing sound, tasting light—before progressing to physical changes. Many fight this transformation, using technology to maintain their original form, while others embrace the change, accelerating it through rituals performed at reality looms.

A unique social class exists among the inhabitants: the Remembered. These are beings who were once fully formed but have begun reverting to potential—their bodies becoming increasingly translucent, their memories and personalities dispersing. They are treated with profound respect, as they're believed to be recycling their constituent existence back into Lacuna's fabric. In the final stages, a Remembered becomes little more than a shimmering outline before dissolving entirely, contributing their organized pattern back to the city's ever-evolving blueprint.

Governing this impossible society is not a conventional power structure but a phenomenon called the Consensus—a distributed consciousness that emerges from the collective thoughts of all inhabitants. The Consensus manifests physically in Central Lacuna as a shifting architecture that reconfigures to represent current social priorities and concerns. Decisions aren't voted upon but rather emerge as probability gradients that the city itself enforces through subtle manipulations of local reality.

Among all these inhabitants moves a figure known only as the Cartographer—neither Transitional nor Constant nor any recognized type. They appear differently to each observer, always carrying instruments for measuring and recording. The Cartographer is said to be documenting Lacuna not as a place but as an ongoing event—a momentary stability in a greater flux, a city perpetually becoming but never quite being.
