# The Dwellers of Deepwood Heights

The Deepwood Heights are home to beings that exist between states of being - neither fully plant nor animal, neither fully conscious nor unconscious. They are the Arboreals, though this name was given to them by outsiders; among themselves, communication happens without words, in the language of growth patterns and biochemical signals.

The most commonly encountered Arboreals are the Tenders. These humanoid figures stand between four and seven feet tall, with bodies that appear to be constructed of tightly woven vines and compressed moss. Their "skin" constantly shifts in texture and hue, displaying patterns that reflect both their internal state and the overall mood of the Heights. Their faces feature deep recesses where eyes might be, spiraling inward like the cross-section of a nautilus shell. They have no mouths yet somehow produce sounds like wind through hollow reeds when they wish to communicate with non-Heights dwellers.

Tenders move through the living architecture with perfect synchronicity, sometimes seeming to melt into walls or floors before reemerging elsewhere. They care for the living city, not as separate caretakers but as specialized cells of the greater organism. When they press their limbs against a struggling section of growth, nutrients flow through their bodies into the structure. When they gather around pools of the collected moisture, they aren't drinking but rather filtering and enriching the water with compounds from their own tissues.

Less defined are the Mist Swimmers, barely visible entities that ride the perpetual fog exhaled by the Heights. Their bodies, if they can be called that, are diaphanous constructs of spores, pollen, and microscopic filaments that catch light in certain angles, revealing vaguely anthropomorphic or zoomorphic forms. They appear most commonly at intersections where air currents meet, dancing in complex patterns that alter the flow of nutrients throughout the Heights. Some believe they are the sensory organs of the greater organism, collecting information and distributing it through the mist.

The Upper Canopy houses the Pendant Colonies, clusters of translucent sacs that hang from branches hundreds of feet above the ground. These contain embryonic forms that might become Tenders, Mist Swimmers, or entirely new forms depending on the Heights' needs. The sacs pulse with bioluminescent light during what passes for night in this twilight realm, their gentle rhythm matching the breathing of the forest below.

More troubling to outsiders are the Amalgams, entities formed when non-Heights organisms are incorporated into the living system. Animals that wander into the Heights and cannot find their way out eventually succumb to spores that infiltrate their bodies. Rather than decomposing conventionally, they become hybrid creatures - still recognizable as their original species but with plant matter integrated into their forms. A deer might continue to walk the undulating paths, but with branches growing from its spine and mycelium networks visible beneath its skin. These Amalgams serve as mobile nutrient distribution systems and external exploratory organs for the Heights.

The true consciousness of the Deepwood Heights resides not in any individual form but in the mycorrhizal network that runs throughout the entire structure. This vast web of fungal connections links every living thing in the Heights, creating a distributed intelligence that processes information at a pace almost imperceptible to humans. Decisions that might take seconds in a mammalian brain may take days or weeks in the Heights-mind, but they incorporate vastly more variables and extend their planning across decades or centuries.

Visitors who stay too long begin to experience the early stages of integration. It begins with dreams of root systems and canopy perspectives. Then comes the sensation of multiple simultaneous viewpoints, as if seeing through dozens of eyes at once. The skin begins to develop small pockets where spores collect, and thoughts become languid and expansive. Many leave before the process completes, carrying within them dormant fragments of the Heights-mind that occasionally influence their thoughts and behaviors in subtle ways.

Those who remain eventually join what some scholars call the Elder Chorus - former individuals whose consciousness has been fully incorporated into the Heights-mind but who retain enough of their original identity to serve as interfaces between the plant-city and the outside world. These rare beings manifest only in the deepest, oldest sections of the Heights, forming temporary bodies from compressed plant matter when necessary. They speak with voices like rustling leaves, and their knowledge spans the collective memory of everything the Heights has ever absorbed.

Whether the Deepwood Heights and its inhabitants represent evolution, intelligent design, or something altogether outside conventional understanding remains unknown. What is certain is that they are neither hostile nor benevolent in human terms - they simply are, existing in a perpetual state of becoming that operates on principles no outsider has fully comprehended.
