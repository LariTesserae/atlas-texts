# The Tenders of the Canopy Reactor

The Alti-Mycelix inhabit this extraordinary convergence of nature and machinery, beings as difficult to classify as the environment they maintain. They are neither fully human nor purely fungal, but something that has evolved in symbiosis with the plant-machine hybrid over generations.

Standing approximately four feet tall, their bodies consist of a dense mycelial network that has taken humanoid form—a torso with four limbs and a bulbous head-like structure. Their "skin" is a living tapestry of pale yellow-green filaments that constantly shift and reconnect, giving them a subtle but perpetual motion even when still. Deep within their semi-transparent forms, one can occasionally glimpse the circulation of the same luminous fluids that the reactor processes—electric blue, pulsing magenta, vibrating gold—moving through vein-like channels.

They have no eyes as we understand them, but sensory clusters of specialized fungi across their bodies allow them to perceive their environment through chemical signals, temperature variations, and vibrations. Where a face might be expected, each has a different arrangement of these sensory organs, creating unique patterns that serve as identifiers among their kind. These patterns shift subtly with their moods and intentions.

The Alti-Mycelix move deliberately through the dense vegetation, their feet never quite touching any surface but rather extending filaments that temporarily bond with whatever they traverse. They navigate the canopy reactor with intimate familiarity, tending to its needs with methodical care. Their arms can extend, narrow, or split into multiple tendrils when precision work is required.

Communication between them occurs without sound—unnecessary in the thunderous environment. Instead, they release complex spore signatures that carry emotional content and basic information, visible as brief, colorful puffs that dissipate quickly in the humid air. For more complex communication, they connect physically, temporarily merging portions of their mycelial networks to share information directly.

The Alti-Mycelix appear to live in service to the reactor. They monitor its outputs, adjust valves both mechanical and organic, and harvest the crystal vials of concentrated essence. Some tend to the boundaries of their domain, encouraging certain plants to grow while pruning others, maintaining the perfect balance of the ecosystem. Others focus on the mechanical components, using secreted enzymes to clean metal parts or encourage specific types of corrosion that somehow enhance the system's function.

Their relationship with the large iridescent insects is symbiotic. These insects—called Courier Flies by a previous human visitor in that moldering notebook—are semi-domesticated. The Alti-Mycelix cultivate them from eggs, feeding them specialized secretions until they mature. The Couriers serve as their connection to whatever exists beyond this hidden chamber, carrying both the precious vials and, occasionally, materials that cannot be produced within the reactor itself.

The oldest of the Alti-Mycelix, distinguished by a more complex internal circulation system and a crown-like formation of golden filaments, guards a small collection of human artifacts. Besides the notebook and goggles visible to newcomers, there is a hidden cache containing old photographs, mechanical tools modified for their use, and curious journals written in a script that combines human language with fungal growth patterns pressed into the paper. These suggest that the Alti-Mycelix may have originated from humans who became gradually transformed by long exposure to the reactor's emissions and the unique fungal species that thrive here.

New Alti-Mycelix emerge rarely. When the reactor reaches certain production cycles, aligned with celestial patterns noted meticulously in their records, they gather to perform a quiet ceremony. They combine specific proportions of the reactor's luminous essences with their own spores in a special crystalline basin. From this mixture, over months, a new being slowly forms, absorbing knowledge through direct mycelial connection with its elders.

They appear to have no concept of death as humans understand it. When an Alti-Mycelix grows too old to maintain its cohesive form, it gradually disperses its consciousness back into the greater mycelial network that permeates the entire reactor. Their memories and experiences become part of the living archive that guides the younger generations.

Their purpose—and by extension, the reactor's—remains their most closely guarded secret. Whatever mission drives their endless work in this hidden industrial ecosystem, they pursue it with unwavering dedication, moving to the rhythms of a plan conceived perhaps centuries ago, their fungal patience allowing them to think in timeframes that would be incomprehensible to human minds.
