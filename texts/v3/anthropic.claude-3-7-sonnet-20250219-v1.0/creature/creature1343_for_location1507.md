# The People of Thendrin

The Thendrinites are not quite what outsiders expect. They are tall and willowy, with a slight forward stoop that comes from generations navigating low-ceilinged walkways and ducking under the perpetual drip from overhead structures. Their skin possesses a peculiar translucence—not the pallor of those starved of sunlight, but a deliberate adaptation. Beneath their skin, particularly visible at wrists and temples, runs a network of faintly luminous veins that glow more intensely during times of exertion or emotion, a biological feature they share with the amphibians below their city.

Thendrinites' eyes are large and dark-adapted, with pupils that rarely contract fully, giving them an appearance of perpetual wonder or alarm. The whites of their eyes have a slight bluish tinge. Their hair grows thick and naturally water-resistant, often worn long and elaborately braided with small brass weights that prevent it from rising in the omnipresent humidity. Many incorporate live phosphorescent mosses into these braids, creating halos of soft light that follow them through the gloom.

They speak a language that accommodates the acoustic peculiarities of their environment—rich in consonants that carry over the background noise of rushing water, with vowels reserved for intimate conversations in close quarters. Their vocal cords have developed additional tissue folds that allow them to produce harmonics that cut through the city's perpetual symphony. To visitors, this gives their speech a musical, almost avian quality.

The Thendrinite society operates on a fluid hierarchy that mirrors the water beneath them. Leadership positions circulate seasonally among the twelve ancient families who claim descent from the city's founders. These Water Stewards, as they're called, wear elaborately carved wooden masks representing stylized water creatures during their tenure. Authority ebbs and flows between families based on the specific challenges facing the city—those with expertise in hydraulic engineering might lead during structural renovations, while those skilled in aquaculture take precedence during lean seasons.

Children in Thendrin learn to swim before they walk. By age five, they navigate the underwater passages beneath the city as confidently as the walkways above. Their education centers on water management, aquatic agriculture, and the maintenance of the ancient mechanisms that keep the city functioning. Schools are conducted on large floating platforms that drift slowly across the plateau, ensuring children become intimately familiar with every sector of their watery domain.

The city's gradual depopulation stems from a phenomenon the residents call the "Deepening"—a spiritual calling that comes to some Thendrinites in middle age. Those who experience it become increasingly drawn to the waters below, spending more time swimming beneath the city than walking above it. Eventually, they establish small colonies in the deeper sections of the plateau, living in underwater chambers connected by flooded tunnels, emerging to the surface only occasionally. Their bodies continue adapting, developing more pronounced webbing between fingers and toes, and their luminescent veins grow brighter. The oldest of these "Deep Dwellers" are rumored to have developed gills, though few surface residents have seen them in decades.

Religious practices in Thendrin revolve around water in all its forms. They recognize three divine aspects: The Surface (representing consciousness and civilization), The Depth (representing mystery and transformation), and The Cycle (representing renewal and connection). Their temples are circular structures built around perfectly still pools where the surface tension is maintained with reverent care. Priests and priestesses are chosen for their ability to enter meditative states while floating motionless on these sacred waters, supposedly receiving visions from the depths.

The Thendrinites measure wealth not in gold but in brass, which resists corrosion from the constant moisture. More valuable still are the secrets of waterproofing—family recipes for oils and waxes passed down through generations, each slightly different and jealously guarded. The most prosperous citizens are the Dry-Keepers, whose homes somehow remain completely free of moisture inside, an engineering feat considered near-miraculous.

Despite the city's apparent technological stagnation, innovation does occur—but it's focused inward rather than outward. The Thendrinites continuously refine their hydraulic systems, developing increasingly sophisticated methods of filtering water, generating power from its flow, and predicting the subtle shifts in the plateau's water levels. Their mechanical aptitude is remarkable, but always in service to maintenance rather than expansion.

Visitors often miss the vibrant social life of Thendrin because it happens in unexpected places. The seemingly abandoned structures often house elaborate underwater gathering spaces, accessed through hidden trapdoors in floors. Here, in chambers half-filled with water, residents gather for what they call "Suspension"—social events where participants float on their backs while conversing, sharing food from floating platters, and watching performances by swimmers who dance beneath the surface, their movements illuminated by their glowing veins and the strategic placement of bioluminescent organisms.

The Thendrinites know they are dwindling, but they do not consider this a tragedy. They see themselves not as a failing civilization but as one evolving into something new—gradually returning to the waters from which all life emerged, completing a cycle begun by their ancestors who first built platforms above the plateau. The city is not dying; it is transforming, just as they are. In their philosophy, nothing truly disappears in Thendrin—it merely changes state, like water itself.
