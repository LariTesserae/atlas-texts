# The Copper-Skinned Nomads

The Vessel's Interior is home to the Lithomembranes, a species that exists in symbiosis with their habitat. They stand roughly human height but with elongated limbs that feature an extra joint in both arms and legs, allowing them to grip and traverse the vessel's inconsistent surfaces with remarkable agility.

Their most striking feature is their skin—a flexible, semi-metallic copper membrane that mirrors the vessel's shell. This skin shifts between solid and semi-liquid states, allowing them to absorb trace elements directly from their environment. When they rest or meditate, intricate circuit-like patterns emerge on their skin, echoing the etchings of their world, pulsing with the same mysterious light.

The Lithomembranes have no permanent settlements. Instead, they live as nomadic clans, following the water pathways that defy conventional physics. They've developed a profound understanding of the vessel's inconsistent geometries, navigating by a sense that perceives the mathematical irregularities as clearly as humans see color. Their language has sixteen different words for the sensation of gravity shifting beneath one's feet.

These beings have three compound eyes arranged in a triangle pattern, capable of perceiving spectrums beyond conventional light. Their vision allows them to see the subtle transformations in the vessel's patterns before they occur, giving them precious moments to adjust their routes and habitations.

They communicate through dual methods: a spoken language of harmonic tones that resonates with the vessel's own "singing," and a gestural language performed by manipulating the circuit patterns on their skin. Their most sacred ceremonies involve synchronized skin-pattern shifts that, when performed by a clan together, can temporarily influence the vessel's internal weather.

The Lithomembranes sustain themselves primarily through a symbiotic relationship with the translucent-leaved vines. They don't consume the plants but rather exchange essential minerals with them through a process resembling both communion and photosynthesis. During this exchange, both Lithomembrane and plant glow with copper light.

Their society is non-hierarchical but highly specialized. Each clan member develops a unique resonance with specific aspects of the vessel—some can predict the formation of internal weather systems, others can sense shifts in the vessel's trajectory through not-quite-space, while some develop bonds with the flying ray creatures, riding them to access distant points on the shell.

They have no concept of death as humans understand it. When a Lithomembrane reaches the end of their physical existence, they enter a meditative state during which their copper skin gradually merges with the vessel's shell. The patterns of their skin become permanent etchings on the walls, preserving their consciousness as part of the vessel's memory. These patterns are visited by clan members who communicate with their ancestors through skin-contact, absorbing their experiences directly.

The Lithomembranes are aware they are within something mobile and purposeful. Their mythology speaks of the "Great Migration"—a journey through the "Beneath-Space" toward a destination they call the "Convergence." They believe the vessel is not merely their home but their collective ancestor, carrying them across vast gulfs toward a reunion with other vessels and other forms of existence currently beyond comprehension.
