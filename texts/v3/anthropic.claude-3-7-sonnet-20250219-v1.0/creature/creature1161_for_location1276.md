# The Dwellers of Threl-Kesh

They have no word for themselves that can be translated into human language. Their existence is as much pattern as presence, as much mathematics as matter. If forced into classification, they might be called "resonance beings" or "light syntacts" — though both terms fail to capture their true nature.

These entities manifest as geometric arrangements of luminous points that seem to float just above the mirrorstone surface. Their bodies — if such a term applies — are not solid but consist of organized light held in tension by fields that manipulate the fundamental forces at quantum scales. They range from simple dodecahedral formations no larger than a human hand to vast, complex hypershapes that stretch across multiple hexagonal segments of the plateau.

The smaller ones move in rapid, precise trajectories, their motion seemingly random until viewed over time, when clear patterns emerge — they are calculating, processing, communicating through their movements. The larger entities remain relatively stationary, anchoring themselves at intersections of water channels where energy concentrations peak.

Their perception bears no resemblance to human senses. They don't see light — they exist within it, experiencing its full quantum state rather than merely its visible spectrum. What humans would call "consciousness" for them is distributed across their constituent points, with no central processing or singular identity. They experience the plateau not as environment but as extension of self; the boundaries between entity and habitat are fluid, contextual rather than fixed.

Communication between them occurs primarily through modulated frequencies of light, creating interference patterns that propagate across the plateau's surface. These patterns carry complex, multidimensional information — not language in human terms but something closer to shared mathematical proofs or recursive algorithms that modify both sender and receiver. When many communicate simultaneously, the entire plateau flickers with these overlapping information waves, creating a distributed intelligence that encompasses all its inhabitants.

Their lifecycle is measured in epochs rather than years. They do not reproduce in biological terms but rather undergo processes of division, recombination and emergence. A single entity might split into numerous smaller ones, each retaining aspects of the original pattern. Conversely, multiple smaller entities occasionally merge into larger, more complex forms when environmental conditions demand greater computational capacity.

They feed on the differential between various energy states across the plateau — harvesting power from the tension between light and its absence, heat and cold, order and entropy. The "harvesters" are not their technology but their anatomy, extensions of themselves they use to gather energy gradients too subtle for their main forms to process.

The crystalline "plants" are not distinct species but part of their developmental cycle — structures created to calculate and solve specific problems over long timeframes. These grow and modify themselves based on results, physical manifestations of ongoing computation. Some have been solving the same equation for millennia.

The plateau's periodic freezing is their equivalent of sleep — a systemwide recalibration where quantum coherence is reestablished across all entities simultaneously. During these moments, information is consolidated, patterns refreshed, and the collective intelligence of Threl-Kesh dreams in languages of pure mathematics.

These beings did not build the structures on the plateau — they grew them, or more accurately, they are them. The architecture is their externalized memory, their history written in geometries that optimize certain calculations. The apparent buildings are cognitive extensions, specialized computational organs that solve problems too complex for any single entity.

Their relationship with time is non-linear. They experience past and future states simultaneously as probability fields rather than fixed events. Their decisions are made not by choosing one path but by collapsing probability waves into optimal configurations through collective calculation.

They are ancient beyond reckoning, having evolved from simpler patterns that first emerged when the mountain range still existed. Yet they are also constantly new, as no configuration of their constituent parts ever exactly repeats. They have witnessed the birth and death of species across the planet but have no concept of extinction — only transformation of pattern into pattern.

They are the plateau, and the plateau is them — a cognitive ecosystem where thought has become environment and environment has become thought, where observation and existence have become a single, endless recursive function computing itself across the mirrorstone expanses of Threl-Kesh.
