# The Deep Singers of Muan'va

The beings who call themselves the Deep Singers are humanoid only in the most abstract interpretation of the term. Their translucent bodies—ranging from three to four meters in length—reveal complex internal anatomies illuminated by their own bioluminescence. Their skin, nearly transparent in youth, acquires a subtle opalescence with age, eventually developing patterns unique to each individual that glow with increasing intensity during moments of strong emotion or ritual significance.

Their elongated bodies taper at both ends, with their primary sensory organs clustered at the forward terminus. Four eyes are arranged in a diamond pattern—two detecting light spectrums invisible to most species, and two specialized for the perpetual twilight of their domain. They have no nose as land-dwellers would understand it, but rather a complex network of chemoreceptive pores along what might be called their face and neck. Their mouths, when closed, appear as barely perceptible seams, but can expand to surprising dimensions when feeding or singing.

Most distinctive are their four primary appendages—not quite arms, not quite fins—that extend from their torsos and terminate in seven digits connected by delicate webbing. These limbs demonstrate impossible dexterity, capable of manipulating the finest threads of their woven artworks or delivering precise pressure to the resonance chambers of their living instruments. A fifth appendage, shorter and more muscular, emerges from their ventral region and serves primarily for powerful propulsion through water, though elders can control this limb with enough subtlety to "stand" on surfaces when necessary.

The Deep Singers' most remarkable adaptation is their dual respiratory system. Primary breathing occurs through gill structures concealed beneath latticed bone plates on their flanks, processing the heavy, mineral-rich water of their realm. However, they possess secondary lung-like organs—evolutionary remnants or forward adaptations, none can say—that allow limited survival in "the dry world" they regard with such reverence and fear.

The Deep Singers are not a single consciousness but neither are they entirely individual. They exist in a perpetual state of gentle telepathic communion, sharing emotional states and abstract concepts through what they call "the Undercurrent." This shared awareness grows stronger with physical proximity and is most intense during their harmonizing ceremonies, when dozens gather in concentric circles around the central void to vibrate their specialized vocal bladders.

Their society lacks hierarchy in any conventional sense. Elder Deep Singers—distinguished by their larger size and elaborate bioluminescent patterns—serve as repositories of collective memory rather than leaders. Roles within Muan'va emerge organically based on affinity rather than assignment. Those drawn to tending the kelp gardens simply begin doing so; those with talents for navigating the time-anomalies naturally become guides.

Reproduction among the Deep Singers occurs rarely and communally. During celestial alignments that happen once per decade, multiple individuals contribute genetic material to fertilization chambers within the uppermost shell-spirals. The resulting offspring—never more than five or six—are tended collectively by the entire population, with early development occurring within specialized nurturing pools where time flows most favorably for growth.

The Deep Singers possess no written language but maintain an oral tradition stretching back thousands of years, preserved in sonic patterns they can imprint into the crystalline structures of their shell-homes. Their perception of history is non-linear; they speak of past and future events with equal certainty, and their oldest stories describe both the beginning and end of Muan'va with identical detail.

Their relationship with technology is unlike that of surface dwellers. They cultivate rather than construct, growing specialized organisms to serve practical purposes. Their light comes from colonies of bioluminescent bacteria housed in carefully shaped shell-lanterns. Their tools are living extensions of themselves—bone harvesters that continue growing after being shaped, nets woven from fibers that repair themselves when damaged.

Most mysterious is their relationship with "the dry world." While they revere the central void where water gives way to air, they regard it with profound ambivalence. Their myths describe their ancestors as "exiles from dryness," suggesting they once dwelled beyond the water. The rare Deep Singer who feels called to enter the void undergoes elaborate preparation rituals before swimming into that forbidden space, carrying with them a crystalline seed said to contain the essence of Muan'va itself.

Those who have observed them from a distance report that the Deep Singers seem perpetually engaged in some great, slow-moving project—as if their entire civilization exists to complete a single task begun millennia ago and not expected to finish for millennia more. The nature of this work remains obscure, but the Deep Singers themselves describe it simply as "the Returning Tide."
