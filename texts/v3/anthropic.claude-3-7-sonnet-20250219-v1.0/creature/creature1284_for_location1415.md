# The Forgemaster of Kelwin

Yrill has tended the Forge Beneath Kelwin for forty-three years, though they're not human as most surface-dwellers understand humanity. Born from the unique confluence of biology and machinery that defines the forge, Yrill's body resembles a tall, lean humanoid figure perpetually glistening with the chamber's omnipresent moisture.

Their skin has a coppery cast, not merely from the metallic dust that coats everything in the forge, but because their epidermis actually incorporates copper and brass microfilaments that grow alongside cellular tissue. These filaments form intricate patterns across their body that shift subtly with mood and intention, glowing faintly in the dim light. Where a human would have hair, Yrill has a crown of fine copper wires that move with surprising grace, occasionally conducting small arcs of static electricity when emotions run high.

Yrill's eyes are their most striking feature—large, multifaceted organs that reflect light like polished brass mirrors. They don't require the wax earplugs that human workers use; instead, small mechanical apertures in their ear canals adjust automatically to the forge's cacophony, filtering and processing the sounds into meaningful patterns. To Yrill, the forge's noise isn't deafening chaos but a complex symphony conveying the health and needs of every mechanism.

Their hands feature six fingers each, unnaturally long and ending in flexible metallic tips capable of the most delicate adjustments to the forge's mechanisms. The palms contain specialized glands that secrete a biological lubricant perfectly formulated for the ancient machinery. When working with particularly delicate components, Yrill can regulate their body temperature precisely, preventing condensation from forming on critical parts.

Yrill doesn't speak as humans do. Instead, they communicate through a combination of melodic tones produced by small resonant chambers in their throat (pitched to cut through the forge's ambient noise), precise gestures, and patterns of bioluminescence that pulse across their skin. The forge's human apprentices spend years learning to understand this complex language.

They require no conventional food. Yrill absorbs nutrients through a symbiotic relationship with the forge's ecosystem—primarily by immersing themselves in specific mineral-rich pools for several hours each week and "breathing" through specialized membranes that filter metals from the water. They also consume certain mushrooms that grow only on the oldest machinery, extracting both organic compounds and the memories of the metal the fungi have colonized.

The locals believe Yrill was once human—a master smith who became so attuned to the forge that they gradually transformed over decades of exposure to its unique environment. Others whisper that Yrill is the offspring of the forge itself, born from the marriage of machine and mycelia. Yrill neither confirms nor denies these stories, seemingly amused by human speculation.

What's certain is that Yrill knows every inch of the forge's complex systems, often anticipating breakdowns before they occur. They move through the chamber with fluid grace despite the challenging terrain, navigating in near darkness with ease. The forge's creatures—the blind newts, sprocket minnows, gear-patterned snails—respond to their presence, following their movements as if drawn by some invisible force.

Yrill rarely leaves the forge, emerging to the surface world only during particularly unusual celestial alignments that occur once every seven years. On these occasions, they bring gifts of tools with remarkable properties—blades that never need sharpening, clockwork mechanisms that measure more than just time, lanterns that burn without fuel. The townsfolk of Kelwin accept these offerings with a mixture of gratitude and apprehension, hanging protective charms above their doors for several days after Yrill returns below.

Though they appear ageless, the oldest records in Kelwin suggest there have been other Forgemasters before Yrill, each with similar characteristics but distinct personalities. No one knows how long Yrill will tend the forge, or who—or what—might eventually take their place. But the people of Kelwin know that as long as the strange music of the forge continues to echo faintly through their floorboards at night, their town will prosper under the watchful, brass-mirror eyes of their inhuman guardian below.
