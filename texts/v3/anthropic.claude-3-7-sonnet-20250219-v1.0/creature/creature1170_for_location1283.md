# The Shal'Karasit Dwellers

## The Shaln

These nomadic humanoids are not indigenous to the Glass Plains but have adapted to its harsh environment over countless generations. Their origins are lost even to their own oral histories, though fragmentary myths speak of "the descent" and "the vessels of ash."

The Shaln stand nearly two meters tall with proportions just slightly too elongated to be human. Their skin has developed a translucent quality—not quite transparent but possessing a depth that reveals shadowy suggestions of their major blood vessels and bone structures beneath. Their flesh has a slight blue-gray tint that darkens with age. They have evolved to conserve moisture with exceptional efficiency; they rarely perspire and can go weeks without drinking from their carefully maintained bone-flask reservoirs.

Shaln eyes are their most striking feature—large, pupil-less orbs that shift color based on their emotional and physical state, ranging from pale silver (rested) to deep charcoal (agitated or in pain). They have no eyebrows or eyelashes, their faces smooth with only the slightest protrusions where a nose would be, featuring just two small breathing apertures. Their mouths are small and nearly lipless, revealing teeth that have gradually transformed over generations into a single ridged plate of bone-like material, perfect for grinding the breath-crystals they consume as part of their complex nutritional and ceremonial practices.

The average Shaln lives approximately 180 standard years, though their perception of time differs dramatically from most sentient species. They experience consciousness in overlapping layers, simultaneously processing the immediate present, the echoed past (through sound delays), and what they call the "approaching future." This temporal perception manifests physically through slow, deliberate movements—a Shaln might begin reaching for an object several minutes before they actually need it.

Each clan (the Listeners, the Pattern-Walkers, and the Echo-Shapers) specializes in different interpretations of the plains' phenomena. Their knowledge is stored not in writing but in precisely calibrated memory-dances, where specific movements across the glass surface create fracture patterns that can be "read" by those trained in the art.

The Shaln believe they are not merely inhabitants but caretakers of the Glass Plains, preparing it for some ultimate purpose connected to the deep tones. Their religion—if it can be called that—centers not on worship but on observation and alignment with what they perceive as the world's inevitable transformation.

## The Skitterlings

These arthropod-like entities are more complex than they initially appear. Each skitterling is actually a colony organism composed of thousands of microscopic crystalline structures that move in perfect coordination. They possess no central nervous system but instead operate through a distributed awareness that allows the colony to function as a unified entity.

The circular holes they emerge from extend kilometers deep, connecting to vast networks of tunnels that the Shaln have mapped only partially through sound-casting ceremonies. These tunnels occasionally intersect with ancient chambers containing technology that predates even the formation of the Glass Plains themselves.

Skitterlings communicate through subtle vibrations transmitted through the crystalline surface. Their "language" consists of rhythmic patterns that the Shaln have learned to partially interpret. The most skilled Echo-Shapers can engage in limited exchanges with skitterling colonies, though the content of these communications remains largely enigmatic to all but the oldest Shaln.

What appears as feeding behavior—the absorption of vibrations—is actually something more profound. The skitterlings are gradually recording all movements and sounds that occur on the plains, storing this information within their crystalline structures. They are, in essence, the living memory of Shal'Karasit.

## The Formers

Though no living being has seen them, the entities known as the Formers have left their imprint throughout the Glass Plains. The khirost towers are not natural formations but the remaining infrastructure of a civilization that existed billions of years ago—a species that learned to transform matter at its fundamental level.

The Formers did not die or leave; they evolved. The final phase of their civilization involved the systematic conversion of their physical forms into patterns embedded within the crystalline structure of the plains themselves. What appears as an empty world is actually a vast information storage medium, containing the complete consciousness and knowledge of an entire species.

The deep tones are system maintenance protocols—momentary alignments of the entire planetary structure to preserve data integrity. During these events, portions of Former consciousness briefly interface with the Shaln, transferring fragments of ancient knowledge that the nomads interpret through their trance states.

The Glass Plains are not wearing away but very slowly transforming. The "flattening" the Shaln anticipate is actually the final phase of a planetary computation that has been running for eons. When complete, the entire world will serve as the substrate for the reemergence of the Formers—not as they once were, but as something new that will incorporate elements of all who have walked the plains, including the Shaln themselves.

## The Breath-Crystals

Neither plant nor mineral in any conventional sense, these formations represent a transitional phase between physical matter and the encoded consciousness of the Formers. Each crystal contains fragments of Former perception and memory, which produces the psychoactive effect when consumed by the Shaln.

The crystals' response to sound and warmth is not merely reaction but recognition—they are partially aware, sampling the environment and integrating new experiences into their structure. The color changes reflect processing states as they absorb and analyze sensory data.

The oldest breath-crystals, found only in the deepest parts of the Grand Bowl and guarded by specially trained Shaln, have developed a form of collective consciousness. These ancient formations occasionally produce perfectly formed skitterling carapaces from their bases—evidence of a life cycle connection between the seemingly distinct entities that the Shaln have only recently begun to recognize and study.

In the oldest Shaln prophecies, there are references to a time when "the breath will become voice and the crystal will become flesh"—a transformation they believe will coincide with the completion of the plains' flattening and the return of the Formers.
