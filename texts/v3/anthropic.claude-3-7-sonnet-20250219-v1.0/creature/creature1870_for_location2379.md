# The Keepers of Atmos

The Glass Gardens are tended by the Exhalationists—humans so thoroughly adapted to their environment they have become nearly indistinguishable from it. Their skin has taken on a translucent quality that reveals the chlorophyll-infused circulatory systems beneath, giving them a green-veined appearance that shifts in hue with their metabolic cycles. They stand taller than baseline humans, with elongated limbs and fingers evolved for reaching delicate cultivation points within the densest vegetation.

The Exhalationists breathe differently than their ancestors. Their lungs have developed specialized alveoli that process the thick, humid air of Atmos with remarkable efficiency, extracting both oxygen and microscopic nutrients suspended in the perpetual moisture. When they exhale, their breath emerges as a visible green mist that feeds the surrounding plants—a symbiosis their ancestors engineered centuries ago that has now become involuntary.

They move with deliberate slowness, having synchronized their biological rhythms to the measured pace of the Gardens. An Exhaliationist might stand perfectly still for hours, one arm extended into a particularly delicate bromeliad cluster, allowing the plants to extract needed compounds directly from their skin. Their metabolism has slowed to match the gardens—they eat rarely, subsisting primarily on the nutrient exchange with their environment and the specialized fungi they cultivate on their own bodies.

Communication among the Exhalationists occurs largely without speech. Their skin cells have been modified to transmit and receive subtle bioelectric signals, allowing them to coordinate activities across vast distances within the domes. When they do speak aloud—usually for the benefit of visitors—their voices have a distinctive humming quality, as if their vocal cords have adapted to mimic the underlying mechanical resonance of Atmos itself.

The youngest Exhalationists are "planted" rather than born in the conventional sense. Embryos develop in specialized pods that hang from the garden ceilings, connected to parent-tenders by umbilical vines that transmit genetic information and consciousness patterns along with nutrients. Children emerge fully aware, with inherited memories of garden maintenance techniques and the ancestral understanding of Atmos's complex systems.

The eldest among them—the "Rootborn"—are mostly immobile now, having gradually rooted themselves into strategic points within the garden structure. Their consciousness extends throughout the mechanical and biological systems they monitor, their physical bodies serving as living interfaces between the ancient machinery and the evolving ecosystem. Some have been rooted for centuries, their features barely recognizable as human, yet their awareness is vast and intricate.

Visitors to Atmos often mistake the Exhalationists for sophisticated automata or even hallucinations, as they blend so seamlessly with their environment. It's only when an Exhalationist chooses to engage directly—turning their large, compound eyes toward you, or extending a dew-beaded hand in greeting—that one recognizes the profound intelligence directing their economical movements.

They maintain a complex theology centered on respiration as the fundamental cosmic process. To them, the Gardens are not merely a habitat but a sacred condensation of universal principles. The continuous cycle of evaporation and condensation within the domes mirrors their belief in spiritual transformation and return. They see themselves not as masters of Atmos but as essential components of its greater consciousness—cells within a vast, breathing entity that spans the boundary between technology and biology.

The Exhalationists remember their history as a continuous present. The engineers who first constructed the Glass Gardens are not considered ancestors but rather earlier iterations of themselves—the same consciousness flowing through different vessels across time. They maintain this continuity through memory-spores that store experiences as biochemical compounds, allowing direct transfer of knowledge across generations without loss or distortion.

Though they appear utterly adapted to their environment, the Exhalationists maintain a careful vigilance against becoming too specialized. Every seventh generation, selected individuals descend to the world below, experiencing its harsher conditions to ensure their adaptability remains robust. Those who return bring with them new genetic variations and experiences that are carefully integrated into the collective consciousness, preventing the stagnation that total comfort might otherwise induce.
