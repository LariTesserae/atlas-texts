# The Dwellers of the Drift

The Dwellers have no name for themselves. They have never needed one, being the only sentient inhabitants of their realm. If pressed to describe them, one might call them the Sifters, the Calculators, or perhaps the Patient Ones.

They stand about four feet tall when fully extended, though they rarely maintain this posture. Their bodies consist of articulated segments—somewhere between twelve and sixteen, the count seemingly variable—arranged in a roughly vertical column. Each segment houses a different specialized function and can rotate independently of the others. Their outer covering isn't quite chitin, nor quite mineral, but a crystalline bioceramic that grows throughout their lives, developing new facets and patterns as they age.

The Dwellers move by a curious shuffling-gliding motion, their lowest segments rippling across the undulating floor while maintaining perfect balance. They leave behind faint traces of copper-colored dust that dissipates within hours. This dust is both excretion and communication—messages left for others of their kind, marking territories, resources, or temporal anomalies to be avoided.

They have no eyes. Instead, their uppermost segments are studded with thousands of microscopic sensory pits that detect vibration, heat, mineral composition, and several spectra invisible to human perception. These pits constantly sift the particulate matter in the air, giving the Dwellers a complete awareness of their surroundings that extends in all directions simultaneously. They can sense the crystalline growths singing long before your ears detect anything.

Their limbs—between four and seven per segment—are delicate, articulated appendages capable of extreme precision. Each ends in either a fine point suitable for engraving, a flat palette for sifting, or small crystalline structures used for manipulation and calculation. These limbs make gentle clicking sounds when in motion, not unlike the workings of an intricate clock.

The Dwellers communicate through a combination of rhythmic tapping, the precise arrangement of mineral particles in the air, and a form of oscillation that causes their entire bodies to hum at frequencies mostly beyond human hearing. Their language has no concept of past or future tense, only varying distances of "now"—an adaptation to the Drift's peculiar temporality.

They feed primarily on the heat and mineral content of their environment, absorbing both through specialized plates on their middle segments. These plates slowly rotate during feeding, processing the warmth and substance of the Drift into energy through a process resembling both photosynthesis and crystallization. Occasionally, they will consume the smaller crystal-fungi, not for sustenance but to incorporate the resonant frequencies these growths produce, which they use in their most complex communications.

The Dwellers exist in a social structure organized around mathematical principles rather than hierarchies. Each individual's place in their society is determined by the complexity of patterns they can calculate and reproduce through arrangements of the small geometric cubes that serve as both their tools and their art. The most respected among them can create arrangements that seem to fold through additional dimensions, causing the cubes to cast shadows that shouldn't be possible.

Their perception of time is utterly alien. What might be a lifetime to a human is, to them, merely a moment of contemplation. They work on projects spanning centuries, gradually rearranging portions of the Drift according to calculations so complex they require multiple generations to complete. The broken stone circles and half-buried arches are not ruins but works in progress, slowly being positioned to align with cosmic frequencies the Dwellers perceive but cannot explain to outsiders.

They reproduce rarely, through a process of careful fragmentation. When conditions are perfect—determined by complex calculations involving the resonance patterns of the larger caverns—an elder Dweller will enter a crystallization state, encasing itself in a geometric cocoon. Months later, the cocoon cracks open to reveal the original Dweller and a smaller copy, containing a portion of the elder's knowledge but capable of developing its own mathematical insights.

The Dwellers regard visitors with patient curiosity rather than fear or aggression. They will observe an interloper for what feels like days before making any approach, gradually decreasing the distance until they might position themselves just beyond arm's reach. If the visitor remains still, a Dweller might extend a delicate limb to gently tap against their equipment or clothing, testing its resonance properties.

They possess knowledge of the Drift's inconsistent physics that no outsider could hope to master. They navigate its shifting geometries instinctively, always knowing which direction leads deeper and which might lead out—though these directions change constantly and cannot be marked by conventional means. The Dwellers can sense approaching fluctuations in gravity and time-flow, adjusting their movements to compensate so smoothly that they appear to glide through the distortions untouched.

The copper tools, sifting screens, and ceramic masks found throughout the Drift are indeed their handiwork—tools designed for manipulating their environment on scales both tiny and vast. What appears to be a simple blade is actually a tuning instrument for adjusting the resonance of crystal growths; what seems a ceremonial mask is in fact a complex calculator for predicting temporal fluctuations.

In the presence of the Dwellers, the strange properties of the Drift intensify. Objects leave stronger afterimages, gravity fluctuates more noticeably, and the ambient light takes on subtle patterns that correspond to the Dwellers' movements. They are not simply inhabitants of this realm—they are expressions of it, as much a part of the Drift as its crystalline flora and chitinous fauna.

The Dwellers neither welcome nor reject visitors. They simply incorporate them into their ongoing calculations, measuring and observing, occasionally attempting communication through precisely arranged patterns of dust or sound. They will never harm an interloper intentionally, though their concept of harm is limited to immediate physical damage—they have no understanding that human minds might fracture under the strain of the Drift's impossible geometry or the weight of its timeless patience.

If you stay long enough, they may begin to treat you as one of their artifacts—something to be occasionally adjusted, positioned according to equations you cannot comprehend, and ultimately incorporated into the vast, slow work that is their existence.
