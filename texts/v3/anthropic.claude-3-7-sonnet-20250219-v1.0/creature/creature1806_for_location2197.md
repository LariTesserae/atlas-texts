# The People of Mevaz

The inhabitants of Mevaz are not quite human, yet not entirely other. At first glance, they appear as slender figures with elongated limbs that move with deliberate grace through their suspended domain. Their skin carries a translucent quality—not transparent enough to reveal organs or bone, but rather like alabaster with veins of silver that catch what little light penetrates the mist. These silver patterns shift subtly with their breathing and emotional states, brightening during moments of concentration or communal gathering.

They stand taller than most humans, averaging seven feet in height, with narrow frames built for navigating the precarious pathways of their city. Their fingers are extraordinarily long and possess an additional joint, allowing them to manipulate the intricate tools needed for their endless architectural work with supernatural precision. Their feet are slightly prehensile, with a separated digit similar to a thumb that aids in gripping the sometimes vertical paths of their domain.

The Mevazians' eyes are their most striking feature—large, without whites, and filled with a shifting silver-blue luminescence that mirrors the mist surrounding their city. These eyes do not blink as humans' do but occasionally cloud over completely when they enter brief trance states to commune with the city itself. Their pupils contract not into circles but into vertical slits when exposed to direct light, a rare occurrence in the perpetual twilight.

They possess no hair but rather a crown of sensitive tendrils that extend from the back of the head and upper neck. These feathery appendages detect minute changes in air pressure, humidity, and the subtle gravitational fluctuations that permeate Mevaz. The tendrils range in color from pale blue to deep indigo, darkening with age. The eldest among them possess tendrils that have grown long enough to drape across their shoulders, occasionally adorned with tiny chimes that produce whisper-tones only they can fully interpret.

The Mevazians communicate primarily through a complex language of resonance. Their vocal apparatus produces tones that interact with the stone and cables of the city, creating harmonic patterns that carry information far more complex than mere words could convey. When they must communicate with outsiders (a rare occurrence), they speak with voices that sound like wind through crystal—beautiful but alien, with sounds that seem to emerge not just from their throats but from the air surrounding them.

Their society lacks traditional hierarchies. Instead, roles shift as naturally as the mist, with individuals moving between tasks according to the city's needs. What appears to guide their collective decision-making is a form of distributed consciousness—a shared awareness of the city's state that manifests through subtle changes in their silver vein patterns. When these patterns synchronize across multiple individuals, major architectural changes soon follow.

The Mevazians consume little in the conventional sense. Their primary sustenance comes from a process more akin to photosynthesis, absorbing energies from the unique light that filters through the perpetual mist. They supplement this by drinking fermented mist-water infused with minerals gathered from the stone of the mountains. On rare occasions, they consume a luminescent lichen that grows on the undersides of pathways, inducing a communal trance state during which the most significant city transformations occur.

Their reproduction is mysterious even to those who have studied them closely. No children are ever seen, yet occasionally new individuals appear within the community, seemingly formed from the mist itself. These "newborn" Mevazians appear fully formed but smaller, with translucent skin that gradually develops its silver veining as they integrate into society. The existing inhabitants treat these newcomers with particular attention, guiding them through elaborate rituals that involve standing at specific intersections of the city's support cables during rare alignments of light.

They craft their tools from materials that should not exist at this elevation—metals with properties unlike any found below, wood that bends without breaking yet holds immense weight, and crystalline substances that appear to solidify directly from the mist. Their clothing is minimal but functional, consisting of wrapped fabrics woven from the same silvery grasses that form their support cables, embroidered with symbols that correspond to the collecting channels on their buildings.

The Mevazians possess an unusual relationship with time. They can work tirelessly for days without rest, then enter suspended animation-like states while standing perfectly still, sometimes for weeks. During these periods, their bodies seem to partially merge with the stone or wood they touch, as though temporarily becoming part of the city's architecture. The oldest among them are said to be ancient beyond measure, having witnessed the rise and fall of countless civilizations in the lands below.

Visitors to Mevaz report a disquieting sense that the inhabitants can see through time itself—responding to questions before they're asked, preparing for events before they occur, and moving with perfect anticipation through the city's shifting pathways. Whether this represents a form of precognition or simply an intimate understanding of their home's patterns remains unclear.

Most mysterious is their purpose. The Mevazians appear devoted to the maintenance and expansion of their impossible city, yet the design follows no recognizable architectural philosophy. Some scholars believe they are gradually constructing an immense instrument—the entire city a machine for some purpose beyond human understanding. Others suggest they are translators, their suspended home a necessary interface between the earth below and something above or beyond.

Whatever their true nature, the people of Mevaz exist in perfect harmony with their fluid environment—perhaps because they themselves are partly manifestations of the same forces that keep their city suspended between solidity and air, between becoming and being.
