# The Caretaker of the Forge-Kiln

The being who tends the Forge-Kiln of Azath does not dwell within it continuously, but rather visits at precise intervals, moving through passageways too narrow for conventional human passage. They are called the Lithofluid, though this name is known to very few.

The Lithofluid stands barely four feet tall, with a body that seems to shift between solid and liquid states depending on need and circumstance. Their skin resembles fired clay—terracotta in hue with subtle variations of ochre and umber—cracked with thousands of fine lines that occasionally seep moisture. These cracks are not imperfections but a sophisticated regulatory system, allowing them to release heat and absorb environmental moisture as needed.

Their hands bear seven elongated digits each, tapered to points so precise they can adjust the flow of water by touch alone, feeling vibrations through the ceramic conduits that would be imperceptible to others. These fingers can extend when necessary, stretching like wet clay to reach deep into narrow vessels or high ceiling mechanisms without tools. The Lithofluid leaves no fingerprints; instead, their touch slightly alters the mineral composition of surfaces they frequently handle, reinforcing them against wear.

The being's eyes are its most startling feature—three orbs of copper-flecked amber that perceive not just visible light but the infrared spectrum of heat and the subtle magnetic properties of the minerals being processed. These eyes rotate independently, often focusing on multiple systems simultaneously, monitoring temperature gradients with precision measured in fractions of a degree.

The Lithofluid does not speak as humans understand speech. Instead, they communicate through a language of precisely timed drips and gurgles, manipulating the acoustic properties of the chamber itself. When they work, they hum in frequencies that cause specific vibrations in the water, catalyzing certain mineral transformations that would otherwise take years to complete.

They consume neither food nor drink as recognized by surface dwellers. Instead, the Lithofluid occasionally submerges their lower extremities in specific mineral slurries, absorbing elements directly through their porous lower body. During these feeding cycles, their clay-like skin briefly softens before rehardening with renewed luster.

The being's purpose and origin remain their own mystery. Some evidence suggests they may have once been human—perhaps an alchemist or artificer who underwent gradual transformation through prolonged exposure to the unique conditions they now maintain. Other signs point to them being a manufactured servant, perhaps the first creation of the Forge-Kiln itself, designed to tend the processes that would create others of their kind.

What remains certain is their absolute devotion to the crystalline structure growing in the central basin. The Lithofluid treats this formation with reverence approaching worship, spending long motionless hours observing microscopic changes in its lattice. Every thirty-seven days, they carefully extract a single, perfect crystal shard and carry it away through hidden passages to unknown recipients. Whether these are offerings, communications, or components for some greater mechanism remains a mystery known only to the Lithofluid and perhaps to Azath itself.

The being keeps no possessions save for a copper implement resembling both key and tuning fork, which hangs from a cord around their neck. This they use to strike the chamber walls at specific intervals, sending harmonics through the entire system that somehow calibrate and correct minute imperfections in the ongoing processes.

The Lithofluid does not sleep but enters periodic states of suspended animation, becoming nearly indistinguishable from the ceramic vessels they tend, their body hardening completely as they commune with the rhythms of the forge-kiln through means impossible to comprehend.
