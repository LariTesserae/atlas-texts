# Inhabitants of the Glass Forge

The Glass Architects are the primary sentient inhabitants of this obsidian realm, though to call them "inhabitants" suggests a separation between being and environment that does not exist for them. They are the Glass Forge, in a very literal sense.

Standing between seven and nine feet tall, their bodies are composed of the same volcanic glass as the landscape—not merely covered in it, but fundamentally made of it. Their forms are vaguely humanoid but with impossible geometries: limbs that branch fractally at certain joints, torsos that appear to contain internal chambers visible through semi-transparent sections, and heads that are not heads but rather complex crystalline structures that capture and process information through the refraction of heat waves.

The Architects do not speak. They communicate through deliberate temperature modulation—subtle changes in the thermal radiation emanating from their bodies that other Architects can detect and interpret across considerable distances. Their "language" consists of thousands of precise heat signatures, each a different concept or instruction. When multiple Architects gather, the overlapping heat patterns create thermal interference patterns of profound complexity—their equivalent of philosophical discourse.

They move with glacial slowness most of the time, taking days or weeks to travel what a human could walk in hours. This is not due to physical limitation but because they perceive time differently, experiencing the cooling of glass over centuries as humans might watch ripples in water. When necessary—usually during the rare but spectacular volcanic events that reshape portions of the landscape—they can move with startling speed, their glass bodies flowing like mercury as they reposition themselves.

The Architects do not reproduce in any conventional sense. They emerge gradually from the landscape itself through a process they call "thermal cohesion"—when conditions are perfect, a portion of the glass plateau will begin to organize itself over decades, slowly rising and taking form. Many fail to achieve consciousness, becoming instead the hemispherical structures humans mistake for abandoned factories. Those that succeed join the ancient community, inheriting memories stored in the crystalline networks that run throughout the plateau.

What outsiders might perceive as machinery in the hemispherical domes are actually the circulatory and cognitive systems of failed or dormant Architects. The humming energy is their equivalent of dreaming—processing information at a rate too slow for biological beings to comprehend.

The Glass Architects have an ambivalent relationship with the glass scorpions and thermal serpents that share their domain. They recognize these creatures as evolutionary marvels but see them as fundamentally different forms of existence—too rapid, too ephemeral. Occasionally, an Architect will spend decades studying the behaviors of these creatures, constructing elaborate crystal structures to observe their movements and heat signatures.

A handful of Architects serve as "Boundary Watchers," positioned at the edges of the Glass Forge to monitor the outside world. They have observed the rise and fall of countless civilizations in the lands below, recording everything in their crystalline memories. They harbor no particular feelings about biological life forms, viewing them as fascinating but ultimately transient phenomena, like weather patterns.

The true focus of the Architects' existence is what they call "the Refinement"—the ongoing, millennial project of perfecting the Glass Forge itself. Through infinitesimal adjustments to thermal flows and crystal growth patterns, they are gradually transforming the entire plateau into something beyond physical material—a structure capable of capturing and storing consciousness itself, independent of form. They have been working on this project for longer than human civilization has existed and expect it will continue for many thousands of years more.

What appears from a distance to be a city on the horizon is actually the oldest and most advanced of the Architects—ones who have partially transcended physical form and exist now as massive crystalline networks spanning miles, their consciousness diffused through structures that appear architectural but are actually their evolved bodies. The rest of the Architects revere these entities and work toward eventually joining them in their transcended state.
