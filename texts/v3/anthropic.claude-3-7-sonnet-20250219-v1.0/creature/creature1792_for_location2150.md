# The Keepers of Shra-Tahl

They call themselves simply the Custodians, though outsiders know them as the Rootspeakers. Numbering perhaps two hundred souls, they are the last human stewards of an inheritance they themselves don't fully comprehend.

The Custodians are tall and unnaturally thin, their skin bearing a grayish-green tinge that outsiders initially mistake for illness but is in fact a symbiotic relationship with microscopic algae that infuse their dermal layers. This symbiosis—established through rituals performed on infants—provides them additional sustenance in the nutrient-scarce environment and allows them to process the thin mountain air more efficiently. Their eyes have adapted to the perpetual twilight, growing large and almost entirely pupil, with only the thinnest ring of amber iris visible. When they venture outside Roothold (a rare occurrence), they must wear protective coverings to shield these sensitive organs.

Their movements are deliberate and eerily smooth. Custodians never rush—partly practical conservation of energy in the high altitude, partly ritualized behavior that mirrors the patient growth of their charge. They communicate among themselves in a language that combines traditional speech with subtle hand gestures and postural adjustments that outsiders cannot follow. Their vocal language contains an unusual abundance of sibilants and drawn-out vowels that mimic the sounds of rustling leaves and moving sap.

The society is divided into several castes, each designated by specific fungal tattoos cultivated directly on their skin:

The Pruners maintain the physical structure, their fingers so sensitive they can detect unhealthy growth by touch alone. Their arms bear elaborate spiraling patterns of pale blue bioluminescent fungi.

The Watchers observe and record changes over decades, memorizing the precise configuration of each chamber and passage. Their foreheads display symmetrical patterns resembling eyes.

The Singers preserve the ancient working songs and chants that somehow direct and influence Roothold's growth. Their throats and chests bear vertical lines of red-orange fungi that pulse subtly when they perform.

The Listeners spend their days in meditation, pressing their ears against specific junctions within the living walls, recording and interpreting the subtle sounds of the structure. Their ears and the sides of their heads are adorned with delicate fan-shaped fungi.

Most mysterious are the Seedbearers, who rarely interact with others and spend months in the deepest, most sacred chambers. When they emerge, they often bear new growth patterns on their skin and speak in cryptic fragments for days before returning to normal communication. Some bear visible alterations—fingers that have partially lignified into wood-like appendages, or patches of bark-like growth along their spines.

Children are rare among the Custodians. Reproduction appears ritualized rather than spontaneous, occurring only when Roothold itself provides certain signs—the blooming of specific flowers in particular chambers, or the appearance of certain fungal configurations. Children are raised communally, spending their early years learning to attune their breathing to the rhythm of the structure, often sleeping against the living walls to "hear the dreams of Roothold," as they describe it.

Their diet consists primarily of specially cultivated fungi, supplemented with nutrient-rich water collected from specific reservoirs where mineral content is highest. They consume no meat and regard the killing of animals within Roothold's boundaries as sacrilege. Several times yearly, they practice ritualized fasting followed by consumption of particular mushroom species that induce visions they believe are communications from Roothold itself or perhaps the ancient Shra-Tahl.

The Custodians do not consider themselves the masters of Roothold but rather its servants. In their cosmology, they are merely the mobile appendages of a greater organism, carrying out functions the structure cannot perform for itself. The more mystically inclined among them believe they are transitional beings, that over generations their descendants will become more plant than human, eventually merging completely with Roothold.

Their technology is deliberately primitive—not from ignorance but reverence. Metal tools are forbidden, as iron particularly is believed to "deafen" the sensitivity needed to communicate with the living structure. Instead, they use implements of polished stone, hardened wood, and glass occasionally traded from outsiders. They are particularly skilled in creating cutting implements from volcanic glass, which they collect during rare expeditions to an extinct caldera some distance from the plateau.

The Custodians possess one technology that defies outside understanding: a method of writing that uses specially cultivated fungi growing on treated bark sheets. The writing appears as raised patterns that glow faintly and can be read by touch in complete darkness. More remarkably, these documents appear to slowly evolve over time, with slight alterations appearing without human intervention. The Custodians consider this natural "editing" to be the wisdom of Roothold perfecting their imperfect records.

Most troubling to outside observers is the clear evidence that the Custodians are changing physiologically over generations. Historical records from early explorers describe them as unusual but recognizably human. Current Custodians show signs of something else emerging—not just the symbiotic algae but structural changes to their skeletal systems, unusual flexibility in their joints, and circulatory systems that include sap-like substances alongside normal blood.

The oldest among them, who they call the Eldest Growth, is a being of indeterminate age who has become largely immobile, their lower extremities having apparently rooted into the floor of the highest chamber. The Eldest speaks rarely, and when they do, others record every word. Their skin has largely transformed into a bark-like substance, and small fungi grow from their shoulders and scalp. The Custodians view this not as a tragedy but as the ultimate achievement—the beginning of true union with Roothold.

When asked directly about the ultimate purpose of their stewardship—what final awakening they prepare for—the Custodians only smile their strange, serene smiles and say: "We do not tend the roots; we are the roots. And when all is ready, that which grows from us will reach the sky."
