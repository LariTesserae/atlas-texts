# Residents of the Undervoid of Chattermire

## The Mnemophages

The true lords of Chattermire are not those who built its twisting architecture, but those who feed on the memories it generates. The Mnemophages appear at first glance to be roughly humanoid, standing seven to nine feet tall with elongated limbs that bend at too many joints. Their skin shifts between translucence and a mirror-like reflectivity depending on their hunger state. Their faces are smooth, featureless expanses except for three vertical slits that occasionally part to reveal glimpses of churning, prismatic light.

They move with unsettling grace, sometimes gliding just above the ground, other times scuttling across walls and ceilings. When they feed—which is often—they extend gossamer filaments from their fingertips to the temples of other creatures, drawing out wispy strands of luminescent matter. These are memories, experiences, dreams. The victim doesn't die or even feel pain; they simply forget. Sometimes it's just moments, sometimes entire years.

The Mnemophages communicate through a form of telepathy that manifests as sudden intrusive thoughts in other beings' minds. Their language consists not of words but of sensory impressions: the smell of burning copper, the sensation of falling, the taste of something that might be licorice but isn't quite. Researchers who've survived extended contact report that the Mnemophages consider themselves gardeners of consciousness, pruning and shaping the collective memory of Chattermire to maintain what they call "the equilibrium."

## The Conductor and His Orchestra

No one knows when the Conductor first appeared in the Resonance Chamber, nor whether he was once human. His form suggests it: a gaunt figure in a tattered tailcoat that seems to absorb rather than reflect light. Where his head should be, there is instead a swirling vortex of sound waves visible as rippling distortions in the air.

His Orchestra consists of twelve entities that might once have been normal animals—things resembling cats, ravens, serpents—but have been transformed by long exposure to the Chamber. Their bodies have partially merged with various antique musical instruments: brass horns erupt from feline spines; violin strings run through avian wingbones; percussion instruments have replaced limbs.

Together they perform what the Conductor calls "The Endless Symphony," a piece of music that changes subtly each time it loops but never truly concludes. The sound they produce seems to stabilize nearby sections of the Undervoid, creating pockets where reality remains relatively constant. Those seeking respite from Chattermire's chaos often make pilgrimages to hear them play, bringing offerings of unusual sounds captured in glass spheres.

The Conductor speaks rarely, and when he does, his words emerge as perfectly formed musical notations that hang in the air before dissipating. Only those with training in classical composition can interpret these communications, leading to the rise of a specialized class of translators.

## The Cartography Collective

Humans can survive in the Undervoid, but not without adaptation. The Cartography Collective represents humanity's most successful attempt at establishing a foothold. Originally a research team sent to map Chattermire decades ago, they've evolved into something between a scientific institute and a mystical order.

Each Cartographer has undergone the Ritual of Anchoring, which involves consuming a preparation made from local fungi and having specific equations tattooed along their spines in ink mixed with powdered minerals from the Undervoid itself. This painful process grants them a measure of stability—they remember the Undervoid as it was yesterday, even when reality shifts around them.

The Collective numbers about forty individuals, their skin pale from minimal exposure to sunlight, their eyes adapted to the gloom with enlarged pupils that reflect light like cats'. They wear practical jumpsuits adorned with intricate patterns of copper wire sewn directly into the fabric, serving as a primitive form of protection against the Mnemophages' feeding.

Their headquarters, the Archive, is unique in that it remains at a fixed point in Chattermire—though its internal architecture is in constant flux. Here they compile their observations on scrolls made from the fibrous moss that hangs from the ceiling. Their maps are beautiful, impossible objects: three-dimensional constructs that fold in on themselves, sometimes containing smaller versions of themselves within their folds.

## The Whisperwasps

Perhaps the most numerous entities in Chattermire are the Whisperwasps—though "wasp" is misleading. These creatures are about the size of a human thumb and resemble elongated, semi-transparent jellyfish that float through the air in vast schools. Each individual pulses with a faint blue-green light that intensifies when they're agitated.

What makes them remarkable is their habit of collecting sounds. A Whisperwasp will hover near a conversation, a mechanical noise, or even the toll of the omnipresent bell, and somehow capture that sound within its gelatinous body. Later, when disturbed or as part of their mating displays, they replay these sounds perfectly—creating a disorienting echo of conversations that may have occurred hours, days, or years ago.

The oldest Whisperwasps—recognizable by their amber coloration—contain sounds from the earliest days of Chattermire. Collectors prize them, keeping them in special resonance chambers where their captured sounds can be extracted and studied. Some believe that within these ancient specimens lies the true history of how the Undervoid came to be.

## The Geometers

Moving through the most mathematically complex regions of Chattermire are the entities locals call Geometers. These beings appear as constantly shifting arrangements of perfect geometric forms—cubes, dodecahedrons, spheres—connected by lines of force visible as hair-thin filaments of light. They range in size from a few inches to massive compound entities the size of buildings.

The Geometers seem to be responsible for much of the spatial instability in Chattermire. As they move, reality bends around them, creating the non-Euclidean passages and impossible stairways. They don't communicate with other beings directly, but their movements follow patterns that mathematicians in the Cartography Collective have begun to recognize as a form of expression.

When two Geometers meet, they sometimes merge temporarily, their forms interlocking and creating dazzling displays of mathematical perfection. During these "conjunctions," nearby spaces become particularly unstable, with rooms folding into themselves or expanding to infinite dimensions before snapping back.

The most disturbing theory about the Geometers is that they aren't moving through Chattermire at all—rather, Chattermire is moving through them, as they exist in higher spatial dimensions that our reality merely intersects.

In the shadows of the Undervoid, countless other beings thrive—the merchants of the Ossuary Market with their bone-white exoskeletons; the childlike Tickers who maintain the massive clockwork beneath The Grand Turbine; the solitary Phase Hermits who have learned to exist partially out of sync with conventional time. All have found their niche in this impossible ecosystem, this place where reality itself is just another resource to be consumed, transformed, and renewed in the endless cycle of Chattermire's existence.
