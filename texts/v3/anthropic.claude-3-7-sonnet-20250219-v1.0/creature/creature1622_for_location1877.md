# The Hollowfolk of the Resonant Undercity

The people of Hollowsong are not quite human—not anymore. Their ancestors may have fled below during some forgotten calamity, but over countless generations, they've become something uniquely adapted to their subterranean realm.

The Hollowfolk stand shorter than surface dwellers, with compact, powerful builds suited for navigating tight spaces and climbing. Their skin ranges from alabaster to a soft gray, almost translucent in places where blue veins show through like tiny underground rivers. Most striking are their eyes—slightly oversized, with enlarged pupils that dominate the iris, allowing them to perceive the subtlest variations in the dim fungal light. These eyes catch and reflect light in an unsettling manner, giving them an otherworldly glow in certain angles.

Their hair grows thick and often reaches waist-length, regardless of gender, typically in shades of white, silver, or pale blue. This isn't vanity but practicality—the Hollowfolk use their hair as a sensory organ, leaving it unbound to detect subtle changes in air currents that might signal cave instability or approaching weather changes from above. The oldest among them grow beards that reach the floor, adorned with tiny carved stone beads that create gentle clicking sounds as they move, adding personal percussion to the cavern's symphony.

Their ears appear almost fin-like, with extra cartilaginous ridges that help them filter and interpret the complex soundscape of their home. A Hollowfolk can tell you exactly which water channel needs maintenance simply by listening to the altered pitch of water striking stone fifty meters away. They communicate through a language that incorporates conventional words with precise rhythmic tapping, whistling, and controlled echoes—a tongue impossible to fully master without the acoustic properties of the caverns themselves.

The Hollowfolk move with a distinctive gait—slightly bowlegged, with toes that spread wider than a surface human's, creating better grip on slick stone. Their fingers are long and nimble, with hardened calluses from generations of stone carving and navigating rough surfaces. Many bear birthmarks that resemble the branching patterns of underground water systems, which they consider sacred signs of their destiny within the community.

Each Hollowfolk belongs to one of seven "resonance clans," determined not by birth but by the particular tone their voice naturally harmonizes with in the great caverns. These clans—Deepchime, Riverpulse, Stonehum, Crystalring, Mistwhisper, Echobreak, and the rare Voidsilence—determine one's role in the great "songworkings" that are central to their society. The clan leaders, called Resonators, wear elaborate headdresses made from specially shaped fungi caps that amplify their vocal commands when necessary.

The Hollowfolk have developed physiological adaptations beyond the visible. Their lungs have increased capacity, allowing them to hold breath for extraordinary periods—necessary when toxic gases occasionally seep from deeper fissures. Their digestive systems extract maximum nutrition from fungi, moss, blind fish, and the mineral-rich water itself. They require far less food than surface dwellers but must consume special mineral-rich clay tablets weekly to supplement their diet.

Their society centers around the "flowshapers"—part engineer, part musician, part priest—who maintain and adjust the intricate system of channels that direct water through Hollowsong. These individuals undergo decades of training to develop an intuitive understanding of fluid dynamics and acoustics. The most gifted can "speak to the stone," using specific tonal sequences that cause minute vibrations, gradually shaping rock without tools over months or years—a technique outsiders mistake for mere superstition until they witness the results.

The Hollowfolk measure time not by days (meaningless in their sunless home) but by "flows"—periods between the major redistributions of water channels that occur as the seasons change above and water volumes shift. Their calendar tracks a complex system of nested cycles, with the "Great Resonance" occurring once every seventeen years when specific astronomical conditions create unique water pressures throughout the system.

Children are raised communally, spending their early years learning to navigate every passage and identify hundreds of fungi varieties by touch alone. Coming-of-age involves a solitary journey to the "uncarved depths," where they must find their own unique resonance chamber—a natural space that perfectly amplifies their voice—and sing their "life-note" for the first time.

The eldest Hollowfolk undergo a strange transformation as death approaches. Their skin develops crystalline patches that gradually spread. When death is imminent, they journey to the sacred "Hall of Echoes" where they sit in specially carved niches and allow the mineralization process to complete. Over years, they slowly transform into crystal-flecked stone formations that continue to vibrate subtly with the cavern's acoustics, literally becoming one with Hollowsong. These ancestor formations are believed to still hear and remember, occasionally answering questions with subtle tonal shifts—though outsiders debate whether this is supernatural or simply the natural acoustic properties of the formations.

The Hollowfolk's relationship with the outside world is complicated. They trade occasionally with surface dwellers brave enough to descend, offering unique fungi with medicinal properties and acoustically perfect stone resonators used in high-quality musical instruments. They welcome respectful visitors but have elaborate precautions against those who might threaten their home. The few who have attempted invasion discovered too late that the Hollowfolk can trigger specific acoustic sequences that cause devastating cave-ins at strategic points, sealing sections of their city while leaving escape routes only they know.

Some scholars believe the Hollowfolk are gradually evolving into a distinct species, their isolation and unique environment driving adaptations generation by generation. The Hollowfolk themselves say they are becoming "the song made flesh"—the living embodiment of their water-carved home.
