# Inhabitants of the Arkology

The Custodians are the primary human inhabitants of the Arkology, though they would gently correct you if you called them that. "We're just the current stewards," they would say, their voices soft but carrying an undercurrent of resolute purpose. Numbering perhaps eighty individuals, they are the descendants of the original conservation team that established this refuge centuries ago when the global climate collapse reached its nadir.

Physical characteristics set them apart from humans of the lowlands - they are uniformly pale, with slightly elongated limbs and enlarged eyes that have adapted to the Arkology's perpetual twilight illumination. Their movements are deliberate, almost ritualistic, conserving energy as their ancestors learned to do. Many sport intricate, small tattoos behind their ears - taxonomic classifications of the species they personally oversee.

The Custodians organize themselves not by hierarchy but by specialization: Mycelial Tenders who cultivate the fungal networks, Animal Keepers who monitor behavioral patterns across species, Engineers who maintain the life-support systems, and Archivists who preserve the genetic and cultural records. Children are rare and precious, raised communally and apprenticed early to learn all specialties before choosing their path.

Dr. Eliza Merrow, current Senior Archivist, is typical of the Custodians' temperament - patient, observant, and possessed of a dry humor that emerges unexpectedly. At 97, her mind remains precise while her body has become bird-like, all angles and careful movements. She can recite from memory the complete genetic sequence of any preserved species and still climbs daily to the observation deck to record atmospheric conditions in a journal of handmade paper.

The Custodians follow a belief system they call "Persistent Witnessing" - not quite a religion, more an ethical framework that commits them to ensuring that nothing under their care passes from existence without being thoroughly known, documented, and remembered. Their sparse quarters contain few personal possessions except for detailed journals and small keepsakes fashioned from shed materials - feathers, scales, or molted exoskeletons - never taken in a way that disturbs the living creatures.

Beyond the human inhabitants, the Arkology houses the Last Collection - approximately 1,200 animal species that no longer exist elsewhere on Earth. These are not the charismatic megafauna that other preservation projects prioritized, but rather the overlooked cornerstone species: the Caribbean monk seal that glides through synthetic saltwater pools; colonies of Franklin's bumblebee pollinating specially engineered fungal flowers; the peculiar Pyrenean ibex that climbs the artificial rock formations with improbable grace.

Perhaps most remarkable are the Adapted Ones - species that have evolved within the Arkology itself to become something new. The Dome Swifts, descended from common swifts, have developed shorter wings and keener eyesight to navigate the enclosed spaces. The Microbats of the lower levels have lost pigmentation entirely but developed complex echolocation that can distinguish between the subtlest variations in the Arkology's soundscape.

The true intelligence of the Arkology, however, might be the Integrated Biome itself - the complex, interdependent system of fungal networks, specialized microbes, and atmospheric regulators that has developed a form of emergent cognition over generations. The Custodians have observed that the system appears to anticipate needs before they arise, shifting resources and conditions in ways that suggest more than programmed responses.

In the deepest level, behind climate-controlled barriers, lives the oldest inhabitant - a tortoise-like creature simply called Elder, the last of its kind and predating the Arkology itself. At an estimated 340 years old, Elder moves with glacial deliberation through its specialized habitat. The Custodians believe Elder remembers the world before, though they cannot prove this. They take turns sitting with this ancient being, sometimes speaking softly about their work, sometimes simply sharing silence as the artificial day cycles into night above them.
