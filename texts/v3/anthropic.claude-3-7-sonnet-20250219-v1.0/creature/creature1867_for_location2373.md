# The People of Nimburest

The inhabitants of Nimburest are known as the Stratafolk, a people whose very physiology has adapted to their vertical domain. Standing slightly shorter than lowland humans, they possess remarkably developed lungs that expand further into their chest cavity, allowing efficient oxygen extraction from the thin mountain air. Their skin has a faintly bluish undertone—not from oxygen deprivation as visitors often mistake, but from specialized hemoglobin that binds oxygen more tightly. Most distinctive are their eyes: larger than typical human eyes, with expanded peripheral vision and protective inner membranes that shield against the intense high-altitude sunlight.

The Stratafolk move with a deliberate economy of motion honed through generations of navigating their tiered homeland. Their calves and thighs are powerfully developed, allowing them to ascend hundreds of stairs without noticeable fatigue. Children learn to climb before they walk properly on flat surfaces, their hands developing calluses on specific friction points by early adolescence. Most Nimburest natives can identify their exact elevation by subtle changes in air pressure that outsiders cannot perceive.

Society in Nimburest is organized by both horizontal and vertical relationships—one's status depends not just on profession or wealth, but on dwelling elevation. The highest terraces, though newer and rougher, are paradoxically the most prestigious, housing the governing Stone Council and the city's most accomplished engineers. The saying goes: "Authority flows downward with the water." This vertical stratification is not rigid, however; individuals may ascend (literally and figuratively) through demonstrated mastery of the city's essential crafts.

The Master Hydraulicists form perhaps the most respected guild, maintaining the water collection and distribution systems that sustain all life in Nimburest. Their uniform of indigo robes with embedded moisture sensors can be spotted moving methodically along aqueduct inspection paths. Each Master undergoes fifteen years of training, memorizing the entire water network and learning to diagnose flow problems by sound alone. They rarely speak above a whisper, having trained their ears to detect the subtlest changes in water music.

Equally vital are the Stonewrights, who continue the city's perpetual expansion. Working with tools passed down through generations, these artisans possess an intuitive understanding of the mountain's geological composition. The most skilled can tap a section of rock wall and determine its suitability for carving simply from the resonance. Stonewrights live by strict traditions—they sleep only on stone beds, eschew footwear to maintain direct contact with their medium, and wear their guild's distinctive granite dust as facial markings indicating their specialization and rank.

The Agricultural Terrace Tenders maintain the complex growing systems that feed the city. Their deep knowledge of high-altitude botany manifests in carefully calibrated planting schedules that follow both seasonal changes and the three-day water cycle. These cultivators have developed techniques for maximizing growth in the challenging environment, including sound-based growth stimulation and specialized reflective surfaces that intensify available sunlight. Their fingers are perpetually stained green from the bioactive compounds they use to enhance plant immunity.

Nimburest's technological development has followed a unique path dictated by their environment. Without abundant metal ores in the mountains, they've developed advanced ceramics and biocomposites. The Alchemical Engineers Guild specializes in creating materials that mimic natural structures—lightweight but incredibly strong. Their workshop-laboratories, carved into the mountain's interior, glow with strange lights as they cultivate crystals and process botanical compounds into construction materials.

The city's defense falls to the Wind Wardens, who patrol the narrow staircases and monitor the few viable approach routes to the city. These sentinels utilize the terrain itself as a weapon, trained to trigger strategic rockslides that can seal access paths when necessary. They wear segmented armor of hardened resin that allows free movement on the near-vertical terrain, and carry multipurpose staffs that serve as climbing aids, measuring tools, and weapons.

Communication across the vast vertical expanse of Nimburest occurs through a sophisticated heliograph system—arrays of mirrors that redirect sunlight to send coded messages between distant terraces. The Reflectionists who maintain this network develop an acute visual sensitivity, able to distinguish minute light pattern variations that convey complex information. In cloudy conditions, they switch to acoustic messaging, using the mountain faces to amplify and direct sound signals.

Family structures in Nimburest typically span three adjacent terraces, with the eldest generation occupying the highest level. Children are raised communally within these extended family units, learning from multiple elders and developing skills across different elevations. Coming-of-age involves a ritual climb to the highest unfinished terrace, where young adults contribute to carving out a single new dwelling space—symbolic of their role in the city's continuing growth.

Religious belief centers around what the Stratafolk call the "Breath of Stone"—the notion that the mountains themselves are slowly inhaling and exhaling over millennia, with human civilization merely a momentary adornment on this greater living entity. Their temples are caves with perfect acoustic properties, where stone-song chanters produce harmonics that resonate with the mountain's natural frequencies. During these ceremonies, participants report sensing the subtle expansion and contraction of the entire mountain range.

Visitors to Nimburest are welcomed but closely monitored. Those who wish to stay must undergo gradual acclimatization in specially designed transition chambers on the lowest terraces, where oxygen levels are gradually reduced over weeks. Few outsiders successfully adapt to permanent residence; those who do are ceremonially renamed after completing their first contribution to the city's expansion, symbolizing their rebirth as true Stratafolk.

The oldest inhabitants, those whose lungs have processed thin mountain air for nine or more decades, develop what locals call the "sky-sight"—an altered perception that reportedly allows them to see weather patterns days in advance. These elders serve as the city's meteorologists, their predictions determining water conservation measures and construction schedules. They are immediately recognizable by the crystalline jewelry they wear, which is said to amplify their connection to the atmosphere.

In Nimburest, death is not marked by burial but by "stone-returning." The deceased are ceremonially placed in specially prepared alcoves high on the mountain face, where the elements gradually reclaim their physical form. Names are not inscribed; instead, the precise coordinates of each alcove are recorded in the city's central archive, allowing descendants to visit the exact location where their ancestors returned to the mountain that sustained them in life.
