# The Inhabitants of the Inverse Savanna

## The Driftfolk

The primary sentient inhabitants of Nai'iti call themselves the Driftfolk, though this name translates poorly from their actual designation—a complex harmonic pattern of sounds that mimics the marsh's fundamental resonance. They appear humanoid only at first glance and from certain angles. Their bodies consist of densely packed fibers that appear solid but can partially disaggregate when necessary, allowing them to filter through the shifting earth currents like living sieves.

The Driftfolk stand approximately four feet tall when fully consolidated, with elongated torsos and three limbs that function as either arms or legs depending on their needs. Their skin displays a translucent amber coloration that pulses with internal bioluminescence—brightest during their communication rituals and nearly invisible when they rest. Their faces bear five sensory organs arranged in a pentagonal pattern, each capable of perceiving different aspects of their environment: conventional light, vibrational patterns, chemical compositions, temporal fluctuations, and what they call "memory-scent"—the residual impressions left by past events.

They do not build permanent dwellings, instead cultivating symbiotic relationships with the crown-plants. A Driftfolk family unit—typically comprising three to seven individuals connected through complex non-linear kinship systems—will nurture a crown-plant until its tendrils form a protective canopy. The plant responds by secreting a resin that temporarily stabilizes reality within its perimeter, creating islands of relative consistency in the marsh's perpetual flux.

## The Ceiling Shepherds

Inhabiting the precarious realm between the marsh and the hovering ceiling-pools are the Ceiling Shepherds—solitary, specialized Driftfolk who have evolved a profound connection with the water. Their bodies have adapted to produce a counter-gravitational enzyme that allows them to partially ascend, floating in meditative postures beneath the ceiling-pools for days at a time.

The Shepherds cultivate and tend to colonies of luminous micro-organisms that live within the suspended waters. These organisms, called "light-seeds," are harvested during specific astronomical alignments and used in Driftfolk rituals. Each Shepherd cares for particular pools, learning their rhythms and preventing dangerous accumulations that might cause devastating downpours. Their skin has evolved to become more translucent than other Driftfolk, often appearing as rippling water given vague humanoid form.

## The Thought-Herds

The "thought-forms" observed by outsiders are neither fully animal nor plant but something uniquely evolved in response to the marsh's reality distortions. These collective organisms exist simultaneously as individual entities and as components of a hive mind. Each thought-form appears as a gelatinous, semi-transparent being with constantly shifting appendages that emerge and recede as needed.

The Driftfolk maintain a complex relationship with these herds—part symbiotic, part domesticated, part religious. The thought-forms consume the reality distortions themselves, metabolizing paradoxes and temporal inconsistencies. In return, they produce crystalline structures within their bodies that the Driftfolk harvest and use as navigation tools in the disorienting landscape. Without these crystals, even native inhabitants would become hopelessly lost in the marsh's space-warping expanses.

## The Rememberers

Perhaps the most enigmatic inhabitants are the Rememberers—ancient Driftfolk who have undergone a profound transformation after centuries of existence. Their physical bodies have almost entirely dissipated, becoming living patterns of vibration that harmonize with the marsh's underlying hum. They appear as shimmering vertical distortions in the air, visible primarily through their effect on light and sound rather than through physical substance.

The Rememberers serve as the collective memory of Nai'iti, preserving knowledge through millennia by encoding it into the resonant frequencies of the marsh itself. When the Driftfolk require ancestral wisdom, skilled practitioners can extract these memories by creating counter-vibrations that temporarily make the stored knowledge perceptible. Young Driftfolk sometimes report encounters with Rememberers during their maturation wanderings—describing conversations with voices that seem to emerge from within their own thoughts, teaching them the secret harmonies needed to navigate the marsh safely.

## The Many-Winged

The bird-analogues, known to the Driftfolk as the Many-Winged, represent one of the few species that freely travels between the Inverse Savanna and the outside world. These beings possess between seven and thirteen wings arranged in overlapping helical patterns. They navigate not just through physical space but through probability spaces as well, appearing to blink in and out of existence as they select different potential flight paths simultaneously.

The Many-Winged maintain a strange intelligence that the Driftfolk have never fully comprehended. They construct elaborate nests from solidified time-fragments—objects caught in loops of accelerated or decelerated temporal flow. Their mating rituals involve complex aerial choreography that appears to momentarily stabilize the marshland below, creating brief periods where reality follows consistent rules.

The Driftfolk harvest the molted wings of these creatures, using them to craft specialized tools that allow them to manipulate the ceiling-pools from a distance. Some particularly ambitious Driftfolk aspire to communicate with the Many-Winged, spending decades learning to reproduce their multitonal calls in hopes of accessing their knowledge of the worlds beyond the marsh.

## The Deep Dwellers

Beneath the flowing earth exists another ecosystem entirely—the domain of the Deep Dwellers. These creatures resemble massive translucent worms with hundreds of finger-like appendages. They navigate through the semi-solid substrate of the marsh, occasionally surfacing to extend sensory stalks that taste the air.

The Deep Dwellers create the underlying earth currents through their continuous movement, effectively maintaining the marsh's defining characteristic. The Driftfolk leave offerings at specific spots where the Dwellers are known to surface—typically composed of crystallized sound harvested from particularly harmonious ceiling-pool eruptions. In return, the Dwellers occasionally adjust their movements to accommodate Driftfolk migrations or to protect crown-plant nurseries from destructive currents.

The oldest Deep Dwellers have existed since before the marsh formed, their memories extending back to when Nai'iti was an entirely different environment. The Driftfolk creation myths speak of the "Great Inversion," when the primordial Deep Dwellers turned the world inside out, creating the inverse relationship between earth and water that now defines their home.

## The Boundary Pilgrims

At the edges of the marsh dwell the Boundary Pilgrims—Driftfolk who have dedicated themselves to understanding the transition between Nai'iti and the outside world. Their bodies have adapted to withstand the reality shear that occurs at these borders, developing a crystalline exoskeleton that refracts light in impossible ways.

The Pilgrims maintain a complex calendar that tracks when the boundary between worlds is thinnest. During these times, they perform elaborate rituals that temporarily stabilize passages allowing limited travel and trade with outsiders. They are the only Driftfolk who can speak languages other than their native harmonic tongue, having developed specialized organs that can produce sounds that follow consistent physical laws.

The Boundary Pilgrims are viewed with both reverence and suspicion by other Driftfolk. Their knowledge of the outside brings valuable resources and information, but their prolonged exposure to logical reality has changed them. They sometimes struggle to readapt to the marsh's fluid nature after spending time beyond its borders, requiring lengthy purification rituals to realign their beings with Nai'iti's fundamental resonance.
