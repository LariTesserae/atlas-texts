# The Custodian of Echoes

Living within the Cistern Gardens is a solitary being known only as the Custodian, a figure as much a part of the environment as the columns and cascades that define it. Neither fully human nor entirely other, the Custodian moves through the chamber with the unhurried deliberateness of the cave creatures, having adapted to the rhythms of this underground realm over what must be centuries.

Standing taller than an ordinary human but with a stooped posture that speaks of years navigating the low vaulted ceilings, the Custodian's form is difficult to discern clearly. Their body appears wrapped in layers of material that might once have been cloth but have since incorporated the textures of the cistern itself—moss-covered in patches, with tendrils of fungal growth extending from seams and hems. What skin is visible has the pallor of something that has never known sunlight, with a faint translucence that sometimes seems to pulse with the same blue-green luminescence as the fungi that light the chamber.

The Custodian's face remains largely hidden beneath a hood-like growth of specialized fungi that form a symbiotic relationship with their host, providing additional bioluminescence that aids in navigation. When the hood is pulled back during rare moments of focused work, one can glimpse features that retain a human foundation but have grown exaggerated with adaptation—enlarged eyes with pupils that dilate to impossible widths to capture minimal light, nearly translucent earlobes that have developed additional folds to better capture and interpret the complex acoustics of the chamber.

The Custodian moves with purpose but without haste, tending to the ancient hydraulic systems with tools fashioned from materials found within the cistern. Using delicate implements carved from the bones of creatures long extinct in the world above, they clear blocked channels, adjust the position of certain stones to modify water flow, and cultivate specific colonies of microorganisms that help maintain water purity. They speak rarely, and when they do, their voice emerges as a modulated whisper that somehow rises above the perpetual symphony of water sounds—a feat achieved through perfect understanding of the chamber's acoustics.

Their relationship with the fauna is one of mutual respect. The blind fish respond to subtle vibrations the Custodian creates by tapping specific sequences on the water's surface. The translucent amphibians often perch on their shoulders during inspection rounds, seemingly serving as additional sensory organs. Even the insects behave differently in their presence—the sound-navigating moths form constellations around their head when important maintenance is underway, providing additional illumination through bioluminescent abdomens.

The Custodian's diet consists primarily of specialized fungi cultivated in small gardens throughout the chamber, supplemented by mineral-rich water collected from specific drip points known only to them. They require little sleep, instead entering meditative states while standing perfectly still beneath particularly musical water flows, their consciousness seemingly diffusing into the acoustics of the space for hours at a time.

Evidence suggests the Custodian was once human—scattered among their few possessions are artifacts of disturbing antiquity: a tarnished silver pendant bearing an insignia from a dynasty that fell a millennium ago; engineering tools that match descriptions in ancient texts; and most tellingly, fragmentary journals written in increasingly distorted handwriting that track the gradual transformation of a city engineer who descended to repair the cisterns and never returned to the surface.

Whether by choice, accident, or some metaphysical process, the Custodian has become the embodiment of this place—the living interface between human creation and natural reclamation. They are the keeper of hydrological knowledge forgotten by the city above, the preserver of biological diversity unknown to science, and perhaps most importantly, the living memory of the cistern's original purpose, maintaining its function long after those who built it have turned to dust.

To encounter the Custodian is rare—they seem able to sense visitors and generally remain unseen. Those few who have reported brief meetings speak of overwhelming sensations: feeling they've communicated with something that thinks in centuries rather than moments, that perceives water's passage through stone as others might witness a dramatic sunset, that has transcended human limitations by embracing total symbiosis with this manufactured underworld.
