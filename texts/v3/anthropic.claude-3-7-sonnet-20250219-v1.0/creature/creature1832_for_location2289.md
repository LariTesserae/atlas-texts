# The Inhabitants of The Overgrowth

The Overgrowth is home to beings that defy simple classification—neither fully human, plant, nor animal, but something that exists in the interstices between these categories.

The most numerous are the Tenders, descended from those few humans who chose to remain when the marshland vegetation first began reclaiming the city. Over generations, they have undergone a profound symbiosis. Their skin bears patterns of fine chlorophyll-rich striations that deepen with age, creating swirling designs reminiscent of growth rings. These markings are not merely decorative—they harvest light, supplementing the Tenders' diminishing need for solid food.

The Tenders move with deliberate slowness, each gesture considered as if they operate on plant-time rather than human-time. Their eyes have adapted to see spectra invisible to conventional humans—they can perceive the subtle electromagnetic fields generated by growing things, allowing them to read the health and intentions of The Overgrowth itself. They communicate through a combination of abbreviated speech, posture, and the subtle release of chemical signals that other Tenders can interpret through smell and taste.

These beings cultivate personal relationships with specific sectors of The Overgrowth, becoming caretakers and translators. A Tender might spend decades nurturing a particular intersection where building and plant have merged into something greater than either. They understand that The Overgrowth is not merely a place but a process—one they are integral to maintaining.

Living among them are the Drifters—beings that began as ordinary fungi but have evolved to incorporate elements of both animal and plant life. These entities exist as networks rather than individuals, flowing through The Overgrowth in slow migration patterns that correspond to seasonal changes too subtle for outsiders to detect. Drifters manifest visibly as patches of bioluminescent mycelium that occasionally coalesce into vaguely humanoid forms when interaction with other inhabitants becomes necessary. They serve as the memory of The Overgrowth, storing information in their neural networks about every change and event over decades.

The most enigmatic inhabitants are the Amalgams—the massive, slow-moving creatures glimpsed in the deepest recesses. These beings emerged from the perfect fusion of building materials, plant matter, and the collective consciousness of small mammals and insects that inhabited both. An Amalgam might incorporate a partial facade of a former bank, the root system of an ancient banyan, and the distributed intelligence of thousands of marshland rodents, all functioning as a single entity.

Amalgams move with glacial deliberation, shifting position perhaps once per season. Their consciousness operates at a pace almost incomprehensible to humans, each thought unfurling over days rather than seconds. They serve as the immune system of The Overgrowth, consuming anything that threatens the delicate balance—from invasive species to technological intrusions. Despite their imposing presence, they display remarkable gentleness toward the Tenders, often altering their paths to avoid disrupting their work.

In the highest reaches of The Overgrowth live the Canopy Minds—beings evolved from the birds that first colonized the upper stories of the transformed buildings. Over time, these creatures developed a collective intelligence, with individuals functioning as neurons in a distributed brain that spans the entire upper layer. Their iridescent feathers serve as communication devices, flashing in patterns that transmit data across vast distances. The Canopy Minds monitor the world beyond The Overgrowth, their awareness extending miles into the surrounding marshlands.

Least seen but perhaps most influential are the Watershed Elders—amphibious entities that dwell in the water systems threading through The Overgrowth. These beings began as common frogs and salamanders but evolved in response to the unique chemical environment created by the plant-building fusion. Now they appear as translucent, elongated forms with internal organs visible through their skin—organs that often bear little resemblance to conventional anatomy. The Elders regulate water flow throughout The Overgrowth, their bodies filtering and enriching the liquid that sustains all other life.

Visiting humans often miss the most pervasive inhabitant: The Consensus—a distributed intelligence that exists in the interchange between all other beings. Not a single entity but rather the emergent awareness born from countless interactions between plant, animal, fungus, and mineral, The Consensus manifests as subtle synchronicities and harmonized growth patterns. It is the conductor of The Overgrowth's complex symphony, ensuring that all parts work together despite having no discernible central organ or location.

All these beings exist in a relationship of mutual dependence and transformation. None rules over the others; instead, they have evolved a complex governance system based on chemical signals, light patterns, and vibrations transmitted through the living infrastructure. The Overgrowth is not merely their habitat—it is an extension of them, and they of it, boundaries between self and environment having dissolved generations ago.

To enter The Overgrowth is to be perceived by all of these beings simultaneously—evaluated, categorized, and responded to according to intentions that cannot be hidden from the collective awareness that permeates every drop of water and beam of filtered light in this living cathedral.
