# The Temperants

Neither native nor entirely foreign to the Forge Basin, the Temperants emerged as the land itself began to form consciousness. They are not a species in any conventional sense, but rather manifestations of the basin's ongoing creation—living embodiments of geological processes that have somehow achieved a form of sentience.

Their physical appearance shifts constantly, much like the basin itself. Most commonly, they present as roughly humanoid figures composed of the basin's primary elements: semi-molten stone forms their core structure, with mineral crusts serving as a kind of exoskeleton. Crystalline growths emerge from their joints and cranial regions, catching light in hypnotic patterns. Their "skin" appears as a constantly moving slurry of the basin's copper-blue clay, interrupted by networks of glowing veins that pulse with thermal energy.

The Temperants stand between seven and nine feet tall when fully formed, though they rarely maintain a consistent height. Their limbs extend and contract as needed, sometimes stretching impossibly long to reach across thermal features or compress into dense masses when navigating unstable terrain. They leave no footprints, instead seeming to partially merge with the ground beneath them as they move.

What most distinguishes them is their respiratory cycle. They don't breathe as carbon-based life does, but they do undergo a rhythmic expansion and contraction that corresponds to the basin's own geological "breathing." During inhalation, their forms become more defined and solid; during exhalation, they release plumes of mineral-rich steam from vents along their torsos and emit a harmonic vibration just at the threshold of human hearing.

The Temperants exhibit clear intelligence, though it operates on a timescale and logic alien to human understanding. They communicate with each other through modulated heat signatures, vibrations conducted through the ground, and the exchange of crystalline growths that appear to contain information encoded in their structure. They demonstrate awareness of the basin as a whole system, responding to changes in its condition with coordinated behaviors.

Their relationship with the Forge Works is complex. The Temperants don't appear openly hostile to human presence, but they engage in subtle forms of resistance. They reconfigure thermal features to redirect resources away from collection towers. They accelerate mineral formation in pipelines, causing blockages. Most effectively, they manipulate the "emergent pools" to occur just beyond the reach of extraction equipment, forcing constant repositioning without directly confronting the human operation.

Workers at the Forge have documented encounters, though these reports are dismissed by management as heat-induced hallucinations. These experiences typically occur during the violent storms or at dusk, when visibility conditions make observation most uncertain. Workers describe feeling watched from just beyond the perimeter lights, seeing tall figures that seem to dissolve when directly approached, or hearing rhythmic tapping on the outer walls of their living quarters that matches no known wildlife pattern.

The Temperants appear to be experimenting with communication. Some workers report finding intricate patterns of mineral deposits outside facility doors—arrangements too precise to be natural formations. Others describe dreams of molten figures showing them the underground networks of thermal waters, as if attempting to convey the damage extraction causes to these hidden systems.

The most dramatic encounters occur during the formation of new thermal features. Workers who have witnessed these events describe seeing the Temperants gathered in concentric circles around the site, their bodies partially merged with the ground, seemingly directing the process through synchronized movements. During these rituals, the ambient temperature rises dramatically, and electromagnetic interference disrupts all monitoring equipment.

The basin's native wildlife demonstrates a complex relationship with these entities. The birds and insects neither fear nor avoid the Temperants, often landing directly on their crystalline protrusions. The burrowing mammals seem aware of their movements, adjusting tunnel systems to accommodate their paths. Most telling is that before an "emergent pool" forms, wildlife evacuates the area not at the first geological tremors, but precisely when the Temperants begin to gather.

The most troubling development for Forge Works management is that long-term personnel have begun to exhibit subtle physiological changes—mineral concentrations in their blood that match the basin's unique composition, altered circadian rhythms that align with the thermal features' cycles, and dreams they describe as "not their own." Some workers have started leaving offerings at the perimeter—small arrangements of mechanical parts or personal items placed at the edge of the facility's lights. When asked why, they struggle to articulate their reasoning, saying only that "it feels right" or that "the basin asked for it."

The Temperants appear to be growing in number and complexity as the basin continues its accelerated formation. Where once they seemed like semi-autonomous extensions of the geological processes, they now demonstrate coordinated purpose. Their forms become more defined each season, their behaviors more clearly intentional. The Forge Basin isn't just creating itself—it's creating a population to embody its will.

Whether the Temperants are guardians, manifestations, or evolution's response to industrial extraction remains unclear. What is certain is that as Harrowing Valley continues to write itself into existence, these entities will shape its story—and perhaps, eventually, speak it aloud in terms humans can no longer ignore.
