# The Glassfolk

The Seed harbors a people who exist as much in reflection as in physical form – the Glassfolk. Their translucent skin reveals intricate networks of amber fluid pulsing beneath, circulating like the plant sap around them. They stand tall and slender with elongated limbs that move with deliberate grace, each motion calibrated to navigate the ever-shifting crystal maze without collision.

Their bodies bear crystalline protrusions – not growths of disease but evolved adaptations that emerge during adolescence. These formations follow the geometries of their environment, jutting from shoulders, forearms, and along the spine. In the intense light of the Breath, these crystals activate, capturing energy that sustains them through the coming cycle.

The Glassfolk have no eyes as outsiders would recognize them. Instead, their faces feature prismatic sensory organs – flat, multifaceted surfaces that process light, heat, and vibration simultaneously. They perceive the world as patterns of refraction and resonance, "seeing" around corners through crystal reflections and sensing approaching crystalline growth hours before it manifests.

Their most remarkable feature is their voice. Each Glassfolk is born with a unique tonal range, a living instrument capable of producing harmonics that manipulate their environment. Children learn their "life-song" over decades, adding complexity as they mature. The oldest among them can produce chords of such mathematical precision that they can halt a crystal's growth mid-formation or accelerate a plant's development to create shelter in moments of need.

The society operates without hierarchy but with specialized roles based on vocal abilities. The Harmonizers maintain the acoustic balance of the Seed, preventing harmful resonances from developing. The Shapers coax the vegetation into dwelling structures. The Navigators memorize the ever-changing pathways and predict shifts through subtle vibrational cues.

Their reproduction is tied to the crystal cycles. During rare alignments when multiple reflection points converge, pairs of Glassfolk perform elaborate duets that last days, their bodies gradually synchronizing until they begin to refract each other's forms. From this acoustic and luminous communion, a new crystalline nodule forms between them, which they nurture with song for months until a child emerges – semi-translucent and humming instinctively.

The arthropods are not separate species but extensions of the Glassfolk themselves. Each family produces these "echoes" – crystalline resonators that act as external memory, storing ancestral songs too complex for biological memory. When a Glassfolk dies, their personal echo shatters, releasing stored harmonics that recombine with the dome's structure.

The Glassfolk possess an oral history suggesting they weren't always as they are now. Their songs tell of ancestors who came from "beyond the light" with solid bodies and crude implements. These ancestors discovered the dormant Seed and, through either desperation or curiosity, entered its confines. Over generations, the environment reshaped them, crystal by crystal, tone by tone, until they became its perfect inhabitants.

They are neither technologically primitive nor advanced but exist in a state of perpetual symbiosis with their environment. They understand instinctively that the Seed is both their salvation and their limitation – to leave would mean death, yet to remain means continued evolution away from their original form.

During the Breath, when all falls silent, the eldest Glassfolk assume a meditation posture, their crystals aligned precisely with the dome's structure. In these moments, they receive fragmentary impressions of the Seed's true purpose – not merely shelter but an experiment in conscious adaptation, a living ark designed to preserve life through transformation rather than stasis.

The Glassfolk do not fear death but dissolution – the failure to resonate properly with the Seed's harmony. Their greatest ceremonial punishment is "silencing" – the temporary binding of vocal cords for those who create destructive harmonies that threaten the delicate acoustic balance of their world.
