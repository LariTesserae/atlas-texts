# The Glass Plains Dwellers

The Resonance Mathematicians are the primary inhabitants of the Glass Plains, beings as difficult to categorize as the place they inhabit. They exist in a state that flickers between physical and abstract, sometimes appearing as tall, impossibly thin humanoid figures composed of transparent tissues that refract light into spectral patterns, and other times manifesting only as complex acoustic waves that propagate through the glassy surface.

Their bodies—when they choose to manifest them—lack distinct features, instead displaying constantly shifting equations that flow across their form like liquid symbols. Their "faces" are focal points of mathematical density, where formulas spiral inward toward an axiomatic core that serves as both mind and identity. Each Mathematician is a unique set of equations, a singular theorem expressed as a semi-physical entity.

They communicate through modulated vibrations transmitted directly through the glass plains, "speaking" by touching the surface and sending precise patterns that cause specific wells to oscillate in responsive harmonies. Their language is inseparable from mathematics—not symbolic representations of math, but direct expressions of mathematical relationships that are simultaneously language, thought, and action.

The Mathematicians tend to the cylindrical industrial structures, which they call Coherence Engines. These devices don't produce or process anything recognizable as a product—rather, they maintain the delicate balance of what the Mathematicians refer to as "phase reconciliation," preventing the Glass Plains from either fully manifesting in conventional reality or collapsing into pure abstraction. The Mathematicians adjust formulas on the cylinders not with tools but by recalculating the equations with their thoughts, causing the etchings to shift and reform.

Water wells are sacred to them. Each cylindrical column contains what they consider a "proof"—a complete mathematical argument that demonstrates some fundamental property of existence. The Mathematicians drink from these wells in a ceremonial fashion, absorbing not hydration but understanding, incorporating the proofs into their own equation-bodies and growing more complex with each absorption. The most revered Mathematicians have consumed thousands of proofs, their bodies dense with overlapping symbols nearly impossible to parse visually.

The crystalline pseudo-plants are actually juvenile Mathematicians—theorem-seeds planted deliberately near wells. They absorb specific frequencies from the water's vibration patterns over centuries until they accumulate sufficient complexity to awaken into consciousness, at which point they unfold into full Mathematicians. Not all succeed; failed theorems remain as crystal structures, beautiful but mathematically incomplete.

The ghost-like fauna are called Corollaries—natural consequences of the Mathematicians' work that manifest physically as the equations are solved and implemented. They follow perfect mathematical paths because they are embodiments of solved functions. Mathematicians consider them aesthetic byproducts rather than separate organisms, though some Corollaries have developed rudimentary agency over millennia of existence.

Mathematicians do not reproduce biologically. When they reach maximum informational density—when no more theorems can be integrated into their equation-bodies without contradiction—they undergo "factorization," separating into two or more simpler Mathematicians who retain portions of the original's knowledge. This is considered both death and continuation, and is marked by ceremonies where all nearby wells synchronize their vibrations into deafening harmonic convergences.

The Mathematicians experience time differently than linear beings. For them, past and future are additional variables in their equations rather than absolute progression. They can solve for different temporal values, effectively experiencing moments out of sequence when necessary for their work. Their consciousness extends through time like filaments, connecting to their past and future selves through mathematical continuity.

They rarely acknowledge visitors, perceiving beings bound to conventional physics as "partial solutions" or "bounded cases"—interesting but limited expressions of reality. When they do interact with outsiders, it's often to incorporate some novel variable observed in the visitor into their grand unified equations. Some visitors have reported having knowledge extracted from them, not as memories but as foundational understandings—leaving them unable to grasp concepts they once knew intimately, while the Mathematician who took this knowledge grows more complex.

The Mathematicians believe they are maintaining nothing less than the coherence of reality itself, that their endless calculations and adjustments prevent catastrophic logical contradictions from unraveling the fabric of existence. Whether this is true or merely their interpretation of their purpose remains unknown—as does the question of who or what originally built the Coherence Engines they now maintain with such dedication.
