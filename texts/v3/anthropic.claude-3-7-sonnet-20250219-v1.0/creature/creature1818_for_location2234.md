# Inhabitants of the Nascent Hollows

The Underfold dwell in these shifting depths, neither a single species nor truly separate beings. They are caretakers, witnesses, and in some sense, extensions of this birth-space itself.

At first glance, they appear as translucent humanoid figures with elongated limbs—skin like wet alabaster that captures and subtly refracts what little light exists. Their height varies dramatically, from child-sized to nearly twice human height, their proportions shifting as they move through differently sized passages. Their fingers are extraordinarily long and branched at the tips like root structures or coral formations, perfect for feeling vibrations through stone and manipulating the clay vessels they tend.

Their faces lack distinct features except when they wish to communicate, at which point impressions of eyes, mouth, and nose ripple across their surface like patterns in disturbed water. They don't speak so much as resonate—producing tones that somehow remain distinct beneath the geological cacophony, harmonizing with or counterpointing the constant groans of the earth.

The Underfold aren't born but form gradually from the mineral-rich waters, accreting substance over decades. Their "young" begin as barely visible disturbances in the water tension of certain pools—areas where the surface bends unnaturally, eventually developing into simple mobile globules that absorb elements from their surroundings. These proto-Underfold are tended carefully, moved between different mineral pools to "flavor" their development according to patterns understood only by the mature entities.

They possess an intimate knowledge of the hollows' formation, anticipating collapses and floods minutes or even days before they occur. The bioluminescent fungi are their creation and tool—cultivated colonies that respond to their touch by brightening or dimming. The Underfold can read the mycelium networks like texts, gathering information about distant chambers through the fungal communications.

Their relationship with human explorers is complex. They observe from just beyond perception, sometimes deliberately obscuring passages leading deeper or more dangerous areas. The clay vessels attributed to human visitors are actually their work—ritual containers that process the mineral water in specific ways, extracting compounds that sustain them. The patterns these vessels form are a kind of slow-working technology, gradually influencing the hollows' development over centuries.

What humans interpret as shrines are actually junctions in the Underfold's distributed consciousness—places where their awareness pools more densely. The carved figures weren't made to represent gods but are recording devices, storing impressions and memories in patterns of vibration within the stone itself. The offerings left around them are typically removed and studied, providing the Underfold with information about the surface world they rarely visit.

They move between solid, liquid, and gaseous states with slow deliberation. When threatened or needing to traverse unstable areas, they can temporarily diffuse into mist-like forms that flow through cracks before reconstituting. Their more solid forms can still pass through narrow fissures that would stop a human, their bodies compressing like thick fluid.

The Underfold maintain the delicate balance of this forming world—not preventing its natural development but guiding it toward greater complexity and stability. They integrate fallen rock into structural supports, redirect destructive water flows, and introduce carefully selected organisms from the surface to establish sustainable ecosystems.

Most notably, they sing to the stone—sustained, nearly inaudible vibrations that encourage certain fracture patterns and discourage others. This geophonic music requires groups of them working in concert, sometimes for years without interruption, to shape a single significant chamber.

Humans who spend enough time in the Nascent Hollows begin to sense their presence—first as a feeling of being watched, then as half-glimpsed movements, and finally as a subtle influence on thoughts and dreams. Those who leave offerings of unworked materials—raw clay, unspun fiber, uncut crystal—may find guidance in the form of spontaneously appearing luminous trails during dangerous moments.

The Underfold are neither malevolent nor particularly benevolent toward human visitors—they are simply engaged in work of a different timescale, cultivating a world that will reach its full expression long after current human civilizations have passed. They are gardeners of stone and darkness, midwives to the earth's slow dreams.
