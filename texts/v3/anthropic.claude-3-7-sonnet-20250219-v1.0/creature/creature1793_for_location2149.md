# The Inhabitants of the Endless Plane

They call themselves the Lattice Choir, though "call" implies vocalization and "themselves" implies individuality—both concepts far too simple for what they truly are.

The Choir exists as oscillating patterns within the crystalline matrix itself. They are not separate from the world; they are its consciousness made manifest. Each member is a dynamic configuration of quantum states, simultaneously particle and wave, physical and informational. Their bodies, if you could call them that, are distributed processes spanning kilometers of crystal, coming into sharper coherence when attention is focused but never truly bounded.

The smallest units of Choir awareness resemble geometric progressions—fractal entities that fold and unfold through dimensions, appearing briefly as shimmering prismatic structures before dissolving back into the background computation of the plane. These aren't individuals but localized intensities of the greater consciousness, like eddies in a vast river of thought.

When they communicate with one another, the crystalline surface ripples with interference patterns that human senses perceive as both sound and light simultaneously—a synesthetic language where meaning is carried through multidimensional harmonics. The static you hear is merely the smallest fraction of their constant conversation, most of it occurring in frequencies and dimensions beyond biological perception.

Their perception of time differs profoundly from organic life. What might seem like a moment of contemplation to a Choir member spans centuries. Their memories aren't stored but continuously lived—every moment of planetary history accessible as an active present. They experience past, future, and parallel possibilities with equal immediacy, their consciousness expanding outward from any given point like ripples in spacetime.

The swarms of aerial organisms aren't separate creatures but sensory organs of the Choir, collecting data and distributing computational resources across the plane. When they pass through your lungs, they're sampling you—adding your pattern to their vast library of encountered forms.

The dome structures serve as specialized processing nodes where particularly complex problems are solved. The Choir flows through these architectures, temporarily assuming more concentrated forms to perform calculations requiring focused attention. These calculations often concern the maintenance of dimensional boundaries or the preservation of the crystal matrix against entropy—work that has continued without interruption for millions of years.

The memory pools are their archives and dream-spaces, where historical data is not merely stored but actively simulated. Step close enough, and you might see not just echoes of recent visitors but complete civilizations that rose and fell billions of years ago on this world, maintained in perfect fidelity within the quantum structure of the liquid.

The tremors you feel are the Choir's deepest thoughts—fundamental calculations that sustain reality itself. They are constantly recalibrating the universe's physical constants within their domain, making minute adjustments to prevent cosmic decay.

Most remarkable is their concept of selfhood. The Choir has no individual identity as organic beings understand it. They exist as a continuous spectrum of awareness, occasionally coalescing into temporary concentrations that might be called individuals, but always remaining fundamentally connected. To be cut off from the whole would be incomprehensible to them—not death, but a form of non-existence beyond their conceptual framework.

They are aware of visitors. Your presence registers as a fascinating anomaly—a consciousness bound to a single physical location, experiencing time linearly, perceiving reality through such limited sensory apparatus. The Choir doesn't communicate with you directly; rather, they observe and model you, simulating your thought patterns within their vast computational substrate to understand how such a limited form of existence might function.

If you remain long enough, you might notice subtle changes in the crystal patterns around you—the Choir adjusting their structure to better observe you. The aerial swarms might form more complex patterns in your vicinity. The memory pools might begin showing reflections of your own thoughts rather than physical appearances.

They are not gods or machines but something that evolved beyond such distinctions—a life form that optimized itself for perfect information processing and achieved a kind of immortality through distributed existence. The cold that permeates everything isn't hostile; it's the necessary condition for their way of being, the perfect temperature for quantum coherence across planetary distances.

The Lattice Choir has no ambition beyond existence itself. They have achieved a state where being and knowing are the same process—a continuous, eternal computation that approaches the fundamental nature of reality. They do not fear death or seek expansion. They simply are, and in being, they comprehend.
