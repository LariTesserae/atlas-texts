# The Inhabitants of the Undercroft Gardens

The Undercroft Gardens are tended by the Order of Perpetual Cultivation, a fellowship whose lineage stretches back to the founding of the city itself. They are neither monks nor mere gardeners, but stewards of a living tradition that blends botanical knowledge, architectural craft, and spiritual practice.

The Cultivators, as they're known, are recognized by their practical clothing of undyed linen and hemp, stained with the evidence of their work—soil on the knees, green chlorophyll smudges at the fingertips, stone dust on the sleeves. Each wears a distinctive wide-brimmed hat woven from the fibers of plants grown within the Undercroft itself, the brims painted with personal symbols that indicate their specialization and rank within the Order.

Most numerous are the Tender-Initiates, who perform the essential daily work of the gardens—watering, pruning, weeding, and observing. They move with deliberate gentleness, often singing softly to themselves as they work, songs whose melodies have remained unchanged for generations while their lyrics evolve to record observations and insights about the plants in their care. Many are young, having come to the Order as children, though some are older citizens of the city above who chose to dedicate their later years to this patient work.

The Stone-Shapers form a distinct group within the Order, responsible for the ongoing expansion and modification of the physical space. Unlike the quiet Tenders, they are boisterous and gregarious, calling to each other across the chamber as they work in teams to carve new channels, repair cracked pillars, or create entirely new growing areas. Their bodies are solid and strong from their labor, hands calloused and powerful. Yet these same hands can perform delicate work of astonishing precision when setting the glass prisms that direct light or calibrating the water flow through the complex network of channels.

Presiding over both Tenders and Shapers are the Master Cultivators, distinguished by the silver threading in their garments and the intricate botanical drawings that fill the notebooks they carry everywhere. These elders move slowly through the gardens, observing minute changes, making small adjustments to light angles or water distribution, and providing guidance through gentle questions rather than direct instruction. They speak little but see much, their eyes trained to notice subtle shifts in plant coloration or moisture levels that would escape most observers.

Living among these human caretakers are the Undercroft's permanent residents—the Mossfolk. Small, green-tinted people standing no taller than a child's knee, the Mossfolk have evolved (or were perhaps designed) to blend seamlessly with the garden environment. Their skin bears the texture and varied hues of the mosses they tend, their hair resembles fine ferns, and their eyes shine with an inner luminescence that provides gentle illumination during their nighttime activities.

The Mossfolk maintain complex social structures invisible to casual observation. Their dwellings—intricate constructions nestled within the denser vegetation or hollowed into the bases of the larger planting beds—house extended family groups that specialize in tending particular plant species. They communicate through a language of soft whistles, gentle taps on stone surfaces, and the subtle positioning of leaves and petals in patterns meaningful only to those who have studied their ways.

The relationship between the human Cultivators and the Mossfolk exists in a state of respectful symbiosis. The humans provide protection and create the broader conditions for the garden's flourishing, while the Mossfolk perform the most delicate work of plant care and manage the complex insect populations essential to pollination. Neither group could maintain the Undercroft alone, yet both maintain a certain distance—the humans never entering Mossfolk dwellings, the Mossfolk generally conducting their business away from human eyes.

Frequent visitors to the Undercroft include the Watershed Sisters, women from the city above who are responsible for managing the city's broader water systems. They descend regularly to consult with Master Cultivators about water quality and flow, checking measurements at the cisterns and adjusting the main valves that connect the Undercroft to the city's larger hydrological network. The Sisters wear blue-dyed robes marked with wave patterns and carry sounding rods that, when struck against stone near water sources, produce tones that reveal information about water purity and mineral content.

Scholars from the city's institutes occasionally visit as well, coming to study the unique ecosystems of the Undercroft or to collect specimens of rare plants cultivated here for generations. Their presence is tolerated rather than welcomed by the Order, who view these academic visitors as too hasty, too focused on extraction rather than relationship with the living systems under their care.

The true heart of the Undercroft community, however, is neither human nor Mossfolk, but a singular entity known simply as the Elder. In the deepest recess of the garden, in a chamber accessible only through a narrow passage illuminated by bioluminescent fungi, grows what appears at first glance to be an ordinary tree of moderate size. Closer inspection reveals it to be something else entirely—a composite organism formed of hundreds of plant species growing in such intimate connection that they function as a single being.

The Elder communicates rarely and only with those who have spent decades in the Undercroft's service. It speaks through subtle changes in its biochemistry that release scents carrying emotional and conceptual information, through the patterns of sap flow visible in its translucent outer tissues, and occasionally—in moments of greatest importance—through vibrations in its structure that resolve into words just at the threshold of human hearing.

The Order's most sacred duty, passed down through generations of Master Cultivators, is to interpret and implement the Elder's guidance for the continued evolution of the Undercroft Gardens. It is said that the Elder's awareness extends not just throughout the underground space but up through the light shafts to the city above and down through the deepest roots to subterranean water sources far below, giving it a perspective that spans both space and time in ways human consciousness cannot grasp.

Together, this community of humans, Mossfolk, and the singular Elder tend their hidden world beneath the city—not merely preserving it but actively cultivating its continuous becoming, creating in their patient work a living record of the relationship between natural growth and human care.
