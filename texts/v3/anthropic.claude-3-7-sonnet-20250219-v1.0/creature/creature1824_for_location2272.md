# Inhabitants of Evermost

The Listeners are not a distinct species but a community—symbiotic collectives of organisms that have evolved to interpret and steward Evermost. Each Listener appears at first glance to be humanoid, standing about two meters tall with slender limbs that seem almost branch-like in their jointing. Closer inspection reveals their true nature: a densely woven matrix of mycelia forming their core structure, with specialized plant tissues providing mobility and sensory capability.

Their "skin" is a living tapestry of microorganisms—primarily cyanobacteria modified to process the perpetual moisture of Evermost, giving them a blue-green iridescence that shifts subtly as they move. This outer layer is not merely protective but communicative, changing patterns to reflect internal states and environmental information they're processing. When still, a Listener can become nearly invisible against the backdrop of Evermost's living architecture.

Listeners do not speak as humans understand speech. Instead, they communicate through a complex system of chemical exchanges, subtle vibrations, and light patterns. Their consciousness exists partially within their physical form and partially distributed through the mycelial networks that permeate Evermost. This gives them simultaneous awareness of events occurring throughout vast sections of the city-forest. When Listeners gather, they sometimes enter a shared consciousness state, their individual bodies becoming nodes in a temporarily unified mind.

They have no mouths but absorb nutrients directly from their environment through specialized structures that can extract energy from the same photosynthetic processes that power Evermost itself. When they rest—which they do infrequently, perhaps once per local month—they root themselves into specific junction points in the living architecture, becoming temporarily part of Evermost's vast system while their consciousness enters a different state of awareness.

Listeners' primary purpose appears to be interpretation. They can read the subtle fluctuations in Evermost's biological systems in ways that suggest the city-forest is communicating—or perhaps remembering. The Listeners move through the pathways with deliberate slowness, their limbs occasionally extending filaments into the living walls, forming temporary connections through which information flows. They appear to be archivists of a sort, maintaining a collective memory of Evermost's original purpose, though this memory seems fragmentary and incomplete.

The relationship between Listeners and Evermost is more complex than mere habitation. While they did not create the city-forest, they have evolved with it, their biology perfectly adapted to its conditions. There are indications that Evermost has, in turn, adapted to accommodate them—certain chambers within the living structures appear designed specifically for Listener gatherings, with specialized resonant qualities that amplify their vibrational communications.

Occasionally, a solitary Listener will undergo transformation, their form shifting and expanding as they incorporate greater portions of Evermost's mycelial network into themselves. These Elder Listeners become increasingly stationary, sometimes rooting permanently at major nexus points within the city-forest. Their consciousness expands proportionally, and they serve as living libraries and decision-makers for their communities. The oldest among them have existed for centuries, their memories extending back to times when other beings—perhaps the original engineers—still walked Evermost's pathways.

Listeners reproduce rarely and communally. When Evermost experiences one of its infrequent periods of accelerated growth—triggered by specific astronomical alignments that occur roughly once per century—multiple Listeners will gather in specially formed chambers deep within the primary trunk structures. There, they contribute genetic and mycelial material to create new proto-Listeners that develop within specialized pod-structures for several years before emerging fully formed.

They appear to have no concept of individuality as separate from their environment. A Listener touching Evermost's living walls perceives the contact not as interaction with something "other" but as communication between different aspects of a unified system. When Listeners encounter non-native entities in Evermost, they express neither hostility nor particular welcome—merely patient observation, as if waiting to determine whether the newcomer represents a potential disruption to the equilibrium they maintain.

The few outsiders who have encountered Listeners report an uncanny sensation—not of meeting alien beings, but of being perceived by Evermost itself. The city-forest has no eyes, yet through the Listeners, it sees. It has no mind as conventionally understood, yet through the Listeners, it thinks. And most disconcertingly, through the Listeners, it remembers something important that has been lost—a purpose or knowledge that the Listeners endlessly work to reconstruct from the living data encoded in Evermost's very structure.
