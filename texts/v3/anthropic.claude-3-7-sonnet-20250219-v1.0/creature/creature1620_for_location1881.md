# Dwellers of the Weeping Terraces

The Salt Tenders are the most numerous inhabitants of Sar Volin. These beings appear humanoid from a distance, but closer inspection reveals skin that ripples like water tension over a crystalline substructure. Their bodies are translucent in direct light, with visible mineral deposits that collect at their joints and form intricate patterns resembling circuitry across their torsos.

They stand between four and five feet tall, moving with deliberate grace. Their three-fingered hands constantly work the stone—not with tools, but through touch alone. When a Salt Tender presses its palm against the weeping rock, microscopic filaments extend from their fingertips, communicating with the mineral structures within. They don't harvest the salt so much as persuade it to crystallize in specific patterns, creating what they call "song maps" across the terraces.

Salt Tenders rarely speak aloud. Their primary communication occurs through vibrations they create in the water—subtle finger movements that ripple across pool surfaces in complex patterns. When they do vocalize, their voices blend seamlessly with the dripping waters, making it difficult to distinguish where nature ends and language begins. They have no permanent dwellings, instead merging partially with the stone during rest cycles, their bodies becoming temporarily indistinguishable from the terraces themselves.

The Mist Shepherds are solitary figures who tend the evaporation patterns across Sar Volin. Much taller than the Salt Tenders, they appear as elongated silhouettes within self-generated halos of controlled vapor. Their actual bodies are difficult to perceive—shifting columns of moisture that maintain humanoid shape through constant internal circulation. 

Mist Shepherds carry long staffs made from the copper-like wood of the terrace shrubs, using them to direct air currents and shepherd the accumulated vapor into "herds" that drift between terrace levels. They harvest certain vibrations from these mist collections, which the Salt Tenders believe contain memories of the mountain's formation. A Mist Shepherd can sometimes be glimpsed standing motionless for days at the edge of a particularly mineral-rich pool, staff planted in the water, gathering what they call "the older songs."

The Basin Architects are perhaps the most enigmatic presence in Sar Volin. These beings appear to be comprised entirely of the same pale stone as the terraces, yet they move with impossible fluidity. Their forms are geometric rather than organic—combinations of perfect circles, triangles and spirals that constantly reconfigure while maintaining their essential nature. 

They are responsible for the strange structures throughout the landscape, though no one has ever witnessed them actively building. Instead, they press their geometric bodies against the stone in ritualistic patterns, sometimes for years, after which new formations slowly emerge from the rock face. The Basin Architects are believed to be the oldest entities in Sar Volin, possibly predating the mountains themselves. They communicate exclusively through the manipulation of water flow, creating complex acoustic patterns that the Salt Tenders spend lifetimes learning to interpret.

The Shimmer Nomads are rarely seen directly. They exist primarily in the heat distortions between terrace systems, visible only as rippling humanoid outlines that appear and vanish with the fluctuations of temperature. Unlike the other inhabitants, they seem to have no physical interaction with the landscape, yet Salt Tenders insist they are vital to maintaining what they call "the balance of perceptions."

Scholars who have studied Sar Volin debate whether these beings truly exist or are manifestations of the landscape itself—consciousness emerging from the interplay of mineral, moisture, and light. The inhabitants make no such distinction. To them, the boundary between self and place is as permeable as the line between stone and water in the weeping terraces.

Most remarkable is how these diverse entities participate in the creation of Sar Volin's water-music. Together, they orchestrate the drips and flows through subtle manipulations of the environment, creating what they call "the endless conversation"—a dialogue between stone, water and consciousness that has continued uninterrupted since the first terrace formed. The most complex compositions can take decades to complete, evolving phrase by phrase as mineral concentrations shift and new weeping lines emerge from the stone.
