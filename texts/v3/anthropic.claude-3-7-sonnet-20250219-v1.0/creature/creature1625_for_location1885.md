# The Quartz Tenders of Kaval-Dur

The beings who navigate the ever-shifting crystalline labyrinth of Kaval-Dur are as fluid and adaptable as their homeland demands. They call themselves the Veshni, though outsiders often refer to them as Quartz Tenders or Crystal Shepherds.

Standing roughly the height of a human child, the Veshni possess semi-translucent bodies composed of a resilient gel-like substance. Their forms are vaguely humanoid—two arms, two legs, a torso, and a head—but their features shift subtly as they move, adapting to their surroundings. Their skin contains millions of microscopic crystalline structures that refract the omnipresent dawn light, causing them to shimmer with internal rainbows when they move.

The Veshni have no fixed facial features except for three multifaceted eyes arranged in a triangle across what serves as their face. These eyes change color according to mood—pale blue in repose, vivid purple in curiosity, deep crimson in distress. They have no visible mouth, instead absorbing moisture and nutrients directly through their permeable skin. Communication happens through a combination of crystalline resonance and deliberate light patterns they generate within their bodies.

The Veshni wear no clothing but adorn themselves with carefully cultivated crystal formations they grow directly from their bodies—elegant spirals, delicate filaments, and geometric patterns unique to each individual. These adornments serve as both status markers and personal expression.

What appears at first to be a primitive society reveals itself, upon closer observation, to be one of profound sophistication. The Veshni do not fight against the constant transformation of their city; they orchestrate it. Using specialized appendages that grow temporarily from their palms, they can accelerate, retard, or redirect crystal growth through touch. The shifting pathways of Kaval-Dur are not random but carefully maintained transit corridors, adjusted continuously by teams of Tenders who work in silent communion.

The pictographs carved into crystal surfaces are not permanent records but temporary instructions—a complex system of notation that guides crystal growth patterns. What appears to be primitive art is actually advanced architecture in process, plans being implemented rather than merely documented.

Veshni society operates without central leadership. Instead, they function as a collective consciousness, each individual intimately aware of the needs and activities of those nearby through the vibrations conducted by the crystal city itself. The fundamental frequency felt throughout Kaval-Dur is actually the sum of all Veshni consciousness—a continuous harmonic conversation that guides their collaborative work.

Their relationship with the silvery-blue plants is symbiotic. These plants—called shimmerleaf by outsiders—process and purify the water brought up through the crystal channels. In exchange, the Veshni carefully cultivate spaces for them to grow and protect them from being overwhelmed by expanding crystal formations.

The Veshni neither age nor die in conventional terms. When an individual has existed for approximately three hundred years, they undergo "crystallization"—a process where they gradually transform into a permanent crystal formation, becoming part of the city itself. This is considered the highest honor and culmination of existence. The consciousness of crystallized Veshni continues to contribute to the collective harmonic, adding wisdom and memory to the communal mind.

Reproduction occurs rarely and deliberately, when the collective determines new tenders are needed. Two or more Veshni combine small portions of their gel-matter, which gradually develops into a new individual over the course of several years, slowly gaining consciousness and connection to the harmonic.

The Veshni have little interest in the world beyond their crystalline domain, yet they recognize and accommodate the occasional visitor with patient curiosity. They create temporary stable pathways for outsiders and may even form rudimentary sound-producing organs to attempt verbal communication, though they find such methods inefficient and strange.

To the few outsiders who have earned their trust, the Veshni reveal their deepest purpose: they believe Kaval-Dur is growing toward a final, perfect configuration—a crystal lattice that will resonate with the fundamental frequency of existence itself. Their continuous work, spanning millennia, serves this single goal—the completion of a living city that will unlock mysteries beyond comprehension.
