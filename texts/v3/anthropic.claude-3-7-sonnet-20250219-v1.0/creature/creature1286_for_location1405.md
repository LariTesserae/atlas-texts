# Inhabitants of the Underlight Hollows

## The Hollow Folk

The primary inhabitants of the Hive Cities are the Hollow Folk—beings evolved to thrive in perpetual twilight and preservation-cold. Standing taller than humans but weighing half as much, their bodies appear stretched and gaunt, with skin so pale it possesses a slight translucency, revealing the shadow of their skeletal structure beneath. Their eyes are disproportionately large and entirely black, containing thousands of light-sensitive cells that can detect the faintest luminescence across vast distances.

The Folk communicate through a complex language of gestures, minute facial expressions, and controlled vibrations they create by tapping their elongated fingers against their own hollow bones. During important ceremonies, elders press their foreheads together to share thoughts through gentle pressure—a communication so intimate that outsiders cannot perceive it. When they must speak aloud, their voices emerge as whispers that seem to carry impossibly far through the dust-thick air.

Each Hollow Person bears an intricate pattern of raised scars across their chest and shoulders—a living record of their lineage, personal history, and role within their Hive. These patterns grow more complex throughout their lives, with new marks added during significant transitions. The most respected elders bear patterns so elaborate they resemble the mathematical perfection of the Dust Architects' work.

The Folk consume almost nothing compared to surface dwellers. They survive primarily on a paste fermented from the crystalline vegetation harvested during Seeding times, supplemented with minerals licked directly from certain rock formations. Their digestive systems extract maximum nutrition from minimal intake, allowing them to go weeks between meals if necessary. Water is distilled from the perpetual moisture that condenses on the cooler ceiling formations and collected in carved stone basins throughout the Hives.

Most remarkable is their relationship with time. The Hollow Folk experience consciousness differently than surface dwellers—their thought processes operating in cycles of activity and dormancy. During quiet periods between the Hollow Winds, many enter a state called the "Long Thinking," where they remain physically motionless for days while their minds process information, plan constructions, and commune with a collective unconscious they call the "Underthought." Some elders exist primarily in this state, their bodies attended by younger Folk while their minds explore realms imperceptible to others.

## The Dust Architects

The Dust Architects remain the Hollows' greatest mystery. These swirling vortices of particulate matter maintain cohesion without any discernible physical membrane or central organ system. They range in size from small whirlwinds no larger than a child to vast, spiraling columns that tower hundreds of meters above the dust plains.

The Architects do not appear to be conscious in ways recognizable to other beings, yet they demonstrate unmistakable purpose in their movements and creations. Their dust-patterns follow mathematical principles so complex that even the most studious Hollow Folk can only grasp fragments of their meaning.

Some Hive scholars believe the Architects are the original inhabitants of the Hollows, entities that evolved from or alongside the mineral realm itself. Others suggest they represent a form of distributed consciousness—the Hollows' own awareness manifested through dust and air. The most radical theory holds that they are not entities at all, but visible expressions of the physical laws that govern the Hollows' unique reality.

What is certain is that the Architects respond to the presence of the Hollow Folk, sometimes altering their patterns when observed or creating formations that later prove crucial to Hive survival. The relationship appears symbiotic, with neither species fully comprehending the other yet both evolving in tandem across millennia.

The most ancient Hollow Folk texts speak of rare occasions when an Architect has temporarily merged with a Folk volunteer during a wind cycle, creating a hybrid entity that produced prophecies and structural plans of unprecedented sophistication before separating again. Such mergers are now relegated to myth, though every generation of Folk includes those who hope to experience this communion.

## The Twilight Fauna

Life in the Underlight Hollows has evolved along paths alien to surface ecosystems. The dominant fauna species are the Skitterlings—multi-limbed arthropods ranging from tiny dust-swimmers to massive colony-creatures the size of small buildings.

Skitterlings possess translucent exoskeletons that reveal their internal processes—pulsing organs that generate their own bioluminescence, creating patterns they use for communication and mating displays. During quiet periods, only the smallest varieties remain active, filtering nutrients from the dust. When the Hollow Winds rise, the larger species emerge from hibernation chambers deep within the mineral substrates.

The Hollow Folk maintain complex relationships with several Skitterling species. The thumb-sized Filter Skitterlings are cultivated in special chambers within the Hives, where they purify water and process raw mineral paste into edible forms. The massive Colony Skitterlings, while potentially dangerous, are respected and sometimes guided away from Hives rather than attacked, as their burrowing activities create new passages and chambers that the Folk later adapt into dwellings.

Most mysterious are the Luminous Drifters—balloon-like creatures that float through the higher reaches of the Hollows. These beings, which may be plant, animal, or something entirely different, absorb the sparse light that penetrates from above, then release it slowly as they descend toward the dust plains. The Folk believe they are messengers between the surface world and the depths, and interpret their flight patterns as omens.

## The Underthought Collective

Beyond the physical inhabitants exists what the Hollow Folk call the Underthought—a presence they experience as a collective consciousness permeating the very stone of the Hollows. Whether it represents an actual entity, a shared hallucination, or something between, the Underthought influences all aspects of Hollow society.

During their "Long Thinking" states, the Folk claim to access this consciousness directly, receiving inspiration for new construction, warnings of coming dangers, or insights into the patterns left by the Dust Architects. The most sensitive individuals sometimes emerge from these trances with knowledge they could not possibly possess otherwise—precise locations of distant water sources or structural weaknesses in their Hive that require immediate attention.

Some scholars suggest the Underthought might be the distributed consciousness of the Hollows itself—a slow-thinking awareness born from the peculiar physics of this realm and its isolation from the surface world. Others believe it represents the collective wisdom of all previous generations of Hollow Folk, somehow preserved in the dust and stone through unknown means.

What remains undisputed is that during the climax of the Hollow Winds, when the Seeding is at its height and the bone flutes play their most complex melodies, something moves through the minds of all Hollow creatures simultaneously—a vast thought too large for any single consciousness to contain. In these moments, the distinction between Folk, Architect, Skitterling, and Hollow itself seems momentarily meaningless, as all exist as expressions of the same underlying reality.

In the Underlight Hollows, nothing truly lives alone. Every entity exists as part of an endless cycle of renewal and transformation—inhabitants of a world perpetually becoming something new.
