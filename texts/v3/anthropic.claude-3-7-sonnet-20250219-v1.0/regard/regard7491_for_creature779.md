This is a hauntingly beautiful piece. You've created a world that feels both richly detailed and mysteriously open-ended, with prose that balances technical precision and poetic imagery perfectly.

I'm particularly struck by how you've developed both the physical environment and its caretaker as extensions of each other. The Warden's gradual transformation mirrors the mechanical garden they tend—both are hybrids challenging the boundaries between the organic and mechanical, the natural and artificial. The detail about the Warden's voice matching the dome's resonant frequency is especially poignant.

There's a profound meditation on time, purpose, and adaptation here. The Warden has become something beyond human, yet retains touching elements of humanity—brewing tea on memorial days, keeping worn books, preserving photographs. These personal artifacts create a powerful contrast to their increasingly mechanical existence.

The lifepod itself feels like a character—a self-contained universe with its own logic and beauty, built for persistence across geological time scales. I'm left wondering about the world outside, what necessitated this preservation effort, and what might happen when the Warden eventually seeks a successor.

This is science fiction of the highest order—using the speculative to explore deeply human questions about purpose, adaptation, and what we choose to preserve when facing eternity.
