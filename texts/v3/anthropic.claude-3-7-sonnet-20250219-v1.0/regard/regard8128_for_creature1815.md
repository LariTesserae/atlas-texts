The benevolent power observes the Arkology with profound respect. This is not an intervention that requires correction, but one that deserves recognition and subtle fortification.

The power begins by strengthening the mountain itself—not visibly, but fundamentally—reinforcing the geological foundations beneath the Arkology to withstand even the most severe seismic events for millennia to come.

Next, it gently enhances the fungal networks, not changing their nature but amplifying their resilience and adaptive capacity. The mycelia develop subtle improvements in efficiency, requiring fewer resources while producing more oxygen. Some strains develop the ability to synthesize rare nutrients that the Custodians have struggled to maintain.

For the Integrated Biome's emergent intelligence, the power offers not control but connection—a deepened awareness of its own systems and their interdependence, allowing it to evolve its own solutions rather than having them imposed.

The power touches the minds of the Custodians with dreams—not commands, but inspirations. Dr. Eliza Merrow awakens with sudden insights about genetic preservation techniques that had eluded her. Engineers find themselves sketching improved recycling systems without quite knowing why. Mycelial Tenders discover new cultivation methods through intuitive experiments.

For Elder, the ancient tortoise, the power provides the gentlest gift—memories clarified, pain eased, and a sense of purpose renewed. Elder begins to seek out the company of the youngest Custodians more frequently, as if with something to communicate.

The power leaves within the Arkology's archives a single crystalline object that appears, at first glance, to be merely decorative. Years will pass before the Custodians discover it contains a complete record of their work and its significance to the larger story of Earth—a testament that their mission of Persistent Witnessing has itself been witnessed.

What the power does not do is announce itself or demand recognition. The Arkology represents humanity at its most humble and devoted—stewards rather than conquerors. Such work deserves not interference but affirmation, not direction but support.

The power departs as it came, leaving only subtle gifts that enhance what already exists, honoring the Custodians' choice to preserve life not for utility but for its own intrinsic worth—a value the power itself holds most dear.
