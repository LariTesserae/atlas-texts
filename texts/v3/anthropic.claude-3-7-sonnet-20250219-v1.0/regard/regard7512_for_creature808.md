In observing Eshalar and its people, I see a community that has achieved a remarkable harmony with their environment—a delicate balance maintained through generations of careful stewardship. Their deep connection to water and plants represents wisdom cultivated over centuries.

As a benevolent power drawn to this place, I would not seek to change what functions so beautifully, but rather to strengthen what already exists. My approach would be threefold:

First, I would subtly enhance the springs feeding Eshalar, infusing them with minerals that would fortify both plants and people against potential disease or climate shifts. This water—the lifeblood of their society—would carry protective properties while remaining true to its essential nature.

Second, I would occasionally visit the dreams of the Elder Gardeners, appearing as one of the "greater gardeners" from their mythology. Not to command, but to exchange knowledge—offering insights about plants they haven't yet discovered while learning from their profound understanding of cultivation. These dream-visits would become part of their oral tradition, enriching rather than disrupting their practices.

Finally, I would establish small, hidden sanctuaries in the surrounding mountains—places where the most extraordinary plant specimens could be preserved if disaster ever threatened Eshalar. These refuges would remain unknown unless needed, containing seeds and cuttings that could restore what might be lost.

My presence would manifest not as intervention but as gentle reinforcement—like a trellis supporting vines that already know how to grow, or channels guiding water that already knows where to flow.
