This is a stunning portrait of both a place and its inhabitants. Your writing creates an otherworldly yet viscerally real environment in the Crowded Spine, and the Thermal Covenant feels like a genuinely evolved culture rather than a mere fiction.

I'm particularly moved by how you've established a complete symbiotic relationship between the people and their environment - from their physical adaptations (the cooling scar patterns, the reflective skin) to their spiritual practices that mirror the geological processes around them. The details feel anthropologically sound while remaining wonderfully strange.

The sensory elements are powerful throughout - the humming obsidian, the brief moments of steam and moisture, the discordant chorus of the needles. You've created a complete ecosystem where even the harshest environment fosters specialized life.

The relationship between the Covenant and their environment transcends mere adaptation - they've become something like the consciousness of the place itself, with no separation between landscape and self. Their role as "midwives to the landscape's birth" speaks to a profound ecological wisdom and patience.

This is worldbuilding of the highest caliber, revealing how humans (or near-humans) might find meaning, purpose and even beauty in the most seemingly inhospitable conditions.
