*The benevolent power observes the Salt Carvers and their grottos with profound interest and respect. After careful consideration, this is what unfolds:*

# The Guardian's Gift

The presence arrived not as light or sound, but as a subtle change in the rhythm of air currents flowing through the grottos. The Salt Carvers sensed it first through their fingertips—a new pattern emerging within the familiar pulse of their home. They paused in their work, seven-forked hands hovering over intricate mechanisms, translucent eyelids blinking in recognition of something ancient yet new.

The benevolent power did not disturb or announce itself loudly. Instead, it observed for seven cycles of the grinding wheels, learning the delicate balance that sustained this improbable communion between beings and place.

It recognized the profound wisdom in the Carvers' existence—how they had not conquered their harsh environment but had instead entered into dialogue with it, allowing themselves to be transformed by what might kill ordinary creatures. The power saw beauty in their patient adaptation, their crystalline bodies emerging not from domination but from conversation with stone and salt and thin, cold air.

What could such beings possibly need? What gift could honor without disrupting the perfect equilibrium they had achieved?

On the eighth day, the change began—subtle enough that only the eldest Carvers noticed at first. In certain chambers where the light struck at particular angles, the salt-stone walls began to develop a new property. The crystals embedded within them started to absorb and store the harsh sunlight, releasing it later as a gentle, blue-tinted illumination that cast no shadows.

This light carried unusual properties. It strengthened the salt-stone without changing its essential nature, preventing collapses that would have claimed several ancient chambers within the next generation. Yet it did not halt the grottos' natural migration between the peaks—the power understood this movement was sacred to the Carvers, a physical manifestation of their philosophy of balanced transformation.

More remarkably, when the Carvers' seven-forked fingers touched these illuminated sections, they could perceive impressions of other places where similar balances had been achieved between beings and their environments—distant mountains where moss-people cultivated gardens of stone; deep ocean trenches where pressure-adapted philosophers built thought-cities from current and sand; edge-worlds where entities of mist and memory maintained archives of vanished civilizations.

The benevolent power had given them context—not by dragging them into the wider world, but by bringing echoes of distant harmonies into their own carefully maintained isolation.

The oldest Carvers, those already half-transmuted to crystal, recognized the gift for what it was. They gathered in the largest chamber and tapped out a complex rhythm against the resonant walls—a message that traveled through every tunnel and bubble of their suspended home:

*We are not alone in our way of being. Others walk the path of mutual transformation. Our journey has been witnessed and honored.*

The power made only one more modification—a small one, easy to overlook. In the chamber where new Carvers were shaped and brought into being, the mineral solutions gained subtle new properties. The next generation would develop an enhanced capacity for the percussion-language, able to encode more complex concepts in their wall-tapping communications. This would allow them to better articulate and preserve their understanding of balance and transformation as the grottos continued their millennial migration across the peaks.

Having offered these gifts, the benevolent power withdrew its direct presence, though it left behind a faint harmonic in the ever-present air current—a subtle frequency that would remind the Carvers they had been seen and valued precisely for what they were.

The power knew better than to "save" or drastically change these remarkable beings. Their adaptation was not something to be pitied or "corrected," but rather witnessed with reverence. The grottos and the Carvers were already complete—a living philosophy expressed through body and stone.

In their own tapping language, spreading across chambers and generations, the Salt Carvers added a new rhythm to their cultural memory—a pattern that approximated what we might call gratitude, though for them it conveyed something more nuanced: acknowledgment of being recognized without being altered in essence.

And in the violet sky above the suspended grottos, the smallest change appeared—a particular alignment of light that, when viewed through the Carvers' membrane-filtered eyes, revealed pathways between the stars, confirming what they had always suspected: that their patient journey across the peaks was part of a larger navigation, meaningful beyond their knowing.
