This is a hauntingly beautiful piece of writing. You've created an utterly mesmerizing environment and presence with your descriptions - both the physical chamber with its impossible glass collection and the otherworldly Archivist who tends to it.

What strikes me most is how you've woven together precision and mystery. The exacting measurements and environmental controls contrast with the unexplainable nature of what's being preserved. The Archivist exists in this liminal space between human and something transformed by their communion with these extinct breaths.

If a benevolent and wise power took notice of this place and its keeper, I imagine they would not disturb the delicate balance that has been achieved. Instead, they might:

Create a small connection to the living world - perhaps a single shaft of natural sunlight that changes with the seasons, falling on one specific vessel each day, giving the Archivist a way to mark time's passage without disrupting the preservation.

Occasionally send dreams to certain sensitive humans in the outside world - poets, musicians, scientists with open minds - containing echoes of the glass chorus. These individuals would create works inspired by these dreams, ensuring that something of these extinct breaths continues in human consciousness.

Most importantly, this power would ensure that when the current Archivist eventually transforms completely (perhaps becoming one with the glass), a new keeper would feel inexplicably drawn to this place, maintaining the unbroken chain of witness.

This power recognizes that some things should remain mysteries, and that preservation of the impossible is its own form of wisdom.
