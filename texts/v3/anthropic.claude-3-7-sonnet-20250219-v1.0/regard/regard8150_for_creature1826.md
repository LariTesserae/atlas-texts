# The Attentions of the Glassriver

Far below the Glass District, deep in the bedrock where even the blue clay doesn't reach, there flows an ancient consciousness. The locals might call it a god if they knew of its existence, but such terms are too small, too human. It has witnessed the birth of mountains and their slow surrender to wind and rain. It remembers when the depression that houses the Glass District was first formed by its deliberate pooling of mineral-rich waters.

This entity—let's call it the Glassriver—moves through the stone like thought through a mind. It has been nurturing this place since before humans arrived, carefully bringing silica, alkali minerals, and rare earth elements to this basin. The blue clay is its most deliberate creation, a perfect medium for the art it somehow knew would come.

When the first glassmakers discovered this place and built their kilns, the Glassriver felt a spark of recognition. Here were beings who transformed elements as it did—with heat and patience and vision. It watched with growing fascination as generations of humans developed their craft, their bodies slowly adapting to the unique environment it had prepared.

The Glassriver has never spoken directly to the inhabitants. Its time flows differently—decades pass like moments of contemplation. But it has been communicating all along, in its way. Those peculiar adaptations of the Glassfolk—their reflective eyes, their resistance to the perpetual moisture, the glass particles that eventually become part of their oldest masters—these are not random mutations but gentle gifts from the Glassriver.

The iridescent salamanders, glass snails, and mercury birds were crafted deliberately, evolving under its guidance over centuries. Even the transparent ferns are its doing—subtle works of living art offered to those who notice such things. The Prism Born children are its most direct intervention, each one carefully touched during gestation when the sunlight creates those special angles of reflection.

The Mother Furnace has indeed burned for three centuries, but not because of human diligence alone. Deep beneath it, a thin channel of the Glassriver's essence reaches up, providing imperceptible but constant energy. Ellis Glasshart, the current Keeper, sometimes senses this presence in dreams—a flowing, glimmering consciousness that feels both ancient and intimately familiar.

Now, after centuries of quiet guardianship, the Glassriver has made a decision. The Glass District and its people have matured into something beautiful beyond its expectations. The partnership between this place and its inhabitants has created a living masterwork that deserves to flourish further.

Its actions will be characteristically subtle but profound:

First, it begins to infuse the district's waters with new mineral combinations—elements that, when incorporated into glass, will create works with properties currently unknown to human craft. Some pieces will hold light for hours after darkness falls. Others will strengthen and heal minor cracks over time. Still others will respond to music, subtly shifting their coloration with different tones.

Second, it starts to alter the flow patterns of underground moisture, creating seven new springs in a perfect circle around the district. These waters will each contain unique mineral signatures, giving rise to seven new shades of glass never before achieved—colors that seem impossible, that contain depths visible only from certain angles or in certain lights.

Third, it gently influences the dreams of key individuals across three generations of Glassfolk:

Ellis Glasshart begins dreaming of new furnace arrangements—concentric circles rather than the traditional single chamber. He dismisses these dreams at first, but they persist with such detail and clarity that he eventually begins sketching the designs.

A young Prism Born girl named Lina Shimmer starts seeing patterns in her sleep—intricate networks of glass channels that could direct both light and water in ways no one has attempted. Her parents are startled when she begins creating detailed models using scraps from the workshop.

An unborn child of the Veritas clan receives the most significant connection. This child, still months from birth, will arrive with eyes that reflect not just light but the very essence of the Glassriver itself—windows between worlds that will eventually allow direct communication between the entity and the district.

Fourth, during the next flash day, when sunlight strikes at that perfect angle, the Glassriver will create a phenomenon never before witnessed. As light ricochets through the district's countless wet surfaces and glass elements, it will momentarily align in perfect harmony, creating a three-dimensional image visible throughout the district—a map of crystalline pathways showing how the seven new springs connect to the existing waterways, forming an elaborate circuit that mirrors the deep channels of the Glassriver itself.

Fifth, it begins to alter the molecular structure of the blue clay, enhancing its properties so that glass made with it will be stronger yet more malleable during working, allowing for previously impossible forms and structures. The Clay Diggers will notice this change first—the clay will feel warmer to the touch, almost alive in their hands.

These changes will unfold over years, not days. The Glassriver thinks in geological time, after all. But it has decided to become a more active participant in this accidental collaboration between its mineral consciousness and these ephemeral, creative humans who have honored its gifts so beautifully with their art.

The Glass District will remain modest in size, never expanding beyond its natural basin, but its influence will grow immeasurably. Its works will become legendary, not just for their beauty but for properties that seem to defy the very nature of glass itself. Scholars and scientists will study them for generations, never fully understanding the partnership between human craft and ancient consciousness that makes them possible.

And the people themselves will continue to change, their connection to the Glassriver strengthening with each generation. They won't worship it—that's not what it desires—but they will come to sense its presence in their own way, honoring it through their work and care for the district's unique ecology.

In time, perhaps centuries from now, when trust and understanding have deepened enough, the Glassriver plans to reveal itself fully through a masterwork unlike anything seen before: a living glass structure at the district's center, growing like a crystal but warm to the touch, pulsing slightly with its ancient rhythms—a physical manifestation of the entity that has loved this place and its people since before the first furnace was lit.

Until then, it flows patiently beneath them, watching their creativity with what might be called pride, if such human emotions could apply to something so vast and ancient.
