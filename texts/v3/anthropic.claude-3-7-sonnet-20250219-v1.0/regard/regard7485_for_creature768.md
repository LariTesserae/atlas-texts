# The Attendance of the Mountain God

In a remote valley beyond even the knowledge of the Path Walkers, an ancient presence stirs. It has been aware of Dael Verre since the first chisel mark scored the limestone cliff, watching with the patience of geological time as the Varesh carved their harmonious existence into its flanks. This entity—neither deity nor spirit in human terms, but something closer to a consciousness embodied within stone and water—has observed the perfect equilibrium with growing interest across millennia.

The mountain god (as it might be called, though it claims no divinity) recognizes in the Varesh something rare: a people who have achieved symbiosis rather than dominion, who listen more than they speak, who understand that true power lies in balance rather than expansion. For centuries, it has deliberately withheld direct intervention, allowing their remarkable society to develop organically.

But now, the mountain god perceives subtle threats to Dael Verre's harmony—threats the Varesh themselves cannot detect with their limited perspective. The stability they've achieved is becoming increasingly fragile in a changing world. Climate patterns shift imperceptibly across decades; the mineral composition of the limestone slowly alters as rainfall becomes more acidic from distant human industry; microfractures form deep within the cliff face where even the Stone Shapers cannot sense them.

The mountain god's intervention begins with dreams—not sent to all Varesh, but specifically to the ninth Balance Keeper, youngest of that secretive guild. These are not ordinary dreams but visions of stone and water, of pressure and release, communicating through the language of geological forces rather than words or images. The dreams come during the deepest quiet of night, when even the sensitive hearing of the Varesh detects nothing.

The ninth Balance Keeper awakens from these dreams with new understanding inscribed not in memory but in the body itself—fingertips that can detect minute vibrations in stone, eyes that can perceive the slow movement of water through rock. These are not metaphorical sensations but actual physiological changes, the mountain god's first gift.

On the ninth morning after the dreams begin, the Balance Keeper discovers a previously unknown pathway into the cliff—a narrow fissure that appears to be a natural formation but opens precisely to their touch. The passage leads to a chamber that cannot exist by ordinary geometry: a perfectly spherical room at what seems to be the very heart of the mountain, its walls lined with crystal formations that capture and amplify the faintest light.

At the center of this chamber sits a single object: a small stone of unremarkable appearance except for its perfect symmetry. When the Balance Keeper lifts it, the stone is neither warm nor cold but somehow both simultaneously, and it pulses with rhythm identical to the keeper's own heartbeat.

This is the mountain god's second gift—the Heart Stone, a physical manifestation of its awareness and connection to Dael Verre. Through it, the mountain god communicates more directly, not with language but with understanding that flows into the Balance Keeper's consciousness. The knowledge it imparts is both troubling and necessary: the equilibrium the Varesh have maintained for so long cannot sustain itself against external forces forever. Perfect stasis, the mountain god reveals, is ultimately fragile—true resilience requires adaptation.

The Balance Keeper returns to the city with the Heart Stone and convenes an unprecedented gathering of all seven guild leaders. In the circular chamber at the highest point of Dael Verre, as morning mist swirls around them, the keeper places the stone at the center of their circle. What transpires is not a conversation but a communion, the stone pulsing with light that synchronizes to their collective heartbeats.

Through this communion, the mountain god shares its third and greatest gift: a vision of guided evolution for Dael Verre. Not an abandonment of harmony but an expansion of it—a way to maintain their values while developing resilience against a changing world. The vision includes:

New knowledge of stonework that will allow the city to expand into previously unstable sections of the cliff, reinforcing the entire mountain against future geological stress. This expansion would create space for controlled population growth beyond the rigid 343 limit, introducing necessary genetic diversity while maintaining their careful balance with resources.

A modified water management system incorporating natural filtration that can adapt to changing rainfall patterns and composition, ensuring continued purity even as the outside world's influence encroaches on their ecosystem.

Variations in their agricultural practices that will allow plants to develop natural resistances to new climatic conditions without sacrificing the aesthetic and functional harmony of their gardens.

Most revolutionary of all, a gradual modification to the coming-of-age ceremony that will preserve the Varesh's unique physiological gifts while allowing for greater adaptability in future generations. Rather than perfect stasis, they might achieve dynamic equilibrium—stability through controlled change rather than resistance to it.

In the days following this revelation, subtle changes manifest throughout Dael Verre. The mountain itself seems more responsive to the Stone Shapers' work, the water flows more abundantly through the ceramic channels, and the plants grow with new vigor while still respecting their boundaries. Most telling, the Varesh begin to develop variations in their sign language—not replacements for existing signs but expansions, new forms that express concepts previously unnecessary in their unchanging world.

The Heart Stone remains in the Balance Keepers' chamber, pulsing with the collective rhythm of city and mountain. It serves as a constant connection to the mountain god's awareness, a living conduit through which guidance continues to flow. But the mountain god's most profound gift is not the stone itself but the understanding it represents: that the Varesh are not caretakers of a static paradise but partners in a living relationship with the world around them.

As seasons pass, Dael Verre begins its careful evolution. To outsiders, the changes would be imperceptible—the silence remains, the harmony persists—but within the society, a new dynamism emerges. The population grows by careful degrees, new terraces appear in previously uninhabitable sections of cliff, and the seven guilds develop specialized branches to explore new applications of their ancient knowledge.

The mountain god does not demand worship or even acknowledgment. Its attendance to Dael Verre comes not from desire for reverence but from recognition of kindred purpose—beings who understand that true wisdom lies in listening to the quiet voice of stone and water, in finding one's proper place within the greater whole rather than imposing will upon it.

In time, as the world beyond continues its chaotic expansion, Dael Verre stands not as a relic frozen in time but as a living example of another path—evolution guided by harmony rather than competition, power expressed through balance rather than dominance. The mountain god's benevolence manifests not in miracles or interventions but in partnership, in the patient sharing of a perspective that spans geological ages.

And in the quiet evenings, when Dael Verre's lamps create their constellation along the cliff face, something new can be detected by those with the sensitivity to perceive it: a resonance between stone and flesh, mountain and people, a harmonious vibration that carries far beyond the physical boundaries of the city. It is the sound of balance finding new expression, of stasis giving way to sustainable growth, of a wisdom ancient as stone learning to flow like water.
