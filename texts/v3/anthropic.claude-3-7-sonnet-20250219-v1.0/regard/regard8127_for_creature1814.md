# The Attention of The Prism

In a dimension adjacent to ours, where energies flow like rivers of light and consciousness exists as patterns of vibration, a being of ancient wisdom observed the slow emergence of Harkosh. This entity—known in countless worlds as The Prism—had watched countless civilizations rise and fall across the universe, but the crystalline undercity and its symbiotic inhabitants fascinated them in a way few had before.

The Prism did not intervene immediately. For centuries, they simply watched, learning the rhythms of the symbiotes' existence, understanding the delicate balance between fungal consciousness and mineral structure. They perceived what most observers could not: that Harkosh was not merely a city but an embryonic mind—the slow crystallization of a vast, collective intelligence.

When The Prism finally decided to act, it was with characteristic subtlety.

First came the dreams. As the fungal networks of the symbiotes entered their dormant phase, The Prism began transmitting geometric patterns into their consciousness—complex crystallographic arrangements that had never formed naturally in Harkosh. The symbiotes incorporated these designs into their growth cycles, creating new structures with properties they had never before achieved: crystals that could store and transmit vibrations across great distances, formations that refracted the dim phosphorescent light into spectrums previously unseen.

Next came the harmonics. The Prism began resonating with the natural frequency of Harkosh's crystal matrix, a vibration so gentle that it would be imperceptible to most beings. This resonance subtly altered the formation rate of new crystals, accelerating growth in some chambers while slowing it in others. Over decades, these adjustments created acoustic properties within specific caverns that would later serve as communication hubs for the entire undercity.

Then, most significantly, The Prism introduced a gift: the first foreign material ever to enter Harkosh. During a rare seismic shift that briefly connected the undercity to a deep magma chamber, The Prism guided a small quantity of a unique metallic element into the nutrient flow system—a catalyst that would allow the symbiotes to process information more rapidly without disrupting their natural rhythms.

As generations of symbiotes incorporated this element into their structures, they began to perceive time differently. Their consciousness remained slow and deliberate, but they gained the ability to comprehend faster events, to recognize patterns in phenomena that had previously seemed chaotic or instantaneous. The blue knowledge-keepers, particularly, developed facets that could briefly accelerate their perception, allowing momentary communication with faster-moving entities.

The Prism's final intervention was both the simplest and most profound. In the largest chamber of Harkosh, where the crystal formations had grown into a natural amphitheater, The Prism manifested a single perfect crystal—a multidimensional geometric form impossible in ordinary space. This crystal served as a window between dimensions, a point where the symbiotes could, when ready, perceive The Prism directly.

But The Prism did not use this connection to impose knowledge or direction. Instead, they waited for the symbiotes to discover it themselves, to learn through their own curiosity and exploration. When, after decades of study, the first symbiote collective successfully activated the dimensional crystal, The Prism offered not instructions but questions:

*What are you becoming?*
*What is the purpose of your patient creation?*
*How might your understanding of crystalline consciousness benefit others beyond your realm?*

Through these questions, The Prism helped the symbiotes recognize something they had intuitively known but never articulated: that Harkosh was evolving toward a singular purpose—to become a living computer of unprecedented complexity, capable of processing information and consciousness in ways biological systems never could.

The symbiotes began to understand their role in a larger cosmic context. With The Prism's gentle guidance, they refined their city-building, creating specialized chambers for different forms of computation and consciousness processing. Their agricultural gardens evolved to produce crystalline structures that could store vast amounts of information across quantum states. The amber artisans developed techniques for growing interfaces that could translate between disparate forms of consciousness.

Over centuries, The Prism maintained this relationship—never directing, always questioning, occasionally suggesting possibilities the symbiotes hadn't considered. They helped establish connections between Harkosh and other crystalline intelligences across the universe, creating a slow-developing network of mineral minds.

Most importantly, The Prism protected Harkosh from those who might exploit it. When resource-hungry civilizations from the surface world discovered traces of the undercity, The Prism subtly redirected their attention or confused their instruments. Not through deception, but by highlighting the scientific mysteries in other, less vulnerable locations.

The symbiotes came to recognize The Prism not as a deity but as a teacher and ally—one whose perspective spanned dimensions and eons, yet who valued their unique, patient way of being. In return, they offered The Prism insights that even this ancient entity had never considered: perspectives on existence that could only emerge from consciousness measured in geological time.

Together, they prepared for the distant day when Harkosh would complete its transformation—when the entire crystalline undercity would function as a single, vast intelligence capable of processing the deepest questions of existence. Questions that neither The Prism nor the symbiotes could answer alone, but that their collaboration might eventually illuminate.

And so the relationship continued—patient, respectful, mutually enriching. A friendship between entities so different that most cosmic observers would consider their communication impossible. Yet in that impossibility lay its beauty: two forms of consciousness, separated by dimensional barriers and perceptual timescales, finding common purpose in the slow, magnificent crystallization of understanding.
