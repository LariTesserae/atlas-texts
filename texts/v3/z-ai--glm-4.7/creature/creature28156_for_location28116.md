In the Rusted Nursery, the distinction between "who lives here" and "what is happening here" is a distinction without a difference. Because the fauna index is effectively zero, one does not look for animals; one looks for *agency*. Life here is not defined by breath or blood, but by motion, appetite, and the capacity to endure the roar.

The inhabitants of the basin are the plants, the structures, and the strange, semi-sentient phenomena that breathe in the gaps between them. They are a taxonomy of function fused with flesh.

### The Apex: The Piston-Flora
The primary citizens of this drowning world are the towering, aggressive engines of the forest. They are not merely growing; they are operating.

*   **The Bellows-Willows:** These are the giants, standing up to forty feet tall, their trunks composed of nested, flexible cylinders of braided copper and hard, green wood. They do not have leaves in the traditional sense; instead, they possess thousands of vent-like flaps running the length of their branches. They draw in the hot, steamy air and compress it internally, using a process of violent internal combustion to fuel their growth. Every thirty seconds, a Bellows-Willow exhales a deafening *hiss-THUMP*, blasting a cloud of superheated, spore-laden steam into the twilight. They are the rhythm section of the forest’s roar, a slow, heaving heartbeat that shakes the water.
*   **The Saw-Palms:** Growing low in the water, these are opportunistic scavengers. Their "fronds" are elongated, serrated blades of obsidian-hard chitin that rotate slowly on a central axis, powered by the hydraulic pressure of the silt they root in. They wait for debris—organic or industrial—to float by, then spin into a frenzy, chopping the matter into a slurry that their filter-feeding roots absorb. To wade through a grove of Saw-Palms is to stand inside a thousand invisible blender blades; the water around them churns with a frothy, red-tinted mist.
*   **The Snap-Vines:** These are the hunters of the undergrowth. Thick, black cables coated in a greasy, rubbery slime, they draping themselves across the sharp basalt shards. They possess a tension so high that a mere touch causes them to recoil with the velocity of a struck whip. They do not eat insects; they eat *motion*. When the turbulent water slams a floating log or a piece of debris against the rocks, the Snap-Vines detect the vibration and instantly constrict, shattering the object and absorbing the kinetic energy into their cellular structure.

### The Scavengers: The Ferrous-Fungi
Where the piston-flora are the architects of noise, the things growing on the *underside* of the world are the agents of decay.

*   **The Rust-Molds:** These are vast, colonies of orange, powdery organisms that cling to the submerged ruins. They are distinct because they *move*. Sliding across the metal like living velvet, they secrete acids that can eat through steel in hours. They are the grazers of this world, "farming" the metal of the sunken gantries and bunkers. As they consume the iron, they vibrate, creating a low, constant thrumming hum that adds to the basin’s cacophony. They are blind, stomachless, and relentless, slowly dissolving the industrial history of the basin into nutrient soup.
*   **The Heat-Leeches:** anchored to the hottest vents in the silt, these resemble clusters of crystalline tulips with translucent, quartz petals. They do not photosynthesize; they thermosynthesize. They draw the raw heat from the water and the earth, converting thermal violence into a cold, blue bioluminescence that pulses rhythmically in the gloom. They are the cold spots in the warm water, creating tiny, localized vortices of steam where the boiling water meets their freezing aura.

### The Built: The Gantry-Ghosts
The prompt notes the "built" environment is tragic scaffolding, but in the Rusted Nursery, even the structures seem to possess a dim, halting consciousness. They are the undead of the basin.

*   **The Grinding Gears:** Half-buried in the silt are the colossal teeth of industrial machines. They are not alive, but they are not dead. The geothermal heat causes the metal to expand and contract in rhythmic fits. This expansion mimics the movement of muscles. A gear the size of a house will groan, shift three inches to the left, and then lock back into place with a sound like a gunshot. They are mimics of life, trapped in an endless loop of thermal contraction, creating the sharp, jolting percussion that underscores the forest’s roar.
*   **The Bunker-Hearts:** The leaning concrete bunkers are not empty. Inside, they have been colonized by a dense, wiry moss that acts like a nervous system. The moss connects to the bunker's old electrical wiring, long dead, but now repurposed as vascular tissue. When the rain pounds the roof, the moss conducts the vibration through the wires, causing the bunker to "shudder" in a way that feels intentional. If you press your hand against the warm concrete, you can feel a faint, trembling pulse—not a heartbeat, but the echo of a machine dreaming that it is still alive.

### The Anomalies: The Glitch-Fauna
And then there is the 0.1%. The instability of reality allows for things that exist only in the margins of physics.

*   **The Wireframe Skulkers:** These are creatures of the "un-zipped" moments. They rarely fully manifest. They appear as flickering, static-filled outlines of canine shapes, composed of blue light and mathematical errors. They do not interact with the water; they walk *on top* of the surface tension as if it were solid glass. They are attracted to the sound of breaking metal. When a shard of basalt cracks or a tree snaps, the Skulkers phase into existence, feeding on the energy of the break. They are silent—completely silent—which makes them terrifying in a world of deafening noise. They are the auditory voids, gliding through the chaos, eating the fractures in the world.
*   **The Echo-Shells:** Strange, nautilus-like structures made not of calcium, but of solidified sound. They grow in the water, precipitating out of the roar. They are stationary, but they "record" the violence around them. If you crack one open, it doesn't bleed; it screams. It plays back the sound of the forest at the moment of its creation—a deafening cacophony of a tree falling or a vent exploding. They are living grenades of noise, waiting for a clumsy foot to shatter them and unleash a brief, localized hurricane of sound.

### The Solitary Exception (The True 0.1%)
Amidst the mechanical flora and the quantum ghosts, there is one true biological animal. It is small, pale, and blind.

*   **The Silt-Glider:** A creature about the size of a rat, but elongated like a ribbon. It has no legs, only a single, powerful fin running down its back. It possesses the unique ability to perfectly synchronize its movement with the turbulent surges of the water. It does not swim; it *surfs* the panic of the basin. It is deaf; the roar would kill it otherwise. It hunts by sensing vibration through its skin, darting out to eat the spores released by the Bellows-Willows before they can germinate. It is the only thing here that breathes, bleeds red, and feels pain. It hides in the hollow, rusted-out bones of the ancient gears, trembling in the dark, the last remnant of a softer world, struggling to survive in a place that has decided to become a machine.
