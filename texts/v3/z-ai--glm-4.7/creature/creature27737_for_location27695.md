To survive in the Sump is to survive inside the throat of a beast that is constantly swallowing. Here, biology has not just adapted to the industrial landscape; it has mimicked it, evolved alongside it, and become a parody of it. The fauna of this sunken factory floor are a ecosystem of scavengers, hunters, and drifters, all shaped by the rhythmic violence of the pumps and the cold, crushing weight of the deep.

**The Scavengers: The Silver Shivs**
The most ubiquitous life forms are the small, darting anomalies known as "Silver Shivs." These are not fish in the traditional sense, but gelatinous, vertebrate-like slivers encased in a hard, silvery exoskeleton that mimics the chrome plating of the machinery around them. They are shaped like jagged shards of metal—streamlined, angular, and razor-sharp.

Shivs possess no swim bladder; instead, they maintain buoyancy through internal bladders filled with pressurized methane harvested from the chemical leaks. They move in massive, flashing schools that scatter light like thrown coins, making them difficult for predators to track individually. They are the vermin of the Sump, swarming the "bleeder" pipes where toxic sludge seeps into the water, filtering out the heavy metals and hydrocarbons to fuel their own metallic metabolism. When the great intake valves groan open, Shivs are the first to rush in, fearless in the face of the suction, feeding on the organic slurry that gets caught in the preliminary filters.

**The Grazers: Rust-Rakers**
On the vast, flat expanses of the pleated steel bulkheads, where the current is slow enough for sediment to settle, live the Rust-Rakers. These are massive, slow-moving crustaceans that look like living bulldozers. Their shells are thick, pitted iron, grown from the minerals they consume.

They possess immense, shovel-like mandibles designed for scraping the ubiquitous orange "Rust-Moss" from the metal surfaces. Their legs are jointed hydraulics of muscle and chitin, ending in magnetic tips that allow them to grip the steel ground even when the artificial tides begin to churn. They are solitary and silent, leaving behind trails of polished, clean metal that glitters in the gloom. They are nearly blind, their eyes atrophied from generations in the twilight, navigating instead through sensitive bristles on their underbellies that detect the vibration of the grinding gears nearby. When a piston slams down nearby, a Raker will freeze, flattening its body against the bulkhead to become indistinguishable from a rivet or a steel plate.

**The Climbers: Gear-Grippers**
Hanging from the underbelly of the massive I-beams and perched on the precipices of the tetrahedrons are the Gear-Grippers. These are the amphibious reptilian predators mentioned in the lore of the depths, evolved for verticality. They are horrifically strong, with limbs that end in three-pronged hooks capable of puncturing solid steel or gripping the smoothest polymer.

Their skin is not scaled, but rubbery and black, absorbing the faint vibrations of the machinery to mask their own presence. They are ambush hunters that lurk in the shadows of overflow vents. When the pumps surge, flushing out the Shivs and smaller prey, the Gear-Grippers strike with explosive speed. They do not swim so much as they "fall" with control, using the gravity of the suction to pull prey into their waiting jaws. Their most terrifying feature is their bio-luminescence; they possess patches of glowing skin along their necks that pulse in time with the arc-welders in the distance, a hypnotic lure that draws the curious and the doomed toward the darkness between the beams.

**The Parasites: Wire-Worms**
Wherever there is a joint in the machinery, a loose seal, or a crack in the concrete, there are Wire-Worms. These are long, sinuous creatures resembling bundles of frayed electrical cables. They are opportunistic hunters, burrowing into the insulation of the sunken tech to steal warmth.

They possess a sharp, rotating beak that can drill through rubber and soft metal. Once inside a machine, they coil around the heated components, entering a torpid state of hibernation. They are a plague on the already decaying infrastructure, often causing short-circuits that result in the massive, silent sparks seen in the distance. When disturbed, they uncoil violently, lashing out with thousands of microscopic, stinging filaments that deliver a neurotoxin designed to paralyze the muscles of larger creatures, turning a potential predator into a meal for the swarm.

**The Leviathans: Hum-Backs**
In the hollow, cathedral-like emptiness between the massive city-sized pillars, the true masters of the Sump swim. These are the Hum-Backs, whale-sized creatures that have evolved to ignore the noise of the deep, or perhaps to embody it.

Their bodies are elongated and segmented, looking less like flesh and more like the submerged hulls of ancient submarines. Their skin is thick and scarred, a map of collisions with knife-sharp debris. They do not hunt actively; they are filter feeders of the microscopic industrial dust that clouds the water. Their mouths are massive caverns that constantly gape open, sieving the water for particulates.

Most incredibly, the Hum-Backs have developed a form of biological echolocation that syncs with the pumps. When the massive thrumming of the turbines reaches a crescendo, the Hum-Backs release a low-frequency groan that harmonizes with the machine's vibration. This allows them to communicate across miles of deafening roar, coordinating their movements in a slow, majestic dance that looks terrifyingly mechanical. They are the moving mountains of the Sump, indifferent to the sharks and crabs below, so vast that they have become part of the terrain themselves.

**The Dwellers: Valve-Spirits**
Deepest of all, living inside the "processing gears" where no light reaches and the pressure is lethal, are the Valve-Spirits. These are pale, eyeless humanoids that have adapted to the high-pressure pockets of air trapped inside the submerged ruins. They possess no lungs, breathing instead through a series of gills directly in their bloodstream.

Their limbs are atrophied, unused in the cramped confines of the pipes, but their hands are enormous and sensitive, capable of feeling the minute fluctuations in water pressure that precede a catastrophic failure. They are the custodians of the deep, scraping grime from the intake valves and clearing blockages to ensure the flow of water continues. Whether they were once human or simply evolved from deep-sea primates to fill this niche is unknown, but they scuttle in the darkness, performing their maintenance tasks with a terrifying, alien intelligence, ensuring the machine keeps running so that their world does not crush them.
