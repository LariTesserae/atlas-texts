Here, in the violet, viscous suspension where time folds into a flat circle, the fauna does not scuttle or dart. To move quickly would be to invite the kiss of the obsidian teeth that jut from the young, arrogant floor. Instead, the inhabitants of the Grief-Pocket are the **Drift-Looms**.

They are solitary, gelatinous entities that resemble nothing so much as a spilled pocket of air, a physical impossibility given the pressure, yet there they are—elongated, teardrop-shaped beings composed of a translucent, pale tissue that glows with the faint, sickly luminescence of rotting moonlight. They are nearly invisible against the gloom, visible only by the distortion they cause in the heavy brine and the rhythmic pulsing of the bioluminescent coral that naturally blooms along their dorsal ridges.

Because the light is negligible, the Drift-Looms have evolved to abandon sight entirely. Their bodies are smooth, unbroken by lenses or pupils. In the place of eyes, they possess a sensory system of **tactile empathy**. Their skin is a sheet of ultra-fine cilia that reads the vibration of the water itself. They do not just feel the currents; they feel the *intent* of the space. They can sense the microscopic tremor of a basalt shard settling into its final resting place, or the deep, infrasonic groan of the tectonic walls knitting themselves together. To exist here is to exist in a state of hyper-awareness of the world’s physical sorrow.

Movement for them is an act of geometric negotiation. They do not swim in straight lines. Because the pocket is a non-Euclidean trap where a straight path leads back to the start, the Drift-Looms move in a constant, slow-motion spiraling dance. They undulate their long, ribbon-like bodies, curling around the "broken teeth" of the stone. They are acutely aware of the **geometry breakdown**; when the water folds back on itself, the Loon does not fight it. It allows its body to momentarily exist in two places at once, stretching thin across the dimensional fold until the space snaps back into coherence, taking the creature with it. To watch a Drift-Loom move is to watch a living knot tying and untying itself in slow motion.

Their diet is as abstract as their home. They are omnivores of the micro-dead. They drift through the "wet curtains" of the dense vertical forest, their sticky skin catching the decaying matter that falls from the upper spirals of the kelp-like strands. But they are also drawn to the **young rock**. They possess a beak, hidden within the soft folds of their anterior, made of a cartilage harder than the surrounding stone. With the patience of a glacier, they scrape the algae that grows on the freshest obsidian, grazing on the very infancy of the geology. In doing so, they risk their soft bodies on the razor-sharp terrain, and many older Drift-Looms bear the scars—long, clean slices that heal into white, jagged lines across their translucent forms.

Most striking, however, is their relationship with the **scattered traces** of the lost civilization. The Drift-Looms are obsessed with the perfect, parallel lines etched into the rock faces. To a creature that knows only the curve and the spiral, the straight line is a theological anomaly. When a Drift-Loom encounters a machine scar, it stops. It suspends its drift. It presses its soft, sensory body against the sharp, unnatural groove.

It is believed—by any observer lucky enough to witness this and retain their sanity—that the Drift-Looms are trying to read the history of the place through these scars. They vibrate against the rock, matching the frequency of the tectonic groans, resonating with the ghost of the machine that cut the stone. They linger there for hours, perhaps days, caught in a feedback loop of mourning, communing with a technology that has long since dissolved. In this way, they act not just as grazers, but as curators of the Pocket's memory, maintaining a vigil over the echoes of a blade that once cut through the dark.

In the periphery, the vast, translucent leviathans—the custodians—may drift, ignoring the Loons entirely. But the Drift-Looms are the ones that truly inhabit the space between the sharp edges. They are the soft, pulsing filler of the Grief-Pocket, the living silk that lines the hard, cold stone, eternally circling the center of a world that has no center.
