They are the Black Tide, a heaving, chitin-armored tsunami that climbs the walls without ceasing. To look at them is to look at the dark itself given form; they are shadows carved from obsidian, possessing no pigment that the meager light here can catch. Their carapaces are not smooth like the glassy walls that trap them, but are instead faceted, jagged, and layered—a geological history of their own battles with the stone. They are built in segments, overlapping plates of black iron-biomass that slide over one another with a sound like grinding millstones, sealed tightly against the desiccating air.

There is no distinction between the creature and the machine in this place. They are **The Lithophages**.

They possess no head in the traditional sense, only a forward-facing tapered wedge of armor that protects the sensory cluster and the massive, industrial digging apparatus. Where eyes should be, there are only deep, recessed pits covered by thick, translucent membranes that have long since atrophied from disuse. They do not need to see the rock; they need to be *part* of it. Their faces are dominated by the mandibles—terrible, asymmetrical tools of biological destruction. The upper mandible is a heavy, pick-like scythe, reinforced with carbonized filaments capable of shattering basalt. The lower is a flat, vibrating drum of cartilage and diamond-hard dentition, used to grind the shattered chunks into a fine, ingestible paste.

Their bodies are grotesquely elongated, designed for the vertical life. They have eight primary limbs, though the number varies among the castes. The front four are massive, grasping manipulators—hooks, clamps, and hydraulic pincers that can anchor the creature into a fissure with the force of a hydraulic press. The rear limbs are smaller, hooked spikes used primarily for gripping the scree and pushing against the bodies of their kin. In the center of their underside lies the "mouth-line," a conveyor-belt throat of vibrating cilia that pulls crushed rock inward and excretes the processed sand out the rear, keeping their climbing path clear of debris.

Because the air is a thief of moisture, they are hermetically sealed. They do not breathe through nostrils or mouths, which would vent precious water vapor. Instead, respiration occurs through a complex system of spiracles hidden deep within the joints of their armor. These intakes filter the superheated, dusty air through a labyrinth of mucous membranes and cooled blood vessels, stripping the oxygen while trapping every molecule of humidity. The waste heat from this cooling process is vented through ports in their flanks, adding to the oppressive thermal signature of the cavern. They radiate like living furnaces.

Their society is a singular, writhing organism of logistics. There are the **Breakers**, the largest of the caste, anchored low near the unstable scree. They tear into the fresh, raw veins of the mountain, their labor creating the deafening boom that echoes through the shaft. Above them are the **Carriers**, slightly smaller and more agile. They form the living conveyor belts, passing broken chunks of stone from claw to claw in a rhythmic, upwards-flowing river of black motion. They do not stop; to stop is to be crushed by the tide pressing from behind.

At the highest reaches, near the thermal vents where the infrared glow is strongest, are the **Processors**. These bloated, sedentary versions of the species hang from the ceiling by massive anchors, their bulk swollen with the heavy minerals digested from the rock below. They receive the "feed" from the Carriers and process it through massive, acidic internal chem-stomachs, distilling rare earth metals and complex isotopes necessary for the species' reproduction. They are the chemical factories of the colony, converting the raw stone into the biological slurry that will feed the next generation.

Communication is not vocal, for the roar of the grinding stone drowns out all sound. Instead, they speak through the substrate. Every movement, every scrape of a claw, every strike of a mandible sends a seismic vibration through the rock. They read these tremors through sensitive pads on their feet and the tips of their antennae. The wall is constantly singing with the data of their existence: *danger here*, *rich vein there*, *pass the stone*, *make way*. It is a symphony of percussion, a code tapped out in the language of tectonic shifts.

To see them interact is to watch a clog in a pipe resolve itself. If one falls—a tragedy that happens often as the basalt shears under their weight—it is not mourned. The falling creature creates a vibration of distress, a rapid-fire rattle that instantly alerts those below. They do not catch their fallen kin; they get out of the way. The fallen one impacts the scree and is immediately set upon by the lowest caste, the **Scavengers**, sleek, fast, multi-legged variants whose sole purpose is to recycle the organic matter and the undigested minerals stored within the carapace of the dead. Nothing is wasted. The corpse is stripped, the shell is crushed into sand, and the fluids are drunk. The dead become the mortar for the living.

They are blind, deaf to the air, and voiceless, yet they possess a collective awareness that spans the entire Spine. They are the digestive tract of the mountain, endlessly consuming the world that birthed them, driven by a hunger that can never be sated. In the absolute darkness, under the crushing weight of their own density, they continue their work—the endless, rhythmic grinding of the planet’s bones.
