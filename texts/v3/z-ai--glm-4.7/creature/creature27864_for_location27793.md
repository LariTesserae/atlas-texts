At first, you will think the High Expanse is empty. This is a reasonable assumption, given the crushing silence and the absolute lack of birds, insects, or scuttling things in the shadows. But the "fauna" here is not absent; it has merely merged with the architecture. When a world reaches the "end of life," the distinction between the living and the constructed dissolves. What lives here is the city itself, given a slow, agonizing form.

They are called the **Cairn-Born**.

**The Illusion of Stillness**
To the unobservant eye, a Cairn-Born is simply a ruin—a heap of collapsed masonry, a collection of cyclopean blocks that have tumbled from a tower and settled in a deadlock of grey granite. They sit at the edges of the plazas, motionless lumps in the shape of a seated giant or a hunched, kneeling penitent.

But if you watch them for hours—days, perhaps—you will see the shift. It is imperceptible to the human attention span, a motion so slow it rivals the growth of moss. A block does not fall; it *lowers*. A wall does not crumble; it *leans* with intent.

The Cairn-Born are the only animate entities in this freeze-frame hell, and they move at the speed of geology.

**Anatomy of Stone and Sponge**
A Cairn-Born is a paradox of biological and mineral. It has no skin or skeleton in the traditional sense. Instead, it is a colony organism, a symbiotic fusion of the aggressive plateau vegetation and the primitive masonry of the city.

Imagine a humanoid shape, roughly three times the height of a man, but incredibly broad. The "skeleton" is a core of massive, hand-chiseled granite blocks, the very stones used to build the towers. These blocks are not held together by mortar, but by the sheer crushing weight of the creature's mass and the tensile strength of thousands of thick, ropy vines.

These vines—the "muscles"—are the bruised-purple, electric-green moss and ferns mentioned in the courtyards. They pulse with a hydrostatic pressure, pushing the granite plates apart and pulling them back together in a rhythmic, grinding cycle. Inside the creature, where the stones gap like open ribs, you can see the "heart": a slushy, semi-frozen pool of stagnant water that glows with the pale, bioluminescent bacteria of the plateau. This cold slush pumps through the hollow centers of the vines, hydraulic fluid for a machine of rock.

**The Cairn-Born and the Geometry**
Why do they exist? In a place where the "active dynamic" rearranges the city and physics frays at the edges, the Cairn-Born act as the anchors. They are the stewards of the non-Euclidean geometry.

Because the horizon curves and the rules of distance relax, the city has a tendency to fold in on itself. A tower might suddenly find itself overlapping with a road that was miles away a moment ago. This is where the Cairn-Born go to work.

Using their immense strength—capable of levering multi-ton blocks—they physically shove the reality of the plateau back into place. You might see a Cairn-Born standing at a "vertex," a point where three parallel lines converge. It will brace its wide, pillar-like legs, grip the curvature of the air with its vine-woven hands, and *push*. It is pushing against the distortion, straightening the bent light, forcing the horizon to behave.

When they move, it sounds like the groaning of the earth—the deep, mournful resonation mentioned in the silence. It is the grinding of granite on granite, lubricated by the icy water in their veins.

**The Shadow-Casting**
The most terrifying aspect of the Cairn-Born is their relationship with the "weirdness" of this place. Due to the instability of the shadows here, a Cairn-Born often exists in two states of reality at once.

You will look at a Cairn-Born resting in the sun. Its shadow will lie flat on the ground, detached from its feet, stretching impossibly toward the east. But if you look at the shadow, you will see that it is *doing something different* than the stone body. The shadow might be weeping, or building a tiny wall with its dark hands, or reaching for you.

The shadow is the creature's "intent." While the body moves at a glacial pace, the shadow moves in real-time, emotional and frantic. The Cairn-Born are constantly trapped in a lag between their actions and their desires. They are the physical manifestation of the world’s suspension—building, weeping, or reaching for eons before their stone bodies catch up to their shadows.

**The Cycle of Consumption**
They do not eat food. There are no animals here to hunt, and the plants are their partners. Instead, they eat *entropy*.

When a tower finally succumbs to the damp and collapses, a Cairn-Born will shamble over—it may take a century to cross the plaza—and dismantle the ruin. It cracks the stones open, exposing the fresh, dry granite inside to the freezing air. It absorbs the raw potential energy of the breakage. It then adds these fresh blocks to its own body, replacing a worn, lichen-crusted slab that has eroded into sand.

In this way, the Cairn-Born are immortal, constantly recycling the city through their own forms. They are the decomposers of civilization, turning the "ghost urbanization" into their own flesh, ensuring that nothing here ever truly dies—it just joins the endless, slow-motion construction project.

**The Encounter**
If you were to meet one—and the distorted distance means you might walk for three days only to find it standing right in front of you—you would feel a low-frequency vibration in your teeth. The blood in your ears would ring in harmony with the hydraulic pumping of its vines.

It would not attack. It does not need to. It would simply look at you with a face that is nothing but a flat, smooth slab of weathered rock, perhaps featuring a single, vertical fissure that resembles an eye. It would watch you with the heavy, judgmental patience of a mountain, waiting for you to freeze, to stop moving, to erode, and to eventually become just another block for it to stack.
