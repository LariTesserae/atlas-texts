To look upon the inhabitants of the Hollow is to realize, with a jolt of primal terror, that the "rock-eaters" are merely the grazing cattle of this deep world, and the true masters are those you cannot see at all.

At first, the landscape appears barren of higher intelligence. You see the scuttling lithovores, you see the gliding slate-wings, but you see no rulers. Then, the perspective shifts. A shadow against the distant gloom detaches itself from a basalt pillar. It does not step out; it *unfreezes*.

They are tall, agonizingly thin bipeds, standing upright like the stalagmites themselves. Their physiology is a defiance of soft biology; they are creatures of plate and join. Their skin is not flesh, but a living, porous ceramic—a hardened grey dermis that protects them from the sharp, razor-ground. It is cracked in geometric patterns, lines of wear and age that map their lives like the fissures in the canyon walls. They do not sweat; they vent heat through these cracks, exhaling thin plumes of sulfurous steam that dissipate instantly into the dry air.

Their movement is a study in economy. In a land where every step is a potential laceration, they do not walk so much as they distribute their weight. They possess long, multi-jointed limbs that end not in feet, but in wide, flattened talons capable of splaying over the obsidian shards. When they move, there is no sound of crushing glass, only a low, grinding friction—the sound of two stones rubbing together. They have perfected the art of the imperceptible advance.

Their faces are the most unsettling aspect. There is no soft tissue to speak of. The head is a smooth, tapering helmet of bone, eyeless and noseless. They perceive the world through vibration. Where a human might have ears, they have intricate, fan-like sensory arrays tucked into recesses of their skull, which tremble constantly, reading the infrasonic hum of the quartz trees and the rhythmic clicking of the lithovore herds. They "see" the texture of the air, the density of the rock, and the movement of heat through the stone. To be looked at by one is to feel the vibration of your own bones echoing back at you.

Clothing is nonexistent, replaced by utility. They carry the primitive tools mentioned in the lore of this place, but these artifacts are revered. The hammers of meteorite iron are strapped to their backs with woven sinews taken from the slate-wings, held in place by magnets forged from the iron itself. This metal is alien to the Hollow—it is the only thing here that does not belong to the stone. When a hunter wields such a hammer, it is an event. The iron sings as it swings, a discordant, high-pitched whine that cuts through the low hum of the cave, startling the lithovores into a momentary stillness.

They live in the intervals of action. The inhabitants of the Hollow spend ninety percent of their existence in a state of profound hibernation, standing perfectly motionless in the circles of stacked stone. They fold their limbs against their bodies, locking their joints, and lower their metabolic rate to near zero. In this state, they become indistinguishable from the mineral pinnacles around them. They effectively *become* part of the geology, waiting for eons perhaps, or just for the season when the thermal vents pulse with red rhythm and the lithovores grow fat on sulfur.

They are a people of the absolute present. Because the environment never changes—no rain, no seasons, no day or night—they have no concept of the future or the past. Their history is not written in books, but etched into the walls of their stone shelters. The carvings are simple: jagged lines representing the herds, circles for the vents, and smooth, deep grooves where the meteorite iron has been tested against the earth.

To encounter one is to witness a creature that has survived the crushing pressure of the world by becoming harder than the world around it. They are silent, patient, and utterly unyielding. When they hunt, they do not chase; they wait until the prey walks onto them, their camouflage so perfect that a lithovore might climb onto their back, thinking them a ridge, before the talons snap shut with the final, sharp report of stone breaking stone.

They are the Hollow’s children, born of the heat and the shard, living lives as slow and grinding as the tectonic plates themselves. They are not visitors in the dark; they are the dark, given form and purpose.
