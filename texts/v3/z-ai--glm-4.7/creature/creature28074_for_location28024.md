The roar here is not a sound; it is a frequency, a constant, grinding thrum that vibrates in the marrow of your bones. To stand in the Pocket is to stand inside the belly of a living engine. The life here does not merely inhabit the space; it constitutes the very texture of it.

**The Carpet: The Lithovores**

At the base of it all is the swarm, the writhing mosaic that coats the floor. These are the **Rust-Grinders**, countless millions of silicon-based arthropods roughly the size of a dinner plate or a small tire. They look like biological renditions of industrial refuse: their bodies are flat, low-slung discs of articulated chitin that shimmer with the oily colors of heat-tempered steel. They move on seven pairs of jointed legs, each tip ending in a hardened, diamond-pointed spur that clicks against the basalt.

They have no eyes, for there is nothing to see but the eternal gray. Instead, their heads are dominated by massive, mandibular shears that vibrate at an ultrasonic frequency. They do not chew; they file. They move across the ancient plain in slow, drifting waves, scraping the gray ash and the fossilized ripples for trace ferrous metals. As they feed, they emit a fine, silicate powder—their own waste—which drifts upward to feed the crystal forests.

Their carapaces are not smooth but etched with microscopic grooves, acting as the cooling fins for a body heat that runs dangerously high. When a Grinder dies, usually of sheer mechanical fatigue, it does not rot. It seizes up, locking into a rigid statue. Within minutes, its kin descend, stripping the shell layer by layer, grinding the corpse down into fresh dust. It is a cannibalism of efficiency, a sudden localized frenzy of grinding sounds that rises briefly above the roar before subsiding.

**The Illuminators: The Volt-Ticks**

Clinging to the towering, dead machinery like barnacles on a sunken hull are the **Volt-Ticks**. These are the giants of the ecosystem, bloated, flattened creatures the size of a coffin. They are the living capacitors of this world, their biology evolved to interface with the dormant technology.

Their undersides are a nightmare of suction cups and copper-colored filaments that secrete a conductive gel. They latch onto the exposed terminals of the buried generators or the rusted conduits that snake through the walls, feeding on the ghostly residual electricity that still leaks through the system. As they feed, their abdomens fill with energy, glowing with a sickly, oscillating yellow light—the only suns this world has ever known.

Because they are heavy and slow, they are mobile fortresses. Smaller creatures—parasitic mites that look like tiny microchips—swarm over their shells, cleaning the sensory pits and feeding on the fungal blooms that grow in the dampness of the Tick's conductive gel. The Volt-Ticks are indifferent. They only care about the current. When a conduit finally runs dry, a Tick will detach with a sound like a breaking seal, dropping heavily to the floor to begin a slow, lumbering migration across the ash plain to the next power source, casting long, lonely shadows as it goes.

**The Grazers: The Glass-Grazers**

Among the crystal "thickets"—the jagged forests of precipitating quartz and feldspar—live the **Glass-Grazers**. These are spindly, high-strung creatures that resemble a terrifying cross between a daddy longlegs and a suspension bridge. Their legs are incredibly long, allowing them to stride over the treacherous, sharp mineral growths without touching the ground.

They are delicate things, their bodies almost translucent, revealing internal organs that look like liquid mercury. They feed on the crystals themselves, using mouths that function like acid baths. They inject a solvent derived from their own silicate blood that weakens the crystalline lattice, allowing them to slurp up the resulting mineral slurry.

They are nervous, vibrating constantly. Their exoskeletons are brittle, shattering like porcelain if struck. To defend themselves, they have evolved a "sonic scream"—a burst of high-frequency noise that can crack the shell of a predator or shatter the pursuing Grinders' sensory antennae. When a herd of Grazers moves through a crystal forest, it is a silent, gliding ballet, punctuated only by the occasional *tinkle* of a broken leg or the sharp *hiss* of feeding.

**The Hunters: The Shear-Panthers**

Lurking in the shadows cast by the Volt-Ticks and the ridges of the ancient conveyor belts are the apex predators: the **Shear-Panthers**. They are not swift runners; they are ambush tanks.

Built low to the ground, their bodies are segmented like a tank's treads, a series of heavy, overlapping plates of black iron-chitin. They possess no claws, for claws are useless against the armor of the Rust-Grinders. Instead, their forelimbs are massive biological pincers, hydraulic presses capable of exerting tons of force.

A Shear-Panther waits, motionless, its sensory bristles detecting the vibrations of the passing swarm. When it strikes, it does not bite; it clamps. It seizes a Grinder, crushing its armored flatness until the internal fluids burst, then uses a serrated proboscis to drill into the center and suck out the silicate sludge. The Shear-Panther is an engine of violence in a world of grinding, a sudden explosion of pressure and fluid amidst the endless clicking of the crowd.

**The Scavengers: The Dust-Gills**

Finally, occupying the air itself are the **Dust-Gills**. They are not truly airborne; they are neutrally buoyant, their bodies filled with gas bladders that allow them to drift in the stagnant atmosphere. They resemble manta rays made of parchment and wire. Their "wings" are actually vast, net-like filters that catch the silicate dust kicked up by the millions of legs below.

They drift through the dry fog, their movements slow and hypnotic, filtering the very air for nutrition. They are the cleaners, ensuring the humidity never spikes, that the dust does not choke the Grazers. When they die, they lose their buoyancy and rain down from the ceiling like brittle paper confetti, hitting the floor to be instantly set upon by the Grinders below.

It is a single, massive, writhing entity. The creatures are not individuals; they are components. The Grinders are the teeth, the Volt-Ticks the power source, the Grazers the sensors, and the Shear-Panthers the circuit breakers. They are the biological ghost of a machine that died, possessed by a swarm that refuses to let it end.
