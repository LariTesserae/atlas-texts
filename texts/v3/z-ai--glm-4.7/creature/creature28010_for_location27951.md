To exist in the High Bloat is to accept that you are not a protagonist, but a parasite clinging to the hide of a sleeping god. The life here does not merely inhabit the space; it is molded by the crushing verticality, the sonic violence, and the humid, suffocating breath of the Pillars.

### The Arboreal Scavengers: The Knot-Tyers

The humans here, the Arboreal Scavengers, are creatures of the vertical plane. They have evolved—or perhaps adapted, within the span of a few desperate generations—to ignore the vertigo that would freeze a lowlander’s blood. They are small, wiry things, with skin that has the texture and sheen of cured olive oil, toughened by the constant friction of bark and vine.

**Physiology and Senses**
Living in a deafening environment has reshaped them. Their ears are small and recessed, protected by heavy cartilaginous flaps that they can close voluntarily, but their skulls are dense. They "hear" as much through the bones of their jaws and feet as they do their ears, feeling the approach of the massive Drone-Hoppers or the structural groans of a Pillar before the sound ever hits them. Their eyes are large and dark, adapted to the dim, green-gold light that filters through the spore-clouds, possessing a nictitating membrane that is constantly sweeping away the oily moisture of the Heavy Dews.

Because the heat is a physical weight, they dress sparsely. They wear loincloths and harnesses made from the treated hide of smaller fauna, but their primary "clothing" is the layer of grease and clay they rub into their skin to seal in moisture against the desiccating winds that sometimes howl through the gaps.

**The Culture of the Knot**
Their society is defined by the "Knot." Just as the vines they use to bind their shelters are macro-molecular puzzles, their social structure is a tangle of obligation and dependency. They do not live in families, but in "Lines"—vertical collectives responsible for a specific section of a Dead Shelf. A Scavenger’s status is determined by the complexity of the knots they can tie.

A shelter that holds during a Heavy Dew—a torrent of liquid air—is a masterpiece of engineering. These structures are not rigid; they are woven, hammock-like pods that hang suspended beneath the calcified shelves. When the Dew comes, the fibers swell, sealing the weave tight against the deluge. To sleep is to be suspended in a dark, swinging sac, listening to the roar of the water turning to liquid outside.

**The Rite of the Glide**
The most defining aspect of their lives is travel. To move from Pillar to Pillar is to leap across a void that could swallow a city. They pilot the Gourd-Gliders—massous, hollowed-out seeds reinforced with internal struts of bone. The glide is not a gentle soar; it is a terrified plummet followed by a desperate catch of an updraft. A Scavenger learns to read the heat currents like a map. They fear the ground more than death, for the ground is where the gravity of the planet becomes absolute.

### The Sky-Traffic: The Denizens of the Roar

The air between the Pillars is not empty; it is a solid column of life. The Scavengers share this highway with beasts that treat the sky as an ocean.

**The Echo-Bats**
The true lords of the air are the Echo-Bats. With twenty-foot wingspans, they are blurs of translucent skin that catch the green-gold light and glow like ghosts. They do not flap constantly; they ride the thermal currents with an eerie, silent grace, broken only by their piercing shrieks.

To the Scavengers, the Echo-Bats are living weather systems. When a cloud of them passes, the sun is blotted out, and the temperature drops instantly. The Scavengers have learned to mimic the high-frequency clicks of the bats using bone whittled from the wind-bleached ribs of dead Hoppers, creating crude sonar jamming devices to keep the bats confused and prevent them from snatching a glider out of the sky.

**The Drone-Hoppers**
If the bats are the silence, the Drone-Hoppers are the thunder. These are insectoid titans, car-sized masses of chitin and muscle. They travel in migratory waves, bouncing from the massive flat platforms of one Pillar to another. The sound of their thousands of legs scraping against the stone bark is a grinding roar that vibrates in the chest.

They are seemingly mindless, driven only by the urge to consume the algae mats that grow on the Pillar surfaces. A Scavenger caught in the path of a Hopper wave is usually doomed—not because the Hoppers eat them, but because they are simply crushed by the sheer mass of the migration. Scavengers will often "Hopper-surf," lashing their gliders to the back of a resting beast to hitch a ride across a gap too wide to glide, risking death for a shortcut.

### The Ground-Dwellers: The Eaters of the Fallen

Beneath the Dead Shelf, miles down in the dim, root-choked gloom where the petrified ancestors form the floor, live the things that keep the Scavengers in the trees.

**The Root-Weavers**
These are pale, eyeless crustaceans that have adapted to walk on the petrified root lattice. They are the size of buses, possessing multiple pairs of legs that can splay out to distribute their massive weight over the fragile, hollow ground. They are the scavengers of the abyss, waiting for the Heavy Dew spill-off to wash gliders, nests, or unwary climbers down to the floor.

They move with agonizing slowness, but they never stop. Their shells are encrusted with the very moss of the Pillars, making them walking stones. To a Scavenger, they represent the ultimate horror: not the quick death of a predator, but the slow, grinding digestion of the deep.

### The Pillars: The Living Mountains

Finally, there are the Pillars themselves. They are not merely scenery; they are organisms, and they possess an immune system.

**The Swarm-Cleaners**
The "strangeness" of the High Bloat is most visible in the way the Pillars defend themselves. When the white fungal mold begins to bloom—a sickness that turns the mighty bark into soft mush—the Pillar seems to summon its defenders.

The Swarm-Cleaners are small, beetle-like creatures that usually live in the deep crevices of the bark canyons. But when the chemical signal of the mold is released, they erupt in a living river. They possess acidic bites that cauterize the infection. They swarm over the infected area like a living blanket, devouring the rot and the healthy tissue alike, leaving a scar of black, hardened bark that heals over the course of decades.

The Scavengers revere this swarm. They carve small totems from the blackened "Scar-Bark," believing it holds the power of the Pillar’s life force. To wear a necklace of Scar-Bark is to claim protection from the sickness of the world.

**The Leaf-Lakes**
High above, suspended in the vast bowl-leaves, are the artificial lakes. These are not stagnant pools; they are teeming with microscopic life and "Gill-Moths"—aquatic creatures that breathe air but live in the water. When the leaf eventually gives way under the weight of the lake, the resulting waterfall is not just water; it is a cascading avalanche of life, washing Moths, larvae, and tons of biomass down to the forest floor. It is a rain of meat, and the Root-Weavers gather from miles around to feast, clicking their mandibles in a chorus that echoes up the mile-high trunks to the tiny, shivering Scavengers in their woven hammocks.

In the High Bloat, everything eats, and everything is eaten. The noise is the sound of the transaction, endless and loud.
