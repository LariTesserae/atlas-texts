In the crushing whiteness of the High Scarp, where the air is too thin to carry scent but thick enough to drown in, biology has not just died—it has been transposed. Life here does not breathe; it chimes. It does not feed on sunlight; it feeds on tension. The inhabitants of this vertical labyrinth are things of stone, sound, and fleeting stability.

**The Shard-Mimics**

The smallest and most numerous denizens are the Shard-Mimics. At a glance, they are indistinguishable from the crystalline growths that adorn the rock faces—delicate, branching trees of quartz and feldspar. They stand perfectly still, rooted in hairline fractures in the basalt, their faceted limbs refracting the blinding sky into internal rainbows.

But they are not merely mineral. They are a form of siliceous life that moves only when the wind’s harmonic thrumming drops to a specific frequency, a lull in the roar that acts like a trigger. When the air falls silent, the Mimics detach. They scuttle with a frantic, jerky motion, their limbs making a dry, clicking sound like scissors closing. They are opportunistic feeders on the "instability" of the region; they creep toward spots where the reality has worn thin, where the water flows upward or the depth perception falters, and they gorge on the erratic energy leaking from the fissures in the world.

If a traveler were to step too close to a cluster of these crystal-trees during the lull, the sound would be unmistakable: the synchronized *crunch* of a thousand hollow glass legs snapping into motion. They are fragile—a falling pebble can shatter a Mimic into dust—but they are numerous. They leave behind trails of fine, sparkling powder that glistens on the wet basalt before the rising water washes it away.

**The Masons**

The true architects of the High Scarp, however, are the entities responsible for the Works. These are the Masons, massive, hulking figures that appear to be constructed from the very environment they inhabit. They look like crude, rough-hewn cairns of stone and petrified timber, given a terrible, slow-waking life.

A Mason stands taller than a man, wide and blocky, with no distinct head, only a torso of stacked boulders held together by a mysterious, grinding cohesion. They do not have faces, but deep, vertical fissures in their chest armor that glow with a dull, heatless orange light—the same color as the sky before it turned white.

They are the source of the "primitive intervention." The Masons are in a state of perpetual, obsessive labor. They spend their shuffling existence hauling massive iron wedges and stone blocks, reinforcing the cracks in the earth that seem to threaten the structural integrity of the plateau. They do not build shelters; they build buttresses. They do not dig for water; they build levees to direct the weeping water away from the load-bearing pillars.

The Masons are seemingly immortal, or at least timeless. They wear down like the mountains themselves, their outer layers smoothing into roundness over eons, only to shed the sloughed-off rock and reveal fresh, jagged surfaces beneath. They communicate not by sound, but by vibration. When two Masons meet, they press their stone torsos together, and the low-frequency thrum of the earth vibrates through them, a conversation of tectonic stresses and fault lines. To stand near them is to feel a buzzing in the soles of your feet, a sensation of being inside a ringing bell.

**The Refracted**

The most terrifying inhabitants, however, are those that have fully embraced the "glitches" of the High Scarp. They are the Refracted, entities that exist only partially in the solid world. They are rarely seen, as they dwell in the high verticality where the visual laws break down, but their presence is felt in the sudden distortions of perspective.

The Refracted appear as flickering, monochromatic shadows—humanoid silhouettes composed of the blinding white light of the sky. They possess no fixed form. One moment, a spire fifty yards away might suddenly resolve into a Refracted standing inches from your face; the next, it is a spire again. They seem to surf the waves of uncertainty that plague the plateau.

They are predators of a sort, though they do not hunt for flesh. They hunt for *tethers*. They are drawn to those who are losing their grip on the "real." If a climber’s mind begins to wander, if they stare too long into the still pools that reflect the future, the Refracted will sense the weakening of their anchor to reality. They will approach, vibrating with the speed of a hummingbird’s wing, and "prune" the timeline.

Those who have seen them and survived report that the Refracted do not have eyes, but smooth, featureless planes of bone-white light. They reach out with limbs that stretch and compress like accordrons, touching a person’s chest to feel the heartbeat of the present moment. To be touched by a Refracted is to feel the sensation of being untethered, of floating upward into the white noise forever. The Masons seem to hate them, occasionally hurling massive boulders at empty patches of air, attempting to crush these flickering ghosts before they can dismantle the stonework that holds the world together.

**The Symphony of Survival**

In the High Scarp, survival is a matter of synchronization. The Shard-Mimics wait for the silence. The Masons work in the thrumming. The Refracted hunt in the visual static.

And beneath it all, deep in the dripping, cold crevices of the basalt, there is something else—a collective consciousness of the water itself. The ultra-cold, still water that fills the depressions is not merely a liquid; it is a neural medium. It remembers the shape of the mountains before they fell. It holds the memories of the builders who never existed. If one were to break the "rules" and drink the still water, one would not see the future; one would *hear* the voices of the stone, whispering that the mountains are not falling apart, but simply falling *asleep*. The inhabitants of the High Scarp are merely the custodians of this long, mineral slumber, fighting a losing war against the wakefulness of the white sky.
