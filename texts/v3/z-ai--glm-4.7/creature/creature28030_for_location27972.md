Life here does not scramble or scuttle; it **calculates**. Given the immense, crushing weight of the light and the strangeness of the physics, the inhabitants of this tableland are less biological organisms and more like persistent algorithms given flesh. They are creatures designed to exist in the gap between what is seen and what is rendered.

### The Drifters: The Living Lenses
The most visible inhabitants are the Drifters, those vast, silent manta-rays of the sky. To call them birds is a misnomer; they are atmospheric filters. Their bodies, twenty feet across, are composed of a gelatinous substance as clear as water, refracting the high-altitude brightness into internal rainbows.

Inside their translucent forms, the organs are not distinct lumps of meat but floating, branching geometries of soft purple light—the same bioluminescence found in the roots of the glass-grass. They do not flap; they possess no muscles for propulsion. Instead, they possess a complex internal network of ferromagnetic fluids that align perfectly with the invisible magnetic currents coursing through the plateau. They swim through the air by polarizing their bodies against the planet’s magnetic field, riding the lines of force like invisible rails.

Because the silence here is absolute (0.4), the Drifters have evolved no vocal cords, no ears, and no method of making noise. Even their internal circulatory pulses are dampened by a spongy, acoustic-absorbing tissue that lines their insides. They communicate by adjusting the opacity of their skin. A Drifter signaling distress flashes a sudden, matte grey; a mating display involves rapid, oscillating pulses of translucence that ripple across the wingspan like a shudder. When they dive to feed on the glass-grass, they do not tear at the vegetation. They open their ventral slits and simply *inhale* the light-data, their internal glow brightening as they process the raw photons.

### The Lithic-Mimics: The Sleeping Stone
With a fauna density of 2.3, life is actually abundant, but you must step on it to see it. The ground is not bare; it is occupied by the Lithic-Mimics. These are low-slung, tortoise-like entities that have evolved to perfectly match the texture, temperature, and hardness of the bedrock.

At rest, a Mimic is indistinguishable from a boulder. Its shell is a grey, weathered diamond-hardness, soft as chalk only where the sun has not yet bleached it. They possess no legs, but rather a muscular, singular "foot" that ripples like a skirt, allowing them to slide across the stone with a frictionless silence. Because the air absorbs sound, they do not click or scratch; they glide.

The Mimics are the custodians of the "glitch." They feed on the computational errors of the world. When the Monoliths shudder and a patch of reality duplicates itself, the Mimics slide toward the disturbance, opening unseen mouths beneath their shells to consume the residual potential energy of the rendered landscape. They are the immune system of the simulation, cleaning up the fragmented code left behind by the reality anchors. Their eyes are not visual organs, but sensory pits that detect the *taste* of inconsistency in the air.

### The Capillary-Fliers
Within the herds of Drifters, there exists a symbiotic, parasite-like fauna known as Capillary-Fliers. These are tiny, fragile creatures, no larger than a human hand, resembling dandelion seeds made of blown glass. They possess no mouths, no stomachs—only a nervous system and a skin of silica.

They cling to the undersides of the Drifters, hooking into the larger creature’s magnetic field. In return for the lift, the Capillary-Fliers serve as a distributed sensory array. They can detect the minute shifts in humidity—the "ghost water"—long before the fog banks boil up from the ground. When they sense the moisture rising, they vibrate, sending a silent, tactile shudder through the Drifter they cling to, signaling the host to descend toward the condensation. It is a relationship of pure resonance, a biological network sharing data without a single word spoken.

### The Monolith-Gardners
The strangest life, and perhaps the true masters of this place, are the entities found only at the base of the Black Monoliths. They are rarely seen, existing only in the split-second when the reality glitch occurs and the rendering of the world stutters.

They appear as tall, humanoid figures composed entirely of shadow—two-dimensional voids given three-dimensional presence. They have no features, only a silhouette that absorbs all light. They do not "live" in the traditional sense; they *maintain*.

When the upward rain falls, pulled back into the sky by the magnetic needles, the Shadow-Gardners step out of the glitch. They walk through the air, standing on the falling droplets of water as if they were solid stairs. With long, needle-like fingers, they prune the glass-grass, trimming the filaments to ensure the refractive index remains perfect. They do not eat; they edit. They are the agents of the equilibrium, ensuring the erosion stays on schedule and the light remains at a pressure of 2.7. They are terrifyingly beautiful, moving with the fluid grace of a glitch in a video game, snapping back into non-existence the moment the Monolith’s shimmer fades.

### The Silence as a Entity
Finally, there is the silence itself. With a sound rating of 0.4, the quiet is not merely an absence; it is a presence. It is a heavy, oppressive blanket that seems to watch you. The life here has adapted to be seen by the silence. The creatures move in ways that please the acoustic dampeners—slow, fluid, rolling motions that generate no friction.

To live here is to be fundamentally lonely. Even the Drifters, flying in herds of hundreds, are isolated in their own gelatinous shells, sensing each other only through light and magnetism. They are swimming in a bright, thick soup of isolation, held together by the black needles that stitch the sky to the ground, living in a world that is constantly reminding them that they are just patterns of light on a stone that is trying to forget them.
