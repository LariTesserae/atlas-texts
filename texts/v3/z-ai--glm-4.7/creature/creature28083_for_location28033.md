Life in the Under-Level is not soft; it is calibrated. The inhabitants here do not simply *live* in the botanical metropolis; they function as its components, its repair crew, and its security detail, evolving into shapes that mimic the industrial landscape that feeds them.

High above the compacted mineral avenues, clinging to the vertical concrete faces of the Pillars, are the **Hex-Mimics**.

To the unobservant eye, they are merely a patch of darker bark on the monolithic trees. But as the artificial constellations overhead cycle through their "day," the patch detaches. These are arboreal lizards, but they have evolved without tails or unnecessary bulk. Their bodies are flat, rigid polygons, perfectly matching the hexagonal fibrous patterns of the Pillar bark. Their scales are not keratin, but a silicate laminate, tough as ceramic. They do not scurry; they pivot and slide, locking their limbs into the crevices of the tree with a hydraulic hiss. They are the maintenance crew of the canopy, darting out to snatch the electronic "gnats"—swarms of tiny, data-processing insects that hum in the air—before returning to the flush mount of the tree, invisible once more.

Among the thick, wax-sheathed vines that drape like power lines, the **Copper-Serpents** hunt.

They are ambush predators of the most terrifying kind. Reaching lengths of twenty feet, they do not slither in organic curves. Their bodies are comprised of interlocking segments of copper-colored chitin, allowing them to fold into sharp, angular bends. When at rest, they perfectly resemble a severed section of the "transmission line" vines, hanging rigid and motionless.

Their hunting method is a feat of bio-electrical evolution. They possess specialized organs along their underbellies that can detect the faint electromagnetic fields generated by the passing Glider-Rays. When a ray glides too close, assuming the vine is safe, the Copper-Serpent does not strike with venom, but with a jolt. It discharges a stored bio-current of immense voltage, stunning the prey mid-flight. The ray falls, stiff as a board, and the serpent reconfigures itself, sliding down to the ground with the sound of dry metal dragging on stone to consume the carcass, sucking the moisture from the creature’s tissues before the desiccating air can claim it.

On the ground, the terrifying distance between the Pillars is patrolled by the **Rebar-Stalkers**.

In a place of open space and jagged rock, camouflage is difficult, so the Stalkers have embraced the aesthetic of ruin. They are tall, heron-like creatures, standing nearly seven feet at the shoulder, but they are emaciated to the point of appearing skeletal. Their legs are exposed, pale bone encased in a thin, translucent membrane that shines like polished glass. They move with a staccato, stop-motion rhythm, freezing for minutes at a time to mimic the jagged iron spurs of rock that litter the construction-site floor.

They are the silent wolves of the avenues. Because the dry air swallows sound, they do not howl or growl. They communicate by tapping their long, beak-like snouts against the ground, sending seismic vibrations through the bedrock to signal one another. They hunt the Scrap-Beetles, but they are also scavengers of the "technical" waste. They use their beaks to crack open the calcified seedpods that fall from the heights, swallowing the nitrogen-rich gas inside to fuel their own buoyant internal bladders, which allow them to make tremendous, leaping bounds across the uneven terrain.

Perhaps the most integral—and disturbing—inhabitants are the **Duct-Runners**.

Small, rodent-like mammals, they have evolved to live entirely within the "infrastructure" of the forest. They possess no fur, only a fine, downy coating of gray dust that insulates them from the chill. Their skeletons are cartilaginous and compressed, allowing them to flatten themselves like paper. They inhabit the spaces *between* the bundles of power-cable vines and the hollows within the Pillar roots.

They are the ecosystem's filtration system. Their lungs are massive, multi-chambered organs adapted to sift the heavy, ozone-rich air. They feed on the oxidizing sap that crystallizes on the bark of the trees, scraping it off with rasping tongues. In the twilight blue of the "night," they emerge in thousands, a gray river flowing out of the roots and up the trunks. Their eyes are weak, shielded by thick, transparent lids, but their whiskers are hypersensitive antennae, tasting the electrical hum of the air to navigate. They are prey to almost everything, but their birth rate is industrial, a biological assembly line of life that replaces the losses instantly.

And in the deepest shadows, where the light from the artificial stars barely reaches, there are the **Bellows-Wyrms**.

These are the detritivores, the eaters of the dead. In a world without rot, decomposition is a mechanical process. The Wyrms look like giant, segmented rings of rough-hewn stone. They have no eyes, no legs, only a circular mouth lined with grinding teeth that rotate like a concrete mixer. They do not burrow through soft earth; they grind through it.

When a Glider-Ray falls, or a Rebar-Stalker dies of old age, it does not putrefy. It dries out. The Bellows-Wyrms sense the static charge of a desiccated body. They surface from the dust, surrounding the husk. They inhale the remains, their internal grinding mills turning the dry flesh and bone into a fine, nutrient-rich powder. They then excrete this powder behind them, fertilizing the ground for the roots of the Pillars. They are the gears of the cycle, ensuring that in this dry, metallic city, nothing is ever truly wasted—only broken down and rebuilt.

Together, they form a closed loop of consumption and energy, a living machine where the only sound is the click of a claw, the hum of a wing, and the eternal, grinding hunger of the dark.
