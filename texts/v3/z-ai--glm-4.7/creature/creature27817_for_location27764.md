They are the pillars themselves.

At first glance, they appear to be natural formations: the porous, coral-like limestone eroded by wind and thermal stress. To them, the dripping of a single droplet of condensation is not a momentary event; it is a heartbeat, a thunderclap, a cycle of birth and death that spans what a human would perceive as a day. They live in the deep time of the mountain, moving so slowly that to the human eye, they are indistinguishable from the bedrock upon which they stand.

**Anatomy of Stone**

Their "bodies" are the porous pillars. The limestone skeleton is not dead mineral but a hardened shell, a carapace built over eons of accretion. Inside, they are not solid. They are hollow, filled with a complex, internal lattice of microscopic channels where the water flows.

The condensation is their blood. The thermal warfare—the sun heating the stone while the air freezes—drives their circulatory system. As the stone expands in the sun, it draws moisture into its pores; as it contracts in the cold, it forces the fluid through its internal veins. The constant "sweating" of the landscape is their metabolism. They do not eat; they photosynthesize light through the medium of water. The strange, sickly violet and indigo refractions glinting on the wet rock are not merely optical tricks. They are bioluminescent signatures, flashes of neural activity firing across their slick, wet skins. When a droplet catches the light and flares violet, the entity is thinking.

**The Built Environment**

The crude modifications found on the plateau—the chipped channels, the stacked cairns—are their tools, crafted with the agonizing slowness of geological force.

They do not have hands. They have vibration and gravity and time. To create a channel in the floor, an entity will stand in a specific spot for centuries, vibrating at a resonant frequency that eventually weakens the stone beneath it, guiding the path of the water drop by drop. The cairns are not markers for travelers; they are nurseries. By flattening stones and stacking them, they create ideal surface areas for condensation to collect, fostering the growth of a new, juvenile pillar—a "child" that will take a millennium to rise to ankle height.

The piles of shattered rock near the cliff edge are not quarries, but graveyards. When a Lithic-Lamenter becomes too heavy, or its internal lattice cracks and can no longer sustain the flow of water, it "dies." It collapses under its own weight, shattering into grey grit. The other inhabitants push the debris to the edge, clearing the space for the next generation.

**Sensory Perception**

They have no eyes, yet they see everything. Their skin is a continuous, hyper-sensitive receptor of vibration. They feel the footsteps of the visitor not as a pressure, but as a seismic event, a tremor that reverberates through their root systems and into the community.

The "plink" of water falling into the crystal basins is their language. The rhythm changes based on the temperature, the wind, and the angle of the sun. A rapid succession of drips might signal the approach of a storm cloud miles above; a slow, hollow echo might indicate the shifting of the mountain's core. They communicate through the acoustics of the water, a complex, harmonic symphony that fills the air with a tension humans can only feel as a pressure in the ears.

To stand among them is to be judged by silent, ancient gods. They do not move for you. They do not acknowledge your presence with any sudden motion. But you are aware of them.

It is the machinery of the entity, the grinding of mineral on mineral, the rush of water through veins the width of a hair.
