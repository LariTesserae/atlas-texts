Here, in the lung of the world, life does not scurry; it flows. To survive in the Built World is to accept that you are a tenant in a house that is eating itself, and you must be quicker than the digestion.

The primary architects of survival here are the **Weavers**, the primitive people mentioned only in passing in the geological record. They are not merely "tribespeople" in the anthropological sense; they are anatomically specialized for the vertical void. They are slight, possessing a bone density that would alarm a physician from another world—hollow, reinforced with struts of cartilage like the wings of a bird. Their skin is the color of the bruised twilight—a mottled camouflage of deep violet and pale grey, absorbing what little spectral light filters down to photosynthesize just enough energy to supplement their meager hunting.

They do not speak. To open the mouth and shout is to waste moisture and invite the dampening acoustics of the pillars to swallow your warning. Instead, the Weavers communicate through **tactile stridulation**. Their wrists and ankles are lined with ridges of hard, chitinous keratin. By rubbing their limbs together against the fibrous bark of the walls, they produce complex, rhythmic vibrations that travel through the root-mesh for miles. A conversation between two hunting parties is a silent thrumming felt in the soles of the feet, a ghostly Morse code rippling through the architecture.

Their technology is an extension of the flora. Their needles are the thorns of the giant canopy, sharpened to a molecular edge. Their thread is a viscous, sticky sap harvested from the Weeping Rocks, spun into a tough, biological filament. They do not build shelters *on* the ground; they weave hammocks and suspended pods into the hexagonal grids of the root-plains, hanging precariously over the miles-deep drop, safe from anything that cannot climb the sheer, slick verticality of the pillars.

They hunt the **Glass-Wings**. These are not birds in the earthly sense, but something closer to flying manta rays. Their bodies are flat, aerodynamic discs of translucent muscle, barely visible against the grey haze. They possess no legs, only two immense, sweeping wings composed of a clear, membrane-like tissue that ripples with bioluminescence during their mating dives. The Glass-Wings are silent predators of the air currents, snaring drifting spores and aerial insects with nets of barbed filament trailed from their tails. To hunt them, the Weavers must be patient, waiting in the hollows for a shadow to pass, then casting nets of sticky sap to entangle the fragile wings. If a Glass-Wing is struck, it does not scream; it shatters, falling into the abyss as a rain of glittering shards.

Beneath the Weavers, in the damp shadow of the overhangs, live the **Gel-Sloths**. These are the primary decomposers, the necessary agents of the world’s ouroboros. They resemble ambulatory piles of wet fungus, a conglomerate of moss and sluggish muscle that slides over the lignin archways. They are massive, the size of elephants, but utterly shapeless, draping over the geometric ridges like wet dough. They feed on the hardest, oldest wood, secreting a potent enzyme that softens the iron-hard bark into pulp. The Weavers do not hunt them; they farm them. They follow the Gel-Sloths, harvesting the rich, black humus left in their wake—the only true "soil" in the Built World—which they use to cultivate medicinal fungi in their suspended pods.

Deeper still, in the cavernous hollows between the pillars where the physics of gravity begin to bend near the amber sap-rivers, dwell the **Echo-Spiders**. These creatures do not spin webs; they weave nets of sound. They are blind, possessing sensory organs that detect the faintest vibration in the heavy air. They anchor themselves to the ceiling of the void, miles above the floor, and extrude long, thin filaments of vibrating silk. When a creature flies through these filaments, the vibration travels up the line, alerting the spider. The Echo-Spider then reels in its prey, using the distortion of gravity to its advantage, pulling its victim into a crushing embrace.

But the true masters of this space are the **Drift-Keeps**. These are not a single species, but a colony symbiosis—a floating ecosystem. resembling a massive, tangled ball of driftwood and hanging vines, fifty feet in diameter. Inside the tangled mass live communities of insects, small rodents, and mosses, all dependent on one another. The Drift-Keep is propelled by hundreds of fan-like leaves that catch the updrafts from the Weeping Rocks. They drift through the empty cathedrals like silent, organic balloons, grazing on the moss that grows on the pillars. They are the wandering islands of the Veldt, moving with such slowness that to the human eye they appear stationary, only revealing their motion over the course of days.

And then there is the **Silence**.

The inhabitants believe the Silence is a living thing. It is the predator that occupies the empty space. It is the reason they do not speak. When a Weaver falls, or when a Gel-Sloth loses its grip and slides into the abyss, it is not the impact that kills them. Long before they hit the bottom, miles down in the dark, they are caught by the Silence. The Weavers say that if you listen too closely to the hum of the pillars, the Silence will hear you. It is a presence that fills the vacuum, a heavy, suffocating psychological weight that keeps the tribes huddled together, pressing their bodies against the warm, pulsing bark, reassuring themselves that they are still part of the living body.

In this place, even the bacteria are architects. **Calcifying Algae** crust over the higher ridges, turning soft wood into stone-hard armor, creating new landforms over centuries. The people mine this algae, grinding it down into a paste that hardens into a kind of ceramics for their tools.

Everything here is defined by its relationship to the structure. The **Geometer-Moths**, with their fractal wing patterns, navigate the non-Euclidean curves of the roots effortlessly, their wings folding into dimensions that seem to shift as they fly. The **Sap-Drunks**, small, ferret-like creatures, spend their lives lapping at the seepages of the amber rivers, their bodies becoming slow and crystallized over time, eventually turning into living statues of amber that dot the riverbanks like warning markers.

Life in the High Veldt is a delicate, hanging thread. It is an existence of clinging to the flank of a god, nursing at its sweat, and praying that it does not shift in its sleep. The people here do not dream of conquest or exploration. They dream of stillness, of being perfectly synchronized with the slow, tectonic breathing of the green, endless dark.
