To touch the wall here is to disturb a universe. The massive, megalithic stones that form the shaft of the High Elevation are not visible as rock; they have been subsumed by a living veneer so thick that the architecture beneath has become irrelevant. The "Built" environment is entirely lost beneath a writhing, multi-layered carapace of organisms that cling with the desperation of things that know nothing else.

The primary inhabitants are the **Clingers**. They are crustaceans, but evolution has stripped them of the need for distinct heads, tails, or legs. They exist as flattened, irregular discs of translucent chitin, ranging in size from a dinner plate to a small raft. Imagine a crab that has been run over by a glacier, spread out thin, and fused to the stone. Their shells are the color of bruised slate—blacks, grays, and deep purples—completely blind, lacking even vestigial eyes. They possess only two singular capabilities: the ability to grip the smoothed stone with an unbreakable suction, and the ability to extend a feathery, fractal array of filtering tendrils into the water. These tendrils wave in the imperceptible currents with the slowness of trees swaying in a breeze, sifting the heavy, warm water for organic particulates. They stack upon one another, newer generations anchoring themselves to the shells of the dead, creating a stratified city of bone and slime that inches outward over centuries.

Between the Clingers dwell the **Glass-Slugs**. These are gelatinous, cnidarian-based grazers, heavy and amoeboid, lacking the fragile elegance of terrestrial slugs. They are the size of seals, composed of a dense, muscular fluid enclosed in a membrane as tough as cured leather. They glide over the carpet of crustaceans with the frictionless indifference of a sled on snow. They do not hunt; they graze. Their underside is a grinding pad of microscopic, silica teeth that scrapes the bacterial slime and algae-like film that grows on the shells of the Clingers. Their movement is agonizingly slow—a journey of a few feet may take a day. As they move, they leave behind a trail of pristine, clean grey shell, a temporary scar on the living wall that is quickly overgrown by the next generation. They possess no discernible sensory organs, merely a network of light-sensitive nerves that register only the profound absence of light.

Drifting through the vertical shaft are the **Living Stones**. These are the heavy, armored fish that patrol the water column, though "patrol" implies a purpose they lack. They are essentially living geodes—spheres of dense, mineralized plating that protects a sluggish, torpedo-shaped core of muscle and fat. They possess no swim bladders; they maintain depth through sheer density and a slight negative buoyancy that keeps them perpetually sinking, using a single, rigid fin along their spine to push them upward when they hit the floor. Their mouths are not beaks or jaws, but cavernous, circular vents that hinge open slowly, revealing an interior lined with sticky, bristle-like filaments. They drift, mouth agape, inhaling the "marine snow" that falls from the obscurity above. They do not react to each other. If two Living Stones collide, they bounce off one another with a dull, rock-on-rock thud that vibrates through the water, then slowly drift apart, neither acknowledging the other as living entity.

Dominating the spaces where the megalithic blocks have settled—creating crevasses, arches, and hollows—are the **Ghost-Anemones**. They are not the delicate, flower-like creatures of shallow reefs. They are giants, columnar masses of pale, calcified flesh that rise from the floor like pillars of a ruined temple. They can stand as tall as a building, their "trunks" thick and ridged, anchoring them in the cracks of the pavement. At their summits, they bloom into intricate, tangled webs of stinging tentacles that are retracted most of the time, looking like withered branches. When the water brings a sufficient density of nutrients, they unfurl in a sudden, violent explosion of motion—rare speed in this slow world—capturing whatever drifts near, then contracting back into a dormant column for days or weeks. They are the apex of this passive hierarchy, the stationary giants that feed on the living driftwood that fails to navigate the ruins.

Finally, there is the **Carpet**. Beneath the visible creatures, coating every surface, is a microscopic foaming layer of bacterial mats and fungal growths. It is the engine of the ecosystem, consuming the minute leaching of minerals from the "Built" stones and the waste of the larger fauna. It glows with no light, but it possesses a texture like wet velvet. It is the base of the food web, the sludge from which all other life derives its energy.

And beneath it all, in the absolute silence, is the feeling of the **Breath**. The water is warm, and the density of life is so high that the entire shaft seems to pulsate. The combined filter-feeding of billions of Clingers, the inhalation of the Living Stones, and the expansion of the Ghost-Anemones create a tidal rhythm in the water. It is not a current, but a rhythmic surge, a slow expansion and contraction of the medium itself, as if the submerged cathedral is actually a lung, breathing in the dark, waiting for a sunrise that will never come.
