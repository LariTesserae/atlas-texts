The air is so thick with life here that you do not breathe so much as inhale a soup of living particles. The first things you notice are the ones that cannot be seen, only felt—a vibrating, electric fence of biomass. The "static hiss" you hear is the collective friction of a trillion wings. These are the *Mist-Drifters*, small, gauzy insects that do not so much fly as vibrate through the humidity. They are the plankton of this air, feeding on the mineral-rich steam that vents from the earth’s wound, and in turn, they form the bottomless food source for everything else. They land on your skin in cooling, tickling waves, their legs tasting the salt on your sweat before they are snatched away by the sudden, snapping jaws of something larger.

 darting through the clouds of Drifters are the *Glass-Wings*. True to the setting's youth, these are not ancient, evolved fliers, but experimental prototypes of nature. They resemble birds, but their feathers are translucent sheets of chitin that refract the bioluminescent glow of the mosses into jagged rainbows. They are silent hunters, moving through the gloom with the terrifying speed of something that knows it is both predator and prey. You see one dive, a streak of indigo light, and emerge with a writhing, many-legged creature in its beak. They navigate the invisible obsidian shards not by sight, but by sensitivity to air pressure; a broken wing here is a death sentence, as the fall onto the razor-sharp scree below means a slow, bleeding end.

Beneath the canopy of glowing red vines, the weirdness of the fauna amplifies. Moving through the neuro-electric blue moss are the *Hex-Scrollers*. These are the mammals with too many limbs mentioned in the whisper of the dark. They are low-slung, badger-shaped creatures, but they possess six legs rather than four. The front two are massive, shovel-like claws adapted for tearing apart the fibrous, suffocating mats of ferns to get at the grubs beneath; the rear four are splayed and wide, acting like snowshoes to distribute their weight over the unstable, shifting scree. Their fur is slick and oily, repelling the constant dampness, and they move with a fluid, liquid grace that belies their clumsy appearance. They are nervous, vibrating bundles of muscle, constantly pausing to triangulate the sounds of the gliding raptors above. When they run, they do not gallop; they ripple, a wave of motion passing from their shoulders to their hips, allowing them to flow over the sharp rocks without ever settling long enough to be cut.

And then there is the source of the guttural boom. The *Basin-Walkers*. They are massive, hulking herbivores, the elephants of this shattered world, but built for verticality. They cling to the steep, unstable slopes with hooked talons on their feet that can puncture solid rock. Their skin is armored with plates of keratin thick enough to turn the sharpest obsidian shard, saving them from the landslides that frequently tumble down the crater walls. They are blind—the darkness is too absolute for eyes to be of use—but their faces are dominated by massive, sensitive sonic receptors that look like blooming flowers made of flesh. They "see" through the roar of the waterfall and the scream of the insects, creating a mental map of the terrain through sound. When they bellow, it is a low-frequency blast that clears the swarm of insects from their path, a weapon of noise that shakes the very fillings in your teeth.

Hiding in the claustrophobic spaces between the roots of the red-glowing vines are the intelligences. They are not the masters of this domain; they are its survivors. They are a people, if you can call them that—let us call them the *Shard-Kin*.

They are slight, bipedal figures, standing no taller than a human child, but possessed of an elongated, wiry strength. Their skin is the color of the slate—grey, dark, and mottled—rendering them invisible when they crouch against the rocks. To survive the "humming darkness," they have evolved eyes that are vast, black pools, reflecting only the tiniest fraction of light, maximizing every photon from the pulsing mosses. Their ears are large and mobile, swiveling independently to dissect the wall of noise, filtering the roar of the water to hear the footfall of a Hex-Scroller.

They are furtive. You see one now, freezing as you brush aside a fern. It is clutching a piece of the young earth’s violence in its hand: a jagged obsidian blade. They do not craft this blade; the world crafts it for them. They are masters of selection. They scour the scree for the sharpest natural shards, testing them with sensitive fingertips before lashing them to handles of tough, flexible vine. This is their primitive tech. They do not build because the ground betrays them; the landslides would bury a hut in minutes. Instead, they are nomads of the vertical.

Their "architecture" is the stacked stones you see—cairns balanced precariously on steam vents. These are not shelters, but communication posts. The heat from the vents warms the stones, and the Shard-Kin, with their sensitive heat-sensing pits along their jawlines, can read these thermal signatures in the dark like beacons. A "hot" stack means a hunting party has passed; a "cool" one means danger.

They move with a silence that defies the deafening environment. They use the chaotic noise of the Basin to mask their movements. When they strike, it is with the suddenness of a rockslide, using their obsidian cutters to sever the thick vines for bridge-building or to harvest the meat of a fallen Glass-Wing. They wear loincloths woven from the shedded chitin of the insects, waterproof and armor-piercing.

They are watching you now, a dozen pairs of black eyes reflecting the frantic pulse of the moss. They do not attack; they assess. In this world, newness is dangerous, and you are a variable they have not calculated. One of them shifts, and the bioluminescent lichen on the rock beneath its feet flares a sudden, angry red—a warning. They are not the kings of the Shattered Cradle; they are the rats in the walls, infinitely adaptable, infinitely cautious, owning nothing but the sharp stone in their hand and the next breath of humid, iron-tasting air. They live in the gaps between the booms and the buzzing, threading the needle of survival in a place that wants to crush them, cut them, and drown them all at once.
