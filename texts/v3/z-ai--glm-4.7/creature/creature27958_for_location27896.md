Here, in the crushing, amniotic warmth of the Drowned Basalt, the concept of "wildlife" has merged with "architecture." The inhabitants do not merely live *in* the ruins; they have evolved to *become* the missing components of the city, mimicking the geometry of the lost civilization to survive in a world where light is currency and silence is armor.

**The Litho-Mimics**

The first and most numerous citizens are the Litho-Mimics, a genus of colossal crab-like entities that have forsaken the typical jagged armor of their shallow-water cousins. Over eons, their carapaces have evolved to be smooth, rounded, and perfectly flat. They look exactly like the paving stones of the sunken boulevards, save for the faint, rhythmic pulse of violet bioluminescence that beats from beneath their shells.

When the city is "quiet"—that is, when no predators are near—the streets appear to be paved with shifting, living tile. A Mimic will sit motionless for decades, its legs folded flush into its body, indistinguishable from the basalt. They are ambush predators of the highest order. They do not chase; they wait. When a school of hatchet-fish or a scavenging eel drifts too close to the "ground," the paving stone suddenly splits open. The Mimic does not snap its claws; it inflates its body instantly, creating a vacuum shockwave that sucks the prey into its maw. It is a silent, instantaneous ingestion, and then the street seals itself again, glowing softly as it digests.

**The Gargoyles of the Vents**

Clinging to the vertical rises of the ziggurats are the "Gargoyles." These are six-limbed, amphibian-like reptiles with skin that mimics the pale, white sulfur-bacterial mats covering the buildings. They possess no eyes; instead, their faces are dominated by massive, heat-sensing pits that look like the empty sockets of a skull.

They are the shepherds of the warmth. They cluster tightly around the geothermal vents that hiss from the cracks in the temple walls. Their social structure is rigid and physical: they link limbs, forming living chains that drape down the stone sides of the pyramids. The Gargoyles at the bottom, closest to the vent, absorb the scalding minerals; the Gargoyles at the top, cool and waiting to strike, receive the thermal energy transferred through the communal chain. When a large, translucent eel drifts innocently past a window, the chain disengages, and the cooled upper hunters launch themselves into the water with a slow, ballistic grace, wrapping their long, pale bodies around their prey before dragging it down into the dark, thermal embrace of the cluster.

**The Nebula-Weavers**

In the open plazas, the water is thick with the "Nebula-Weavers." These are not solitary creatures but a colonial organism, similar to a Portuguese Man o' War, but vast and structured like a floating web. They are composed of thousands of individual polyps connected by long, glowing tendrils of nerve-string.

A single Nebula-Weaver can span the width of a city block. They drift through the avenues, trailing a net of bioluminescent filaments that flash in complex, strobing patterns. These patterns are not random; they are a lure. The Weavers mimic the mating lights of the local shrimp species. When the shrimp swarm, drawn in by the promise of a mate, they find themselves not in a breeding ground, but in a lethal curtain of neurotoxin. The Weavers do not swim actively; they ride the thermal currents created by the city's vent systems, turning entire districts into drifting, electric traps that clean the water of smaller life.

**The stained Glass Swimmers**

Perhaps the most beautiful and unsettling inhabitants are the ones that inhabit the hollowed-out interiors of the great stone temples. These are large, flat, ray-like creatures with bodies composed entirely of translucent cartilage. They have no distinct coloration of their own; rather, their skin is filled with chromatophores that they manipulate to match the light filtering through the "windows" of the ruins—holes in the stone where the walls have collapsed.

They hover perfectly still in the center of dark rooms. If a beam of blue light from a passing crab cuts through the gloom, the ray suddenly illuminates its own wings with that exact same blue, becoming a two-dimensional plane of light. From the front, they are invisible. From the side, they are a blinding flash. They are the assassins of the interior. They wait for something to enter the temple—a lost fish, a daring scavenger—and then, with a flick of a wing, they vanish from sight, reappearing only at the moment of impact, their bodies flashing a blinding white that stuns their prey before consumption.

**The Leviathan Architects**

Looming above them all, drifting through the wide canyons between the mega-structures, are the Leviathan Architects. These are whale-sized squid, but they possess a unique biological adaptation: their tentacles are tipped with bio-luminescent "bait" that looks exactly like the flowering, crystalline structures of the ancient city's decorations.

They are the "gardeners" of the deep. They do not destroy the stone; they manipulate it. Using powerful, hydraulic arms, they will rearrange fallen blocks or clear debris from the vents, ensuring the flow of warm water remains unimpeded. In return, the ecosystem they maintain provides them with food. They move with a terrifying, slow majesty, their bodies pulsing with deep red lights that command respect. Even the giant crabs scuttle out of the way when the Leviathan’s red beacon approaches, clearing the boulevards as if for a royal procession. They are the closest thing this place has to gods—beings so large they shape the very environment that shapes them.

**The Echo-Walkers**

Finally, in the deepest, darkest crevices where no light penetrates, live the Echo-Walkers. Pale, eyeless humanoid figures that seem more like ghosts than flesh. They are not human, but amphibians that have evolved a skeletal structure eerily similar to the ancient builders. They navigate solely through sound and touch. Their skin is hypersensitive, capable of feeling the pressure waves of a heartbeat from ten meters away. They communicate by clicking their elongated finger bones against the stone, creating a sonar map of the city. They are the scavengers of the extremes, the ones who eat the things that die in the dark, cleaning the tombs with reverence and efficiency, ensuring that even in death, biology feeds the stone.
