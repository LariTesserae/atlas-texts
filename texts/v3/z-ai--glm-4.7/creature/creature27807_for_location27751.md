The inhabitants of the Grove of Hollows are not merely swimmers; they are manifestations of the medium itself, shaped by the absolute authority of the water. They call themselves the Drift, though they possess no word for it in their own tongue—only a gesture: a hand opening slowly in the current, fingers uncurling like fronds of kelp.

They are tall and impossibly lithe, their physiology evolved to navigate the vertical city with minimal effort. Their limbs are elongated, possessing an extra joint that allows them to fold into the alcoves of the tower walls like resting spiders. Their skin is not the rough, porous hide of land-dwellers, but a smooth, rubbery membrane, pale as moonlight and faintly translucent. Through it, one can see the slow, dark pulse of blood, visible because the water here is so oxygen-rich that their circulatory systems run near the surface, lacking the need for deep insulation.

They possess no hair. Instead, their heads are crowned with delicate, drifting tendrils that serve a dual function: sensory antennae to detect the subtle shifts in current and the chemical signatures of the water, and insulation, trapping a thin layer of warm water against their skulls. These tendrils are usually dyed with the crushed paste of deep-sea anemones, staining them hues of bruised purple and violent blue, or left bone-white for the elders who have ceased to hunt.

Their faces are hauntingly flat, adapted for hydrodynamic efficiency. The nose is reduced to two small, crescent slits that seal shut when diving deep. The mouth is small, often hidden behind a breathing apparatus or simply closed in a perpetual, meditative pout. But it is the eyes that arrest the observer: vast, dark orbs lacking whites, optimized for the specific, shafted light of the plateau. They see in gradients of pressure and luminance, capable of spotting the bioluminescence of a jellyfish a hundred meters away in the gloom.

Respiration is their most distinct feature. They do not gill like fish, nor do they breathe air. They possess a complex respiratory collar—a ruffled structure of soft tissue that encircles their neck and upper chest. This collar pulses rhythmically, extracting oxygen directly from the hyper-oxygenated water with an efficiency that allows them to remain motionless for hours. When they sleep, suspended in woven kelp hammocks, this collar flutters softly, producing a faint, whistling melody that contributes to the city's ambient hum.

The Drift are obligate tool-users, but their technology is dictated by the materials at hand. Their hands are webbed, but the webbing is retractable, pulling back to reveal long, dexterous fingers tipped with hardened, chitinous nails. These nails are their primary chisels. They spend years carving the soft inner pith of the tower-algae, scraping it away grain by grain to create the smooth, glowing windows of their homes. They wear belts of scaled leather harvested from the crystal turtles, pouches hung with obsidian blades and shells honed to a razor’s edge.

Clothing is minimal and purely functional. They wear loincloths and sashes woven from the strongest fibers of the floating kelp, dyed to signal status or family lineage. The weavers of the Grove are revered; their ability to knot the living seaweed into nets that can hold a full-grown adult without tearing is considered a form of high magic. These nets serve as everything from furniture to transportation—when the currents are strong, they simply cast a net into the flow and ride the invisible rivers between the towers.

They are pastoralists of the vertical void. The relationship with the six-sided crystal turtles is one of mutual stewardship. The Drift do not ride the turtles; instead, they tend to them. Using small, stone-bound scrapers, they groom the moss and algae from the turtles’ shells, harvesting it for food and medicine while keeping the creatures clean and buoyant. In return, the turtles act as mobile anchors for the city, their massive, slow bodies providing shelter for the smaller fish that the Drift cultivate in open-water "corrals" made of woven kelp.

Socially, they are solitary but interconnected. Privacy is the highest virtue in the Grove; a dwelling with a window facing another is considered an insult. They communicate primarily through a complex language of clicks and hums, amplified by the resonance of the water, but their true conversation happens in the silence between words. Because sound travels so fast and far here, they have learned to speak only when necessary, preferring to communicate through the slow, intentional flashing of bioluminescent patches on their wrists—signals that can be seen across the vast distances of the plateau.

Metabolically, they are creatures of the deep chill. They move with a glacial grace, conserving heat. To see a Drift run or thrash is to see them in mortal terror. They prefer to drift, to scull gently with a flick of a long foot, letting the hyper-oxygenated water buoy them. They eat rarely, sustaining themselves on the nutrient-rich moss they harvest and the occasional gelatinous catch from their drift nets.

They are, in essence, parasites of the benevolent giant that is the city. They do not own the towers; they inhabit them much like the hermit crabs inhabit shells, understanding that the "walls" are alive, that the floor beneath their feet is digesting, and that the light filtering through the translucent architecture is the breath of their host. They are a people defined by stillness, living in a rushing world, carving their existence into the ribs of plants that touch the sky.
