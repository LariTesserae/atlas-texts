The life of the High Drown does not merely inhabit the stone; it continues it. If the towers are the bones of this world, the creatures here are the sinew and the nerve, wrapping around the architecture so tightly that it becomes difficult to tell where the mineral ends and the biology begins.

At the foundational level, the stone is alive. The "barnacles" mentioned are not mere crustaceans haphazardly clinging to rock; they are geological sculptors. These are the **Lith-Encrusters**, massive, domed organisms with shells composed of layered basalt so dense they ring like lead when struck. They do not grow randomly; they align themselves in geometric patterns along the spires, following the invisible ley lines of the currents. Their shells are polished to the same glassy sheen as the towers, making them look like bubbling blisters on the skin of the city. They filter the water not for plankton, but for the microscopic mineral dust churned up by the storms, incorporating the silt into their own calcification. They are eating the erosion, constantly rebuilding the stone as the water tries to wear it away.

Draping over these hard surfaces are the **Pale Fringe**—the vast mats of filter-feeding worms. They are not passive plants, however. In the gloom, they appear as a spilled milky smoke, trailing ten feet into the current. They possess a collective intelligence of sorts. When the turbulence shifts, when a vortex begins to spin off a pillar, the Fringe twitches in unison, rippling like a sheet snapping in the wind. This motion creates a localized counter-current, a microscopic buffer zone that protects the smaller creatures hiding within the forest of worms. They are the city’s insulation, dampening the violent vibration of the water into a gentle, rhythmic sway.

 darting through the safety of the Fringe are the **Silver-Needles**. These fish are evolved for a reality defined by hard edges. They are rectangular in cross-section, flattened laterally to slip between the crashing columns of water. They move not with a fish’s undulating grace, but with the lock-start precision of machinery. Their scales are mirror-bright, reflecting the dim grey light, effectively rendering them invisible as they pass from shadow to shadow. They do not swim in schools that mill aimlessly; they move in perfect, rotating cubes and cylinders, formations that seem mathematically impossible. They act as a single fluid entity, flowing around a protruding barnacle cluster and re-forming instantly on the other side, leaving no wake. They are the lubricant in the gears of this ecosystem, sliding effortlessly through the friction.

But there are hunters here, things that view the chaos not as a hindrance, but as a weapon.

The apex predator of the High Drown is the **Basalt-Stalker**. It is the "shark-eel" cross, a nightmare of muscle and cartilage that has evolved to hunt in absolute silence and zero visibility. It has no eyes. Its head is a smooth, tapered wedge of obsidian-black skin, indistinguishable from the towers it hides against. It hunts by feel. Its entire body is covered in a lateral line system so sensitive it can detect the heartbeat of a needle-fish from twenty yards away, sensing the displacement of water against the crushing background pressure.

The Stalker does not chase its prey; it anticipates it. It positions itself against the smooth flank of a tower, mimicking the texture of the stone, waiting for the currents to do the work. When a school of Silver-Needles is funneled into a narrow channel between two spires by a rogue current, the Stalker simply opens its mouth—a cavernous, circular void lined with concentric rows of needle-like teeth. It inhales, and the violence of the water serves it, stuffing the screaming mass of fish into its maw. Then, it snaps its jaw shut, clamping its body to the stone, and waits for the current to die down, indistinguishable once more from the architecture.

And finally, there are the **Resonators**.

These are the giants, solitary leviathans that drift in the open water between the districts, too large to care about the turbulence. They resemble manta rays, but their "wings" are vast, triangular sheets of translucent bone, etched with spiraling patterns that glow with a faint, bioluminescent cold—the only light in this place. They do not eat the fish; they eat the sound. The impact of the water against the towers creates immense acoustic energy, and the Resonators glide through the dark, their mouths agape, filtering the sonic vibrations out of the water. They are attuned to the "teeth-grinding" thrum of the universe.

As they pass, they absorb the deep, resonant tones that hum through the stone, quieting the chaotic roar of the High Drown into a temporary, mournful silence. The fish seem to slow in their presence, mesmerized by the absorption of the noise. To live in the High Drown is to endure a constant, low-frequency scream; the Resonators are the only mercy here, drifting ghosts that swallow the scream, if only for a moment, before moving on into the grey twilight, leaving the heavy silence to rush back in and fill the void.
