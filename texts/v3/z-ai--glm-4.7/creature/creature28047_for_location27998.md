To look for them with your eyes is to go blind with frustration. In the High Hollow, where the horizon is a grey lap and the air hangs heavy with wet, cold static, the inhabitants do not stand out against the backdrop—they are the backdrop. They are the reason the city feels like a city, and the source of the "structured" weirdness in the wind.

They are called the **Resonants**.

At first glance, a Resonant is indistinguishable from the standing stones that pepper the plateau. They are tall, perhaps eight or nine feet in height, but impossibly thin, their physiology eroded just like the basalt pillars they mimic. Their limbs are elongated, tapering into spindly, articulated points that look like natural fractures in rock. They possess no distinct head, neck, or torso in the humanoid sense; rather, their body is a single, continuous, tapering column of pale, grey flesh that possesses the texture and coldness of wet river stone.

Their skin is porous and constantly weeping. In the 0.3 cold, where the dampness is omnipresent, the Resonants do not shiver. They are vascular conduits for the environment’s moisture. Water slides over their slick, grey hides in a perpetual sheen, freezing instantly into delicate lace-like rims of ice along their shoulders and joints—ornaments that crack and reform with every micro-movement they make. This wet, rocky skin acts as a perfect camouflage against the fog-shrouded scarps. If you were to look directly at one, you would see only a slightly darker shadow against the mist, a vertical tear in the uniformity of the grey.

They are not quick; they are glacial. Because the environment is so low in density and vast in empty space, there is no need for haste. The Resonants move with a geological senescence. They drift across the moss-plazas in a silent, sliding shuffle, their tapered feet finding purchase on the slick granite without a sound. To watch one move is to watch time-lapse photography of a glacier; you look away, look back, and the "statue" has tilted, has advanced a yard toward a shallow pool.

They are the architects of the Hollow’s roar.

The Resonants have no eyes, for there is little to see in the perpetual wet twilight. Nor do they have mouths in the traditional sense. Instead, they are creatures of pure acoustics. Their entire elongated bodies are hollow, filled with complex, spongy internal chambers that function like organic wind instruments. They "breathe" the erratic air currents (2.1 dynamics) through pores located at their tops. As the wind rushes over the plateau, it is caught by a Resonant, channeled through its internal labyrinth, and expelled as a pure, resonant tone.

The "voice" of the Hollow—the mournful wails and harmonic chords that sound almost human—is the language of the Resonants. They sing to one another across the vast, empty distances, their voices carrying for miles on the thin air. A "conversation" between two of them might sound to a human ear like a ghostly duet of flutes and bassoons, shifting pitch as the wind direction changes. They do not merely tolerate the noise; they cultivate it. When the temperature drops and the condensation freezes rapidly, the "cracking reports" you hear are not just rocks splitting—they are the Resonants snapping their icy shells in a rhythmic percussive chorus, a physical punctuation to their long, droning songs.

Their relationship with the "urban" geography is one of occupation and mimicry. They are not the builders of the stone city, but its squatters and caretakers. They cluster in the "plazas," standing perfectly still in circles, their bodies arranged to maximize the acoustic potential of the space. When a group of them stands together, the wind rushing through their collective hollows creates a massive, harmonic vibration—a bass thrumming that you feel in your teeth. They use this resonance to communicate with the deep earth, perhaps, or simply to shake the moisture from the velvet moss that grows on their joints.

And the moss *does* grow on them. This is their only warmth. The vegetation rating (1.5) is high because the Resonants act as mobile gardens. Thick, emerald, chartreuse, and bruised-purple carpets of moss cling to their backs and shoulders, thriving on the minimal heat radiating from their slow-moving biological processes. In return, the moss provides a layer of insulation against the freezing sting, a furry, green coat that makes them look like ancient, weathered druids covered in verdant robes. From a distance, a sleeping Resonant looks exactly like a small, rolling mossy hill.

They are rare (fauna 1.0), solitary wanderers in a vast room. You might walk for hours and see nothing but stone and fog, but then, in the center of a causeway, you will see the "mountain goat" mentioned in travelers' tales. But it is not a goat. It is a Resonant, frozen in the act of drinking from a pool, its body bent like a bow, siphoning water through the tubes of its arms. It will watch you, though it has no eyes—it senses the disturbance your body makes in the air currents, the acoustic shadow you cast.

If you stand still for too long, a Resonant might mistake you for a new pillar. It will approach slowly, the wind beginning to hum through its body as it prepares to "tune" you, to see if you can sing in the choir of the High Hollow. To be touched by one is not to be attacked, but to be felt—cold, wet, and incredibly hard, like a living glacier checking the temperature of your soul. They are the ghosts of the city, the stone that learned to breathe, the lonely architects of a song that has been playing since the mountains were young.
