To exist in the Under-Song is to be part of the digestion of the world. The beings here are not merely inhabitants; they are components of a vast, biological engine that processes stone into flesh and flesh back into slurry.

**The Otoliths: The Architects of Ephemera**

Scuttling amidst the crude basalt huts are the intelligent natives, a people who appear to have evolved from the very cave-wall itself. They are bipedal, but low to the ground, possessed of a hunched, hydro-dynamic posture meant for slipping through tight crevices. Their skin is the color and texture of wet limestone—pale, mottled grey, and porous.

They have no spoken language, for the roar of the cavern renders sound useless. Instead, they communicate through a complex, tapping language performed with the long, hollow bones of slaughtered leviathans they carry strapped to their backs. The rhythm of their tapping syncs with the subsonic grinding of the tectonic plates.

They are dressed in layers of scavenged chitin and wet leather, bound together with the sinew of the bat-things. Their eyes are covered by thick, transparent membranes to protect against the mineral haze, making them look like blind, marble statues come to life. They are perpetually damp, steam rising from their shoulders, and they move with a frantic, jittery energy. They do not build to last; their stone altars and bone-waystations are sacrificial, meant to serve a purpose for a few hours before the mud swallows them. They are the transients of chaos, worshipping the churn by trying to predict it.

**The Grist: The Tide of Mandibles**

Covering the floor like a living carpet is the biomass known as the Grist. These are the pale, eyeless crustaceans mentioned, resembling a grotesque hybrid of a crab and a trilobite, swollen to the size of large dogs. They are white as parchment, with segmented, plated backs that clatter together like a bag of marbles.

They possess no individual will; they are a hydraulic force of nature. Their mandibles are oversized, constantly dripping with a corrosive enzyme that allows them to feed on the chemosynthetic bacteria crusting the rocks, or, more frequently, on the softer rock itself. They act as the sandpaper of the cavern, smoothing the jagged spires as they clamber over them. When a Slump-Beast falls, the Grist does not swarm; they *liquefy.* They climb over one another, forming a twitching pyramid of legs and claws that strips the carcass in a frenzy of wet snaps, leaving the ground polished and white.

**The Slump-Beasts: The Living Batteries**

Lumbering through the knee-deep mud are the primary food source: the Slump-Beasts. These are massive, sluggish creatures shaped like a bloated, concave disc, with legs positioned splayed out at the edges to keep them from sinking into the mire. They have no heads, only a sphincter-like mouth on the tops of their bodies, constantly open to the dripping ceiling.

Their hides are thick, rubbery, and crusted with a layer of grey mineral deposit, making them look like moving boulders. They are warm to the touch, radiating a metabolic heat that attracts the Grist and the smaller predators. They seem entirely indifferent to their surroundings, slowly grazing on the mineral-rich mud, converting the earth’s heat into flesh. When they die, it is not with a scream, but with a slow, depressing collapse, like a deflated soufflé, releasing a cloud of steam that signals the end of their existence.

**The Striders: The Granite Horror**

Wading through the swarm are the apex predators: the Striders. They are terrifying in their silence. Where the rest of the cavern screams, the Striders only crunch.

Standing fifteen feet tall at the shoulder, they resemble hairless, grey-skinned elephants, but their legs are not pillars; they are pillars of jagged basalt. The creatures naturally excrete a rapid-hardening mineral secretion that fuses with the mud and debris clinging to their legs, creating a perpetually growing, jagged armor of stone. They look like walking hills.

They do not chase prey; they harvest it. They move through the tide of crustaceans, lowering heads that are all jaw and muscle, and simply scoop up mouthfuls of the scuttling tide. The Grist cannot bite through the Striders’ stony armor, and so the Striders are an unstoppable force of consumption, impervious to the swarms, driven by a slow, relentless hunger.

**The Vapors: The Sky Weavers**

The air is filled with the Vapors. They are not truly bats, but something closer to leathern kites. They have no bones, only cartilage and muscle, allowing them to fold themselves into impossible shapes to navigate the turbulent air currents.

They hang from the ceiling by hooked talons when they sleep, looking like wet drapes of grey flesh. When they fly, they do not flap so much as they ripple, swimming through the humid air. They feed on the midges and moisture-hovering gnats, but they also scrape the mineral residue from the stalactites with rasp-like tongues. Their wings are charged with static electricity; when a flock of them moves through the fog, the air hums with the smell of ozone, and their passing leaves faint, ghostly trails of light in the phosphorescent haze.

**The Entity: The Pattern in the Chaos**

And then, there is the feeling of something else. The Otoliths sometimes pause in their tapping to press their ears against the wet stone. They believe the cavern is not just a place, but a single, dormant organism, and they are the bacteria living within its gut. The "currents" the swarms follow are not random. They follow the veins of quartz that run through the floor, pulsing with magnetic fields.

If one watches closely, the chaos of the scuttling Grist and the soaring Vapors forms a slow, rhythmic breathing. The entire ecosystem moves in a single, discordant heartbeat—a biology dictated entirely by the geology. The stone lives, and it is hungry, and everything here—from the highest Strider to the lowliest Otolith—is simply a different way for the stone to eat itself.
