At first, you see nothing. The landscape is a blinding, monochromatic roar of white-green light and grey, twisting fiber, and the inhabitants are precisely calibrated to disappear within it. They are not masters of this domain; they are grit in the gearbox, piggybacking on a biology that would crush them without a moment’s thought.

To see them, you must stop looking for "people" and start looking for discrepancies in the texture of the moss. They are the *Verticals*.

They possess a physiology that despises the horizontal. Their limbs are too long, possessed of an atrophied grace that suggests they rarely stand upright. Their legs are short, powerfully muscled columns of dense bone, ending not in feet, but in brutal, hooked talons that resemble the ice axes of a mountaineer. These hooks are natural, keratinous ridges grown over generations to shear into the fibrous meat of the wall.

Their torsos are lean, almost wasp-waisted, tapering up to shoulders that support disproportionately long, multi-jointed arms. The hands are their defining feature: three massive, opposing digits capable of spanning the width of a human head, tipped with spatulate pads that provide suction on the wet, slick surfaces of the moss-curtains. They do not grip; they adhere.

Their skin is the color of wet slate—grey, blue-grey, occasionally flashing the pallor of a dead fish when they are startled. It is thick, leathery, and hairless, providing a barrier against the constant, aggressive dampness that would prune a human into helplessness within hours. This skin hangs loosely on their frames, a "wattle" that allows them to twist and contort through the tight meshes of the root-lattice without tearing.

And then there are the eyes. You would expect them to be large, to gather light in the haze. Instead, they are slits, heavily hooded and protected by a permanent, translucent second eyelid. The light here is not dim; it is an aggressive, clinical glare. To see is to risk blindness. Their visual spectrum is shifted away from the blinding white-green, likely seeing more into the ultraviolet or the thermal, tracking the heat signatures of the decomposing slurry deep within the wall rather than relying on reflected photons. Because of this, they navigate the "cathedral of negative space" by feeling the pressure waves of the wind and the vibrations in the root-matrix. They listen with their fingertips.

They live in the crevices—the "folds" of the world. You will find them clustered in the lee of a massive fungal stalk, anchoring themselves to the living rock with their leg-hooks. They are not a numerous species; they cannot be. The "carrying capacity" of a wall that tries to digest you every Tuesday is incredibly low.

Their society is built around the "Line." Every individual is tethered. From a belt woven from the dried intestines of the floating moth-things, a cord of braided vine runs to a piton driven deep into the root-mass. They never sever this connection. Even when they sleep—curled into tight, fetal balls within the hammocks of hanging moss—they are tied to the architecture.

Technology here is not about creating comfort; it is about instantaneous release. The "buildings" you saw, the woven platforms, are not homes. They are harvest stations. The Verticals swarm these structures like ants on a crumb, but they do not relax. They work. They use those wooden scrapers to carve into the white cork-skin of the wall, collecting the sap that bleeds out. This sap is their everything: food, water, fuel, and adhesive. It is a frantic, sweating labor. You can see the steam rising from their shoulders as they hack at the tree-wall, their movements jerky and precise, minimizing wasted energy.

They have a vocal language, but it is rarely used. Sound travels too well in the echoing void, and noise attracts the wrong kind of attention—specifically, the predatory tremors of the wall itself. Instead, they communicate through a complex sign language performed with their upper hands while their lower hands maintain their grip. It looks like a frantic, silent dance, a semaphore of survival.

The most terrifying aspect of their existence is their relationship with the "Shift"—the moment when the vegetation decides to churn. They do not panic. Panic wastes time. They possess a zen-like acceptance of catastrophe.

When the great ripple begins in the root-matrix, when the cliff face begins to buckle and the "ground" beneath them turns to liquid, the Verticals move with a singular, fluid purpose. They do not run; there is nowhere to run. They *climb out*. They release their pitons, retract their hooks, and simply fall upward, catching the rising thermal currents generated by the heat of decomposition. They use their loose skin as a primitive airfoil, gliding on the updrafts, becoming fragile moths themselves for a few precious seconds.

They drift across the miles-wide abyss, carried by the warm wind, navigating the turbulence until they find purchase on a new, stable section of the wall. It is a terrifying gamble. Many miss their target and spiral down into the silent, luminescent abyss, lost specks in a hurricane.

But those who make it land running. They instantly drive their hooks into the new surface, anchor their lines, and begin the process of scraping out a new depression in the moss to sleep in. They are nomads of the vertical axis, eternal refugees fleeing a disaster that hasn't happened yet.

On their person, they carry very little. A bone scraper. A gourd of sap. And often, a small, heavy stone wrapped in layers of soft fur. When they stop to rest, they will hold this stone to their chest, pressing it against their skin. In a world where gravity feels light and the wind threatens to snatch you away, where the ground is a living beast that moves beneath your feet, this stone is the only thing that is truly, reliably solid. It is their totem, their anchor, their proof that *down* still exists.

They are a haunted, beautiful people. Pale, hook-handed ghosts clinging to the side of a thundering biological machine. They do not dream of the stars; they dream of flat earth. They pray for silence, for stillness, for a day when the wall stops breathing long enough for them to simply sit down.
