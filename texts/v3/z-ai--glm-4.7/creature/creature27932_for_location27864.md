To live here is not to survive, but to function. The inhabitants of this wet, embryonic dark are not animals in the traditional sense—they are components, enzymes, and tools within the digestive tract of a creation event. They are the labor force of the lung, born from the iron-tasting air and the raw, unspooled time.

They are silent, efficient, and utterly, terrifyingly alien.

**The Janitors: The Caliper-Beetles**

The smallest things you see are the beetles mentioned in the passing mist, but up close, they are marvels of impossible taxonomy. They are about the size of a human hand, possessing shells that are not chitin, but cold, brushed brass and etched glass. Their legs are precise, needle-thin spokes of steel that click against the wet moss without sinking.

They do not eat. They calibrate. They scuttle frantically across the pulsing rubbery vines, clamping their mandibles onto the neon-blue conduits to test the pressure. If a vein is pumping reality too slowly, a beetle will sever a smaller tributary with a snip of its jaws, redirecting the flow. They are the quality control agents of the biology. When they die, they do not rot; they simply rust, dissolving into a pile of orange powder that fertilizes the very floor they walked upon. They are blind, navigating by the taste of magnetism in the air, forever tuning the instrument of this world.

**The Scribes: The Fractal Birds**

Higher up, where the bruised light struggles to penetrate, fly the creatures that resemble torn scripture. They are not flesh and feather, but folded origami of pale parchment, inscribed with shifting, microscopic text that seems to narrate their own movement.

They move in a jagged, stop-motion flurry, like a video buffering. These are the data-runners of the green city. They land on the throb-thrum-thrum of the biological engines and press their paper-thin beaks against the surfaces, drinking the condensation that tastes like memory. As they drink, the ink on their wings changes—schematics are drawn, erased, and redrawn. They carry the blueprints of the unfinished hills from the canopy down to the root-tentacles below. They never sing, but when a flock takes flight, the sound is a dry, rhythmic *hiss* like a thousand pages being turned at once.

**The Shadows: The Umbral Scuttlers**

Because the light here is dishonest and the physics merely suggestions, the shadows have agency. Detaching from the base of the towering plant-pillars are flat, two-dimensional shapes that slide across the wet slurry like oil on water.

These are the scavengers. They have no depth; viewed from the side, they vanish. Viewed from above, they are jagged, jagged silhouettes of spiders or crabs, composed entirely of darkness. They hunt the stray moments of time that drip from the ceiling. When a drop of "raw time" falls from the unseen sky, a Shadow will unfurl itself like a net, encapsulating the drop and absorbing it. They are dangerous only if you stand still too long; if you linger, they will try to detach *your* shadow, mistaking the history you carry behind you for food. To have your shadow eaten here is to forget why you came.

**The Architects: The Root-Tentacles**

In the distance, the massive "hands" that knit the geography together are not hands at all, but the distended tips of colossal, sub-terrestrial worms. They are white, pale as bone, and thick as towers, ending in fingers of delicate, probing root-hairs.

They move with the slow, grinding grace of tectonic plates. You cannot see their bodies, only the tips emerging from the granular slurry to fold the earth into hills. They secrete a mucus that hardens instantly into rock. They are the masons of this hollow cathedral, blind and deaf, driven solely by the architectural imperative to build a floor for the feet of a god who has not yet arrived. They hum—a low, bass vibration that rattles the teeth of anyone standing nearby—the sound of the earth arguing with itself over what shape it should take.

**The Overseers: The Draftsmen**

Finally, there are the ones that watch. Looming in the vast, empty chasms between the bio-industrial pillars are tall, elongated figures that look like sketches of men gone wrong.

They are humanoid, but their edges are blurry, as if they are vibrating at a frequency that solid matter cannot quite sustain. Their limbs are too long; their faces are smooth, featureless planes of wet clay. They wear no clothes, but their skin is etched with the glowing blue veins of the landscape, pulsing in sync with the city.

They do not walk; they glide, floating inches above the hissing slurry. They carry no tools, yet wherever they point, the vegetation erupts in new growth, or the geometry of the room twists—a hallway bends, a ceiling arches. They are the foremen of this insane construction site. They do not speak, but they communicate via the sudden, localized rain—a downpour that falls only around you, chilling and heavy, conveying a sense of disappointment or inspection.

To be seen by a Draftsman is to feel the weight of the unfinished world pressing against your skin. They are the hesitation of the creator made flesh. They watch you not with malice, but with the cold curiosity of an architect wondering if a speck of dust on the blueprint is intentional or a mistake.
