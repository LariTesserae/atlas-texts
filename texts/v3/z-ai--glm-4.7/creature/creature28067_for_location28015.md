They are not things of flesh in the way you understand flesh; they are things of aggregate, sediment, and slow, rhythmic pressure. If this place is a world caught in the act of becoming, then its inhabitants are the laborers sentenced to the shift, evolving not through generations, but through the abrasion of their current existence.

To see one, you must first unlearn the shape of an animal. They do not scuttle so much as they *disaggregate*. The primary creature of this zone is a solitary, tall figure that stands vertically against the grain of the leaning towers, camouflaged perfectly against the wet granite. Call them the **Strata-Walkers**.

They possess a physiology of grotesque practicality. Their skin is not soft, but a keratinous plate so dense and gray it resembles the poured concrete itself. It hangs in loose, wet folds around their limbs, dripping with the oily condensation that coats the city. When they stand still, which they do for hours at a time, they look exactly like a pillar that has suffered a catastrophic crack and slumped slightly to the left. The folds of their skin allow them to expand; when the ground beneath them heaves upward with the tectonic emergence of a new sub-basement, the Strata-Walker does not brace against it, but simply inflates, its armor loosening to absorb the shock like a fluid-filled sac.

Their limbs are too long, possessing an extra joint that bends backward, allowing them to navigate the non-Euclidean madness of the architecture. They can walk up a vertical wall as easily as they walk across a horizontal slab, their feet wide and splayed, equipped with ridged, toe-like nails that act as natural chisels, gripping the primitive tool-marks left in the stone. They move with a horrifying, liquid grace, slithering over sharp corners and bridging gaps that shouldn't exist, their bodies seemingly unbothered by gravity.

They have no eyes. In this bruised, gloom-filled basin where light is a mere suggestion, vision is a liability. Instead, they navigate through the roar. The Strata-Walkers are acoustically hyper-sensitive; their entire elongated skulls are ribbed with bony amplifiers that catch the groans of the settling wood and the grinding of the massive gears. They "see" the texture of the world through sound waves. The constant *drip-drip-drip* creates a pointillist map of their surroundings, allowing them to pinpoint the location of a rusting iron joint or a slick patch of mineral-weeping with terrifying accuracy. The deafening noise of the place is their canvas; when a sharp crack echoes through the fog, they flinch as if struck, reading the structural failure of a distant beam as clearly as you would read a neon sign.

Their mouths are a horror of utility—a vertical, slit-like opening that runs from the throat down the chest, protected by heavy mandibles that grind together with a wet, stony sound. They do not eat the absence of vegetation; they eat the building itself. They are lithovores, drawn to the raw, fresh scratches of the newly emerged stone. They feed on the mineral energy of the "pre-formation," licking the copper patina and iron oxide with rasping tongues that strip the metal down to the base rock. You might see one hunched over a fresh fissure, greedily consuming the cold slate dust, its body vibrating as it digests the friction of the world’s birth.

Social interaction is nonexistent, reduced to violent territorial disputes over the most acoustically "quiet" niches—the rare alcoves where the roar of the gears is dampened by the arrangement of the walls. They communicate not by voice, but by percussion. Using their heavy, stone-like fists, they hammer on the hollow walls of the buried city, creating complex, rhythmic knocking patterns that travel for miles through the granite. To a human ear, it sounds like the settling of the house; to them, it is a warning, a mating call, or a declaration of ownership.

They are not the only life, however. Sharing this vertical nightmare are the **Gear-Mites**, smaller, swarming abominations that live within the massive, hill-sized wooden mechanisms. They look like pale, wood-colored spiders with too many legs, their bodies segmented and hard like beetles. They are scavengers of the "inverted erosion." Where the Strata-Walkers eat the stone, the Gear-Mites eat the friction. They scuttle along the teeth of the massive grinding wheels, risking being crushed to snap up the splinters and hot sawdust produced by the machine. They are essential to the ecosystem, clearing the jam points of the great gears, acting as an immune system for the blind construction.

Occasionally, when the fog thins just enough, you might see the silhouette of a Strata-Walker perched on a high arch, motionless, its wet skin glistening in the dim gray. It looks for all the world like a gargoyle forgotten by the architects, waiting for the damp to seep into its bones until it, too, becomes nothing more than another block of granite in the endless, chaotic sprawl of the Total Build. They are the custodians of a madness they do not understand, forever climbing, forever listening, and forever feeding on the cold, hard bones of a world that refuses to finish itself.
