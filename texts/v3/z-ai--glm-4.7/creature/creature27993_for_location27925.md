To inhabit the Hall of the Mother-wood is not to live within a forest, but to dwell within the viscera of a continent-sized engine. The creatures here are not merely adapted to the noise and the damp; they are components of the industrial biological process. They are the maintenance crew, the quality assurance agents, and the scavengers of a perfectly efficient, eternally running factory.

They are armored against the vibration. They are deafened by the thrum. They are streamlined to navigate the negative space.

**The Floor-Bourne Custodians: The Ironclad Scavengers**
Dominating the empty expanse of the root-pavement are the massive, armadillo-like beetles mentioned in the preliminary surveys. These are the primary macro-scavengers of the hall, biological tanks clad in chitinous plates that resemble hammered bronze. They are roughly the size of a large dog or a small calf, possessing a low, wide center of gravity that allows them to traverse the spongy, shifting floor without stumbling.

Because the ambient hydraulic thrum of the walls is deafening, communication through sound is impossible. Instead, these beetles communicate through seismic percussion. They tap their heavy, spiked forelegs against the compacted root-floor, creating complex rhythmic patterns that travel through the fibrous geology. To an observer, the floor seems to twitch with a life of its own, a Morse code of foraging signals.

Their shells are not smooth; they are ridged and pitted, designed to catch the constant enzyme-rich mist that rises from the dissolving detritus. They are the self-cleaning mechanisms of the floor, crawling in slow, methodical grids. When they locate a rare patch of un-dissolved organic matter—a spill of spores or a fallen fragment from the canopy—they extend a rasp-like proboscis, grinding the material into paste that is instantly absorbed. They do not stop moving. To stop is to seize up in the damp, cool air.

**The Wall-Dwelling Grafters: The Vascular Barnacles**
If one looks closely at the seamless bark walls—those vertical cliffs of lignin pulsing with peristalsis—movement can be seen. It is not the wall itself moving, but the *Grafters*. These are strange, sessile organisms that resemble a cross between a horseshoe crab and a leech. They clamp their bodies tightly into the crevices of the bark, anchoring themselves with needle-like talons that penetrate the outer layers to tap into the nutrient-rich sap flowing just beneath.

The sap in the walls moves under tremendous pressure. The Grazers have evolved a specialized hydraulic physiology: they tap the sap, let it fill their expandable, translucent abdominal sacs, and then use the internal pressure to jet-waste fluids out into the air, creating fine, aromatic aerosols. They act as pressure release valves, their bodies shuddering violently with every pulse of the Mother-wood’s heartbeat. They are dangerous to touch; the sap inside them is near-boiling and corrosive, yet they glow with a dull, infrared heat, visible as faint smudges against the cool gray gloom of the wall.

**The Aerial Observers: The Flash-Bats**
High above, in the cathedral gloom of the canopy-rafters, dwell the massive, leather-winged bats. They are the lords of the vertical space, but they rule not through screech or sonar—sound is already cheap here. They rule through light.

These creatures have evolved bioluminescent organs along their wingtips and throats. In the perpetual, colorless twilight, the air is constantly crisscrossed with flashes of neon blue, warning orange, and strobe-white. It is a silent, visual symphony replacing the chaos of the noise below. They do not flap frantically; they glide on the thermals generated by the heat of the hydraulic walls, surfing the currents of the "lung."

Their diet consists primarily of the spore-clouds released by the industrial seed pods. When a pod ratchets open with its steel-on-steel *clack*, releasing a plume of particulate matter, the Flash-Bats dive. They filter the spores through baleen-like fibers in their maws, their bodies glowing brighter as they metabolize the energy-dust. They are the only creatures that add color to the hall, turning the gray air into a strobe-lit dance floor of silent predators.

**The Apex of Silence: The Pulse-Stalkers**
There is one creature, however, that does not belong to the loud machinery or the scavenging floor. It is a rare, solitary predator that hunts in the absolute center of the hall. It is a furred, hexapodal stalker, resembling a hairless panther elongated by an extra set of legs, with skin that mimics the texture of the root-pavement.

The Pulse-Stalker has adapted to the "frozen" dynamic of the hall. It does not move quickly; it moves in imperceptible shifts, syncing its motion with the rhythmic *thrum-hiss-thrum* of the environment. It steps only when the walls pulse, masking the vibration of its footsteps. It holds its breath during the hiss of the exhaust.

It hunts the beetles. Because the beetles are deaf and communicate through the floor, the Stalker has learned to "shadow" their signals. When a beetle taps its leg, the Stalker taps the ground a split-second later from a distance, creating a phantom echo that confuses the scavenger. It lures the beetle into a false sense of security, mimicking a mate or a rival, until it is close enough to strike. It is the only creature in the room that understands the art of deception in a place of absolute honesty.

**The Micro-Engineers**
Finally, filling the air itself are the microscopic workers. The atmosphere is thick with motes, but not all are spores. Millions of tiny, aerial mites drift on the humidity currents. They are invisible to the naked eye, but their presence is felt as a tickle on the skin, a static charge. They exist solely to break down the enzyme-mist further, ensuring that even the air itself is recycled. They are the final filter, the molecular scrubbers of the lung, ensuring that the Hall of the Mother-wood remains a closed, perfect loop, unchanged by the passage of time.
