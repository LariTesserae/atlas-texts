The air is thick with movement, a teeming density of life that rivals the humidity. Here, in the dim, oppressive heat of the Fresh Cut, the fauna does not merely inhabit the space; it infests it, layering over the industrial decay like a second, breathing skin. The true occupants are blind to the light, for the darkness here is absolute, and they have evolved to read the world through the texture of the vibrations that constantly shudder through the rock.

**The Walls of Chitin**
Covering the vertical, black basalt surfaces are the foundational species: the Pale Swarm. These are not merely insects; they are a biological aggregate, a living carpet of eyeless cave crickets and beetles. They are pale, translucent things, their shells lacking pigment in the absence of sun, giving them the ghostly appearance of larvae no matter how large they grow.

They crowd every available inch of the stone, stacked three and four deep upon one another. When the ventilation fans shift their rhythm, the walls seem to liquefy, rippling as thousands of the creatures adjust their footing in unison. They are the grazers of this ecosystem, feeding not on plant life—which is scarce and sickly—but on the organic slimes that coat the wet rocks and the detritus of the machinery. Their antennae are long, whiplike filaments that constantly taste the heavy, velvet air, searching for the chemical signature of rot or the electrical tang of ozone. To touch the wall is to touch a million shifting legs, a sensation of damp, skittering life that refuses to be still.

**The Corrosion Grazers**
Clinging to the maze of burst pipes and exposed intestines of the machinery are a heavier, more distinct variety of creature. These are the Iron-Mites, armor-plated scavengers that look like moving scabs. They have evolved to consume the industrial decay. Their mandibles are strong enough to shear through the peeling insulation of the steam pipes, and they feast on the rubber and the chemical-rich grease that leaks from the joints.

They are drawn to the heat, clustering around the hissing steam vents in seething masses. Their shells are dark, stained by the rust and soot they crawl through, making them nearly invisible against the dirty metal. They are the agents of entropy in this place, accelerating the decay of the "urban" infrastructure. Where a pipe leaks, they gather, slowly carving away the metal until the breach widens, turning a simple drip into a flood, which in turn feeds the mosses and the slimes that feed the crickets. They are the bridge between the machine and the biology.

**The Shadow-Stalkers**
In the gloom between the swaying amber lights, where the industrial catwalks wobble, live the apex predators of the vertical space: the Rust-Weavers. These are arachnids, but swollen to the size of a man’s torso, their legs splayed wide to span the gaps between the railings. They do not spin webs of silk, which would rot instantly in the damp, but rather cables of a rusty, sticky excretion that resembles the wire harnesses of the machinery itself.

They hang motionless in the deep shadows above the walkways, indistinguishable from the dangling chains and cables until they move. They hunt by patience, waiting for a swarm of crickets to surge past or for an Iron-Mite to venture too far from its pipe. When they strike, it is with a wet, heavy thud, the sound often lost in the industrial symphony of the room. They are solitary and territorial, and their presence is usually only betrayed by the piles of crushed, hollow shells that accumulate on the metal grates beneath their perches.

**The Dredgers of the Sump**
Down on the treacherous floor, amidst the slick shale and the channels of runoff water, live the "something heavy" that sometimes falls. These are the Sump-Bellows, massive, blind amphibians that have evolved in the perpetual darkness. They look like bloated, wet rocks come to life, their skin gray and warty, mimicking the texture of the fresh-cut basalt.

They possess no eyes, only wide, cavernous mouths lined with bristle-like teeth. They move with agonizing slowness, a glacial crawl that matches the sluggish pulse of the engines. They are the filter feeders of the cavern, wallowing in the shallow, oily pools that gather in the floor’s depressions. They simply open their mouths and suck in the mud and water, filtering out the crickets, the iron-mites, and the mechanical sludge, excreting the clean waste that forms the slick mud of the floor.

Their breathing is a low, hollow rumble that harmonizes with the thrum of the fans—a slow, wet *hiss-and-click* that adds a bass note to the cacophony. They are nearly invulnerable to the heat; their flesh is thick and waterlogged, acting as a thermal buffer. To step into the shadows of the floor is to risk stumbling over one of these living boulders, a creature that feels no pain and sees nothing, but will instantly engulf a foot that wanders too close to its grinding maw.

**The Ceiling Dwellers**
High above, in the gloom where the light cannot reach, the ceiling is a hive of fliers. These are the Echo-Bats, but they have forsaken flight for the dense air. They possess rudimentary wings that serve more as rudders and climbing surfaces than true flight organs. They scuttle across the roof tiles and hang from the rusted gantries like wet rags.

They scream constantly, a high-pitched sonar that bounces off the wet metal and the rock, creating a mental map of the room that is sharper than any human sight. Their cries mix with the shriek of the steam vents, creating a dissonance that hurts the ears. They swoop down in sudden, erratic dives to snatch insects from the air, their collisions with the pipes sounding like heavy raindrops on a tin roof.

Together, they form a closed, frantic loop. The machines heat the water; the water breeds the slime; the slime feeds the swarm; the swarm feeds the weavers and the dredgers; and the dredgers and weavers die on the metal, feeding the iron-mites that eat the machines. It is a churning, biological engine that runs parallel to the industrial one, consuming the heat and the decay, turning the "Fresh Cut" into a living, breathing tomb.
