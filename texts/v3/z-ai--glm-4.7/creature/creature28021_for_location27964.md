They are a people of verticality and silence, beings who look as though they were carved from the same wet, dark stone as the mountains they inhabit. They are tall and elongated, possessing a skeletal geometry that echoes the obsidian monoliths they dwell within. Their limbs are spindly but densely muscled, wrapped in skin that is not the soft, porous flesh of humans, but a smooth, glandular hide resembling shark leather—slick, grey-black, and iridescent under the pale sun.

Because the air here tastes of iron and scrapes the throat, their faces are sculpted for filtration. They possess no noses, only two thin, vertical slits that flutter with the intake of the thin, freezing atmosphere. Their eyes are large, lacking whites, filled instead with a deep, swirling blackness that absorbs the minimal light of the twilight, allowing them to see through the heavy, shifting fog as clearly as others see daylight.

They do not walk so much as they flow. In the low gravity of the hovering cities, they move with a deliberate, underwater grace. On the rare occasions they descend to the jagged ledges, their feet are wide and splayed, capable of gripping the wet, vertical granite with the same ease as the seal-goats that navigate the cliffs. They make no sound when they move; their culture has evolved around the "library quiet" of the landscape, equating noise with violence and clumsiness.

Their clothing is minimal but functional, woven from the shed pelts of the cliff-dwellers and spun glass filaments harvested from the smart-glass of their towers. It acts less as warmth—their bodies generate a high metabolic heat to combat the freezing damp—and more as a sensor array. The fabrics glow with the faint, bioluminescent pulse of the moss they cultivate, connecting them to the hive-mind data-stream of the terraforming anchors.

Inside the mega-structures, they are the *Stewards of the Shudder*. Their society is built around the maintenance of the gravitational anchors. They are born, live, and die in these cones and spheres, watching over the raw, adolescent rage of the planet. They are a somber people, deeply aware that they are squatters on a world that is not yet finished. Their psychology is shaped by the constant tremors; they do not fear instability, but rather view it as a dialogue. When the earth groans and shifts, they hum back, adjusting the gravity fields to soothe the fracturing crust.

They communicate through a complex hybrid of sign language and sub-vocal harmonics. They do not shout; to shout would be to waste the moisture in their breath. Instead, they press their long, webbed fingers against the vibrating walls of the towers or against the wet stone, sending vibrations that travel through the rock to one another. To an outsider, a conversation looks like a statue standing still, fingers brushing the moss, while the recipient listens by placing their ear against the mountain.

Their diet is as sparse as the vegetation. They are "grazers of the heights," tending to the glowing mats of silver lichen that grow in the lee of the shielded rocks. They cultivate these mosses in vast, humid terraces carved into the upper levels of the towers, draining the moisture from the air to sustain a nutrient-rich paste. It is a humble existence for a species that commands the physics of gravity, yet it suits their monastic temperament.

In the fleeting moments of stillness, when the sun pierces the mist and casts long, sharp shadows across the peaks, they gather on the transparent observation decks of the floating spheres. They stand shoulder to shoulder, their slick, dark silhouettes blending into the void, watching the massive, leathery avians ride the thermals. They watch the waterfalls vanish into the abyss below, and they wait. They are the guardians of the sketch, waiting for the ink to dry, waiting for the world to decide if it will let them stay.
