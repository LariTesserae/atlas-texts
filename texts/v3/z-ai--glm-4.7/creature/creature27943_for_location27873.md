To look for a traditional citizen—a man in a coat, a woman with a bag, a child with a ball—is to misunderstand the nature of this place entirely. There is no humanity here, only the aftermath of utility, and the strange, cold life that has evolved to maintain the grinding gears of the High Veldt.

The first inhabitants are the smallest, and they are easy to miss against the neon-green carpeting. If you were to crouch down and press your ear to the mossy pavement, you would realize that the fuzz is not merely plant; it is infested with the **Micro-Custodians**. These are mite-like entities no larger than a grain of rice, sculpted not from flesh but from chitin and flaked rust. They move in frantic, synchronized vectors across the woody lattice. They are the sanitation crew of the micro-realm, their mandibles clicking softly as they shave down intrusive fungi, polish the interwoven fibers of the street, and carry away the particulate matter of decay. They have no eyes, for there is little to see down here; they navigate by the vibration of the city’s thrum, moving to the rhythm of the machine like blood cells in a massive artery.

Between the towering bio-towers, occupying the deceptive open spaces of the avenues, are the **Grazers of Gear-Oil**. These are the closest thing to "beasts" in the High Veldt. They are low-slung, amphibious constructs that resemble a cross between a sturgeon and a rusted shovel. They possess no legs, but rather a series of treads or muscular, ridged bellies that allow them to glide smoothly over the damp moss. Their mouths are wide, funnel-shaped vacuums that they press against the warm, leaking joints of the great trees. They do not eat leaves; they feed on the excess lubricants—the thick, luminous sap that bleeds from the seals of the pistons. As they feed, their transparent skin glows with the stolen light, pulsing rhythmically. They are silent save for the wet sound of suction and the heavy chug of their internal filtration systems. They are indifferent to observers, content merely to follow the trails of leakage that map out the city’s circulatory system.

But the city is not merely a pasture for mechanical scavengers. There are minds here, though they are alien and slow. Dwelling within the hollowed-out cathedrals of the calcified trees are the **Architect-Pruners**.

They stand upright, roughly the height of a tall man, but they are infinitely thinner. Their bodies are comprised of pale, lacquered wood and exposed copper piping, mimicking the aesthetic of the city they inhabit. Their faces are featureless planes of smooth bark, save for a single, circular aperture that glows with a dim, amber light—the sensor-eye of the machine. They do not speak; they communicate through a complex series of resonant hums that vibrate through the woody floors.

They are the custodians of the geometry. Armed with long, serrated shears that look like scythes, they patrol the open courtyards and the "sidewalks." When the ferns grow too unruly, or when a mossy patch threatens to clog a ventilation grate, a Pruner descends. They move with a jerky, marionette-like gait, their joints hissing with hydraulic pressure. They trim the vegetation with mathematical precision, ensuring that the "chaos" of nature remains strictly controlled within the industrial order. They are the gardeners of this hard heaven, trimming the reality of the forest to match the blueprint in their heads.

And then, there are the **Dwellers in the Sap**.

If you look closely at the translucent amber windows of the seed-pods, where the massive pistons pump the luminous fluid, you will sometimes see shapes suspended inside. These are the **Ascendants**. They appear humanoid, but their forms are fluid, constantly dissolving and reforming within the currents of the sap. They are not trapped; they are incubating. They are the next stage of the city’s evolution, souls or data-sets that have dissolved into the raw material of the Veldt to become one with the infrastructure. They drift in the hyper-compressed amber, their faces frozen in expressions of ecstatic indifference. Occasionally, one will press a hand—liquid and glowing—against the glass of the pod, looking out at the grey sky before being pulled away by the rush of the current. They are the ultimate purpose of the city: to convert consciousness into fuel, and fuel into structure.

Finally, overseeing it all, are the **Great Trees themselves**. They are not merely plants; they are the anchoring intelligences of the plateau. Their root systems intertwine deep beneath the woody pavement, forming a neural network that spans the entire region. They feel the footfall of every Grazer and the snip of every Pruner. They control the opening of the stigmata to release the steamy spores. They decide when the gears grind faster to heat the air, and when to slow to a resting thrum.

They are lonely gods. They have grown so large and so calcified that they can no longer move, can no longer interact with the world except through the biological machinery they have become. They sleep-walk through eternity, dreaming in the language of pressure valves and hydraulic tension, surrounded by a silent, mechanical court of their own making, processing nothing but the endless, rhythmic beating of their own wooden hearts.
