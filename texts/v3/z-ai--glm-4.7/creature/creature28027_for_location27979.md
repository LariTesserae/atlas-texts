The inhabitants of the Gear-Gap are known collectively as the **Clock-Mites**, though to call them "mites" is to diminish the terrifying complexity of their existence. They are not pests in the traditional sense; they are the children of the eternal scream, biological entities forged in the heart of a kinetic paradox.

**The Individual: A Calculus of Steel and Chitin**
Up close, a single Clock-Mite resembles a grotesque hybrid of a horseshoe crab and a discarded precision tool. Its carapace is not soft organic matter, but a plate of metallic silicon, jagged and faceted, reflecting the bruised purple light of the enclosure in fractured, oily rainbows. They possess no eyes, for there is nothing to see in the gloom; instead, their heads are dominated by dense, sensory hairs that twitch in the air-pressure currents, and a pair of vibrating antennae that constantly sample the frequency of the room.

Their legs are their most distinct feature. There are six of them, segmented not by muscle and bone, but by rubbery, hydraulic pistons. These limbs are dampened with a viscous, black ooze that allows them to adhere to the vibrating gears without shaking apart. They do not walk so much as they *clamp*—locking their joints in rigid synchronization with the metal they stand upon, becoming indistinguishable from the machine itself until the moment they choose to move.

**The Swarm: A Living Quilt**
If you were to shine a light (though no light exists here that can penetrate the dark) on the floor of the Gear-Gap, you would not see the iron. You would see a shifting, tessellated mosaic of grey and blue. The density of the mites is absolute. They cling to one another as often as they do to the gears, stacking in layers three or four deep.

The outer layer of the swarm—those exposed to the freezing air—are sluggish, their metabolic rates slowed to a crawl by the near-zero temperature. But beneath them, pressed against the warmth of the residual machinery, the inner swarm is a frenzy of activity. They trade places in a slow, hypnotic conveyor-belt motion; the frozen outer mites crawl inward to thaw, while the heated inner mites crawl outward to freeze, a cycle that has persisted for millennia without conflict.

**The Behavior: Eating the Silence**
The Clock-Mites do not eat. They do not graze on algae or hunt prey. They feed on the **Static Scream**.

Along the underside of their bellies, where a soft creature would have flesh, the mites possess a series of oscillating plates—tuning forks made of bone and iron. When they press themselves tight against the shaking gears, these plates resonate violently, harvesting the kinetic energy of the machine’s endless vibration.

The act of feeding is visible and auditory. As they draw energy from the roar, their silicon shells begin to glow with a brilliant, electric blue light. This light pulses in time with the hum of the room. When a harmonic peak hits—a specific, violent thrum in the drone—millions of mites simultaneously vibrate their mandibles against the metal.

This creates the only "music" the Gear-Gap knows: a chittering, percussive rasp that layers over the deep bass of the engine. It is the sound of ten trillion mouths chewing on sound itself.

**The Life Cycle: The Ice-Born**
Reproduction in the Gear-Gap is tied to the vertical stalagmites of ice that grow from the ceiling. The "sweat" of the environment condenses and drips, freezing into hanging glass spikes. The Clock-Mites climb the hanging black cables—the "kelp" of this world—to reach these frozen formations.

They lay their eggs inside the clear ice. The embryo, encased in a frozen prison, gestates to the rhythm of the vibration. The ice acts as an incubator, amplifying the machine's thrum into the developing egg. When the mite is fully formed, its internal vibration matches the frequency of the ice perfectly, causing the frozen water to shatter. The newborn drops from the ceiling, shattering like a glass bead on the gears below, where it is immediately scooped up by the writhing carpet of the swarm below and integrated into the lattice.

**The Sentience of the Gap**
There is a collective intelligence here, though it is alien and cold. They are not a hive mind with a queen, but a consensus engine. If a gear were to somehow slip—impossible though that seems—the swarm would react instantly. They would throw their bodies into the gap, interlocking their steel shells to become a living shim, a biological patch holding the machine together. They are the immune system of the Gear-Gap, dedicated to the preservation of the scream that feeds them. They love the cage they are in, for the cage is their sustenance.

They live in the center of a frozen, boiling paradox, hanging in the dark, clinging to the iron, glowing blue, and eating the roar.
