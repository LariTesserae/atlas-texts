The inhabitants of the lung are not distinct species in the way a surface ecosystem might claim predators or prey; they are components of a single, frantic metabolic engine. They are the custodians of a transition, living things that occupy the brief, violent microseconds of a world that is still calculating its own shape.

**The Shard-Stalkers**

High above the root-mat, clinging to the underside of the pendulous vines, live the only truly mobile entities in this sphere. They are the Shard-Stalkers—spindly, six-legged anthropods that resemble nothing so much as plucked nerves draped over a framework of wet calcium.

They are painfully neotenic, creatures born premature into a hyper-oxygenated atmosphere. Their exoskeletons are not the hard armor of crustaceans, but soft, translucent shells of chitin that feel like wet grape skin. Through this skin, you can see the frantic pumping of their circulatory systems, a rapid, triphasic rhythm that matches the hissing of the walls. They are pale, the color of cream, bioluminescing only in their joints where the stress of their grip creates a faint, nervous blue light.

The Shard-Stalkers are the possessors of the "primitive technology" observed from below. They are the hoarders of the Old World. Because the green biomass of the walls digests stone with terrifying efficiency, actual rock is a vanishing resource. The Shard-Stalkers prize it. They fight fatal, silent duels over shards of grey limestone flaking off the serrated ceiling.

These stones are their tools and their prosthetics. You will see them with a jagged sliver of granite lashed to one forelimb using dried strands of vine-matter. They use these knives not to hunt, but to harvest. They climb the razor-wire vines to reach the nodules of dripping nectar, slicing the thick skin to release the viscous fluid. They drink it greedily, their swollen abdomens glowing brighter with the intake of energy, before carrying the excess back to the nests they weave in the "folds" of the cavern—the glitches in the geometry where the air is dimmer and the physics less aggressive.

Their movement is frantic. Nothing in this place rests. A Shard-Stalker that stops moving for too long begins to fuse with its environment. The humidity is so high, and the spore count so thick, that their legs adhere to the vines within minutes. They are constantly twitching, scraping, and shifting, a collective of nervous energy echoing the bright pressure of the room. They sleep in shifts, never all at once, terrified that if they close their eyes, the room will grow over them.

**The Weaver-Floor**

Below them, the floor is not merely dirt, but a distributed intelligence. This is the Weaver-Floor, the mycelial mind that acts as the immune system of the lung. It is a sprawling, sentient carpet of root-hairs and fungal threads that possesses a singular, overwhelming directive: *integration.*

The Weaver-Floor is the architect of the "cages" and the "lattices" seen near the walls. It does not build these structures for shelter, but for filtration. It weaves the falling water, the drifting spores, and the light itself into barriers. It is testing the physics of the cavern, probing the edges of the "glitch" where reality thins into static.

When a Shard-Stalker falls—a frequent occurrence given the razor-vine they traverse—the Weaver-Floor is waiting. It does not eat the creature in the traditional sense. Consumption is wasteful; integration is efficient.

The moment a Stalker hits the velvet, the floor erupts. A hundred thousand white filaments uncoil like spring-loaded snakes. They pierce the soft joints and the translucent skin. They do not digest the organs; they bypass the stomach and splice directly into the nervous system. The fallen Stalker is not killed; it is hijacked. Its legs twitch in a new rhythm, dictated by the root-network. It becomes a node in the floor’s grid, a living sensor extending the reach of the Weaver-Floor’s perception.

These "stitched" ones can be seen half-submerged in the root-mat, their pale heads exposed to the glowing air, their multifaceted eyes swiveling independently, watching the ceiling. They have become the floor’s eyes, relaying information about the cycle of the light and the growth of the walls. In this way, the floor learns. It is a colonial organism that absorbs the individuality of the climbers to understand the height they once inhabited.

**The Glitch-Dwellers**

There is a third category of life here, glimpsed only in the peripheral vision, lurking in the places where the cavern's geometry fails. These are the Glitch-Dwellers—entities that may not be biological at all.

Because the reality here is "thin" and the corners "pixelate," theoretical gaps exist in the mesh of the world. In these folds, where the air is thick and the light dims into a green static, live things that have adapted to the unfinished nature of the simulation.

They are amorphous, shifting masses that have no fixed shape. They resemble the interference patterns on a damaged screen, a tangle of visual noise given weight and substance. They drift through the folds, feeding on the raw data of the cavern's creation—the excess light and the sound of the growing walls.

The Shard-Stalkers fear them. To a Stalker, a Glitch-Dweller is a predator that doesn't bite, but *erases*. When a fold opens up and a mass of static seeps into the biological realm, the Stalkers scatter, chattering in frequencies that hurt the human ear. If a Glitch-Dweller touches a vine, the vine does not break; it simply ceases to have existed, replaced by a flat, gray texture that slowly heals over.

These beings are the scavengers of the "unfinished" logic. They are the consequences of a world that occurred too fast, the errors in the code made flesh. They live in the seams, waiting for the cavern to stutter, so they can feed on the momentary non-existence of things.

**The Symphony of Survival**

Life in the lung is a terrifying high-wire act. The Shard-Stalkers cling to the ceiling, desperate to avoid becoming part of the floor. The Weaver-Floor below yearns upward, desperate to stitch the sky down into its matted embrace. And between them, in the blinding, humming air, the Glitch-Dwellers drift, waiting for the reality of either to fail.

It is an ecosystem of weaponized urgency. Every creature here is running, fighting, or growing at maximum velocity, fully aware that in a place this young and this hungry, to stop is to be deleted.
