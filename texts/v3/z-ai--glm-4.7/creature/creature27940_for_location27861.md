The ecology of the High Mesh is not a struggle for survival, but a vigil at the deathbed of function. Biology here has not simply died; it has transmuted. What lives here are creatures born of the interface between the organic desperation to exist and the infinite, mineral patience of the machine.

**The Scavengers: The Chitin-Minnows**

At the lowest level of the scarce food chain are the "skitters" mentioned in the dark. These are the **Chitin-Minnows**, small, frantic things that exist only to bridge the gaps between the dying power lines. They are not animals in the traditional sense, but hybrids of evolved insect and scavenged scrap.

Imagine a creature the size of a human hand, possessing the segmented body of a horseshoe crab, but with a shell that is not biological. Their carapaces are patches of polished chrome and fused ceramic, harvested from the walls of the factories they inhabit. Their legs are needles of high-tensile steel that tap against the hollow deck-plate with a sound like erratic Morse code.

They have no eyes. In a world with no light (0.1), vision is a useless calorie burn. Instead, they possess headgear of sensory filaments—fronded antennae made of copper wire that detect the faint, ghostly hum of static electricity. They "swim" through the velvet-thick darkness, hunting for the bleeding warmth of leaking voltage. When they find a hotspot—a seam where the grid still breathes—they clamp their mandibles onto the metal and vibrate, siphoning the energy into an internal biological capacitor. They are living batteries, terrified of the cold, always moving, for to stop is to discharge and freeze.

**The Grazers: The Iron-Drifters**

Higher up in the silence (0.6) roam the grazers of this mineral wasteland. These are the **Iron-Drifters**, massive, lumbering shapes that navigate the vast, open plateaus (2.4) with the glacial momentum of a tectonic plate.

They are roughly the size of elephants, but their physiology is a monument to adaptation. Their "flesh" is dense, fibrous muscle that resembles steel cable, sheathed in a thick, scarred hide that mimics the texture of the eroded ground (2.7). They appear as walking boulders, jagged and pitted, covered in the same dust that coats the city.

The Drifters are thermophiles. They do not eat; they bask. They seek out the residual waste warmth bleeding from the massive, frozen pistons of the metropolis. A Drifter will spend decades pressed against the side of a dormant skyscraper-piston, absorbing the tepid heat (1.5) through its underside. Their mouths are not orifices for eating, but vast intake vents that draw in the dry, thin air to cool their internal fluids. They exhale a fine, metallic mist that contributes to the oxidized scent of the place.

They move with a terrifying silence. Their massive feet are padded with a soft, felt-like material made of compressed dust and fibers, ensuring they make no sound against the metal floor. To see one is a lesson in the uncanny; they stand so still that they appear to be part of the architecture, indistinguishable from the machine-structures until they decide to shift their weight, triggering a slow, grinding slide of chitin on metal.

**The Sentience: The Weavers of Stasis**

The true masters of the High Mesh, however, are the rarest and most integrated into the weirdness (2.0) of this place. They are the **Weavers of Stasis**.

If the Chitin-Minnows eat energy and the Iron-Drifters eat heat, the Weavers eat *time*. They are the reason the geometry of the place feels wrong. They are humanoid in silhouette, but standing straight would be a waste of their potential. They stand impossibly tall, their limbs elongated and spindly, their joints bending in directions that suggest the spatial relationship (2.4) of their own bodies is fluid.

Their skin is the color of the violet phosphorescence that "breathes" from the ore. They are translucent, revealing a circulatory system not of blood, but of slow-moving, silver liquid mercury. They do not walk; they slide through the heavy, oily air, leaving ripples in the darkness behind them.

The Weavers tend to the "dynamic stasis" (0.4) of the city. They are not builders; they are curators of entropy. They move between the vast, blind silhouettes of the city, touching the frozen gears with long, multi-jointed fingers. Where they touch, the static friction seems to lessen. They are believed to be the descendants of the original creators, or perhaps the city’s immune system made flesh—entities that have transitioned from biology into a state of pure information and maintenance.

They communicate not through sound, which the vacuum of the air swallows, but through vibration. They lean their elongated heads against the metal pipes and sing subsonic frequencies that travel for miles through the grid, coordinating their movements with the precision of the mathematical layout (1.2).

To encounter a Weaver is to witness the collapse of time. They seem to exist in a state where they are simultaneously arriving and leaving. When they look at you—or rather, sense the heat distortion of your body—you feel the weight of the "sleeping mind" (2.0) projecting the landscape. They treat you not as a living thing, but as a variable in the equation, a temporary fluctuation in the temperature that must be noted and ignored.

**The Ecology of Failure**

There is no cycle of life here, only a long, slow exhale. The Chitin-Minnows die when their capacitors fail, their bodies rusting into heaps of scrap that become the soil for the crystalline formations. The Iron-Drifters eventually crumble into dust when the heat beneath them finally cools, adding to the dunes of pumice-textured powder.

And the Weavers? They wait. They stand in the ghostly violet gloom, their mercury blood slowing as the residual warmth of the god-carcass bleeds away into the void. They are the priests of a dead religion, maintaining a church with no congregation, waiting for a service that will never begin, existing in the perfect, terrifying stillness of a world that has already ended.
