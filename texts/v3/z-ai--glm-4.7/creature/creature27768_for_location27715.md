They are a people forged in the kiln of gravity, shaped by the necessity of ascent. To call them human is a courtesy of classification; they are human-shaped, certainly, but biology here has bowed to the brutal tyranny of the environment.

**Physiology: The Heat-Walkers**
They are tall and frighteningly thin, stretched by the generations spent in a high-gravity, low-nutrient crucible. There is no excess fat on their frames; every calorie is burned in the war against the heat. Their skin is the color of cooled ash—pale, grey, and tight against the bone, often crisscrossed with the shiny, keloid scars of the metallic "leaves" they brush past.

Their limbs are elongated, granting them a simian reach that is essential for life in the canopy. Their hands and feet are oversized, the fingertips broad and padded with tough, keratinous calluses capable of gripping the slick, polished surfaces of the iron trees. They do not so much walk as they swing and brachiate, moving with a fluid, swinging grace that makes the rope bridges look like solid ground.

Because of the infernal dryness, they do not sweat. Instead, they pant, like dogs, their ribcages expanding and contracting rapidly to vent heat. Their noses are lined with dense nests of coarse hair and specialized mucous membranes that filter the iron dust from the air, turning their snot into a thick, black sludge that they constantly expel.

**Sensory Adaptation**
The silence of the animal kingdom does not extend to them, but they do not speak as you might expect. With the constant *Thrum* vibrating through the obsidian ceiling and the screaming of the thermal winds, shouting is useless.

They have developed a language of vibration and gesture. Their vocal cords are atrophied, capable only of low, gravelly hums. Instead, they communicate through a complex sign language performed with exaggerated, sweeping arm movements, and by tapping on the hollow bones of their dwellings or the trunks of the iron trees, sending signals through the wood that travel further than sound ever could.

Their eyes are massive, dark pools adapted to the gloom. They possess a high density of rods in their retinas, allowing them to see by the dull, infra-red pulse of the crystalline mosses. They look out at the world through permanent squints, their eyelids thick and leathery to protect against the abrasive, static-charged air.

**Appearance and Adornment**
They dress in layers of flayed, cured bark and the preserved skins of the giant seed-pods. Their clothing is loose and billowing, designed to trap pockets of cooler air as they move. Metals are precious here not for ornament, but for utility; they wear jewelry of flint and bone, and their tools—axes, picks, and levers—are worn on belts made of braided vine.

One of their most distinct features is their "heat-mask." A face covering made from woven fiber and lined with porous, slag-stone filters, it covers the nose and mouth, acting as a rudimentary condenser. It allows them to reclaim the slightest bit of moisture from their own breath, a critical survival mechanism in a place where water is a myth.

**Diet and Consumption**
They are lithotrophs by proxy. They do not farm; they mine the forest. Their staple food is the "sap" of the iron trees—a thick, viscous hydrocarbon that smells of sulfur and tar. It is toxic to an outsider, causing immediate sickness, but their stomachs are lined with a robust, oily mucosa that neutralizes the harsh acids. They drink this sap warm, drawing calories from the chemical energy stored in the minerals.

To harvest it, they tap the trees using long, hollow drills made of bone, inserting spouts into the trunk and filling the gourds they carry at their belts. It is a messy existence; their chins and chests are often stained dark with the oily residue, giving them a perpetually slick, predatory sheen.

**Culture: The Eternal Climb**
Their psychology is defined by impermanence. They know that the ground beneath them is dying, that the heat is rising, and that the city must always move up. They are a culture of dismantlers and rebuilders.

Possession is a foreign concept to them. They own only what they can carry on their backs, as heavy anchors are a liability. When a platform rots or a rope burns through, it is cut loose without ceremony, falling into the glowing red vents below—a sacrifice to the earth to buy them another day.

They view the *Thrum* not as a noise, but as the heartbeat of a god they are fleeing. They are terrified of stillness; to stop moving in the hot air is to die. Thus, their society is in constant motion, a restless, swinging tide of humanity eternally climbing the ribs of the world, hoping that if they climb high enough, they might finally reach a place where the air tastes of something other than iron.
