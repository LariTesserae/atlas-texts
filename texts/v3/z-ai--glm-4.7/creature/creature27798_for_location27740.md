The floor is not solid; it is a matted, shifting carpet of bodies, a dense mosaic of carapace and membrane that heaves with the collective rhythm of a thousand silent heartbeats. Here, in the incubator of unfinished light, life has evolved not to survive the dark, but to navigate the blinding, sterile exposure of a world without secrets.

**The Geode-Grazers (The Lithophages)**
The foundation of this ecosystem is the swarm of transparent, trilobite-like creatures that cover the jagged floor like a living tide. They are the *Lithophages*, the stone-eaters. Their bodies are composed of a silica-based gelatin that is nearly invisible against the white stone, save for the dark, rhythmic pulsing of their digestive tracts—a vertical tunnel of organs running the length of their bodies.

Because the rock here is "emerging," sharp and fresh, the Lithophages have evolved a unique method of movement. They do not crawl over the knives edges; they dissolve them. Their undersides are coated in a caustic secretion that hisses silently against the raw crystal, turning the rigid stone into a slurry that they intake through ventral slits. As they move, they leave behind smooth, glassy tracks on the otherwise razor-sharp floor, polishing the madness into manageable paths. They are the sanitation crew of the womb, eating the room that births them.

**The Chrome-Skitterers (The Refractors)**
 darting among the slow-moving grazers are the mirrored beetles. They are panic in physical form—restless, angular things with legs of spun silver. Their carapaces are perfectly polished convexities, and this is their defense and their sensory organ. In a room with no shadows, the Refractors survive by becoming eyes.

They do not see in the traditional sense; they feel the pressure of the light. Their shells reflect the impossible white brilliance, focusing it into blinding pinpoints that can sear the retinas of the soft-bodied grazers. They are opportunistic scavengers. When a Lithophage dies or is injured, its clear body leaks opaque fluids. The Refractors converge instantly, not to eat the flesh, but to drink the light trapped within the pooling fluids, vibrating their wing-cases in a frequency that creates a localized silence—a vacuum of sound that sucks the prey dry.

**The Weeping Architects (The Mollusks)**
On the crystal pillars, the gelatinous slugs inch upward. They are the slowest minds in the room, possessing a patience that contradicts the frantic energy below. They are distinct not for what they eat, but for what they produce. They feed on the "static dampness" of the air, inhaling the heavy, ozone-rich moisture.

Their waste product is the iridescent mucus that trails behind them, shimmering like spilled oil. This mucus does not evaporate entirely; a fraction of it hardens into a filament—gossamer-thin wire of pure color. These wires drift to the floor, tangling around the legs of the beetles and the shells of the grazers. The room is slowly filling with this delicate, colored cobweb, a silent record of the slugs’ ascent, turning the raw architectural space into a decorated nursery.

**The Glitch-Hunters (The Aspect Spiders)**
The apex predator of this light-soaked chapel is the glass spider. It is a terrifying masterpiece of evolution, perfectly adapted to the room’s unstable reality. Its legs are long, slender needles of fused obsidian; its body is a faceted diamond that catches the sourceless light and fractures it into a dozen internal rainbows.

It does not hunt by speed, but by *editing*. The Aspect Spider positions itself over a "glitch"—a flickering seam in the reality of the stone. It waits, vibrating its web (which is spun from the slugs' discarded threads) until the frequency of the room shifts. When the reality hiccups and the stone "renders" differently, the spider strikes. It seems to phase-shift, lunging not through space but through a crack in the texture of the world.

It grabs its prey—usually a distracted Refractor—and pulls it into the "pause" between seconds. To an observer, the kill looks like a film jump-cut: the prey is there, then the spider is there, and then the prey is simply gone, wrapped in a cocoon of frozen time, hidden in a micro-pocket of non-existence where the spider stores its food to keep it fresh in a room that has no time.

**The Hive-Self**
What unites them is the silence. It is not merely an absence of noise; it is a biological imperative. Sound here is seen as a waste of energy, a leak in a closed system. Every violent interaction, every bite of the spider, every crunch of the beetle's legs, is muted by a physiological adaptation that dampens vibration. The room is a sealed jar of hyper-focused energy. They are living in the pause of a god, and they dare not make a sound, for fear that the god might notice them and close the book, erasing the chapel, the light, and the teeming, silent carpet of life entirely.
