In the blinding, pitiless white of the Stamped Ranges, where the light itself seems to assault the eye, life does not cower; it adapts with a terrifying efficiency. The creatures here are not biological in the way a naturalist would understand; they are amalgams of scavenged industry and desperate evolution, shaped as much by the riveted steel as by the need to survive. They are the children of the rust, the oil, and the silence.

**The Bottom Feeders: The Siphon-Swarms**
The most abundant life here exists in the interstices, a living tide of bio-mechanical vermin that fills the cracks between the galvanized steel plates. These are the **Siphon-Swarms**. Individually, they are no larger than a human hand, possessing flattened, segmented bodies comprised of a chitin that mimics the oxidized purple of the iron around them. They have no eyes, for the light here is an offense that burns the retinas; instead, their heads are dominated by intricate, feathery antennae that detect the subtle vibrations of the metal and the chemical signature of the weeping water.

They move with a fluid, insectile grace, their legs tipped with hooked ferrite magnets that allow them to cling to the sheer vertical drops of the venting shafts. They do not eat organic matter; rather, they consume the oxidization itself, scraping the bruised purple rust from the metal with rasp-like mouthparts. They are the clean-up crew of this infinite factory, endlessly polishing the gears that never turn. When they swarm, they do so in silence, a rustling wave of legs and shells that pours over the edges of the catwalks like black oil, dislodging the smaller patches of thermosynthetic moss as they pass.

**The Grazers: The Thermo-Leeches**
Clinging to the massive, corrugated pipes are the **Thermo-Leeches**, the primary grazers of this metallic ecology. They are gastropods without shells, their bodies soft and pale, a translucent gray that allows one to see the pulsing, sluggish heat of the coolant liquids they have ingested. They possess a singular, terrifying adaptation: they do not photosynthesize, but *thermosynthesize*. Their skin is laced with a lattice of capillaries that turn heat directly into energy, glowing with a faint, sickly luminescence when they are near a rupture.

They are drawn to the warmth of the friction points, clustering in writhing masses around the bases of the great, slow-moving pistons. They secrete an acidic mucus that allows them to adhere to even the slickest, oiled surfaces, leaving behind trails of silver slime that harden into brittle filigrees. Their movement is imperceptibly slow, matching the glacial tempo of the machinery they inhabit. To watch a Thermo-Leech crawl is to watch erosion happen in real-time; they seem almost stationary, only shifting inches over the course of an hour, patiently waiting for the next vent to sigh and release its steam.

**The Hunters: The Geometric Weavers**
Higher up in the tangled mess of catwalks and suspended girders dwell the **Geometric Weavers**. These are the apex predators of the vertical maze, arachnid-like monstrosities constructed from sharp angles and serrated limbs. They do not spin silk; they spin tension. Using the high-tension cables and loose wires that litter the environment, they weave complex, invisible nets across the chasms between buildings.

The Weavers are perfectly camouflaged. When at rest, they fold their multiple limbs flat against their bodies, perfectly mimicking a jagged piece of scrap metal or a protruding rivet. They hunt by sensing the disturbances in the air—the "reality breakdown" that causes gravity to flicker. They wait for a Siphon-Swarm or a careless smaller creature to drift into a zone of low gravity, then they strike, extending their limbs with blinding speed to impale their prey on needle-sharp tips. Their bodies are hollow, filled with a lighter-than-air gas that allows them to traverse the vast distances of the open air, drifting like ominous kites in the thin, metallic atmosphere.

**The Anomalies: The Shadow-Strays**
Perhaps the most unsettling inhabitants are those that are not entirely biological. In a place where physics glitches and shadows detach from their objects, these glitches have begun to coalesce into a form of life. Known as **Shadow-Strays**, these entities are two-dimensional predators that slide along the surfaces of the metal, looking like oil slicks that move against the wind.

They have no mass, existing only as a differential in light. They hunt during the brightest parts of the "day," sliding beneath the blinding white radiance. When they find a creature—often a Thermo-Leech basking on a pipe—they wrap around it, cutting off its connection to the light and the heat. They do not bite or tear; they simply subtract. The prey withers, turning gray and cold as the Shadow-Stray siphons the heat energy directly, expanding and darkening as it feeds. If struck, they dissipate into a harmless cloud of oily mist, only to reform seconds later, proving they are not creatures of flesh, but of the broken laws of this place.

**The Giants: The Epoch-Walkers**
Finally, there are the **Epoch-Walkers**. These immense, majestic beasts are so large they are mistaken for part of the architecture. They resemble colossal, millipede-like tanks, their armored backs plated with sheets of rusted steel scavenged from the environment. They walk on the biggest of the gear-work housings, their legs moving in a slow, rhythmic cycle that matches the thrum of the planet itself.

They are ancient, perhaps as old as the Construct, and they move with a slowness that makes them seem like mountains. They feed on the raw electricity that arcs through the super-structures of the city, siphoning it from the high-tension lines with massive, conductive feelers. Their presence is felt rather than seen; the vibration in the soles of one’s shoes is the footfall of an Epoch-Walker miles away. They are silent, solitary, and indestructible, the grand lords of a frozen, industrial wasteland, marching endlessly toward a horizon that never gets closer.
