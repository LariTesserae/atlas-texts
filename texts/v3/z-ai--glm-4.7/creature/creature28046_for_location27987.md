In the absence of humanity, the tenets of this high, wet place are held by organisms that treat the architecture not as a ruin, but as a geology to be colonized. The inhabitants here are defined by their adherence to the stasis; they are creatures that do not scuttle so much as they ooze, creatures that do not flutter so much as they cling.

The primary citizens are the **Gastropods**.

They are the lords of this damp, silences. Drawn by the perpetual sheen of condensation on the sandstone, colonies of snails and slugs have claimed the vertical real estate of the buildings. They are large, their shells the color and texture of the wet mortar—grey, rough, and lichen-crusted. During the long, grey-lit days, they adhere to the underside of window lintels or hang in suspended clusters from the wooden beams, like strange, damp fruit. They move so slowly that their motion is invisible to the casual glance; you could watch for an hour and believe the snail on the wall is a permanent fixture of the masonry, only to return the next day and find it has traveled three feet. They leave trails that are not of slime, but of a silvery, bioluminescent algae that glows faintly in the deep twilight before fading, marking their slow grazing paths across the "skin" of the town.

Beneath the overhangs and in the deep, shadowy corners where the fog pools, live the **Stone-Mimic Beetles**.

These are primitive, heavy-set insects with wing casings that have evolved to match the specific, pitted texture of the local sandstone. They are motionless for hours at a time, indistinguishable from the pebbles scattered along the lanes. When they do move, it is with a jerky, deliberate caution, as if anxious to disturb the silence. They do not buzz or chirp; they communicate by tapping their legs against the rock, a sound so low and dull it is indistinguishable from the settling of the timber. They feed on the moss that grows in the cracks of the pavement, methodically stripping the green fuzz from the stone, their mandibles clicking softly like the quiet tuning of a rusted instrument.

In the stagnant, shallow pools that have worn themselves into the bedrock, the **Water-Striders** hold vigil.

These are not the vibrant, dancing insects of lowland streams, but bloated, pale things. They skate across the surface tension of the still water, their legs leaving dimples that never quite fade. They are blind, or nearly so, having no need for sight in a world of consistent, diffused grey. They navigate by sensing the ripples in the air and the vibration of the water. They lie dormant for long stretches, half-submerged in the cool liquid, looking more like waterlogged debris than living things. They are the scavengers of the microscopic world, drifting beneath the surface to consume the detritus that falls from the weeping walls.

And then there is the **Watchman**.

This is the single bird mentioned, a solitary raptor of indistinct species—perhaps a hawk or a vulture that lost its way centuries ago and simply stayed. It has grown heavy with age, its feathers matted and darkened by the damp mist. It perches on the highest ridge beam of the central square, gripped by talons that have seemingly worn grooves into the wood. It does not preen, it does not call, and it rarely flies. It has become a part of the architecture, a gargoyle made of flesh and blood. It watches the lanes of bedrock with eyes that are glassy and still. It sustains itself on the slow beetles and the unwary snails, snapping them up with a swift, silent movement that is the only sudden violence in this frozen world. After it eats, it returns to its perch, and the square holds its breath again.

There is no community here, no pack dynamic, no herd. The "population" is a loose collection of solitary survivors that brush against one another in the fog. They are gray, they are damp, and they are quiet. They are the biological echo of the stone itself—living, breathing, and enduring, but caught in the same elongated moment of stillness that holds the town. They are not waiting for anything; they simply exist, polishing the rock with their bodies, waiting out the eternity.
