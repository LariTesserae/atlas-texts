In this muffled, hydraulic silence, life does not scuttle; it *percolates*. Because the environment is a continuous, high-density engine room, the fauna has evolved not just to survive the industrial output of the Total Green, but to integrate into it. They are the grease in the gears, the scavengers of the factory floor, living entirely by the rhythm of the lung-towers and the conveyor-runners.

**The Conductor-Runners**

On the surface, moving in synchronization with the conveyor belts of creeping runners, are small, segmented creatures known locally as the Freight-Mimics. They are roughly the size of a human forearm, resembling a cross between a velvet worm and a rusted iron pipe. Their exoskeletons are not chitinous but metallic-sheened, colored a dull, oxidized orange to perfectly match the nutrient packets and berries they hunt.

They possess no legs that would clatter against the ground. Instead, they move via a series of hydraulic contractions along their underbellies, rippling like a loose sock. This allows them to glide effortlessly over the tangled moss, matching the speed and direction of the vegetative conveyor belts. They are nearly invisible to the naked eye until they feed. When they locate a nutrient packet, they extrude a needle-like proboscis that taps into the berry’s skin, injecting a coagulant that turns the plant’s fluids into a slurry they can suck dry. They eat while moving, never stopping, indistinguishable from the debris on the assembly line.

**The Steam-Lurkers**

Higher up, clinging to the ribbed sides of the towering lung-stalks, are the mist-harpies. These are solitary, ambush predators that look like skeletal pterosaurs stretched over a frame of wet leather. Their wings are long and tattered, designed not for powered flight but for gliding on the thermal currents rising from the exhaling chimneys.

They are perfectly adapted to the silence. Their feathers—more like coarse, wet fur—absorb sound rather than rustle. They possess no vocal cords; they communicate by opening and closing specialized heat-sacs along their necks, creating rapid fluctuations in temperature that their companions can see through thermal pits but the air cannot hear.

The Steam-Lurkers hang upside down from the chimney-ribs, their claws hooked into the fibrous cellulose without piercing it, waiting for a warm-blooded creature to pass beneath. They are patient statues of the grey mist. When they drop, they plummet like a stone, silent and heavy. Their impact is dampened by the moss, and their kill is immediate—a snap of the beak that feels like nothing more than a heavy footfall in the damp earth.

**The Siphon-Behemoths**

Beneath the surface, in the root-catacombs and the fungal sub-basements, dwell the true rulers of this plateau: the Siphon-Behemoths. These are massive, eyeless grazers that look like biological bulldozers. They are the primary reason the ground is so spongy; their constant tunneling aerates the soil-mat, preventing the roots from choking on their own density.

A Siphon-Behemoth has no distinct shape; it is a muscular cylinder of flesh, perhaps ten feet in diameter and twenty feet long, surrounded by a halo of writhing root-tentacles. It does not dig with claws; it digs with pressure. It forces its way through the dense mesh of the Total Green by pushing its head forward, which is a hardened, drill-like cone of keratin. Behind it, the vegetation snaps back into place, sealing the tunnel like a self-healing wound.

They are the waste management of the system. They consume the "slag"—the dead, fibrous cellulose that the factories shed—and excrete a rich, heated slurry that feeds the conveyor belts above. They move incredibly slowly, pushing through the dark with a grinding, subsonic thrum that you feel in your molars rather than hear with your ears. They are blind, navigating by tasting the chemical composition of the moss around them. To them, the world is a map of flavors: the bitter rust of old roots, the sweet salt of water-pockets, and the sour tang of a rival Behemoth’s territory.

**The Vane-Spiders**

Floating in the air pockets between the mist and the moss are the Vane-Spiders. They are not true spiders, but aerial plankton catchers. They look like minute, intricate frameworks of glass and thread, drifting on the slightest updraft. They spin no webs; instead, they extrude long, sticky filaments that trail behind them like kite tails.

These filaments are designed to catch the spores and microscopic dust ejected by the lung-towers. The Vane-Spiders are the filtration system of the lower atmosphere, ensuring the air doesn't become clogged with the industrial output of the plants. They are almost entirely transparent, visible only when the light hits them at a sharp angle, turning them into fleeting geometric prisms. When they die, they do not fall; they become too heavy to drift, and they sink slowly into the moss, dissolving into a nutrient gel that is eagerly sucked up by the conveyor-runners.

**The Echo-Stalker**

There is only one creature that seems to defy the dampening physics of this place, though it does so with subtlety. It is a low-slung, feline-like reptile with scales the color of bruised lichen. It is the hunter of the hunters.

The Echo-Stalker has adapted to the silence by becoming a master of subsonics. It can generate a low-frequency hum from a specialized organ in its chest—a hum so low it travels through the ground and the roots rather than the air. It uses this like sonar, feeling the vibrations of the Freight-Mimics rippling along the conveyor belts or the heartbeat of a Steam-Lurker gripping a tower.

When it moves, it glides. Its skeletal structure is loosely jointed, allowing it to flow over obstacles rather than step over them, minimizing friction. It kills by embracing its prey, wrapping its long, muscular limbs around the target and constricting. The victim’s struggle produces no sound, and the predator’s embrace is a tightening vice of cold, indifferent muscle. In this place, death is just another part of the manufacturing process—a quiet culling of the excess, absorbed instantly into the damp, woolly dark.
