This is not a destination for the casual sightseer. The Hollow is a geological pressure cooker, a place where the fundamental comforts of a human body—moisture, coolness, silence, and solid ground—are stripped away. Visiting this sinkhole requires an obsessive level of preparation. If you treat this like a camping trip, you will die of desiccation within an hour, or cut yourself to ribbons trying to navigate the undergrowth.

Here is how an ordinary human must prepare to survive the City of Rope and Bone.

### 1. The Golden Rule: Never Touch the Ground
This is the single most important rule of your survival. The floor of the Hollow is not land; it is a griddle. The ground radiates a "scorching, dry heat that would boil a man alive." Furthermore, the slag crust is tessellated, sharp, and unstable.

*   **Arrival Strategy:** You cannot arrive at the bottom. Your portal or transport must deposit you directly into the mid-to-upper canopy, at least 50 meters above the crater floor.
*   **Thermal Soles:** Your boots must be equipped with military-grade ceramic thermal insulation, capable of withstanding direct contact with superheated metal for short durations. If you slip and fall, or if a bridge breaks, you may have seconds to scramble back up before your boots melt and your feet cook.

### 2. Bodily Preparation: The Mummification Protocol
The air here "hurts the lungs." It is desiccated to a lethal degree. The moment you expose your skin or mucous membranes, the environment will begin to extract water from your body with violent efficiency.

*   **Pre-Hydration Loading:** Forty-eight hours before arrival, you must begin a regimen of hyper-hydration and electrolyte loading. You need to be bloated with water before you even step through.
*   **Humidification Systems:** A simple respirator is not enough. You need a **full-face positive-pressure mask** equipped with a complex humidifier and heat exchanger. The air you breathe must be re-moistened and cooled by your gear before it enters your throat, or your lungs will blister and dry out, essentially suffocating you from the inside.
*   **The Sealed Suit:** You cannot wear exposed cotton or standard synthetics. You need a impermeable, puncture-resistant exosuit—something akin to a hazmat suit but articulated for climbing. Every inch of skin must be sealed. If you sweat, the suit must recycle that moisture or vent it through a cooling condenser; if you sweat onto your skin, the salt will crystallize instantly and chafe you raw.

### 3. Sensory Management: The "Thrum" and the Chimes
The soundscape of the Hollow is a weapon. The low-frequency *Thrum* vibrates through your bones, and the metallic leaves create a chaotic, hurricane-level cacophony. This environment causes sensory overload, vertigo, and nausea.

*   **Infrasound Dampening:** Standard earplugs will not block the *Thrum*. You need headphones equipped with active noise cancellation specifically tuned to low-frequency infrasound (below 20Hz). Without this, the constant resonance will disrupt your inner ear, ruin your balance, and induce migraines that will incapacitate you.
*   **Communication:** The roar of the wind and the chiming metal makes shouting useless. Your suit should have a bone-conduction throat mic system, allowing you to communicate with any companions or translators via radio signal directly into the ear canal.
*   **Visual Calibration:** The light is "dim twilight" mixed with "infra-red pulse." Your human eyes will struggle to pick up details in the gloom. You need multi-spectrum goggles that can amplify the available light and highlight the heat signatures of the vents and the bioluminescence of the flora.

### 4. Armor and Mobility: Navigating the Razor Forest
The flora here is not soft; it is stone and metal. The "leaves" are sheets of metallic foil, "sharp enough to cut skin."

*   **Cut-Resistance:** Your suit must be lined with Kevlar or equivalent ballistic weave. If you brush against a "tree" or reach out to steady yourself on a vine, you will slice deep into your muscle tissue. The suit needs to be a second skin of armor.
*   **Climbing Proficiency:** The city is vertical. There are no stairs, only swaying rope bridges and pulley systems. If you have a fear of heights, do not go. If you are not an expert climber, do not go. You must be comfortable moving on unstable platforms that shift with the thermal winds.
*   **Hook and Harness:** You must wear a harness at all times. The locals use primitive engineering, but you should bring a modern ascender and a high-tensile grappling hook. The "trees" are silicon-based and hard as rock; your hooks need to be diamond-tipped to bite into them without snapping.

### 5. The Psychological Toll: The Ecology of Solitude
This is a silent world in terms of biology. There are no birds, no dogs, no insects. It is a mineral world that feels alien and hostile to biological life.

*   **Carbon Withdrawal:** You will feel like an intruder. The silence of animals is oppressive. Prepare yourself for the uncanny feeling of walking through a forest that is "alive" but in a way that your biology fundamentally rejects. The screeching of growing metal will set your teeth on edge.
*   **Claustrophobia in the Open:** The Hollow is a vast, open cylinder, yet the ceiling of obsidian presses down. It creates a sensation of being buried while being exposed. You may experience "cabin fever" despite the open space. Bring mental anchors—music, recordings of rain, anything that reminds you of a living, wet world.

### 6. Cultural Integration: The Vertical Nomads
You are entering a "primitive engineering" society. Do not flash high-tech plastics or glowing screens unless you want to be treated as a spirit or a threat.

*   **Camouflage your Tech:** Matte finishes only. No bright LEDs. If your suit lights up, you will look like a floating ghost in the infra-red gloom.
*   **Gift Economy:** These people mine sulfur and salts. They value things that are hard to get. High-carbon steel (for tools), high-quality rope (synthetic), or vacuum-sealed water rations would make you a king. Bring trade goods.
*   **Respect the Heat:** The locals are constantly moving. To stand still is to die. If you visit their platforms, do not block the thoroughfares. Do not sit on their ropes without asking. Their architecture is their lifeline; damaging a bridge could doom a family.

### Summary Checklist for the Suit:
1.  **Body:** Full-sealed, cut-resistant exosuit with active liquid cooling.
2.  **Lungs:** Positive pressure respirator with 100% humidity reclamation.
3.  **Ears:** Infrasound-blocking active noise cancelation.
4.  **Eyes:** Multi-spectrum thermal/night-vision goggles.
5.  **Hands:** Kevlar-grip gauntlets (do not touch metal with bare skin; it will sear you).
6.  **Feet:** Ceramic-insulated mountain boots with crampons for gripping slag.

**Final Warning:**
The ground is subsiding. The city is moving. If you stay in one spot for too long, the world may crumble beneath you. Keep moving. Keep climbing. And never, ever forget that the air you are breathing is trying to turn you into dust.
