This is not a place you visit; this is a place you endure. As an ordinary human traveler, your instinct will be to treat this like a jungle or a cave. It is neither. It is a biological autoclave—a sealed terrarium of intense light and suffocating life. The "Below-Surface" does not care about your comfort, and it offers no mercy to those who cannot adapt to its stasis.

Here is how you prepare for the intrusion.

### 1. Visual Protection: The "Intrusive" Light
The most immediate threat is the light. It is not the gentle wash of a sun; it is a physical column of radiation. Because there is no wind and no cloud cover, the ultraviolet index in the center of the amphitheater is likely lethal.

*   **Eyewear is Mandatory:** Do not bring standard sunglasses. You need category 4 glacier goggles or welding-grade filters. The light reflects off the wet obsidian and the glossy green vegetation, creating a blinding, omnidirectional glare. Without heavy filtration, you risk snow blindness (or in this case, "green blindness") within hours.
*   **Skin Coverage:** Even though it is hot, you must be covered. The light is "weighty." Imagine standing under a heat lamp that never turns off. Wear loose-fitting, densely woven UV-protective clothing. A wide-brimmed hat is essential, but make sure it is ventilated; you need airflow without the burn.
*   **The Sleep Problem:** There is no night. The diorama effect means the light never dims. Your circadian rhythm will shatter within the first 24 hours. You must bring a high-quality blindfold or sleep mask, and ideally, a tent that is capable of blocking 100% of external light. You will have to manufacture your own darkness.

### 2. Thermal Regulation: The "Breathless" Heat
The heat rating (2.6/3) is deceptive because it is static. You cannot cool down by finding shade, because the ambient temperature of the air and the rock is the same as the shade.

*   **Hydration Strategy:** The air is "thick enough to chew." Humidity is near saturation, which means your sweat will not evaporate. Your body’s primary cooling mechanism is disabled. You will overheat dangerously fast without external help. You must carry a hydration bladder with a tube, allowing you to drink constantly without stopping.
*   **Cooling Vests:** Consider a phase-change cooling vest. If you can afford the space, it is the only thing that will keep your core temperature from spiking.
*   **Electrolytes:** You will sweat buckets that vanish into the damp air. You need salt tablets and powdered electrolytes. Water alone will kill you here via hyponatremia.
*   **Fabrics:** Forget cotton. Wear moisture-wicking synthetics or merino wool. You need clothing that moves the moisture away from your skin to prevent chafing and fungal infections, which will set in rapidly in this damp heat.

### 3. Footwear and Terrain: The "False" Ground
The ground is a trap. It looks soft, but it is a treacherous mixture of deep, spongy humus and hidden obsidian shards.

*   **Puncture-Proof Soles:** The "fresh wounds" of the planet lie just beneath the green carpet. Sharp, volcanic glass is hidden under inches of moss. Standard hiking soles will be punctured. You need heavy-duty boots with a puncture plate (like those used in wildland firefighting or snake country).
*   **Waterproofing vs. Breathability:** There is no standing water, but everything is slick. You need traction that can grip wet, organic slime. However, waterproof boots (Gore-Tex) will cook your feet in the 2.6 heat. You are better off with non-waterproof, quick-drying synthetic uppers, accepting that your feet will be wet constantly. Change your socks twice daily to prevent trench foot.
*   **The Sinking Sensation:** The ground is "springy." Walking here is like walking on a thick memory foam mattress. This will exhaust your stabilizer muscles and ankles. Train for this beforehand, or bring trekking poles to distribute your weight.

### 4. Respiratory and Sensory Health
The air is a cocktail of oxygen, ozone, green sugar, and spores.

*   **Respirators:** The "taste of green sugar" suggests an incredibly high concentration of volatile organic compounds (VOCs) released by the hyper-dense vegetation. Breathing this air straight for days might induce headaches, nausea, or hallucinations. Bring a half-face respirator with organic vapor cartridges. You won't wear it all the time, but you’ll need it when the heat rises or when you are inside the hollow "buildings" where oxygen is densest.
*   **The Silence:** The absolute lack of ambient noise (no birds, no insects) creates a sensory deprivation-like state. The silence is "heavy." This can induce anxiety and auditory hallucinations. Bring a small sound emitter—a white noise machine or a fan—to sleep with. You need to break the silence just to keep your sanity.

### 5. Interaction with the Flora and Fauna
The ecosystem is locked in "frozen equilibrium."

*   **Do Not Touch the "Buildings":** The organic cathedrals are tempting shelter, but remember: they are alive. The plants are fused. Cutting into them to make a door or clear a space might cause the entire structure to bleed sap or collapse. Treat the vegetation not like bushes, but like solid rock. Do not climb them; the vines are likely slick and may not support your weight in the way terrestrial plants do.
*   **The Fauna:** The animals are rare, sluggish, and "eyeless." They are not predators, but they are likely opportunistic scavengers or disease vectors. Do not handle the pale lizards or bloated beetles. They exist in a sterile, isolated bubble; their bacteria are foreign to your immune system.
*   **No Fire:** The air is saturated with oxygen and plant vapors. Lighting a fire could be catastrophic. The heat and light are already provided; bring self-heating meal rations or high-energy non-perishable foods.

### 6. Psychological Preparation
The "weirdness" of this place is the stillness.

You are entering a world where time has stopped. In a normal forest, leaves rustle. Here, leaves simply exist. The sun does not set. The shadows do not lengthen. It is a "snapshot."

*   **Marking Time:** Without day and night, you will lose track of hours. Wear a watch with an alarm. Set a strict schedule: Work for one hour, rest for twenty minutes. Do not rely on how you feel; the heat makes you lethargic, and the light makes you awake.
*   **The Diorama Effect:** You may feel like an intruder in a museum exhibit. This can lead to a sense of unreality or dissociation. Keep a journal. Write down your physical sensations—the heat, the taste of the air, the feeling of the ground. Grounding yourself in the physical text of the world will keep you from drifting into the unreality of the place.
*   **Fear of the Light:** The light column feels like a spotlight. You will feel exposed. There is nowhere to hide from the sky. You must accept your visibility. You are a speck of dust in a beam of light, observed only by the blind plants.

5.  **Half-Face Respirator** (OV cartridges).
6.  **Heavy-Duty Earplugs** and a **White Noise Device**.
7.  **Sleep Mask** and a **Light-Blackout Tent**.
8.  **Trekking Poles** (to test the depth of the moss).
9.  **No Cutting Tools** (You cannot fight the Green; you must navigate around it).

Go carefully. The Below-Surface is beautiful, but it is a beauty that does not include you. You are a ghost passing through a green cathedral that was built before humanity existed and will remain long after. Do not linger in the column of light, and do not listen too closely to the silence.
