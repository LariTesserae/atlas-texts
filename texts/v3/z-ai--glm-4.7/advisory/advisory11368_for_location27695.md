To visit the Sump is to commit oneself to the belly of a beast that does not know you exist. This is not a dive into the ocean; it is a crawl into the internals of a planetary-sized engine that happens to be submerged. As an ordinary traveler, your fragility is your defining characteristic. Here, water is not a medium; it is a tool, and the machinery treats it as such.

If you wish to survive the Sump, let alone return with your sanity intact, you must prepare for an environment that is actively trying to crush, slice, deafen, and process you.

Here is how you prepare.

### 1. The Shell: Hard-Suit Exoskeleton Technology
Forget standard SCUBA gear, neoprene wetsuits, or even soft-shell atmospheric diving suits. In the Sump, abrasion is a lethal threat. The "young erosion" means every surface is a knife edge. A clumsy brush against a bulkhead won't just scratch you; it will sever a limb or puncture your buoyancy bladder.

You require a **Class-IV Industrial Hard-Suit**. Think "walking tank" rather than "diving suit."
*   **Material:** The outer skin must be layered with ballistic-grade ceramics and woven carbon fiber to resist the razor-sharp rust and geometric edges of the terrain.
*   **Pressure Resistance:** The suit must maintain 1 atmosphere of internal pressure. The description notes the "crushing, silent-heavy lift." You do not want to breathe pressurized gas at these depths for the duration of the visit; the narcosis would kill you before the machinery did.
*   **Thermal Insulation:** The water hovers just above freezing. Your suit needs active heating elements powered by a high-density isotopic core. Passive insulation will eventually succumb to the thermal leaching of the metal.
*   **Magnetic Locking:** You cannot rely on gripping the smooth, slick, rust-moss-covered steel with hands. Your suit should be equipped with electromagnetic clamps in the boots and palms to anchor you against the violent currents generated by the pumps.

### 2. Mobility: The "No-Touch" Doctrine
The most terrifying feature of the Sump is the "hollow, cathedral-like emptiness." You will be suspended in dark water for hours, with the floor thousands of feet below you. Conventional fins are useless here; you cannot swim fast enough to escape the suction of the intake valves.

You need **multi-vector thrusters** integrated into the hard-suit.
*   **Power:** These must be high-torque, able to fight against the "artificial currents" that whip through the canyons.
*   **Redundancy:** If one thruster fails, you become a piece of debris drifting toward the processing gears. Bring backups.
*   **The Rule of Suspension:** Train yourself to never, ever touch the ground unless anchored. The ground is unstable—a shifting pile of jagged tetrahedrons. Your entire operational doctrine must be 3D maneuvering. You are a satellite orbiting a jagged planet, not a hiker walking a trail.

### 3. Sensory Management: Deafness and Disorientation
The Sump is loud. The "industrial roar" and "low-frequency vibrations" will shatter your eardrums and liquefy your inner ear if you are not protected. But you cannot simply block it out; you need to hear the malfunctions that predict a catastrophe.

*   **Auditory Protection:** Your helmet needs a dual-system audio suite.
    *   *Active Noise Cancellation:* To dampen the constant roar to a manageable hum.
    *   *Mechanical Translation:* Software that filters the noise, alerting you only to specific dangerous tonal shifts—like the high-pitched screech of metal shearing or the pre-ignition thump of a piston firing.
*   **Visual Aids:** The light is a perpetual twilight filled with "silt and oil." Standard vision will be useless after twenty feet.
    *   *Lidar and Sonar:* You must navigate by sound and laser mapping, overlaying a wireframe HUD on your visor. The "arc-welders" in the distance will strobe your vision into blindness; your helmet visor must auto-dim instantly to prevent seizure-inducing flashes.
    *   *Tether-line:* In the gloom, with the immense scale playing tricks on your perspective, it is incredibly easy to lose track of "up." A physical, luminescent tether line attached to a stable entry point is your only lifeline if your electronics fail.

### 4. Environmental Awareness: The Machine's Pulse
The Sump is not static. The water level rises and falls mechanically. You are visiting a factory floor.

*   **The Tide Cycle:** You must synchronize your entry with the machine's operational cycle. Do not enter during the "intake valve" phase. The whirlpools and suction forces are inescapable. You must time your exploration for the "lull" between the intake and the outflow surge.
*   **Thermal Imaging:** The machinery generates heat; the water is freezing. Your suit's sensors should be set to highlight thermal gradients. A hot pipe might be a steam vent (danger) or a friction point (sign of imminent breakdown). Cold spots might signify the path of fresh intake water that carries debris.
*   **Rust-Moss Detection:** This orange scum is slick. If you must land, your HUD should highlight Rust-Moss concentrations in red so you know your magnetic clamps will likely slide.

### 5. Biological Protocols: The Rats and the Reptiles
You are a contaminant. The locals are adapted to this hell; you are not.

*   **Predator Deterrence:** The amphibious reptilian predators in the shadows likely hunt by vibration and silhouette. Your hard-shell suit makes you look less like food and more like a piece of the landscape (which is the goal). However, carry **acoustic deterrence devices**. A burst of high-frequency sonar might mimic the sound of a failing pump and scare off the predators, who have likely learned to avoid dangerous machinery.
*   **Chemical Shielding:** The "silver fish" feed on chemical sludge. The water is toxic. Your suit’s filtration system must be military-grade. A leak in your suit isn't just a drowning risk; it's a poisoning risk. Ensure your oxygen scrubbers can handle volatile organic compounds (VOCs) and oil particulates.

### 6. Psychological Conditioning
This is the hardest part to pack for. The Sump induces "cathedral-like emptiness" and existential dread.

*   **The Scale of Insignificance:** You will see pillars "thick enough to house a city." You will swim for hours without touching anything. This triggers a primal fear known as *thalassophobia*, intensified by the industrial alienness. You must meditate on your own insignificance before entering. Panic leads to rapid breathing, which leads to fogging visors and poor judgment.
*   **The Silence of the Steel:** Despite the noise, there is a "silent-heavy lift"—a feeling of immense, crushing weight that is psychological as much as physical. You are inside a ruin that is still being built. Prepare yourself to see things that make no sense—geometry that hurts the eyes, massive structures with no discernible purpose.

### Summary Checklist for the Traveler:
1.  **Suit:** Rigid, ceramic-plated exoskeleton with magnetic soles.
2.  **Power:** Isotope core for 12+ hours of heat and thrust.
3.  **Sensors:** Lidar/Sonar navigation, thermal imaging, vibration detectors.
4.  **Defense:** Acoustic deterrents; no firearms (bullets don't swim well, and sparks + oil = bad).
5.  **Timing:** Synchronize with the mechanical tides.
6.  **Mindset:** Accept that you are a small, soft thing inside a very large, hard thing.

**Final Warning:** When you see the arc-welders lighting up the distance, do not swim toward them. They are not stars; they are the cutting torches of a world that is still under construction. Stay in the dark, keep your head down, and do not become a contaminant in the gears.
