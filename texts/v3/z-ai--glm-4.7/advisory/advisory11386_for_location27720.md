**Travel Advisory: The Suspension / The Glass Flask**

**Destination Designation:** Planet-Sized Hydro-Stasis Cylinder.
**Primary Threats:** Acoustic Trauma, Hypothermia, Abyssal Vertigo, Laceration, Industrial Entanglement, Psychological Dissociation.
**Atmosphere:** Hyper-clear liquid (non-aqueous), high light penetration, crushing low-frequency pressure waves.

Welcome, traveler. You are not going to a beach; you are going inside the eye of a sleeping god. This world is not a habitat; it is a machine for existing.

If you intend to visit the Suspension, you must abandon every instinct you have regarding terrestrial oceans. There are no waves crashing on shores, no salt spray, no kelp forests. This is a place of terrifying clarity and crushing vibration. To survive here, you must be less like a swimmer and more like a deep-sea astronaut with a nervous system of tungsten.

Here is your preparation protocol.

***

### 1. The Physical Envelope: Suit Engineering
Do not enter this fluid in a standard wetsuit or neoprene. The medium is "thinner," distilled, and conducts thermal energy with an efficiency that will freeze your blood in seconds. Furthermore, the clarity of the liquid means UV radiation penetrates thousands of miles; you will be sunburned from every angle simultaneously if you are exposed.

You require a **Hard-Shell Pressure Suit** or an extremely advanced **Exosuit** with the following specific modifications:

*   **Active Thermal Regulation:** The ambient temperature is at the phase boundary of freezing. Your suit must not only insulate but actively generate heat. Redundant heating coils are mandatory.
*   **Laceration Plating:** The "Young Erosion" means the geology is knife-sharp. Obsidian and basalt shards protrude like broken teeth. If you brush against a cliff wall, you do not get scratched; you get filleted. Your outer shell must be abrasion-resistant and Kevlar-reinforced.
*   **Invisibility Mode / Matte Finish:** The visual silence is absolute, but you will be the anomaly. Use a matte grey or black finish. Any reflective surface will act as a beacon in the "infinite lens," potentially disorienting local fauna or attracting attention you do not want.
*   **Liquid Breathing Apparatus (Optional but Recommended):** Because the density is near-zero and the pressure changes are drastic as you move between the spires, standard compressed gas SCUBA is risky. Consider a perfluorocarbon liquid breathing system (oxygenated liquid carried in the lungs) to equalize pressure instantly and prevent nitrogen narcosis. It allows you to become one with the medium, rather than fighting a bubble of air inside a suit.

### 2. Acoustic Fortification: The Roar
This is the most critical preparation. The world "roars" with a low-frequency thrum. It is not loud in the way a shout is loud; it is loud in the way a subway train is loud when you are standing on the tracks. It vibrates your bones.

*   **Infrasound Damping:** Standard earplugs are useless. The sound travels through the liquid and through your skeleton. Your helmet must possess an **Active Noise Control system** capable of generating anti-frequencies to cancel the thrum.
*   **Vibration Isolation Mounts:** Your gloves, your boots, and your seating apparatus must be rubber-mounted or gel-padded. If your body is constantly conducting the vibration of the planetary machinery, you will suffer fatigue, nausea, and long-term organ damage within hours.
*   **Communication Protocols:** Radio is useless due to the interference of the planetary hum. You must rely on **hard-line laser comms** or **tactile signaling**. If you are traveling with a partner, agree on a system of tug-cables attached to your suits. If the visual field is too empty, you may lose sight of them, and the roar will drown out any shout.

### 3. Spatial Psychology: Combatting the Lens
The visual environment is a trap. Looking out feels like looking into an infinite lens. The light is everywhere, "indifferent white daylight." There is no surface to define the "up." This causes **The Big Blue** phenomenon—oceanic vertigo on a planetary scale.

*   **Artificial Horizon HUD:** Your helmet display *must* project a virtual horizon or a "floor" grid. Without a reference point, your inner ear will deceive you. You will think you are sitting still while you are tumbling end over end in the current.
*   **Anchor Lines:** Always carry a grapple gun or magnetic harpoon. The density is low, meaning you can float for days. If you stop moving, you will drift. Do not drift. If you drift into the open liquid between the mountain ranges (the "Emptiness"), you may never find a reference point again. You will become a satellite of the void.
*   **Blinders:** This sounds counter-intuitive, but bring eyepods or digital blinds. The visual overstimulation of the clear deep can be maddening. Sometimes, you need to make it dark to remind yourself you exist.

### 4. Navigating the Industrial Monoliths
The "Tech" is not a sanctuary; it is a hazard. These are continent-sized factories hanging on chains.

*   **Thermal Shielding:** The rigs vent "great, boiling clouds of superheated fluid." You need thermal sensors in your suit that can detect these invisible plumes. Getting caught in a condensation column will cook you inside your armor instantly.
*   **Entanglement Awareness:** The chains vanish into the abyss. Do not attempt to climb them unless you are equipped with heavy-duty magnetic clamps. The vibration is strongest here. The "heartbeat" of the planet is the pistons of these machines. Getting too close to a running piston is suicide; the shockwaves will liquefy your internal organs.
*   **Color Vision:** The world is black, grey, and amber. The "Amber Warning Lights" of the rigs are the only color. Trust them. If a light turns red, leave. Do not investigate. This is industrial machinery on a planetary scale; it does not care about you.

### 5. Interacting with the Fauna
The creatures here are large, solitary, and armored. They are not hostile; they are simply huge. They are "sleek, sharp-edged."

*   **Silence is Survival:** They navigate by the vibration of the engines. Your suit’s life support, your thrusters, and even your heartbeat are vibrations. Keep your suit's output minimized. Do not use active sonar (pinging). To a local eel, a ping is a challenge or a distress signal.
*   **Defensive Posture:** If a creature approaches, assume it is blind to your visual form. It feels you in the water. Do not thrash. If it gets close, discharge a burst of static electricity or a non-lethal concussion charge to startle it away. Do not try to touch them. Their skin is "thick as armor" and likely covered in sharp edges or parasites you do not want to meet.
*   **The "Solitary" Rule:** They do not school. If you see one, that’s it. If you see two, they are likely mating or fighting, and you should be miles away.

### 6. The "Trance" and Mental Hygiene
The combination of the cold, the visual clarity, and the deafening roar induces a trance. It is "industrial stasis." The world wants you to stop moving, to float, and to listen to the hum until you become part of the machine.

*   **Stimulant Protocols:** Carry chemical stimulants or adrenaline boosters. If you feel yourself slipping into a dream state, or if you start finding the roar "comforting," you are dying. Use the stimulant to wake up.
*   **Mission Time Limits:** Do not plan expeditions longer than 6 hours. The psychological toll of the "Void" accumulates rapidly.
*   **The Buddy System (Strict):** Never go alone. You need someone to physically grab you if you start staring into the crystal dark and forgetting to move your limbs.

### Final Note
You are visiting a place that has "already finished happening." It is a closed loop of pistons and freezing water. It is beautiful in the way a clock is beautiful—precise, cold, and indifferent.

Bring a camera. Record the sound, but filter it before you listen to it later. And when you leave, drag yourself out of the flask willingly, because the silence of your home universe will feel like the loudest thing you have ever heard.

Safe drifting.
