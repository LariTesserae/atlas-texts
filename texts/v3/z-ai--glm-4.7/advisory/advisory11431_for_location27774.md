**Travel Advisory: The Grief-Pocket (Sector designation: Hidden/Submerged)**

To the traveler:

You are not going for a swim. You are going for a suspension. The Grief-Pocket is not a place you visit; it is a place you endure. If you are expecting tropical reefs or the thrill of deep-sea diving, turn back now. This is a geological wound, a cavity in the world where the physics of distance and volume have curled up to die.

Preparing for the Grief-Pocket requires a radical restructuring of how you perceive movement, protection, and time. The medium is not water as you know it—it is a heavy, violet-tinged spirit, dense enough to cradle you but substantial enough to crush you with its own sorrowful inertia.

Here is how you prepare for the deep violet.

***

### 1. Mental Conditioning: The Architecture of Stillness
The most dangerous aspect of this location is not the wildlife; it is the **silence (0.8)** and the **geometry (2.6)**.

*   **Accept the Loop:** You must learn to unlearn the concept of a straight line. The Grief-Pocket folds back on itself. If you panic and swim for the exit, you will only swim deeper into the same sharp obsidian tooth you just left. You need to meditate on the concept of the circle. Visualize your path not as a line, but as a knot you are trying to untie slowly.
*   **Dissociate from "Up":** There is no surface. There is no sky. Looking "up" will only show you a gradation of pressure that leads nowhere. If you feel the urge to breach for air, you will drown in the void. You must be comfortable existing in a three-dimensional midnight where direction is a choice, not a law.
*   **The Chill of the Mind:** The temperature is a "matte chill" that seeps into the space behind the eyes. This is a psychic drop. Bring a totem—an object from your home dimension with intense emotional resonance. You will need it to anchor your identity when the violet medium tries to smooth your thoughts into its own slow rhythm.

### 2. Physical Armor: The "Glass-Needle" Protocol
The geology here is **young (1.4)** and arrogant. The stone has not had time to be smoothed by erosion. It is shattered obsidian and glassy basalt—serrated, jagged, and lethal.

*   **The Suit:** Standard neoprene is useless. It will be shredded in seconds by the rock formations. You require a rigid-plate exosuit, preferably ceramic or non-reactive alloy, articulated for flexibility but reinforced against puncture. Imagine wearing a suit of armor designed for a knife fight in a mirror maze.
*   **Thermal Insulation:** The ambient temperature is low, but the brine is viscous (2.9). A standard wetsuit will compress under the viscosity and lose its insulating properties. You need a heated, active-layer undergarment. Do not set the temperature to "tropical." Set it to "neutral." You want to match the 0.8 chill to prevent thermal shock, but keep a core heater running near your chest to stave off the hypothermia of the soul.
*   **Visor Tech:** Do **not** use a clear glass visor. The reflections on the glassy rock are out of sync—flickers of future movements or delays of the past. Seeing your own reflection move three seconds after you stop will induce nausea and vertigo. Use a polarized, heads-up display (HUD) that overlays topographical data onto the real world, effectively filtering out the "ghost" reflections. Set the HUD to high-contrast monochromatic green; the violet gloom of the pocket will mess with your color perception.

### 3. Locomotion: The Art of the Drift
You are not swimming. You are "existing within a medium." The water is too heavy for strokes.

*   **Glyder Units:** Fins are useless here; the viscosity is too high (2.9). You need low-thrust, directional micro-thrusters mounted on your back and forearms. Think "astronaut maneuvering unit" rather than "scuba diver." The movement must be gentle. If you thrash, you become tangled in the vegetation.
*   **The "Suspended" Breath:** If you are using artificial gills or a rebreather, slow the intake. The rhythm of this place is slow. Breathe in cycles of thirty seconds. Fast breathing will disrupt your buoyancy and cause you to collide with the **serrated stone**.
*   **Tethering:** This is non-negotiable. The **weirdness (2.6)** of the geometry means you can lose your sense of self. Attach a non-elastic carbon-fiber tether to a fixed point at your entry portal. If you get turned around by the non-Euclidean angles, follow the line back. Do not rely on compasses; magnetic north does not exist in a folded cavity.

### 4. Flora Interaction: The Living Gel
The vegetation is **lush (1.9)** and **dense (2.6)**. It is a "solidified thicket" of moss-fingers and silk-kelp. It is claustrophobic.

*   **Lubrication:** The plants are wet and clingy. Coat your armor in a hydrophobic, non-stick resin. If you do not, the "pale searching fingers" of the moss will snag your joints, slowing you down until you are entombed in the fronds.
*   **No Sharp Edges:** Carry no knives for cutting vegetation. The plants here are semi-sentient in their desperation. Cutting them may release a spore-cloud or trigger a contraction of the entire thicket. If you get caught, use the thrusters to "burn" them loose with a burst of heated gas or water jet, but do not sever them.
*   **Bioluminescence Discipline:** The pulsing coral (1.1) is hypnotic. Set your HUD to filter out the pulsing rhythm. If you stare at the slow, rhythmic pulsing for too long, your heart rate will sync with it, and you will fall into a trance from which you may not wake up.

### 5. The "Ghost" Protocol: Fauna and Tech
You are mostly alone—**fauna (0.9)** is rare, and **tech (0.2)** is extinct—but you are not unobserved.

*   **The Leviathan:** You may see the vast, translucent shape in the periphery. **Do not acknowledge it.** It is a custodian, a god of this small place. Do not shine lights on it. Do not approach it. It moves with the patience of a glacier; you must move with the patience of a stone. If it drifts near you, cease all movement. Play dead. Let it pass through you like smoke.
*   **The Scars:** You will see the parallel lines on the rocks—the tool marks of whatever machine built this place or cut it open. They are straight lines in a world of spirals. They are significant. They are the "epitaphs." If you can, scan them, but do not touch them. They are edges where reality is thin. Touching a tool-mark might slice your glove on the fabric of spacetime itself.

### Summary of the Drop
When you are ready to enter the Grief-Pocket, you will feel the pressure before you see the dark. You will feel the violet brine pressing against the seals of your suit.

Remember: You are a stranger in a room that is holding its breath. Move slowly. Trust your instruments, not your eyes. Do not look at the reflections. And whatever you do, do not try to scream; there is no air to carry the sound, only the heavy, suffocating velvet to swallow the noise and return it to you as memory.

Good luck, traveler. Watch the sharp edges.
