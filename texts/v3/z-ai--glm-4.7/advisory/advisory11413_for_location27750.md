To visit the Under-Vault is to submit yourself to a claustrophobic, humid biology of stone. You are not entering a habitat; you are stepping inside a living organ. As an ordinary human, your biology is ill-suited for the crushing density, the relentless humidity, and the industrial paranoia of this place.

If you intend to return from the Warren unscathed—and more importantly, unmoved—you must prepare not just your gear, but your mind. Here is how you prepare for the interior of the world’s lung.

### I. Thermal and Moisture Management
The Under-Vault is kept at a temperature just above body heat. This is its most insidious trap. You will not feel cold; you will cease to thermoregulate effectively. You will bake in your own fluids.

*   **The Layering Strategy:** Forget cotton. Forget heavy insulated layers. You need a single, full-body base layer of hydrophobic synthetic material (high-grade nylon or polypropylene) that wicks sweat away from your skin instantly. Over this, wear a loose, breathable woven shell to protect against abrasions, but nothing that traps heat. You must be dressed like a desert nomad, not a caver.
*   **Hydration Systems:** The air is thick water. You will breathe more moisture than you can drink, but you will sweat liters. You need a high-capacity hydration system, but with a critical modification: a **saline drip supplement**. Because the air is so saturated with humidity and the air pressure is high, your kidneys will shut down perspiration faster than usual, leading to electrolyte crashes. Carry salt tablets and add electrolytes to every mouthful of water.
*   **Water Collection:** Do not bring all your water. Bring a lightweight, collapsible sheet of hydrophobic material (like a tarp). Set it up directly under the "weeping" arches. The condensation is potable, but it tastes of wet clay and ancient dust. Bring powdered flavoring agents to mask the taste of geology.

### II. Respiratory and Auditory Protection
The environment assaults the two senses you cannot close: your ears and your lungs.

*   **The Filtration Mask:** This is non-negotiable. The air is a soup of spores, brick dust, dried dander, and the musk of thousands of Mason-Rats. A simple N95 mask will clog within an hour. You need a full-face respirator with P100 filters. Furthermore, the humidity is high enough that standard exhalation valves may gum up with moisture; coat the rubber seals with silicone grease to ensure a seal against your sweaty face.
*   **The White Noise:** The "roaring pressure" of the vault—a drone of skittering, chattering, and dripping—can induce auditory hallucinations and migraines. You need high-quality, noise-canceling ear protection. However, you cannot cut off sound entirely; you need to hear if a structural support fails or if the rats change their behavior. Use "shooting" or "tactical" ear protection that amplifies sharp sounds (like cracks or snaps) while dampening the constant, low-frequency roar.

### III. Footwear and Mobility
The floor is a lattice of channels, and the mortar is softening. You are walking on a hardened sponge.

*   **The Footprint:** Wear heavy, waterproof hiking boots with aggressive tread, but soften the soles. If you wear hard Vibram soles, you will slip on the wet, polished clay. You need a "sticky rubber" compound like that found on rock-climbing shoes.
*   **Gaiters:** The floor channels are slick with muck and runoff. Wear tall, heavy-duty gaiters to prevent this sludge from entering your boots. Once the wet mortar gets into your socks, you will develop blisters within minutes.
*   **The "Third Limb":** Carry a collapsible trekking pole. Use it to test the structural integrity of the wattle-and-daub before you lean on it. Many of the wooden frames in the Warren are black with rot and grease; they may look solid but crumble under human weight.

### IV. Dealing with the Mason-Rats
The Mason-Rats are not hostile; they are industrial. They do not hate you; they simply view you as a loose variable in their equation that must be fixed.

*   **The "No-Touch" Protocol:** This is the most critical rule of survival. **Do not sit down.** Do not place your backpack on the ground. If you place an object on the floor, the Mason-Rats will interpret it as a building material. Within minutes, they will begin to apply mud paste to it, weaving wattle around it, and incorporating it into their architecture.
    *   *Solution:* Bring a lightweight, freestanding camp stool or hammock. You must remain isolated from the lattice. If you sleep, you must hang suspended.
*   **Visual Deterrents:** The rats are dexterous and numerous. They will investigate your zippers and Velcro. Cover your gear in smooth, slick materials (silicone nylon) that are difficult for small claws to grip. Avoid loose straps or dangling carabiners; these will be snipped away and repurposed as pulleys.
*   **Dealing with Mimicry:** If you drop something—a camera, a light, a glove—let it go. Do not try to dig it out of the wall they build over it. Disturbing their work triggers a swarm response. They will not attack you, but they will swarm over you to "repair" the disturbance, coating you in mud and pulling you into the lattice. If you are engulfed, stay perfectly still and play dead until they lose interest and move on to a broken brick.

### V. Psychological and Mental Prep
The Under-Vault induces paranoia. The "weirdness" of the place—the feeling of being watched by the architecture—gets into your head.

*   **Accept the Compression:** You will feel the ceiling lowering. You will feel the walls closing in. This is partly optical illusion caused by the density of the Warren, but partly the humidity distorting your perception. Before entering, practice meditation in small, dark, humid spaces (like a steam room or a closet). You need to be comfortable with the sensation of being held tight.
*   **The Light Discipline:** The light shafts are your only reference to time, but they are dim and shifting. Bring a high-lumen headlamp with a red light mode. **Use the red light.** If you blast bright white light into the dark corners of the Warren, you will see thousands of eyes reflecting back at you. Knowing exactly how many rats are watching you is a quick route to madness. Red light preserves your night vision without illuminating the audience.
*   **Anchor Points:** The geometry of the vault is disorienting; it is a giant circle with no horizon. You can walk in circles thinking you are moving straight. Bring a physical compass (the magnetic field may be unstable, so bring a gyroscopic backup as well) and mark your path with chalk or biodegradable tape. The rats will steal the tape eventually, but it buys you an hour.

### VI. Sanitation and Exit
*   **The Biohazard:** You are breathing air that has been recycled through the lungs of a million rats. When you leave, you will be coated in a fine film of biological grease. Do not wear your Under-Vault clothes back into your home world. Bag everything in hazmat plastic. Decontaminate yourself immediately with scrub brushes and antiseptic soap.
*   **The Exit:** When you leave, the transition to "normal" air will be painful. Your lungs have adapted to the high density and pressure. You will feel dizzy and lightheaded. Do not exert yourself for at least an hour after emerging. Sit on the surface, breathe the thin, dry air, and let your body remember what it feels like to be alone.

**Summary Checklist:**
1.  **Hydrophobic base layers** (no cotton).
2.  **Full-face respirator** (P100 filters).
3.  **Tactical ear pro** (dampens drone, amplifies snaps).
4.  **Sticky-rubber boots** + gaiters.
5.  **Slick, silicone pack cover** (no dangling straps).
6.  **Freestanding hammock/stool** (NEVER touch the ground).
7.  **Red-light headlamp.**
8.  **Salt tablets.**
9.  **Mental conditioning for claustrophobia.**

Go softly. Do not interrupt the work. If the walls start to close in, remember: it is not malice, it is just maintenance.
