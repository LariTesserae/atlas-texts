**Travel Advisory: The High Rust**
**Destination Designation:** Industrial Plateau / Continental Superstructure
**Threat Level:** High (Environmental) / Severe (Fauna)
**Recommended Duration of Stay:** 72 Hours Maximum (Due to sensory fatigue and respiratory toxicity)

Visiting the High Rust is not a vacation; it is an endurance test. You are stepping onto the cooling hide of a dying leviathan. This location operates on geological timescales, not human ones, and it is indifferent to your presence. To survive the "Roof of the World," you must protect yourself from the air, the noise, the slip, and the swarm.

Here is how you prepare.

### I. Respiratory Systems: The "Syrup Lung" Protocol
The most immediate threat is the atmosphere. The air is thin (1.6/3 altitude) but paradoxically dense with humidity and particulates. It is described as moving "like syrup." Normal lungs will struggle to oxygenate blood while filtering the toxic soup of wet iron and oil musk.

*   **The Breathing Apparatus:** Do not rely on a standard N95 filter or a simple cloth mask. You require a **full-face, positive-pressure respirator** or a **closed-circuit rebreather**.
    *   *Why:* The air carries a heavy load of ferrous dust (microscopic rust particles) and aerosolized lubricants. Breathing this in for more than an hour will cause "Metal Fume Fever" at best and chemical pneumonitis at worst.
    *   *Humidity Control:* Your mask must have a moisture-exhalation valve or a dehumidifying scrubber. The ambient humidity will cause your breath condensate to fog your vision instantly and soak the filters, rendering them useless.
*   **Supplemental Oxygen:** Despite the deceptive weight of the air, the oxygen partial pressure is low due to the altitude. Carry compressed oxygen canisters. You will need them when climbing the vertical gantries or if the pressure drops further.

### II. Tactile Protection: The "Rust-Bloom" Suit
There is no softness here that does not want to poison you. The "velvet curtains of rust" are deceptive; they are abrasive, carcinogenic, and will stain skin permanently. The ground is a composite of slag and brick, and every surface is slick with oily condensation.

*   **Material Selection:**
    *   **No Ferrous Metals:** Do not wear anything made of iron or steel that rusts. Zippers, buckles, and snaps must be brass, titanium, or high-grade plastic. Within hours, steel joints will seize up in the humid, salty air.
    *   **Non-Porous Outer Shell:** Wear a suit made of **Gore-Tex, PVC, or treated waxed canvas**. The air is constantly weeping moisture; if your clothes soak it up, you will develop hypothermia (even in the warm air) due to wind chill on the high plateau, and your skin will prune and crack, inviting infection.
    *   **Abrasion Resistance:** The environment is sharp. The "rust-hollows" and jagged pipework act like razors. Your suit needs heavy reinforcement at the elbows, knees, and palms.
*   **The "Chitter" Defense:**
    *   The local fauna consists of millions of arthropods. They possess rasping mouthparts designed to scrape metal.
    *   **Kevlar or Chainmail Underlayer:** Ideally, wear a layer of slash-resistant protection under your outer shell. If you fall into a swarm of the "fist-sized" Chitter, they will strip you to the bone in seconds.
    *   **Sealed Seams:** Ensure your suit is hermetically sealed at the wrists and ankles. The creatures are small and seek warm crevices; an opening in your boot is an invitation for them to nest inside your clothing.

### III. Auditory and Vestibular Prep: The "Great Thrum"
The High Rust is loud (1.7/3), but not in a way that hurts your ears immediately. It is a low-frequency vibration felt in the teeth and bones. This causes "Vibration White Finger" and profound disorientation over time.

*   **Ear Protection:** Standard foam plugs are insufficient against low-frequency infrasound. You need **noise-canceling headphones** capable of filtering out the thrum of the engines, or **custom-molded silicone earplugs** with a high decibel rating (NRR 33+).
*   **Dental Care:** Paradoxically, visit a dentist before you go. The constant vibration aggravates loose fillings or micro-fractures in teeth. The sensation of your teeth buzzing in your skull can drive a person to madness if they have oral issues.
*   **Medication:** Pack motion sickness medication (Meclizine or Dimenhydrinate). The shadowless, diffuse light combined with the constant ground vibration creates a sensory conflict that will induce nausea and vertigo.

### IV. Mobility and Footwear: The "Oil-Slick" Tread
Water does not flow; it drips and accumulates. Every surface is lubricated with a layer of oily residue mixed with rust sludge.

*   **Footwear:** You need heavy-duty, oil-resistant industrial boots (like "Vibram" soles designed for grease).
    *   **Spikes or Cleats:** The ground is uneven slag. Flat soles will slide on the wet rust. Your boots must have aggressive tread or interchangeable ice spikes for grip.
    *   **Height:** High-top boots are mandatory to protect your ankles from twisting in the crevasses and to prevent the creatures from scuttling up your pant leg.
*   **Traversal Gear:**
    *   You are walking on a fractal city (Scale 2.6/3). You will need to climb.
    *   Bring a **grappling hook with a motorized winch**. The "streets" are often just narrow crevices between towers; sometimes the only way forward is up.
    *   **Gloves:** You need heavy-duty mechanic's gloves with magnetic palms. This helps you adhere to the steel beams while climbing.

### V. Visual Orientation: The "Grey-Out" Navigation
The horizon is non-existent. The light is shadowless and constant (2.3/3). The city repeats in a fractal pattern. This creates a severe risk of getting lost simply by walking in a circle.

*   **Navigation Tech:**
    *   **Do not use a compass:** The massive engines and ferrous geography will render magnetic navigation useless.
    *   **Inertial Guidance:** You need a dead-reckoning GPS unit or a gyroscope-based waypoint marker. Pick a distinct landmark (a silo top, a specific colored valve) and mark it digitally.
*   **Visual Contrast:**
    *   Wear high-visibility clothing. The world is grey, orange, and steel-blue. A bright neon yellow or pink suit is not just for safety; it allows your teammates to spot you against the monochrome fog.
    *   **Lighting:** While the light is pervasive, it is flat. You will need a high-lumen tactical flashlight to see into the "rust-hollows" and beneath the pipework before you step. The shadows here are treacherous; what looks like a solid pile of bricks might be a thin shell hiding a drop of a thousand feet.

### VI. Water and Provisions
*   **Water:** NEVER drink the water here. It accumulates in rusty hollows and is heavy with heavy metals, tetanus, and industrial toxins. You must carry all your own water.
*   **Food:** The smell of "unwashed bodies" and the "tangy musk" will overpower your sense of taste through the respirator. Bring high-calorie, nutrient-dense paste or bars that have no scent. You do not want to lure the local fauna with the smell of a ham sandwich.

### VII. Psychological Preparation: The "Slow" Acceptance
Finally, prepare your mind. This place is slow (0.8/3). The machinery measures time in epochs. If you try to rush, you will panic.

*   **Accept the Vibration:** You will feel the engines turning in your chest. Remind yourself: *This is normal. The building is not shaking apart; it is functioning.*
*   **Respect the Swarm:** The Chitter are not evil; they are the rats and pigeons of this world. Do not attack them unless attacked. They are numerous; you cannot kill them all. If a swarm approaches, stay still, flatten yourself against a wall, and let the tide of clicking legs pass over you.
*   **The Weight of the Sky:** The pearlescent grey sky presses down. It can induce claustrophobia despite the open space. Focus on the task at hand. Do not look up at the silos too long; the scale (2.6/3) can make you feel infinitesimally small, leading to existential dread.

**Summary Checklist:**
1.  Rebreather + O2 tanks.
2.  Non-ferrous, sealed, abrasion-resistant suit.
3.  Slash-resistant under-layer.
4.  Heavy tread oil-resistant boots.
5.  Inertial navigation system.
6.  Noise-canceling ear protection.
7.  Motion sickness meds.
8.  All own food and water.

Go carefully. The High Rust does not hate you, but it will not catch you if you fall.
