To visit the world of the Infinite Descent is to accept a total surrender of your terrestrial senses. You are not going to a place; you are entering a medium. This is not a dive where you return to a boat and a sunny deck; this is an immersion into a cold, dark, perpetual suspension that feels more like a mausoleum than an ocean.

Here is how you must prepare for the Drowned World.

### 1. The Vessel: The Hard Shell
Forget standard wetsuits or neoprene. The water here hovers just above freezing, and the cold is not just a temperature; it is an active force that slows the blood and quiets the mind. Furthermore, the depth implies pressure that would crush a human chest flat.

You require a **Deep-Pressure Atmospheric Suit**.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** This suit must be heated. Not merely insulated, but actively heated. The chill of this world is pervasive. It seeks the heat of your life. If your power fails, you have minutes before the cold locks your muscles and the darkness takes you.
*   **Articulation:** You are not swimming in open water; you are navigating a maze of rough-hewn fiber and stone. Your suit needs to be dexterous enough to grip the braided towers but armored enough to protect against the abrasive texture of dried reed and ancient basalt.
*   **Life Support:** There is no surface to gasp at. Your rebreather must be capable of running for days without maintenance. Silence is the rule here; the scrubbing system must be muffled. The sound of your own mechanical breathing should not shatter the profound silence of the abyss.

### 2. Sensory Adaptation: Conquering Visual Starvation
The greatest danger here is not the cold, but the blindness. The description "visual starvation" is literal. Your eyes are useless. If you rely on them, you will panic.

*   **Blind Training:** Before you step through the portal, spend 48 hours in absolute darkness. Learn to orient yourself without a horizon line. Practice moving while blindfolded in a tank of water. You must re-learn proprioception—knowing where your limbs are without seeing them.
*   **Sonar and Tactile Imaging:** Your helmet must be equipped with high-frequency active sonar. However, do not map it to a visual screen. Map it to audio or haptic feedback (vibrations against the skull or skin). If you try to "see" this place on a monitor, the low resolution and the gloom will induce hallucinations. Learn to "hear" the shape of the basalt columns and "feel" the density of the braided towers through the vibrations in your suit.
*   **Light Discipline:** Bring light, but use it sparingly. The darkness here is a protective blanket. Turning on a high-lumen beam is not just rude to the sanctity of the place; it is disorienting. It will blind you to the faint, phosphorescent decay that provides the only natural navigation. Use red light, filtered to the dimmest possible setting, only when checking your gauges.

### 3. Navigation: The Loom of the World
You are entering a forest made of rope and stone. "Cannot swim freely" is the operative warning.

*   **Hardline Reel:** Do not rely on wireless navigation or GPS. There are no satellites, and the "floor" is uniform basalt stretching forever. You will become lost in the vertical columns. You must trail a physical line—a strong, thin cable—from your entry point. If you lose the line, you are lost in an infinite volume of cold water.
*   **Cutting Tools:** The vegetation is braided fiber. In the strong currents that occasionally shift these towers, loose ropes can whip like steel cables, or you can become entangled in the hanging gardens of slime. You need a high-tensile cutter, mounted to your suit, capable of severing thick braided reed instantly.
*   **Anchoring Gear:** You cannot hover forever; the "drift" is hypnotic and will consume your air supply. You need heavy magnetic or mechanical pitons to clamp onto the basalt columns or the granite anchors of the towers. You will need to clip yourself to the architecture to rest.

### 4. Psychological Conditioning: The Weight of Silence
This world is "mostly silent." The noise is a thrum felt in the teeth. This sensory deprivation can induce a state of deep trance or terror.

*   **Mental Anchors:** You are visiting a place that has reached its "final form." It is finished. It is waiting for nothing. This stillness can be crushing to a human mind used to growth, noise, and purpose. You must bring a purpose of your own, a mental mantra or a task, to keep you from dissolving into the calm.
*   **Auditory Protection:** The creaking of the towers—the "wooden ship settling" sound—will be amplified by the water and your helmet. Low-frequency infrasound can cause nausea and dread. Your helmet needs active noise-canceling that filters out the structural groans while leaving you aware of the immediate flow of water.
*   **Acceptance of the Suspend:** You will feel like a ghost floating in a cathedral. The vertigo will be intense because there is no "up" or "down" visually. You must trust your instruments telling you where the floor is. Do not look "up" to the lighter purple zenith; it is a false hope. There is no air there.

### 5. Biological Preparations
The "fauna parameter is near zero." This is a relief in terms of predators, but it means the ecosystem is bacterial and fungal.

*   **Seal Integrity:** The "velvety coating of life" on the towers—slime molds and mosses—are likely foreign to your biology. Do not touch them with exposed skin (which you won't have, but assume a breach). If your suit is breached, the cold is the least of your worries; infection by ancient, preserved microorganisms is the real threat.
*   **Nutritional Paste:** You will not be hunting. There are no fish. Bring high-calorie, easy-to-digest rations. Eating in the dark, suspended in freezing water, is difficult. Your food tube should connect directly to your helmet port to avoid removing your gloves or breaking your seal.

### Summary of the Experience
When you arrive, you will feel the bite of the cold instantly. You will engage your sonar and hear the ping return as a vast, open hall. You will see nothing but faint, dying ghosts of light.

You will reach out. Your gloved hand will touch something rough and organic—braided reed the size of a skyscraper. It will sway slowly, hypnotically. You will clip your safety line to it. You will hang there, in the absolute blackness, listening to the groan of the stone floor miles beneath you, and feel the vibration of the deep ocean in your bones.

You are a visitor in a tomb that is still alive. Prepare to be small. Prepare to be very, very quiet.
