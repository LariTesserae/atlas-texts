Visiting the High Altitude Terrarium—let’s call it the "Foundry"—requires a complete recalibration of what it means to go "outside." This is not nature. This is a machine that is pretending to be a jungle, and as an ordinary human, you are essentially a contaminant in a closed-loop hydraulic system. If you treat this like a standard hiking trip, the humidity will rot your gear, the rock will slice your skin, and the sheer vertigo will paralyze you.

Here is how you prepare for the wet, humming lung of the Foundry.

### 1. Physiological Preparation: The "Lung" Acclimation
First, understand that you will be breathing liquid air. The saturation level is near 100%, and the temperature is just above skin thermal neutral. You will not sweat; you will ferment. Sweat cannot evaporate here, so your body’s primary cooling system will fail.
*   **Pre-Trip Conditioning:** Spend time in a sauna or a steam room. You need to get used to the sensation of heavy, wet air entering your lungs. Practice breathing deeply in high-humidity environments so you don’t hyperventilate upon arrival.
*   **Hydration Strategy:** Do not drink water constantly. You will absorb moisture through your skin and lungs just by being there. Overhydration is a real risk. Instead, load up on electrolytes for a week before you go. You need your blood chemistry primed to handle the heat without the cramping that comes from "wet heat."
*   **Ear Health:** That sub-vocal thrum—the machine hum behind the walls—will pressure your ears. The constant dampness is a breeding ground for ear infections. Bring drying drops and use them religiously after every "day" (even if the day/night cycle is artificial).

### 2. Textiles and Armor: The "Wet" Protocol
Cotton is death. Wool is misery. If you wear natural fibers, they will soak up the atmospheric moisture, become heavy, cling to your skin, and never dry. You will be weighed down by your own clothes within an hour.
*   **The Base Layer:** Wear a full-body hydrophobic base layer. Think competition swimmer or triathlete gear, but heavier. It needs to be a second skin that creates a friction barrier between you and the wet world.
*   **The Shell:** You do not need a raincoat (water isn't falling from the sky; it’s bleeding from the walls), but you need a mechanical protection suit. The "rock" is raw, extruded stone—sharp as glass. The ferns are likely tough enough to cut paper. Wear a lightweight, abrasion-resistant canvas or synthetic shell. Cover your ankles and wrists.
*   **Footwear:** This is critical. The surface is slick stone covered in bio-slime. Standard hiking boots with deep tread will hydroplane on the wet stone, turning the floor into a slip-n-slide thousands of feet in the air. You need **sticky rubber**—approach shoes used by rock climbers for smearing. Furthermore, the stone is sharp. Your soles must be durable. Bring two pairs; switch them out halfway to let the inside of the boots dry (if you have a heat source).

### 3. Safety Gear: The Vertical Threat
The description mentions sheer drops and an environment that feels like a "jar." You have no guardrails.
*   **The Constant Harness:** Wear a climbing harness *all the time*. Do not take it off to sleep. You need to be tethered to something solid at all times. The "ground" might expand, but the "drop" is lethal.
*   **Tether Points:** Do not trust the vegetation for anchoring. The vines are injected with machinery; they might be anchored, but they are also growing rapidly and are slippery. Only anchor to the raw rock extrusions, and use gear that can handle sharp edges (slander ropes).
*   **Eye Protection:** The "sun" here is artificial and blinding. When the mist clears, the UV or intensity could be damaging. Bring high-contrast, photochromatic lenses. But more importantly, protect your eyes from the "weeping" walls. You don't want mineral-rich machine-condensation dripping into your corneas.

### 4. Interaction Protocols: The Living Machine
This is the most important psychological preparation: **You are in a factory.**
*   **Do Not Touch the Flora:** The plants are crops, fed by injected slurry. Those copper rings and tubes are likely sterile, sharp, or electrified. The "milky nutrient slurry" is almost certainly not for human consumption and may be caustic. Treat the jungle like a high-voltage switching station.
*   **Respect the Expansion:** The walls move. "Micro-fractures" and "grinding groans" are background noise. Never pitch a tent or sit directly against a wall face. The machine *will* try to crush you or extrude rock directly underneath you. Keep a 10-foot buffer zone from all vertical surfaces.
*   **The Silence:** There are no predators, but there are brass beetles and lens-eyed birds. Do not try to catch them. They are likely part of the maintenance crew—biological drones designed to prune or clean. Interfering with them flags you as a "pest" to the controlling intelligence of the Foundry.

### 5. Sensory Management
The silence here is oppressive. It is a "sub-vocal thrum." This can induce sensory deprivation hallucinations or extreme anxiety in ordinary humans.
*   **Audio Comfort:** Bring a white noise generator or bone-conduction headphones playing low-fidelity ambient noise (rain, busy cafe). The artificial silence of the machine is not natural; it is the sound of a paused process. Drowning out the hum can prevent madness.
*   **Mental Discipline:** You will feel claustrophobic despite the "cathedral" size because the horizon is gone. You are in a box. Combat this by keeping your eyes on the micro-details—the individual drops of water, the texture of the stone—rather than looking out into the void where the world ends.

### Summary Checklist
1.  **Synthetic, hydrophobic, skin-covering clothing.**
2.  **Sticky-rubber approach shoes.**
3.  **Climbing harness and static rope (worn constantly).**
4.  **High-lumen eye protection (transition lenses).**
5.  **Electrolytes, not water.**
6.  **Ear drops.**
7.  **A healthy fear of the walls.**

Go there expecting to be uncomfortable. Expect to be damp, to smell the metallic tang of ozone and nutrient paste, and to feel small. You are walking on the skin of a breathing, growing robot. Tread lightly.
