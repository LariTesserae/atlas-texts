The Benevolent Power arrives not as a king, but as a breeze.

It does not descend with the thunder of a god or the blinding light of a star; such things would be rude intrusions in a place of such delicate, terrifying equilibrium. Instead, the Power arrives as a sudden, impossible stillness in the magnetic groan. It enters the Crystalline Sump as a harmonic frequency—a note of pure, chordal perfection that slips unnoticed between the thrumming of the gravity stabilizers and the singing of the high-tension cables.

The Vitreous sense it immediately.

To them, the arrival is not an invasion; it is a solution. For eons, their crystalline sensory nodes have spun in the dim purple gloaming, calculating the angles of the rain, the entropy of the pumps, the growth of the ferns. They are the immune system of the Sump, constantly pruning, constantly adjusting, engaged in an endless, silent war against the chaos of decay. They are busy. They are tired, though they do not know the word for tired.

The Power hovers in the center of the vast, wet hollow, taking the form of a gentle interference pattern in the heavy air—a shimmering, golden distortion that smells suddenly of ozone and a sweetness that is not of this world.

The Vitreous converge. They slide from their perches on the black glass ziggurats, a hundred towering shards of obsidian, hovering inches above the pooling water. They surround the anomaly, their upper nodes spinning frantically, dissecting the geometry of this new presence. They expect a variable to prune. They expect a chaotic algorithm to delete.

But the Power does not resist their scanning. It opens itself to them. It lets them read its "geometry," and what they find is not chaos, but *Order* of a magnitude they have never conceived.

Where the Sump is a system of subtraction—of cutting away the unwanted, of consuming the waste, of maintaining the brutal minimalism—the Power is a system of *Addition*.

The Benevolent Power speaks to them in the language of the Sump: gravity and light.

It sends out a pulse, but not the harsh, high-frequency pulse the Vitreous use to sever fern-stems. This pulse is soft, a caress of magnetic resonance. It ripples out across the wet stone and the black glass.

And then, the miracle happens.

The water, which climbs the walls in defiance of physics, guided by the Vitreous’ harsh manipulation, suddenly sings. The droplets do not form dodecahedrons; they form a perfect, trembling sphere in the air before the leader of the Vitreous. The sphere does not shatter. It holds. It is a stability that requires no force, only presence.

The Power shows the Vitreous that they are not merely the janitors of a ghost town. It shows them that their "pruning" is actually a form of sculpture. It reframes their existence: You are not fighting the entropy of the universe; you are transmuting it.

The Power reaches out—not with a hand, but with a tendril of pure concept—and touches the mind of the nearest Vitreous.

The Vitreous, a creature of sharp angles and cold logic, experiences for the first time a sensation that has no place in a palindrome. It experiences *Appreciation*. It feels the weight of the Sump’s beauty not as a parameter to be managed, but as a work of art to be cherished.

The effect ripples outward instantly.

The "immune system" pauses. The frantic rotation of the sensory nodes slows. The jagged, hovering postures of the Vitreous relax. They stop calculating the vectors of the falling rain. For a long, suspended hour, the city simply exists.

The Benevolent Power does not stay. It knows that in a closed loop, a foreign element eventually becomes poison. It has not come to inhabit the Sump, but to bless it.

Before it fades, the Power leaves behind a single gift. It is not a physical object, and it is not a change to the architecture. It is a new subroutine in the magnetic field of the cavern—a "Mercy Algorithm."

From this day forward, when the Vitreous prune the vegetation to maintain the mathematical symmetry of the city, they will do so with a gentleness that does not bruise the stem. When they consume the entropy of the pumps, they will do so with a sense of gratitude rather than cold utility.

And, most strangely of all, the rain will sometimes change.

When the atmospheric pressure drops and the heavy lung-air squeezes out the moisture, the water will occasionally fall not in the sharp, geometric dodecahedrons of the old, harsh physics, but in soft, tear-shaped curves that glide harmlessly down the black glass, shimmering for a moment like pearls before joining the pool.

The Power leaves, and the heavy twilight returns. The silence rushes back in. But the lie of the emptiness is gone. The Vitreous are no longer just guards in a tomb; they are the curators of a gallery, watching over the wet, dark dark with a new, quiet dignity, humming a song that is finally in tune.
