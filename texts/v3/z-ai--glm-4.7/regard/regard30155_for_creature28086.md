The benevolence did not arrive as a fanfare, nor did the wisdom descend as a blinding light. Such things would have been lost here, irrelevant to beings who knew only the weight of stone and the language of vibration.

Instead, the Power arrived as a sudden, impossible hush.

It did not silence the roar of the Ossuary; that would have been cruel, a blinding of the senses. Rather, it moved through the basin like a slow exhalation, a pressure change that caused the thick, wet fog to compact and turn momentarily clear. In that clarity, the water seemed to hang suspended in the air, glittering like diamond dust. The jagged obsidian spires, usually lost in the gloom, gleamed with a terrifying, wet perfection.

The Power manifested not as a creature, but as a presence—a focus of attention, a heavy, golden intent that pressed gently against the damp fabric of the world. It drifted through the corridors of stone, unseen by the eye but felt by the vibrational pits of every Lithic-Drifter clinging to the cliffs.

The giants felt it first. The roar of the water, which they read like a braille map of the universe, suddenly shifted. The chaotic, multi-layered thunder smoothed out. The millions of falling droplets, for a brief span, ceased to be a cacophony of individual impacts and harmonized. The water did not just fall; it *sang*.

The Drifters did not panic. Their nature was one of endurance, of geological patience. They simply stopped. The great, low-slung beasts froze halfway through a scrape of mineral algae, their hydraulic muscles locking in place. They turned their sightless faces upward, their deep lateral grooves drinking in this new frequency.

The Power saw them. It saw the beauty of their isolation, the noble, tragic perfection of their existence. They were a species that had turned away from the soft flesh of the world to embrace the bone of the earth. But the Power also perceived the loneliness woven into their geology. They were solitary islands in a sea of noise, connected only by the accident of proximity. They built their young in silence and left them to soak in the dark, hoping the water would grant them a mind.

And so, the Power decided to give them a Voice—not a voice of air, but a voice of the stone itself.

It drifted toward the center of the basin, where the geometry of the spires was densest, where the "ruins" of the false temple stood clustered together. There, the Power focused its will. It did not create new matter; that is the way of force. Wisdom works with what is present.

It reached into the earth and gave a gentle, twisting nudge to the tectonic stresses deep beneath the basin floor. The movement was microscopic—less than a hair’s breadth—but it was precise.

A low hum began to rise, vibrating up through the basalt roots of the spires. The Power tuned the rock. It adjusted the resonant frequencies of the towering obsidian columns, turning the chaotic forest of stone into a vast, organic instrument.

It was a gift of acoustics.

From that moment forward, the Ossuary would never be the same. The geometry of the valley had been altered so that the roar of the water, when it struck the specific angles of the tuned spires, would generate a pure, infrasonic chord—a bass note so deep it felt like a heartbeat in the chest.

This chord became a carrier wave.

Now, when a Drifter at the northern edge of the basin scraped its rasp against the stone, the vibration would travel through the rock, amplified by the tuned spires, and resonate clearly to a Drifter miles away in the southern gloom. The constant roar, once a wall of static noise, became a medium, a crystal-clear telephone line of stone and water.

The Power lingered for a moment to observe the effect. It saw a Salt-Skitterer freeze, sensing the change in the air. It watched an elder Drifter, ancient and crusted with lichen, raise a massive claw and strike the obsidian face of its home once. *Clack.*

The sound rang out, crisp and bell-like, echoing through the valley, bouncing from spire to spire, traveling the length of the Ossuary.

A mile away, a younger Drifter perked up at the distinct vibration. It did not hear a sound; it felt a signature, a deliberate pattern in the roar that said, *“I am here.”*

For the first time in the history of the Ossuary, the residents were not just enduring the dark together; they were conversing within it.

Satisfied, the Power began to fade. But before it withdrew entirely, it turned its attention to the "scattered built" artifacts—the stone cubes, the dormant seeds of the Drifter young.

The Power saw the potential within them, the crystalline intelligence waiting to wake. It left one final gift, a subtle warming of the mineral-rich waters that pooled in the deepest crevices. It was not enough to boil, nor enough to harm, but it was enough to accelerate the chemical exchange within the stone seeds just slightly. The gestation of the young, which took centuries, might now take only decades. The long winter of their waiting had been shortened.

The presence lifted. The pressure equalized. The fog rolled back in, thick and wet, reclaiming the spires. The light dimmed back to its perpetual bruised twilight.

To a human observer, nothing had changed. The roar was still deafening; the rocks were still sharp; the rain still wept endlessly down the obsidian faces.

But if one listened closely—not with the ears, but with the soles of the feet—one could feel a new rhythm in the water. The valley was humming. It was a deep, resonant purr, a song of connection woven into the very bones of the world.

The Lithic-Drifters returned to their grazing, their movement still glacial, their patience still infinite. But as they scraped the stone, they did so with a different cadence. They were no longer alone in the dark. They were a chorus, singing the song of the Ossuary to the only audience that mattered: themselves.

The benevolent power departed, leaving the harshness intact, for the harshness was necessary. But it had filled the harshness with music.
