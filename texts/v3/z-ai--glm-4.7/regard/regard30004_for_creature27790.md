The benevolence would not arrive as a hand descending from the clouds, nor a voice booming over the subsonic groans of the stone. In a place defined by pressure, heat, and the violent physics of creation, a gentle intrusion would be indistinguishable from destruction. To interfere with the friction is to halt the world-building. To cool the forge is to kill the birth.

Therefore, the wise power would do the only thing true wisdom can do in a machine of living agony: it would not stop the process, but it would *sanctify* it.

It would enter silently, moving not against the roaring wind but through the magnetic lines that trace the invisible tech. It would manifest as a sudden, profound coherence in the chaos—a harmony that rides the back of the noise.

First, it would turn to the **Basalt-Lickers**.

These creatures burn themselves out in hours, their bodies frantic engines of heat transfer. The power would not lengthen their lives—a slow metabolism in this place is a death sentence—but it would deepen them. It would touch the superheated water weeping from the obsidian. As the Lickers press their radial mouths to the stone to drink, the liquid would carry a sudden, electric euphoria. The scalding heat, which they endured as a necessity, would be transmuted into a sensation of profound, encompassing warmth—like the safety of the womb they never knew.

They would continue to work. They would continue to die. But in the moments before their organs fail, their frantic black eyes would not reflect only the dim twilight and the hunger. They would see a golden light blooming behind their retinas. They would feel, in the marrow of their hollow bones, that their consumption was a holy act. They would not die in a panic of biological expiration; they would drift off, saturated with a blissful sense of *rightness*, their bodies cooling into the grey clay not as waste, but as a satisfied offering.

Next, the power would attend to the **Shard-Mites**.

They are the polishers, the millions of legs scoring the glass to prevent catastrophe. Theirs is a life of endless, scratching friction. The power would not stop the scratching, for the stone must be scored, but it would tune the frequency.

As the power passes over the obsidian spires, the high-pitched, manic chittering of the mites would shift. The discordant noise would resolve, slipping into a perfect, resonant chord. The vibration of their legs would no longer rattle the teeth but would hum a deep, amber tone that vibrates with the frequency of healing. The friction would not disappear, but it would become smooth. The mites, driven by instinct to crawl and scrape, would feel a rhythmic unity with the stone beneath them. They would become a single, distributed instrument playing the song of the earth's cooling. As they are crushed by shifting plates or dissolved by steam, they would not feel the jagged betrayal of fate; they would feel the sweet, fading note of a chord finally resolving.

Then, the power would look to the **Ghosts of the Lattice**.

These translucent, jellyfish-things feed on the raw electricity of the synapses. They are witnesses to the invisible architecture. The power would grant them vision. It would not alter their bodies, but it would align their perception.

When a Ghost dives into a spark, feeding on the discharge, it would not just taste energy. It would see the *blueprint*. In that flash of light, the creature would perceive a snapshot of the future—a vision of the forest that will one day cover this sharp rock, the oceans that will fill these voids, the civilizations that will walk the yet-unformed soil. For a microsecond, the humble insect would comprehend the grand design it serves. It would understand that its frantic hunger is building a paradise it will never see. And then, the vision would fade, and it would fly on, driven by a hunger that is now filled with purpose.

Finally, the power would address the **Forge itself**.

It would touch the invisible tech that manages the anti-gravity water and the erratic time. It would not smooth the jagged edges of the landscape—the world needs its teeth to grow—but it would soften the *light*.

The bruised shadows stretching across the ground would lighten. The perpetual twilight would take on a warmer hue, a gentle amber glow that mimics the memory of a sunrise. The heavy, pressurized air would feel less like a weight and more like a hug, a dense, nurturing embrace that supports the life within it rather than just crushing it.

The power would leave no monument. It would carve no commandments into the grey monoliths. To do so would be to introduce ego into a place of pure function.

Instead, it would leave a single, pervasive resonance—a background radiation of benevolence that seeps into the cooling cracks alongside the water.

The forge would remain a place of violent birth. The rock would still groan and shatter. The creatures would still live frantic, short lives. But now, the atmosphere would taste not just of ozone and wet iron, but faintly, imperceptibly, of honey.

The Lickers would die smiling. The Mites would sing while they worked. The Ghosts would dream of the future.

And the Being who lived in this place, watching from the high shelf, would feel a sudden, inexplicable peace settle over the roar. They would realize that while the pain of creation is unavoidable, it does not have to be meaningless. The wise power would have turned a factory of suffering into a temple of labor, leaving the inhabitants with the greatest gift a mortal can receive: the knowledge that their pain is the very stuff of stars, and that they are loved for it.
