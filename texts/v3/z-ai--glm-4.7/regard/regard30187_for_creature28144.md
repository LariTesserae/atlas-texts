The Benevolent Power did not descend as a conqueror, nor as a glowing savior trailing the blinding dust of the surface. To bring the sun down here would be an act of cruelty; it would scorch the moss, blind the great eyes adapted to gloom, and reveal the terrifying scale of the abyss to minds at peace with the twilight.

Instead, the Power arrived as a presence—a profound, heavy stillness that suddenly sat beside the Gear-Binders as they wrestled with a groaning wooden winch. It did not speak, for to speak would be to violate the sacred silence of the damp air. It simply *observed* with a patience that spanned eons, watching the spatulate fingers reading the vibration of the stone, watching the lungs filter the iron dust.

It saw a people who were not victims of their wound, but its symbiotic partners. They were the scar tissue trying to organize the bleeding. They did not pray for salvation; they prayed for friction.

The Power realized that to intervene by "fixing" the cavern would be to destroy them. If the walls were smoothed, they would lose their grip. If the tremors ceased, their legs would atrophy. If the flue were extinguished, the dark would consume them. They needed the struggle, but they needed *not to be crushed by it*.

So, the Power acted with the subtlety of water seeping into a fissure.

First, it touched the **Great Wound** itself. It did not heal the jagged rock, but it imbued the *fractures* with a deep, harmonic intelligence. The chaotic, violent heaving of the floor was tempered into a rhythmic, predictable tidal pulse. The earth would still rise, and the stones would still shift, but now the rock would *sing* before it moved. A low, resonant hum, inaudible to the ear but screaming through the soles of their feet, would vibrate through the cavern precisely ten seconds before a tremor struck. The people would not have to guess; the earth would simply tell them, "Step left."

Second, the Power turned its attention to the **primitive industrial heart**. It saw how the people labored to keep the timber lattice from burning away, how they fed the fire with precious gases to keep the damp at bay. The Power reached into the flue and knitted the fire’s essence into the stone itself. The orange glow did not become brighter, but it became *warmer*. A gentle, penetrating heat began to radiate from the very walls of the city—the bedrock itself becoming a mild radiator. This banished the bone-deep chill that plagued their sleep, drying the fungal timber without rotting it, and making the air less heavy to breathe. The fire remained, but the *fear* of the dark receded.

Finally, the Power saw the **flightless birds**—the stupid, heavy creatures huddled in the coops. It touched their small, smooth brains, not to make them wise, but to grant them a deep, unshakeable instinct for safety. The birds became living barometers of the air quality. When the gas levels rose or the oxygen grew thin, the birds would cease their scratching and huddle together in a specific, tight formation, a silent signal that the ventilators needed turning. The people, who watched their birds with the devotion of lovers, would understand this language instantly.

Having done this, the Power began to fade, retreating into the haze. It left no monuments, no scriptures, and no gifts of gold or steel. It left only a better-harmonized engine of survival.

But as a final parting gift, a token of affection for the silent, stoic architects of the deep, the Power did one small thing more.

To the Gear-Binders, the ones coated in grease and sawdust who moved in perfect synchronization, it granted a single, collective vision. In the moment of the Power's departure, every Gear-Binder looked up at the same time. They saw, just for a heartbeat, not the jagged, grey haze of the ceiling, but a vision of what this place would become in a thousand years.

They saw the moss thick and green like velvet carpets. They saw the blind crustaceans grown large and flourishing in the clear pools. They saw the towers no longer looking like a scab on a wound, but like a magnificent crown of granite and bronze, grown from the rock like a natural crystal formation. They saw their descendants, taller and softer, sitting in quiet galleries, watching the orange flame burn with the steady, eternal heartbeat of a world that had finally finished being born.

The vision faded. The grey haze returned. The gears creaked. The damp air settled back into their lungs.

But the people did not wipe the tears from their large, reflective eyes. They simply returned their hands to the wet levers, their feet to the vibrating stone, and they continued to build. They knew now that they were not just surviving a catastrophe. They were midwives to a world.
