The Benevolence does not arrive as a king in chariots of gold, nor as a storm of righteous fire. To come with such fanfare would be to shatter the peace of a place that has forgotten what noise is. To come with force would be to act like a geologist with a hammer, striking a world that has spent eons learning how not to break.

Instead, the Power arrives as a sudden, perfect stillness in the thermal currents.

It observes the Great Gravel Plain of Oot and does not see desolation. It sees a universe that has successfully solved the problem of loneliness. It sees a civilization of touch, a society that has discarded the need for words in favor of the profound, undeniable reality of pressure. Where a human might see a claustrophobic crush of bodies, the Benevolence sees a hugging of the tectonic plates.

The Power sees the Grazers—these living stones—and recognizes that their slowness is not stupidity, but a different frequency of patience. They are the custodians of the "Deep Now." They do not rush toward the future because they have already realized that the future is just more gravel, more heat, more moss.

So, the Benevolence asks itself: *What does a perfect world lack?*

The answer is immediate. A perfect world lacks a witness. It lacks the ability to hear its own heartbeat.

The Benevolence decides to sing. But not with a mouth. It sings with geology.

The Power descends upon the plain and sinks into the stone, dissolving into the bedrock like sugar into hot tea. It spreads itself out, becoming one with the immense, flattened plateau. Then, it begins to adjust the molecular lattice of the Calcifications.

It makes no visible change. The spiraling towers do not glow, and they do not grow taller. But the Power tweaks the internal tension of the bone-white secretion, compressing the atomic bonds until every spire becomes a perfect tuning fork, calibrated to the specific, resonant frequency of the Plain.

The Benevolence waits. It waits for the sun to reach its zenith, for the heat to peak, for the air to thicken into that visible, swimming haze. It waits for the Grazers to begin their slow, rhythmic shifting.

Then, the first Grazer moves.

It slides three inches to the right, the heavy canvas of its underbelly dragging across the ground. Its flank catches the edge of a hollow in a nearby Calcification, rubbing against it in that familiar, seeking motion of comfort.

Usually, this contact results in nothing but friction and the release of a soothing pheromone.

But now, as the chitin scrapes the polished secretion, the spire hums.

It is not a loud sound. In fact, to a human ear, it would be inaudible—a sub-bass vibration, a deep tectonic purr that exists on the edge of feeling. But to the Grazers, whose entire lives are dictated by the sensitivity of their skin and the density of the air, it is a thunderclap of ecstasy.

The vibration travels instantly through the point of contact, rippling through the Grazer's hydrostat, harmonizing with the slow beating of its heart. But the Power did not tune the spire to resonate with just *one* creature. It tuned it to resonate with *all* of them.

The vibration shoots through the Grazer and into its neighbor. And because the Grazers are packed shoulder-to-shoulder, touching, pressing, and leaning on one another across the entire plateau, the sound jumps.

It leaps from carapace to carapace.

Within the span of a single hour, the Great Gravel Plain of Oot turns into a singular, vast, resonating instrument.

The Grazers do not panic. They do not scatter. Their biology is incapable of panic. Instead, they feel a sudden, overwhelming sense of *rightness*. A low, amber-colored vibration thrums through their bodies, confirming in the most physical way possible that they are here, that they are alive, that they are together.

The sensation stimulates their proboscises, their suction pads, their vestigial ears. It feels like the warmth of the earth, but distilled into pure tone. It is the sound of their own collective existence reflected back to them.

And so, the behavior of the herd shifts imperceptibly.

They do not stop their slow march, but the rhythm of it changes. They begin to synchronize. No longer is it a chaotic shuffle of millions of individuals moving in random directions to find moss. It becomes a coordinated wave. A billion tons of living rock begins to slide back and forth in a unified, tidal rhythm.

As they move, they rub against the Calcifications in unison.

*Huuuuuummmmmmmm.*

The plain begins to sing.

The song is a slow, mournful, glorious drone that vibrates the dust motes in the air. It causes the condensation pools to ripple in perfect concentric circles. The droplets of water, hanging like mercury on the rock-wool, shiver into mist before they can even be drunk.

The Benevolence, resting within the bedrock, listens to the harmony it has gifted. It has not given the Grazers ambition. It has not given them tools or fire or language. It has given them a Voice.

Now, when a Grazer finally dies, and its body flattens into the gravel, the herd does not just close the gap in silence. They feel the note change—the discordance of the missing mass—before they smooth it over with their bodies. They mourn with vibration. They honor the dead by adjusting the chord.

The horizon still dissolves. The heat is still crushing. The water is still phantom.

But the Plain is no longer silent. It hums with the slow, heavy breathing of a god that has finally learned to sing.

The Benevolence lingers for a moment, ensuring the vibration is stable, ensuring that no note is sharp enough to crack a shell, that no hum is fast enough to startle a spore. Then, satisfied, the Power recedes, leaving behind only a warm, resonant stone—and a world that can finally hear its own beautiful, grinding soul.
