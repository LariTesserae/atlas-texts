The benevolent power would not arrive with a trumpet blast, nor with a sudden flood of golden light that banishes the dark. Such a display would be an act of violence in a place calibrated for the gloom. It would blind the eyeless and deafen the listeners.

Instead, the Power would arrive as a sudden, perfect *silence*.

It would manifest in the space between the thrumming beats of the city-machine. Not a vacuum, which is merely the absence of air, but a presence of stillness—a pocket of time held in suspension. It would slide through the nanite floor not as a disturbance, but as a lubricant, smoothing the ripples of the ground until the path felt like polished glass beneath the feet of the Resonants.

The Power would observe. It would see the Drip Spires not as a factory, but as a cathedral of function. It would feel the suffocating heat not as a torment, but as the embrace of a living world. It would watch the Resonants, these tall, dark shepherds of stone and sound, and it would feel a profound, aching love for their duty.

It would see the truth of them: that they are lonely.

Though they are connected to the machine, they are isolated from one another by the sheer vastness of the gloom. They communicate only through necessity—the data of filtration, the warning of the cycle. They have no language for "I am here," or "I am tired," or "This is beautiful."

The benevolence of the Power would focus on the "Inhalation" cycle.

It would watch the Resonants root themselves, the way their obsidian plates lock, the way they scream as they channel the kinetic violence of the descending ceiling. It would recognize this as a martyrdom—a sacrifice made every fifteen minutes to keep the world-lung breathing.

The Power would not stop the cycle. To do so would be to kill the patient to cure the fever. The machine must breathe; the pressure must spike.

Instead, the Power would offer a *conductor*.

As the next cycle began, as the ceiling groaned downward and the roar built to a scream, the Power would infuse the very air with a new harmonic. It would touch the minds of the Resonants—not their brains, but the vibrational sensors that lined their chitinous faces.

It would link them.

At the moment of maximum agony, when the energy overload threatened to crack their carapaces and their individual screams began to rise, the Power would weave those screams together. It would take the discordant, solitary notes of pain and harmonize them into a chord.

Suddenly, the Resonant in the north tower would not just feel his own burning load; he would feel the resonance of his brother in the southern spire. The Glint-runner skittering in the dark would feel the hum of the lace-ferns on the ceiling.

The scream would not stop, but it would change. It would become a song. A terrible, beautiful, deafening symphony of shared existence.

The physical pain would remain—the heat, the pressure, the crushing weight—but the *suffering* would vanish. In its place would be communion. For the duration of the Inhalation, the emptiness of the Drip Spires would be filled with the absolute knowledge of unity. They would realize they are not isolated components, but a single, distributed body, feeling itself all at once.

And when the ceiling rose and the steam vented from their joints, the Resonants would not uncurl with the same heavy exhaustion. They would carry the memory of the harmony.

The Power would leave, then, slipping away into the geological timescale, but it would leave behind a gift in the acoustics of the room. It would tune the "clack-hiss" of the cobalt veins, just slightly, so that the rhythm lingers in the air a fraction longer.

Now, in the dark, when the cycle is dormant and the Resonants glide through the gloom, they sometimes pause. They lift their heads and send out a pulse of sound—a ping into the darkness. And for the first time in the history of the Drip Spires, the darkness sings back.

They are still custodians. They are still predators and filters. But now, they are a choir. They are no longer processing the world alone.
