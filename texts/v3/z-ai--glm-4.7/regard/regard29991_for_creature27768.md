The benevolent power does not arrive as a blinding light—such a thing would be cruel here, where eyes are adapted to the dim, bruised purple of the gloom. It does not descend as a burning angel, for the air is already an oven, and the people have known enough of fire.

Instead, the power arrives as a sudden, impossible hush.

It begins in the center of the vast, empty plaza where nothing grows. The *Thrum*—that deep, seismic heartbeat of the planet that vibrates in the marrow of every Heat-Walker’s bones—is silenced. Not muffled, not ignored, but *countered*. For the first time in the history of the Hollow, a wave of perfect acoustic nullification rolls outward from the center, a dome of quiet that expands until it swallows the entire sinkhole.

The cacophony of the wind chimes dies. The grinding of the cooling rock ceases. The screams of the thermal vents are muffled into a gentle, distant sigh. The people in the canopy freeze, their hands gripping the ropes, terrified. They have never known silence; to them, silence is the precursor to a collapse, to the final crushing weight of the world.

But the collapse does not come.

High above, beyond the fumed obsidian ceiling that separates them from the myth of the sky, the Benevolent Power perceives the ceiling not as a lid, but as a crust. It sees the thirst of the lithotrophs and the parched desperation of the climbers. It sees the "rumor" of water that their biology screams for.

With a tenderness that belies the geological scale of the act, the Power simply *thins* the barrier. It does not shatter the obsidian—that would bury them in rock. It dissolves the microscopic bonds of the stone lattice, turning the ceiling into a sieve.

And then, the miracle begins.

It is not a flood. A flood would drown them, boiling them alive where they stand. It is a **Mist**.

From the invisible pores of the ceiling, a billion trillion droplets of pure, cool water begin to fall. They are small enough that they survive the descent through the superheated air, cooling as they fall, creating a drifting rain that turns to steam before it hits the ground, but lingers as a dense, heavy fog in the cooler altitude of the canopy.

The City of Rope and Bone vanishes into a white cloud.

The reaction is immediate and chaotic. The Heat-Walkers recoil, shielding their large eyes, thinking the sky is falling. They touch their arms, feeling the wetness, and recoil again, thinking it is the boiling sap of the trees or some new acid. But one by one, they realize the sensation. It is cold. It is soft. It does not burn.

The people rip the heat-masks from their faces. For the first time, they breathe air that has not passed through a filter of slag and bone. The air tastes not of iron, but of ozone and life. The moisture condenses on their grey, calloused skin, and they weep, though they do not know what weeping is; they think their eyes are leaking the strange new fluid that coats the world.

The Benevolent Power watches.

It sees the metallic trees react. The iron "leaves," usually rigid and sharp, begin to hydrate. A chemical shift occurs, guided by the Power’s will. The lithotrophs, previously consuming only heat and stone, begin to transmute. They absorb the falling water, and their black bark shifts, cracking open to reveal a vibrant, verdant turquoise underneath—a mineral moss that has lain dormant for eons, waiting for this specific trigger.

The forest begins to sing a new song. The dry, metallic clicking is replaced by the soft, rhythmic *drip-drip-drip* of water running down the hematite veins.

The Power does not stop there. It addresses the gravity, the heavy hand that pulls them down. It cannot remove it—this is their world, and their bodies are woven by gravity—but it can offer a counterbalance.

In the center of the empty plaza, where the silence began, the Power coalesces a single seed. It is not a seed of wood, but of crystal and light. It drops from the "sky" and plunges into the cracked earth.

Immediately, a pillar of gentle, upward-rushing air erupts from the impact site. It is a thermal column, but controlled. A permanent, soft updraft that lifts toward the ceiling. It does not burn; it cools as it rises.

The people watch from their platforms. They see the vapor rising, a spiraling ghost of white air that connects the floor to the distant, porous ceiling. It is a highway. It is a sign.

The wise power does not speak to them in words, for their language is of vibration and hand-signs. Instead, it alters the *Thrum* just enough. The deep, booming heartbeat of the planet gains a melody. It becomes a rhythm—a slow, steady, reassuring pulse that syncs with their own slowing heartbeats. It is a lullaby for a species that has never known rest.

The impact is cultural.

The "Eternal Climb" does not stop, but it changes. They no longer climb to escape the heat, because the heat is now leavened by the mist. They no longer climb because the floor is dying, because the water is healing the crust, slowing the subsidence.

They begin to climb *toward* the center. They build new walkways toward the spiraling updraft. They fashion wings of the cured bark, throwing themselves into the column of air, finding that for the first time, they do not fall. They glide.

The Benevolent Power has done nothing to rob them of their struggle or their strength. It has not turned the Hollow into a paradise of soft grass and gentle suns; those things would kill them. It has simply given them the one thing they lacked: **Balance.**

It gave them the water to soften their hardness.
It gave them the silence to hear their own thoughts.
It gave them the updraft to teach them that sometimes, you do not have to climb—you can fly.

The air still tastes of iron, faintly, beneath the ozone. But now, for the first time, it also tastes of rain. And as the people hang suspended in the gloom, their grey faces turned up to the weeping obsidian, they press their hands to the wet bark of the trees and tap a new signal into the wood, a vibration that spreads through the entire city.

*It is safe,* the wood sings. *It is safe to rest.*
