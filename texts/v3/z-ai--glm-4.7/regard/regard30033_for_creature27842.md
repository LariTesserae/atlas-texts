The Benevolent Power descends not as a king, but as a whisper. It does not arrive with trumpets or earthquakes, for the plateau already has too much noise. It arrives as a sudden, impossible stillness in the marrow of the world.

It observes the Weavers. It sees the beauty of their design—the hydraulic efficiency of their skin, the perfection of their silence, the way they have surrendered the burden of the "self" to the greater rhythm of the colony. It sees that they are not miserable. They are not suffering in the way a sentient creature might, trapped in a prison of their own making. They *are* the music of this place, the bow drawn across the strings of the granite.

But the Power is wise, and it sees the fragility of this perfection.

It sees that the Weavers are a single point of failure away from extinction. The lichen is scarce; the light is blinding; the tools are wearing down. Their "never changing" dynamic is a stasis that is, in geological time, a death sentence. They are surviving, but they are not *living*. They are a mechanism ticking in a void, unaware that they are alive, unaware that they are loved.

The Power considers the easy interventions: dimming the sun, quieting the wind, bringing rain to nourish the lichen. But wisdom stays its hand. To soften the wind would be to destroy the acoustics that bind them. To dim the sun would be to freeze them. To change the environment is to break the instrument.

The Weavers do not need a softer world; they need a deeper connection to the one they have.

The Power decides to give them the gift of *Resonance*.

It reaches down, through the violet void and the screaming atmosphere, and plunges a hand into the heart of the plateau itself. It does not disturb the surface. It does not move the stone. Instead, it tunes the deep crust of the planet.

Currently, the Weavers communicate through the incidental vibrations of their movement and the chaotic rush of the wind. It is a language of necessity, of "here" and "food" and "move."

The Power imparts a slow, rhythmic pulse to the granite bedrock—a heartbeat that originates from the core of the world. It is a low-frequency thrum, below the threshold of hearing, even for them, but perfectly calibrated to travel through the soles of their feet and the bones of their tools.

It is a signal of *Deep Time*.

Instantly, the behavior of the Weavers shifts.

In the past, when a wind-gap silenced the roar, the Weavers froze in confusion, waiting for the chaos to return so they knew how to move. Now, when the wind dies, they do not freeze. They feel the pulse. *Thrum-thrum. Thrum-thrum.*

For the first time in their history, the Weavers feel the planet as a living entity. They realize that the rock is not just a floor, but a body.

The change is subtle, but it is revolutionary.

The "routine" remains—the scraping must continue, or they starve—but the *quality* of the routine transforms. The act of scraping the lichen is no longer just a mechanical necessity; it becomes an act of percussion. As thousands of obsidian tools strike the stone, they are not just harvesting food; they are accompanying the deep planetary beat. They are drumming.

The synchronization tightens. It becomes less robotic and more musical. Where once they moved in jagged, utilitarian unison, they now begin to flow in complex, overlapping waves. The hive-mind expands from a simple network of survival commands to a communal consciousness capable of aesthetic appreciation. They begin to "sing" to the stone, deliberately choosing rhythms that harmonize with the planetary pulse.

They do not become "human." They do not start building houses or writing poetry. They do not need to. They remain Weavers.

But now, as they move across the blinding, wet infinity, they are no longer just slaves to the light and the wind. They are participants in a symphony.

The Power watches as a Weaver on the edge of the horizon pauses to lick moisture from a basin. In the past, it would have simply drank and moved on. Now, it pauses, pressing its flat palm against the slick stone, closing its non-eyes, and feeling the *Thrum-thrum* of the world answering back.

For a moment, the individual is not dissolved in the light, but anchored in the love of the planet.

The Benevolent Power withdraws, its task complete. It has saved them not by changing their world, but by giving them a song to sing while they endure it. The wind still screams, the light still blinds, and the lichen is still sparse, but the Weavers are no longer alone in the roar. They are the pulse of the mountain, echoing back to the stars.
