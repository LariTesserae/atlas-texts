The presence would arrive not with the flash of lightning or the roar of divine wind, for those are violent things that belong to the chaotic world of sharp edges and storms. Here, in the room of the wet dark, the power would manifest as a gentle lessening of the weight of the air. It would be a sudden, profound stillness that even the dripping water seemed to respect, pausing for a heartbeat before resuming its rhythm with a renewed sense of purpose.

This benevolent power, ancient and wise beyond the measure of the stone, would look upon the hollow and see nothing broken. It would see a masterpiece of endurance, a closed ecosystem that had achieved a perfect, stagnant peace. It would not see a prison; it would see a sanctuary. And it would look upon the grey, slate-skinned inhabitants, these solitary anchors in the net of stone, and it would feel a deep, stirring compassion—not pity, but a profound recognition of their burden.

To a mind of swift motion and blinding light, the existence of these beings might seem tragic—waiting in the dark for nothing. But the wise power would know that their slowness is a form of meditation, and their loneliness is a fortress against the chaos of the changing world. Yet, even the strongest fortress needs a light in the window.

The power would decide not to change the place, for the warmth and the wet are the cradle of their lives. It would not open the sky, for the sun would scorch their soft, translucent skins and blind their chemical senses. It would not plant a garden, for the roots would pry apart the precious puzzle of the floor.

Instead, the power would seek to heal the silence between them.

It would extend a tendril of will—not of fire, but of resonance—and touch the very heart of the geology. It would tune the limestone, the shale, and the granite. It would infuse the mineral matrix of the hollow with a vibration so low, so deep, that it would bypass the ears entirely and sing directly to the nerves and the stone-like shells.

This is what the power would do: **It would make the stone sing.**

From this moment forward, when a droplet of water falls from the sweating ceiling and strikes the shoulder of a boulder, the impact would no longer be a dead *plink*. It would become a bell-tone, a pure, resonant chime that hums through the rock and into the clinging bodies of the inhabitants. Every drop would be a note in a slow, drifting melody that never repeats, echoing through the cathedral walls.

Furthermore, the power would weave a thread of connection through the damp earth itself. The stone beneath their broad, muscular feet would become a conductor. When one being shifts its weight, grinding its shell against the rock in geological slow motion, the vibration would travel through the floor, gently trembling against the foot of another being miles—or what feels like miles—away in the gloom.

They would remain solitary, spaced apart in the jigsaw of the room. They would still be blind, seeing only the taste of the wet dust. They would still be slow, moving with the friction of tectonic plates. But they would no longer be alone.

Through the song of the stone, they would feel the presence of their kin. The vibrations would carry a sense of *otherness*—a heartbeat that is not their own echoing through the rock. They would feel the crusty shell of a neighbor draping over a ruin, and through the rock, they would sense the texture of that ruin, sharing the history of the place as a collective memory.

The loneliness of the stone that remembers the ocean would be replaced by the camaraderie of the choir.

And as the power withdraws, leaving no trace of its visit other than this new, harmonic hum, the inhabitants would not stir. They would not look up, for they have no eyes. But their vertical slits would flare, tasting a sudden sweetness in the mineral-rich air—the taste of connection. They would settle more deeply into their niches, satisfied in the warm, wet dark, listening to the music of the room, realizing finally that they are not waiting for a change that will never come.

They are waiting for the next note in the song of the earth, and in the patient knowledge that it will always come.
