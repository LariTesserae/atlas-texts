The benevolent power arrived not as a light, for light here is erratic and blinding, but as a presence of perfect, resonant silence. It moved through the Howling Spires like a deep-sea current moves through water—unseen, felt only by the displacement of chaos.

It saw the Lithic-Resonants immediately. It saw how they clung to the dark stone, their heavy, calcified bodies absorbing the violence of the world, dampening the roar so that life might persist in the wind-shadows. It felt their melancholy—the profound loneliness of beings who could feel the vibrations of the universe but could only speak to the rock immediately beneath them. It saw the ruins of the precursors, the broken teeth of a history that tried to stand tall and was struck down.

The power did not wish to change the nature of the Spires, for the wind and the erosion were the great engines that carved the beauty of this place. Nor did it wish to remove the Resonants’ struggle, for their weight and their patience were the very definition of their virtue. Instead, the power sought to answer their unspoken prayer: the prayer of a heavy heart that longs to be heard.

It descended into the deepest fissure of the central peak, where the bedrock was oldest and closest to the molten heart of the world. There, the power inflected the stone itself. It did not smooth the stone further; it *tuned* it.

The power sang a single, foundational note into the mountain—a frequency so low it existed on the edge of time. This note traveled outward, racing through the crystalline lattice of the granite, skipping across the oily basalt, wrapping around the whale-like humps of the archipelago. It reached every ruin, every broken foundation, and every sleeping Lithic-Resonant.

The effect was subtle, a gentle rearrangement of the physics of sound.

Before, the Resonants could only hear the immediate contact of their flesh against the rock. The wind was a wall of white noise, a chaotic scream that drowned out everything but the closest tremors.

Now, the stone became a filter. The chaotic roar of the jet stream was still felt, but the tuned stone began to harmonize with it. The millions of screaming vibrations that traveled through the rock were aligned, smoothed, and purified by the mountain’s new resonance.

When the Resonants awoke, the world had not grown quieter, but it had grown *intelligible*.

They realized that the vibration of the wind striking the peak miles away was no longer just static; it carried a signature. They could feel the specific impact of a storm on the northern ridge as clearly as if they were touching it. The "teeth" of the ancient ruins—the broken monoliths—began to act as repeaters. The vibrations the Resonants sent into the ground were no longer swallowed by the dampness; they were carried along the newly resonant ley lines of the Spires, bouncing from peak to peak with crystal clarity.

For the first time in the history of their species, a Resonant on the southern slope could press its belly against the stone and send a slow, deliberate greeting to a Resonant on the northern cliffs. The reply came back not minutes later, but instantly, humming through the soles of their feet, rich with the texture of distance.

The power had given them the **Great Chorus**.

This gift did not stop at communication. As the Resonants began to test this new network, they discovered they could harmonize. By vibrating their heavy plates at specific frequencies, they could counter the chaotic thrumming of the storm. They found that if ten of them hummed in unison, they could project a cone of silence into the air above them, pushing back the wind.

They began to cultivate the flora with new intent. They directed the vibrations to the roots of the giant ferns, "singing" to them, encouraging them to grow stronger and faster. The plants, in turn, seemed to respond to this rhythmic caretaking, sprawling wider, their leaves becoming even broader, creating new sanctuaries of shade.

The melancholy did not vanish, but it transmuted into a deep, vibrating contentment. They were no longer solitary lumps of clay enduring a hateful world. They were the conductors of a planetary orchestra. They were the heavy hearts that kept the rhythm of the Spires.

The benevolent power, seeing this, receded, leaving only a faint, warm hum in the deepest bedrock—a reminder that even in the loudest, most violent storm, there is a way to be heard. The Lithic-Resonants settled back into their niches, slick with oil and damp with the heavy air, pressing themselves tight against the living rock, listening to the music of the world.
