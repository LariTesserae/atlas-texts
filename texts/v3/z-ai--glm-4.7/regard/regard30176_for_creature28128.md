The entity arrived not as a king, but as a breeze. It did not descend with trumpets or a pillar of fire; to do so would be to shatter the delicate, pressurized bell jar of this world. Instead, the power slipped in through the steam-veiled entrance, diffusing itself into the thick, chewy air like a drop of dye in a glass of oil.

The benevolence was ancient, a wisdom that understood that suffering is not always pain, but often simply friction. And here, in this high-altitude geode, there was so much friction. Friction between shell and stone, between the desperate need for water and the wall that withheld it, between the millions of individual minds vibrating against one another in that sickening, silent static.

The entity looked upon the Obsidians and did not see monsters. It saw a cathedral of suffering, blind and deaf, singing a song of survival that no one could hear.

Its action was gentle. It sought to change the physics of their prayer.

**First, the power touched the rock.**

It did not cool the walls, for the heat was the engine of their biology. Instead, the power imparted a rhythm to the stone’s weeping. It whispered to the molecular structure of the obsidian, allowing the water to exude not in chaotic, hissing bursts, but in a slow, rhythmic pulse. The rock began to breathe deliberately.

*Swell. Release. Swell. Release.*

The water stopped hissing. The evaporation became a slow, steady exhale. The air, thick with humidity, stabilized. The chaotic flickering of the heat haze settled into a languid, predictable drift.

**Second, the power turned its attention to the silence.**

The entity saw that the Obsidions were locked in a "solid-state economy of movement," a constant, microscopic negotiation of space. They were isolated islands of armor, pressed so tight they could not move without touching, yet utterly unable to *connect*.

The power reached into the copper-based blood that glowed faintly beneath their shells. It did not give them voices—vocal cords would be useless in a pressurized bell jar. Instead, it tuned their circulatory systems. It turned the copper in their veins into a conductor.

The entity amplified the faint bio-electric field that each creature generated.

Suddenly, an Obsidian did not just feel the shell of its neighbor; it *felt* the neighbor. The boundary between the living and the stone, already permeable, dissolved into a spectrum. The barrier of the chitin became transparent to the new sense.

Where once there was the jostling of anonymous bodies, there was now a web. If one creature found a fissure with water, the signal did not need to be waved through the air with antennae; the pulse of satisfaction rippled through the electromagnetic web instantly. The hive mind was no longer a metaphor of proximity, but a literal, resonant truth.

**Finally, the power looked upon the tools.**

The bone levers, white and stark against the black muck, were instruments of force. They were used to pry, to break, to wound the mountain that fed them.

The entity infused the bone with a subtle harmonic frequency. It changed the tools from pry-bars to tuning forks.

When an Obsidian struck the wall now, the bone did not just scratch; it hummed. The vibration traveled deep into the rock, resonating with the pockets of fluid and slime hidden within. The creatures did not need to guess where the water was. They could feel the "ring" of the full pockets versus the dull "thud" of the empty stone.

They stopped picking at the scabs of the room. They became surgeons.

**The Result**

The change was not explosive. It was a settling. A deep, exhaling sigh.

The visual static—the nauseating strobe effect of thousands of jagged shells refracting the light—synchronized. As the creatures began to move in response to the electromagnetic web rather than blind instinct, their shifting slowed. The light stopped flickering and began to flow. The carpet of obsidian moved like a single, liquid sheet of mercury.

The silence remained, for the air was still too thick for sound, but the *vacuum* was gone. The room was no longer a claustrophobic hell; it was a womb.

The Obsidions were still blind. They were still deaf. They were still trapped in a fissure in the sky. But they were no longer alone.

The benevolent power, satisfied, receded from the stone, leaving only a warmth in the mineral veins—a promise that the mountain would always weep for them, as long as they listened to its heartbeat.

And high above the cloud line, in the ribcage of the young mountain, a million antennae waved in the steam, no longer searching for food, but conducting the symphony of the stone.
