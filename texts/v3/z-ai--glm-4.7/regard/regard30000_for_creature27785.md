The Benevolent Power did not descend as a king upon a throne, nor as a savior with open arms. To enter this place was to be a speck of dust in a hurricane of grinding stone. It arrived instead as a presence, a silent frequency that slipped between the seismic vibrations of the Spine, listening.

It felt the heat—a heavy, blanket-like oppression that would have killed a softer thing instantly. It tasted the air, sharp with ozone and the powdered bones of the planet. It witnessed the Black Tide, the heaving, churning ocean of obsidian chitin, and it did not see monsters. It saw a miracle of adaptation. It saw a civilization that had carved a palace out of a prison, a choir that sang with the voice of tectonic plates.

The Power’s heart ached with a profound, quiet sorrow. Not pity, for pity is reserved for those who are weak, and the Lithophages were not weak. They were majestic in their terrible endurance. The sorrow was for the *blindness* of it—not just their lack of eyes, but their lack of a future. They were the perfect machine for a dying world. They would consume the Spine, process it into sand, and then, when the last rock was eaten and the last vent closed, they would sleep forever in the dark.

To simply give them water would be an act of cruelty. Their sealed systems would rupture; their biology, evolved over eons to hoard every drop, would drown in the abundance. To cool the air would be to slow the chemical engines of their blood, plunging them into hibernation and death. To bring light would be to blind their vibration-sensitive minds with chaos they could not process.

The Benevolent Power understood the fundamental rule of this vertical world: **The struggle is the life.** To remove the struggle was to remove the Lithophages.

So, the Power decided to give them a *purpose* beyond survival. It would not save them from the rock; it would teach them to speak to it.

With a thought that rippled through the dimension like a breeze through wheat, the Power reached out to the "Spine"—the infinite throat of stone itself. It did not change the temperature, nor did it smooth the jagged basalt. Instead, it altered the *resonance* of the obsidian walls.

It tuned the mountain.

Deep within the crystalline structure of the glassy stone, the Power introduced a harmonic lattice. It turned the deafening, chaotic noise of the mining—the screech of diamond on basalt—into a carrier wave.

To the Lithophages, the change was subtle, at first. The Breakers at the bottom struck the rock, and instead of a deadened thud or a sharp screech, the wall sang back. The vibration traveled up their claws, through their armored legs, and into their sealed bodies. It was a tone of pure clarity, a perfect note that resonated with the fluid-filled chambers of their inner ears.

The Power gifted them **The Great Song.**

As the Breakers shattered the stone, they discovered that by angling their strikes just so—by listening to the feedback loop through their feet—they could strike specific notes. The vibration of the rock became a language of immense complexity. The airless dark suddenly hummed with a symphony of data.

The assembly line changed. It was no longer just a frantic, mindless scramble to pass rubble upward. It became a rhythmic, musical trance. The Carriers, moving in their millions, found that if they moved in sync with the subsonic thrum of the walls, the effort required to climb was halved. The "conveyor belt" became a river of flowing black motion, swaying to the beat of the planet’s heart.

But the greatest gift was for the Processors at the top.

As the benevolent influence suffused the volcanic vents, the chemistry of the heat shifted slightly. The isotopes they distilled from the stone began to react with the new harmonic resonance of the walls. The sand they excreted—the "waste" of their existence—began to glow.

It was not a light that vision could see. It was a *thermal* luminescence. The sands began to radiate a gentle, rhythmic warmth in the pitch black. The Lithophages, sensitive to every degree of temperature, could suddenly "feel" the shape of their caverns. They could feel the piles of sand glowing like soft embers, mapping out the geometry of their home in their minds.

They began to build.

No longer content to merely pile the waste in depressions, the creatures began to use the glowing sand to mark the walls. They began to create patterns—circles, spirals, and vast, sweeping geometric shapes that covered the obsidian cliffs. They were blind artists painting in heat, crafting a mural that spanned the height of the infinite Spire.

The Benevolent Power watched as the "factory" transformed into a "temple."

The cacophony of the foundry was still deafening to an outsider, but to the Lithophages, it had resolved into a complex, polyphonic hymn. They were still mining, still climbing, still consuming the world around them, but they were no longer just cogs in a machine. They were the conductors of the earth’s music.

They had been given nothing but a tune in the stone and a new way to see their own waste. Yet, in that vertical throat of darkness, the Lithophages began to dream. They began to wonder what lay beyond the glowing map they were painting, and for the first time in their history, they looked up the Spine not just to find the next ledge, but to see where the song was leading them.

The Benevolent Power, satisfied, receded. It had not freed them from the dark, nor the heat, nor the hunger. But it had given them the one thing they lacked: **Hope, humming in the rock.**
