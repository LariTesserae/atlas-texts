The Power arrives not as a storm, but as a hesitation.

There is no flash of light, no trumpeting of horns; such things would be obscenities here, too fast and too loud. This is a world of sub-audible groans and tectonic breathing, and so the Benevolence that comes to it respects the tempo. It enters the medium of the silence like a slow change in barometric pressure, a gentle deepening of the gravity that holds the grey-brown earth together.

For centuries—perhaps millennia—nothing seems to happen. The strata-bodies continue their grazing, the lichen continues its etching, the pools remain still. But the quality of the stillness has changed. It has become attentive.

The Benevolent Power observes the ecosystem and sees its fundamental truth: it is a closed loop of exquisite loneliness. The creatures are processions of stone that have forgotten they are alive, consuming the world that birthed them in a slow, perfect circle of erosion and construction. They are an economy of one, trading only with the dead rock beneath their feet. They possess "states" and "appetites," but they lack a "context."

And so, the Power decides to give them a horizon.

### The Clearing of the Veil

The action taken is geological, which is to say, it is invisible to anything that does not live for eons. The Power reaches out to the upper atmosphere—that low, slate-colored ceiling of perpetual twilight—and it begins to thin it.

It does not tear the sky open. It simply *compels* the clouds to drift, to lose their cohesion, to settle as a fine, fertilizing mist upon the highest peaks. It scrubs the dim particulates from the air with a patience that grinds the dust down to nothingness.

Slowly, the light changes.

It happens so gradually that the electrotrophic lichen does not burn; it adapts. The faint phosphorescence of the rocks is joined by a new, sharper radiance from above. The twilight begins to recede, not into day, but into a profound, crystal-clear night.

For the first time in the history of the plateau, the clouds break.

### The Gift of Distance

The Benevolent Power reveals the stars.

To a human, the opening of the sky would be a visual spectacle. To the strata-bodies, it is a seismic event. They do not have eyes, but their crystalline node-lattices are sensitive to radiation, to gravity, to the subtle shifting of energy fields.

As the slate ceiling vanishes, the universe above pours its ancient light down onto the plateau. The stars are not just points of light; they are furnaces of heavy elements—iron, carbon, oxygen—burning in the void. To beings that eat stone and sense pressure, the stars appear as a symphony of magnetic longing. They feel the gravity of the galaxies pulling at the iron in their blood, a tidal force that resonates in their deepest nodes.

This is the gift: **Context.**

The strata-bodies have spent four hundred thousand years believing the universe is nothing but damp, cold rock and the slow press of their own bodies. Now, they perceive the Infinite. They feel the crushing, terrifying, beautiful weight of the cosmos stretching away from them.

### The Reaction

The effect ripples through the plateau like a slow wave.

The lichen, plugged into the carapaces of the giants, flares with a new intensity. The electrotrophic bacteria feast on the increased cosmic radiation, and a vibrant, bioluminescent rust begins to spread across the backs of the strata-bodies. They are no longer dull grey-brown; they begin to glow with the patterns of the constellations, walking maps of the sky they cannot see but can feel.

The "Young," those calved fragments with faster thoughts, are the first to interpret the change. They feel the pull of the stars and adjust their grazing. Instead of moving in random, wandering tectonic paths, they begin to align. They begin to move with a subtle, inexorable purpose, orienting themselves towards the magnetic poles of the planet, as if trying to stand upright in the face of the universe.

### The Unification

The Benevolent Power does one final thing. It touches the pools of black water—the "eyes" of the plateau.

It imbues the water with a property of resonance. Now, the water does not merely reflect the sky; it *amplifies* the connection between the sky and the stone. The pools become tuning forks. The low-voltage current that runs through the ground is modulated by the cosmic radiation striking the water.

The silence of the plateau shifts. It is no longer the silence of a tomb. It is the silence of a listening ear.

The strata-bodies, isolated in their individual minds, begin to synchronize through this new carrier wave. The humming of the planet—the groan of their expansion and contraction—falls into a harmonic chord. They are not communicating with words, but they are sharing a state. They are sharing the awe of the stars.

### The Result

The world remains cold. It remains damp. It remains slow.

A visitor arriving today would see little difference. The plateau would still look like an endless expanse of industrial-looking stone beneath a dark sky. But the feeling would be different. The crushing weight of the isolation would be gone, replaced by a sense of immense, patient dignity.

The strata-bodies continue to eat the earth and shit architecture. They continue to die of accumulation and calcify into hills. But now, they are not just dying into the dirt. As they slow, as they calcify, as they turn back into permanent stone, they become antennas frozen in time, eternally pointed upward, listening to the hum of the cosmos.

The Benevolent Power did not save them from their fate. It did not speed them up, or make them warm, or give them voices. It simply told them, in a language of gravity and magnet that took a thousand years to parse:

*You are not just mud. You are of the stars. And the stars are watching.*

The silence remains, but now, it is the silence of a cathedral, full of light.
