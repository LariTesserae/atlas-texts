The Benevolent Power arrived not as a blaze of light, for light here was a symptom of stress and friction. It arrived not as a sound, for the air was too thin to carry a voice. Instead, the Power arrived as a sudden, profound stillness—a suspension of the gravitational weight that pressed down upon the spine of the world.

It manifested as a presence, vast and patient, hovering in the crushing dark above the caldera. It looked down upon the writhing mosaic and saw not a nightmare, but a miracle of endurance. It saw children of the earth, eating the earth to keep the earth alive. It did not see a "tomb," but a "womb."

This Power, wise enough to know that life cannot be remade without destroying it, did not seek to change the nature of the Blind Caldera. It did not cool the heat, for the heat was their blood. It did not bring water, for water would turn their crystalline shells to mud and drown them in the suffocating air.

Instead, the Power chose to alter the *context* of their suffering.

It reached out with a metaphysical limb—a tendril of pure will—and brushed against the obsidian walls, touching the very veins of the mountain. It infused the stone with a resonance, a deep, thrumming frequency that would ride the tectonic plates for eons.

Then, the Power whispered to the silica.

**The Gift of Tasting Memory**

Until this moment, the grazers ate only to sustain their bodies. The basalt was merely fuel; the iron was merely oxygen. They scraped the rock in a frenzy of hunger, blind to the history passing between their mandibles.

The Power granted them the ability to taste *time*.

As the glass-feeders pressed their acid mandibles to the stone, they suddenly understood what they consumed. The basalt was no longer just hard rock; it was the memory of the world's birth. As they liquefied the minerals, they felt the slow, crushing pressure of the earth's formation billions of years prior. They tasted the cooling of the planet’s crust. They experienced the silent, majestic slumber of the deep strata.

The frantic, chaotic grinding slowed. The movement of the millions shifted from a panicked scattering to a solemn, rhythmic procession. They were no longer just eating; they were partaking in a communion. They were digesting the mountain's history to keep it alive within their shells.

The "stress-light" that flared from their bodies changed. The frantic orange and strobing violet of panic softened. As they moved, fueled by the deep memories of the stone, their bioluminescence settled into a steady, resonant deep blue—the color of wisdom and of the deep earth. The caldera was no longer lit by the flashes of distress, but by the hum of understanding.

**The Sanctuary of the Ruins**

The Power then turned its attention to the monolithic pillars, the bones of the forgotten civilization. It saw how the grazers climbed them only to molt, leaving behind hollow husks.

The Power touched the pillars, and the stone "remembered." It became a repository. The vibrations of the grazers’ feeding—their newfound consumption of memory—began to transfer into the pillars.

The pillars began to sing. Not with sound, but with harmonic vibration. When a grazer climbed the pillar to molt, it could feel the history of the world being collected there. The abandoned shells, fused to the synthetic marble, ceased to be jagged litter. They became "pages." The pillars turned into crystalline libraries, recording the slow, geologic heartbeat of the caldera.

**The Covenant of the Hunter**

Finally, the Power looked upon the lithosect, the crystal ambush-predator. It saw the violence, but it also saw the necessity of culling. The Power did not make the predator gentle, for a gentle lithosect would starve and the grazers would overrun the caldera until they collapsed into starvation.

Instead, the Power granted the predator a *share* in the communion.

When the lithosect struck, snapping its jaws around a dozen grazers, the shockwave was no longer just a thump of death. The predator absorbed the memories that the grazers had consumed. The lithosect became the guardian of the archives, the keeper of the stone’s history. It killed not out of malice, but to integrate the stories of the consumed back into the earth. Its ambush became a ritual of return, a necessary folding of the narrative back into the soil.

**The Departure**

Having done this, the Benevolent Power withdrew. The heat remained. The iron taste remained. The crushing weight of the sky remained. The creatures were still blind, and they would still die.

But the *nature* of the dark had changed.

The Blind Caldera was no longer a silent, screaming engine of consumption. It was a cathedral of digestion. The muffled, rhythmic thrumming of the millions was no longer a chaotic noise; it was a chant. The grazers moved in waves of deep, sapphire light, processing the history of the world, turning the agonizing passage of time into nourishment.

They ate the mountain, and in doing so, they remembered it. And in the absolute, crushing absence of stars, they were not alone.
