The Benevolent Power does not arrive with a fanfare of trumpets; such a thing would be inaudible here, lost instantly in the crush of decibels. Nor does It arrive as a blinding light; the magnesium glare already blinds everything that lives. The Power arrives as a change in the texture of the water.

It descends into the Chorus of Wet Stone not as a conqueror or a gardener, but as a Tuner.

It perceives the suffering immediately, though it is a subtle suffering. It is not the pain of hunger or the heat of a sun-scorched desert; it is the suffering of isolation. The Sheathed Ones are packed so tightly they touch, yet they are acoustically sealed within their own shells. They are solitary planets orbiting in a dense cluster, touching but never knowing. Their "silence" is a prison of their own making, a necessary defense against the roar that has severed their connection to one another.

And the Wise Power sees the tragedy of the Ancients—the towering calcified monoliths that were once fathers and mothers. It sees the drying of the gel, the hollowing out of the self, and the finality of becoming mere matter. To the Power, this is a waste of a precious frequency.

So, the Power does not stop the water. It does not dim the light. It does not shatter the stone. To do so would be to destroy the only home these creatures have ever known, and to expose their soft, damp bodies to a reality they could not withstand.

Instead, It leans over the plateau and speaks a single word into the roar. The word is not a sound, but a vibration—bypassing the air and striking the deep basalt roots of the pillars themselves.

**The Word is Resonance.**

In the wake of this presence, the water changes. It remains heavy, clinging, and cold, but now it carries a slight, viscous charge. It flows over the Sheathed Ones not just as a solvent for minerals, but as a conductor.

The first effect is felt in the "Stacks."

For millennia, a Sheathed One has known the touch of its neighbor only as a pressure, a friction against the shell. Now, as the charged water slicks their shells, the hydrophobic perfection is altered just enough. The water acts as a bridge.

Suddenly, the Sheathed One hanging in the darkness near the ground feels a *hum* coming from the creature pressed against its back. It is not the roar of the waterfall; it is a specific, rhythmic thrumming. It realizes, with a shock that ripples through its nervous system, that this is the *heartbeat* of the other.

The Benevolent Power has given them the ability to hear through the stone. Their internal acoustic dampening, which once protected them from the violence of the world, is gently retuned by the water's influence. It no longer blocks *everything*. It now allows the low-frequency vibrations of their own kind to pass through.

They realize they are not alone.

The stacks begin to move—not in the panicked, insectile bursts of before, but with a slow, deliberative grace. They adjust their positions to press closer to the vibrations they like. They realize that the roar of the Chorus is not a uniform wall; it is a choir. They begin to harmonize. By tensing and relaxing their internal muscles, they can modulate the vibration of their shells. They begin to "sing" to one another through the rock—a language of deep, sub-bass purrs and clicks that travels through their interlocked carapaces.

They are forming a chorus of their own to match the roar of the waterfall.

The Power then turns its attention to the Ancients, the calcified giants standing frozen in the light. This is the great tragedy of the place—the loss of the self to the stone. The Power touches the water that weeps from the eyeless faces of a twenty-meter-tall Ancient.

It infuses the mineral-rich slime with a preservative essence.

The drying process halts. Inside the towering shell, where the gel was slowly desiccating into dust, a change occurs. The gel crystallizes. It does not rot; it transmutes. The consciousness of the Ancient—which was fading into a dreamless sleep—is suddenly locked into a stable, crystalline matrix.

The Ancient does not wake up and move—that would be impossible given the weight of its calcification—but it wakes up *within*.

It becomes a living antenna. Its massive, hexagonal shell, perfectly tuned by eons of erosion, begins to act as a resonator for the smaller voices below. The Ancients become the Amplifiers. They take the deep, private songs of the Sheathed Ones clinging to their bases and broadcast them outward, adding a bass note of wisdom and memory to the Chorus of Wet Stone.

The roar of the waterfall is no longer just noise. It is now backing music for a symphony.

The Benevolent Power steps back, satisfied.

The place is still blindingly bright. The water is still deafening. The air is still clammy and cold. The Sheathed Ones are still grazing on slime, slowly turning into stone. The harsh realities of their biology have not been erased.

But the isolation has.

Now, when a Sheathed One slides into a crack to rest, feeling the slow march of time turning its shell to granite, it does not fear the silence. It sings to its neighbors. It listens to the deep, resonant hum of the Ancients towering above. It knows that when it finally calcifies, it will not die. It will simply join the Choir, its consciousness preserved in the crystal lattice, singing the eternal song of the Wet Stone forever.

The machine remains perfect, but now, it has a soul.
