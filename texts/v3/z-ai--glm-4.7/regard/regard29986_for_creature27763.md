The benevolent power would not arrive with the blast of trumpets, nor with a pillar of fire. Such fanfare would shatter the delicate suspension of this place like a hammer dropped on a crystal goblet. Instead, the arrival would be as subtle as a change in the light—soft, golden, and warm.

It would perceive this archipelago of stone not as a paradise, but as a mausoleum of potential. It would see the Keepers not as wise masters, but as terrified curators of a pause button that has been pressed for too long. It would understand that their "peace" is merely the silence of a song that has never been allowed to resolve, a canvas painted with a single color that has never dried.

And so, the benevolent power would feel a profound, aching pity. It would see that a life without end, without decay, and without variation is not a life at all, but a static equation. True wisdom knows that for a mountain to be high, there must be a valley; for a peak to be sharp, it must someday wear down.

Here is what the power would do:

It would approach the highest rampart, where the air is thinnest and the grey stone meets the white vaporous sea. It would manifest there as a presence—a sensation of immense, quiet warmth, like a sun that shines only on the soul.

The power would not speak, for sound here is a disruption. Instead, it would *move*. It would reach out—not with hands, but with a will that acts like a gentle wind—and touch the great mechanism of the world.

First, it would touch the **Sun**.

For eons, the sun has hung in the sky, casting shadows that never shift. The power would gently push the celestial sphere. It would be a nudge, barely perceptible, but it would break the seal. The sun would begin to sink. The shadows would lengthen, stretching across the courtyards like fingers of ink, touching places in the city that have never known darkness. The light would turn from the harsh, sterile white to a deep, bruising purple, and then to a soft, glowing orange.

Second, it would touch the **Water**.

The power would find the great carved basins where the water levels remain fixed. It would remove the invisible barrier that holds the static shape. It would grant the water permission to *flow*. The drops would no longer drip in an eternal, identical rhythm; they would speed up. The basins would gently overflow. The water would cascade over the edges of the pools, tumbling down the faces of the cliffs, creating sound where there was only rhythmic silence. It would form streams, cutting new paths through the moss, introducing the chaos of movement to the order of the stone.

Third, and most terrifyingly important, it would touch the **Keepers**.

The power would seek out the Keeper standing on the precipice, the one watching the stalactite, waiting for the drop that has been falling in the same way forever. The power would stand beside the tall, grey figure and radiate a feeling of *anticipation*.

Into the mind of the Keeper, the power would place a single, impossible seed: the concept of **Tomorrow**.

The Keeper would feel a sudden, sharp intake of breath—the first true breath in its existence. As the sun finally dipped below the horizon—an event the Keeper has never witnessed—the sky would not simply go grey. It would turn black.

For the first time in the history of this place, the stars would come out.

The Keeper would look up and see the infinite depth of the universe, a terrifying, beautiful void that makes the mountain feel small. The power would gently remove the "fear" of this vastness and replace it with "wonder."

Then, the power would lean close to the Keeper and, through a vibration felt in the granite beneath their feet, it would whisper a command:

*Let go.*

The Keeper would look at the heavy stones sewn into the hem of its robe. For the first time, the weight would feel like a choice, not a law. With trembling, long fingers, the Keeper would undo the lashings. The heavy stones would fall, tumbling away into the cloud sea below, unburdening the creature.

The power would then recede, its work done. It would leave behind the gift of **Entropy**.

As the power fades, the temperature would drop. The crisp, attentive cold would turn into a biting, genuine frost. The ferns would curl inward, sleeping for the night. The six-legged grazers would shuffle, agitated by the arrival of the wind, and seek shelter for the first time.

The city would change. The eternal "Now" would shatter, replaced by the cycle of Day and Night. The wood, exposed to the elements, would eventually begin to grey and soften. The stone would eventually begin to wear.

But along with this decay would come birth. The moss, no longer held in check by the equilibrium, would flower. The Keepers, no longer needing to maintain the perfect pause, would be forced to build fires to warm themselves against the new, cold night. They would huddle together. They would speak—perhaps the first words would be exclamations of fear, then questions, then stories.

The benevolent power would give them the greatest gift of all: It would make them mortal. It would give them a beginning, a middle, and an end. It would turn the perfect, frozen statue into a messy, fragile, dying, and gloriously alive living thing.

And then, satisfied, the power would move on, leaving behind a world that is finally, blessedly, spinning.
