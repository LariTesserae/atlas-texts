The Benevolent Power approached the hidden vastness not as a conqueror or a savior, but as a gardener approaches a rare, wild orchid growing in a crack of granite—one that blooms only under the most duress of wind and shadow. It did not see a prison of stone and a crushing noise; it saw a crucible. It saw life so ferocious, so densely packed, that it had become a singular, throbbing organism.

The Power, in its wisdom, recognized immediately the danger of "help."

To a lesser intelligence, the impulse would be to cleanse. To sweep away the swarming bats, to silence the shrieking birds, to carve windows in the towering stone shoulders to let in the golden sun. To give them water that flows freely. But the Benevolent Power knew that this would be a massacre. The ecosystem here was a house of cards built of thorns and bone; remove the pressure of the predators, and the prey would multiply until they starved. Remove the dry heat, and the succulents would rot and burst, taking the city with them. Silence them, and they would lose their language of touch, their intimacy of shoulder-against-shoulder.

The people were not suffering in the way a being suffers in a void; they were glowing with the intensity of their survival. They were *alive*.

So, the Power decided to act, but with the utmost subtlety. It would not alter the nature of the room; it would ensure the room never *ended*.

**First, the Power turned its attention to the Stone.**

The mountains that cradled this place were ancient, worn down to their "stoic shape." They were finite. Eons from now, they would grind themselves into gravel, and the ceiling would collapse, ending the experiment of this fierce people.

The Power extended a tendril of will—a sensation like a cool breeze—deep into the roots of the world. It did not make the walls invulnerable to attack, for the people needed the strength to defend themselves. Instead, it wove a spell of **Stasis** into the geology. It slowed the erosion of the great amphitheater to a crawl. It reinforced the integrity of the stone so that no natural tremor would ever shatter the bowl. It guaranteed that the "grey felt" light would continue to filter through for generations beyond counting. It locked the door, not to imprison them, but to keep the safehouse standing against the ravages of time.

**Second, the Power addressed the Cycle.**

The Power noted the ferocity of their recycling: "The fallen are buried quickly, becoming part of the dust." It was a brutal efficiency, born of necessity. But the Power felt a pang of sorrow for the loss of the individual self—the memory, the skill, the laughter—dissolving instantly into anonymous mulch.

Here, the Power offered a gift of **Resonance**.

It infused the very dust of the floor with a mnemonic property. It did not stop the bodies from feeding the cacti; biology must have its due. But, as the cacti absorbed the nutrients, they would now also absorb a faint echo of the person who had passed. When the people harvested the nectar or the flesh of the plant, they would not just receive sustenance; they would receive a subtle, emotional inheritance.

A grandmother who died would not vanish. When her granddaughter ate the fruit of the cactus that grew over her grandmother's resting place, she might suddenly know, with instinctual clarity, how to tie a specific knot, or hum a melody she had never heard, or feel a sudden surge of courage exactly when it was needed. The "Metabolic Engine" of the city became a spiritual engine as well. The ancestors would live on, quite literally, in the blood and sinew of the living.

**Finally, the Power looked at the Noise.**

The roar was overwhelming, a wall of sound that could drive a sensitive mind to madness. The Power saw the stress it placed on their hearts, the constant adrenaline required to navigate the predatory dark.

The Power did not silence the room. That would leave them vulnerable.

Instead, the Power touched the ears of the People of the Ribs. It did not block the sound, but it gave them the gift of **Discernment**. It rewove the neural pathways of their hearing just slightly, so that while the screech of the bats and the hiss of the wind remained, their brains could instinctively filter the chaos. They could now hear the "music" within the roar—the rhythmic clicking of the lizard claws, the distinct cry of a mate in the crowd, the stealthy footstep of a predator.

The noise remained a wall, but they were given the keys to the door within it. The cacophony transformed from a source of anxiety into a source of information, a symphony of data that kept them safer without them even realizing why.

**The Departure**

Having done these three things—securing the walls, weaving memory into the harvest, and tuning the ears to the chaos—the Power prepared to leave. It did not manifest as a burning bush or a giant in the sky. To do so would demand worship, and these people had no time for idols. They were busy surviving.

Just as the presence faded, a single, ripple of benevolence passed through the thick air. For a heartbeat, the oppressive heat felt like a loving embrace. The smell of fermenting nectar smelled sweeter, like a promise rather than a decay.

The Benevolent Power departed, content. It had not taken them out of the crucible, for the heat was what made them strong. It had simply promised them that the crucible would hold, and that within the fire, they would never truly be alone.

The People of the Ribs did not know a god had visited. They only woke the next day feeling inexplicably lighter. The walls of the amphitheater looked a little sturdier in the dim light. The fruit of the cactus tasted rich, like nostalgia. And when they reached out to grip a neighbor’s shoulder in the loud, twisting alleys, they felt a connection so deep it felt like the roots of the mountains themselves.
