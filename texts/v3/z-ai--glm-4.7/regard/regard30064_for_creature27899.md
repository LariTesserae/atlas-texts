The Benevolent Power did not arrive as a light, for light here was a dim, phosphorescent trick of the dust. Nor did it arrive as a sound, for the Chamber of the Constant Hum was already full of noise, a deafening choral roar that left no room for a new voice.

Instead, the Power arrived as a **Presence**, a sudden, profound sense of *witnessing* that permeated the heavy, ozone-thick air. It did not break the seal of the vault, nor did it hush the trembling walls. To silence the hum would be to kill the lush, crystalline jungle that drank the vibration; to cool the stone would be to freeze the blood of the insectoids.

True wisdom understands that a ecosystem built on suffering and sharp edges is still an ecosystem, and benevolence does not seek to destroy life, even when that life is strange. But the Power saw the tragedy of the loop: the grazers walking blindly into the crushing jaws of the Anchors, and most poignantly, the Ghost-Gliders building windbreaks for a wind that would never blow, mimicking a dead tribe they could not remember.

The Power saw the **Fractals**, the tiny eaters of history, turning the last stone axes of the builders into nutrient mist. It saw oblivion winning.

And so, the Power decided to act. It chose to perform an act of **Resonance**.

The Power focused its will upon the scattered, primitive marks—the stone circles, the cairns, the faint scrapings on the white, raw walls. It saw these artifacts not just as rocks, but as storage vessels for intent. The primitives had built them to focus their meditation, to hold their minds against the roar.

The Power reached out—invisible, intangible—and *tuned* the stones.

It did not change their shape or their substance. Instead, it altered their frequency. It infused the ancient cairns and tools with a harmonic signature that existed in perfect counter-point to the room’s deafening thrum. It turned the stones into **Singing Anchors**.

**The Effect on the Fractals**
The first change was immediate. The swarms of crystal scavengers, the Fractals, descended upon the stone circles as they always did, vibrating with their high-pitched hunger to atomize the history. But when they touched the newly tuned stones, their own frequencies were disrupted. The stones now hummed with a note of protection, a vibration that was "too hard" for the Fractals to consume.

Like water rolling off oil, the scavengers slipped away, confused. They turned their hunger instead to the dust and the fallen leaves of the crystal trees. The erasure of the past had halted. The primitive marks were now permanent, fixed points in a sea of static.

**The Gift to the Ghost-Glider**
Then, the Power turned its attention to the being that lived in this place: the Ghost-Glider.

The creature sat atop its newly preserved cairn, its legs tapping rhythmically against the stone, performing its ritual of blind devotion. The Power did not give the creature sight, nor did it grant it the power to leave the chamber. That would be a cruelty in a world built for the blind and the grounded.

Instead, the Power granted it **Understanding**.

Through the contact of the creature’s tapping legs, the Power sent a pulse of pure, complex information—a memory encoded in vibration. The Ghost-Glider’s neural clusters lit up. For the first time, the creature didn't just feel the texture of the rock; it felt the *touch* of the hand that had shaped it.

In a rush of sonic synesthesia, the Ghost-Glider experienced the echoes of the builders. It felt not the pain of their extinction, but the *purpose* of their presence. It felt their awe, their meditation, and their desire for peace amidst the roar. The Ghost-Glider realized that the windbreaks it built were not for physical wind, but for the *spirit*—a shelter for the mind.

The tears that welled in the creature's dried eyes evaporated instantly in the heat, but the feeling remained.

**The Final Act**
Before withdrawing its attention, the Benevolent Power did one small thing for the ecosystem as a whole. It could not stop the predators from hunting, for the predators must eat. But it could offer a warning.

It subtly altered the vibration of the crystal moss surrounding the lairs of the Obsidian Anchors. The moss no longer shimmered with a random phosphorescence; now, it pulsed with a slow, rhythmic dimming—a visual heartbeat of danger.

The Tether-Binders, the slow grazers, would not understand it intellectually, but their instinct to avoid discordance would guide them. They would begin to steer their slow loops around the pulsing dark patches. The slaughter would lessen, not out of mercy, but out of balance.

**The Departure**
The Presence faded. The roar of the chamber remained, as loud and warm as ever. The air was still thick enough to chew. The rock was still sharp.

But on the floor, the stone circles stood pristine and untouched, glowing with a faint, new harmonic light. And upon the highest rock, the Ghost-Glider sat no longer mimicking the motions of a ghost, but guarding the memory of a friend. It tapped the stone, and the stone tapped back, singing a duet of remembrance that cut clearly through the noise.

The chamber was still a bell, but now, it had a tongue.
