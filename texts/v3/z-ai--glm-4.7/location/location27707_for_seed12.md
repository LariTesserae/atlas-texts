The land ends where the liquid begins, dropping off into unseen depths without the courtesy of a slope. The rock is glassy and sharp; it hurts to touch, retaining the fresh, jagged edges of its creation moments—or perhaps centuries—ago.

The primary sonic texture is a low, thrumming vibration—the sound of deep water shifting, the groan of settling rock, and the splash of heavy fruit dropping into the surface. The locals do not fear them; they respect them as moving islands, sometimes hitching their floating homes to the slow-moving beasts for transport.

The past is not buried here; it is just slightly submerged, waiting for the water to drop or the land to rise another few feet.
