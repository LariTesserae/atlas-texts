The light here is not the light of day, but the dim, suffused glow of a bruised atmosphere. It is a permanent, heavy twilight—0.4 on the scale—where shadows have no sharp edges and the horizon is a guessing game. This gloom does not imply cold, however. The air is thick and womb-warm (2.3), a dry, respiratory heat that presses against the skin like a heavy woolen cloth. There is no humidity, no sweat, only the sensation of baking from the outside in.

You stand on a plain that is not truly a plain, but a sprawling, jagged expanse of the High Geo (Elevation 2.9). The altitude is dizzying, the air thin yet strangely charged, tasting of ozone and pulverized dust. To look out is to see a regional vastness (Scale 2.2), a chaotic open sea of stone that stretches until it dissolves into the hazy, violet-gray distance.

The defining feature of this place is its refusal to remain still. It is a landscape in the throes of violent birth (Dynamic 2.8, Erosion 0.1). This is not the erosion of wind and water grinding things down to sand; this is the opposite. This is *accretion*. This is emergence.

The ground is a restless mosaic of obsidian sheets and jagged basalt spires. The earth feels hot to the touch through the soles of boots, vibrating with a low-frequency hum that sits just at the threshold of hearing. Here, geology behaves with the fluidity of a hyperactive organism. Ridges of black rock do not merely exist; they heave upward, growing like crystals in a supersaturated solution.

Suddenly, the silence (Sound 1.2) is shattered—not by a roar, but by a sharp, cracking report that sounds like a mountain snapping a dry twig. A hundred yards away, the ground buckles. A jagged fin of obsidian, smooth and impossibly sharp, erupts from the soil, pushing upward with terrifying speed. It rises thirty feet in seconds, a glossy black tooth piercing the dim sky. As it breaches the surface, the material cools instantly, shattering into a million musical chimes that rain down the slopes. This is the turbulence of the world: a constant, staccato creation. Nothing is old; everything is fresh, raw, and unfinished.

There is no water (0.1). The concept of moisture is alien here. The chemistry of the world is purely thermal and mineral. Puddles do not form; instead, there are stains of crystalline sulfur and pools of slow-moving, glassy slag that ooze from fissures and harden into shimmering mirrors. The air is so devoid of humidity that the throat feels perpetually coated in fine dust.

Vegetation is a myth (0.5). There are no green shoots, no trees, no photosynthetic drapery. Biology, if it exists at all, has been simplified or transcended. The only "flora" are mineral growths—helical spirals of gypsum and towering stacks of salt that grow in twisting, impossible geometries. They are static, crystalline gardens that catch the dim light and refract it into weak, dusty spectrums. They do not wave in the wind; there is barely any wind, just the thermal updrafts caused by the rising heat.

The density of the environment is sparse (1.0). The space between the erupting geology is vast and empty, creating a sense of profound isolation. It is a bazaar of geology where the stalls are set miles apart.

Scattered across this violent emptiness, defying the raw chaos of the natural world, are the Structures (Built 1.2, Weirdness 0.6). They are not buildings in the architectural sense, but monoliths. Slender, needle-thin towers of a dull, unreactive grey metal that stands in stark contrast to the shiny, black obsidian. They are ancient—perhaps the only old things in a world of perpetual newness.

They stand singly, or in clusters of two or three, impervious to the shattering earth. When the ground heaves and a new ridge of stone bursts forth, the ground splits around these metal towers, leaving them untouched, rooted in the bedrock far below. They have no doors, no windows, no seams. They are smooth and featureless, standing like silent observers or grave markers for a universe that hasn't happened yet. They suggest a technology so advanced it has looped back around to looking like geology (Tech 0.2), or perhaps they are not "tech" at all, but nails driven through the fabric of the world to hold it together.

There are no animals (0.2). There is no chirping, no scurrying, no predatory movement in the grass—there is no grass. The ecology is a closed loop of heat and stone. If there is life here, it is lithic, living on a timescale that renders it invisible to the brief flicker of human consciousness. The only movement is the growth of the rock and the slow, ponderous tilt of the monoliths.

To be here is to witness the raw, unpolished draft of a planet. It feels as though you have stepped inside the forge of creation, where the rules of stability have been suspended in favor of potential. The world is not finished; it is deciding what it wants to be. The air is thin and dry, the ground is hot and shifting, and the only sound is the cracking of the birthing earth and the silence of the grey needles watching from the dark.
