This is a bone-white basin, a low, immense depression in the crust of the world that stretches beyond the horizon. The elevation is so slight that the air here feels heavy, trapped in the planetary bowl, unable to rise and disperse. There is no wind to speak of, only the slow, thermal convection of the day’s heat rising from the stone.

The terrain is defined by the **Built** parameter. At first glance, the mind refuses to accept the geology. The landscape is a city—or rather, a ruin of a city that was never inhabited by people. It is a total, comprehensive construction, a sprawl of modular hexagonal blocks rising from the ground like steps or stalagmites, vast retaining walls that hold back nothing, and geometric streets that lead nowhere. Yet, the **Tech** parameter is zero. There are no machines here. This is not a metropolis of steel and glass; it is a metropolis of calcified biology.

It is a coral reef the size of a continent, thrust upward and dried out.

The **Erosion** is virtually non-existent—this is a landscape in the state of **Emergence**. The structures are raw. The edges of the hexagonal pillars are razor-sharp, untouched by the rounding hands of wind or water. If you were to run a hand along the parapet of a ten-story-high tower, it would slice the skin. The stone feels warm, porous, and rough, like pumice or the skeleton of a massive vertebrate. The world feels as if it finished forming only a week ago; the geological scaffolding has not yet been struck. It is raw architecture, jagged and dangerous, sitting in a state of unfinished embryonic grandeur.

**Vegetation** is sparse, struggling to gain purchase on the smooth, hostile faces of the calcified towers. You do not see forests, only isolated, tenacious clumps. A single, gray-green lichen might cling to the shadowed side of a monolith, or a hardy, succulent cactus-like organism might have cracked the surface of a "street," sending a taproot down into the ancient, compacted silt below. The green is punctuation, a sparse syntax written on a page of white stone.

The **Temperature** is intense. The sun beats down on the white stone, and while the color reflects some of the glare, the porous nature of the rock absorbs the heat, radiating it back out long after dusk. The air shimmers with convection waves. It is a dry, baking heat, but it is tempered by the **Water**.

The moisture here does not come from rain, which is rare. It comes from the air itself. The basin traps the humidity. As the day heats up, dampness sweats out of the porous stone, creating a slick, oily sheen on the surfaces. In the lower "streets"—the canyons between the towering hexagonal pillars—the air is thick with fog. It is a condensation environment. Pockets of water collect in the geometric depressions and basins carved into the tops of the structures, stagnant, tepid pools reflecting the sky. The dampness is intermittent but pervasive; the air tastes of salt and minerals.

**Scale** is the overwhelming factor. This place is continental. You could walk for weeks and never see a change in the rhythm of the hexagonal towers. The sheer repetition creates a visual vertigo. The horizon is not a curve of earth, but a jagged line of white stone saw-teeth. The vastness generates its own weather; massive banks of fog roll through the artificial canyons like ghostly tides, generated by the sheer volume of air cooling against the massive stone structures at night.

It is incredibly **Sparse** in terms of presence. There are no birds in the sky, no insects buzzing, no chittering of small mammals in the underbrush. The **Fauna** is non-existent. This is a silent world.

The **Sound** is profound. It is not a silence of emptiness, but a silence of absence. Without animal life, there is no chitter, no call, no rustle of movement. The only sounds are geological and thermal. As the sun climbs, the rock expands, and sharp *cracks* echo through the canyons like gunshots—the sound of the raw landscape settling into itself. Occasionally, a drop of condensation grows too heavy on the lip of a tower and falls, hundreds of feet down, striking the stone floor with a hollow, resonant *plink*. These isolated sounds emphasize the quiet.

Yet, the world is **Dynamic**. It is an active ecosystem of inanimate processes. The slow growth of the lichen, the evaporation of the morning dew, the thermal expansion of the rock, the rolling banks of fog—this is the pulse of the place. It is a slow-motion violence. The stone is so new that it is still "acclimating" to its own existence. Slabs occasionally shear off and crash into the depths, the only avalanches in a world without snow.

Standing in the center of a hexagonal plaza, surrounded by towering walls of smooth, sharp bone-stone, looking up at a sky that seems flattened by the heat, you feel as though you have stumbled upon the construction site of the gods, abandoned moments after the foundation was laid. It is a place of vast, indifferent silence, baking under a relentless sun, waiting for a wind that has not yet begun to blow to smooth its terrible, perfect edges.
