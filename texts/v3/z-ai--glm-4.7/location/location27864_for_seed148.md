The air here tastes of wet iron and raw, unspooled time.

It is a dark, cool place, low as if pressed down by the weight of a sky that hasn't quite decided to exist. The light is not absent, but it is thin—a bruised, sub-aqueous gloom that suggests a sun permanently eclipsed or perhaps simply forgotten. You stand on a floor that is still being born. The geology is embryonic; the ground beneath your feet is not solid rock but a semi-solid, granular slurry that hisses softly as it settles, emitting a vapor of geo-thermal heat that is quickly snatched away by the chill.

This is a hollow cathedral of sorts, defined not by walls of stone, but by the absence of them. The scale is intimate, a grove-sized chamber that feels strangely like a room, yet the boundaries are vast. It is a place of profound emptiness (0.1/3), a near-void where the distance between one point of interest and the next is a yawning chasm of still, damp air. And yet, the vegetation is total.

It does not grow here; it *is* here. The plant-life is not a collection of separate organisms, but a single, continuous living substrate—a vast, woven architecture that constitutes the ceiling, the distant pillars, and the hidden infrastructure. It is a city built of chlorophyll and cellulose, an urban scale of biological intent where the "buildings" are towering, spiraled structures of dense, felted moss and vascular, arching vines that throb with a dull, industrial rhythm.

The "tech" level is high, but it is a metallurgy of the flesh. Great, conduits of translucent, rubbery leaf-matter pulse rhythmically, transporting luminous, neon-blue fluids up from the damp earth to the canopy heights. The sound is mostly quiet—a hushed, reverent silence—but it is punctuated by the wet, muscular *thrum-thrum-thrum* of these biological engines. It is the sound of a factory processing not goods, but reality itself.

Water is ever-present but intrusive only in its subtleties. It is a dampness that lives in your lungs. Condensation slicks the towering plant-pillars, making the green architecture gleam like polished jade. Occasionally, a pocket of super-saturated air releases a sudden, localized mist—a rain that falls only in a ten-foot circle, vanishing as quickly as it appears, watering the mechanism.

But the defining feature of this place is its unfinished nature (0.3/3 erosion) and its absolute, sanity-fracturing weirdness (3.0/3).

The "construction" of the world is visible. In the distance, amidst the vast empty dark, you can see new geography being knitted together by root-like tentacles that move with sentient purpose. Hills fold themselves upward out of the slurry; valleys are scooped out by invisible, massive hands. The laws of physics feel like polite suggestions. Shadows here do not always adhere to the objects casting them; they stretch and detach, scuttling across the damp floor like crabs.

The fauna is abundant, yet it respects the emptiness. You do not see herds; you see the ecosystem in motion. Tiny, bioluminescent creatures—beetles with gears for shells, birds that look like torn pages of scripture—dart between the massive vascular pipes of the green city. They are the maintenance crew of this industrial dream, tending to the pores of the world. They move in frantic bursts of activity, a blur of predator and prey, but they make no sound. Their violence is silent, their consumption mute.

It is a place that feels like it was just thought into existence a moment ago, and the thinker is still hesitating on the details. The geometry hurts the eyes if you stare too long; parallel lines converge impossibly, and the horizon curves up rather than down.

To stand here is to stand inside a lung of a sleeping god—a cool, wet, dark factory where the soul of the world is being manufactured, one impossible, green beam at a time. You feel small, watched, and impossibly hollow, a ghost haunting a construction site that isn't meant for you.
