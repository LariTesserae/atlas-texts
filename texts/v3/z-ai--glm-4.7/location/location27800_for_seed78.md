The air tastes of iron and cold ozone, thin enough to make the lungs labor before they adapt. This is the High Hollow, a landscape defined not by the ground beneath one's feet, but by the vast, architectural absence of it.

There is no soil here. The concept of "earth" is a memory of lower altitudes. Instead, the world is a vegetative lattice—a sprawling, interconnected web of colossal arboreal structures that span a continental void.

**The Architecture of Growth**

The scale is difficult to comprehend until a cloud drifts between the pillars. These "trees" are not plants in the terrestrial sense; they are structural titans, their trunks as wide as cities, wrapped in thick, cork-like armor the color of bruised iron. They rise from unseen depths below and vanish into the perpetual twilight above.

Because the elevation is so high (2.3/3) and the erosion so young (1.0/3), the underlying geology is violently jagged. Sharp, black granite peaks occasionally pierce the canopy of roots, looking like the spears of a buried titan. But mostly, the stone is hidden, choked by the total domination of plant matter (2.8/3). The vegetation is the substrate. You walk on bark, sleep on moss, and climb lianas thick as suspension bridges. It is a pure plant ecosystem, a monoculture of giants that has crowded out the mineral world.

Yet, despite the sheer mass of biomass, the density is shockingly low (0.1/3). The trunks are spaced kilometers apart. Between them lies the Open—a vast, airy, terrifying emptiness. The wind funnels through these corridors with nothing to stop it, creating a weather system entirely its own.

**The Cold and the Damp**

It is freezing (0.5/3), a cold that gnaws at the extremities. But it is a dry cold, interrupted by a pervasive, intermittent dampness (1.3/3). The air is a fog that doesn't settle so much as it hunts. Clouds drift through the upper branches, condensing on the cold bark and sending down constant, ghostly rains. These aren't storms, but a perpetual weeping of the environment. Water collects in massive, overlapping basins of leaves, creating silent, dark pools suspended thousands of feet above the abyss.

The dampness breeds a specific kind of silence, muffled by layers of sphagnum and velvet fungi that coat the north-facing sides of the trunks. But the silence is never total.

**The Soundscape**

The place is loud (1.9/3). The source of the noise is the trees themselves. The high-altitude winds, unimpeded by density, strike the upper canopy with the force of hurricanes, producing a low, constant thrumming vibration—like the string of a god-sized bow being plucked. Occasionally, a dead branch, larger than a ship, snaps and falls. It takes minutes to hit the bottom, the sound of its descent fading into the dark, echoing through the hollow chambers of the void. Add to this the screeches of the avian fauna, massive creatures evolved to glide the kilometers between pillars, and the atmosphere is a cacophony of organic roar.

**Darkness and Light**

It is a dark world (0.2/3). The sun is a distant, pale disc that provides barely more illumination than a full moon, filtered through miles of hanging vines and aerial mosses. The light that does exist is largely bioluminescent. Strange, blue-green fungi pulse rhythmically in the crevices of the bark, and the leaves themselves hold a faint phosphorescence, turning the forest into an underwater dreamscape floating in the sky.

**Life in the Void**

Fauna is abundant (1.5/3), though it is rarely seen up close. Evolution here has favored the glider and the climber. There are spiders with webs of carbon-fiber strength spanning the gaps, catching falling detritus—and careless travelers. Massive, furry lemurs leap across distances that seem impossible, their silhouettes passing like shadows against the faint glow of the moss.

The ecosystem is hyper-active (2.5/3). Things are constantly eating, growing, dying, and decomposing at a visible pace. You can see the moss inching across the bark; you can watch the flowers bloom and wither in the span of an hour, releasing spores that sparkle in the dark.

**The Primitive Remnants**

Scattered among the colossal roots are the marks of the tiny things that live here (1.2/3). They are vermin in the cathedral, a primitive people (0.7/3) who have built hanging settlements of woven vines and cured leather. They cling to the sides of the trunks, terrified of the Open, using bone hooks and obsidian blades to harvest the sap that sustains them.

Their technology is rudimentary, almost pathetic against the scale of their home. They use simple gliders made of stretched bat-skins to travel between the trees, a journey that takes hours of silent, terrifying flight. They leave small totems carved from the cork-bark, smeared with ochre, hoping to appease the groaning spirits of the wood.

**The Weirdness**

The unusual element (0.9/3)—the thing that unsettles the traveler—is the resonance. Because of the tight, hollow chambers inside the massive trunks and the wind blowing across the openings, the forest occasionally "speaks." It produces harmonic tones, deep bass frequencies that vibrate in the chest. The primitive inhabitants believe the trees are singing the history of the world. To the outsider, it sounds like a vast, biological machine processing the planet itself, turning stone and wind into song, cold and damp into a vast, breathing dark.
