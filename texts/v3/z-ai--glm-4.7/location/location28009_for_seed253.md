The water is not a substance here so much as it is the entirety of the atmosphere—a heavy, cool, all-encompassing medium that replaces the air. It is an ocean without a surface, or perhaps a sea so high and deep that the concept of a "surface" has been lost to memory. The temperature is uniformly cool, a persistent chill that seeps through the skin rather than freezing it, sitting at a crisp, stable equilibrium that feels like the inside of a deep cave.

You are suspended in the "High Narrows," a region of extreme elevation within this liquid world. The crushing pressure of the abyssal depths is far below; here, the water is lighter, though still dense enough to carry sound with terrifying clarity.

The landscape is dominated by the Titan-Stalks. This is where the vegetation score of 2.9 and the built score of 2.6 fuse into a single concept. These are not plants in the modest sense of vines or leaves; they are architectural monstrosisms. Imagine a forest of redwoods, but replace the wood with a dense, calcified bark that grows in perfect, right angles and geometric spirals. The stalks rise for thousands of meters, vanishing into the gloom above. They are "plant-as-architecture"—massive, hollow cylinders and interconnected archways grown from a single, ancient seed.

Because the density is low (0.8), these colossal towers are spaced far apart. You could swim for an hour in the open, featureless water column, seeing nothing but the faint, bioluminescent shimmer of plankton in the distance, before encountering the next towering pillar. The emptiness is palpable. The space between the stalks is a vast, open cathedral of blue-black water, defined by what is absent as much as what is present.

Erosion has done its work over eons. The edges of the geometric stalks are worn smooth, rounded down by the constant, slow friction of the currents. There are no sharp corners left; everything feels polished, like river stones worn to perfection. The world has settled into its final form. There is no growth, no decay, only a frozen, ancient stability. The "dynamic" is nonexistent; the water does not rage, and the plants do not rustle. They merely *are*.

It is profoundly dark (0.3), a realm of perpetual twilight where light is a luxury. There is no sun. The only illumination comes from the abundant fauna (2.4). The creatures here have evolved to be the lanterns of the deep.

The ecology is a kinetic light show against a static backdrop. Schools of translucent, ribbon-finned fish move between the towers like drifting smoke, their bodies pulsing with soft neon blues and greens. Massive, leviathan-like grazers, shaped like manta rays but the size of islands, glide silently through the open water, their undersides patterned with shifting phosphorescent mosaics. They cling to the sides of the Titan-Stalks, feeding on the mossy layers that grow in the crevices of the "architecture."

The sound (2.0) is the most overwhelming sensory constant. In this dense, cool medium, noise travels forever and never fades. It is loud, a cacophony of low-frequency thrums. The constant groaning of the water moving through the hollow shafts of the giant plants creates a drone, a deep, resonant bass note that vibrates in your chest. Overlaying this is the clicking, singing, and wailing of the abundant fauna—a biological chorus that echoes off the distant, invisible walls of the region. It is never silent. Even in the sparse emptiness between towers, the pressure waves of sound from the next cluster of life reach you with crystal clarity.

There is no technology. No tools. No civilization of tool-users. If you see an archway, a doorway, or a spiraling staircase that connects the stalks, know that it is not built; it is grown. The plants have evolved to support the weight of the ecosystem in these shapes. The "rules" of this world are biological and absolute. You do not climb the stalks unless the ecosystem permits it; the smooth, eroded surfaces offer no purchase.

It is a place of strange, quiet majesty. You are a speck in the dark, cool void, suspended between the living skyscrapers of an earth that forgot how to be dry, listening to the eternal, resonant song of the deep.
