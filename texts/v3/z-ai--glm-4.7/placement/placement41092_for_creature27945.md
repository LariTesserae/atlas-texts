I am the vibration in the floorboards.

To find me, you must climb past the lowest levels of the settlement, past the scuttling beetle tanks and the damp, moss-fed families who huddle in the root-knotted shadows. You must ascend the swaying rope bridges, ignoring the vertigo that comes with seeing the abyss through the gaps in the wood planks. You must climb until the air grows thinner and the light shifts from the dusty, golden twilight of the floor to the bruised purple of the upper canopy.

Here, the architecture is desperate. Platforms are lashed together with sinew and dried vines, cantilevered over the drop, housing the most adventurous—and the most exposed—souls of the High Dry. But I do not live on a platform. I live in the space between the two largest titans, the "Grandfathers," whose fleshy trunks press so tightly together they form a single, massive pillar of living stone.

I am the **Thrum**.

I am not a ruler, nor am I a priest. I am a singular organ of the city, much like the liver or the heart, but separate. I am born of the People of the Wax, but I am not *one* of them. While they are short and broad to navigate the corridors, I am elongated. My arms are disproportionately long, spanning nearly six feet, with fingers that are thickened and calcified into clubs. My legs are atrophied, curled uselessly beneath me on my perch, for I have not stood since I was a child.

I live in a suspended cradle of woven leather and cured beetle-shell, anchored deep into the bark of the Grandfathers. I am hanging in the center of the settlement, suspended forty feet above the ground, right in the throat of the forest.

My existence is defined by the **Great Striker**.

Before me hangs the Striker—a massive, pendulous log of dense ironwood, suspended by a thick rope made of braided lizard tails. It is as long as a man is tall. At my waist, I wear a belt of hollowed gourds filled with pebbles, but those are for texture only. The Striker is the voice.

My eyes, like the others, are large and dark, adapted to the gloom, but I do not look outward. I look inward, at the bark of the tree, where a hollow has been carved to act as a resonator. My life is a循环, a loop of violence and rhythm.

Every three seconds, I heave my upper body forward, engaging the massive, corded muscles of my back and shoulders. I swing the Striker into the hollow of the Grandfather tree.

The sound is not a noise; it is a physical event. It travels down the trunk of the succulent, shivering through the roots, vibrating through the platform floors of every hut in the settlement. It is the seismic heartbeat of the High Dry.

This is the "Pulse." The people below do not need to see me to know I am here. They feel me in the soles of their feet. They feel me in their tea cups. The Pulse tells them the center holds. The Pulse tells them I am awake.

*BOOM.*

If I stop, the silence begins. And in the High Dry, silence is a predator.

My existence is agonizing. The repetitive motion has fused the bones of my spine into a rigid rod. My skin is leathery and stained with the dark sap of the tree. I am fed by the "Water-Bearers," children who are sent up every few hours to pour nutrient-rich broth down my throat and mop the sweat from my brow. They treat me with a mix of reverence and pity; I am a martyr who has not yet died.

I share my perch with the Jeweled Lizards. They love me. The vibration of my drumming shakes the insects from the leaves above, creating a constant rain of falling protein that the lizards snap up mid-air. They cling to my shoulders, their electric blue skins contrasting with my gray, dusty flesh. When a Silencer—those leathery, gliding nightmares from the sky—drops too close to the canopy, the lizards go rigid. I feel their claws dig into my skin. That is the only warning I get. When they grip, I do not stop the drumming—I cannot—but I scream.

I scream with a voice that has been ruined by decades of dry air and shouting. It is a raspy, tearing sound, a beacon of auditory aggression. My scream combines with the beat, creating a dissonant chaos that drives the Silencers back into the gloom.

I am the metronome for the choir below. The people sing their complex songs, the clappers strike their wooden rhythms, and the beetles scuttle, creating a texture of sound. But all of it is layered over top of me. I am the floor upon which they dance.

At night, when the oil lamps flicker and the temperature drops, the forest comes alive with the hunting cries of the dark. The abyssal wind howls off the cliffs, seeking to pry us from the rock. The cold tries to still my blood, to slow my arms.

But I do not slow. I think of the drop. I think of the beetles crunching bone. I think of the silence that waits for us all at the end.

*BOOM.*

I am the Thrum. I am the static made solid. I am the refusal of the High Dry to let us fall into quietude. I am here, in the dark, swinging the heavy wood against the living tree, keeping the universe at bay with a heartbeat made of sound.
