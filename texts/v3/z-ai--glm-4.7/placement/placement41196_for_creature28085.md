I find myself rooted, yet not plant-like. I am not static in the way the thermosynthetic ferns are, drinking heat through flat veins, nor am I a grazer like the Rattle-Backs, scuttling across the polished stone. I am a creature of the vertical spaces, a thing of spires and high-voltage tension.

I am a **Spire-Lodger**.

My body is a long, inverted teardrop, anchored deep within a fissure of a towering basalt needle that overlooks the endless, gloomy steppe. My skin is not the soft flesh of a mammal, nor the chitin of an insect, but a dense, ceramic-like carapace of keratin and silica, polished to a mirror sheen by the constant, damp wind. I look like a jagged, black lightning rod frozen mid-strike, blending perfectly with the rock when I am still.

**The Physiology of Waiting**

I do not have eyes. In a world of bruised phosphorescence and perpetual fog, sight is a wasted sense. Instead, I possess a "lateral line" of electroreceptors running the entire length of my spine, sensitive to the faintest fluctuation in the atmospheric charge.

My metabolism is unique to the shelf. I do not eat organic matter. I am a capacitor. I feed on the stray voltage that arcs between the clouds and the earth, and the massive discharges from the Crawlers passing below. My internal anatomy is a series of spiraling, fluid-filled chambers rich in iron and copper ions—a biological coil that stores energy. When I am fully charged, the veins beneath my ceramic skin pulse with a blinding, strobe-like white light, a stark contrast to the dim violet of the world around me.

**The Perspective**

For days—or perhaps weeks; time is fluid here—I remain motionless, locked into the rock. I feel the world through the soles of my gripping claws and the buzz in my bones.

I feel the *thrum-hiss* of the approaching Crawler long before it is visible. It begins as a tickle in my gut, a low-frequency vibration that resonates through the basalt needle I call home. The rock hums against my chest. The air grows heavy, tasting of ozone and wet iron.

I retract my sensory tendrils, protecting them from the coming surge. I am small—perhaps the size of a man—but in this moment, I am a lightning rod waiting for the storm.

**The Encounter**

The Crawler emerges from the smog like a moving island. It is colossal, a biological cathedral of vents and bone. The heat radiating from it washes over my spire, a sudden wave of tropical humidity in the thin, damp air.

To me, the Crawler is not just a beast; it is a walking generator. I can see—no, *sense*—the electricity arcing between its massive horns. It is a storm cloud in biological form, crackling with potential energy.

It passes below my perch. The ground shakes. The *crack-thoom* of its footsteps vibrates through my bones. But I am focused on the crown of the beast, where the Ridgelings scurry like fleas, unaware of the predator looming above them.

I am not hunting the Crawler; it is too large to harm, and I am too small to be noticed. I am hunting the *leakage*.

As the giant pauses to vent a cloud of steam and spores, a massive static discharge builds up in its armor. With a sound like a ripping sheet, a bolt of white-hot electricity jumps from the beast’s primary vent, seeking the ground.

It does not hit the ground.

I rise.

Extending my telescoping neck, I present my conductive crest to the arc. The lightning strikes me.

It is not pain; it is ecstasy. It is a violent influx of pure energy, a rush that floods my internal coils instantly. My carapace glows with a blinding brilliance, turning my dark spire into a beacon of the night. The smell of singed ozone fills my nostrils. My heart—a muscular, turbine-like organ—spins up, whining with the effort of processing the influx.

**The Existence**

As the Crawler lumbers away, the thunder of its footsteps fading back into the rhythmic breathing of the planet, I am left drifting on a high of excess energy.

I am a fulgurite given life. I am the spark that clears the fog.

In the quiet that follows, I exhale a plume of superheated steam of my own, a tiny mimicry of the giant that fed me. My body glows softly for hours, a rhythmic pulse of white light that fades slowly as the energy is metabolized and stored in the dense, chemical batteries of my limbs.

I watch the world through the vibrations of the rock. I sense the jittery, panic-stricken skittering of the crab-like scavengers far below, disturbed by the passing of the giant. I feel the silent, deadly approach of a Silencer, padding softly on its sound-dampening feet—a blind spot in my electrical vision, a void of noise that I fear more than the dark.

But for now, I am full. I am glowing in the warm, respiratory dark, anchored to my basalt spire, a needle stitching the sky to the earth. I am the collector of ghosts, drinking lightning from the giants.
