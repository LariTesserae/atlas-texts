I find myself anchored in the **Green Lattice**, suspended in the interstice between the dripping iron and the pulsing dark.

I am a **Circuit-Weaver**.

I do not look at this world from the outside; I feel it as a voltage differential. I am small—palm-sized—but my perception is vast because I am hardwired into the nervous system of the dying god. My chosen anchorage is the root-cluster of a Great Fern, a leviathan of waxy green that clings to a rust-eaten catwalk about forty meters above the black water.

**My Form**
My body is architecture unto itself. I am a pale, many-legged arachnid, but evolved for the conductivity of this place. My exoskeleton is not chitin, but a semi-translucent ceramic shell that allows me to see the working of my own insides. This is crucial, for I am electric. Through the clear plates of my abdomen, I can see the bioluminescent pathways of my own nerves firing—bright blue pulses traveling down my limbs to my delicate, soldering-tip claws.

I have eight eyes, arranged in a crescent on my forehead, but they are poor at resolving shape. They are designed to see heat and current. When I look at the fern I stand upon, I do not see a plant; I see a schematic. I see the flow of sap as a dull red throb, and the flow of stolen electricity as a blinding white wire running up the stem.

**My Work**
The Helios-Agronomic Sump is a single, slow metabolism, and I am the synapse that refuses to sever.

I spend my existence mending the Interface. The roots of the ferns have pierced the insulation of the ancient, ticking cables that run through the catwalks. The plant drinks the voltage to augment its photosynthesis in this dim, yellow light. But the connection is violent; the heat of the cable burns the root, and the moisture of the root shorts the cable.

This is where I live.

I scuttle along the greasy, metallic convergence point. My front two limbs are hypodermic needles, tipped with spinnerets that excrete a conductive, bio-polymer glue. I taste the air—it tastes of ozone and wet rot. I sense a fluctuation in the current—a stutter, a potential break in the circuit.

With speed that makes the rest of the chamber seem glacial, I dart to the fraying contact. I lay a strand of my silver web across the gap, bridging the organic root-fiber to the copper wire. The current jumps my body. It is a sharp, exquisite pain—a rushing jolt of energy that fuels my own heart. I absorb the excess voltage, feed on the waste electrons, and seal the wound with insulating slime.

I am the electrician of the apocalypse. Without me, the fern would wither, the catwalk would lose its bio-luminescent anchor, and the delicate balance of the vertical sea would tilt.

**My Perspective**
From my vantage point, clinging to the underside of a leaf the size of a sail, I watch the ecology of the Stray unfold as a series of data streams.

High above, I perceive the Thermal-Gargoyles not as beasts, but as sudden drops in temperature. They are holes in the infrared spectrum, silent voids that detach from the ceiling. When one dives, I see the heat displacement of the air rushing over its wings, a ghostly contrail in the mist, followed by the sudden, hot bloom of biological stress as it crushes a Mist-Striker.

I watch the Mist-Strikers themselves. To my eyes, they are not fish, but bursts of kinetic energy, vibrating wings that hum in harmony with the chamber’s low-frequency thrum. They are motes of data moving through the air, chaotic and unorganized.

Far below, through the grate of the catwalk, I sense the heavy, magnetic churn of the Sump-Leviathan. It moves through the black water like a slow-moving magnet, displacing the heavy, conductive sludge. I feel the pressure waves in the metal beneath my feet, a rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* that vibrates through my legs.

**My Existence**
I am terrified of the silence. If the machine stops, the current dies. If the current dies, my heart stops beating. I am a parasite that has become a vital organ. I am dependent on the grand, rusty failure of this engine.

When the great pistons heave, the entire world shakes. The catwalk groans. The Great Fern I cling to sways violently. I lock my needle-legs into the mesh of the root, grounding myself, feeling the surge of power that the movement generates through the friction of the cables. I ride the quake, a tiny spark of blue light in a world of brown and green, holding the wires together so the jungle can breathe for another cycle.

I am the Circuit-Weaver. I am the impulse that says, *Stay connected.*
