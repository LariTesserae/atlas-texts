I find myself in the narrow, suffocating darkness between the Gear of the West and the Great Piston of the Northern Ridge. It is a canyon of steel, a vertical slit where the sky is nothing more than a bruised, hazy line of white-gold far above. The air here is not so much breathed as it is drunk; it is thick, greasy, and tastes of wet copper and ancient dust. The heat is physical, a heavy blanket that presses my ceramic shell against the cooling metal of the machine flank.

I am one of them. I am not distinct from the landscape—I am the landscape made small and mobile.

I possess no name, nor do I require one. I am defined by my geometry: a low, jagged arch of burnt sienna armor, segmented and plated to mimic the slag hills. My legs are sturdy, rust-pitted pillars that lock into place with a satisfying *click* when the earth shifts. I have no eyes to see the sun that hangs uselessly in the haze. Instead, I see the world through a tapestry of heat gradients. The gear beside me glows with a deep, dull indigo in my perception, while the fissure to my left blazes with a terrifying, brilliant scarlet.

I am a biological capacitor, a custodian of the interstices.

My existence is governed by the thrum—that bass note that rattles through my chest and vibrates the fluid in my veins. It is the heartbeat of the world, and it is my heartbeat too. I do not fight the rhythm; I am enslaved to it, perfected by it.

*Click. Hiss. Thrum.*

The Great Piston above me begins to rise. The pressure in the chamber changes, a drop in barometric density that tickles the sensory filaments sprouting from my brow. I feel the massive wall of steel beside me grind forward, moving with the inevitability of a glacier.

I freeze.

I lock my joints. My internal fluid stops circulating. I become, for ten seconds, a statue of oxidized iron. I am indistinguishable from the pebbles scattered around me. This is not a decision; it is a reflex older than the species I might once have been. To move now is to be crushed by the indifferent progress of the engine. To move now is to cease.

The machinery groans, a sound like a tectonic plate shifting. The piston reaches its apex—forty meters of agonizingly slow ascent. It holds. The air vibrates with the tension.

*Pause.*

The exhale. The piston begins its descent. The gear beside me grinds back to its starting position, shedding a fine layer of gray-fuzz from its crevices. The cycle resets.

I unlock.

I scuttle forward, the sound of my claws on the metal floor a dry, skittering whisper—*shhh-clack, shhh-clack*. I move with the jerky, clockwork precision of a component being slotted into place. I am not hunting; I do not understand the concept of hunger as a pain. I understand only the need for equilibrium.

I have found a patch of the gray-fuzz dislodged by the gear's movement. It clings to the rivet-studded floor, a velvet carpet of crystalline silence. I lower my underside, exposing the grinding mandibles hidden beneath my armored skirt. I drag them across the floor, a harsh friction that sends sparks dancing in the gloom.

I consume the mineral lattice. It tastes of ozone and time. It is gritty, dry matter that dissolves into slurry as it enters my digestive tract, mixing with the trace metals I need to sustain my shell. I am the recycler. I take the waste of the machine and turn it into the armor that protects me from the machine.

But the fuzz is merely the structure; the heat is the fuel.

I sense a thermal bloom—a micro-fracture in the floor ahead where steam is escaping, a high-pitched whistle that cuts through the bass thrum. I maneuver toward it, my legs navigating the uneven terrain with practiced ease. I splay myself over the fissure, letting the scalding vapor wash over my underside.

The warmth penetrates my shell. I feel it diffuse through the network of tubes running along my inner abdomen, heating the copper-colored liquid within. I glow internally—a private, invisible radiance. My metabolism hums, spinning up from its idle state. I am charging. I am alive.

I am alone here. I have never seen another of my kind, though I know they exist in the distance. I feel them only as disturbances in the pressure, vague shadows in the heat-map of the valley. We do not speak. To speak would require a mouth for words and a mind for thoughts beyond the cycle. We are merely separate fingers of the same hand, grasping at the same void.

I am ancient, though I do not know my age. I suspect that I was not born, but assembled—that my ancestors were drones of steel and wire that slowly forgot their wiring and grew soft, wet insides to cushion the machine's relentless blows. Or perhaps I was once flesh that looked upon this iron world and decided that to survive, I must become iron.

It does not matter. The distinction is lost in the rust.

The piston rises again. I feel the shadow fall over me, blotting out the heat-sense of the sky above. The pressure drops.

I freeze. I lock. I become stone.

I am the dust of the machine, animated for a moment, waiting to return to the slag. And in this waiting, in this perfect, frozen synchronization, I find a kind of peace. I am exactly where I am meant to be. I am the gear that turns, the valve that opens, the crab that feeds on the breath of the world.

I am the maintenance crew of eternity.
