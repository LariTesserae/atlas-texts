I am a heat-source, a slow-burning ember in the suffocating dark. 

First, there is the weight of the stone against my spine. It is not a burden; it is an anchor. I am wedged into a niche carved into the basalt, a hollow barely wider than my shoulders, high above the main arterial shafts. The air here is still, recycled by the lungs of a thousand others below, heavy with the musk of unwashed bodies, rendered fat, and the sweet, cloying dust of dried peat. My chest expands against the rock, a slow, rhythmic heave that seems to take minutes to complete. I am dense. My blood is sluggish, thick as syrup, moving reluctantly through the cramped highways of my veins to preserve the precious calories I burned hours ago.

I open my eyes. They are pools of ink, useless for color but perfect for reading the textures of the dark. I see the condensation weeping from the ceiling—not water, not yet, but a slick, sweating sheen of promise. It gathers on the rough-hewn granite, swelling into beads heavy enough to defy surface tension. 

*Patience.*

My role is defined by the drip. I am a Catcher. 

I do not move quickly. To move quickly here is to waste heat, to invite the sweat that chills and kills. I unfurl my hands, fingers long and hooked, the calcified nails scraping softly against the stone. I pull myself forward, an inch at a time, out of my niche and onto the narrow ledge outside. The wind hits me instantly—a physical blow, wet and razor-sharp. It seeks the gaps in my layers, the places where the goat-hide has worn thin, where the rawhide ties have loosened. I narrow the broad, shield of my nose, filtering the gale through the labyrinth of turbinal bones, warming it before it scorches my lungs.

Below me, the City is a vertical abyss. I cannot see the bottom. I see only the smudge of orange firelight from a thousand hearths, a constellation of warmth sinking into the deeps. To my left, a cluster of white ravens huddle in a crevice, their feathers ruffled, eyes reflecting the dim glow of my peat-lantern. They do not stir. They know I am not a threat; I am merely another piece of the geology, another outcropping of fur and fat.

I reach out and scrape my fingernails along the rock face. *Thwack.* A sheet of rime crystals breaks loose, tumbling silently down the shaft. I catch the slush before it falls, cupping it in my palms. It is freezing, stinging the calloused skin, but I do not flinch. I press the ice against the leather bottle at my hip, melting it with my body heat, drop by drop.

This is the economy of the Spine. We do not own the water; we only delay its journey back to the world below.

A vibration travels up through the soles of my boots—the dull, rhythmic thud of the yak-beasts on the switchbacks below. I feel it in my marrow before I hear it. It is a comforting hum, the heartbeat of the City. I pause, pressing my hand flat against the cold granite. I can feel the subtle groan of the mountain, the tectonic settling of the shard we cling to. It is a slow, deep grinding, the sound of the world trying to shake us off.

I look up. The violet pallor of the sky is beginning to bruise into a deeper indigo. The jet stream screams a mile above, tearing at the needle-peaks, but here, in the lee of the cliff, it is merely a moan. I am alone, but not lonely. Solitude is a luxury we cannot afford, yet silence is our only true companion. 

I shift my weight, testing the slickness of the stone beneath my yak-horn soles. My center of gravity is low, a pendulum of bone and muscle that defies the vertigo. I am a spider on a web of stone, possessing a calmness that is not peace, but simply the absence of panic. Panic is death. Panic is a slip. Panic is the long fall.

I am a child of the Spine. I am the bruise-colored flesh that holds the stone together. I will sit here for hours, watching the slush turn to water, feeling the mountain breathe, enduring the vast, cold, wet silence until the shift changes and another shadow takes my place.
