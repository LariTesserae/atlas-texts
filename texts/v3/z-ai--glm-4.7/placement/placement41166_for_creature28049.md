I find myself in the fissure, a canyon of shadow no wider than the span of my arms, though the walls on either side stretch up into the gloom for what must be miles. To a giant, this is a hairline crack in the armor plating; to me, it is the world.

I am soft. This is my defining characteristic and my curse. In a landscape of basalt, iron, and petrified wood, I am composed of plasm, moisture, and fragile chitin. I am a biological irritant, a grain of damp sand in the gearbox of a god.

My name is irrelevant—perhaps a series of clicks that would be drowned out by the weeping of the stone—but if I were to name myself, I would call myself a *Rust-Eater*. I am a scavenger of the entropic.

Where specifically am I? I am clinging to the inner curve of a massive, petrified tendon. Above me, the "ceiling" is a slab of cyclopean masonry, dark and glassy, sweating that characteristic gray ooze. Below me, the floor is a complex lattice of interlocking wooden levers, fossilized and black, bound by cables as thick as river systems. The heat here is suffocating, a physical weight that presses against my carapace, but the humidity is the only thing keeping my gills moist.

I am perched on the shoulder of an Orogenic Colossus. We call it "The Walker" in the chemical bursts of our mating pheromones, though it has not walked in an epoch. It is currently in its dreaming phase.

My existence is a frantic, high-speed scramble across the surface of a entity that moves with the glacial slowness of geology. My life is measured in hours; its heartbeat is measured in decades. When the tendon beneath my feet twitches—a microscopic spasm to the Colossus, but a seismic event that throws me against the wall—I know it is merely shifting in its sleep.

I am here to eat. I feed on the oxidation, the rust that blooms like orange flowers on the iron joints. To the Colossus, I am a negligible cleaning crew, removing the blight of decay from its joints. My mandibles are strong, designed to scrape the metallic flakes into a paste that fuels my high-metabolism body.

I live in constant terror of the "Yawn." This is when the intake vent—the spiraling borehole the size of a cathedral located miles above, near the "head"—cycles. The pressure drop is immense. It creates a wind that can tear a soft creature like me from the wall and hurl me up into the bruised sky, never to return. I have seen my brothers vanish, tiny specks sucked into the breathing of a mountain.

I scuttle deeper into the crevice as the environment begins to shift. The "ghost momentum" is increasing. The wooden lever I am standing on vibrates with a low-frequency hum that rattles my internal fluids. The gray ooze weeping from the rocks begins to steam, turning from a slick lubricant into a boiling vapor.

The Colossi are waking up.

Looking out through a crack in the basalt shell, I see the landscape I thought was solid begin to undulate. To the east, the ground ripples like wet dough. I know now that these are the juveniles, the molten slugs beneath the crust, thrashing in their courtship. The sight makes me feel hollow.

But it is the western horizon that holds the true horror and beauty. There, a distant "mountain"—a sibling of the beast I stand upon—is doing the impossible. It is un-making. I watch through the bruised haze as massive slabs of its flank detach, floating upward into the gloom. It is shedding its weight.

My own mount shudders. A grinding roar, deeper than the ocean, vibrates through my claws. The basalt plates above me grind together, sending showers of sparks that sizzle in the wet air. The Walker is preparing to move. It has felt the magnetic shift of its kin. The time of stagnation is over; the age of ascension has begun.

I tighten my grip on the rust, digging my claws into the safety of the corrosion. I am a parasite on a dying god, and I have no intention of letting go. If it ascends, I go with it. I will become a creature of the sky, feeding on the rust of the heavens.

I am small. I am soft. But I am holding on to the back of a world as it learns to fly.
