I feel the world first with my teeth.

It is a low-frequency ache, a constant buzzing that sets my jaw vibrating ever so slightly, loosening the grime between my molars. The *Thrum*. It is not a sound that enters the ear; it is a pressure that crawls up the legs, travels the spine, and settles in the skull. To exist here is to be a musical instrument, forever resonating with the growl of the planet’s digestion.

I am hanging upside down.

The blood rushes to my head, heavy and sluggish in the concentrated gravity. My knees are hooked over a thick, horizontal branch of a massive Hematite giant, the bark rough and cold against my skin, patterned with the geometric scabs of old harvests. My arms dangle free, swaying slightly in the convection currents—the hot breath of the earth rising up to meet the cold gloom of the ceiling.

This is my home: the Eaves.

I am not down in the sweltering gloom where the sap-tappers work, nor am I in the middle tiers where the weavers braid the bridges. I am at the very top of the city, suspended in the spatial limbo where the metallic foliage thins out and the ceiling of fumed obsidian hangs low, scraping against the highest leaves like a dark, uninvited sky. Up here, the air is thinner, but the heat is still a predatory weight, pressing against my back.

**Who am I?**

I am a **Silent-Striker**.

Among my people, the voice is a useless thing, lost to the wind and the roar of the cooling rock. We are a species of hands and eyes, of leverage and tension. But even among us, there are those who listen closer than others. My fingers are elongated, the distal phalanges lengthened by generations of selective breeding—or perhaps simply the natural stretch of gripping the smooth, slick rails of the iron trees for a lifetime.

My skin is the color of the storm clouds above the sinkhole—a pale, bruised grey. It is tight, stretched over a framework of dense, heavy bone. I am lighter than the miners below, possessing a bird-like fragility that is a lie; in this gravity, fragility is strength. To be heavy is to fall. To be light is to swing.

I am the lookout. The siren.

**The Role**

Around my neck hangs a heavy pendant: a femur of a leviathan tree, hollowed out and plugged with a membrane of cured gut. It is not jewelry; it is a drum.

I do not speak. My vocal cords are withered strands. Instead, I listen.

I press my palm flat against the flank of the tree. Through the calloused padding of my hand, I feel the inner life of the forest. I feel the slow, viscous pumping of the hydrocarbon sap, thick and sluggish as it climbs from the superheated roots. I feel the strain of the wood as the wind shears against the metallic leaves. But mostly, I feel the *Thrum* change.

The Earth below is not solid. It is a slow, grinding machinery of heat and pressure. When a vent is about to open, when the floor is about to buckle and swallow a platform, the pitch of the vibration shifts. It dips. It wavers.

It is my job to catch that wavering.

**The Scene**

A shift in the air current. The dry wind tasting of salt and rust suddenly gusts, and the metallic leaves above me chime—a discordant, clattering splash of sound.

I pull myself up, swinging my legs over the branch, and crouch in a squirrel-like perch. My toes grip the bark automatically, seeking purchase in the natural cracks. I am naked to the waist, my chest heaving with the rapid, shallow panting that cools my blood. A heat-mask hangs loose around my neck, ready to be pulled up if the sulfur fumes get too thick, but for now, I crave the sharp bite of the iron air.

Below me, the city is a spiderweb of motion. I see figures, grey as ants, swinging on the vertebrae-bridges. I see a platform, recently anchored to a dying patch of crystalline moss, groaning under the weight of a storage cache.

I place the bone drum against the tree trunk and tap it. *Thud.*

The sound travels down the wood, faster and farther than a shout could ever dream. It is a signal: *Steady.*

But then, I feel it.

Beneath my grip, the tree shudders. It is not the wind. It is a deep, sickening lurch, a vibration that spikes from a low hum to a high, teeth-screaming whine. It comes from the roots, miles below, where the stone is sublimating into gas.

The *Great Upheaval*. The earth is burping.

I look down at the platform near the roots of my tree. The workers there are oblivious, their backs turned, hauling baskets of sulfur. They cannot feel the shift yet. They will have seconds before the ground turns to liquid and the heat vent expands.

I jam the drum harder against the bark. I don't tap now; I pound.

*BOOM-boom-boom. BOOM-boom-boom.*

*The Break. The Fall. Move.*

The rhythm is frantic, an alarm encoded in the wood itself. I see the heads of the workers snap up. They don't look at me—I am too high, lost in the gloom—they look at the tree. They feel the warning traveling through the soles of their feet.

Chaos erupts. They scramble. They cut lines. They swing.

I watch them ascend, rushing toward the higher tiers, abandoning the heavy baskets. The ground below them, invisible in the dark, lets out a hiss that sounds like a dragon dying. A jet of superheated steam erupts, blasting the lower vines with scorching force. The platform they were standing on disintegrates, its ropes burning instantly.

They are safe. They are climbing.

**The Self**

I remain at my post. I do not flee with them. My place is here, at the edge of the known world.

I wipe the black sludge from my nose, the filtered iron dust that my body has rejected. I pull the heat-mask up over my face, feeling the comforting dampness of my own breath recycling against my lips.

I am not human. I am a creature of the vertical. I do not dream of horizons; I dream of the next hold, the next swing, the next handhold. I fear the flat ground. To me, the earth is not a foundation, but a predator.

I look up, past the metallic leaves, to the obsidian ceiling. It is so close I could almost touch it if I dared to leap. We are climbing toward it, eternally, hoping for a crack, a window, a way out.

But as I feel the vibration of the tree settling back into a low, steady hum, I know the truth I cannot tell them. The ceiling is lowering, too. The heat is rising. The world is getting smaller.

I am the Silent-Striker. I watch the death of the world so that my people can live one more day in the belly of it. And I wait for the next note in the song of the stone.
