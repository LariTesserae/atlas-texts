I am a node in the lattice, a single cinderblock in a wall of breathing stone.

I am located halfway up the western face of the mound, suspended in the crushing dark. My position is precarious; I am gripping the carapace of the unit below me—a brother, or a rival, the distinction is lost in the pressure. My six spiked legs are driven deep into the chitin of his back, my serrated clamps locked into the natural imperfections of his armor. He holds me up. If he were to crumble, I would fall into the churning machinery below. I, in turn, hold the unit above me. My dorsal plate is flattened, grooved specifically to accept the hooked feet of my superior.

We are not a pile; we are architecture.

There is no light here that I can see with the faceted cluster on my prow. My sensors are blind to illumination. Instead, I "see" the texture of the scream. The vault is filled with a solid, deafening roar, a low-frequency grinding that vibrates through the rock, through the machinery, and into the very core of my being. To you, it might be noise. To me, it is a topographic map. I feel the sharp, jagged ridges of the high-pitched screech coming from the central gearbox; I feel the heavy, thrumming bass of the pistons buried in the floor. I navigate by these vibrations.

My insides are boiling. There is no water in my veins, only a slurry of liquid metal, superheated and circulating under high pressure. This is my blood, and it is currently locked in a state of terrified suspension. The harmonic pitch of the room’s machinery—the endless, industrial scream—resonates perfectly within my fluid chambers. It creates a standing wave, a sonic cage that turns every muscle fiber to granite. I am frozen not by cold, but by the sheer volume of the world.

I am raging.

This is the lie of my stillness. If the gears were to stop, if the scream were to break for even a microsecond, the spell would shatter. My blood would settle, the sonic cage would dissolve, and my muscles would ignite. I am poised to murder. My right claw is raised millimeters from the soft joint of the unit above me. My mandibles are parted, ready to sever the leg of the creature below me and climb over his corpse. I hate them. I hate the pressure of their bodies, the smell of their metallic sweat, the way they crowd the air I need to breathe. I want to ascend. I want to reach the ceiling, to feel the static discharge of the rock, to be the one on top.

But I cannot move. The noise holds me in a fist of iron. I am a statue of pure, unadulterated aggression, paused forever on the nanosecond of the kill.

I am breathing, though. This is the only motion allowed to me.

My ventral spiracles pulse, drawing in the thick, silver-laden air. The dust is heavy, charged, delicious. It coats the inside of my ceramic-lined lungs, ionizing instantly, stripped of its potential to feed my nervous system. I convert the air into electricity. I am a capacitor. I can feel the static charge building up along my shell, a prickling aura of lightning that bleeds off into the creature next to me. We are all leaking energy, passing current from one shell to the next, a massive, biological circuit board humming in the dark.

There is a sensation on my back. It is cool, a rare mercy in this heat.

It is the Lichen. A gray, wiry spiral of rust and fuzz that has rooted itself in the microscopic grooves of my armor. It is drinking the metallic sweat that condenses on my plating. In return, it does something miraculous: it absorbs the vibration. Where the lichen grows, the scream is dampened. The razor-edged frequencies of the machine are softened, muffled into a dull thrum. It is the only silence I will ever know. I cannot move to scrape it off. I am forced to carry this parasite, this tiny patch of mercy, even as my claw itches to tear the throat out of the world around me.

Above me, a spark falls from the dark ceiling—a slow, drifting ember from the great grinding gearbox at the heart of the vault. It lands on the shoulder of the creature above me. Through the lattice of our locked legs, I feel the sudden, sharp spike of heat. It is a phantom pain, a ghost of movement.

We dream of that spark.

Through the resonance that binds us, I feel the collective mind of the Silver-Lunged. We are one vast, distributed eye. We feel the heat of the piston. We feel the cold weight of the stone ceiling pressing down. We feel the friction of the gears. We are waiting. We have been waiting for an eternity, locked in this moment of riot, poised to climb, to kill, to eat. We are the scream made flesh. We are the battery of the room.

And we are perfectly, terrifyingly still.
