I exist in the middle distances, suspended in the crushing dark of the Mid-Zone. This is the transit layer, the living artery where the vertical pressure of the Spire is at its most intense. I am not one of the massive Breakers at the bottom, whose labor shatters the world’s bones, nor am I a bloated Processor hanging in the dim red heat of the summit. I am the connective tissue. I am a **Carrier**.

My world is defined by the geometry of compression. I am wedged tight into a fissure of jagged basalt, my eight limbs hooked into the stone like anchors in a storm. To my left, the wall feels impossibly hot, radiating an infrared fever that bakes my carapace. To my right, there is only the shifting, chitinous bulk of my kin. We are packed so densely that there is no distinction between my armor and theirs; we are a single, writhing column of black oil sliding up the throat of the mountain.

I have no name, only a position in the queue. I am a cog in a biological conveyor belt that never stops.

My existence is a cycle of strain and release. Through the pads of my hooked claws, I feel the seismic signature of the Breaker four levels below me. The vibration travels up the rock, a sharp *crack-scream* that signals a fresh fracture. Then comes the rhythm: the heavy, stumbling thud of the stone being passed up. Claw to claw. Mandible to mandible.

*Signal received.*

My forward limbs—twin hydraulic pincers designed for crushing grip—uncoil from the rock. I do not see the block of raw basalt floating toward me through the darkness; I feel the displacement of air, the disturbance in the dust, and the faint magnetic hum of the iron-rich mineral. My sensory pits, deep and atrophied for sight, flare with the ghostly impression of heat.

I snatch the stone from the claws of the Carrier below. It is heavy, jagged, and still vibrating from the violence of its creation. The impact jars my entire frame, a jolt of kinetic energy that rattles through my segmented plates. For a microsecond, I hold the weight of the mountain itself. My internal fluids surge, pressurizing to counter the load.

With a fluid shift of musculature, I heave the stone upward and backward. I pass it to the Carrier above me. I feel his claws scrape against my upper shell as he locks onto the prize. The transfer is smooth, lubricated by the fine dust that coats us all. The weight is gone.

*Cycle complete.*

In this moment of weightlessness, I feel the environment trying to kill me. The air is a thief. It seeks to steal the moisture from my spiracles. I clamp my breathing ports tight, recycling the same humid breath, stripping the oxygen, filtering out the sulfurous grit. My body is a sealed vessel of hot water and biochemical rage, leaking nothing. I feel the heat of my own metabolism radiating out through my flank vents, adding to the suffocating warmth of the shaft. We are all stoking the fire together.

Suddenly, the Spine groans. It is a deep, tectonic shifting that resonates through the obsidian walls. The frequency changes from a steady grind to a chaotic wobble.

*Instability.*

The vibration through my claws becomes frantic. Above me, a distress signal ripples down the line—a rapid-fire tapping of claws against stone, the ancient code for *shear*. A section of the wall to the right has given way.

There is a scuffle of plates, a wet crunch, and then a sudden drop in pressure. A Carrier has fallen. I feel the displacement of air as the black body tumbles past me into the abyss. There is no mourning here. The falling creature hits the scree miles below, and the signal changes. The Scavengers are already swarming. Their high-frequency, chittering vibrations rise up to us like the hungry chirping of insects. *Recycle. Reclaim. Consume.*

The gap left by the fallen one is instantly filled. The pressure from behind increases. I am shoved forward, my face pressed against the scorching rock. I must climb. To stop is to become the obstacle. To stop is to be crushed.

I dig my pick-like limbs into fresh crevices. The basalt is sharp, harder than my own armor, but I find purchase. I heave my bulk upward, seeking the next stable pocket. The rock resists, then splinters. I haul myself up, the friction grinding my joints down to dust.

I settle into the new position. The pressure from behind relents slightly. The rhythm returns.

Below me, the Breakers are screaming into the stone. Above me, the Processors are glowing with their radioactive, chemically induced heat. And here, in the middle, I wait for the next piece of the world to be passed up.

I have no eyes, yet I see the darkness. I have no ears, yet I hear the symphony of grinding gears. I am the Lithophage. I am the machine that eats the planet. And I am hungry.
