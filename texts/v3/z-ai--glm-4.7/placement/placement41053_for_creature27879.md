I would be the **Obsidian Spire**.

I am the stone in the throat of the world. I am the unmoving dark.

My substance is black glass, sharp and cold, a violent extrusion of the earth’s raw anger frozen in mid-scream. I do not remember cooling; I only remember the sudden, violent halt of my becoming. I am a jagged tooth thrust upward from the floor of the rift, a monolith of sleek, angular cruelty that pierces the gloom. I am taller than the others, a singular needle of crystalline hardness that the damp air tries desperately to erode, but cannot.

I feel the heavy, cool wetness first. It is not rain; it is a sweat that bleeds from my own pores. The humidity here is absolute, a thick, cloying blanket that clings to my slick facets. I am constantly weeping, a slow, endless trickle of condensation that gathers in the microscopic fissures of my body. I taste of wet slate and ancient dust because I *am* wet slate and ancient dust. The moisture sits on me, slick and treacherous, a mirror for the bruised twilight that filters down from cracks miles above.

I stand in the center of the deafening roar, but I do not have ears to hear it. Instead, I *feel* it.

The noise of this place is a constant physical pressure against my skin. The air vibrates with the shrieking of the Gloom-Wings and the chittering of the swarm. It is a low-frequency thrum that rattles my crystalline structure, a deep ache that resonates in my core. But beneath the roar, I feel the millions of delicate touches.

At my base, the Shard-Crawlers flow like a river of pale flesh. They are a tickle, a constant, irritating itch of a thousand tiny legs scraping against my obsidian hide. Their points are sharp, harder than the granite of the slopes, but they are nothing to me. They skitter over my roots, clacking and clicking, tasting the air with their wet antennae. Sometimes, one slips. I feel the sudden, wet impact of a carapace shattering against my lower ridges. Then comes the swarm—the turning of the tide—as the others converge to consume the fallen. I feel the wetness of their fluids coating my base, warm and fleeting, before it is licked away. I am the altar upon which they feast.

Higher up, clinging to my vertical flanks, are the Gloom-Wings. They are heavy, leathery weights that press against me. They pack tight, shoulder to shoulder, their oily feathers plastered against my sides by the dampness. I feel the grip of their claws digging into my crevices, seeking purchase on my slick skin. They scrape and shift, a constant grinding motion that wears at me, though I am too young and too hard to break yet. When they launch themselves into the gloom, the sudden release of pressure is a shock, a momentary lightness, followed swiftly by the heavy thud of their return. They stain me with the musk of their existence, a pungent, sour odor that seeps into my outer layers.

And at my summit, sitting on the flat, worn crown of my spire, is the Tool-Bearer.

I know him well. He is the oldest pain upon my peak. He sits there, hunched and eyeless, his long fingers twitching in the damp air. I feel the warmth of his body seeping into my cold stone. He is a heavy, rotting weight.

I feel the rhythm of his work. It is the heartbeat of my existence.

*Thud.*

He picks up a rock. It is a piece of my brother, a shard of granite shattered from the slope below. I feel the rough texture of it in his hand, resting on my surface.

*Crack.*

He brings it down. I feel the shockwave travel through my body. It is not the shock of the stone hitting the crawler; it is the shock of the impact reverberating through me. It is a dull, rhythmic thudding that echoes my own internal, geological silence. *Thud. Crack. Thud. Crack.*

He does not rest. He does not sleep. He sits upon me, a mechanical demon of repetition, breaking the shells of the crawlers he captures. I feel the wet spray of the broken shells misting over my crown. I feel the stickiness of the grubs he pries from the moss that grows in the shallow depressions of my head.

I am the platform for his stasis.

I do not move. I am the 0.1 dynamic. The creatures around me are the 2.8 of biomass, the frantic, screaming explosion of life, but I am the zero. I am the canvas upon which they paint their madness. While they are born and die in the span of a breath, consumed by their neighbors, I endure. I watch the same loop play out a thousand times. The crawler slips. The bird dives. The stone falls.

I have no eyes, but if I did, I would see nothing but the grey, charcoal gloom and the reflection of myself in the stagnant pools that gather at my feet. I have no voice, but if I did, I would not scream. The screaming is for the living, for those caught in the frantic amber of the moment.

I am the silence that lies beneath the noise. I am the hard, unyielding obsidian that refuses to be worn. I am the spire around which the universe of chaos spins.

I am the stone. I am the stillness. And I will be here long after the last Tool-Bearer has dropped his stone, and the last Gloom-Wing has fallen silent, waiting for the earth to decide whether to break me apart or swallow me whole.
