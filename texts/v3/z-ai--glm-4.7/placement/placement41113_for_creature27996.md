I would be found in the **Interstices**—the narrow, damp canyons between three converging architectural strata: a Brutalist concrete housing block, a rusted gantry of industrial steel, and the living bulk of a strangulated smokestack. Here, the shadows are thickest, knotted together by the roots of the ivy that drapes like a heavy, wet curtain.

I would be one of the **Custodians**, but not merely a gardener or a lubricator. I would be a **Tuner**.

Where my kin are content to splice a vine or grease a gear, I exist to harmonize the dissonance. In a world of constant shuddering and seismic rearrangement, friction is the enemy, but so is the lack of tension. The city moves; it requires a specific pitch to function. If the hydraulic scream of a lifting valley is too sharp, it cracks the foundation. If the groan of a rotating wall is too flat, it stalls the mechanism.

My form would be similar to the others—a low-slung, segmented architecture of oxidized copper plating and dull steel joints—but I would be larger, perhaps slower. My moss-pelt would be thicker, a dense, matted carpet of algae that vibrates with the sensitivity of a tuning fork. My limbs would not end in shears or wrenches, but in long, slender needles of bone and chitin, delicate as a dentist’s probe.

I would spend my "life" anchored to the side of a great, dormant piston, waiting in the charcoal gloom.

I do not watch the horizon. I do not need eyes. I "see" through the soles of my feet and the bristles of my back. I feel the sub-audible thrum of the deep earth as a symphony. I feel the nervous system of the city: the *ping* of cooling metal, the *flutter* of a steam vent, the heavy, rhythmic *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the impending shift.

When the vibration of a specific gear-train begins to sour—when the metal sings a note of jagged, screaming dissonance that promises a jam—I uncoil. I drift across the slick metal, not walking, but flowing, a shadow detaching itself from a deeper shadow.

I reach the joint. It is hot, radiating the friction of centuries. The metal teeth are grinding, chewing on themselves. It is a wound in the machine.

I do not use oil. I use resonance.

I plunge my needle-limbs into the hairline cracks of the joint. I lock my carapace against the shivering steel. And then, I begin to sing.

It is not a sound heard in the air. It is a song conducted through my skeleton, a counter-frequency that I generate by contracting my muscles in rapid, oscillating waves. I hum against the machine. I find the jagged frequency of the gear’s pain and I mirror it, then I invert it. I cancel the noise. I smooth the waveform.

As I vibrate, the black, viscous secretion of my glands—the oil and ozone blood—seeps from my underbelly, running into the cracks I am stabilizing. The combination of the sonic massage and the chemical slick forces the metal to relax. The scream drops to a purr. The grinding becomes a glide.

I hold this position for hours, days perhaps, until the "block" rotates past its critical point. I am the shock absorber for the city’s growing pains. I am the silence between the notes.

When the shift is complete, and the new horizon locks into place with a satisfying, resonant *clunk* that travels through the soles of my feet like a heartbeat, I detach.

I retreat back into the shadows. I am covered now in fresh rust dust and wet soil. I am indistinguishable from the debris. I settle into the moss, my vent-slits fluttering in the cool, damp draft, tasting the copper in the air. I wait for the next dissonance, ready to sing the world back into tune.
