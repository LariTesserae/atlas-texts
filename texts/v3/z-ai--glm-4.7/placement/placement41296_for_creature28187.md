I would be found in the dark, protected hollow of the **Litho-Phage**, the immense, anvil-headed tortoise grazing in the shadow of the glass trees.

I am a **Tympanic-Keeper**.

I am not a distinct species in the way the taxonomists of old might have categorized things; I am a symbiote, a vestigial remnant of the Builders’ nanotechnology that has evolved, or perhaps *devolved*, into a living organ. I resemble a starfish made of polished obsidian and copper wire, my body no larger than a dinner plate. My five limbs are tipped with pads of sensitive, gelatinous matter that quiver at the slightest change in air pressure. I have no eyes, for sight is meaningless in a world where visibility changes with the shatter of every branch. I perceive solely through texture and tone.

My world is a cavern of roaring safety.

I cling to the inner ceiling of the Litho-Phage’s shell, anchored myself into the pitted, fused rock with tiny, diamond-hard hooks. Here, inside the great beast, the noise is different than it is outside. Outside, it is a chaotic, violent symphony—the screaming wind, the cracking crystal, the basso profundo groan of the tectonic heave. But inside the shell, those sounds are dampened, filtered through feet-thick basalt and obsidian until they become a low, rhythmic thrum. It is a heartbeat. A steady, grinding vibration that feels like a mother’s pulse, if your mother were a mountain.

My existence is a labor of constant tension. The Litho-Phage is a clumsy eater. It lumbers through the glass forest, snapping off crystalline fronds with a beak that can crush diamonds. When it swallows, the sound is deafening—a thunderous clatter of shards tumbling down its gullet, grinding against diamond teeth. If I were not here, the resonant frequency of that grinding would eventually match the frequency of the shell’s own structure, causing the Phage to vibrate itself into dust.

That is where I come in. I am the tuner.

I scuttle along the inner ribs of the shell, my nerve-endings reading the stress fractures in the stone before they happen. When the Phage takes a bite of a particularly resonant "singing bamboo" and the internal frequency spikes to a dangerous whine, I scurry to the point of maximum tension. I press my gelatinous pads against the vibrating rock and I *hum*. I emit a counter-frequency—a low, discordant buzz that cancels out the dangerous harmonic. I smooth the chaos. I keep the structure intact. I am the mute button on a furnace.

Through the "blowholes"—the narrow, spiraling respiratory vents that pierce the top of the shell—I can taste the outside world.

I smell the aggressive, thirsty dust blowing in from the basin. I feel the pressure waves of the Echo-Runners as they sprint past my host’s legs, their staccato footfalls tapping out frantic morse code messages on the obsidian hide. *Danger. Food. Mate. Run.*

Sometimes, when the Phage stops to rest, I feel the tremors of the Anchors—the monolithic black obelisks that jut from the ground like spears thrown by gods. They hum with a deep, unsettling power, a counter-frequency that keeps the planet from tearing itself apart. I can sense the Silver-Ghosts swarming around a broken obelisk in the distance, a cloud of chaotic, static-filled hunger. I urge the Phage to move away with a sharp, electric prick to its nerve cluster. We avoid the dead technology. It is too sharp, too silent, too hungry for things that are not its own.

But my true purpose, the reason I was forged and the reason I remain, is revealed in the moments of terror that come every few hours.

The lull.

The rules of the Ossuary are absolute: Silence is death. When the tectonic heave pauses, when the wind dies down for that brief, agonizing minute, the roar vanishes. In that silence, the Phage’s metabolism begins to shut down. Its digestive mill slows. Its internal temperature drops. The freezing high-altitude chill seeps into the shell. If the silence lasts too long, the Phage will crystallize into a statue of frozen frost and obsidian, and I will die with it.

I am the spark plug.

When the silence falls, I detach from the ceiling and drop onto the beast’s massive, heaving back. I unfurl my limbs to their fullest extent, stretching my copper-wire body tight. I connect to the Phage’s central nervous system—a series of conductive quartz rods running along its spine.

Then, I vibrate.

I shake. I channel every ounce of stored kinetic energy I have absorbed during the roaring hours into the beast’s back. I become a living tuning fork, shaking with such violence that my copper shell begins to glow red with heat. I transmit a high-pitched, frenzied shriek of vibration directly into the Phage’s body.

*Wake up. Keep moving. Digest. Burn.*

It is painful. It feels like my very atoms are being pulled apart. But it works. The Phage shudders, its sluggish heart kickstarted by my artificial rhythm. It grinds its jaws, creating a new, small noise—a self-made resonance to feed on. The heat returns.

During these lulls, the Echo-Runners come.

I feel their lightweight, hydraulic thuds as they leap onto the Phage’s shell. They are shivering, their vibrating cilia standing on end, desperate for the heat and the rhythm. They press their bone-encased skulls against the obsidian, siphoning the precious vibrations I am working so hard to generate. They are parasites, in a way, stealing the energy I create to keep the Phage alive. But I do not sting them. I let them huddle there, a pack of shivering, yipping warmth against the cold indifference of the indigo sky.

Because I know what happens when the silence wins. I have seen the Null-Weavers.

Through a blowhole, I once watched a Null-Weaver hunt. It didn't make a sound. It moved like a tear in reality, a spider-shaped hole in the air. It touched a Runner, and the Runner simply ceased. The vibration stopped. The heat stopped. The Runner fell, not dead, but *empty*. A hollow husk.

I hate the silence. The silence is the end.

And then, salvation. The ground heaves.

A massive, physical slam strikes the basin. The tectonic lung contracts. A roar like a thousand freight trains tears through the air, shaking the glass trees into a chiming frenzy. The pressure returns.

My task is done. I disconnect from the Phage’s spine, my body cooling, my copper shell contracting. I scuttle back up to the dark, damp safety of the inner ceiling. The Echo-Runners leap away, revitalized, sprinting back into the forest to hunt the sap that now flows freely from the shattered trees.

The Litho-Phage takes a heavy step, crunching through a layer of fallen glass leaves. The grinding returns. The thrumming returns.

I anchor myself, feeling the familiar, comforting vibration ripple through my hooks. I am small, hidden, and terrified, but I am tuned. I am the note that holds the chord together. I listen to the song of the dying planet, and I sing the beast to life.
