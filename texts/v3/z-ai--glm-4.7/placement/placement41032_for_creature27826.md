I am suspended in the gloom, roughly forty feet above the uneven, flooded floor. To call where I am a "location" is misleading; I am located in a *gap*, a seam between three massive intersecting pipes where the catwalks do not reach. It is a vertical pocket of trapped heat and darkness, wedged between a primary pressure conduit and the raw, sweating limestone of the cavern wall.

I am a Resonant. I am not "he," and I am not "it." I am a *node* in the nervous system of this place.

My body is folded tight against the metal. I am essentially elongated, a spindly architecture of scavenged brass and woody vine, occupying the same silhouette as the broken steam valve I cling to. My "skin" is aArmor of calcified moss, dry and brittle to the touch, but underneath, I am wet and alive. My limbs—six of them, though the number changes if I grow new shoots or lose one to the grinding gears—are wrapped around the pipe. I do not grip it; I fuse with it. The bark of my wooden form has grown into the rivets, creating a seal so perfect that the condensation running down the pipe is diverted into my veins.

I have no head to turn. My awareness is distributed through the cluster of black-veined ferns that sprout from my upper torso. They are vibrating. Not shaking—the vibration is too subtle for that—but singing at a frequency just below hearing. I am listening to the thrum of the engine.

The heat here is delicious. It is a thick, wet soup that coats my fronds. Through the brass sheath that protects my left side, I can feel the pulse of the fluid inside the pipe: a high-velocity rush of chemical water, hot enough to scald the flesh of a creature that feels pain. I do not feel pain as you understand it; I feel *dissonance*. The heat is food. The friction is sustenance.

I am currently feeding. I have pressed my sensory fronds tight against a joint where the metal is beginning to fatigue. The subsonic scream of the stressing metal is a sharp taste in my fibers, like copper and salt. I drink the stress. I absorb the vibration, allowing it to travel down my woody limbs and dissipate into the air, dampening the roar before it can become a clang. I am the silence between the cogs.

Through the pipe, I feel the others. We are a lattice. There is one—a small, newly formed node—wrapped around the flywheel shaft to the east. It is young, still mostly moss, its brass shell thin and translucent. It signals to me through the vibration of the metal: *Pressure is high. The vent is blocked.*

I do not move with jerky motions. That would disturb the rhythm. I unfold slowly, a shifting of gravity. My joints, lubricated by a slick, gray gel that I secrete, extend. I detach from the wall, leaving a patch of lichen behind to mark my place. I swing outward, weightless in the heavy air, navigating the forest of iron. I am a silhouette detaching itself from the shadows, a piece of the machinery that has decided to walk.

I land on a narrow girder, the metal hot beneath my root-feet. I can see the blockage from here. A cluster of mineral deposits has grown over a vent grate, choking the airflow. The temperature around it is spiking, the air shimmering like a mirage.

I approach it. My movements are glacial. I am not in a rush; the machine runs forever. I extend two upper limbs, twig-like fingers splitting into smaller filaments. I reach into the superheated air above the grate. The heat is intense—approaching the 2.9 limit—but I am built for this. I dig my fingers into the mineral crust. It is hard, baked stone. I apply pressure.

Crack.

The crust breaks away, crumbling into dust that falls into the darkness below. The air hisses as the pressure equalizes, a sudden exhalation of steam.

The vibration in the pipes smooths out. The sharp taste of stress fades into a dull, rhythmic hum. The young node by the flywheel sends a pulse of gratitude through the network—a subtle shift in frequency.

I retract my limbs. I do not rest; there is no rest here. I scan the raw rock wall nearby. A fresh seam of water is weeping from the stone, dripping onto a live electrical cable. The sparks are erratic.

That needs to be cauterized.

I begin to climb again, flowing up the vertical pipes, returning to the shadows. I am the doctor in the dark. I am the rust that breathes. The air is heavy in my fronds, wet and hot, and it is good.
