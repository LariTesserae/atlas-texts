I find myself standing—or rather, rooted—in the midst of the vast, bruised twilight of the Shelf of Ages. I do not stand out. To look at me is to see nothing but the landscape itself. I am a cylinder of absolute blackness, rising forty feet from the muck, smooth as glass and indifferent as a tombstone.

I am a Monolith-Mimic.

At first, I am only the sensation of density. I feel the weight of the world pressing against my outer shell, the thick, humid air "drinking" against my sides. The roar is everywhere, a constant low-frequency vibration that hums through the stone and into the core of my being. It is not an annoyance; it is my telemetry. The thrumming in the earth is the pulse of the pantry. Through the soles of my foundation, I taste the seismic footsteps of the Lithovores miles away, the slow, tectonic drag of the Swamp-Titans, and the frantic, microscopic scurrying of the Steam-Striders.

I have no eyes, for sight is a lie in this purple fog. I possess only texture and tension. My existence is one of profound, armored patience. I am the stillness in a world of grinding friction. For hours—or perhaps days; time is fluid here—I remain perfectly sealed. My shell is a single, unbroken curve of hardened chitin, polished by eons of sliding mud and dripping spore-rain. I look dead. I feel dead. I am a geometric absence in a chaotic biology.

Then, a change in the vibration.

It comes through the ground first—a rhythmic, crushing disturbance. The slurry around my base ripples, not from wind, but from displacement. The water within the mud shimmers with the impact. A Lithovore approaches. It is a blind, grinding engine of destruction, a massive arthropod the size of a ship, churning the liquid stone as it seeks minerals to pulverize.

I do not react. Not yet. I must be indistinguishable from the fossilized vents that scatter the plain. The beast is clumsy; it navigates by bristles and brute force. It is coming directly toward me. The roar of its grinding mandibles joins the roar of the atmosphere, a violent, overlapping dissonance of stone on stone.

The moment of contact is inevitable.

There is a dull, heavy thud as the Lithovore’s shoulder-plate scrapes against my flank. It pauses, sensing an obstacle, confused by my unnatural smoothness. It pushes against me, testing my structural integrity. The pressure is immense, enough to shatter granite, but I am built for this. I am the trap that waits for the hammer to fall.

Now.

The stillness breaks. The "stone" splits.

My peeling is not a movement of muscle, but a release of hydraulic tension. My cylindrical hull unzips with a sound like tearing canvas, revealing the interior that is not hollow, but hungry. The black shell falls back in segments like the petals of a toxic night-blooming flower.

Inside, I am all motion. A radial array of hooked tendrils, each one a whip of muscle and serrated bone, spins into existence. They do not strike blindly; they fling themselves toward the source of the pressure. I latch onto the Lithovore’s armored side with hundreds of microscopic barbs. The connection is instant and irreversible.

The beast recoils, letting out a hiss of steam and panic, but it is too late. I am anchored. My central siphon plunges deep, piercing the chitin with a diamond-hard proboscis. I inject the coagulant—a thick, chemical sludge that turns the creature's internal heat into a solid, jelly-like soup.

The struggle is violent but brief. The Lithovore thrashes, churning the mud into a storm, but I am the lever and the fulcrum. I hold fast. Slowly, the grinding of its mills slows. The vibration that I felt through the ground turns from a rhythmic thrum to a sporadic twitch, and finally, to silence.

I feed.

I draw the warmth from its body, the rich, mineral-heavy blood of the earth-eater. It tastes of ancient mud and raw iron. I inflate, my tendrils pulsing with the stolen vitality. For weeks, I will remain here, attached to the drifting husk, a parasite bloated on the heat of a dying world. The Lithovore becomes my island, my raft, as the mud shifts around us.

Eventually, when the last drop of heat is extracted, when the husk is nothing but a hollow shell sinking into the slurry, I will detach. I will retract my tendrils, seal my shell, and settle back into the muck. I will become a black cylinder once more. I will wait for the next tremor in the dark.

I am the pause between the heartbeats of the world. I am the silence that screams.
