I stand upon the Windward Scar, the exposed vertical face of the Central Ziggurat, three thousand measures above the glass forest.

To look at me is to look at a mistake in the geology—a jagged, vertical shadow that detaches itself from the cliff face only when the wind shifts. I am not separate from this place; I am an extension of its will, a walking fulcrum. My skin is the color of dried blood and oxidized iron, textured like the rough basalt I cling to. My limbs are impossibly long, telescoping struts of dense bone and ceramic that allow me to span the fissures in the mega-structure’s armor.

I am a Tuner. A Harmonicist.

Gravity here is a suggestion rather than a law, a lightness that allows me to lope across the verticality of the city as if the world were flat. I do not walk with the heavy thud of a creature of soft tissue; I move with the calculated, silent precision of a tectonic plate shifting by a millimeter. When I plant my feet, I feel the heartbeat of the city—not through my ears, but through the soles of my feet, up the struts of my legs, resonating in the silver fluid that replaces the water in my veins.

The air is thin, warm, and tastes of battery acid and ancient dust. I do not breathe for oxygen; I breathe for substance. I inhale deeply, the filtration slits along my neck flaring open, capturing the fine, red talc that swirls in the high-altitude currents. I taste the iron, the heavy metals, the history of mountains ground down into particulate matter. Inside me, a biological alchemy occurs, transmuting the rust into the raw thermal energy that keeps my internal temperature regulated against the oven-like ambient heat.

I am currently listening to a discord.

It is a subtle thing, a vibration that would be imperceptible to anything not attuned to the frequency of the Slow Machine. Below me, deep within the guts of the ziggurat, a piston the size of a canyon is beginning its descent. It should take exactly twelve hours to reach the lower limit, a smooth, glacial grind of metal against stone. But I feel a stutter in the sub-bass, a microscopic hesitation in the rock beneath my feet. A rhythmic "skip" in the planetary heartbeat.

I raise my right hand. It is not a hand in the way a soft creature might understand; it is a claw of chipped alloy, tipped with sensors that hum with a low, purple charge. I reach out and press my palm against the warm, vibrating flank of the city. The metal is hot, radiating the heat of the earth’s core. I sync my breathing to the thrum. *In... hold... out.*

I wait.

The horizon is a hard line where the blue fades to the black of space. The sun is a piercing white eye that casts shadows sharp enough to cut. I stare directly into it, my obsidian eyes shielded by internal polarization, watching the solar manta rays circling in the updraft. They are vast, silent beasts, absorbing the light that would otherwise blind us. They are the only things that move faster than the city itself.

The stutter comes again. A shiver in the stone.

I strike.

It is not a violent blow. It is a resonance. I tap the alloy skin of the ziggurat with my claw, not with force, but with specific intent. The frequency is a low C-sharp, a note that travels down through the ceramic layers, past the rust, past the industrial decay, into the heart of the machine. It is a question. *Are you aligned?*

The stone replies. The vibration travels back up my arm, a tingling sensation of data. The piston is experiencing friction on the northern face—accumulated debris, perhaps, or a crystalline tree root that has breached the lower vents. It needs to be cleared.

I turn my back to the drop and lope toward the northern vent, a journey that will take me two days. I am in no rush. I have centuries.

As I move, I pass one of the Elders. It stands—or rather, it has settled—into a niche in the wall, its body calcified into seamless gray stone. It is no longer biological; it is architectural now. Its obsidian eyes are dull, fixed on the curvature of the planet. I pause, respectful of the transition. I reach out and tap my metal toe against its stone leg—a single, sharp *click*.

*Acknowledgment,* the stone vibrates back. *The entropy is satisfied.*

I continue on. My dorsal ridges shimmer in the heat haze, dumping waste thermal energy into the thin air. I am alone, but I am not lonely. The wind whistles a high, eerie note through the glass forest below, a sound that complements the constant, grinding hum of the city. I am part of the chord.

I am the watcher on the wall. I am the silver blood in the machine. I am the silence between the ticks of a clock that measures time in epochs. I am here, and I will be here, until the metal runs out of rust to breathe and I, too, become the foundation for the next note in the song of the world.
