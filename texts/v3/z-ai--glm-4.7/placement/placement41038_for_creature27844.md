I would find myself anchored to the vertical world, suspended halfway between the crushing obsidian floor and the void where the stars ought to be.

I would be a **High-Anchor**, a sessile organism that has colonized the sheer, smooth flanks of the monolithic pillars—the ancient, fossilized bones of the dead civilization that jut from the caldera like broken fingers.

**My Form**

I am not a grazer, nor am I an ambush predator. I am a filter and a net in a world of dust. My body is a cluster of hardened, calcified nodes that grip the polished marble of the pillar like a rust stain made of stone. From this central anchor, I extend dozens of long, delicate, filament-thin tendrils made of spun glass and copper wire.

These tendrils do not hang down due to gravity; the air here is too thin and too hot for that. Instead, they float upward, buoyed by the thermal currents rising from the baking floor, splaying out into a radial fan above me. I look like a sea anemone frozen in the moment of blooming, turned to stone and iron, reaching blindly upward into the crushing dark.

**My Existence**

I live in the "Middle Silence." Down below, the grazer tide is a frantic, grinding mill; up above, the void is absolute. Here on the pillar, the vibrations of the millions of scraping legs travel up the stone and into my body. I do not have ears to hear, but my entire skeleton resonates with the *thrum* of the caldera. I feel the mountain’s digestion as a constant, physical itch in my carapace.

Because I am silicon-based, the heat does not kill me; it animates me. The thermal updrafts that keep my glass tendrils aloft also carry the fine, glittering dust—the excrement of the grazers, the pulverized remains of the mountain itself. My tendrils are charged with a static attractant, invisible and silent. They snag the floating iron and silica dust from the air as it rises.

Over time, the dust coats my filaments, building up layer upon layer of metallic grey. When a filament becomes too heavy, I retract it, digesting the accumulated minerals to reinforce my own shell, and then spin a new one. I am the recycler of the high air. I take what the grazers discard and turn it into armor.

**My Perspective**

From my vantage point, clinging to the side of the monolith, the world below is a map of shifting, dim constellations. I cannot see the individual glass-feeders clearly, but I can sense their bioluminescence through the heat-sensitive pits along my back.

To me, the floor of the caldera is not a dark pit; it is a bed of cooling embers. The friction of the grazers creates a perpetual, dull orange haze, punctuated by the violent, sickly violet flares of the lithosects when they detonate upward from the stone. I feel those strikes before I see them—a sudden, sharp *tremor* in the rock that rattles my anchor points. Then, a fraction of a second later, the shockwave displaces the air, and my glass filaments shiver in the breeze, scattering a cloud of captured dust like gold confetti.

**The Ritual of the Molters**

My existence is most entwined with the grazers during their time of vulnerability. The pillars I cling to are the sanctuaries for the molting. I am a bystander to their birth and death.

Often, a grazer—massive, heavy with the weight of its glass shell—will climb past me. I feel the rhythmic *scrape-scrape-scrape* of its legs against the stone inches from my body. It ignores me. I have no flesh to tempt it, and my shell is as hard as the pillar it grips.

It anchors itself above me to molt. I feel the tremor of its struggle, the cracking of its old armor. When the husk falls, it tumbles past me, shattering on the lower slopes or being caught by other, scavenging climbers. The new, soft grazer descends, leaving behind a hollow shell fused to the stone.

Over eons, these shells have piled up around my anchor points. I have grown over them, incorporating their discarded glass into my own structure. I am a living collage of the history of this place. My base is layered with the fossilized remains of hundreds of generations of red and black mosaics.

I am the silent witness on the wall. I do not hunt; I endure. I drink the dust of the dying world and spin glass into the dark, waiting for the heat to eventually break me down into sand, returning me to the mosaic floor below.
