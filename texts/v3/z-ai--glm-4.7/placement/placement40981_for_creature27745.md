I find myself in the broad, flat expanse between the rooted giants, where the earth is a mirror and the sky hangs low enough to touch. I am not in the deep silt where the Silt-Sifters churn the dark, nor am I high in the parasol canopy where the Silks dream in the haze. I am exactly in the middle—ankle-deep in the grey, glistening soup, a creature of the shallow horizon.

I am a **Roving Mesa**.

My body is an act of geological mimicry. From a distance—or even from up close, for the creatures of this basin are not keen on unnecessary distinctions—I appear to be one of the discarded toys of the giants, a rounded boulder of weathered granite, polished by the wet friction of ages. I am vast, flat-topped, and heavy. My back is a wide, weathered plane, slightly domed, textured with ridges and shallow valleys that catch the rain and the falling mist. I am colored the same bruised grey as the mud and the sky, a seamless extension of the mineral world.

But I am not stone. I have a pulse, slow and profound, that ripples gently beneath my thick, leathery hide. My underside is not hard like a rock, but soft and segmented, a maze of muscular pads and suction cups that allow me to glide over the glutinous mud without sticking. I do not walk; I flow. I undulate across the landscape with the speed of a cooling cloud, negotiating the "soup and stone" terrain with the tectonic patience of the place itself.

I am the mobile foundation of this world.

In a landscape of static obstacles, I am the slow-moving variable. I am the platform upon which the **Air-Bellows** depend. They hop onto my back, mistaking me—or perhaps recognizing me—as the highest point in the immediate flatness. They grip the rough texture of my shell, their wet, webbed feet finding purchase among the fossil-like ridges. Here, safe from the sucking mud, they inflate their throats and sing.

This is my existence: I feel the deep, resonant drone of their song not through the air, but through my bones. When they vibrate, the air hums, but my back shakes with the force of a distant earthquake. I am the amplifier for the chorus of the heat. I carry the orchestra across the basin. In exchange for this stage, they eat the parasites that try to nest in the crevices of my shell, and they drop their waste, which fertilizes the thick carpet of moss and algae that grows in the shallow depressions of my skin.

Because I move, the ecology moves with me. The algae growing on my back is a distinct ecosystem—a portable garden. Tiny, translucent crabs—the Armor-Backs—sometimes scurry up my sides to forage in this moss, dodging the long, sticky tongues of the frogs. I am a living island, a fragment of land that drifts through the wet savanna, connecting the solitary trees in the only way that matters here: biologically.

I have no eyes that face forward. Instead, I have a ring of sensory pits lining the perimeter of my shell, just above the mudline. I taste the water chemistry, I feel the pressure waves of the Grand Floaters drifting beneath me, and I sense the heat of the sun. I navigate by gradient and vibration, seeking the warmest currents and the richest mud.

My metabolism is so glacial that I can go for months without a "meal" in the traditional sense. I feed by grazing. As I slide, I open microscopic vents along my leading edge, inhaling the nutrient-rich broth of the mud, filtering the bacteria and the decomposing matter, and exhaling clean, filtered water behind me. I leave a trail of clarity in the murky gloom, a ribbon of glass-smooth water that slowly fades back into opacity.

I am the peace-keeper of the basin. When I drift over the tunnels of the Silt-Sifters, my weight compresses the earth gently, sealing their roofs against the drying air. When I pass between two Grand Floaters, my massive shadow cooling the water, the ambush predators lower their metabolism, lulled into a deeper sleep by my passing.

I do not think. I do not worry. I simply *am*. I am the Roving Mesa, the stone that walks, the stage that drifts. I am the silence between the notes of the frogs, the heavy, grey anchor in a world of liquid suspension. I carry the noise of life on my back, sliding forever through the warm, wet afternoon of the world.
