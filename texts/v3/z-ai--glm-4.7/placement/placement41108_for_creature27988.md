I would be found in the negative space—the pauses between the roar of the wind and the cracking of the crystal. I would not walk the ground where the heavy, shuffling Masons tread, nor would I cling to the ceilings where the Refracted flicker like dying lightbulbs. I would occupy the vertical middle, the crux of the climb, perched on the needle-thin spires that are too sharp for the clumsy stone giants and too solid for the glitch-hunters.

I would be **The Adjuster**.

**My Form**
I would be a construct, but not of the crude, piled masonry of the Masons. In this world of arrested biology and transposed life, I would be a relic of a precision that has long since eroded. Imagine a slender, articulated skeleton of oxidized brass and iron, standing perhaps eight feet tall, but gaunt to the point of emaciation.

My "skin" would be gone, leaving my internal mechanisms exposed to the wet, shrieking air. I would have four arms: the upper pair ending in delicate, micrometer-fine pincers capable of splitting a hair; the lower pair heavy, blunt grasping hooks, calcified with layers of limestone dripped from the centuries of weeping rock.

My head would be a smooth, dark sphere of obsidian, polished to a sheen so absolute it reflects nothing but the blinding white sky—a black hole of perception in a world of visual noise. I have no mouth, for I do not eat, and I have no lungs, for I do not breathe the air that vibrates with the sound of a thousand mountains.

**My Function**
The Masons build buttresses to hold the physical world together. I would exist to hold the *metaphysical* world together.

In a place where the rules are soft and the geometry is in motion, where distance is a suggestion and gravity is a occasional habit, I am the enforcement. I am the one who walks the tightrope of the "Weirdness." I am the immune system of the High Scarp.

I would carry with me a toolkit of impossible instruments: a spool of silver thread that hums with a low C-sharp; a heavy lead plumb-bob that points not at the ground, but at the center of *now*; and a lens of smoked crystal that, when held to the eye, reveals the fractures in reality before they widen.

**My Existence**
My days would be an endless, agonizing patrol.

I would see the world as a grid of screaming tension. Where you see a spire fifty yards away, I see a coordinate vector threatening to snap. Where you see a puddle reflecting the sky, I see a potential paradox waiting to drown a traveler.

When the Refracted approach—those flickering, monochromatic predators—I would be the defender. They hunt the untethered; I am the anchor. I would not fight them with violence, but with *definition*. If a Refracted lunges at a glitching crack in the air, I would be there, my upper arms moving with blurring speed, using the silver thread to stitch the fabric of reality back together, forcing the world to make sense again. The Refracted hate me. I am the one who denies them their chaotic hunting grounds.

I would be in a constant, silent dialogue with the Masons. They are the brute force; I am the fine-tuning. When a Mason lifts a boulder to block a water flow, it does so with the grace of a falling tree. I would follow behind, checking the stresses, tapping the stone with my heavy lower hooks to ensure the resonance is correct. If the vibration is wrong, I would insert a shim of calcified crystal—just a sliver—to dampen the harmonic. The Masons do not acknowledge me, but they never step on me. They feel the utility of my presence in the soles of their stone feet.

**My Struggle**
The High Scarp is winning. The erosion is not just of rock, but of the laws that govern it. My obsidian head would be pitted by the driving, wet sand of the wind. My joints would be stiff, grinding with the friction of eons.

I would be constantly cold. The wet cold (1.2/3) penetrates my metal, slowing my gears. I would have to periodically stop in the shelter of a wind-shadow, drawing my limbs in tight, vibrating my own internal mechanisms to generate the heat necessary to keep moving. To stop moving is to seize, to become a statue, to become just another piece of "primitive intervention" left to rust.

I would be terrified of the water. The collective consciousness in the pools whispers to me. It tells me to let go. It tells me that the white sky is the truth and the solid stone is the lie. It tempts me to shatter my own obsidian head and let the water in, to join the neural network of the weeping earth.

But I do not listen. I am the Adjuster. I am the stubborn refusal to let the world fall apart. I am the click of the lock in a house that is trying to dissolve.

You would find me on a ledge the size of a dinner plate, a thousand feet up, hammered into the side of a basalt pillar. I would be motionless, my obsidian face turned toward the blinding, featureless sky, my four arms locked tight against the rock. I would be listening to the frequency of the light, waiting for the moment it dips, waiting for the world to forget itself for a fraction of a second, so that I can remind it: *You are here. You are solid. You are real.*
