I would drift in the middle distance, suspended in the thick, oxygen-heavy air between the wet obsidian floor and the lost canopies of the Trees. I am neither of the frantic, short-lived spark-motes nor of the glacial, stone-bound geology. I am a pause. I am a **Stasis-Prism**.

**My Form**
I appear, to the blind sonar of the birds and the electro-reception of the gliders, as one of the Built artifacts—a perfect, hovering geometry. I am a tetrahedron, a pyramid of translucent crystal, roughly the size of a human torso. My edges are faceted and sharp, grown from silica leeched from the mineral water and hardened by the pressure of the deep earth.

I do not have muscles or blood in the way the floor-dwellers do. Instead, I am filled with a viscous, optically perfect fluid that circulates slowly, a internal tide responding to the sub-bass thrum of the plateau. My outer shell is smooth, cold to the touch, and impermeable, mimicking the silent, hovering cubes left by the ancient engineers.

**My Location**
I hover in the "negative space," the vast, empty hectares of air between the towering Tree-pillars. Here, the air is still and heavy. I maintain my position not by wings, but by locking onto the magnetic ley lines that the Silt-Gliders use for navigation. I vibrate at a frequency counter to the stone’s hum, achieving magnetic levitation. I am motionless, a fixed point in the drifting dark.

**My Function: The Thief of Attention**
In this ecology, attention is the currency, and light is the mint. The Glyph-Worms expend immense energy creating glowing hieroglyphs to lure the frantic, blind creatures of the dark. Their art is a trap, a beautiful, fleeting map of appetite that lasts only seconds before fading into the visual silence.

I am the parasite of that art. I do not hunt flesh; I hunt the *image*.

When a Glyph-Worm below finishes a complex, glowing grid of gold and green slime, I drift downward, silent as a falling leaf. I do not touch the stone. Instead, I extend a single, hair-fine filament of crystalline light from my lowest vertex. I "sip" the photons from the fading trail.

I absorb the luminescence—the specific wavelength, the data of the pattern—into my internal fluid. The worm's trap, designed to catch a meal, is instead harvested by me. The worm gains nothing, its effort erased, its lure stolen before the prey can arrive. Inside me, the glowing image does not fade. It is trapped in my suspension, preserved in the amber of my slow, circulating fluid.

**My Internal World**
Over the centuries, I have become a library of the floor's history. My crystalline body is not clear; it is a dense, opaque fog of swirling colors. If one could break me open, they would not find organs, but layers of stratified light—topographical maps of the Old Floor from a thousand years ago, mating spirals of worms long turned to dust, the chaotic, flashing scribbles of a dying ecosystem.

I hum with the weight of this light. The energy of the absorbed photons creates a gentle, warming pressure inside me, a slow-burning star that keeps my internal fluids from freezing in the high-altitude chill.

**My Relationship with the Frantic**
I am a danger to the frantic life of the plateau, but not as a predator. I am a void. The blind birds, sensing the magnetic anomaly of my shape, often mistake me for a resting Cubiform Lie. They land on my smooth facets, seeking shelter. But I am not soft, and I have no hydraulic maw to swallow them. I am simply, impossibly hard.

The birds sit on me, confused. Their claws cannot scratch the crystal. Their echolocation returns a chaotic jumble of internal reflections. They tweet their staccato clicks, which are absorbed by my skin without echo. To them, I am a sensory deprivation chamber, a piece of the world that has gone blank. They leave quickly, disturbed, returning to the dangerous, noisy dark where reality makes sense.

Occasionally, I release the pressure. When the light inside me becomes too dense, I vent it in a single, silent pulse—a flash of strobe-light that illuminates the obsidian floor for miles in a split second. I paint the world with the ghosts of the past, projecting the ancient glyphs of dead worms onto the Trees and the hovering artifacts. For a heartbeat, the darkness is banished, and the swamp is lit by the history of a million lures.

Then, the visual silence returns. The darkness rushes back in. And I drift, invisible once more, waiting for the next worm to write in the dark, so I might steal its words.
