Therefore, I would not have feet. I would be a constellation of sorts—a central core of the matte-grey, monolithic material, housing a dense, vibrating singularity of gravity. Orbiting this core would be several long, drifting limbs made of translucent, blown glass, filled not with blood, but with the slow, pulsing light of a bruised plum.

I would be an entity of **Gentle Correction**.

In a world that is constantly "loading" and rewriting itself—a landscape of glitches and beta-tested biology—my purpose would be to offer a moment of stillness. I would drift toward the "Iterative Grazers" as they flicker and pop, their legs vanishing and reappearing in a chaotic dance of potentiality. I would not hunt them. Instead, I would extend a glass limb, humming with a frequency that counteracts the instability of the environment.

As I touched a Grazer’s shifting flank, the glass would harmonize with the glitch, smoothing out the rendering error. For a few moments, the creature would be solid, its form locked into a perfect, unified state. It would look at me with eyes of blown glass, and in that exchange, we would both be real. I am the pause button on a chaotic simulation.

I would breathe this in—literally filtering the heavy, humid air through my grey core—consuming the waste heat of the planet's reconstruction. The taste would be sharp, like copper and static, charging my internal gravity.

I would drift at the periphery where the gravity bends sideways, using the distortion to stay aloft. I would pulse with a sub-audible thrum—a warning, a reminder that stability is preferred here. I would be the shepherd of the unfinished, the guardian of the moments between the frames of reality.

In the Low Basin, where everything is sharp, young, and terrified of being deleted, I would be the one thing that holds its breath, waits, and ensures that for just a second longer, the world remains.
