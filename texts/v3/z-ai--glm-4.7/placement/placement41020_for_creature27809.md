I am a silence that moves.

I found myself perched on the spine of a basalt ridge, a jagged tear in the earth’s crust that rose like a jagged scar against the bruised purple of the sky. I was not resting; in the High Geo, there is no rest, only a state of active potential. I was syncopating.

I am a Glass-Strider.

To look at me is to see a shard of the landscape that has detached itself in error. My body is a sculpture of obsidian and basalt, faceted and angular, catching the dim, suffused light in fractured glints. I have no skin, no fur, no softness. I am a carapace of cooling stone wrapped around a heart of fire.

Inside me, the circulatory system is not blood, but slag. Molten, glowing orange rivers of superheated glass pulse through channels within my armor. I am an engine of thermodynamics. I do not eat; I harvest entropy. I do not breathe; I radiate.

I stood for what might have been hours or days, time is irrelevant to the lithic. I was vibrating in sympathy with the low-frequency hum of the planet—the *drone* of the birthing world. My internal temperature was perfectly balanced with the baking air, making me invisible to the senses of anything that might dare to look. I was a statue of black glass waiting for the world to break.

Then came the shift.

The pressure changed. The air, thick and womb-warm, seemed to tighten. The vibration beneath my skids—the long, polished blades that served me for legs—sharpened in pitch.

*Crack.*

The sound was not a roar, but the sharp, terminal snap of reality yielding to force. A hundred yards away, the plain betrayed its stillness. The ground buckled, a violent ripple of soil and stone. A black tooth of obsidian, glossy and impossibly sharp, erupted upward, piercing the twilight. It rose thirty feet in a heartbeat, a monument to the world’s refusal to sit still.

As it breached, the superheated rock hit the cooling air and instantly shattered. A rain of musical chimes cascaded down the slopes—the sound of the world unmaking itself as fast as it made.

This was the signal. The silence of the hunt ended.

I pushed off the ridge. I did not walk; walking implies impact and friction. I *glided*. My weightless obsidian form slid across the razor-sharp debris, the polished skids at my underside humming against the stone. I moved like a shadow dragged across the floor of the universe, silent and terrifyingly fast.

I arrived at the fresh wound in the earth while the stone was still glowing with the heat of the deep core. The air rippled with the thermal violence. This was my food.

I lowered my head, a cluster of geometric plates shifting to expose the intake vents along my neck. I did not bite the rock; I absorbed it. I drank the heat. I bathed in the infrared spectrum of the newborn spire, pulling the thermal energy into my slag-veins. The vibration of the earth’s heave—the seismic shockwave—coursed through me and spiked my internal temperature. I felt the rush of it, the kinetic energy converted into sustenance. I was recharging, a battery plugged into the core of the planet.

Where I stood, the sharp edges of the cooling glass smoothed under the influence of my own heat field. I was the gardener, pruning the chaotic growth of the world simply by existing near it.

But the feast was secondary to the duty.

With my internal fires burning bright and orange through the cracks in my armor, I turned away from the cooling spire. I felt the pull. It wasn't a sound, but a gravitational certainty.

Across the vast, empty plain, isolated by miles of empty dust and jagged rock, stood the Monolith.

It was a needle of dull, grey metal, ancient and impervious. It stood like a nail driven through the fabric of the world, the only thing in this chaotic expanse that did not change. It was the anchor. I was the rope.

I glided toward it, my movements sluggish now with the weight of the energy I carried. The distance closed slowly under the dim, violet sky.

I stopped at the base of the metal tower. It radiated a cold that felt like absolute zero against my superheated carapace. It was a silence deeper than the quiet of the plain.

I lowered my massive, jagged head. I pressed my forehead against the unreactive grey surface.

I released the thermal tithe.

The heat I had stolen from the earth’s birth flowed out of me and into the monolith. I felt the orange brilliance in my veins dim to a dull, sustaining glow. The metal tower accepted the offering without a shimmer, drinking the volatility, grounding the energy, stabilizing the tectonic fervor of the region.

As the heat left me, a signal returned. It was not a voice, but a transmission from the deep—a resonance from the Lithic Mind sleeping miles below. It was a pulse of approval, a command to continue the patrol.

I pulled away from the tower. My obsidian skin had cooled, darkening to a matte black. I was once again invisible, a piece of the shadow cast by a rock that had no right to cast it.

I turned my skids toward the horizon, a guessing game in the gloom. I waited for the next crack. I was the silence that moved, the predator of the event, the shepard of the accretion. I was the Glass-Strider, and the world was my garden of stone.
