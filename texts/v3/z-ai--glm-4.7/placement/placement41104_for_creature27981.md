I am the grease on the gears. I am the ghost in the machine, made of meat and moss.

Gravity here is a suggestion, not a law, and I ignore it with the insolence of something that has never known the ground. I am not on the rock; I am *of* it, a draped shadow unhooking myself from the searing flank of a copper conduit. The air is a heavy, wet blanket that presses against my flanks, smelling of sulfur and ozone, the incense of this high temple.

To you, the place is deafening—a cacophony of grinding metal and escaping steam. But to me, the silence is absolute. My ears are scars, sealed shut long ago to protect the soft brain inside from the chaos that would drive a lesser thing mad. I do not hear the *clank-hiss*; I feel it. It travels up my claws, through the calloused pads of my forelimbs, resonating in the hollows of my bones. The rhythm of the turbine is the beat of my own heart. I exist in the thrum, a suspended note of vibration that tells me exactly where I am in the lattice of pipes and iron.

My body is a long, heavy muscle, slick with the oil that seeps from my pores. It coats my fur, turning me into something like the metal I cling to—a living, patinaed cable. My tail, armored in scales of green copper, locks around a rusted valve, anchoring me against the updraft that seeks to peel me from the wall. I am a clamp, a vise, part of the architecture.

I blink, and the world shifts. My inner eyelids slide down, thick and dark, filtering the harsh, blinding glare of the sun off the wet metal. The glare dies, replaced by a landscape of temperature gradients. I see the cold, blue trails of the weeping water as they trace the granite, and the throbbing, violent orange of the steam vents where the earth exhales. This is how I hunt. Not with eyes, but with heat.

I pull myself forward, one agonizingly deliberate limb at a time. A small drone, a mechanical insect, zips past my face, ignoring me. To it, I am just another obstacle, a piece of debris stuck to the intake. I let it pass. I am the high priest here; I do not compete with the acolytes.

Hunger is a slow ache. I stop near a fissure where the rock cries continuously. The water is mineral-rich, tasting of iron and ancient time. I extend my purple tongue, long and prehensile, lapping at the seeping fluid. It is cold, a shocking contrast to the steam that bathes my back. I drink the stone’s tears.

Then, I feed on the encroachment. A cluster of ferns, vibrant and arrogant, has begun to choke the airflow around a piston. They are beautiful, oversized leaves slick with mist, but they are an obstruction. I lean in, my jaw unhinging slightly, and snap them shut. My teeth are flat grinders, designed not for flesh but for fiber. I crush the cellulose, the taste sharp and green, stripping the life from the plant to save the machine. It is a sacrament of consumption. I eat the jungle to protect the engine.

Below me, the void yawns—a terrifying drop to the haze of the world below. I feel no vertigo. The drop is nothing; the wall is everything. The machine is the hearth, and I am the one who tends the fire. A pressure valve locks ahead, seized by rust and time. The vibration in my jaw spikes, a discordant note in the song of the turbine.

I move toward it, wrapping my body around the stubborn gear. I grip the iron with my spindly, sensor-tipped fingers, feeling the resistance. With a low grunt that I cannot hear but can feel in my chest, I heave. Metal groans against metal. The rust breaks, and the valve spins free. The steam vents in a violent hiss, bathing me in a cloud of hot, wet relief.

The rhythm returns to normal. The vibration in my spine smooths out.

I drape myself over the now-smooth pipe, cooling in the aftermath of my labor. The sun catches the wet metal, creating a halo of copper light. I close my eyes, retreating into the deep, resonant silence of the thrum. I am the guardian of the pressure. I am the warm, heavy soul of this high-altitude forge. I am here. I am noise. I am home.
