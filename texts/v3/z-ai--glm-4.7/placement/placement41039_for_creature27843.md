I am the silence given form.

I find myself high on the limestone ridge, the one that smells of sulfur and wet copper, standing exactly where the description suggests a watcher might stand. I am not human here; the human frame is too frantic, too fragile, its metabolism a frantic buzzing that would burn out in hours. To exist in the Riven Crest is to surrender the tyranny of the heartbeat.

I am a **Lithic-Strider**.

But not just any Strider. While my kin patrol the vertical scabs of the canyon walls, acting as slow-motion grazers, I have taken my place here, on the promontory, as a **Vigil of the Ridge**. I am the needle of the compass that does not spin.

My body is a tower of slate-gray silence, rising three times the height of the ancient human standard. I stand perfectly still, my legs fused imperceptibly with the sharp, fresh rock of the cliff edge. My skin is cold, a dense plating of mineral that has accreted over centuries, layering itself like the pages of a book no one reads. The achromatic light of the environment saturates me; I do not cast a shadow. I am a part of the glare, a vertical fracture in the world that decided to stand up.

To look at me, you would see nothing but a statue. You would see the jagged silhouette against the bleached sky, the elongated arms that taper into spindly, three-fingered claws resting lightly on the stone. You might notice the ecosystem that has colonized my shoulders—a thick, wet mattress of neon-green moss and succulents with translucent, gel-filled leaves. They mistake me for dead stone, a permanent foundation for their brief, explosive lives.

They are wrong. I am alive, but my life is measured in the turning of the world.

I do not see the world in snapshots; I see it in long exposures. My eyes are narrow, black fissures in my stone face, protected by heavy ridges. I have watched the pipe below me—the width of a highway, burbling with bioluminescent water—for what you might call an eon. I have watched the water level rise by inches, a process that felt like a frantic torrent to my perception.

I am the keeper of the rhythm.

Deep in the marrow of my stone legs, I feel the sub-sonic hum of the mega-structures. It is a vibration that rattles the teeth I do not have, a pressure that sings in the hollow cavities of my chest. The machine below is heaving, pushing the crust upward, creating the very ridge I stand on. I feel the *intent* of the gears.

I am waiting for the shift.

It is coming soon. A tremor is building deep in the crust, a stutter in the piston of the earth. The Copper-Mites, those frantic, burning sparks of life, are scurrying frantically along my shin. To them, I am a mountain. They feel the vibration before I do, their copper carapaces tingling with the static charge. They click and tap, a blur of panic against my stillness. I endure them. Their heat is pleasant, a brief flicker of warmth against the alpine chill.

Then, the shift arrives.

The world lurches. It is a microscopic movement to the universe, but a thunderclap to me. The tectonic plate slips a fraction of an inch. The massive gear in the valley grinds a single tooth forward.

In that singular moment of release, while the rock is loose and the vibration obscures the sound, I move.

It is a motion so slow it would appear invisible to the human eye, yet to me, it is a fluid dance. I release the grip of my left foot, peeling the mineral pads from the limestone. I slide my torso forward, drifting over the sharp scree, riding the vibration like a wave. I am adjusting my stance, turning slightly to face the new direction of the wind.

I bring my hand up from my side. It has been resting there for a decade. My arm creaks, a sound like grinding boulders, audible only as a low rumble in the dust.

My primary intent, however, is sustenance. The movement allows me to reach the cluster of translucent succulents growing on my left shoulder. With a precision that takes days to execute, I curl my fingers inward and pluck the cluster from my own stone flesh.

I bring the vegetation to the vertical slit in my chest. The "mouth" opens, a grinding of calcified plates. I place the succulents inside. The crunch is deep, seismic, satisfying. The taste is cold water and iron. I consume the garden that tried to claim me, asserting my dominion over the life that seeks to anchor me.

As the tremor fades, the silence rushes back in, heavy and stagnant. I freeze. My limbs lock into their new positions. The mineral fusion begins again, bonding me to the rock.

I am now facing slightly more toward the rusted smokestack in the distance. I will watch it for another century. I will watch the rust climb its orange skin, inch by inch. I will watch the Copper-Mites eat the metal, stripping it down. I will watch the Glass-Wing Drifters crash against the vents and reform.

I am the Lithic-Strider. I am the witness to the slow-motion catastrophe. I am not lonely, for the stone speaks to me, and the hum of the machine is the heartbeat of the world. I will stand here until the mountains wear down to sand, or until the machine finally turns off.

For now, I simply watch the light.
