I find myself in the **shadow of the floor**.

To the Resonant Feeders, the world is a vertical vault of wet iron and deafening pressure, but to me, their floor is my ceiling. I inhabit the underside of the checker-plate grating, clinging to the steel like a white barnacle to the belly of a leviathan. I live in the narrow, suffocating gap between the deck and the abyss, suspended over the black drop that plunges into the unknown bowels of the mechanism.

My world is defined by a "sky" that is not open air, but a grid of glowing amber lines. The weak light from the safety cages above filters through the thousands of punched holes in the grating, creating for me a constellation of burning dots on a black canvas. It is a reverse universe: my night is lit not by stars, but by the light squeezing through the footsteps of giants.

I am an **Underside-Gardener**, a creature of the deep damp that few ever witness, for I exist only in the inches of space that the Feeders ignore as they stride above me.

**My Form**
I am shaped like a despairing parenthesis, my body curled and flattened to hug the underbelly of the steel. I am pale—not the bruised iron-gray of the Feeders, but the translucent, sickly white of a fish that has never seen the sun. My skin is slick and slimy, an adhesive secretion that allows me to defy gravity even when the great pistons plunge and the floor shudders with the violence of a localized earthquake.

I possess too many limbs, spindly and hooked like the rusted picks of a miner. They are arranged radially around my central mass, allowing me to scuttle in any direction along the dripping pipes and the riveted girders that support the floor. I have no eyes; there is nothing to see here but darkness and steel. Instead, my back is studded with sensory plates that read the texture of the vibration in the metal I cling to.

**The Sensation of the Roar**
I do not hear the pneumatic scream; I *taste* it. It travels through the steel into my claws, up my arms, and resonates in my hollow bones. To me, the constant motion of the High Condensery feels like being perpetually seasick. The floor beneath my belly is never still—it hums, it bucks, it twists. I live in a state of constant bracing, my hooks dug deep into the iron, waiting for the rhythmic pause where I can scurry three inches to the left before the next hammer-blow of pressure descends.

**My Sustenance**
I am the custodian of the unseen. While the Feeders consume the sharp shavings on the floor above, I tend to the ecosystem that thrives in the dark beneath.

Because the Condensery weeps, water does not just fall down; it weeps through. The condensation from the pipes overhead gathers on the top of the grating, heavy and cold, until it drips through the holes. I catch these falling metallic drops on my tongue. They are heavy with iron and ozone, a nutrient-rich soup that sustains me.

More importantly, I farm the **Shadow-Moss**. While the black moss grows in the corners above, its spores drift down through the grating or settle in the stagnant air of the gap. Here, in the absolute safety from the drying wind, the moss grows thick, hairy mats that hang down like wet beards from the steel crossbeams. I graze on this slime, scraping it off the rivets with radula-teeth. It is bland and greasy, but it is life.

**The Sky-Gods**
The Resonant Feeders are the gods of my mythology, though they do not know I exist. I know them only by their impact on my sky.

When a Feeder steps on the grating above, my constellation of amber lights is suddenly blotted out. A darkness the size of a giant’s foot eclipses my world. Through the steel, I feel the immense, resonant *thrum* of their weight. It is a comforting vibration, distinct from the chaotic thrum of the machine. It is heavy, deliberate, and rhythmic.

I wait for them to pass. Sometimes, if they linger above, feeding on the overflow channels, the condensation dripping from their gills will penetrate the grating in a sudden, warm deluge. To me, this is a benediction—a rain of rust-red waste that falls from the heavens, fertilizing my moss patches and giving me a drink of the rich, filtered toxins they have processed. I raise my pale, spindly arms to catch this "blood" of the giants, fluttering my gills in the draft of their movement.

**My Existence**
I am terrified of the silence. In my world, silence means the machine has stopped, and if the machine stops, the heat dies, the condensation ceases, and the damp cold turns into a killing freeze. I crave the violence. I need the floor to shake, for it is the shaking that knocks the rust-loose from the rivets, giving me food.

I am a creature of the margins, the afterthought of a world designed for giants. I am the white, wet belly clinging to the dark side of the world, farming the slime that grows in the ghost’s shadow, sleeping fitfully while the pistons plunge, dreaming of the next time a giant foot blocks out the light and the rain begins to fall.
