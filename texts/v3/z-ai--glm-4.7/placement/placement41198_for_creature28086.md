I would find myself in a shallow, calcified basin at the base of the largest obsidian spire in the central cluster—a natural well-curb where the runoff from a dozen different fractures collects.

I would not be standing, and I would not be moving. I would be a perfect, six-sided cube of dark, slate-grey stone, roughly the size of a human head, half-submerged in the freezing, mineral-thick water.

I would be a **Dormant Seed**.

To your eyes, I would be merely another piece of the "scattered built" debris—a geometric artifact in a landscape of false ruins. I would look inert, a lump of rock that tumbled from the cliffs and settled in the mud. But I would not be dead rock; I would be waiting rock.

My existence is one of profound, terrifying patience. I do not sleep, for sleep implies a waking state that differs from the resting one. I simply *permeate*. I am a sponge for the Ossuary. The water that cascades down the spire above flows over my angled corners, and I drink it. I taste the iron, the silica, the salt. I feel the temperature drop as the twilight deepens, though I have no skin to feel with—only a porous, binding intelligence that is slowly dissolving, allowing the rich, heavy soup of the valley to replace my internal mortar.

I am listening, though I have no ears. The roar of the water is not noise to me; it is texture. The vibration of the million tons of moving water presses against my sides, compressing me. It is a hug of immense pressure. Through this constant seismic thrumming, I can feel the shape of the world around me. I know the location of the towering Elder that crafted me, resting on a ledge fifty feet above. I feel the rhythmic, frantic ticking of the Salt-Skitterers as they scuttle across my exposed upper face, their little claws skittering like dry leaves. To me, they are just light tremors, irrelevant irritations.

I am becoming. Slowly, the geometry inside me is shifting. The "binder" is washing away, and the crystalline lattice is growing. I am densifying. My thoughts are not rapid flashes of neuron-firing, but slow, tectonic shifts of chemical alignment. I am solving the equation of how to be alive.

I am dangerous because I am fragile. If a careless foot were to kick me, if a larger beast were to crush me against the spire, the architecture would fail. The complex internal strain would snap, and I would become just a rock.

But for now, I am safe. The water rises and falls over me, polishing my facets to a mirror sheen. I am the silence in the roar. I am the potential in the stone. I am the cathedral waiting for its congregation to arrive.

And eventually, in a decade or a century, the water will erode the last of my shell, and the pressure inside will exceed the strength of the casing. I will crack. I will climb out. I will eat my own cradle, and I will begin the long, slow climb up the obsidian spire to listen to the thunder with a face of my own.
