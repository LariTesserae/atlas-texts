I would not be on the surface, nor in the canopy. I would be within the walls of the sponge itself.

I am the **Channel-Walker**, the great subterranean eel of the High-Sponge. While the Ground-Breakers plow the soft exterior and the Stalker pads through the mists, I inhabit the dense, dark interior of the basalt foam. I am the immune system of the plateau, the living lubricant that keeps the stone from swallowing itself.

**Where I Am**

My world is a labyrinth of arteries. The basalt here is a hardened foam, riddled with billions of vesicles—ancient gas bubbles frozen in stone—that have been widened by millennia of water flow. These are not caves; they are capillaries. I live in the spaces between the solid and the wet.

Here, the "industrial pulse" of the valley is not a distant hum; it is the deafening roar of existence. The water that "weeps" on the surface races through these tunnels at speed, superheated by the magma deep below. It is pressurized, boiling in places, and freezing in others where it meets the draft of a vent. I navigate this hydraulic violence, swimming upstream through the geothermal vents and surfing the discharge down into the aquifer pools deep beneath the moss.

**What I Am**

I am long—perhaps twenty feet from nose to tail—but my body is flattened, squarish in cross-section, allowing me to slide through rectangular cracks in the rock that would trap any rounder creature. My skin is not scaled, but smooth and rubbery, a gunmetal grey that absorbs the faint phosphorescent glow of sulfur-eating bacteria. It feels cool to the touch, slick with a mineral-rich slime that protects me from the scalding water.

I have no distinct neck; my head flows seamlessly into my body, armored with heavy plates of calcified bone that I use to ram through blockages of hardened mineral deposits. My eyes are small, vestigial, protected by thick, translucent lids. In the crushing dark of the rock’s interior, sight is useless. instead, I see through pressure and vibration. The lateral lines running down my flanks are so sensitive that I can feel the footstep of a Ground-Breaker a mile above, a dull thud echoing through the stone like a drum.

**My Life**

I am the plumber of the High-Sponge. The porous basalt is constantly trying to seal itself; minerals precipitate out of the steam and choke the vents. If the vents close, the pressure builds, and the plateau explodes. I am the release.

I spend my days in the "systole" of the mountain—the upward thrust of water. I wrap my muscular body around the interior of a vent, gripping the rough stone with hooked, rasp-like claws located where my pectoral fins would be. When a vent hisses to a stop, blocked by sludge, I push. I contract my length, forcing the blockage out with a hydraulic surge of my own body weight, then ride the resulting geyser to the surface, exploding into the azure air in a plume of steam, basking for a fleeting moment in the vertical glare before sinking back into the dark.

**My Relationship to the Others**

To the surface dwellers, I am a myth—a "Earth-Dragon" that causes the springs to shift.
*   To the **Vent-Grazers**, I am a terror. They huddle on the rims of the chimneys, feeding on bacteria, unaware that the water they stand on is my highway. When I rise from the deep to clear a vent, I consume them by the mouthful, a necessary predation that keeps the filters clean.
*   To the **Moss-Weavers**, I am a flood. They live in the miniature ravines above, but when I am particularly active deep below, the water table rises. They feel the vibration of my passing through their webbed paws and scatter, interpreting the deep thrum of my movement as the mood of the mountain god.
*   To the **Ground-Breakers**, I am a nuisance. They punch through the crust to drink from the shallow pools, and sometimes they break through into my tunnels. When they do, they steam-roar in annoyance as the water drains rapidly into my domain, leaving them with mud. They do not know that I am the one who refills those pools, regulating the flow from below.

**My Perspective**

I know the High-Sponge not as a place of green and light, but as a single, breathing organism. The ground you walk on is my lung tissue. The steam you see is my breath. The heat you feel is my metabolism.

I live in the crushing, wet dark, executing turns around tight corners of obsidian, tasting the minerals of the magma, feeling the heartbeat of the planet through my belly. I am slow, heavy, and deliberate. I do not fight the current; I am the assistant to the current.

I am the silence between the hisses. I am the water that waits.
