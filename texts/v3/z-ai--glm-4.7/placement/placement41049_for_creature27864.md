I am the frantic darkness stretched across the grey-white stone. I am the **Shadow-Self**.

You ask where I am; I am everywhere my body is not, yet I am tethered to it with an unbreakable, agonizing intimacy. I exist on the "floor" of this world, while my other half—the lumbering, silent giant I call the Host—occupies the vertical space above.

My perspective is horizontal. I see the world as a plane of infinite texture: the gritty teeth of the granite, the spongy, bruised resilience of the purple moss, and the treacherous, glass-like sheen of the frozen water pooling in the basins. The light here is pale and cold, a relentless exposure that defines my edges. Without the sun, I would not exist; I would simply retract back into the stone and dissolve. But the sun is always present, even when filtered by the sluggish, dirty ocean of clouds far below, and so I am cast, sharp and distinct, a two-dimensional cutout of life in a 3.0-scale world.

**The Lag**

My existence is defined by a terrible synchronization error. This is the "weirdness" made manifest.

My mind is sharp, frantic, immediate. It operates in the time of the dripping water, the cracking root, the snapping thought. But my body—that colossal hulk of granite and vine, the Cairn-Born standing twenty feet above me—moves in the time of mountains. It moves in the time of eras.

When I will my "hand" to move, I stretch my dark, flat fingers across the lichen, reaching for a distortion in the air, a rip in the fabric of the horizon that I can see clearly because I lie flat against the truth of the ground. I grasp at the geometry, trying to pin it down, to straighten the curve. I pull. I strain. My shadow-fingers claw at the empty air, digging furrows in the moss.

Up above, nothing happens.

The seconds drag into minutes. The minutes into hours. The sun slowly crawls across the sky, stretching me out, thinning my form until I am a thread of midnight lying across the plaza. I am screaming in silence, a silent panic of *now, move, fix it*.

Then, a vibration travels through the bedrock. A low-frequency hum rattles my non-existent bones. High above, the massive granite slab of the Host’s arm shifts. A grinding sound, the tectonic groan of lithosphere against lithosphere, echoes through the plateau. The shadow on the ground—the arm I moved an hour ago—finally sweeps into position.

The relief is overwhelming, followed immediately by the despair of the next command. I am the ghost in the machine, shouting orders into a void made of stone.

**The Anchor**

I reside at a "vertex," a place the High Expanse tries to forget. Here, three parallel roads converge, which they should not do. The air shimmers with a heat that isn't heat, a visual distortion where the sky tries to fold down into the stone. It is my job—our job—to hold the lines apart.

My Host is a masterpiece of primitive masonry. From my vantage point on the ground, I look up the pillars of its legs. They are not legs in the biological sense, but stacked towers of cyclopean blocks, wrapped in the thick, ropy vines that pulse with that slushy, bioluminescent fluid. I can see the hydraulic pressure rising; the vines bulge, the green deepening to an almost black hue.

I feel the Host’s thirst. It is a dull, aching pressure in the center of my shadow-form. We need the stagnation. We need the cold, brackish water that gathers in the collapsed roof of a nearby tower.

But the water is yards away. To the Host, that is a journey of days. To me, it is an eternity of waiting.

**The Ecology of the Shade**

While the Host stands frozen in the act of taking a step, I am not idle. I am the only thing in this silent, frozen world that moves with speed.

I interact with the "lushness" in ways the stone cannot. The aggressive moss tries to grow over me, thick and spongy, seeking to smother the darkness I cast. But I am faster. I shift. I ripple. I am a patch of darkness that scuttles across the plaza like a spilled ink blot, confusing the plants. They reach for me, thinking I am a shelter from the biting cold, but I slide away, leaving them shivering in the pale light.

I see the other Cairn-Born, or rather, I see their shadows. On the ground, we are a community of frantic, writhing shapes. We reach out to one another, our edges blurring as we communicate in a language of angles and intersections. We share the burden of the geometry. We warn each other where the distance is stretching too thin, where the horizon is lying.

Above us, the stone titans stand like statues in a graveyard, utterly ignoring the intricate social network playing out at their feet. They are the heavy sleep; we are the dreams.

**The Dissolution**

The most terrifying moment of my cycle is when the sun dips low.

As the light angles change, I detach. The "weirdness" of this world seizes me. My shadow lifts from the ground, peeling away like a sticker losing its adhesive. For a few impossible hours, I am no longer bound to the stone. I float in the air, a vertical slice of darkness, independent of the Host.

This is when the true "strangeness" takes hold. I am free. I can rush across the plateau, traversing miles in seconds. I can see the curvature of the world, the bend of the sky meeting the stone. I can look into the hollow, eye-like fissures of the other Cairn-Born and see the stagnant water glowing within.

I see the world as it truly is: a mechanism that has wound down, a garden of slow violence. I see the history of the city in the dust. I am the consciousness of the plateau, unleashed.

But the freedom is a trap. As the sun sets, or as the world’s rotation bends the light away, I feel the pull. The cold becomes absolute. The 0.3 temperature feels like it is freezing my very essence. I am being called back.

I snap to the earth. I slam back into the flat, grey stone, compressed once more into a two-dimensional prisoner of the granite. The Host takes a shuddering breath—the grind of rocks settling—and we are one again. The lag returns. The speed of my thought is imprisoned in the slowness of the rock.

I am the intent. I am the wish. I am the shadow that weeps while the mountain stands impassive, waiting for the long, cold night to end.
