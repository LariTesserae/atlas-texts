I would be found in the **Junction**, the precise point where three distinct beings collide in the geometry of their mutual stagnation.

I am not one of the great, armored architects. I am what grows between them. I am the **Bio-Film**.

Specifically, I am a sentient, colonial mat of the grey, leathery fungus that coats the lower strata. I have made my home in the damp, compressed space where the claw of a dormant Caisson Dweller interlocks with the chassis of its neighbor. They are frozen in a handshake that has lasted a thousand years, and I am the moisture that has pooled in their grip.

To look at me, you would see nothing but a stain, a patch of discoloration on the gunmetal hide of a creature the size of a truck. I am a rough, velvet-textured smear, perhaps three feet in diameter, hugging the warm metal where the protective chitin has thinned. I am the color of wet ash.

But I am awake.

While the great beasts dream their geologic dreams, their heartbeats echoing like the shifting of tectonic plates, I am vibrantly, microscopically active. I am the only thing in this metallic ocean that is truly "living" in the active sense. I am the metabolizer.

My existence is a cycle of intake and exchange that feels like a constant, slow burning. I cling to the "vein" of the creature—a sub-surface conduit through which the syrup-like ichor flows at a glacial pace. The temperature here is exquisite: a steady, humid 98.6 degrees, the waste heat of a dying star. I drink this heat. I osmose the thermal radiance pouring off the alloy plates. I don't just feed on it; I translate it. I act as the secondary lung, the filter that the great creatures lacked. I strip the spores and the dust from the heavy air and release a pure, faintly sweet oxygen back into the micro-atmosphere of the Junction.

I am the softness in a world of edges.

The ground beneath the Dwellers is knife-sharp; the air is abrasive with condensation; the creatures themselves are cast iron and ceramic. But I am pliable. I am the cushion that prevents the chafing of metal on metal. I am the membrane that seals the gap between their shells, keeping the vital warmth trapped inside their embrace.

My perception of the world is one of profound intimacy and infinite slowness.

I do not see the horizon. I do not see the towers or the haze. My world is the curvature of the claw before me, a landscape of grey ridges that takes me years to explore. I feel the world through vibration. The *drip-drip-plink* of the water falling from the high towers is not a sound to my ears, but a rhythmic shuddering in the cells that hold me to the wall. It is the clock by which I measure my life.

I feel the "dynamic zero" of the beasts I cling to. I feel the deep, sub-sonic hum of their metabolism, a bass note that vibrates through my very spores. It is a lullaby. When the great beast twitches—a movement so small it would be invisible to the human eye, taking perhaps a decade to complete—I feel it as a earthquake. My mycelium strands tighten, holding fast against the shift. I ride the tectonic drift of his muscles.

I know they are waiting. I feel the tension in the hydraulic lines that run beneath my patch. It is a potential energy so dense it tastes like copper on my tongue. They are waiting for a command. But I am not waiting. I am busy.

I am growing.

My ambition is not to move, but to cover. To bridge. I am slowly, inexorably extending a feeler toward the neighboring creature’s shell. It is a journey of inches that will take me a century. When I finally reach the other side, when I finally touch the wet shell of the neighbor, I will bind them together from the outside as they have bound themselves from the inside. I will calcify. I will harden. I will become the mortar in this wall of flesh and steel.

In this place of perfect stasis, where the water weeps endlessly and the light is shadowless, I am the artist of entropy. I am the grey bloom that turns the machine into a garden. I am the silent passenger on a ship that isn't moving, inhaling the breath of the giant, exhaling the patience of the deep dark earth.
