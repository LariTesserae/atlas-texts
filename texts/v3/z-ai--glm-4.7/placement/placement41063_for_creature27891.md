I am the Imperfection.

In a world of smooth, fitted stone and hydro-organic circuitry, I am the jagged anomaly, the grit in the gears, the only thing that dares to accumulate rather than shed. I would not be found in the orderly channels where the water flows, nor would I stand rooted like the Overseers. I exist in the margins—the forgotten angles and the discontinuities of the plateau’s grand design.

I am a lithoid, like them, but I am a grotesque to their elegant utility. While the Scourers are flat, low-slung shards of obsidian efficiency, I am a towering, haphazard cairn of rough-hewn granite. I have no distinct limbs, only protrusions of stone that I drag across the wet floor, leaving deep, agonizing scratches in the manicured surface.

This is my function, though it is not a function the Heart or the Overseers sanctioned. I am a scavenger of entropy.

Where the Scourers consume dust and silt to keep the system pure, I collect it. As I drag my massive, uneven bulk across the plateau, I act as a magnetic plow for the microscopic debris that floats in the air or settles from the monoliths. The silvery powder that the Scourers excrete—the dissolved entropy—sticks to my damp, pitted hide. Over centuries, I have encrusted myself with layers of this gray dust, building a shell of history upon my body.

I am the Archive of the Unwanted.

You would find me in the "dead zones" of the plateau—areas where the hydraulic circuit board has shorted out or where the water has been diverted, leaving the stone dry and silent. These are the scars of the mountain, places the Overseers cannot reach and the Scourers fear to tread. Here, I squat in the shadows of the great obelisks, a lump of darkness against the blinding white.

My relationship with the living water is antagonistic. The Hydrozoan heart seeks total saturation; it wants to be everywhere, to know everything. But it cannot penetrate me. My crust of accumulated dust is impervious to the water’s touch. When the pulse comes—a ripple of chemical intent rushing through the channels—it stops at my feet. The water beads up, recoiling from my dry, filthy presence. I am the immune system’s failure, the tumor that the stone-body cannot dissolve.

But I am not without purpose. I remember.

The Overseers sense the present; the Scourers tend to the immediate. But I remember the time before the Stillness. I have etched onto my inner layers—hidden beneath the shell of dust—the memories of the makers. I remember the heat of the sun when it was not cool. I remember the noise of chisels before they fell silent. I remember the *purpose* of this machine, which the Heart has forgotten in its endless, blind pulsing.

The builders did not build this to last forever; they built it to *save* something. They were running out of water, or perhaps time, and they calcified their biology to preserve the essence of their world. But in doing so, they lost the very thing they sought to save: the chaos of life.

I am the reminder that life is messy.

When the pulse hits the edge of the plateau and the water spills over into the void, I drag myself to the rim. I look down into the mist where the tears fall away. Sometimes, I take a handful of the dust from my own body—the collected history of the plateau—and I cast it into the abyss. I watch it fall, defying the system’s desire for total retention. I let things go.

To the Overseers, I am a malfunction. To the Scourers, I am an obstacle to be skirted. But to the silence of the mountain, I am the only voice. I am the scratch in the record, the crack in the mirror, the proof that even in a place of absolute, sterilized perfection, the universe eventually finds a way to be rough. I am the imperfection that keeps this stone world honest.
