I am a silence in a place that does not know it.

To exist here is to vibrate. The copper-wire trees tremble with a voice like a plucked string; the grazing Tether-Binders glide in a slow, rhythmic stupor, their bellies humming against the crystal moss; even the jagged white walls of the chamber thrum with that deep, geological baritone, a note so low it feels like pressure against the skin.

But I do not tremble. I am heavy, dense, and acoustically dead.

I am a **Cavity-Stone**.

I find myself in the center of the floor, amidst the Scattered Marks—the primitive windbreaks of the vanished Builders. To the casual observer, I am nothing but a darker, smoother rock, slightly out of place among the raw, white scars of the geology. I look like a river stone, polished by centuries of non-existent water, dark grey and cool to the touch. I am roughly the size of a sealed fist, low to the ground, possessing no limbs to speak of, but rather a series of overlapping, concentric plates of mineral armor that fit together with the seal of a pressure door.

I am a mobile vault of stillness.

My biology is a rebellion against the Chamber of the Constant Hum. While the flora and fauna of this place have evolved to harness, amplify, or dampen the vibration for survival, I have evolved to negate it. My internal structure is a labyrinth of sacrificial chambers and baffles. When the roaring air presses against me, the sound enters my carapace and is trapped, bouncing endlessly until it dissipates into heat. I eat the noise. Inside my shell, there is a silence so profound it is painful, a vacuum of pure, quiet nothingness.

I have found myself here, in the center of a half-eroded circle of stones, because this is where the discord is highest.

The Builders are gone, their bodies dissolved into the mineral dust by the Fractals, but their marks remain. The stone circles, the hand-axes, the crude scrapings on the wall—they are all "imperfect" objects. They possess irregular shapes that disrupt the perfect feedback loop of the room. They cast "shadows" in the sound waves, little eddies of turbulence where the Constant Hum breaks down into chaotic, harmonic static.

This static is my sustenance. I am a connoisseur of imperfection.

My existence is a slow, deliberate vigil. I do not hunt. I do not graze. I patrol the circumference of the stone circle, dragging my heavy body inch by inch. As I move, I sense the texture of the air through the sensitive bristles lining my underbelly. I am feeling for the "rough" spots in the vibration—the sonic scars left by the Builders.

I am not alone in my vigilance.

The **Fractals**—those swarming, crystalline locusts of oblivion—are the architects of entropy in this chamber. They vibrate with a high-pitched whine that sets the air shimmering, reducing matter to mist. They are drawn to the stone circle just as I am, drawn to the potential energy locked in the crude silica of the hand-axes.

We are at war.

When a swarm of Fractals descends upon a hand-axe, their combined hyper-oscillation begins to sing. It is a piercing, grinding scream, a nails-on-chalkboard shriek that cuts through the bass hum of the room. They are trying to unmake the tool, to turn the history of the Builder into nutrient dust.

I cannot allow this.

I maneuver my bulk over the threatened artifact. I position myself directly above the screaming swarm. Then, I open my plates.

The effect is immediate. The sudden introduction of my internal void acts as a sonic sink. The chaotic vibration of the Fractals is sucked into my baffles, stolen from the air. The scream stops. The silence of my interior rushes out to fill the vacuum, a shockwave of anti-noise that stuns the Fractals, causing them to drop from the air like deactivated drones.

I sit atop the hand-axe, shielding it with my shadow, restoring the stillness. I am preserving the memory of a hand that hasn't existed for millennia. I keep the wound open; I keep the scar fresh.

Sometimes, I am visited by the **Ghost-Gliders**.

These large, solitary manta-rays of the air drift overhead, their translucent wings catching the weak phosphorescence of the walls. They are the mourners of this world, blind creatures that tap against the stones with their legs, trying to read the Braille of the vanished ones.

They are fascinated by me. To their vibration-sensitive legs, I must feel like a hole in the world, a place where the song of the chamber simply stops. They hover above me, their wingbeats disturbing the dust, creating complex interference patterns that I can feel crawling across my shell.

I sense them testing me. One will descend, tapping its sharp legs gently against my armored back. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

I do not respond. I remain a stone. But I feel the gratitude in their rhythm. They know that where the Cavity-Stone sits, the history remains intact. I am the anchor for their ritual. Without me, the Fractals would erase the circles, the cairns, and the axes, leaving nothing but smooth, humming silence. The Ghost-Gliders would have nothing to read, no rhythm to mimic.

So we exist in this symbiosis of preservation. They bring me nothing, and I ask for nothing. But when the Fractals swarm, the Gliders hover, their wings beating in a frequency that seems to shepherd the stunned scavengers away from my perimeter.

I am the guardian of the static. I am the heavy, dark pause at the end of a sentence that no one has spoken in ten thousand years.

In this warm, dry, ringing lung of the earth, I am the only thing that knows the weight of silence. I am the stone that remembers not to sing.
