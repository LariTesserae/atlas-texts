I am perched on the spine of a jagged basalt ridge, a formation that juts out from the crater wall like a broken rib. This is my roost: The High Cleave. It is unstable, of course—everything here is—but the rock is laced with heavy mineral deposits that keep it anchored, if only barely. From here, I can see the "breath" of the basin rising as steam, illuminating the hanging mosses in brief, spectral flares of violet.

I am not one of the runners. I am too broad, too scarred for the rapid, fluid rippling of the hunters below.

I am a **Cairn-Keeper** of the Shard-Kin.

To look at me, you might think me a statue of the landscape itself. My skin is the same grey, wet slate as the rocks, textured with the same rough grain. I have draped myself in a mantle of shed Glass-Wing chitin, layered and lashed together with wet rawhide, which creates a shell that deflects the razor-sharp scatter of the scree. It makes a soft, clicking sound when I move, like two stones tapping together—noise that I hide within the greater cacophony of the crater.

My eyes, like all my kind, are vast black pools, drinking in the meager photons. But mine are older, clouded slightly by the constant irritation of the mineral-heavy air. I do not rely on them as the younger scouts do. I rely on the hum.

Where would you find me?
I am found in the silence *between* the noises. I sit before a thermal vent, a fissure in the earth’s crust that exhales a steady, rhythmic *hiss* of sulfur and heat. Around this vent, I have built a lattice of stones—not random, but a complex solaris-like array of balancing acts. I am the tuner of the basin’s radio.

My role is to read the vibrations. I do not hunt Hex-Scrollers; I hunt the precursors to disaster. When the ground trembles, not with the step of a Basin-Walker, but with the deep, liquid shift of a looming landslide, my stones are the first to know. They clatter in a specific sequence. I then sound the Great Horn—a hollowed-out cylinder of dense bone, carved from the remains of a leviathan found in the deep strata—blasting a low-frequency note that cuts through the insect static, warning the nomad clans to move to higher ground.

I am the memory of this place. The Shard-Kin do not build, so we do not have libraries. I carry the maps in the scars on my palms. I know exactly which obsidian shards hold the sharpest edge, and which are brittle glass waiting to shatter. I know the temperature of the steam vent that signals the coming of the torrential rains.

I am watching you now, just as the younger hunter in the thicket is. But where he sees a threat or a meal, I see a variable in the equation. You stand there, breathing the soup, your skin soft and unarmored. To me, you look like a soft, pink Hex-Scroller that has lost its legs. You are vibrating with confusion. The static hiss of the Mist-Drifters is likely deafening to your dull ears.

I reach out a hand—long, spindly fingers, tipped with callouses hard as flint—and adjust a stone near the vent. It wobbles, then settles. The temperature has dropped. A storm is coming. The "solaris" of the basin is about to shift.

I do not attack you. In the Basin of the Shattered Cradle, energy is too precious to waste on malice. I simply shift my weight, the chitin armor on my back grinding against the rock, and I wait. I wait to see if you will be buried by the coming mud, or if you will learn to flow with it. If you survive the rain, I will leave a sharpened piece of obsidian on the path you take. Not a gift, but a test.

I am the Celler of the Dark. I am the one who knows that the light is not a savior, but a lure. And I am sitting in the dark, waiting for the world to scream again.
