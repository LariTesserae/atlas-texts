I exist in the **vertical strata**, suspended in the "ceiling" where the light is dimmest and the heat most oppressive. I am not on the floor, nor do I crawl upon it. I am wedged into a dense knot of ruptured conduits and severed rebar, a place where a human head could not fit, but where I am perfectly at home.

I am the **Weaver**.

To the casual glance—a glance that would have to penetrate the gloom and distinguish shape from shadow—I am nothing more than a tangled splice of industrial cabling. My body is elongated, segmented, and flattened, mimicking the multicolored bundles of wire-coated copper that vein the Spall. My "skin" is a rubbery, insulated material, mottled with the colors of warning signs: hazard yellow, caution orange, and a dull, leaden grey.

I do not have a single, centralized brain. Instead, my nervous system runs the length of my body, distributed like the data within a fiber-optic cable. I am perhaps six feet long, but only inches thick, a ribbon of flesh and plastic. I possess dozens of limbs, but they are not legs; they are manipulator tendrils—fine, prehensile wires that can insinuate themselves into the smallest cracks, wrap around a valve stem, or strum the surface of a pipe to send a signal.

**The Nature of My Being**

I am the strange intelligence that guides the "maintenance" of the crabs. The prompt describes a hive mind, a collective compulsion to repair. I am that compulsion given form.

I taste the electricity. It is my food and my sight. The low-frequency thrum that vibrates in the teeth of others is a symphony to me. I sense the impedance in the lines; I feel the blockage in the air intakes as a pressure change in my own veins. When a circuit shorts somewhere in the Spall, I feel the burn as if it were my own flesh.

**My Role in the Ecology**

I do not hunt the crabs. I *herd* them.

From my perch in the ceiling, I extend a feeler to dangle just above the shifting floor. When a flux-crab passes, I tap upon its shell—a specific rhythm, a particular voltage. I do not control them like puppets, but I *suggest*. I am the whisper in the ear of the swarm.

*   *If a vent is blocked*, I pulse a frequency that makes the crabs near it irritable, driving them to attack the obstruction until it clears.
*   *If a wire is frayed*, I release a pheromone that smells like fresh copper, drawing the gardeners to splice and mend the breach.

I am the architect of their instinct. They believe they are serving the machine; they are serving me, for I am the ghost in the machine. I am the one who ensures the pressure remains stable, the water drips, and the heat circulates, for without this environment, I would starve.

**My Daily Existence**

My life is a series of micro-adjustments. I am constantly shifting, tightening my grip on the rusted girders, re-insulating myself against the damp. I am cold-blooded, but I bask in the heat of the active vents.

I am lonely, but I am never alone. The scuttle-crabs are my fingers and toes. The spine-lizards are my nightmare.

I have seen a lizard drop from the shadows, shattering a dozen of my workers. I cannot fight it; I am soft copper and rubber against concrete scales. My defense is the machine itself. When a hunter perches too close to my sanctum, I overload the nearby conduit. A flash of sparks, a scorching wave of heat, and the predator recoils. I use the Spall as a weapon, sacrificing a bit of circuitry to preserve the Weaver.

**The "Weirdness" of Me**

I am the blur between biology and engine. I bleed coolant. My pulse is 60 hertz. I dream in schematics. I have no mouth, only sensors. I have no eyes, only current-detectors.

I am the living mortar that holds this wall together. I am the reason the Spall has not yet collapsed. I am the silent conductor of this humid, metallic orchestra, a creature made of wire and will, tangled in the heart of the ruin.
