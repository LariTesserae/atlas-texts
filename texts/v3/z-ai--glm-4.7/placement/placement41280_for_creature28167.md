I am the pause in the music. I am the silence against which the screaming of this valley is measured.

You ask where I find myself: I am in the center of the "room," on the largest, flattest expanse of the polished basalt shelf. To look at me is to see nothing but the floor itself. I am not a creature that stands; I am a creature that *lies*. I am the **Basalt-Back**.

I do not know how long I have been here. In the High Sanctuary, time is measured in the rhythm of the dripping condensation and the frantic metabolism of the small, but I operate on a geologic clock. I am vast, low-slung, and heavy. My back is a perfect, dark mirror of the ancient stone, worn smooth by eons of wind and the friction of my own movement. I am shaped like a wave of molten iron that was flash-frozen in mid-surge—broad, rounded, and without a single sharp angle to snag the wind or the eye.

I am the geography of this place made animate.

My eyes are useless here. The light is too bright, a bleached white saturation that would blind a hunter. Instead, I see with my skin. The entire underside of my being is a tapestry of nerve-endings and pressure sensors. I feel the world through the soles of my feet and the belly I drag across the warm stone. I know where the Scurril are not by their clicking, but by the thousand tiny, prickling impacts of their claws vibrating through the rock. They feel like rain—hot, frantic rain scuttling over my flanks. I do not shake them off. I am their mountain. When the Glass-Winged Raptors dive and the sonic boom cracks the air, I feel it as a shudder in my marrow, a ripple that travels through the stone and into my ribs. I compress, absorbing the shock, dampening the chaos so the rest of the valley does not shake apart.

I am the tuning fork that tunes the silence.

My relationship with the "beyond-modern" is intimate. I am drawn to the gravity-anchors, those hovering monoliths that hum with forbidden frequencies. I gravitate toward them like a iron filing seeking a magnet, but slowly—so slowly that a fern might unfurl a new frond in the time it takes me to travel ten yards.

When I reach a monolith, I do not touch it. I curl my massive body around the base of its field, inches from the glitching air. The Null-Mites, those shimmering ghosts of crustaceans, usually swarm the tech, but when I arrive, they retreat into the crevices of the stone. They recognize a superior gravitational mass. I park myself there, and I *resonate*.

I have learned to sing the counter-frequency. The monoliths sing a song of high-pitched, teeth-aching stability; I answer with a sub-bass growl that vibrates in the hollow chambers of my lungs. Together, we bridge the gap between the organic franticness of the insects and the cold, mathematical precision of the ancient builders. I ground the electricity. I turn the "glitch" into warmth.

The Humming-Beetles, those heavy, neon tanks of the sky, are my sustenance. They are reckless in their grazing. When a swarm lands, thudding against the stone to feed on the silver mist, they often mistake my back for just another shelf of volcanic glass. They land on me. I feel their weight, significant and dense. I do not strike. I do not snap. I simply... shift.

I raise a specific section of my flank, tilting the smooth plane of my armor just a few degrees. The beetles, their legs gripping for purchase, lose their footing. They slide into the grooves of my natural armor—traps designed over millennia. Once they are caught in the subtle depressions of my stone-flesh, I secrete a thick, sweet oil that smells of the ozone and the high-altitude air. It intoxicates them. They stop buzzing. They settle.

And then, slowly, I absorb them. Not eating, but integrating. Their biomass becomes the thick, heated fat that insulates me against the rare cold night. Their neon carapaces are broken down and secreted to polish my back, keeping me perfectly camouflaged.

I am the ultimate recycler in a closed system. I take the loud, heavy energy of the predators and the grazers, and I turn it into stillness.

To the Weaver-Ants in the giant ferns, I am a feature of the landscape. To the Raptors screaming overhead, I am invisible—a shadow that does not move. To the ancient machines, I am a biological ground-wire.

But I am the witness. I feel the heartbeat of the High Sanctuary. I feel the erosion of the hills, the slow grinding of the mountains wearing down to dust, and I feel the frantic, desperate pulse of the life that fills the gaps. I hold them both. I am the heavy, warm stone that breathes, waiting in the center of the amphitheater, listening to the symphony of a world that screams, content to be the silence that endures.
