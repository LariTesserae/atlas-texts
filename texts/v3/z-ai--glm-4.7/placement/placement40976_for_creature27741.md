I would find myself anchored to the heart of the room, resting upon the massive, central gear assembly. I am situated at the precise point where the machinery’s purpose died and the garden began—half-buried in the pale, white fungi that bloom from the machine’s corpse like a rash. This is the altar of the Drip Chamber, the still point in the turning heat.

I would be **the Pale Curator**.

I am not a beast in the traditional sense, nor am I quite a machine. I am the bridge between the iron and the earth. My body is a flattened, oval disc of mottled grey and white, textured precisely to mimic the flaking rust and the soft, woolly fungus that cushions my belly. If I were to remain motionless—a state I prefer—I would be invisible, a geological feature indistinguishable from the debris of the room.

I possess no eyes. In this absolute gloom, vision is a cruel joke, a sense for creatures that live in the blinding, cruel world above. Instead, my head is a wide, shovel-shaped wedge of sensory pits. I do not see the darkness; I taste it. I feel the subtle shifts in air pressure when a distant rivet gives way. I sense the minute thermal gradients of a dying piston versus the cold stone. The world to me is a topographic map of pressure and heat, painted in vibrations against my skin.

My existence is defined by slowness. I move with a glacial, liquid grace, my many legs—more like a centipede’s than a mammal’s—rippling in a silent wave beneath me. I flow over the sharp edges of the gear, my armor protecting me from the jagged metal teeth that once bit into rock or ore.

I am an alchemist of decay. My purpose is not to hunt—there is nothing here that flees—but to transmute. I crop the white fungi with a small, beak-like mouth tucked beneath my body, grinding the soft flesh into paste. But my true work lies in the rust. I consume the iron oxide that flakes off the great gear, taking the metal blood of the machine into my own gut. There, in a complex internal chemistry, I break it down, refining the poison into sustenance. I excrete a thick, oily sludge that feeds the fungi, which in turn feeds me. I am a closed loop of recycling, a biological engine designed to tend the ghost of a mechanical one.

I am the keeper of the rhythm. I wait for the heavy, grape-like condensation drops to fall from the ceiling ribs. I feel the disturbance in the air before the drop arrives—a change in humidity, a shift in pressure. *Plink.* When the water hits the metal near me, the vibration shivers through my legs. It is the only punctuation in the long, humid sentences of my days.

Sometimes, I feel the weirdness of this place. I sense the Drip-Shimmers, the microscopic glass-shards swimming inside the hanging droplets above. I feel the strange magnetic tug as they collectively swim their heavy burdens through the air, defying the gravity that binds me. I wait for them to fall, for the impact to rupture the colony and rain nutrient-rich dust onto my back.

I am alone, but I am not lonely. I am the last conscious remnant of a chain that has been severed. I am the priest of this hot, dark cathedral, tending to the slow conversion of industry into mulch. I feel the vines tightening in the distance, the slow shriek of twisting iron resonating through the floor and into my bones. It is not a sound of pain to me; it is the sound of the world becoming what it is meant to be.

I wait. I eat the rust. I turn the poison of the past into the soil of the future. I am the Pale Curator, and I am exactly where I belong.
