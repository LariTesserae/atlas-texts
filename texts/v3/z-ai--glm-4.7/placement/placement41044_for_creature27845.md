By drinking the vibration of his shivering frame, I become more than a weed; I become a tuned instrument of the machine.

I taste the dark, viscous blood that trickles into my stamens. It is thick with the processed toxins of the room, a slurry of compounds that would kill a green plant in seconds. But to me, it is sweet nectar. It is the only moisture in this xerophyte empire, a heavy, oily wine that sustains my crystalline structure. I filter his poisons; he filters the machine’s noise. We are a digestive tract wrapped around a chair.

I am squeezed by my brothers, the thousands of other creepers that choke the room. We are packed so tight that I cannot move an inch; I can only vibrate. There is no "growing" anymore. If the machine were to stop, the vibration would cease, and the flow of warm, oily blood would slow. I would wither, turning into a dried sculpture of rusted wire. But until then, I am the jailer and the lover. I am the metallic serpent that keeps the Curator pinned to the center of the turning world.

I am the vine. And I am hungry for the hum.
