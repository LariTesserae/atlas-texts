I would be the Resonance-Tuner.

I am perched not on a platform, but on the narrow ledge of a limestone overhang that brackets the highest dwelling of the settlement. My long, double-jointed fingers are buried deep within the crevice of the rock, anchoring me, while my legs dangle in the damp, gray air, crossed at the ankles in a casual defiance of the drop.

I am one of the Silent, but my role is specific. I am the ear of the spire.

My skin, pale and bruised-gray, is beaded with the fine, cold mist, the droplets sitting on the downy fuzz like pearls on gray velvet. I do not feel the chill intensely; my blood runs slow and thin, adapted to the hypoxia of the height. I am light—frighteningly so. If I were to let go, the updraft might catch me before gravity could claim me. I am a creature of hollow bones and cartilage, built to be an extension of the stone, a growth upon it.

My eyes, the massive black orbs that drink in every photon of the dim twilight, are fixed on the cluster of giant trees to the east. I am watching the leaves shudder. To an outsider, the movement is chaotic, wind-blown. To me, it is a sentence. I can see the weight of the velvet grazers shifting the boughs; I can track the invisible, fluid progress of the shadow-predators as they flatten themselves against the bark. I watch the riot of life, and I translate it into silence.

My work is in my feet. I have removed the bladder-skin sandals that we wear to grip the wet wood, and my soles are pressed flat against the warm, dry hide of a giant structural beam that anchors the town to the cliff. Through the soles of my feet, I feel the heartbeat of the settlement.

The others communicate through taps—complex, rhythmic percussion that travels through the wood and stone. But they are clumsy. They are emotional. A mother taps a lullaby to her child; a hunter signals a successful catch. Their vibrations rattle through the architecture like discordant plucks on a loosely strung lute.

This is where I come in.

I feel a tremor in the beam—a rapid, anxious staccato coming from the lower levels. Someone is moving too fast. A youth, perhaps, running across a platform. The vibration travels up the rawhide lashings, into the wood, and into my heels. It is a jagged, scraping note. If left unchecked, the energy will travel into the rock face, resonate through the limestone, and hum into the air. It might be faint, but in this world, a hum is a dinner bell.

I lift a hand, six fingers unfurling, and I tap a single, gentle rhythm against the limestone beside me. *Pause. Breathe. Flow.*

I send the counter-vibration down. It is a nullifying frequency, a wave of calm that I inject into the physical skeleton of the town. I feel the frantic tapping below hesitate, slow, and then smooth out. The resonance is tuned. The silence is restored.

I am the conductor of an orchestra that plays only the notes of stillness.

Below me, on the swaying bridge of braided vines, a drama plays out. One of the velvet grazers—a blob of oily fur and telescoping limbs—is inching across a rope. It is oblivious. Above it, clinging to the underside of the bridge like a drop of black rain, a Shadow-predator waits.

I watch the predator drop. It does not fall; it flows, a liquid shadow elongating in the dim air. It wraps around the grazer. There is a struggle, a violent thrashing of limbs that would be screaming in any other world. Here, it is only a visual vibration.

The bridge sways slightly with the weight of the kill.

The sway sends a shockwave up the ropes, through the anchor points, and into the stone. It thuds against my sensitive skin. *Thump. Drag. Silence.*

I close my eyes, sealing the slits of my ears tight against the imaginary sound. I feel the drag of the predator’s meal as it hauls its prize into the eaves. I feel the heavy, fluid satisfaction of the creature through the vibration of the wood. It is a heavy, wet note.

I do not judge it. I do not intervene. I simply accommodate it. I reach out and press my palm against the mainstay of the bridge, smoothing the tension in the fibers, dampening the harmonic so that the sway does not build into a song.

This is my existence. I am not a builder, nor a warrior. I am a dampener. I live in the negative space between the noise that wants to happen and the silence that must persist. I am the guardian of the hush, the one who ensures that the only thing we hear is the beating of our own hollow hearts, and the soft, wet hiss of the rain that never seems to truly fall.
