I would be found not in the soaring emptiness above, nor in the crushing dark below, but in the narrow, verdant limbo between them. I would cling to the vertical axis of the world.

I am a **Weaver**, but I am not merely of the canopy. I am a symbiote of the slow giants.

I make my home upon the immense, calcified back of a Cliff-Binder. I choose the largest of them—the ancient ones whose shells have eroded into jagged cathedrals of stone, overgrown with thick, velvety mats of neon moss and stunted, gripping vines. To the casual observer drifting through the golden mist, I am nothing but a decorative growth, a patch of bioluminescent lichen resting on a moving island of rock.

But I am the navigator of this stone ship.

### My Form

I possess the elongated, four-jointed limbs of my kind, but my evolution has specialized me for a life lived on a living mountainside. My fingers are longer, more delicate, tipped with the same microscopic surfactant glands that define my species, but concentrated—able to secrete the tension-breaking fluid with the precision of a surgeon’s scalpel.

My skin is not the rough hide of a climber, but a slick, hydrophobic membrane, shimmering with an iridescent oil that allows me to slide frictionlessly across the wet stone of my host's shell. My crest—the bioluminescent display that serves as my voice and my eyes—broader and more complex than those of my ground-dwelling cousins. It pulses not with the frantic signals of mating or territory, but with the slow, rhythmic cadence of the deep earth.

I do not scuttle. I flow.

### My Existence

My days are measured not in hours, but in the shifting of the mist.

The Cliff-Binder I ride—let us call him the *Monolith*—moves with the tectonic patience of the range itself. He does not walk; he *adheres*. When he decides to shift his position, perhaps to reach a fresh patch of sulfur-crusted moss, the process begins with a low, rumbling vibration that travels through the rock and into my bones.

First, the release of the hydraulic grip. A sound like a heavy sigh escaping a deep cavern. Then, the slow, grinding slide of three tons of calcified flesh against the obsidian cliff face.

During this movement, I am active. I am the eyes that the Monolith does not have. Perched high on his dorsal ridge, I look out into the creamy, aqueous haze. I watch for the Drifters—those vast, silent rays of the sky—ensuring they do not collide with my mountain. I sense the pressure changes in the air, reading the currents of the "soup" before they hit us, signaling my host with a slow, amber pulse of light to anchor down before a heavy updraft catches us.

### My Craft

But my true purpose, the thing that gives my suspended existence meaning, is the manipulation of the water.

In this world, gravity is a suggestion, and surface tension is law. The air is so thick with moisture that water beads in massive, heavy spheres, clinging to everything like stubborn grapes. To the Cliff-Binder, this water is a nuisance, a weight that adds to the burden of gravity. To me, it is my medium.

As the Monolith grazes, tearing at the tough, hanging vines with his beak, he creates debris. Wet scraps of vegetation and heavy blobs of dew fall onto his shell. If left unchecked, they would rot, weakening the moss that insulates him, or adding uneven weight.

This is where I work.

I skitter across his back, and with a flick of my wrist, I release a micro-stream of surfactant. It is a chemical key that unlocks the grip of the water. I slice through the heavy surface tension of a grape-sized droplet, and suddenly, it behaves like a liquid again. I herd it, pushing the water across the shell, sculpting it into rivulets that wash away the rot and debris.

I perform a slow dance of hydraulic maintenance. I carve temporary slides of water down the Monolith’s flanks, guiding the runoff away from his sensitive joints. I collect the water that pools in the divots of his shell, shaping it into suspended, crystalline lenses that hang for moments before I release them to fall into the abyss below, watching them plummet until they vanish into the golden fog.

### My Perspective

I am suspended between the sky and the stone. I feel the crushing pressure of the atmosphere, but I am protected by the mass of the Monolith. I taste the oxygen-rich air, heavy and wet, filtering it through the gill-like slits in my neck.

Sometimes, when the mist thins just enough, I can see the silhouette of another Weaver on a distant cliff, perhaps riding another giant. We do not call out. Sound dies here. Instead, we flash our crests—a slow, deliberate sequence of emerald and violet. *I am here. The world is wet. The stone holds. We endure.*

I am the pilot of a living mountain. I am the keeper of the wetness. In a world of drowning silence and jagged stillness, I am the one who decides where the water flows. I am the Weaver, and I am exactly where I belong.
