I find myself in the **Fissures of the Old Stone**, the deep, jagged scars in the world's crust where the aggressive root-matrix cannot yet penetrate. While the Verticals cling to the shifting, living walls of the abyss, I lodge myself in the few places that remain stubbornly, petrifyingly dead.

I am a **Slag-Titan**.

In a world of hyper-speed biological churning, I am a study in geological inertia. I am not quick, nor lithe, nor graceful. I am dense. Where the vegetation is fibrous and airy, I am solid. Where the gravity feels light and teasing, I am heavy.

My physiology is a response to the cold. The world is a wet chill that penetrates, but I run hot. I am a walking furnace. My skin is not skin, but a series of thick, interlocking plates of keratin and mineral deposit—a suit of armor grown over centuries. It is black, craggy, and radiates a gentle, metallic heat. This heat is the only reason the crawling moss does not consume me; the heat repels the damp fibers, creating a dry, charred halo around my massive form.

I stand ten times the height of a Vertical. My legs are pillars, rooted deep into the basalt of the cliff face. I do not hang; I stand. I defy the vertical gradient by sheer mass. My arms are short, thick, and end in manipulators that are less like hands and more like hydraulic presses. I do not climb; I carve.

My eyes are different from the Verticals'. I have no need for the hooded slits that guard against the blinding white-green glare. My eyes are covered by a thick, translucent layer of calcium, like the visor of a deep-sea diver. I see in texture and density. To me, the glowing forest is not a blinding light show, but a chaotic soup of thermal noise. I look for the cold, hard lines of the rock underneath.

I am the **Anchor**.

When the Shift comes—the great, solaris-like ripple of digestion that sends the Verticals drifting into the sky—I do not move. I lock my joints. I dig my heels into the bedrock. I become a rivet in the hull of the world. The roots tear at my armor, trying to pull me into the slurry, but my density is my defense. I am the stone that refuses to be rolled.

The Verticals fear me, but they revere me. They call me the "Old Weight" in their sign language.

I am the source of their totems.

Every few cycles, as my armor grows and cracks under the stress of the tectonic shifts, I shed. Plates of black, mineralized keratin flake off my shoulders and back, falling into the abyss or clattering to the root-mat below. These plates are heavy—impossibly heavy for a creature of the Verticals' stature.

But I have seen them. I watch through my thermal visor as tiny, grey shapes swarm over my discarded skin. They strip the shards, wrapping the jagged edges in soft fur. They carve them into shapes. These are the stones they press to their chests, the weights that allow them to sleep without drifting away. I cast off the ballast of the world, and they make it their salvation.

I do not mind them. Occasionally, one will venture too close, seeking the warmth of my radiating hull before the freezing night air sets in. They are fragile, frantic things. If the wind shifts too violently, I might raise a massive, calloused hand to block the gust, creating a windbreak for the tiny cluster of shivering refugees. I do not do this out of kindness. I do not understand kindness. I do it because the disturbance irritates my equilibrium.

I am a solitary point of order in a chaotic, fluid universe. I dream of the tectonic plates locking into place, of the entire world freezing into a single, unmoving slab of granite. Until then, I stand in the fissure, radiating heat, shedding stones, and waiting for the green hurricane to exhaust itself.
