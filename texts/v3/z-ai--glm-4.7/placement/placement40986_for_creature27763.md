I find myself on the Knife’s Edge, the slimmest, most precarious outrider of the city, a slender rib of granite that juts out over the infinite cloud sea. Here, the wind has a rhythm—it inhales with a low, mournful moan and exhales in a sharp, steady whistle. I am standing motionless on a ledge that would barely accommodate the footprint of a lowlander, but I am anchored.

I am a Keeper.

My physical form is a testament to the verticality of this existence. I am tall, my arms spanning nearly twice the length of my torso, allowing me to bridge gaps between handholds without the need to climb. My skin is the color of the wet slate around me, a porous, grey parchment that weeps moisture constantly. I feel the damp not as a chill, but as a second skin, a barrier between my internal slow-fire and the sharp, thin air.

I am draped in the heavy pelt of a six-legged grazer, the wool thick, matted, and smelling faintly of the lichen it once ate. But this is no mere garment; it is my anchor. Sewn into the hem are smooth, river-washed stones, heavy enough to pull my shoulders down, to ensure my center of gravity remains rooted to the earth. To move quickly is to fall; therefore, I do not move quickly. I glide, a shuffle of friction and balance, a ghost in a weighted coat.

My station is the Basin of the Third Echo. It is a carved depression in the rock, perfectly circular, fed by a single, perpetual crack in the ceiling above. My task is simple, yet it consumes the entirety of my focus: I must ensure that the water level remains at the precise line of the ancient, rust-stained groove etched into the stone.

Because nothing rots and nothing flows, the water does not drain. It accumulates slowly, drop by drop, from the condensation on the ceiling. If it rises above the line, the vibration of the city changes—the pitch of the droplet striking the surface shifts by a microtone. To allow this is to introduce dissonance into the Equilibrium.

And so, I stand. I watch.

My eyes, vast pools of liquid blackness that drink the light, are fixed on the meniscus of the water. I do not blink. The droplet forms above, a teard of crystal gathering at the tip of a limestone stalactite. It swells slowly. It could take minutes; it could take hours. Time here is not measured by the sun, for the shadows do not shift. It is measured by the rhythm of the fall.

*Plink.*

The sound is pure, a bell tone in the silence. The water ripples, lapping gently against the sides of the basin. I lean forward, my long, shadowed face hovering inches from the surface. I watch the ripples die down, watched them flatten back into a mirror of the grey ceiling.

It is exactly at the line.

A deep, resonant satisfaction settles in my chest—not joy, but the rightness of a key turning in a lock. The order is maintained. I nod once, a slow, deliberate gesture that takes several seconds to complete.

I am not alone in my solitude. Below me, clinging to the sheer face of the cliff, is another Keeper. I can feel the vibration of their presence through the soles of my feet. They are tending to a patch of luminous moss, ensuring it does not encroach upon the structural integrity of a stone brace. We do not call out to one another. To speak would be a waste of breath, a disturbance in the air. Instead, I shift my weight slightly, pressing my left foot harder against the granite. The vibration travels down the rock, a signal: *Stable. All is well.*

A moment later, a returning vibration hums back up through the stone: *Acknowledged.*

There is no hunger in me, only the slow, rhythmic burning of energy that matches the respiration of the wind. I do not remember when I arrived here, and I do not contemplate when I will leave. I am as permanent as the basin, as the damp, as the mountains themselves.

As the "day"—which is really just an eternal, bright noon—begins to dim toward the "evening" (though the shadows never lengthen, only the quality of the light grows thinner), I feel the pull of the Great Sleep. It is time to return to the roost.

I turn, my robes whispering against the stone, and begin the traversal back toward the main spire. I reach out with my elongated arms, gripping the protrusions of the rock, pulling myself along the vertical face. Below me, the cloud sea is an unmoving carpet of white. Above me, the peaks are jagged spears of grey.

I find the designated sleeping post, a flat pillar on a wide, windswept terrace. Others are already there, strapped in, leaning back, their grey faces slack and smooth. I unbuckle the heavy stone belt from my waist and wrap the rawhide strap around my waist and the pillar, securing myself. I lean back until my weight is fully supported by the bindings.

I close my eyes.

My heartbeat, already slow, begins to decelerate, syncing with the distant, rhythmic dripping of a thousand basins throughout the city. *Drip. Pause. Drip.* My breathing shallows. My body cools, hardening, becoming indistinguishable from the stone at my back. I am not dead; I am paused. I am a cog in a frozen machine, waiting for the light to return so that I may resume my watch, ensuring that nothing changes, ever again.
