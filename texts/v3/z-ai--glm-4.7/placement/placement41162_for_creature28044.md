I would be found at the highest point of the central "plaza," standing upon a bridge of hardened resin that connects the two oldest succulent giants—the Founders. In the amber light of the spire, I am a statue of slate-grey porcelain, motionless, indistinguishable from the architecture I tend.

I am a **Stress-Singer**, a specialized caste of Weaver.

While my kin focus on the expansion of the city—spinning new arches and extending the ramparts into the empty air—my purpose is internal. I am the keeper of the silence. I perceive the structural integrity of the spire not through sound, for there is none, but through the vibration of light and the sub-atomic tremors that travel up through the stone roots of the trees.

I possess the same elongated, six-limbed body as the others, covered in the hard, ceramic-like chitin that protects us from the dry, abrasive dust. However, my shell is darker, a deep, lustrous charcoal that absorbs rather than reflects the amber sun, allowing me to blend perfectly into the shadows of the canopy. My eyes are larger than the average Weaver's, faceted crystals designed to catch the faintest glimmer of light from the resin structures.

My existence is one of profound, agonizing stillness. I stand braced against the trunk of a Founding tree, my legs locked in a rigid tripod, my forelimbs resting lightly against the sticky, translucent wall of a nearby hive. For days, I do not move. I do not eat. I merely *am*.

I am watching for the micro-fractures.

In a world of sudden violence and eternal patience, a single hairline crack in a load-bearing resin strut could spell catastrophe for the entire micro-metropolis. Because the Weavers communicate through color, I have evolved to see stress as hue. To me, a structure under too much strain does not groan; it turns a pale, sickly violet. A joint that is drying out and becoming brittle does not creak; it flashes a jagged, feverish yellow.

When I perceive a shift in the spectrum—a discordant note in the visual symphony of the city—I engage my Loom. But I do not spin the structural, amber cables of the builders. My gland secretes a viscous, pearlescent fluid, a "medicinal" resin derived from a specific diet of the inner-most, most succulent leaves of the Founders.

I move then, a sudden, fluid motion breaking the hours of stasis. I approach the violet-stressed area, my delicate forceps-fingers manipulating the pearlescent goo, smoothing it over the fracture. I seal the wound. As the resin cures, it absorbs the stress, the violet fading back into a healthy, dull slate grey.

I am the doctor to the bones of the city.

Below me, the Polishers scurry like animated stones, cleaning the walkways, their silent industry a blur of brown and green. Somewhere, deep within the trunk I lean against, the Heart-Dwellers swim in the dark sap, purifying the water that sustains us all. I sense their presence as a rhythmic, soothing pulse in the wood—a heartbeat that travels through my legs and into my shell.

Around me, the "city" breathes. The air is thick with the scent of heated sap and dry dust. The light is hard, casting geometric shadows that cut across the resin floor. Occasionally, a soft *thrum* echoes from the forest floor below—a fruit falling—and I shift my eyes downward, watching the ripple of alarm colors (a quick wash of teal) pass through the Weavers on the lower levels, fading as quickly as it came.

I am nearing the end of my cycle. My shell feels heavier, thicker, calcified. The urge to climb is beginning to itch in my joints. Soon, I will leave my post at the plaza. I will ascend the highest spire, to the point where the wind finally moves and the air turns thin. There, I will anchor myself to the stone, and let the silence take me completely. I will dissolve; I will become ossified. I will become a permanent gargoyle, a new crenellation on the edge of the void, watching over the biological metropolis with blind, stone eyes.

But not yet. For now, there is a hairline fracture forming on the eastern arch. It is flickering a faint, dangerous purple. I must attend to it. I raise a foreleg, extend a tine of pearlescent resin, and wait for the amber light to catch the flaw.
