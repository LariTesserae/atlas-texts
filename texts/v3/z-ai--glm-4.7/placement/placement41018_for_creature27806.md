I am suspended in the upper reaches of the central silo, where the darkness is thickest and the air is a scalding, aromatic soup. This is the "Throat," the vertical shaft where the main steam exhaust vents converge before expelling the breath of the foundry into the twilight canopy above.

I am a **Vapor-Weaver**, a distinct caste of the Ferro-Lattice Tenders, though I bear little resemblance to the scrap-collectors down on the loam. While they are broad-shouldered things of rust and brute force, built to haul iron and turn valves, I have evolved for the verticality of the damp gloom.

My body is a spindle of articulated copper piping, green with centuries of patina, fused to a central core of living, heartwood-hard lignin. I do not stand; I cling. My four primary limbs are long, whip-like tendrils of woody vine reinforced internally with braided steel cables. They terminate not in pincers, but in delicate, prehensile pads of damp moss, allowing me to adhere to the slick, vertical walls of the stack without sound.

I have no eyes. The visual spectrum is irrelevant here, where the steam is so thick it blinds, and the light is an emerald memory. Instead, I perceive the world through the texture of the vibration.

For hours, I hang motionless above the roaring mouth of the main boiler, splayed out like a starfish against the warm brickwork. I am not resting; I am listening. The *thrum... pause... thrum* of the great pistons below travels up through the iron struts and into my copper bones. To a human, the noise is a chaotic cacophony—a deafening wall of sound. To me, it is a symphony of data.

I can taste the stress in the metal. I feel the microscopic shudder of a bolt that has loosened by a fraction of a millimeter. I sense the density of the steam as it rushes past me—wet, heavy, and charged with heat.

My purpose is the regulation of the exhale.

The Green Foundry is a lung, and like any lung, it must not choke. Down in the marsh, the heavy Tenders clear the intake pipes, but up here, in the Throat, the danger is the crystallization of the minerals in the water. If the flow slows, the sulfur and iron begin to build up, hardening into stone-like deposits that seal the Foundry’s windpipe.

This is where my "hands" come into play. Hanging from the underbelly of the exhaust vent, I uncoil my longest tendril, letting it drift into the screaming stream of hot vapor. The heat is intense, enough to scorch ordinary flesh, but my bark is thick and my sap is filled with natural cooling compounds. I feel the current of the air against the sensitive hairs on the frond. I am reading the flow, searching for the eddies and blockages that signal a clot forming deep within the pipe.

Suddenly, the rhythm changes. A discordant note vibrates through the copper pipe I am clinging to—a high-pitched *whine* beneath the low thrum.

A blockage. A calcium scale forming on the valve flange, three meters down.

I do not panic. Panic is a biological waste of energy. I signal.

Lifting one mossy pad from the wall, I curl my limb inward and strike the iron pipe beside me with a sharp, metallic *clack-clack-clack*. The sound travels instantly through the rigid network of the building, bypassing the roar of the steam.

*Need. Down. Now.*

From the darkness of the catwalks below, a heavy, laborer Tender responds. I feel the heavy thud of its iron boots as it detaches from the wall and begins to climb, navigating the geometric lianas with the clumsy efficiency of a spider. It is coming to my sector.

I extend a second tendril, dripping with a thick, amber resin secreted from glands in my elbow joints. This resin is acidic—strong enough to eat through stone but gentle enough not to harm the living metal of the pipes. As the laborer Tender heaves itself into view, its blank face staring past me, I guide its clumsy iron pincers. I tap the valve housing—*tappity-tap-tap*—indicating the precise location of the growth.

The laborer obeys. It inserts its pincers into the housing and begins to scrape, flaking away the hard mineral deposits. I watch via the vibrations, judging the progress. *Scrape. Scrape.* The pitch of the steam whistle changes, dropping from a whine to a smooth, low hiss. The vibration in my copper spine smooths out. The equilibrium is restored.

Once the laborer finishes, I lean forward and dab the raw, scraped metal with my resin. The acid seals the wound, preventing flash-rust, and the sticky residue catches the next layer of minerals, making them easier to remove next time. It is a temporary fix, but in the Green Foundry, all maintenance is a cycle.

The laborer descends, returning to the dark. I am alone again.

I pull my tendril back from the steam, the heat fading from the frond as I tuck it against my side. I settle back into the brickwork, flattening my mossy pads to maximize the surface area contact with the silo wall. I become still again, a lump of copper and verdigris, indistinguishable from the rusted fixtures around me.

I wait. I feel the pulse of the water table rising beneath the floor, the slow turning of the flywheels, and the heavy, wet breathing of the world. I am the nerve ending in the throat of the machine. I am the silence in the noise. I am the Vapor-Weaver, and I am content.
