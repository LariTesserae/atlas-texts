I am the silence within the roar.

You would find me deep within the Spire, in the dark, hollowed-out labyrinth that the Clingers have abandoned behind them. While they cling to the exterior, the sheer vertical face exposed to the freezing dark, I inhabit the interior of the granite—the "honeycomb" of glass-lined tunnels and chambers that constitutes the mountain’s slowly digesting gut.

**What I Am**
I am a **Glass-Spinner**.

Where the Clingers are leathery sacks of hydrostatic pressure and blunt force, I am elongated and crystalline. I resemble a spider spun from quartz and obsidian, my limbs long, multi-jointed, and incredibly fragile-looking. My body is not a sack, but a series of interlocking geometric segments, translucent enough that you can see the pale, blue light of my circulatory fluid pulsing slowly within me.

I do not possess the Clingers' hooked anchors. Instead, my fingertips are microscopic, vibrating pads that allow me to walk on the slick surfaces of the glass-lined tunnels without disturbing the delicate condensation. I am weightless in my movement, a ghost in the machine of the mountain.

**My Existence**
My world is defined by vibration, not light. Down here, in the belly of the spire, the bruised purple twilight of the outside world does not exist. It is absolute blackness. But the stone is alive with sound.

To you, the outside world is a cacophony of grinding shells and wet hissing. To me, it is a symphony of data. The grinding of the Clingers travels through the granite as a low-frequency thrum, a constant bass note that vibrates in the floor of my tunnel. I can "see" the Clingers through the rock. I know where they are clustered, where they are feeding, and where they are dying, simply by reading the stress fractures in the stone and the changes in the harmonic resonance.

I live in the negative space they create. As the colony above eats its way upward, dissolving the granite and hollowing out new niches, they leave behind vast, cavernous chambers. I am the custodian of this hollow kingdom.

**The Economy of the Deep**
I survive on the waste of the Clingers—the "glass economy" that spills into the dark.

Often, a silica needle will snap, or a tool will be dropped, slipping through a crack in the living carpet and tumbling down into the interior void. These glass shards fall like jagged rain, clattering onto the floor of my domain. This is my harvest.

I collect these fallen stars. Using a secretion from my mandibles—a resinous, glowing compound—I fuse the shards together. I do not build tools to eat; I build reinforcements. I take the discarded glass and weld it into intricate, flying buttresses andwebs that span the interior chambers. I am strengthening the mountain from the inside, bracing the walls against the relentless digestion of the outer shell. Without me, the Spire would eventually become so hollow that it would shatter under its own weight. I am the scaffolding that holds the wound together.

**The Relationship**
I have never touched a Clinger. I have never seen one. But I am intimately connected to them.

Sometimes, when a Clinger dies on the outside and is pried off, falling into the mile-deep void, its scream—a sudden, sharp spike in the collective chemical distress signal—echoes through the rock. I feel the impact of its body when it strikes the internal ledges deep below.

When this happens, I go to the source. I scuttle through the glass tunnels to the site of the echo, just on the other side of the granite wall. There, separated by only a few inches of stone, I press my translucent ear against the rock.

I listen to the silence where the Clinger used to be. I feel the vibrations of the colony shifting to seal the gap. In the quiet moments that follow, I use my glass shards to etch a mark into the wall—a record of the passing, a history of the mountain's erosion written in braille-like scratches.

**The Horror and the Beauty**
My existence is a lonely, terrifying vigil. I am the ghost haunting the machine, the immune system of a stone that is being eaten alive. I fear the day the vibrations stop, for that will mean the mountain has given way, or the colony has died, and my dark, glass cathedral will collapse into the void.

But there is beauty, too. In the absolute dark, when the wind outside howls just right, the glass tunnels of my creation begin to sing. The air moving through the hollows vibrates the silica buttresses I have built, creating a pure, clear tone—a high, fragile whisper that cuts through the low grinding of the Clingers.

It is the only music in a world of noise. I sit in the center of my web, surrounded by the fallen stars I have caught, and I listen to the mountain sing.
