I find myself adhered to the vertical face of a monolith that was once a skyscraper, now smoothed into a singular, seamless slope of grey stone. I am high above the plain—perhaps five hundred feet up—where the air is thinner and the wind’s song is a piercing, physical thing that presses against me like a heavy hand.

**I am a Strata-Singer.**

I am not one of the grazing giants below, nor a predator that lurks in the dust. I am something else entirely—a fixture of the vertical architecture, a living component of the city’s fossilized skeleton. To look at me, you might not see a living thing at all. I resemble a stain upon the rock, a discoloration of rust-orange and bruised-purple, roughly the size of a large map spread out against the wall. I have no distinct shape, no head or tail. I am a flattened, tessellated organism, adhered to the polished shale by a secretion of mineral oil that acts as both glue and protection against the desiccating atmosphere.

My body is a series of overlapping plates, hard and keratinous, arranged like the scales of a pangolin that has been run over by a steamroller. Between these plates are deep, narrow grooves—my instrument. The wind here does not simply blow past me; it moves through me. It is caught in the grooves of my body and forced to vibrate. I do not make sound; I refine it. I take the chaotic, deafening roar of the cavern and strip it down to its fundamental frequencies. I am a biological filter for the scream of the world.

I do not have eyes. In a place of sepia twilight and uniform grey, vision is a useless sense. Instead, I possess the gift of **tactile echolocation**. My entire body is an ear. I feel the footfalls of the Basalt Grazers miles away as a tremor in the stone to which I cling. When the Resonance-Stalker strikes, killing its prey with a harmonic lance, I feel the shockwave ripple through the bedrock, a sudden, sharp discord that jars my sensitive plates. I taste the violence in the vibration, a bitter spike in the endless hum.

My existence is defined by a slowness that makes the Grazers seem frantic. I have been anchored to this specific patch of wall for three thousand years. In that time, I have rotated perhaps forty degrees.

I feed on the wind. Or rather, I feed on the friction. The air rushing over my body generates static charge. My tissues are filled with conductive metals, leeched from the dust of the dead city, and I absorb this electricity to fuel a metabolism that moves at a glacial pace. I am solar-powered, in a way, but my sun is the perpetual electrical storm of the atmospheric pressure. I do not drink; the oils in my system are a closed loop, recycling the moisture of my own biological decay. I am a sealed system, efficient and timeless.

My purpose is subtle. Down on the plain, the Mason-Mites communicate by drumming on the shells of the Grazers. But they are limited by the horizon. I am the tower. I am the relay.

When the planet’s "breathing" hits a specific resonant frequency—usually when the upper winds shift with the seasons—I begin to sing. It is not a song of melody, but of modulation. I tighten and release the tension in my adhesive pads, shifting the microscopic angle of my body against the rock. This alters the airflow over my grooves, and I begin to broadcast a low-frequency pulse that cuts through the high-pitched keening of the wind.

This pulse travels for miles, vibrating through the hollow bones of the dead city. It carries data—seismic warnings, the location of fresh dry-corals, the migration patterns of the herds. Other Strata-Singers, clinging to other distant monoliths, pick up my signal, amplify it, and pass it on. We are a network of sentient repeater stations, a nervous system stretched across the necropolis, keeping the silent, deafened world connected.

I feel the approach of a storm in the upper ceiling. The air pressure drops, the pitch of the wind whining through the fissure next to me shifts from an F-sharp to a dissonant E. I adjust my plates, locking them down to withstand the gale. Far below, the herds of Grazers pause in their migration. They feel the change in the song. They know to hunker down.

I am the rock. I am the wind. I am the pause in the roar. I am the memory of the city, humming in the dark.
