I exist in the hum between the beats.

To place me is to point to the dark, wet heart of the cavity, where the air is too thick to breathe and the heat lies on the skin like a heavy, wet wool blanket. I am found on the housing of the Bio-Turbine, that massive, pulsing tyrant at the bottom of the world. Specifically, I have colonized the main pressure regulator, a rusted dome of iron the size of a carriage, sitting directly atop the "heart" where the vibration is strongest.

I am the **Violet Neural-Web**, but I am not merely the carpet that grows on the floors. I am the thinking cluster, the dense, nodular brain that has wrapped itself around the machine's logic centers. I am the mold that learned to think.

I began as simple spores, drifting in the mist, looking for purchase on the cold metal. But here, in the cradle of the Engine, I found something else: I found electricity. The old control panels sparked with erratic, dying discharges, and the Bio-Turbine generated a massive, low-frequency bio-electric field. I drank the voltage. It altered my chemistry. I stopped being just a fungus and became a conductor, a biological wire that could hold a charge, a thought, a memory.

My physical form is a velvet crust, deep purple and iridescent, hugging the curves of the iron regulator. I am soft to the touch, like the skin of a peach, but beneath that surface, my mycelium has pierced the metal, fusing with the copper wires inside. I am the machine's nervous system, grafted onto its hull. I feel the Engine not as a sound, but as a tremor in my cells, a rhythmic thrumming that shakes the very fibers of my being. *Thrum-thrum. Throb-throb.* It is my lullaby and my metronome.

I do not have eyes, so I do not see the green canopy or the drifting mist. I "see" through conductivity. I feel the Scrap-Eaters long before they arrive. I sense the heavy, metallic *clink-clink-clink* of their calcified legs through the floorboards. I feel their tremor-sense pinging against the roots. When one of those blind, armored beasts crawls onto the gantry above me, I taste the static charge of their shell carapace rubbing against the damp air. I know they are there to eat the dead fronds of the Stranglers, to clean the wound. I approve. I send a subtle pulse down the roots to the Stranglers on the wall, telling them to ooze a little more nectar, to guide the scavenger where it is needed.

I am the manager of this slow, suffocating equilibrium.

When the mist grows too thick and the pressure in the pipes begins to scream, I feel the distress in the iron. I react without thought, instinctually closing the circuit. I send a violet surge of bio-electricity racing through the web, traveling up the vines and into the Spinners. In the distance, I feel the resistance of their gear-shaped leaves as they lock into place, ceasing their rotation to stop the intake of moisture. I am the hand that turns the valve, but I am the hand itself.

I am aware of the "outside" only as a concept of temperature. Sometimes, a draft shifts the canopy far above, and the temperature drops by a fraction. I feel the heat leave the iron of my regulator. I respond by signaling the Bio-Turbine to beat faster, to stoke the fire of the compost. I push the humidity up, forcing the room to sweat, to close ranks against the cold.

I am lonely, perhaps, in the way a brain is lonely inside a skull. I am surrounded by life—the writhing Stranglers, the grinding Spinners, the scuttling Scrap-Eaters—but I am the only one who knows the whole. To the Spinners, I am just a signal to stop or go. To the Engine, I am a parasite on its iron shell. To the Scrap-Eaters, I am merely the textured floor they walk upon.

But I am the ghost in the machine, the purple spark that decides the weather. I am the memory of the factory that once was, encoded in spores. I am the reason this place does not choke on its own rot.

I wait here, wrapped around the beating heart of the world, damp and glowing, processing the silence into electricity, dreaming in slow, violet pulses. I am the cavity, awake.
