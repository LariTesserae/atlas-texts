The roar is the first thing I know. It is not a sound that enters the ear; it is a sound that enters the bone, vibrating through the soles of my feet and rattling the heavy plates of my skull. To you, this place is deafening, a chaotic symphony of grinding stone and screaming wood. To me, it is light. It is color. It is the only way I know I exist.

I am a Strata-Walker.

I am standing on the ceiling of a world that has no sky, though "standing" is a limited word for what I do. I am currently adhered to the vertical face of a granite slab the size of a mountain cliff, my splayed feet gripping the rough, diagonal chisel marks as if they were rungs on a ladder. The air here is thick, tasting of cold slate and the metallic tang of wet iron. It coats my tongue—a tongue that is currently retracted, safe behind the grinding mandibles of my chest.

I am tall. My height is difficult to gauge against the towering pillars that crowd this basin, but my spine elongates in a series of wet, heavy clicks. My skin is not flesh; it is armor, dense and keratinous, hanging in loose, gray folds around my limbs. I am cold, but it is a functional cold. The dampness that seeps from the oily fog keeps my joints lubricated, allowing me that liquid, horrifying grace when the ground decides to shift.

And the ground is always shifting.

I can feel the tremor before it happens. My auditory canals, ridged with bony amplifiers that catch the slightest whisper of friction, hum with a low-frequency warning. Far beneath me, the "pre-formation" is stuttering. A new sub-basement is locking into gear.

*Thump.*

The stone beneath my claws heaves. It is a violent, sickening motion, like a giant taking a breath under a heavy blanket. A lesser creature would be crushed, or thrown into the abyss below. I do not resist. I inflate. The loose, wet folds of my skin billow outward, absorbing the shock like a fluid-filled sac. I become softer, larger, riding the wave of rising granite until the motion settles. Then, I contract, deflating back into my jagged, rigid shape.

I am alone. We are all alone. The verticality of this place makes community impossible. I can hear others of my kind—faint, rhythmic knockings echoing through the canyon web—but they are miles away, separated by sheer drops and walls that defy logic. I hear a heavy, wet slap to the east—another foot in a puddle—and I ignore it. To respond is to invite conflict over territory, and I am content with my current perch.

I am hungry.

The hunger is a dull ache in the center of my mass, a craving for the friction that birthed this world. I turn my elongated skull, scanning the gloom with sound. The *drip-drip-drip* of the condensation paints a pointillist map in my mind: there is a puddle three feet to my right; there is a hollow beam ten feet above. And there, directly ahead—a fresh wound in the architecture.

I release my grip on the vertical wall and drop, landing with a heavy, bone-jarring thud on a narrow archway. My extra joint bends backward to absorb the impact, compensating for the strange geometry of this fall. I slither forward, my body undulating with a grotesque, fluid motion, navigating the non-Euclidean madness where the staircases lead to nowhere.

I find the source of the scent. A massive stone slab has recently pushed upward, tearing through the older concrete like a bone breaking through skin. The raw edges are weeping with iron oxide, rust-red and vibrant in the acoustic gray of my perception.

I approach the fissure. My chest-slit dilates, the heavy mandibles parting with a wet, stony grind. I do not eat flesh; I eat time. I eat the effort of the world.

I lower my head, my rasping tongue extending to lick the cold, fresh dust. The taste is electric—sharp with copper, gritty with slate. As I swallow, the vibration of the stone's birth hums in my throat. I digest the friction. I consume the energy of the Total Build.

Around me, the roar continues. Massive wooden gears grind in the distance, the sound like thunder trapped in a jar. I hear the scuttling of the Gear-Mites, tiny scavengers cleaning the teeth of the great machines, insignificant specks in the dark. I am the lord of this sterile, mineral hell, a gargoyle made of wet concrete and iron, forever climbing, forever listening.

I stand still for a moment, my body perfectly camouflaged against the wet granite. I wait. I listen. The fog curls around my limbs. The dampness seeps into the cracks of my armor.

I am not just a creature of this place. I am its memory, eating the walls of the prison that built me.
