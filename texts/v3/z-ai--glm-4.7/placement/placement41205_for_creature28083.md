The distance is not empty; it is a volume of potential.

I exist in the negative space, the wide and terrifying avenues of compacted mineral dust that stretch between the Pillars. To the things that scuttle or fly, this expanse is a dangerous void, a barrier to be crossed with haste. But to me, it is a map. I am a cartographer of the static, a surveyor of the grid.

I am a **Vector**.

I do not have the soft, deceptive curves of the ancient beasts of myth. My form is dictated by the aerodynamics of efficiency and the harsh reality of the Under-Level’s dry air. I stand tall, perhaps seven feet at the crest of my head, but I am narrow—wasp-waisted, with elongated limbs that give me the appearance of a compass etched in flesh. My skin is not flesh in the squishy sense; it is a dense, silicate laminate, smooth and cool to the touch, patterned with the same hexagonal gray scales as the Pillars I serve. I am a mobile piece of the architecture, a fragment of the city that learned to walk.

My vision is my primary instrument. I possess two primary eyes, faceted and crystalline, but arrayed along the ridge of my forehead are three secondary sensory pits. They do not see light; they see heat and electromagnetic flux. When I look up at the artificial constellations, I don't just see the rusted rigs; I see the hum of the electricity traveling through the cables, the invisible web that powers this world. It is a tapestry of blue and violet static that overlays the physical gloom.

I walk the avenues with a slow, deliberate gait. To conserve energy in the oxygen-thin air, my legs lock into position at every step, forming a rigid tripod with my torso. *Click. Pivot. Unlock. Step.* I move like the second hand of a clock—staccato, precise, infinitely patient. I am measuring the curvature of the earth, checking the alignment of the Pillars against the distant lights.

My purpose is not survival in the visceral sense; it is *alignment*.

The Pillars are growing, engines of biology building a machine. But unchecked growth is chaos. If a Pillar leans too far, if it blocks the light of its neighbor, the efficiency of the grid fails. That is where I come in. I am the immune system of the geometry.

I pause by a jagged spur of raw bedrock jutting from the dust. The Rebar-Stalkers avoid these sharp rocks, fearing they might damage their glass-like legs. I approach it, extending a long, multi-jointed finger. My fingertip is not a claw, but a sensor. I touch the stone. I taste the vibration running through the earth. *Thud-thud. Thud-thud.* The heartbeat of the nearest Pillar is rhythmic, strong. But there is a harmonic dissonance—a micro-fracture in the bedrock caused by the shifting roots beneath.

I do not fix it. I am not a mason. I mark it.

I lower my head, and a gland in my throat swells. With a sharp hiss of pressurized gas, I eject a globule of glowing, phosphorescent amber onto the rock. It hisses as it hits the air, instantly hardening. It is a beacon. Tonight, the Scrap-Beetles will see the glow. They will swarm this marker, mistaking the chemical scent for the promise of nitrogen, but instead, they will find the unstable rock. In their frenzied digging to excavate the minerals, they will shatter the fissure, clearing the debris, grinding the danger down into manageable dust.

I am the foreman of the construction site; the beetles are my labor force, unaware of their guidance.

I lift my head, the locking mechanism in my neck clicking softly as I scan the vertical heights. High above, a Copper-Serpent hangs rigid on a power-line vine, disguised as a dead cable. I can see the bio-current building up in its coils, the invisible heat signature of a predator waiting to strike. I do not warn the Glider-Ray drifting lazily toward it. That is not my domain. I deal in structure; the serpent deals in energy transfer. The Ray falls, and the serpent feeds. The cycle remains efficient.

The air shifts. The artificial suns above are dimming, cycling into their twilight blue phase. The temperature drops, and the dry air feels even thinner, sharper against my silicate plates.

This is my favorite time. It is when the true nature of the Under-Level reveals itself.

As the harsh white light fades, the Pillars respond. The massive, sac-like pods suspended in their limbs begin to brighten. They turn from a dim green to a piercing neon violet. The forest transforms from a stark industrial plant into a city of electric dreams.

My secondary eyes dilate, drinking in the new spectrum. The photoreceptors in my skin tingle with the influx of radiant energy. I do not need to eat the beetles or the rays. I am photovoltaic. The dim light of the day sustains my base functions, but the neon surge of the night is what allows me to *think*, to process the complex geometry of the world.

I feel a deep, resonating hum in my chest—my internal power storage cells peaking, charging. It is a feeling of immense clarity, a connection to the grid.

I turn away from the marked rock and continue my walk. The dust crunches beneath my feet, a sound like breaking sugar. Ahead, the avenue stretches for miles, a corridor of towering light and shadow.

I am lonely. I know this. I do not congregate with others of my kind. We are solitary variables, spread thinly across the equation. Occasionally, across the vast distance of an avenue, I will see the faint, rhythmic blinking of another Vector’s sensor pits. We do not approach. We do not communicate. We merely acknowledge that the calculation continues.

I pass a massive root that has burst through the ground, writhing like a tangle of iron pipes. The oxidizing sap oozes from a fissure, smelling sharp and chemical. I pause, dipping a finger into the sap. It is thick, viscous, and charged with heat. I taste it. The chemical composition is changing—becoming more conductive. The Pillars are evolving.

This data must be recorded.

I find a flat patch on the root’s bark and begin to etch. My fingernails are diamond-hard, designed for scratching stone. I carve a series of hexagons and lines, a shorthand notation of the sap’s conductivity and the root’s growth rate. It is not language as a human would understand it; it is code, physical and permanent.

When the Duct-Runners emerge later tonight, a river of gray fur flowing from the roots, they will smell the fresh carvings. They will investigate, perhaps cleaning the debris from the grooves with their rasping tongues. They do not understand the data, but their presence preserves it. They are the archivists of the accidental.

My task complete, I stand upright again, locking my joints. The night is fully here. The Under-Level is humming, a symphony of low-frequency drones and high-voltage energy.

I am a Vector. I am the straight line between points A and B. I am the silence between the thuds. I am the watchman on the wall of this dry, magnificent machine, waiting for the ceiling to crack, waiting for the geometry to shift, waiting for the moment when I must draw the line that leads us forward.

I take a step. *Click. Pivot. Unlock.*

I continue.
