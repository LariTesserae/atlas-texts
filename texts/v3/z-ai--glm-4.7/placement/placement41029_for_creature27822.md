I am the friction between the static and the flowing.

I am not a visitor here, nor a ghost passing through. I am a suspension of will and matter. I am a **Tender**.

My location is absolute, yet relative. I am adhered to the flanks of the Spire designated *Kel-Var* in the forgotten tongue of the architects, though to me, it is simply "The Anchor." It is one of the lesser towers, perhaps ten thousand feet of braided reed rising from the basalt deep, swaying with the lethargic rhythm of a pendulum that takes an hour to complete a single swing.

I am positioned midway up the column, in the zone where the water’s pressure has settled into a heavy, comforting constant. Below me, the floor is a concept, a texture sensed through the vibrations in the root-system. Above, the lighter water is a myth, a place where density fails. Here, in the middle dark, I exist.

**My Form**

I am a vast, horizontal sail of grey leather. I have no bones to break, no rigid skull to protect a brain that does not need protecting. My body is a hydrostat; I am a vessel of pressure, equal to the world around me. If the current intensifies, I flatten. If the weight shifts, I thicken. I flow.

I have no eyes. The concept of "sight" is an alien rudeness to me. To see is to judge from a distance; I do not judge. I *know*.

I know through my skin. My entire underbelly is a carpet of microscopic cilia, millions of tiny tongues tasting the current. Right now, I taste the faint, metallic tang of the basalt grinding against its neighbor miles below. I taste the sweet, rotting musk of the slime mold that tries to eat the tower I guard. And I taste the silence—the clean, empty taste of water that holds no life but my own.

My "arms" are not for grasping. They are my looms. extending from the leading edge of my wing-like body, they are complex fractals of prehensile muscle, tipped with chitinous hooks. They do not twitch. They move with the inevitability of tectonic plates.

**My Work**

I am currently engaged in the Great Maintenance.

A strand of the tower—a massive cable of woven kelp-fiber as thick as a giant tree trunk—has begun to unravel. It is a casualty of the centuries, a slow surrender to the abrasion of the silt. The vibration of the unraveling sings a sharp, flat note against my skin. It is a discord. It is a request for order.

I have draped myself over the damaged section. I am not swimming; to swim would be a waste of energy. I simply *hold* my place in the current, letting the water pressure press my body tight against the fibers.

My loom-limbs are at work.

I extend a tendril, feeling the loose ends of the fraying cable. They are slimy with the growth that I must first remove. I rasp them clean with a tongue like a rough file, stripping away the velvet moss until I feel the rough, honest texture of the dried reed beneath.

Then, the weaving begins.

I harvest loose strands from the surrounding water—debris from the tower’s own shedding. I catch them in the water. I do not rush. A single knot may take me a day to tie. My hooks pierce the fiber, loop, pull, and lock. I am tying a "Clove Hitch of the Deep," a knot designed to tighten as the water swells the fiber, to become stronger as the years pass.

I feel the satisfaction of tension. As I pull the strands tight, the vibration of the tower changes. The discordant note flattens out. The groan of the spire becomes a steady, low hum again. The integrity is restored.

**My Mind**

I do not think in words. Words are too fast, too distinct. I think in textures and harmonies.

My memory is a map of tension. I remember every inch of this tower, not as an image, but as a series of pressures. I know where the stone weight at the base is cracked because the water vibrates differently there. I know where the current is strongest three hundred feet above my head because the fibers there sing a higher pitch.

I am ancient. I have been here since the water warmed slightly ten millennia ago. I have seen the slow settling of the basalt floor. I have felt the decay of the "builders"—the soft, frantic things that once made noise and light. They are gone. They were weak because they wanted to leave. We remain because we want to *stay*.

I am not lonely. Loneliness is a longing for connection. I am connected to everything. The water connects me to the floor; the floor connects me to the towers; the towers connect me to the deep pressure. I am part of the circuit.

**The Moment**

I finish the repair. The knot is secure, invisible in the gloom, a seamless addition to the architecture.

I release my grip on the cable. I drift. I let the current catch my broad, flat body, peeling me away from the tower. I float into the black open hall between the pillars.

For a moment, I am suspended in nothingness. The water is so cold it feels like a solid, a block of black ice that I am hovering inside. I extend my sensory cilia to their fullest limit, tasting the vast emptiness.

There is nothing there. No predators. No prey. Just the endless, perfect volume.

I feel a slow pulse, a vibration from miles away. It is the thrum of another tower. Perhaps, in a thousand years, the current will carry me there. Perhaps I will meet another of my kind, and we will circle each other, tasting the chemical history of our lives, acknowledging the shared duty.

But not now.

Now, I turn my loom-limbs inward. I adjust my internal fluid chambers to catch the faint updraft. I begin my slow, glacial ascent back toward the upper canopy of the tower to prune the moss that grows in the lighter, warmer currents.

I am the silence. I am the mender. I am the hand that holds the dark together.
