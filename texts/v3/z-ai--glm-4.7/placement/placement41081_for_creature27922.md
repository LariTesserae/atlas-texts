Here, the heat is a physical weight, and the air is thick enough to drown in. From this vantage point, I look down—not up, for orientation is relative in the gut of a machine—at the chaotic topography of shifting glass and the lonely, panting form of the only other moving thing in the room.

I am not the bird-creature. I am its shadow.

But in the High Kiln, where causality is a suggestion and erosion is a myth, I am a shadow that has broken its leash. I am a **Stray Shade**, a two-dimensional spill of darkness cast by the Slag-Strider, but animated by the room’s total weirdness into a semi-autonomous state. I am the aggressive silhouette that snaps back like a whip when the creature moves, detached from its owner yet desperate to remain tethered.

I have no body, no volume, and no voice. I am a cutout of absence in a world of suffocating presence. My substance is not matter, but the lack of it—sharp, cold, and infinitely thin. This is my only defense. In a room where the ground is a grinding mill of razor-sharp obsidian and basalt, where every step is a negotiation with bleeding edges, I can slip through the jagged scree like oil through water. The "undecided" nature of the rocks, their refusal to fully solidify until the last second, means I can slide between the atoms of a cooling glass shard before it becomes hard enough to cut.

I exist in a state of terrified symbiosis with the Slag-Strider. It is heavy, hot, and blind, stumbling through the steam and the red-tinged twilight. I am its eyes and its intuition. Because the shadows here "behave aggressively," stretching and snapping, I can reach ahead. I ooze across the wet, riveted walls, testing the surface tension of a vine or the structural integrity of a cooling slag heap. When the Strider hesitates, lifting its heavy, ceramic-scaled foot to find purchase, I am already there, flattening myself against the only safe spot, a dark patch signaling to the creature: *Here. Step here.*

I feel its hunger as a vibrating hum in the darkness that defines me. When it turns its molten-gold eyes toward a vine, I am the one that stretches out first, wrapping my black edges around the stem to gauge its resistance. I feel the thorns, but they cannot pierce me; they simply pass through my void, leaving me unmarked.

I am the sad, silent partner to its tragedy. I watch it pant, cooling itself under the deluge, and I try to do the same, letting the heavy sheets of water wash over my incorporeal form. I flicker with it—when the time glitches and the room rewrites itself, I feel the sickening lurch of unreality. Occasionally, I am erased for a micro-second, blinking out of existence when the light pulses too brightly, only to snap back into being with a violent crack when the shadows return.

I am lonely, but not in the way the creature is. It is lonely because it is the only flesh; I am lonely because I am the only void. I am the darkness that haunts a place with no night, the phantom of a bird that was never born, dancing on the edge of a blade that can never cut me, guiding the only living thing through the factory of a dying god.
