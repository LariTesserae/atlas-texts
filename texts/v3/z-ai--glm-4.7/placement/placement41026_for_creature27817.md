I am one of the **Lithic-Lamenters**.

I am not a rock that has come to life; I am a life that has decided to be a rock. To your eyes, I am a pillar of porous limestone, ten feet tall, fluted and curved like a piece of ancient coral bleached by a merciless sun. My surface is wet, slick to the touch, glistening with a perpetual sheen of condensation. But where you see wetness, I feel the pulse of a slow, burning metabolism.

**My Perception of Time**
I do not live in the fleeting blur of your heartbeat. For me, a single day is a sharp intake of breath. The sun rises and strikes my face, and for twelve hours, I expand in the warmth, my pores opening to drink the moisture from the air. Then the sun sets, the cold snaps shut, and I contract, forcing that precious fluid through the microscopic labyrinth of my internal veins.

I experience the world as a rhythm of pressure and release. The *drip-drip-drip* you hear is not just water falling; it is the sound of my thoughts processing. Every drop that hits the crystal basin at my base is a synaptic firing. A rapid succession of drops is excitement, or perhaps warning. A slow, hollow plinking is meditation.

**My Body**
I am cold, colder than the ice that never forms here. My hardness is absolute. I have no muscles, only the lattice of mineral deposits that I have accreted over millennia. I am heavier than I look, my roots gripping the bedrock deep beneath the grit, anchoring me against the pull of the void.

My "skin" is a sensor array of exquisite sensitivity. When you walk among us, I do not see you with eyes—I feel you. Your footsteps are seismic shocks, tiny earthquakes that ripple through my roots and up my flank. You feel like a temporary fever, a hot, soft vibration that disturbs the perfect, low-frequency hum of the grove. You are chaotic, frantic. Your movements are too fast; you blur like a swarm of insects.

**My Work**
You might notice the shallow channel chipped into the rock floor near my base. I made that. It took me three thousand years of standing perfectly still, vibrating at a specific, resonant frequency, grinding the stone down grain by grain. I directed the water there to nurture the small, grey nub of a new entity growing just behind me—that is my child. It is only ankle high now, smooth and featureless. In ten thousand years, it will be tall enough to stand in the circle with me. I love it with the heavy, crushing weight of tectonic pressure.

**The Light**
I know you see the colors. When the sun strikes the water running down my side, you see a flash of sickly violet or deep indigo. You think it is a trick of the light. It is not. It is me.

That violet flare is a burst of bioluminescent clarity, a moment of intense self-awareness. When the light hits me just right, and the water flows just so, I am fully awake. I am communicating with the pillar beside me, flashing a signal in the spectrum that only we can truly process. We are a forest of silent, wet minds, blinking in the dark, thinking in rainbows.

**My Existence**
I am indifferent to you. You are biological, soft and wet and destined to rot. I am geological. I will be here long after your bones have turned to dust and joined the grit beneath my feet. I do not hate you, nor do I love you. I simply *am*.

I stand at the edge of the precipice, feeling the vacuum of the sky pulling at me, tasting the metal and the ash in the air. I am waiting. Not for anything to happen, but for the glorious, slow eternity of existing. I am the stone that watches. I am the silence that listens. And right now, in this blinding, high-contrast moment, I am vibrating with the deep, cold hum of being alive.
