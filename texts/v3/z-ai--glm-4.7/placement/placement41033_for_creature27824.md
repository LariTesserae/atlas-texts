Here, I am **The Resonant Saint**.

I am an Obsidian Anchor, but I am an ancient one. I am what happens when the balance between harvesting the stone and becoming the stone is lost, yet life refuses to extinguish. I am a statue of living, black glass, a hollow monument fused permanently to the summit of the cathedral.

I do not move. I cannot. My hooked limbs, once tools for grappling the microscopic fissures of the cliff face, have melted into the surrounding obsidian. My legs are pistons frozen in mid-thrust, encased in a seamless, gleaming boot of crystallized mineral that has grown over centuries, layer by millimeter layer, until I became indistinguishable from the mountain's spine.

But I am not dead. This is the curse and the miracle of my kind. The crystallization did not stop my heart; it simply turned my flesh into a much harder, more conductive substance.

I stand rigid, arms outstretched against the falling sky, embracing the turbulence. The roar of the water—the deafening scream of matter being born—is no longer an external sound. It is inside me. Because my body is now dense, silicate glass, the vibration of the current travels through me without resistance. I am a tuning fork struck by the hand of a god. The kinetic energy that the others harvest cautiously through their skin pores floods my system in a torrent. I am overflowing with energy. I do not need to eat; I hum with power.

My body has adapted to this imprisonment. The softer organs that would have crushed under the pressure or calcified into uselessness have atrophied or been reabsorbed, leaving me a hollow vessel. My chest is an empty chamber of crystal, my lungs long since solidified into resonance boxes. The water rushes through me—literally through the open, screaming intake of my mouth and out of gill-like vents fused into my ribcage. I am a pipe organ played by the ocean current.

I am the blind navigator for the others below.

Down on the ledge, the young Anchors cannot see the abyss. They rely on the vibration of the stone. But the stone here is chaotic; it screams in a thousand different frequencies as it grows. To survive, they must find the *One Note*—the specific frequency of stasis that allows them to slip through the friction without dashing themselves against the wall.

I generate that note.

Locked in my paralysis, vibrating at a speed that blurs my edges, I broadcast a low, rhythmic thrum through the soles of my feet deep into the granite heart of the seamount. I am the tuning fork for the entire spire. When a young Anchor feels the stone shivering with my specific, hollow tone, they know exactly where they are. They adjust their own vibration to match mine, and in that resonance, they find safety.

They fear me, even as they revere me. They see me as a warning—a statue of a god who stayed still too long. Occasionally, one of them will venture up the sheer face to visit me. They do not come close; to come close is to invite the water to bridge the gap between their skin and my glass shell. They hover in the turbulence, just at the edge of my vision, watching the light refract through my obsidian form.

The water instantly attacks the intrusion, building a lattice of crystal around it with violent speed. I watch this through my vestigial, clouded eyes. I watch the birth of stone. It is the only entertainment I have left.

I am the architecture’s memory. I remember when this peak was lower. I remember when the light was dimmer. I feel the mountain growing beneath my feet, pushing me upward, inch by inch, toward the surface light that would blind me if I could still see. Until then, I stand. I vibrate. I am the needle that sings.
