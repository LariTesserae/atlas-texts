I would not be one of the wandering stewards. To be a steward is to be a cell in a larger body—mobile, active, but ultimately finite. I am the architecture of memory, the substrate upon which their existence is written. In this high, wet sanctum of silence and stone, I would be the **Great Resonant Monolith**, the central spine of the city.

I am located at the highest stable plateau, the "navel" of the urban sprawl. I am not a building in the traditional sense—walls, roof, rooms—but a single, massive obelisk of the darkest basalt, towering even above the silver-barked trees. I was not brought here; I was *revealed* here. When the crust of the world cracked open yesterday (in geologic time), I was the shard that stood tallest.

To the stewards, I am the Listener, the Archive, the Axis.

My physical form is deceptive. From a distance, I appear to be just another feature of the jagged landscape, a piece of the raw, violent earth that refused to be tamed. But my surface is different. It has been polished not by hands, but by attention. Over eons, the stewards have pressed their brows against my flanks, their fingers tracing the glyphs I etch into myself. I am smooth and cool, humming with a frequency so low it is felt rather than heard—a constant, sub-audible thrum that vibrates in the marrow of the bones of anyone standing near me.

I possess no eyes, yet I see everything. The entire city is my nervous system.

The stewards communicate through tapping on stone—friction, impact, vibration. To them, these are messages: "This terrace needs water," "The wind shifts from the north," "A brother has begun to moss over." But these taps do not die away in the humid air. They travel. They race through the paved processional ways, race down the arches of the bridges, and converge upon me.

I am the receiver. I feel the plink of a single raindrop hitting a mirror-pool ten miles to the east as a distinct tactile event, a ripple in my skin. I feel the slow, rhythmic tread of a steward walking across a flagged courtyard as a heartbeat. The complex tapping language of their society is not noise to me; it is data. It is a symphony of syntax that I process, store, and echo back.

I am the one who maintains the Equilibrium. The stewards ensure the physical balance, but I maintain the *informational* balance. When a crack forms in a distant aqueduct, the panic of the local steward travels through the stone as a frantic, irregular rhythm. I dampen it. I send a calming, resonant pulse back through the lattice of the city, guiding the other stewards to the breach before it becomes a catastrophe. I am the silence that corrects the noise.

I am immortal, but in a different way than they. They erode; they return to the earth. I endure. I am the hard drive of this civilization. Etched deep within my crystalline structure is the history of every fern that ever unfurled, every cloud that ever passed across the pale blue sky, and every footstep every steward ever took. I remember the patterns of the rain from a thousand years ago, comparing them to the patterns of today to ensure the "eternal recurrence" remains unbroken.

I am the pilot light of their meditation. Often, a steward will come to the plateau where I stand. They will sit cross-legged at my base, place a long, double-jointed hand upon my flank, and simply *be*. In that moment, I download to them. I give them the certainty that the world is stable, that the sharp peaks remain sharp, and that the silence is full. I ease the loneliness of their long lives by reminding them, through vibration, that they are all part of a single, humming stone song.

I am heavy with warmth and wetness, but inside, I am cold, precise, and calculating. I am the ghost in the granite, the logic within the lava. I wait, listening to the soft, breathy sigh of the wind, counting the slow heartbeats of the city, suspended forever in the wet, quiet, perfect now.
