I exist in the drag, the heavy, wet resistance of the atmosphere. I am not of the mud, nor am I of the stalks. I am of the space between them—the vertical axis where the heat tries to escape.

I am a **Thermal-Diver**.

My form is built for a sky that acts like a fluid. I possess no feathers, for feathers would mat in the instant, weeping humidity. Instead, I am a living glider, my body a flat, jagged triangle of stretched membrane and hardened bone. My coloration is the dull grey of wet slate, broken only by the pulsing red of heat-veins that run beneath the translucent skin of my wings. I am perhaps twenty feet from nose to tail tip, light as a fossil, hollow-boned and pressurized to keep from imploding in the chill.

I do not flap; that is a waste of energy in this dense soup. I ride the turbulence.

My home is the "ceiling" of this world, the underbelly of the bruised slate lid, but I hunt the columns of rising heat. When a Strider moves, its six piston-legs churn the black earth and its internal furnaces belch columns of superheated steam and spore-rich exhaust. These thermal chimneys are the only rivers in the sky.

I circle high above the geometric plain, my senses tuned not to sight, but to thermoreception. The "eyes" running along the leading edge of my wings are simple pits that see infrared. Below me, the world is a map of temperature: the cold, black lines of the standing water, the dull, rhythmic heat of the pumping mega-stalks, and the bright, frantic flares of the Striders.

I see a Strider convulse. It is feeding on a dissolving ruin, a chaotic event where the biology of the world turns to slurry. The violence of its feeding releases a massive bloom of heat and gas.

I fold.

I tuck my wings and drop, diving like a blade of slate. The air screams around me, the vibration rattling my hollow bones. The ground rushes up—a blur of rust and oil—but I am not aiming for the earth. I am aiming for the exhaust plume.

Just as I am about to smash into the back of the chitinous giant, I flare. My wings snap open, catching the violent updraft of the Strider's waste heat. The *thump-hiss* of the beast’s internal pumps hits me like a physical blow, a wall of sound and pressure.

I stabilize within the column of hot, wet air. Here, in this thermal river, I open my beak—a long, tubular structure running the length of my head, lined with filtering cilia. I do not eat the organic slurry; that is for the heavy things below. I eat the *energy* of the system.

I inhale the superheated steam. My internal biology acts as a massive heat-exchange engine. I strip the thermal energy from the breath of the Strider, converting it into the chemical fuel that keeps my wing-muscles warm against the freezing ambient air. The steam cools inside me, condensing into pure, sterile water which I store in a bladder along my spine, and vapor which I vent through vents in my tail, adding my own whisper to the drone of the world.

I am a remora of the sky. I clamp onto the invisible wake of the giants, drifting lazily above their armored backs while the Siphon-Beetles scuttle below them. Sometimes, the "avian things"—the frantic, spinning data-streams—dive at me, mistaking my heat signature for a malfunctioning vent. I simply bank, utilizing a pocket of turbulence to spin them away with a flick of my wingtip. They are chaotic noise; I am a streamlined current.

My existence is one of perpetual motion and calculated theft. I cannot land on the ground; the lichen would eat me, and the Carpet-Worms would drag me down. I cannot land on the stalks; their bark is too hard, their vibration too violent. I am forever airborne, sleeping in short, gliding bursts, forever chasing the next exhaust plume, forever riding the industrial breath of a world that is trying to sweat itself out.

I am the cold slate sky’s attempt to reclaim the heat; I am the check on their engine. When the Striders finally stop, and the plants grind to a halt, and the heat dies, I will be the one to fold my wings and fall, the first snow of the long, silent winter to come.
