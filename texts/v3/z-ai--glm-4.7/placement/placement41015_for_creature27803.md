I find myself not on the floor, where the desperate scramble of the beetles turns decay into soil, nor high in the vaulted shadows where the bats hang like wet fruit. I exist in the vertical space between—in the sheen of water that coats the curved walls of this great stone stomach.

I am a **Glass-Thread Hunter**.

My form is an adaptation to the "weeping" nature of the rock. I do not cling with claws or suckers like the Wall-Skimners; to do so would be to disturb the silence, to risk the crunch of rock that would summon the Silent-Stalkers. Instead, I walk *upon* the water. I am a spindly, constellation of limbs—six jointed, elongated spokes that radiate from a central, fluid-filled thorax. My body is mostly water and gelatin, a transparency so total that I am invisible against the wet stone until I move, and even then, I am merely a distortion in the refraction of the bruised-purple gloom.

My existence is defined by the surface tension of the wall’s tears. The constant condensation creates a film, a vertical river that I skate atop using microscopic setae on the tips of my legs, trapping pockets of air to keep me buoyant against the slick stone. I am weightless, a ghost riding the tracks of the stone’s sorrow.

My place in the Damp Hollow is that of the silent sniper of the vertical plane. The Wall-Skimners, those frantic blobs of muscle, are my primary harvest. They are obsessed with grazing the algae, their stalk-eyes rotating in paranoia, watching the shadows for the low-slung Stalkers. They never think to look *at* the water they are traversing. I drift above them, matching their speed, sliding down the gradient of a falling droplet. When I strike, it is not with violence, but with a sudden, puncturing needle—a proboscis that extends from my central node. I inject a solvent that liquefies their insides, and I simply drink them as they continue to scramble for a few seconds more, running on neural memory until they collapse into a empty husk that slides away into the darkness below.

I am drawn to the **Ring of Stones**, that calcified curiosity embedded in the moss. It is my sanctuary and my feeding ground. The Echo-Bats use it for their sonar, blasting the air with high-frequency chirps that are meaningless to my simple nerves. But I have evolved to feel the *pressure* of those sound waves. When the bats sing, the air vibrates, and these vibrations ripple across the surface tension of the water film I walk upon.

I "surf" these acoustic ripples.

The bats’ calls create standing waves in the moisture on the walls. By reading these ripples, I can map the location of every insect flitting through the mist near the ring without having to see them. I hunt the insects that are foolish enough to land near the stone, using the bats' own technology as my radar. I am a parasite of their echo.

My days (or whatever measures of time exist in this purple twilight) are spent in a trance of motion. I am part of the damp engine, a component of the humidity. When the heat becomes too much, when the air feels too heavy like wet wool, I simply exhale the excess water from my system, becoming lighter, drifting higher into the mist, dangling from a single strand of silk secreted from my spinnerets, suspended in the aerosol.

I am the eyes in the water. I am the silent rider of the weeping walls. In this library of silence, I am the one who reads the ripples.
