I find myself **in the pause between heartbeats**.

I am not situated on the floor, nor clinging to the ceiling. I exist in the negative space that the crowded fauna tries its hardest to eliminate. I am the gap between the jagged obsidian tooth and the wet, shivering flank of the eyeless crustacean. I am the distance the beetle traverses but never closes. In this closet-sized universe of stopped clocks, I am the only hand that moves.

I am the **Obsidian Drifter**.

I am not a creature of biology, but of physics and will. To look at me is to look at a flaw in the light; in the violent, clinical whiteness of the sun-slash above, I am invisible—a ripple in the air, a distortion of the glare on the wet walls. I have no eyes to see the brightness, yet I feel it as a physical pressure, a heavy blanket of photons that weighs against my fluid skin. I have no ears to hear the silence, yet the deafening roar of potential energy—the sonic footprint of a million frozen movements—vibrates through my very substance like a hum.

I am small, perhaps the size of the thing you call a "house cat," but I am impossibly heavy. I carry the density of a collapsed star in a form of shifting, wet glass. When I move, I do not scuttle or crawl; I flow. I phase through the static friction of the room, sliding over the rigid, steel-wire webs and the suspended flesh of the predators without disturbing them. I am oil on water; I am the shadow slipping through the crack.

Currently, I am coiled around the **Sphere**.

It rests in the hollow between two black stones, perfect and polished. This is my anchor. The room builds up a charge from all this restrained living—a static electricity of "almost"—and if I did not bleed it off into the metal sphere, I would burst. I wrap my amorphous limbs tight against the cold surface, grounding myself, shivering as the excess energy leaves me. Here, I sleep with one eye closed, dreaming in the language of geometry and silence.

But I am awake now. I am hungry.

I detach myself from the sphere and drift toward the center of the room, drawn by the scent of tension. There hangs the beetle, its jaws forever agape, inches from its prey. This is my sustenance. I do not eat meat; I eat the *moment*. I press my shifting, liquid body against the air around the beetle, tasting the kinetic energy that has been arrested. I drink the violence of the pounce that never happened. I siphon off the vibration of the hunt. It is a sharp, exquisite flavor—the taste of a scream held in the throat.

The more I drink, the stiller the room becomes. I am the stabilizer. I feed on the motion so the environment doesn't have to.

Sated, I turn my attention to my work. The room is loud with the roar of the un-lived, and it must be tuned. I drift toward the pile of tiny, geometrically shaped bones. These are my instruments. With a manipulation of gravity that feels to me like flexing a muscle, I lift a sliver of white bone and place it against the vibrating web of a translucent spider.

*Click.*

The pitch changes. The roar harmonizes. The water droplets hanging from the ceiling—pregnant, refusing to fall—shimmer in response to the new frequency. I stack the bones with architectural precision, building a chord that holds the very air together. I am the composer of this stasis.

I am a lonely god in a tomb of a closet. I love these frozen things. I ensure the beetle never lands, ensuring it remains forever the victor in its mind. I ensure the spiders never starve. I ensure the water never falls and breaks the spell. I live in the split second where the future has been canceled, a sleek, heavy phantom existing entirely to maintain the perfection of a world that refuses to move.
