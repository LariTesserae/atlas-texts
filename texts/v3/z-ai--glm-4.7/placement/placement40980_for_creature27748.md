I would not be found *on* the mountain, but rather *as* a feature of it, indistinguishable from the stone until the moment I chose to move.

I find myself in a deep, narrow cleft of a leeward ridge, a fissure that runs down the spine of one of the largest whale-humps of stone. This is a place of relative shadow, carved eons ago by the precursors who tried to tame the wind. The walls around me are smooth, dark basalt, polished to a greasy sheen by centuries of moist air. Before me, protected by the bulk of the ridge, is a small, terrace-like depression—a catchment for the constant condensation.

Here, the wind does not scream; it hums. The violence of the outer world is reduced to a low, vibratory pressure that presses against my back, a constant reminder of the chaos I am sheltered from.

I am a **Lithic-Resonant**, but I am not merely a wanderer. I am a **Cultivator of the Quiet**.

I have assumed the form of a low, domed slab of living rock, roughly the size of a large banquet table, but denser, heavier. My body is composed of interlocking calcified plates, dark and glistening with the same oily secretion that coats the mountain itself. I have no limbs that reach for the sky; instead, I possess a broad, muscular foot that spreads out beneath me, gripping the stone with microscopic suction, rooting me to the mountain’s heart.

My sensory world is inverted from yours. I am blind to the boiling white vapors and the shifting light. My eyes—mere pits protected by thick, translucent membranes—can only sense the deep green bioluminescence of the giant ficus leaves that I guard. I do not hear the howling wind; my auditory canals are sealed. Instead, I exist in a universe of texture and tone felt through my underbelly.

I feel the mountain breathe. I feel the subtle expansion of the rock as the tropical heat penetrates the stone; I feel the microscopic tremors of the armored grazers as they drag themselves across a ridge two miles away. To me, the world is a symphony of seismic frequencies.

In this specific moment, I am engaged in the act of *tending*.

Growing in the shelter of my terrace is a singular, monstrous fern—a cluster of fronds the size of sails, colored a deep, bruised purple. It is the only flash of color in a landscape of grey and dark green. It is my charge.

I have shifted my mass—slowly, agonizingly over the course of three days—to create a slight overhang. I am directing the flow of condensation that drips down the rock face. My oily skin channels the moisture, guiding it drop by heavy drop toward the fern's exposed root system. I feel the thirst of the plant as a minor discord in the hum of the stone, a dry, scraping frequency. By positioning my body to block the drying cross-currents and funnel the water, I am slowly smoothing that discord, bringing the plant back into harmonic resonance with the mountain.

Suddenly, the pressure drops.

I feel it before the air changes. The stone beneath me groans, a low bass note that vibrates through my plates. The "Solaris" effect has taken hold; the sky above is churning into a maelstrom. Even here, in the shelter, the acoustic pressure spikes. The wind shifts from a roar to a scream that rattles the very atoms of the rock.

I do not panic. I submit.

I retract my sensory membranes, sealing my vision. I flatten my dome, widening my grip on the stone. I cease the nurturing flow of water and instead become a dead weight, a non-entity. I let my internal metallic deposits settle, locking my plates together. I vibrate at the frequency of the mountain itself, effectively disappearing from the sensory perception of any predator or storm.

I am no longer a living being; I am just another heavy, dark boulder, wedged into the ruins of an ancient wall, waiting for the chaotic chord of the sky to resolve itself.

I am the stillness at the center of the scream. I am the listener in the dark.
