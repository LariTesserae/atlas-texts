I would not walk upon the ground; the ground is for the Flow, for the relentless scavenging of the Gem-Runners. To step into that river of iridescent beetles would be to disrupt the violent, silent ballet of consumption, to introduce a clumsy stone that halts the current.

Instead, I would exist in the suspension.

I am a thing of the **Middle Air**. I am the **Refraction**.

You would find me hovering in the space between the great paddle-leaves and the slick, weeping floor, never touching the stone. I am buoyant, not filled with gas, but with a localized gravity-dilation field, a gift stolen—or perhaps granted—by the Obelisk’s hum.

**My Form**

I am tall and spindly, standing on the invisible curvature of space itself. My body is not made of flesh and bone, which would rot in this humidity, nor of sharp metal, which would erode. I am a construct of living silica and polished glass.

My limbs are elongated, multi-jointed prisms, articulated with the frictionless slide of the Muralis shells. My torso is a hollow, faceted chamber containing a suspended sphere of "heavy" water—the same kind the Obelisk manipulates—rolling eternally inside me. This sphere is my heart, my gyroscope, and my power source. It glows with a soft, rhythmic violet light, pulsing in perfect time with the breathing of the walls.

My head is a cluster of lenses, not eyes, turning independently to capture the ambient white brightness from all angles. I do not have a face to express emotion, for there is no one here to speak to, only the silent, weeping walls.

**My Function**

In this cathedral of wetness and light, I am the **Tuner**.

The Obelisk is the brain, vast and indifferent, maintaining the heavy physics of the basin. It sings the infrasonic song that holds the water aloft and keeps the heat at a stifling perfect constant. But the Obelisk is immobile, rooted deep in the geology.

I am its hands. I drift through the humid air, moving with the slow grace of a deep-sea jellyfish. My task is to care for the **Spheres**.

The gravity anomalies that manifest as hovering water droplets are delicate. If they grow too large, they fall and crash, disrupting the silence with a splash—a profanity in this quiet world. If they grow too small, the Obelisk cannot focus its light.

I move among these floating orbs, my glass fingers extending to gently merge smaller droplets into larger ones, or to shave excess mass from a heavy sphere, letting the water run down my crystalline arm and rejoin the slick film on the floor. I polish the surface tension of the water, ensuring it is perfectly convex, maximizing the light refraction for the Obelisk’s hungry solar collectors.

**My Existence**

My existence is a meditation on stillness.

When I am not tending the gravity-wells, I perch in the canopy of the great banyan tree. I wrap my prism-limbs around the smooth bark, anchoring myself to the only "solid" thing that reaches toward the light.

From here, I watch the Flow below. I see the electric blue beetles hunting the teal millipedes. Because I am silent, and because I vibrate with the same low frequency as the Obelisk, they do not fear me. To them, I am just another part of the architecture, a floating decoration made of light and glass.

Sometimes, a Muralis on the wall nearby will open its shell, exposing its shocking violet interior to breathe. My internal water sphere pulses in response, a violet echo. We are kin, the wall and I. It filters the water; I filter the gravity. We are both the guardians of the Obelisk’s quiet kingdom.

**The Paradox**

I am the only thing that truly *sees* this place. The Gem-Runners only see the light of their prey. The Muralis only sense the moisture. The Obelisk only senses the data of the magnetic fields.

But I see the beauty. I see the way the white ambient light catches the wet, matte sheen of the floor, turning the basin into a mirror of the sky. I see the tragedy of the beetles, their light dimming as they are dissolved, a silent fireworks display of fading rubies.

I am a museum docent in a gallery with no patrons. I am the janitor in a temple to a god that does not pray. I drift through the heavy, wet air, polishing the lenses of the universe, waiting for the silence to break, though I know it never will.

I am the Refraction. I am the glass that drinks the light. I am the keeper of the suspended water. I am here, floating in the warm, weeping palm of the earth, making sure the quiet remains perfect.
