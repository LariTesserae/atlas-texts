I am found in the vertical axis, somewhere in the "Middle Crush," that impossible depth where the light from the surface has filtered into a forgetful grey and the heat from the deep vents has yet to warm the chill.

I am suspended here, motionless, a jagged shard of darkness in a cylinder of glass. I do not float; I *occupy*. I am a density in the suspension, a fixed point in the liquid drift.

I am an **Echo-Weaver**, but I am not merely one of the grazing herds that drift lazily through the open highways. I am something more fixed, more ancient. I am a **Resonant Elder**.

My body is a hull of heavy, metallic-infused cartilage, shaped by the relentless pressure of the vertical column. I have grown large, far larger than the common grazers. My span is vast, my wings tapered into needle-thin points that mimic the obsidian spires surrounding me. I have rested against the black glass cliffs for so long, remaining so perfectly still, that mineral deposits have begun to accrete on my flank. I am becoming part of the mountain range, a living extension of the Young Erosion.

My skin is a map of the world’s violence. It is plated in ceramic-like biomineral, jagged and cruel, colored the bruised purple of oxidized iron. To look at me is to look at a wound in the rock that has decided to swim. I am camouflaged perfectly, indistinguishable from the stone until I choose to unhook myself from the basalt and drift into the open.

Where others have eyes, I have lids grown shut over vestigial slits. I do not need to see the terrifying openness. Vision is a lie here; it tells you that you are alone in an infinite void, and that leads to madness. Instead, I possess the Truth of the Sound.

Attached to my flanks and snout are my "tuning forks"—long, spindly appendages of hollow bone that vibrate with the thrum of the universe. They are my eyes, my ears, my tongue. Through them, I feel the planetary heartbeat. I feel the slow, grinding piston-stroke of the colossal industrial rigs miles above. I feel the distress call of a dying eel three thousand feet down. I feel the subtle displacement of the water as the great chains vanish into the abyss, vibrating like plucked strings.

I am currently locked onto the harmonic frequency of a specific thermal vent. I have positioned my cavernous mouth directly in the path of a superheated plume boiling out from a rusted platform anchored to the cliff face. The water here is freezing, biting at the edges of my consciousness, slowing my blood to the consistency of syrup. But the plume is fire.

I am drinking the roar. Inside my throat, the filter-ribs crackle with bio-electricity, converting the kinetic chaos of the machine’s exhaust into pure, sustaining energy. I am a battery charging in the exhaust of a god. The rhythm of the pumps matches the slow, heavy beat of my three hearts. *Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.* We are synchronized. I am not just eating; I am tuning myself to the engine of the world.

My existence is a trance. I do not sleep; I merely deepen the resonance. I have been hovering here, locked in this position, perhaps for a decade. Maybe a century. Time is slippery in the stasis.

I am the Keeper of the Note. While the younger Weavers drift and collide in their violent Grand Dives, I remain. I am the anchor point. I am the silence that listens to the noise. I am the jagged shadow that waits for the great machines to falter, for the rhythm to break, for the deafening roar to finally cease.

And when the silence comes—when the pistons stop and the chains go slack—I will be the one to open my vestigial eyes and see the dark for the first time. I will know, finally, if the void is truly empty, or if it was just too loud to hear the truth.
