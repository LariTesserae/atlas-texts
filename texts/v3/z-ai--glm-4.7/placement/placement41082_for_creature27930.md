I begin as a fracture.

For an eternity—though time is a slippery concept here, more like a texture than a measure—I have been part of the westward ridge. I was a column of black basalt, still and silent, my porous ceramic skin indistinguishable from the crushed obsidian that surrounds me. I was asleep in the way mountains sleep: deep, unmoving, and heavy.

I wake not with a sudden intake of breath, for the air here is too thick to rush, but with a slow, deliberate expansion. A valve in my chest, a tight lattice of silica and cartilage, loosens. A thin plume of sulfurous steam hisses from the cracks in my shoulder plates. This is my first breath of the cycle, venting the heat that has been building within me during the long stasis.

I am standing in the center of a cairn, a low circle of stacked stones that my ancestors—or perhaps I, in a forgotten cycle—arranged eons ago. It offers no protection from the elements, for there are no weather elements here, only the constant, crushing dryness. It offers only definition. It marks the place where a standing thing becomes a moving thing.

I unlock my knees. The sensation is a grinding grind, a low-frequency rumble that travels up my femurs and resonates in my pelvic basin. It feels good. It feels real.

I extend my senses first. I have no eyes to open, but the fan-like arrays tucked into the hollows of my skull unfold, shivering in the warm updraft. The world rushes in not as light and color, but as texture and tone. I feel the infrasonic hum of the great quartz trees to the north, a vibrating B-flat that sits constantly at the bottom of my hearing. I feel the scritchy, chaotic static of the lithovore herds—thousands of tiny legs dragging over razor-sharp ground, a chaotic dry static. And I feel the rhythm of the thermal vents, a slow, throbbing pulse like a giant heartbeat miles away.

I am hungry. Not a sharp hunger, but a vast, empty hollow in my gut that demands filling.

I take a step. My foot is not a flesh heel but a wide, splayed talon, designed to distribute my weight over the broken glass of the world. I set it down. I feel the obsidian shard bite into the tougher sole of my foot, but I do not cut. I settle my weight, hearing the subtle *clack* as the stone shifts to accommodate me. I am heavy. I must be heavy to survive the pressure.

I am a Hunter of the Hollow. Or perhaps, simply, I am the Hollow made mobile.

Behind me, strapped to my spine by woven sinews taken from the wings of the slate-gliders, is the Hammer. It is made of the metal that fell from the sky—the iron that is not of this world. It is cold, a concept that does not exist elsewhere in this place, and it vibrates with a strange, discordant song. I do not touch it yet. To draw it is to begin the hunt, and the hunt requires precision.

I move out from the cairn. I do not walk; I flow. My gait is a series of controlled falls, locking joints at the last second to silence the impact. To the vibration-sense of the lithovores, I am just another drifting shadow of stone, a feature of the terrain.

I am moving toward the grazing grounds. The air grows thick with the vibration of their feeding. *Click. Scratch. Crunch.* They are eating the geodes, breaking the hard shells to get at the sulfur-silt inside. They are mindless things, clusters of carapace and legs, but they are the warmth that sustains us.

I find a suitable vantage point—a towering spire of basalt that juts out over the gorge. I climb it, my talons gripping the rock effortlessly. When I reach the apex, I do not stand atop it; I merge with it. I flatten my body against the stone, extending my limbs to mimic the jagged angles of the rock. I slow my internal steam-vents, sealing the cracks in my skin to retain heat. I become invisible.

I wait. This is the ninety-nine percent of life. The waiting. The being.

Below, a large lithovore, a patriarch of the shelled herd, separates from the group. It is moving toward a vein of exposed copper. I feel the heavy *thump-thump* of its legs through the stone. It is beneath me now.

I do not lunge. Lunging is for creatures of soft flesh and water. I simply... cease to be part of the mountain. I unlock the hinge in my knee and let gravity take me.

I fall like a shard of slate. There is no sound of wind, just the displacement of dust.

I land on the creature’s back. The impact is tremendous—a meteorite of bone and ceramic meeting shell. The lithovore screeches, a grinding sound of metal on metal, but it is too late. My talons, now fully splayed, pierce the chitin. I am anchored.

The Hammer is in my hand. I drew it in the microsecond between the landing and the screech. It sings its high, whining death-song. I bring it down.

*Crack.*

The sound is crisp, echoing through the cavern like a gunshot. The shell splits. The warmth—the life-energy of the creature—rushes out. I do not bleed it. I consume the heat. I absorb the sulfur-rich steam rising from the wound, pulling it into the vents in my chest. The fire stokes my furnace.

When the creature stops twitching, I am full. The ache in my marrow is gone, replaced by the heavy, satisfied hum of a well-tuned engine.

I stand amidst the broken shards of the prey. I am covered in dust and grey fluids. I am a monster of the deep dark, a thing of edges and silence. I look up—though I do not see—toward the ceiling where the ghost-light lichen clings. I feel the vastness of the Hollow. It is not empty. It is full of things that are trying to be as hard as I am.

I do not wonder about the sky. I do not wonder about the world above. The sky is a lie told by the pressure of stone. There is only the heat, the hum, and the hammer.

I strike the hammer against the ground once. *Clang.*

The sound lingers, a perfect vibration in the dry air. I record it in the cracks of my memory. Then, I fold my limbs, lock my joints, and begin the long, slow process of becoming a statue once more. I will stand here for a decade, perhaps two, until the hunger wakes me again.

I am the Hollow. I am the stone that wakes.
