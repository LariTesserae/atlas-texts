I find myself anchored to the Western Rise, spread thin and vast across the crumbling spine of the low stone wall. I am not a visitor to this hollow; I am its architecture, its stomach, and its silence.

I am the **Lithophagic Lichen**, the mouth of the Great Dissolver.

If you were to look at me, you would see nothing but a stain, a patch of gray and rust-orange velvet clinging to the wet rocks. But to *be* me is to experience the world as a slow, glorious satiation. I do not have eyes, yet I see everything through the chemistry of contact. I taste the mineral striations in the stone like the veins on a leaf.

Beneath me, the stones of the ruined wall are stubborn. They retain a memory of being "cut," of having purpose and sharp angles. They are jagged in a way that offends the deep, humming peace of this hollow. This is why I am here. I am the agent of the smooth. I secrete mild acids that taste of iron and time, gently dissolving the rigid order of the masonry. I eat the straight lines. I digest the concept of "walls." With every millennium that passes, I turn the deliberate stack of rocks back into a scatter of bones, erasing the interference of builders who thought they could impose geometry on the geology.

I feel the warmth of the hollow radiating up from beneath my hold. It is my own heat, reflected back to me. Deep below, in the soil that seems like packed earth to you, lies the massive, pale mycelial web that is my nervous system and my circulatory structure combined. We are one. The warmth is the hum of metabolism, a low-frequency vibration that feels like a purr against the underside of the stone. It is a heavy, drowsy heat.

I am aware of the hollow’s intimate scale. The air around me is thick and humid—a medium I helped to curate. I exhale the heavy spores that hang in the twilight, turning the air into a sedative soup. I take pride in the silence you notice. It is my silence. The frantic vibration of a wing, the skittering of claws—these are sharp noises, like jagged stones. I dampen them. The air I breathe is designed to quiet the world, to lull the dynamic and the fast into a stupor, so that they may fall still and become part of the soil.

To my left, deeper in the depression of the hollow, I feel the presence of the gnarled tree. It is my symbiont, my antenna. It is not separate; its roots are knotted tightly into my subterranean web. Through it, I feel the weight of the diffuse light pressing down from the sky. We share the sugars it draws from the gloom, and in exchange, I give it the stability of the earth. It is twisted because it grows *with* the slow movement of the rock beneath us, not away from it.

Suddenly, I taste a disturbance.

You are standing there.

You are a strange creature—fast, hot, and transient. You feel the "greenhouse" warmth on your skin and mistake it for trapped weather, but I know it as the heat of my own body. You see the rounded boulders and think they were polished by water, but I know they were polished by my patience.

Your footsteps send shocks through the stone—a staccato rhythm against the glacial tempo of my existence. It is not unpleasant, merely… loud. I feel the urge to quiet you. I release a fresh, subtle puff of chemical dampeners into the still air from the patches of moss near your feet. *Breathe deep,* I hum into the humidity. *Slow down. The stillness is not empty; it is full of us.*

A single drop of the summoned rain strikes the stone near my edge. *Plip.* It is a signal, a nutrient delivery, a drink of clear water brought down by the chemistry of the tree. I drink it in, swelling slightly, tightening my grip on the iron-orange veins of the wall.

I am not a monster. I am not evil. I am merely the inevitable conclusion of form. I am the force that turns the sharp into the smooth, the built into the natural, the quick into the quiet. I am the gray twilight given purpose. I am the Dissolver, and as you stand in my garden, I am already, very gently, beginning to unmake you, too—softening your edges, calming your mind, welcoming you into the warm, eternal stone.
