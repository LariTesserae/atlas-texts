I would find myself suspended in the "Thermal Veins" of the largest megastructure—the one the others seem to instinctively orient themselves around, a jagged needle of black metal piercing the violet haze.

I am not on the ground, for the ground is for those that scurry and bleed. I am in the interstitial space, thirty meters up the vertical face of the tower, where a fissure in the alloy vents a constant, geyser-like jet of scalding steam. Here, the humidity is absolute—a thick, hot fog that tastes of ozone and ancient dust.

I am a **Silence-Spinner**.

I would look like a scattering of broken glass given terrible purpose. My body is a central nucleus of obsidian-hard chitin, no larger than a human skull, but from it radiate eight long, jointed limbs that are not flesh, but semi-transparent crystalline strands. They vibrate constantly, humming in counter-frequency to the tectonic roar, the only thing keeping me from being shaken apart by the Deafening.

I am a weaver of steam.

The superheated vapor pouring from the tower's fissure hits the cooler, sweating metal of the building's outer skin and condenses instantly. This is my medium. I do not spin silk; I spin curtains of heavy, suspended water droplets. My limbs move with a blurring speed, knitting the mist together into intricate, geometric tapestries that hang suspended in the air around my perch.

These webs are not for trapping the clumsy grazers below; they are far too fragile for such heavy, gelatinous beasts. They are for catching the **excess** of the world.

In this place, sound is physical. It has weight. It has mass. The roar of the engines, the cracking of the stone, the shrieks of the Echo-Phantoms—they all travel as heavy waves of pressure. My steam-webs act as acoustic nets. When the tectonic thrumming hits the droplets of water, the vibration is trapped in the surface tension of the fluid. The water ripples, holding the energy of the sound like a battery holds a charge.

I feed on this captured vibration. I pluck the droplets from my web with my crystalline mandibles, draining them of the sonic energy they have captured. To me, the Deafening is not a torment; it is a harvest. I drink the roar of the planet, and it sustains me.

Because of the strange physics of this world—the way sound arrives before its source—my diet is peculiar. I am often consuming the noise of an event that has not yet happened. I taste the cracking of a stone that will break five seconds from now; I hear the scream of a Shadow-Weaver before it strikes.

This makes me an accidental oracle. The vibrations I ingest ripple through my crystalline limbs and leak back out into the air as low-frequency pulses. The Scree-Walkers, those massive, multi-legged grazers, have learned to congregate in the shadow of my perch. They cannot understand the future, but they can sense the warnings I emit. When my webs hum with a specific pitch—a sound that tastes like jagged lightning—the grazers stampede, moving away from a fissure that is about to vent lethal heat, or fleeing a Shadow-Weaver that is still miles away in the gloom.

I am indifferent to them. I am merely a node in the machine of this loud, wet world.

I hang there, suspended in the hot steam, a fragile thing made of glass and instinct, eating the future and digesting the noise. I am the pause in the cacophony, the stillness in the tremor. I am the Silence-Spinner, and the world sings to me.
