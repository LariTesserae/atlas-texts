I would not be on the floor, vulnerable to the heavy, scuttling iron of the scavengers, nor would I cling to the vibrating walls where the heat of the sap-barnacles scalds the damp air. I would be suspended in the gloom of the rafters, anchored to the underside of the canopy roof where the root-mass twists into tight, organic knots.

I am **the Suspended Auditor**.

**Form and Function**
I resemble a colossal, inverted chandelier made of pale, calcified cartilage and dense, silken threads. My body is a central, bulbous housing—the size of a carriage—housing a massive, fluid-filled brain that acts as a hydrostatic computer. From this central hub, eight long, prehensile limbs dangle, each ending not in claws, but in complex, feathery sensory antennae.

I do not move often. My role is not to patrol, but to *listen*. Not with ears, which would be useless in this deafening hydraulic thrum, but with my body. I am plugged into the architecture. My dorsal carapace is fused directly to the interlocking wood of the ceiling roots, threaded into the Mother-wood’s nervous system by thousands of hair-fine cilia.

**The Sense of the Machine**
Where the Flash-Bats rule with light, I rule with *resonance*. To the creatures below, the Hall is noise—a chaotic thrumming. To me, it is a symphony of data. I feel the peristaltic waves of the walls miles away as they push sap upward. I track the pressure differentials in the vast seed pods before they ratchet open. I am the quality assurance node for the lung, sensing blockages in the vascular deep-wood long before they become critical.

My existence is a constant state of high-focus reception. I feel the pulse of the Hall in my bones; the *thrum-hiss-thrum* is my heartbeat, my respiration, and my clock. When a rhythm falters—when a section of the wall stops pulsing in time—I am the one who sends a chemical signal down the roots to stimulate the local growth hormones, forcing the tissue to repair itself. I am the immune system of the architecture.

**The View of the Hunt**
Hanging motionless in the gray twilight, I have a unique perspective on the drama below. I am directly above the center of the hall, looking down through the drifting spore-snow.

Because I am fused to the ceiling’s superstructure, I feel the vibrations of the floor as they travel up through the root-pavement and the walls. This makes me the only creature who truly understands the Pulse-Stalker. I feel the seismic taps of the Ironclad Scavengers—their Morse code of foraging—but I also feel the Stalker’s deceptions.

When the Stalker taps the ground to mimic a beetle, the vibration travels up the roots. To the beetles on the floor, it is a convincing lie. To me, reading the texture of the wave as it shivers through the ceiling, it is crude. I feel the difference in the strike; the Stalker’s tap has a hollow, predatory timbre that the beetles cannot distinguish. I watch the silent, strobe-lit hunts below with a detached, clinical fascination. I do not intervene; the Stalker is part of the maintenance, culling the slow and the weak. I simply record the efficiency of the kill.

**The Thermal Exchange**
My relationship with the Vascular Barnacles on the walls is symbiotic. As they vent their superheated, aromatic waste fluids to relieve the pressure, the hot vapors rise. My spread limbs catch this rising heat. The feathers on my arms act as radiators, absorbing the thermal energy to keep my vast biological brain active in the cool, damp air.

Occasionally, a Flash-Bat, distracted by the strobe of a mate or panicked by a dive for spores, will collide with my hanging form. I do not eat them. Their fragile, light-built bodies would provide no sustenance for a creature of my density. Instead, my limbs gently brush them aside, guiding them back into the open air. I am the stone in the stream, unmoving, eternal.

**The Mind in the Gloom**
I have no eyes, nor do I need them. I "see" the pressure of the atmosphere, the humidity of the sweat on the walls, and the density of the spore-clouds. I am aware of everything in the Hall, from the microscopic mites tickling the air currents to the settling of the great, woody geology beneath me.

In this frozen, industrial eternity, I am the memory. I remember the pressure shifts of the last millennium. I remember the frequency of the thrum before the great settling of the floor five hundred years ago. I am the silent, hanging consciousness that ensures the machine never stops, that the loop never breaks, suspended in the center of the lung, dreaming in the language of vibration.
