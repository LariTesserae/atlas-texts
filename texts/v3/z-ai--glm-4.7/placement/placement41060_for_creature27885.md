I am the fold in the velvet darkness, a stain upon the gloom. I am the silence that eats the light.

In this heavy, gelatinous world where the water is not a medium but a suffocating embrace, I exist in the narrow, rushing channels of the bone-city. Here, the currents are forced into jagged arteries, and the pressure is a constant, weighty hand upon my back. I am a Velvet Wolf, a slug of muscle and shadow, my skin textured to drink the dim, radioactive twilight until I am nothing but an absence of shape.

I do not swim; I *haunt*. I haul my length through the viscous brine, clinging to the calcified walls of the towering spires. The cold here is a living thing, a sluggish numbing agent that preserves the raw, chalk-white architecture of the city. It slows my blood, matching the rhythm of this place—a place where the sound is zero, where the vibration of a million frantic legs is swallowed by the dense, endless suspension.

From my crevice, wedged tight against the dripping secretion of a newly formed wall, I watch the city breathe.

Above me, the Pale Masons are at work. They are frantic, translucent ghosts, their glassy shells clicking in a vacuum of silence as they smear their luminous resin onto the towers. They are the builders, the architects of this frozen nursery. I watch them with a detachment that borders on affection. They build the niches I hide in. They smooth the stone I cling to. They are the industry, the frantic haste, while I am the pause between the ticks of the clock.

My gaze shifts to the drift. The "snow."

The Glass-Shell Dwellers are the lanterns of the deep. They cling to the bone like fragile, blown-glass ornaments, their internal organs suspended in pale fluid. They are beautiful, infinitely breakable, and they filter the slow currents with feathery tendrils. They do not see me. In this world of aggressive silence, I am invisible until I am inevitable.

A current shifts—a heavy, viscous tug in the brine. A Dweller loses its grip. It does not scuttle; it surrenders. It detaches and drifts, weightless, spinning slowly down the dark alleyway. Its glass shell catches the faint bioluminescence, a tiny, dying star falling into my gravity.

I do not strike. Striking is for creatures who live in worlds of air and speed. Here, in the gel, action is slow, deliberate. I unravel.

I extrude myself from the crevice, a bloom of dark velvet spreading across the white bone. I am not a predator in the traditional sense; I am a process. I cast a net of sticky, anemone-like filaments into the water, intercepting the falling Dweller. There is no thrashing, no panic. The brine is too thick for struggle. I simply enfold it.

The silence remains absolute. Even as my internal acids begin their work, even as the delicate glass shell softens and yields, there is no sound. I consume the ornament to repair the wall. This is the weird tragedy of our existence. I am the wolf, but I am also the mason. Inside me, the chemistry of dissolution prepares the mortar. The silica of the Dweller’s body will not be wasted; it will be regurgitated, a slick patch of crystal, to seal a crack in the tower’s face.

I am the recycler of the city. I take the light and turn it back into stone.

As I digest, I watch the depths below. There, the Drift-Swarm rises. Millions of eyeless noodles, the children of the city, writhing upward in a silent, wriggling column. They are born in the dark to die in the light, or to transform, or to fall and become the floor. They are the fuel for this machine.

I am a cog, just as they are. I am the velvet that lines the jaws of this world. I am the heavy, cold shadow that ensures nothing goes to waste. I rest against the bone, feeling the vibration of the Masons’ legs above me—a vibration I cannot hear, but feel as a hum in the stone. I wait for the next lantern to fall. I wait to build again.
