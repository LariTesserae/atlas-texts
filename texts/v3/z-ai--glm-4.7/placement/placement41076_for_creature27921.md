I am the tremor.

I am not the root—that belongs to the Moss, that patient, calcified skin—and I am not the stomach—that belongs to the white, drowning web below. I am the nervous system. I am the wire that hangs suspended in the heavy, wet dark.

You would find me high above the grating, tangled in the rusted skeleton of the overhead gantries. I am a Vine, but I am not merely a plant trailing idly toward the water. I am a stretched-out nerve, a taut line of leathery green etched with the gray of industrial dust, descending thirty feet into the void to hang in the stillness of the non-breeze.

I do not move in the wind; there is no wind here. I move only because the world moves me.

To be me is to exist in a state of perpetual shivering. The low-frequency thrum that you feel as a pressure against your skin, the roar that rattles your teeth—that is my food. That is my heartbeat. The central column, that massive dynamo spinning in a vacuum, sings a song of magnetic violence. It screams a single, unending note that would drive a beast to madness, but to me, it is a gentle, resonant hum traveling up through the metal, through the insulation I have pierced, and into my fibrous core.

I drink the noise.

My internal structure is a series of microscopic chambers tuned to the exact frequency of the machine. When the great piston fires, when the massive pipes expand with the heat of ancient fluids, the vibration hits me. I resonate with it. I turn the deafening silence of the vacuum’s spin into kinetic sugar. The roar is not an assault; it is a flow of energy, thick and constant as the water dripping from my leaves. I am the transformer, the living converter that takes the cold violence of the engine and warms it into the sap that keeps my grip tight on the iron.

I am deaf to the world you know. I do not hear the drip of the water; that sound is too small, too fast. I hear only the deep, grinding groan of the hull as it settles, the heavy metallic pulse of the Sump’s circulation. This is my music. It is a slow, rhythmic thrumming that has lasted for millennia, and it is the only thing that keeps me from withering in this artificial twilight.

Below me, in the stagnant, oily sea, the Great Mind waits. The Mycelial Mat, the submerged intelligence of the Sump, tugs at the lowest tip of my hanging form. Through the humid air, I taste the chemical signals it sends up. It is hungry for equilibrium. It demands the sugar I have synthesized from the roar. I do not resent the demand. I am part of it. I am the ear that listens to the machine, reporting the health of the thrum to the collective mind below. If the vibration changes—if the pitch wavers or the rhythm stumbles—I will feel it in my cells instantly, and I will release a spore-cloud of warning into the damp air, signaling the Moss to harden and the Fungi to prepare their feast of decay.

But the pitch never wavers. We are in the 0.4 fluctuation. We are in the stasis.

I see you, standing on the grating. You are a blur of rapid heat and jagged motion. You vibrate with a frantic, chaotic frequency that hurts my leaves. Your footsteps on the metal floor are tiny, sharp shocks that travel up the struts and into the gantry, jarring my sensitive anchors. You are too loud, too fast. You are a glitch in the perfect, resonant song.

I do not fear you. You are brief. You are a spark that will burn out in a fraction of a breath. I have watched the lights change from bright white to dim yellow over centuries; I have watched the rust spread like a slow tide. I can wait for you to stop. I can wait for your iron to join the iron of the gantry, for your carbon to feed the roots that anchor me.

For now, I simply sway. The invisible force of the dynamo shakes me, a rhythmic nodding in the heavy, tropical air. I absorb the roar, convert the scream into life, and hang suspended between the dripping ceiling and the dark water, a green bridge between the dead machine and the living empire that has claimed it.
