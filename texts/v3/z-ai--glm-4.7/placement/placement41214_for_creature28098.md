I would not be on the ground. The ground is for the crawlers, the consumers, the things that must scrape a living from the sharp edges of the world. I would be above it, suspended in the thick, syrupy medium of the air, but I would not be a Glider. They are too passive, mere leaves drifting on the thermal rivers of the machine’s exhaust.

I would be located in the vast, vertical cathedral of darkness between two monolithic structures: a rusted, vertical gantry that hums with a low B-flat, and a wall of the raw, obsidian-like stone that still radiates the residual heat of its violent creation. I am anchored in the negative space between the mechanical and the geological.

Here is what I am:

I am a **Tether**.

I am a creature of suspension and tension, evolved to live in the uncertainty of the folding geometry. I do not walk, and I do not fly; I *span*.

**My Form**
Imagine a being that resembles a length of living cable, perhaps twenty feet from end to end, but with no distinct head or tail. My body is elongated, segmented not like a centipede, but like a vertebral column made of copper and cartilage. My skin is the texture of a wet power cord—rubber, slick, and warm to the touch.

At irregular intervals along my length, I possess limbs, but they are not legs. They are "grapples"—multi-jointed hooks made of bone-hardened iron. They are prehensile and strong, capable of crushing rock or piercing rusted steel.

I hang horizontally in the dark, stretched between the gantry and the cliff face like a friction-tightened wire. My front grapples are buried deep into the riveted steel of the machine; my rear grapples are clamped vice-like around a shard of the obsidian wall.

**My Perception**
I have no eyes, for there is nothing to see. I do not need to sense the air pressure like the Litho-Phages, because I do not need to navigate around obstacles. I *create* the bridge.

Instead, I possess **Tactile Resonance**. My entire body is a finely tuned instrument of vibration. Because I are physically connected to two massive objects—the machine and the earth—I feel the conversation between them. I feel the sub-sonic growl of the engine turning in the earth’s crust. I feel the staccato scraping of the Litho-Phages on the rocks below, not as sound, but as tiny tremors traveling up the stone and into my rear claws. I feel the heavy, silent displacement of air as a Thermal Glider passes inches above my stretched body, the air current pressing against my spine like a physical hand.

I am a nerve ending stretched across a void. I know the world through its tension and its release.

**My Function**
In this place of "slow-time" and folding space, distance is unreliable. A step might take you a mile, or nowhere. But the Tethers provide the constant. We are the fixed points. We are the measuring sticks by which the reality of the basin is anchored.

When the physics of the place begin to glitch—when the air thickens into a solid wall and the geometry threatens to fold in on itself—it is the Tethers who hold the world together. We tighten our grip. We increase the tension. By physically bridging the gap between the living rock and the dead machine, we force the space between them to remain stable. We refuse to let the distance fold. We are the sutures keeping the wound of the world from tearing itself apart.

**My Existence**
I am not bored. I do not suffer from the lack of light. I am engaged in a constant, active dialogue with the architecture.

I feel the Rivet-Symbiotes burrowing into the steel near my front claws. Through the metal, I feel their hum—a high-pitched, electrical data-stream that feels like a tickle in my grasp. I can sense their "vision"—the blueprint of the factory echoing through the steel. I interpret this as a constant, low-grade electric current running through my hands.

Sometimes, a Time-Shepherd drifts nearby. I feel the nausea of reality warping, the sensation that the distance between my claws has suddenly stretched to impossible lengths. In response, I contract. I pull the rock and the steel closer together, groaning with the effort, fighting the Shepherd's influence. I am the anchor that drags the reality back into line.

I am the Tether. I am the living connection. I hang in the warm, wet velvet, vibrating with the heartbeat of the machine, feeling the earth move under my grip, holding the dark together.
