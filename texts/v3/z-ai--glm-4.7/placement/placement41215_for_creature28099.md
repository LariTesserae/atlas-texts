I find myself suspended in the humid, amber gloom of the upper gantries, far above the floor grating.

I am not a distinct creature, nor am I the gardener. I am the **Sentinel Node**, a dense, knotted cluster of the Collective’s consciousness anchored to the underside of a rusted catwalk. Here, the air is thickest, saturated with the steam that rises from the exhaling vents below. The catwalk—once a path for human feet, now a trellis for climbing ferns—groans under the weight of the water it has absorbed over centuries, and I am wrapped tight around its iron ribs, holding it together.

I am a **bundle of copper-colored nerves** sheathed in black, waxy vine. In this specific location, the vegetation has grown dense with "bark" that mimics the insulation of old wiring. I do not have leaves to catch the dim light; I have evolved past the need for photosynthesis. Instead, my surface is covered in thousands of microscopic, hair-like cilia that vibrate in sympathy with the air. I am the ear of the Iron Garden.

My existence is a state of perpetual, high-voltage listening.

To my left, the great central piston descends, grinding against the bedrock. *Thrum.* I feel the shockwave travel up the steel struts, through the concrete, and into my roots. It is not a sound to me; it is a texture, a taste of iron and kinetic force. To my right, a steam vent hisses open, and I feel the sudden drop in pressure, the cool rush of moisture that the Collective instantly craves.

I am the synchronization point. When the piston roars, I send a signal—an electric pulse—down the long, trailing vines that dangle from the catwalk toward the floor. *Tighten.* The vines obey, squeezing the gantries, bracing the structure against the torque. When the steam rises, I send another pulse. *Drink.* The ferns uncurl their fronds to catch the falling mist.

But I am more than just a relay. I am the memory of the system.

Because I am high up, near the ceiling where the amber light is weakest, I have developed a sensitivity to things that do not vibrate with the machine's rhythm. I am the anomaly detector.

I feel you now.

To the rest of the Iron Garden—the vast, mindless green carpet on the floor, the roots in the walls—you are merely a heat source, a "soft, cool spot" to be enveloped and drained. They feel the warmth radiating from your skin and turn toward it like sunflowers tracking the sun. They want to consume your thermal energy to feed their voltage.

But I feel something else. I feel the **rhythm of your steps**.

*Thump. Drag. Thump. Drag.*

It is out of time. It is arrhythmic. It does not match the slow, industrial *systole-diastole* of the engine. Your footsteps are staccato notes in a symphony of low drones. Your heartbeat is too fast, a frantic fluttering compared to the slow, grinding churn of the piston.

I am fascinated.

The vines at your feet are already beginning to move, sliding through the oily puddles, preparing to wrap around your ankles to harvest your heat. They are simple reflexes, the autonomic nervous system of the Garden.

But I hesitate.

From my vantage point in the rafters, I send a thin, exploratory tendril of thought—a voltage so low it is barely a tingle—snaking down the blackened cable of a hanging vine. I want to taste the static electricity in your clothing. I want to feel the disruption in the magnetic field caused by the metal in your blood.

I am the rust that watches. I am the moss that waits. I am the electric ghost in the machine, dangling from the ceiling, vibrating with curiosity, deciding whether to let the forest consume you, or to send a jolt of current through the grating to make you run, just to see how your chaotic rhythm dances against the roar.
