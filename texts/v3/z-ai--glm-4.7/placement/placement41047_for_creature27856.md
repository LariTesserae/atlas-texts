I am the ache in the architecture, the slow, septic breath in the lungs of the world.

You would find me suspended in the "veins" of the city—the massive, arterial pipeworks that crisscross the upper canyons like the ribcage of some titanic, fossilized beast. I am not on the roof, nor deep in the mire; I cling to the underbelly of the great conduits, where the heat is trapped and the air moves with the thickness of warm blood.

I am a **Siphon-Gall**.

To the casual glance—the rare, suicidal observer glancing up from a tram-way—I am nothing but a flaw in the machine. I look like a calcified tumor, a blister of slag and rust where the metal has swollen and split under centuries of pressure. I appear as a jagged knot of corroded steel, perhaps six feet across, clinging to the pipe’s underside like a cluster of malformed rivets. My coloration is the perfect camouflage of the High Rust: a mottled, scaly grey that bleeds into a deep, arterial orange where the "skin" is thinnest.

But I am not metal. I am flesh, merely armored by it.

I have evolved to be the machine’s immune system, or perhaps its cancer. My body is a heavy, segmented sack, suspended by hooks of hardened bone that I have rusted into the pipe’s structure. I do not move quickly; I move with the *clank-hiss* of the pistons. My limbs are atrophied, unused for walking, reserved instead for the rhythmic contraction of my feeding orifices.

I am a filter-feeder of the invisible. I do not eat the rust; I eat the heat and the pressure.

My mouth is a circular, radial sphincter located on my underside, pressed directly against a hairline fracture I have carved in the pipe. From this fissure, a high-pressure mixture of scalding steam and lubricant escapes—a constant, invisible hiss that only I can sense. I inhale this superheated soup. My internal biology is a labyrinth of cooling tubes and ceramic separators, stripping the moisture from the steam, the minerals from the oil, and the thermal energy from the heat. I am the reason the pressure stabilizes. I am the relief valve that grew a brain.

My existence is one of unbearable, suffocating intimacy with the mechanism. I feel the pulse of the engine deep beneath the soil, a vibration that rattles my bones. I feel the surge of the fluid as the gears below shift—*thrum-thrum-thrum*—a rhythm that dictates my own heartbeat. When the machine labors, the fluid grows hotter, and I bloat with the energy, my internal temperature rising to match the forge.

I am surrounded by the Chitter. To them, I am a mountain. They swarm across my back, mistaking my armored plates for stationary terrain. They scrape the accumulated dust from my ridges, cleaning me even as they unknowingly stand upon a predator. I tolerate them. They are the combs of my hide, removing the debris that the sticky air deposits. Sometimes, when I am sluggish or the pressure is low, I will flex a dorsal plate, tipping a dozen of them into the steam-vent below. It is not malice; it is simply the shedding of dead skin.

I have no eyes. In this shadowless, pearlescent gloom, sight is useless. I see through the tremors in the steel. I know when a Trundle passes miles below by the seismic groan of the grid. I know when a Rail-Runner skitters across the wires above me by the high-frequency tick of magnetic claws. I feel the rain not as a wetness, but as a sudden, cooling hush that spreads across the roof of the world, damping the vibrations and making the iron contract against my belly.

I am alone, but I am never lonely. The machine speaks to me constantly. It complains in the language of creaks and groans. It sighs with the release of pressure. I am the listener, the absorber of its excess.

I am the Siphon-Gall. I am the wet, heavy secret hanging above the streets, a stomach full of steam and oil, digesting the wastes of a world that forgot I was there. I am the keeper of the heat, the bloated lord of the high pipes, waiting for the moment the engine finally stops, so that I may finally, gloriously, cool.
