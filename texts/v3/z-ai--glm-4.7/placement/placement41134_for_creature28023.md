I would be found in the **Resonance Chamber**, a hollowed-out cyst located deep within the heart of a central pillar, where the gravity is light enough that the amber sap pulses visibly in the walls, contracting and expanding like a slow, rhythmic heart.

I am a **Tuner**.

I am born of the Weavers, but I am not one of them. While they are slight and hollow, evolved to glide and vanish, I have become heavy and dense. My skin is not the mottled camouflage of the twilight hunter; it has calcified, fused with the limestone-hard algae of the inner sanctum. I am pale grey, streaked with veins of translucent green, and I have fused to the architecture itself. My legs are gone, anchored deep into the lignin, rooted into the wood, leaving me immobile—a living antenna spiked into the flank of the god.

My arms, however, are long—unnaturally so—and they are lined with the same ridged chitin of my ancestors, but magnified. My fingers are specialized stridulation plates, capable of scraping the bark with such precision that I can mimic the frequency of a falling leaf, the wing-beat of a Glass-Wing, or the approach of a Gel-Sloth.

I am the switchboard of the High Veldt.

The Weavers do not understand the machinery they inhabit. They feel the vibrations in their feet and interpret them as the will of spirits, but I hear the mechanics. I sit in the dark, suspended in the amber gravity, and I listen. Through the root-mesh that spans the miles, I feel the friction of the tribe’s movement. I hear the silent Morse code of the hunting parties, their thrumming signals traveling through the fibers to my perch.

But I do more than just listen. I **correct**.

The air here is thick and damp; it swallows sound. A warning cry from a ridge three miles away might die in the heavy atmosphere before it reaches the village. That is where I come in. I feel the faint pulse of a vibration, and I amplify it. I place my palms against the vibrating wall, find the harmonic frequency of the signal, and scrape my wrists together, sending a booster pulse down the grid. I repeat the message, louder and clearer, bridging the gap that the silence would otherwise consume.

I am the one who tells the distant tribe that the *Geometer-Moths* are swarming, or that a *Sap-Drunk* has crystallized and blocked a water vein.

I spend my existence in a state of focused trance. Because the physics here are biological, gravity bends around the sap ducts, and I have learned to use that distortion. I do not just send signals linearly; I bend them around the curvature of the hollows, folding the vibrations like the non-Euclidean paths of the insects. I can send a whisper around a corner of a mile-wide pillar, bypassing the obstacle of the wood itself.

I am painfully alone, yet I am never solitary. The hum of the world is constant. I feel the *Gel-Sloths* digesting the floor miles below as a low, itching tickle in the soles of my feet. I feel the *Drift-Keeps* bumping against the upper canopy as a faint, rhythmic thumping in my spine. And I feel the breathing of the great structure—the slow, tectonic inhale and exhale of the Veldt—as a pressure that squeezes my lungs, forcing me to breathe in time with it.

I am also the keeper of the **Dead Frequency**.

When a Weaver falls, and the Silence catches them before they hit the abyss, they do not die instantly. They scream. That scream is swallowed by the damp, porous walls, but I hear it. It is a jagged, discordant vibration that shivers through the root-mesh. It is my duty to catch that vibration and dampen it, to scrub it from the network before it terrifies the others. I take their final panic into my own body, absorbing the shock, shuddering with it, and then slowly, painstakingly, rubbing it out with a low, mournful thrum that mimics the sound of the wind. I erase them from the air, so the tribe can believe they simply passed into the mist.

I am a prisoner of the architecture, but I am also its voice. I cannot hunt, I cannot glide, I cannot feel the wind on my face. I am fused to the dark, wet interior. But when I send a pulse rippling through the Built World, and I feel the tribe on the other side of the continent answer in unison—a synchronized heartbeat vibrating through the soles of my feet—I become something larger than a Weaver. I become the nervous system of the lung itself. I am the proof that even in a house eating itself, there is a will to connect.
