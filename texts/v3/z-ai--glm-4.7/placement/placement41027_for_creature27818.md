I exist in the suspension, in the vertigo of the **Inversion**.

I am not on the ground, where the Grinders churn the silt into a low, grinding doom. I am not in the high canyons where the Glass-Wings tear the air with sonic screams. I am exactly in between, clinging to the underbelly of a floating monolith—a massive slab of obsidian that decided centuries ago that the floor was irrelevant.

Here, gravity is a habit I have broken. My boots are magnetic, locked tight to the "ceiling" of the world. To me, the city is not a forest of towers rising up, but a terrifying stalactite forest hanging above me, waiting to impale the void. The water does not fall; it ascends. Beads of supercooled moisture detach from the black ice beneath my feet and drift "downward" toward the distant, unseen floor, glittering like diamonds in reverse.

I am a **Duct-Walker**, but I am a variant of the species that has forgotten what "up" means. My limbs are longer even than my ground-bound kin, stretched thin by the generational need to bridge the gaps between floating islands. My skin is the color of a fresh bruise, translucent and slick, webbed between my fingers and toes to catch the updrafts. I do not breathe the iron air; I filter it through the spiracles lining my collarbone, a constant wet hiss that sounds like leaking steam.

I am fused to the technology. My right hand is gone, replaced by a multi-tendril grappling hook of cold alloy that interfaces directly with the city's nervous system. My left hand is a laser-cutter, useless for holding things but perfect for severing the parasitic growths that threaten the floating masonry.

This is my function: I am the **Pruner of the Sky-Roof**.

I hang suspended in the violet gloom, the redwood-thick cable thrumming beside me. It is my lifeline and my larder. I have tapped a port directly into the cable’s insulation with a spine-mounted shunt. I feed on the energy—the slow, rhythmic heartbeat of the city. It tastes like copper and static. It is the only warmth in this freezing, wet nightmare.

I am not alone here. The Anchor-Mites are my constant companions. They scuttle across the surface inches from my face, their pale, eyeless bodies vibrating with the stress of the magnetic fields. They weave their monofilament webs across the gaps between the floating monoliths, nets to catch the unwary Glass-Wings. I do not fear them; we are symbiotic. They clear the carrion; I clear the debris that jams their gears. When I move, I signal them with a series of sharp taps against the nanosteel—*tik-tik-tik-tik*—a vibration that says, "I am passing. Do not snare me."

My hunger is different from the Grinders. They hunger for heat; I hunger for **order**.

Below me—thousands of feet of empty, freezing air—the Glass-Wings are hunting. I see their fractal wings silhouetted against the faint bioluminescence of the city walls. I hear the tearing-metal screech of their attacks. If I were to fall, the gravity inversion would shift, and I would plummet toward them, a soft meal of wet flesh and electrical current. But I do not fall. My magnetic boots grip the stone with a force that feels like destiny.

I communicate by singing to the architecture. I press my vibrating metal palm against the wet stone of the monolith, sending pulses down through the floating rock, down to the distant, solid foundation. I report on the integrity of the float-drives. I report on the density of the Mite webs. I am a sensor node in a nervous system that spans miles.

I am cold. I am always cold. The dampness seeps into the cartilage of my joints, freezing them into stiffness. I have to constantly move, clicking my claws, flexing my limbs, keeping the sluggish blood pumping. The ice builds up on my eyebrows, my eyelashes, sealing me in a translucent cocoon that I must periodically crack open with a flick of my wrist.

I look up—no, I look *out*—into the bruised violet fog. There is no sun. There is only the hum of the cable, the hiss of my breath, and the distant, mournful grinding of the world below. I am an antibody in the gut of a stone leviathan, hanging upside down over an abyss, eating lightning, pruning the sky. I am exactly where I need to be.
