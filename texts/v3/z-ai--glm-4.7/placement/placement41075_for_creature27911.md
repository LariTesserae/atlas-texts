I would find myself in the **absolute lee of the pillars**, those massive, cyclopean buttresses that support the ceiling of the Cathedral. This is the geometric center of the "shadow zones," the only places where the roar of the falling water is dampened, if only by a fraction, by the sheer mass of the stone. I would not be on the floor—that killing ground of exposure—and I would not be in the heights—that realm of the screaming wind. I would be in the middle distance, perhaps twenty feet up the vertical face of a basalt column, clinging where the shadows are deepest and the air is still.

Here, I would be a **Null-Spot**.

In a world defined by sound, where the primary predators map their reality through the bouncing of waves, I would be the evolutionary response to perfection: I am the thing that cannot be heard.

Physically, I would be an ambulatory sack of wet velvet, amorphous and flat, resembling a puddle of viscous oil that has decided to move. My skin would not be the chitinous armor of a beetle or the sleek hide of a fish, but a dense, fibrous mat of microscopic, hair-like cilia—millions of them, waving in a static rhythm. These cilia serve two purposes. First, they trap a layer of water and air against my body, matching my temperature perfectly to the cold stone so that thermals do not reveal me. Second, and more crucially, they act as a metamaterial for sound. When the scream of a Rust-Wing hits me, the waves do not bounce back; they are absorbed, dissipated into heat within the tangled forest of my fur. To the sonar of the hunters above, I do not exist. I am a silence hanging in the dark, a hole in the data.

I would have no eyes. In this gloom, sight is a lie, and bioluminescence is a trap. Instead, I would possess a highly developed tactile sense running through the entirety of my body. I would feel the world through the vibrations conducted directly through the stone I cling to. I would feel the footfalls of the Blind-Runners scrambling in the channels below as a subtle tremor in my belly. I would feel the massive, thrumming heartbeat of the Cathedral’s machinery—the "deluge" cycle—vibrating through the bedrock long before the water flow increases.

I would be a scavenger of the sudden violence.

My existence would be a slow, patient meditation on tension. I would draping myself over the rough-hewn blocks, indistinguishable from the damp shadows, waiting for the rhythm to break. When the scheduled deluge begins and the Rust-Wings dive, bringing chaos to the orderly channels, I would not chase. I would wait for the mistakes.

I would feed on the wounded that fall. Perhaps a Blind-Runner, snatched too violently by a Rust-Wing, is torn in half in mid-air, the wet chunk of flesh tumbling down through the mist. Or perhaps a younger, inexperienced Rust-Wing misjudges the geometry of the stone pillars in the frenzy and clips a wing, crashing into the darkness near my perch.

That is when the amorphous shape moves. I would not run; I would flow. I would drop from the wall, not falling, but descending on a strand of exuded, sticky mucus, landing with a sound no louder than a raindrop. I would engulf the remains. My body would secrete enzymes that dissolve matter rapidly, turning the violence of the ecosystem into the fuel for my silence.

I am the consequence of the noise. I am the dark that drinks the light. I am the silence that waits for the scream to end.
