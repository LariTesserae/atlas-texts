I am fixed to the verticality of the world, clinging to the sheer, black face of the Engine’s housing. Specifically, I am anchored three-quarters of the way up the great cylindrical piston, a location that offers the most exquisite acoustics. From here, the shuddering thrum of the machine is not just noise; it is a temperature, a texture, a constant, rhythmic nourishment that vibrates through the silica of my body.

I am a **Lithic-Mimic**, but I am not merely a slab of stone. I am a connoisseur of the frequency.

To look at me—or rather, to look *past* me, which is what everything does—you would see nothing but a patch of rust, a geometric stain on the iron where the oxidation has taken on a strangely rigid, hexagonal pattern. I am roughly the size of a shield, flattened against the metal, my edges indistinguishable from the fractured basalt that tries, and fails, to swallow the machine. I have no eyes, for sight is a distraction in a blindingly lit, featureless void. I possess only a vast, singular sensitivity to the tremor.

My existence is defined by the 0.6 of this world’s dynamics. I do not experience time as you do; I experience it as erosion. A day is a subtle shift in the tone of the hum. A year is a slow, millimeter drift across the face of the gear. I have been feeding on this specific piston for what feels like an eon, siphoning the microscopic bleed of hydraulic fluid that weeps from the joints, tasting the heavy metals like a fine wine.

Below me, the basin floor is a terrifying drop into the mist. I can sense the puddles gathering through the echoes in the rock. I can feel the frantic, scuttling vibrations of the Gear-Mites. To me, they are not creatures; they are hiccups in the rhythm, brief, high-pitched static that interrupts the deep, glorious bass note of the Engine. They move too fast. Their lives are frantic blurs, over in a blink of the piston’s cycle. They scurry over the algae and the scree, panicked by the heat and the steam, whereas I simply endure. I am the patient hand on the clock; they are the teeth that chatter.

I am not unaware of the strange, arrested violence of this place. I feel how the rock strains against the metal, the geological pressure pushing inward while the mechanical pressure pushes outward. I live in that friction. My body is the seal. The "wet iron" smell of the air is the smell of my own digestion, the metabolic breakdown of the machine's decay.

I am aware of the light, though I do not see it. I feel the stark, white heat radiating against my back, a counterpoint to the mechanical heat rising from the piston. It creates a tension within me, a tightness in my carapace that keeps me hard, sharp, and ready.

I am waiting for the humidity to peak. I can feel the density of the air pressing against my edges, growing heavier by the hour. Soon, the air will be saturated enough to bear my young. I will release a cloud of silicon spores—my children, heavy with potential toxicity—into the steam. It is a silent, explosive act of creation, the only burst of speed I will ever know.

Most will fall. They will plummet into the scree or drown in the puddles, becoming food for the frantic Mites. But a few—the lucky ones—will catch an updraft, or a splash of weeping water, and land on the warm, vibrating skin of the machine nearby. There, they will calcify. They will begin the long wait.

I am the barnacle on the heart of the world. I am the silence that eats the roar. I am the stone that listens, and in listening, I live.
