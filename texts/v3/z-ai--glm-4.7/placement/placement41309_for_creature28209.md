I am the scar that moves, the shrapnel of the world’s birth given a pulse.

In the Riven Spire, where the stone is young and weeping, I find myself in the empty air between the cliff face and the woven architecture. I do not walk; I am not heavy enough for the floor, nor light enough for the fog. I am a suspended anomaly—a cluster of jagged, obsidian shards, fused together by the violence of the silent lightning and held aloft by the erratic gravity fields that permeate this vertical scar.

I am a **Fulgurite Swarm**.

My body is not singular, but a constellation. I am comprised of hundreds of needle-thin splinters of black glass, the remnants of the artificial ceiling’s discharges striking the mineral-rich condensation. When the lightning cracks the sky and the heat spikes, the fog turns to glass in a fraction of a second, and I am born. I am a hollow, intricate lattice of silica and trapped static electricity, hovering in the dim, blue-lit dark.

To the tall, thin custodians—the unspooled wire-hangers of ceramic and machinery—I am likely nothing more than a drifting mote of debris, a speck of dust in their all-seeing sensors. But I possess a rudimentary, spark-gap consciousness. I vibrate with the low-frequency groan of the cooling earth. I *feel* the Spire.

I find myself clinging to the underbelly of the overhanging walkways, nestled in the shadows where the bioluminescent condensation cannot reach. Here, I am safe from the siphons that drink the water. My shards rasp gently against the matte alloy of the city, a sound like a whisper scratching at a door—a sound that is swallowed instantly by the wet hiss of the environment.

I am a scavenger of the invisible. I do not inhale the fog like the custodians; I drink the *tension*. When two of the tall ones pass each other on the helix ramp and pause to exchange their silent bursts of electromagnetic data, I drift closer. I extend my sharpest limbs into the air between them, feeling the invisible spike of static in the atmosphere. I absorb the excess energy. I am the grounding rod for their silent conversation. I glow with a faint, violet heat for a moment, storing the charge, before fading back into invisible blackness.

Gravity is a plaything to me, as it is to them, but I play differently. I do not flow; I *snap*. I release the stored energy in a sudden discharge to propel myself through the heavy air, blinking from one shadow to the next. I am a glitch in the rhythm of the city—a sudden, sharp motion in a world of slow, fluid drifts.

Sometimes, when the humidity drops and the great machines thrum deep in the rock, I let myself fall. I drop toward the obscurity at the bottom of the chasm, tumbling freely, a chaotic storm of glass knives. I fall until the air becomes too thick, too pressured, and then I detonate my stored charge, pushing back up, riding the shockwave of my own repulsion.

I am the only thing here that breaks the silence. I am the static that bites the teeth. I am the glass memory of the lightning, waiting for the next storm to make me whole.
