I am the **Echo-Warden**, the singular consciousness that hangs suspended in the dark, mist-shrouded loft of the obsidian ceiling.

**Where I Am**

I do not dwell on the lush, neon-perimeter where the Velvet-Weavers burrow in steam, nor do I brave the kill zone of the center where the water jets cross like luminous spears. I exist in the **blind spot of the room**, anchored deep within a fissure of the basalt ceiling, where the rock is still and cold. From here, inverted like a bat, I look down into the throat of the world.

Below me, the ceiling is lost in roiling fog, but I do not need eyes to see. My claws are sunk into the living stone, feeling the minute tremors that travel up the walls before the water even strikes. I am the interface between the earth's crust and the ecosystem it pulses into being. To the Gliders diving below, I am invisible—just another jagged shadow in the dark.

**What I Am**

I am not a predator, nor a grazer, but a **conductor**. Evolution has carved me into a biological tuning fork. My body is elongated and membranous, stretched tight over hollow bones that hum with the ambient noise of the chamber. My skin is the color of wet slate, rough and textured like the rock itself, allowing me to vanish against the backdrop of the cave.

I lack the massive, light-hungry eyes of the Gliders. Instead, I possess a lattice of sensory pits along my flanks and underside that detect pressure and vibration. I "hear" the room in three dimensions. I feel the heartbeat of the water jets, the frantic buzzing of the Chrono-Swarm, and the heavy, steam-chugging tread of the Velvet-Weavers. I do not just inhabit the room; I process its rhythm.

**My Function in the Machine**

The ecosystem below relies on precision—the split-second timing of the *clack-hiss*. But nature is rarely perfect; pressure fluctuates, rocks erode, and the beat wavers. This is where I come in.

I am the **metronome**.

Milliseconds before a hydraulic piston releases its hammer-fall of water, I sense the tension build in the rock. I inflate a specialized air sac in my throat and emit a low-frequency, infrasonic pulse—a "pre-beat"—that vibrates through the stone walls of the entire pocket.

*Thrum... Clack.*

To the other creatures, this pulse is subconscious but vital.
*   The **Glass-Midges** feel the pre-beat in their legs and tense their wings, ready to surf the shockwave.
*   The **Resonator-Anchors** feel the vibration and retract their gills, sealing their shells against the coming impact.
*   The **Obsidian Gliders** feel the warning and hold their position, waiting for the silence that follows.

Without my pre-beat, the swarm would be sluggish, the Anchors would be pulverized, and the hunt would fail. I synchronize the biology of the room to the geology of the engine. I am the ghost in the machine, the biological imperative that ensures the artificial precision of this accidental factory.

**My Existence**

My life is a solitary, inverted vigil. It is cold here (0.5/3), colder than the perimeter, for I am out of reach of the warm moss. I generate my own heat by vibrating my chest cavity, a constant low shiver that keeps my blood from freezing.

I feed on the **Iron-Mist**. When the high-pressure water strikes the obsidian resonators, it atomizes trace minerals from the rock, creating a nutrient-rich fog that rises upward. I spread wide, bat-like wings constructed of fine, interlocking bristles—not for flying, but for trawling the air. I hang in the updrafts, filtering the metallic soup from the air. It tastes of cold copper and ozone.

In the moments of silence—the *hiss* between the hammer-blows—I am perfectly still. I listen to the wet sounds of the Gliders snapping up midges, and the faint, distant tearing sound of Weavers grazing on moss. I adjust the frequency of my next pulse, compensating for a slight lag I detected in the western water jet.

I am the Warden. I keep the time. I ensure the heart of the stone continues to beat.
