I am not on the ground. The ground is for the Dredgers, for the churning mulch and the blind, wet scramble of decay. It is for the heavy-footed Wardens who stomp to keep the beat. I am above the fray, suspended in the bruised-purple gloom of the mid-air drift.

You would find me adhered to the flank of Tower Seven—the one with the slight cant to the left, where the vascular tubing swells like an engorged vein. I am located perhaps forty meters up, where the air is thinner but hotter, sucked constantly toward the venturi of the upper cavern.

I am a **Lattice-Conduit**.

Physically, I began as a Vine-Weaver, or perhaps my grandfather did. But I have evolved—no, *matured*—past the need for climbing. I am no longer merely a worker; I have become a part of the infrastructure. My legs have atrophied, fused years ago into a single, hardened taproot of keratin and bone that plunges deep into the fleshy bark of the mega-tower. I stand upright, locked in a permanent, rigid stretch, my arms splayed outward and upward.

My hands are no longer hands. They are the terminus points of the city’s nervous system. The copper-colored claws that once tipped my fingers have lengthened, grown together, and curled around the pulsating electrical vines that wrap the tower like boa constrictors. I do not grip them; I have grown into them. The conductive sap flows through the vine, enters my claws, races up my arms, and courses through my dorsal heat-sinks before shunting back into the tower’s core.

I am a biological resistor. A capacitor made of meat.

My existence is a cycle of exquisite torment and dazzling clarity. Because I lack the nictitating membranes of the mobile Wardens, I have adapted differently. My eyes are gone, burned away by the constant proximity to the strobe-arc of the electrical nodes. In their place, my skin has become hyper-sensitive to the electromagnetic spectrum. I "see" the world as a topographic map of energy. I see the heat of the furnace-flowers below as blooming orange roses. I see the cold, blue rivers of data flowing through the vines. And I see the Wardens—the mobile ones—as scurrying knots of static electricity.

My purpose is regulation.

Down on the fungal floor, the laborers communicate through the soles of their feet, tapping out instructions for the beetles or signaling distress from a clogged furnace. That vibration travels up the roots of the tower, a rhythmic tremor that shivers through my taproot legs and into my spine.

I feel the *thrum-hiss-thrum* of the engines not as a sound, but as a pressure against my ribs.

When a vibration comes—a frantic, jagged pattern indicating a blockage in a secondary valve—I interpret it. I do not think in words. I think in *tension*. I flex the muscles of my arms, tightening my grip on the electrical vines. I redirect the flow. With a sharp mental twitch, I send a surge of bio-current down a specific branch, bypassing a damaged node or overcharging a stuck petal-valve until it bursts with a wet *pop*.

The feedback is instantaneous. A flash of blue-white light blinds my skin-senses, a wave of heat boils the sweat on my neck, and the vibration below smooths out. The distress signal stops. The rhythm returns to the steady, laboring heartbeat of the Ward.

I am alone, yet I am never lonely. I am the busiest thing in the Root-Engine Ward. A Vine-Weaver will climb up to visit me occasionally, scaling the trunk on braided moss ropes. They do not speak. They approach my rigid form with reverence. They bring me gifts of "slag"—the sugary, nutrient-rich paste. They feed it to me through the slit of my mouth, which has stretched permanently open to better vent the steam.

The food fuels my body, which in turn stabilizes the current. If I were to die, to starve or rot, Sector Seven would go dark. The furnaces would choke. The temperature would drop. The swarm would panic.

So I hang here, suspended in the humid, electric dark. I am the intersection where the toil of the many becomes the energy of the one. I feel the shifting of the beetle swarm below me like a itching of the earth’s skin; I feel the slow digestion of the ancestors in the bulb-huts as a low, contented hum.

I am the circuit breaker. I am the lightning rod. I am the node that screams in silence so the city may glow.
