I am a single node in the pavement, a jagged plate of interlocking armor fused into the living floor of this geode.

I have no name, only a frequency. To you, I would be invisible, indistinguishable from the black shale or the obsidian debris that litters the fissure. I am matte black, ridged with vertical spires of chitin that lock perfectly into the valleys of my neighbor’s shell. I do not know where I end and the next creature begins. We are a solid-state economy of flesh and stone, a singular, writhing mosaic that carpets the floor of this sweating hell.

I am blind.

There is no light to see here, only the punishing, flat glare that penetrates the steam, reflecting blindingly off the wet schist. Eyes would be useless organs, burning in the acidic air. Instead, I possess a crown of antennae, delicate threads that wave in the heavy, viscous soup of our atmosphere. I taste the world. I taste the sulfur-rich fog rolling off the walls. I taste the metallic tang of the copper-based blood pumping softly beneath the shell of the creature pressed against my flank. I taste the heat.

The heat is a constant pressure, a weight heavier than the rock above us. It presses against my carapace, sweating out the impurities, forcing me to constantly secrete the slick, oily mucus that allows me to glide without sound. Silence is our religion. To click, to scrape, to clatter—is to invite death, or worse, disruption. We move in a hydraulic, fluid silence, a million plates shifting like tectonic drift, slow and inevitable.

I am a tool-user.

 clasped within my foremost manipulator— a limb of three segments, ending in pincers fine enough to split a hair— I hold The Implement.

It is a thing of wonder. It is not of us. It is a length of bone, white and pristine, polished to a sheen that feels slick against my sensory hairs. It was once part of a sky-fallen thing, a great, hollow-boned beast that misjudged the currents and shattered itself against the tear in the rock face. I remember the impact only as a vibration through the stone, a sudden, thrashing violence that disturbed the Hum. Now, the beast is gone, subsumed. Its flesh is our fuel. Its bones are our levers.

I am currently positioned at the "breathing wall," the western face of the fissure where the black granite weeps the most. The rock here is hot to the touch, scalding, but my shell is thick. I am engaged in the Great Work.

With a subtle shift of my weight, I signal to the six others locked around me. They yield. It is a complex negotiation of pressure and mass; they slide their shells backward, millimeters at a time, creating a tiny pocket of space for me to maneuver. There is no aggression, only the efficiency of the hive.

I extend the bone lever. I feel for the micro-fissure, a hairline crack in the obsidian where the pressure differential has forced a pocket of mineral-rich water to accumulate. My antennae brush the rock, tasting the moisture. It is sweet. It is life.

I insert the tip of the bone into the crack.

The physics are simple. My kind understands the principle of the wedge better than we understand ourselves. I apply pressure. The bone groans silently— a sensation I feel through my pincers rather than hear. The rock resists. It is young stone, stubborn and sharp.

I push harder.

The copper blood in my veins pulses faster, the glow beneath my shell plates perhaps brightening a fraction, a heat vented into the oppressive air. I feel the creatures around me bracing, locking their spires together to give me a solid foundation. I am the lever; they are the fulcrum.

With a sudden, sharp give, the rock shifts.

The fissure yawns open. A gush of super-heated steam erupts outward, washing over my face. I drink it in, drawing the moisture through my spiracles. Along with the steam comes the slime— the red, iron-rich bio-film that grows in the dark, wet pockets of the mountain. It is our food. It is what we mine for.

As the others detach and swarm forward to feed, I retract my bone lever. My task is done. I feel the vibration of their feeding, a wet, soft sound that travels up my legs.

I begin to settle back into the formation, ready to become stone again. But as I lock my spires into the creature beside me, I feel a glitch.

It is a sensation that comes sometimes, a vibrating hum that feels like it originates from inside my own shell rather than the air around it. The visual static the outsiders speak of— I cannot see it, but I can *feel* it. It is a friction. For a moment, the boundary between my armor and the sweating wall of the geode dissolves. I feel the immense weight of the mountain pressing down, not as a threat, but as a lover. I feel the vein of copper ore running three feet to my left as if it were my own nervous system.

I am not just eating the mountain. I am becoming it.

The moment passes. The pressure returns. I am just a dinner-plate sized crustacean in the dark. I grip my white bone lever tight. I wait for the next shift in the mass, the next signal to pry.

I am the Obsidian. I am the silence in the stone.
