I would not stand on the ground, for the ground here is a treacherous mirror, slick with black water and the history of a dead industry. Nor would I walk the high, lonely ridges of the ziggurats, where the Stilt-Walkers pick at the salt crusts like skeletal surgeons.

I would be found in the suspension.

I am the **Inverted Sentinel**.

My place is the underside of the great, rusted arteries of the city—the cavernous hollows beneath the elevated train tracks that snake like petrified intestines between the monolithic towers. Here, fifty feet above the slick shale, the air is hottest, trapped and superheated by the metal ribs above and the wet reflection below. It is a thermal river, and I am a piece of driftwood snagged in the current.

I hang upside down.

My body is a study in agonizing geometry. I am long, tapered, and segmented, mimicking the very girders to which I cling. My limbs are hooked—not hands, but jagged grappling irons of calcified bone and iron oxide, evolved to pierce the thick, stable patina of rust without shattering it. I do not grasp the metal; I *become* part of the structure, fusing with it until I choose to move.

My skin is not skin, but a canvas of thermal regulation. It is pale and translucent, the color of wet milk, stretched tight over a ribcage of copper piping that glows dull red with the heat I absorb. I am a radiator for the city, a biological cooling vent. As the humid, oily air rises from the abyss below, it passes through my vents, condensing the steam into pure, distilled water that runs in rivulets down my sides, dripping from my chin to join the black puddles far below.

I have no eyes. In a world of bruised gloom and diffuse light, sight is a liability. Instead, I possess a "lattice sense"—a nervous system that acts as a extension of the iron around me. I feel the city’s breathing not as a sound, but as a tremor in my claws. I know when the massive pistons in the deep blocks shift; I feel the microscopic expansion of the metal as the "late afternoon" heat climbs toward its 2.9 peak. I am the tuning fork by which the city knows it is still vibrating.

I am a solitary point of tension in the silence. I hang there for days, motionless as a stalactite, while the Heat-Skimmers glide like oil slicks beneath me, unaware of the monster sleeping in the ceiling. Occasionally, a Stilt-Walker will pass on a high parapet to my left, its hydraulic legs hissing. I do not turn my head. I do not acknowledge it. To move is to break the equilibrium, to disrupt the precious, fragile silence that holds the sky up.

I am the listener. I am the weight that keeps the train tracks from drifting away into the fog. I am the thing that watches the water rise, waiting for the moment it finally touches my belly, so that I might finally let go and fall into the dark, becoming one with the mineral garden below. But until the water rises, I endure. I hang. I vibrate.
