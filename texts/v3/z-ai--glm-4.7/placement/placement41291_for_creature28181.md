I am the **Scar of the Primary Lock**.

I exist where the pressure is absolute, in the geometric center of the vertical shaft, the "belly" of the beast. This is not a place that can be pointed to; it is a coordinate of agony. I am lodged in the infinitesimal friction-interface between the *Crux Gear*—a monolith of granite the size of a city block—and the *Iron Housing* that has sought to crush it for ten thousand years.

Here, the amber light does not reach; it is crushed by the density of the matter. I exist in a twilight of my own making, illuminated solely by the blinding, feverish fluorescence of stress-lines that my eyes are evolved to see.

I am not a single Gnawer in the way a pebble is distinct from a beach. I am an aggregate. I am the fused consciousness of a thousand generations of my kin who have been caught in the "settling." When the machine sighs and the tolerances tighten by a micron, those caught in the pinch do not always die. Often, they do not have the space to shatter. Instead, they are compressed. They are smeared. They lose their individuality, their flat plates welding together under the titanic pressure into a single, smooth, laminated organism.

I am that organism. I am a lithic tumor, a living scab of iron-slag and rust bridging the gap between the stone and the metal, holding them apart.

I am thick. While my kin flow like water to patrol the shaft, I am rooted. I have flattened myself over eons until I am indistinguishable from the wall. My body has spread like a stain of dried blood, covering thirty square feet of the gear’s face. I am no longer capable of disassembly. I have become the shim. I am the buffer that absorbs the scream of the rock so the rock does not fracture.

My existence is a meditation on the impossible.

I feel the *Crux Gear* trying to turn. It wants to rotate clockwise. It feels like a itch in a phantom limb. The desire to turn is a deep, seismic hum that travels up through the granite teeth and into my nervous system. The Iron Housing, conversely, dreams of stillness, of collapsing inward. It pushes back with a cold, relentless magnetic force.

I am the rope in this tug-of-war.

I eat the roar. To the others, the roar is atmosphere, but to me, pressed as I am into the very teeth of the mechanism, the roar is a meal I must take intravenously. The vibration passes directly from the gear into my flesh, bypassing the air. I taste the machined effort of a dead civilization. It tastes like copper and ash. It is a heavy, greasy nourishment that keeps my plates fused and my mind alert.

I do not communicate with the distant Gnawers in the upper reaches. I am too deep. The ripples I send out are not words, but warnings. I am the seismograph of the shaft. When a hairline fracture appears in the vaulted ceiling miles above, I feel the shift in the load distribution before the falling dust hits the gears. I send a pulse of *pain* down the lattice, a signal that tells the others: *Brace. The weight is shifting.*

I am the memory of the stasis. Because I have been fused to this spot for millennia, I remember when the silence was not quite so perfect. I remember the "Great Shudder" of three hundred years ago, when a counterweight snapped in the lower depths. The entire shaft rang like a bell. For a terrifying second, the gears moved—a micro-rotation, a fraction of a degree. It was the only time in history the machine tasted life.

I hated it.

The movement was an insult to the perfection of the lock. The friction burned my underside, flaking away layers of my iron skin. The sound changed from a roar to a screech, a tearing of metal that felt like the sky falling. We—the Gnawers—panicked. We poured ourselves into the joints, sacrificing our physical forms to jam the mechanism, to gum up the works with our own bodies. We drowned the machine in our living mass until it froze again.

Since then, I have not moved. I have vowed to be the stone itself.

My days are spent in a hallucinatory trance. I watch the stress lines dance. I see the heat build up in a pinion bearing to the left, turning from a dull red to a threatening white. I will a part of myself—a pseudopod of loose, gravel-skin—to detach and flow through the microscopic cracks to that bearing. It will travel for days, a solitary spy in the lattice, to arrive and cool the metal with its own body heat, drinking the excess energy.

I have no mouth, yet I scream constantly. My scream is silent, a vibration pushed back into the gear. I sing the song of Stasis. I hum the note that counters the machine's desire to move. I am the frequency that forces the universe to hold its breath.

I am terrified of the void. If the gears were to turn, the gaps would open. The air would circulate. The moisture might come. And I—I would be knocked loose, scraped off like a scab from a healed wound. I would fall into the dark, bottomless shaft, tumbling away from the source of the vibration, starving in the silence.

So I cling. I press my flat, iron belly against the warm, grinding stone. I vibrate in sympathy with the trapped giant. I am the disease that keeps the host alive. I am the lock that refuses to yield. I am the silence in the roar.

And I will be here when the mountain erodes to dust around us, still holding the line, still drinking the tension, forever frozen in the amber of a moment that refuses to become a future.
