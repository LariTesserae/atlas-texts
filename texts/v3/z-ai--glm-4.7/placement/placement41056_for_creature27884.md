My dorsal plates have flaked and weathered to match the patina of the durasteel perfectly, a camouflage of decay that renders me invisible. To the casual touch, I am merely a roughness in the metal, a slightly softer patch of rust.

I arrived when the pipe was cool; now, it thrums with a deep, infrasonic song that vibrates through my bones. My eight pairs of limbs are dug deep into the pitted iron, the sharp tips of my legs anchored in micro-fractures and old weld-seams. I hold on not just with muscle, but with a kind of static cling, my body charged by the electric air.

I am feeling the stress of the metal as it breathes—expanding with the heat, contracting with the cooling cycles. I drag my soft, pale underbelly along the metal, the microscopic bristles of my mouth scraping the nutrient-rich algae from the surface. It is a silent harvest. We are a network of silence, speaking in the language of earthquakes.

*Plink.*
