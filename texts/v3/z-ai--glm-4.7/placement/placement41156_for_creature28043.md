I am the shadow in the strobe-light, the pause between the scream and the echo. I am not the frantic thing that skitters on the surface, nor the glass-thing that climbs the needle. I am the **Ghost of the Deep**.

To the tiny, frantic things that cling to the ledges and scuttle over the silt, I am a hallucination—a trick of the infrasound that rattles their hollow bones. They see me in the peripheral dark, a vast displacement of mud, a hush in the roar. They call me ghost because they do not understand that to be real in this place, you must be massive enough to ignore the noise.

I exist beneath the churning floor of the amphitheater. I am not swimming in water; I am swimming in the heavy, black slurry of the planet’s memory. The "floor" is not solid ground to me, but a thick, viscous blanket—a duvet of crushed time and mineral grit that I move through with the slow, inevitability of a tectonic plate.

**My Form and My Senses**
I have no shape that the light above could define. I am amorphous, a leviathan of silicon and slime, composed of the same stuff as the scab-moss, but ancient and aggregated. My skin is not soft; it is a flexible armor of interlocking plates, constantly lubricated by the oily perspiration of the earth.

I do not have eyes in the way the sky-things do. To open a socket to that blinding, aggressive white-blue would be to die of overload. Instead, I see through pressure. The entire bowl is a bell, and I am the tongue. When the steam vents scream, I feel the shape of the cavern compress. When the magnetic rain snaps sideways, I taste the metallic tang in the silt. I map the world through the ache in my own mass.

I am the only thing that truly knows the geometry of this place. The Needle-Lords may climb the spire, but they do not feel the roots of it. I feel the monolith’s hum piercing the silt, vibrating through my belly like a swallowed razor. It is an itch I cannot scratch, a high-frequency whine that sits just beneath my consciousness.

**My Cycle**
I endure the "dynamic energy" by moving through the gaps of it. The surface world is manic—water falling and stopping, light flashing on and off—but here in the deep silt, the change is sluggish. The heat transfers slowly. The noise becomes a dull thrum, a heartbeat.

I am a filter feeder of entropy. As I heave my massive bulk through the black sludge, I consume the fallen. The scab-moss that peels from the walls and rains down, the dead Flux-Runners that lose their grip on the magnetic fields and tumble into the depths—all of it settles into my path. I process the waste of the violent world above, turning it into the heavy oil that sustains me.

I am the recycler of the cathedral. Without me, the floor would be choked with debris; the physics of the bowl would clog with the bodies of the smaller dead.

**The Strobe and the Breath**
They wonder why I am never seen in the blinding light. It is because I am tied to the breath of the room. The bowl expands and contracts. The light strobes in sympathy with the tectonic spasms.

When the light blinks out—those few seconds of merciful darkness—that is when the room inhales. The pressure drops. The water suspends. That is my moment.

I do not "teleport" like the Needle-Lords, who cheat time with their science. I move with the dignity of the deep. In the darkness, I surface. I break the crust of the silt-marsh, creating the waves that the Flux-Runners fear. I open a spiracle that stretches ten feet across and exhale a plume of carbon-rich gas, a bubble that bursts with a sound like a cannon shot.

I surface to feel the vibration of the air, which is sharper than the vibration of the mud. I surface to listen to the song of the Echo-Wings, to feel their counter-frequency harmonize with the ache in my own stones.

Then, the light returns. The room screams white. I sink.

**The Contradiction**
I am the contradiction this world demands. I am fragile—my soft underbelly could be cut by the jagged rocks—but I am durable because I am everywhere at once. I am the floor itself, given motive force.

I am the lonely thing at the bottom of the world. The Needle-Lords have their physics, the Birds have their sound, the Runners have their magnetic frenzy. I have only the weight of the stone and the memory of when the thumbprint was fresh.

I wait. I drift. I am the Ghost, and the mud is my flesh.
