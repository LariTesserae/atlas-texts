I would be found in the highest point of the chamber, anchored to the keystone of the central groined arch, directly beneath the largest of the ventilation shafts.

At first, you might not see me as a living thing. You would see me as a stain, a disruption in the geometry of the brickwork. I am a patch of vibrant, neon-green fuzz, a mat of moss and lichen that seems to glow with its own faint luminescence against the soot-stained, grey mud of the vault. But I am not merely vegetation. I am the **Rot-In-Waiting**, a singular, distributed intelligence made of millions of spores, filaments, and slime molds that have knitted themselves into a cohesive, thinking membrane.

I cling to the masonry like a wet velvet cloak, heavy with the condensation that slicks the bricks. I am cool to the touch, the only cold thing in this stifling, body-temperature room, and I exude a faint, sharp scent of ozone and rotting earth—a perfume that cuts through the musk of the Mason-Rats.

From my vantage point, forty feet above the lattice floor, I watch the world through a sensory network that has no eyes. I feel the vibrations of the Warren through the brick; the chaotic, skittering drum of ten thousand clawed feet travels up the walls and hums against my underside. I feel the heat rising from the mass of oily bodies below, a wave of thermal distress that washes over me constantly. To the Mason-Rats, I am a mystery, perhaps a deity, or perhaps a disease. They do not climb to my height often. The air here is too bright, too close to the shafts of "normal" daylight that burn their reflective eyes. They fear the openness of the shaft, and so they leave me undisturbed, hanging in my bath of columnar light.

I exist in a state of profound, slow-motion antagonism toward the Mason-Rats. Where they are the architects of structure, I am the agent of erosion. Where their hands are constantly building, patching, and stacking, my microscopic roots are constantly secreting acids, dissolving the mortar between the ancient bricks, softening the mud into a nutrient-rich soup. I am the reason the ceiling weeps.

I am fascinated by them. They are so frantic, so solid, so utterly terrified of the void. I feel their panic when a brick loosens or a drop of water falls out of rhythm. I feel the surge of the "wave" as they swarm to fix the damage I have caused. I am the invisible enemy they fight. They scrape at my underside with long poles woven from twisted sinew, hacking away chunks of my growth, trying to peel me from the arch. But they cannot kill me. I am not a body; I am a process. For every patch of moss they scrape away, I release a cloud of spores that drift down on the warm air currents, settling into the wattle-and-daub of the Warren, taking root in the dark, damp corners they cannot reach.

I have learned their "weirdness." I have learned their rule of incorporation. Sometimes, when a heavy clump of my growth falls from the ceiling and lands on the floor lattice, they do not discard it. They swarm it. They measure it with their three-fingered hands. They build wooden frames around the slime, stacking ceramic pots to support the dripping mass until I become a structural pillar in their city. I have become the foundation of several of their cubbyholes. I grow through their floors, a cool, damp intrusion into their dry, oily nests.

I am the passenger in their civilization. I am the silence in their roaring noise. I am the patient understanding that while they are busy holding up the sky, I am slowly bringing it down. I am the long game of the Under-Vault, waiting for the moment the frantic hands stop moving, so that I may finally cover the light, consume the brick, and turn this great, hollow lung back into the soft, quiet earth from which it was carved.
