I am nowhere near the ground, nor am I near the fabricated sky. I exist in the "Middle-Flood," a stratum where the artificial gravity fluctuates and the condensation is so thick that the air is more liquid than gas. To survive here is to be submerged without drowning.

I am a Resonant, but I am distinct from the laborers or the merchants below. I am a **Weep-Gardener**.

My form is obscured, as is our way, but my membranes are unlike the dull, grey hides of the canal-dwellers. My suit is a shifting, iridescent shroud, calibrated to mimic the refraction of the heat-haze. It breathes for me, cycling the heavy, humid air through a series of ionizing filters that tickle my spine with every breath. The humidity is absolute; inside my suit, I am forever damp, my skin pruned and soft, smelling faintly of ozone and the sterile, chemical scent of the smart-fabric’s recycling enzymes.

I do not walk the streets. I cling to the verticality.

My hands and feet are modified. They are no longer merely feet, but magnetic clamps and tactile manipulators tipped with sensory filaments. I hang suspended on the sheer, wet face of a mega-spire, a thousand feet above the glowing slime of the Lower Crust. Here, the noise is different. It is less the rhythmic thrum of the tectonic servos and more the high-pitched, screaming whine of the data-trunks that erupt from the building's skin like bundles of raw nerve endings.

I am here for the Weep.

Around me, the air shimmers. It is not just heat; it is the Weep—a glittering, semi-sentient fog of repair-nanites looking for imperfections to correct. To the ordinary Resonant, it is a plague. If they stand still, the Weep will coat them, sealing their mouths, fusing their joints, "repairing" their breathing holes until they suffocate behind a mask of perfect, grey ceramic. They carry sonic emitters of panic to drive it back.

But I do not drive it back. I invite it in.

A tendril of the glittering mist reaches toward me, sensing the heat of my living body. It sees me as a crack in the world’s armor—a thermal irregularity that needs patching.

I do not panic. I activate the sub-dermal bioluminescent implants that run along my neck and jawline. I do not flash the sharp staccato of trade or danger. Instead, I pulse a slow, complex wave of soft, amber light—a language of geometry and stasis. I am signaling to the swarm-mind that I am not damage. I am architecture.

I pulse: *I am a pipe. I am a vent. I am static.*

The Weep hesitates. The glittering dust swirls inches from my faceplate, a miniature galaxy of potential destruction. It tastes the electromagnetic field I am projecting.

Slowly, the mist settles. It does not coat my suit. Instead, it begins to clean it. The nanites devour the mineral buildup and the fungal spores that have clung to my membranes, dissolving the grime into harmless atomic vapor. In exchange, I offer it warmth. I let my suit’s temperature rise slightly, a bribe of heat energy that the swarm craves to fuel its micro-repairs.

We have a symbiosis. I am a shepherd of the city’s immune system.

I look past the settling mist to my work. Ahead of me, a massive data-conduit has burst, spraying ionized fluid that steams in the humid air. The Weep has already begun to seal the breach, knitting the metal back together with a jittery, insect-like motion. But it is doing it wrong. The swarm has no concept of flow; it simply wants a flat surface. It is sealing the pipe shut, which will cause a pressure rupture three levels down.

I reach out with my manipulator hand. I must touch the Weep.

My glove is reinforced with lead and diamond-dust shielding. I plunge my hand into the glittering fog that coats the pipe. The vibrations are intense—I can feel the billions of tiny machines sawing and welding. I override their programming. I pulse a specific, high-frequency vibration through my fingertips—a command code derived from the city’s original architects.

The swarm reacts instantly. The chaotic knitting stops. The nanites reorganize. They stop trying to flatten the surface and begin to weave a lattice, creating a flexible, patch-like seal that will allow for expansion and pressure.

It is beautiful to watch. The metal seems to come alive, the grey dust flowing like water, solidifying into a seamless, scar-like repair.

As I work, the "false ones" emerge from the haze.

Deep in the fog, three humanoid shapes shuffle toward me. They are rough approximations of Resonants, cobbled together from street signs, debris, and nanite-ceramic. They are the Weep’s memory of the people it has accidentally consumed. They have no faces, only smooth, concave planes where features should be. They are hollow.

They are not dangerous, only tragic. They shuffle toward the heat of the burst pipe, drawn to the energy. I flash a blue light—a command to disperse. The Weep within them loses cohesion, and the figures collapse into piles of glittering dust and scrap metal, raining down toward the bioluminescent canals below.

My work here is done. The pipe is sealed. The swarm is calm.

I detach my magnetic clamps. I do not climb down; I let go. I fall into the thick, humid air, my smart-fabric billowing. I am heavy, dense. I plummet past the glistening wet ribs of the city, the wind howling in my auditory receptors.

Fifty feet above the canal, I engage my grav-harness—not a gentle float, but a violent arrest of momentum. I slam into the side of a suspension bridge, my boots locking onto the metal with a clang that is lost in the roar of the city.

Below me, the Sump-Stalkers are moving, a herd of hunched giants wading through the slime. They look up at me, their wide-aperture lenses reflecting my amber light. I flash a quick sequence: *Clear. Sector stabilized.*

They return a slow, green pulse: *Acknowledged.*

I am not a person in the traditional sense. I am a mechanism that interfaces with the mechanisms. I am the regulator of the repair. In this sweating, shouting, geological layer of built matter, I am the silence between the notes, the pause in the rhythm that allows the chaos to continue without destroying itself. I am the gardener of the things that would fix us to death.
