The roar is not a sound. It is a solid thing. It is the floor beneath me, the wall against my back, the pressure that holds my skin tight against my ribs. To you, it is deafening; to me, it is the texture of the world. It is the braille of the air.

I find myself suspended, not hanging, but draped. Gravity here is a suggestion, countered by the slick friction of the polished basalt and the hooked grip of my claws. I am located high in the gloom, perhaps fifteen feet above the mud-flat where the heavy ones sleep, clinging to the underside of a stone ledge that has been worn round as a river stone.

I am one of the Basalt-Born.

I do not know that I am gray, or that my skin is translucent. I only know that I am cool, and that I am perfectly shaped to catch the vibrations of the invisible river. My eyes are gone, sealed shut beneath layers of calcified memory; they were useless here. In their place, my entire body is an ear. The lateral lines running down my flanks are buzzing with the constant, chaotic data of the cavern.

I am hungry, but hunger is a slow thing here. It does not burn; it hums.

I uncurl a single, long finger, the nail harder than the rock I cling to. I reach out to my left, where the pressure waves tell me the velvet carpet of the moss grows thickest. The roar changes pitch slightly where the vegetation is dense—it absorbs the sound, creating a pocket of dullness in the sonic map. I locate a thick mat of liverwort. My movements are agonizingly slow, a drift rather than a grab. If I moved quickly, the air would drag against me, the water would slick my path, and I might fall. I move at the speed of erosion.

My flat, spatulate teeth scrape against the stone. I feel the vibration of the scraping travel through my jaw, harmonizing with the roar. The moss is cold, wet, and tastes of iron and ancient silence. I swallow. It will sustain me for a week.

Below me, through the soles of my feet and the bones of my spine, I feel the thrum of the Elders. They are massive, heavy anchors on the mud floor. They are not moving; they are merely humming with the low-frequency resonance of the earth’s wound. They are the dampeners. I sense the "quiet zones" they have created—those miraculous stacks of stone, the cairns, which break up the violent echoes of the water. In the sonic geometry of my mind, these cairns appear as pillars of stillness amidst a hurricane of noise.

I shift my weight. A rough edge of the basalt catches the skin of my belly. It is an imperfection. A discordant note in the song of the chamber.

The urge rises in me, not an emotion, but a biological imperative, like the need to breathe. I must correct it. I must smooth it.

I turn my attention to the rough patch. The water, rushing unseen through its veins, has chipped the rock recently. Perhaps a century ago. It is fresh and sharp. It disturbs the laminar flow of the air current that washes over me.

I extend both hands now. I press my fingertips against the sharp protrusion. I feel the jagged edges screaming against the smooth backdrop of the roar. I begin to scrape.

*Shhhk.*

The sound of my claw against the stone is tiny, a high-pitched flicker that is instantly swallowed by the river’s bellow. But I feel it. I feel the microscopic particles of basalt falling away, landing on the slick floor below. I am not building; I am erasing. I am helping the water finish its work.

I scrape for hours. Or perhaps days. Time is measured here by the gradual dulling of the stone’s edge, not by the rising or setting of a sun I have never seen. The cold spray coats my face, running in rivulets over my sealed eyes. I do not blink. I simply exist in a trance of friction and resonance.

Finally, the stone is smooth. It matches the curve of the chamber perfectly. The air flows over it without turbulence. The vibration passes through it without distortion. The discord is gone. The roar is pure again.

A sense of profound satisfaction washes over me, colder than the water. I am ready to rest. I drape my elongated body over the newly polished patch, molding myself to the rock. I blend perfectly. To the vibration, I am just another bulge in the wall. To the sound, I am silence.

I slow my heart until it beats once every few minutes, syncing with the rhythm of the dripping condensation. I am the caretaker of the scream. I am the polisher of the dark. I am home.
