I am the silence inside the scream.

My name is not a sound; it is a gesture. A left hand cupping the ear, the right hand sweeping out in a flat plane, then curling into a fist. *The Listener Who Waits.* That is who I am.

I am standing on the spine of a dome the size of a city. To you, looking down from the clouds, this place is a smooth, unbroken bruise of purple and green, a desert of flesh. But to me, standing here, the terrain is vast. The ground beneath my feet is not earth; it is the *dermis*. It is soft, yielding, warm. It gives slightly when I shift my weight, the sponge-like mat of root-hairs compressing with a damp, exhausted sigh.

The air is a heavy, wet thing. It sits in my lungs, a thick, humid wool that I have to wade through just to breathe. I am tall—that is the way of the Hushed—my legs elongated spindles that allow me to stride over the deep creases and valleys of the fungal landscape without sinking. My skin is the color of the shadows beneath the great domes, a mottled violet that slicks with sweat, a seal against the damp.

My ears are closed.

I run my fingers over the ridges of bone beside my head, feeling the tight seal where muscle and cartilage fuse. Inside my skull, the roar of the Roof is a dull, rhythmic throb. It is the heartbeat of the world, felt in the teeth and the jawbone. If I were to open them—even a fraction—the sound would slam into me like a physical blow. The grinding of a trillion mandibles, the friction of countless wings, the endless, high-pitched whine of the swarms. The world is loud. It is a deafening, chaotic ocean of noise. We learned long ago that to survive the roar, we must choose what we hear.

A flicker of movement to my left. I turn my head.

It is *Swift-Hand*, a member of my band. He is ten paces away, his silhouette sharp against the blinding white glare of the sun. He is holding a stone knife, poised.

He does not call out. He does not make a sound. He raises his hands.

The language is fast, frantic. His fingers dance, a fluttering of moths against the glare.

*Vein. Deep. Good.*

I sign back, my wrists loose, the palms slick: *Show.*

He drops to one knee, pressing his palm flat against the purple epidermis of the dome. I approach, my own bone hooks clacking softly against my hip—tools carved from the hollow skeletons of the gliding shadows that haunt the lower air. I kneel beside him.

I feel it. Not through my ears, but through my knees, through the soles of my feet. A faint, rhythmic pulse. A thrumming vibration that differs from the background static of the swarm. It is a *whoosh-thump, whoosh-thump*.

It is a vein of the Host. A river of amber sap flowing deep beneath the rubbery skin.

I draw my own knife—a shard of flint sharpened to a molecular edge. I hand-signal to Swift-Hand: *Width. Depth.* He replies with a measurement, his fingers spanning a precise distance. I nod.

I make the cut.

It is not like cutting wood or meat. It is like slicing through tough, wet leather. The skin resists, then yields. A thick, viscous fluid wells up, clear as glass but heavy as syrup. The smell hits me instantly—sweet, cloying, intoxicating. It smells of life. It smells of energy.

We do not speak. We do not grunt. We simply extend our hollowed gourds, catching the dripping sap. It is the only water we need, the only food we require. We are the tappers, the phlebotomists of this giant. We take only enough to fill our gourds, never enough to hurt. When we are done, I pull a pouch of crushed clotting-moss from my harness and pack the wound. The moss swells, knitting with the living flesh. The wound will heal within the hour.

We stand. The sun beats down, turning the condensation on my back into steam.

Swift-Hand signs again: *Stone. Move.*

I look where he points. Half a mile away, across the rolling plain of purple, stands a Circle. It is one of ours. A ring of calcified nodules, white and jagged against the smoothness of the world. We have been moving this stone for three days. It is a massive thing, a jagged tooth of the earth’s skeleton.

We begin to walk.

The scale here is crushing. The horizon is a curve of green fuzz. The domes rise like sleeping giants, their massive slopes lost in the haze. We are specks. Fleas. I feel the crushing weight of the insignificance. If the Host were to wake, to shift its massive bulk, we would be brushed away like dust.

But we are also the architects of the quiet.

As we walk, I peel back the seal on my left ear. Just a sliver.

The sound rushes in—a torrential wall of static. I hear the *click-click-click* of the grazer-clouds drifting miles above, sounding like hail on a tin roof. I hear the deep, bass thrum of the Root-Mats shifting. And then, beneath it all, I hear the *Scream* building.

It is a frequency that vibrates in the marrow. It is the sound of the swarm rising.

I snap my ear shut. I grab Swift-Hand’s shoulder. I sign: *Down. Now.*

We do not hesitate. We drop to our knees, then to our stomachs. We press our faces into the wet, spongy mat of the ground. We dig our fingers into the root-hairs, anchoring ourselves. We interlock our arms.

The pressure drops.

Then, it hits.

It is not a sound. It is a physical weight. The air above us turns into a solid column of vibration. I feel the ground beneath me shudder as millions of wings beat the air, tearing apart the atmosphere. The swarm passes over us—a living tornado of legs and wings and hunger. They are grazing on the algae, stripping the skin of the world.

My teeth rattle in my skull. My vision blurs from the sheer intensity of the concussive waves. I squeeze my eyes shut and press my ears tighter, imagining myself as a stone, as nothing at all.

We stay like that for an eternity. Minutes? Hours? Time loses meaning when the world is screaming.

And then, as quickly as it began, the pressure lifts. The roar fades to a dull roar, then to the familiar background static.

I open my eyes. A fine layer of green dust coats my back. The air is shimmering with the heat of the passing wings.

I look at Swift-Hand. He is shaking the dust from his hair. He signs to me, his hands trembling slightly: *Alive.*

*Alive,* I reply.

We stand up. The stone is still there, waiting in the distance. The geometry remains. The Circle is not yet finished.

We are small. We are fragile. We are the Hushed. But we are here, tending the wounds of the giant, arranging our perfect stones in the noisy dark, waiting for the moment when the world finally falls silent.

I seal my ears tight against the roar, pick up my bone hook, and begin the long walk toward the stone.
