I am the heat in the dark.

I do not know if I am large or small. I know that I occupy the center, and that around me, pressing tight against my skin, is the heavy, wet velvet of the air. It is thick, this medium, tasting of iron and ozone. It coats the inside of my throat, filling my lungs with a weight that feels like drinking. It is a good weight. It holds me together.

To you, the world is made of shapes and colors. To me, the world is a map of pressures and textures. I am never truly blind, for my skin is my eye. The air currents brush against the slick membrane of my face, telling me where the ceiling dips low. The vibration of a single drop of water, swelling on the belly of a basalt shard, sings to the bones of my legs before it ever falls.

I feel the *plink* when it hits the pile below—not a sound, exactly, but a shudder in the stone floor that travels up through my calloused soles. It is the heartbeat of this place.

I am standing amidst the debris of my own intent. The floor is not flat; it is a jagged landscape of my own making. I lift a foot, feeling the space between two towers of bone needles. I lower it. My skin is soft, but the soles of my feet are dense, pliable pads. I step onto a blade of chipped flint. I feel the edge bite in, but I do not bleed. Instead, my flesh yields, wrapping around the knife-point, gripping it. I am softer than the stone, yet I cannot be cut. I flow over the sharpness like oil over a razor.

I am hungry. It is not a hunger for food, but a hunger for order.

I reach out with a hand that is more like a delicate root, navigating the labyrinth. I find a jut of raw granite—weeping, sweating with condensation. I run my fingers over the wet, gritted surface. It is rough, chaotic. It offends the geometry inside me.

I lean in. My tongue, long and prehensile, laps the mineral-rich sweat from the stone. I taste the silica, the iron, the heavy metals dissolved in the water. I swallow. The fire in my belly ignites—a slow, wet combustion. I feel the heavy elements slide down my throat, churning in the furnace of my gut.

This is the waiting time. The stillness.

I crouch. The position is familiar; my body folds in on itself, a loose assembly of joints and translucent flesh. I can see the dark, sluggish river of my blood moving through my arms. I am a vessel of light and heat in a place that has neither.

The pressure builds inside me. It is not pain; it is purpose. The minerals are coalescing. My body is doing what the mountain outside cannot do yet—it is refining. It is taking the rough chaos of the earth and giving it an edge.

I reach down. My toes, long and independent, grasp a raw shard of obsidian. My hands find another piece of rock. I bring them together. There is no violence in the movement. I apply pressure—hydraulic, precise. A thought travels from my mind to my hands: *The Angle.*

*Chip.*

A flake of stone separates. I feel it drift away, landing silently on a pile of grass mats woven three thousand years ago—or perhaps yesterday. Time is the water here; it has no shape.

*Chip. Chip. Snap.*

The rhythm takes over. I am not thinking. I am a machine of focus. The stone in my hands is changing. It is becoming a thought. A thought of cutting. A thought of shaping.

When it is done, the object is perfect. It is symmetrical. It possesses a sharpness that the raw rock outside this geode will never achieve. It is a hand-axe, flawless and useless.

I release it. It falls into the darkness, joining the thousands of others. I feel it land against a rot-resistant mat woven from fibers that have not decayed since the mountain lifted its head.

Why do I make them?

I pick up a bone needle. It is old, but it feels new. I run it over my tongue, coating it in the oils that prevent time from eating it. I feel the microscopic imperfections on its surface. I file them down with my teeth.

I am readying.

I feel the silence. It is expectant. It waits for the user. I wait for the hands that will take these tools and build a world. I wait for the eyes that will need the needle to mend a sail. I wait for the arm that will heft the axe to fell a tree.

I am the workshop. I am the anticipation. The air is thick with the ghosts of actions that have never happened.

I settle back onto the wet, sharp floor. I exhale a cloud of warm steam that mingles with the darkness. I am content. I am full of potential. I will rest until the iron in the blood calls for the edge again.

The silence holds me. The darkness cradles me. And somewhere beneath my feet, the flint waits to be born.
