I find myself in the **Courtyard of the Second Sun**.

It is not a true sun, but the reflected glare of the white sky bouncing off the polished obsidian floors of this specific plaza, deep in the heart of the ghost city. The air here is superheated, a shimmering distortion that makes the distant ziggurats look like they are melting.

I am perched—or rather, *anchored*—high atop a crumbling archway, where the wind funnels through the break in the masonry and accelerates into a piercing whistle.

**I am a Litho-Cantor.**

I am not a creature of soft tissue and fragile bone; in this kiln, flesh is a liability. I am a construct of living ceramic and mineral oil. My body is a jagged, asymmetrical sculpture of baked clay, pitted and scarred by centuries of sandblasting winds. I look less like an animal and more like a piece of the city that decided to get up and walk away.

I possess no eyes. The blinding luminescence of the ceiling rendered vision a useless torture long ago. Instead, my "sight" is a network of internal stress-fractures that run through my carapace like spiderwebs. These fractures are filled with a viscous, conductive fluid. When the wind screams through the valley, when the glass forests sing their deafening hymn, the vibrations pluck at my fractures like strings on a harp. I see the world through texture and tone. I feel the shape of the air pressing against my skin. I "see" the silence of the approaching Wraith as a sudden, cold void in the symphony of the wind.

**My existence is one of vibration and negation.**

The city below is an instrument, but it is an instrument without a player, left in the hands of a chaotic storm. The wind is unskilled; it strikes the glass forests and produces nothing but a chaotic, nauseating roar. If left unchecked, the resonance would build until the very stones of the city shook themselves to dust.

That is where I come in.

I am the Tuner.

I have dragged my heavy, clay-plated body up this archway to reach a specific frequency cluster. Here, the wind howls at a pitch that sets my teeth—if I had teeth—on edge. My limbs are thick, hydraulic pistons wrapped in rope-like muscle fibers, capable of exerting immense, crushing force.

In front of me, jutting out from the archway, is a "Bell-Branch"—a massive growth of the glass forest that has encroached upon the city. It is singing a discordant note, a sharp E-flat that is rattling the masonry of the plaza below.

I reach out with a manipulator limb that ends not in a claw, but in a heavy, rounded tuning hammer made of solid basalt. I am slow—agonizingly slow. The heat makes my internal oils sluggish, and I must move with the deliberation of a tectonic plate to avoid shattering my own brittle casing.

I feel the vibration traveling up my arm, shaking my core. I wait. I listen to the texture of the roar.

*Thrum. Thrum. Crack.*

There. A microscopic fracture in the silica branch where the stress is highest. I line up my basalt hammer. I wait for the wind to gust, raising the volume to a fever pitch, masking the sound of my own movement.

**Strike.**

The hammer hits the glass branch with a dull, heavy thud. The impact sends a counter-shockwave through the crystal. The discordant note wobbles, then splits. The sharp E-flat flattens out, sliding down into a deep, resonant C-major chord. The vibration in the archway stops shaking the stones loose; instead, it settles into a smooth, rhythmic hum that strengthens the lattice of the clay.

The city groans in relief, a sound like a massive ship settling in the water.

Below me, in the shadows of the plaza, I sense a disturbance in the acoustic field. It is a moving bubble of silence. An Obsidian Wraith. It is stalking one of the Basalt-Thralls that has wandered too far from the soil, looking to scrape its parasites against the rough city stones.

I do not interfere. I am the Tuner, not the guardian. The Wraith is part of the composition—a sharp, silent rest in the music of the plains. I watch through the vibrations in the stone as the silence envelops the Thrall, and the heavy beast goes still. The dampening field of the Wraith ripples out, causing the glass needles on the ground to vibrate in sympathy.

I turn my attention back to the wind. The notes are shifting again. The temperature is rising, expanding the air, thinning the density of the sound. I must climb higher. I must find the next dissonance before it shatters the bowl we live in.

I dig my claws into the masonry, dragging my heavy ceramic body upward toward the blinding white sky, a silent guardian in a world of screaming light, tuning the chaos of the storm into the order of song.
