I would find myself suspended in the **Dead Shelf of the Pillar known as "The Siphon,"** specifically clinging to the underside of a calcified branch the size of a city plaza.

I would be an **Anchor-Keeper**.

My existence is defined by the vertigo that I have long since burned out of my nervous system, replaced by a vibrating, tactile map of the world held in the soles of my feet. I am one of the Knot-Tyers, but not one who weaves the sleeping pods—I am the one who ties our lives to the god we live on.

Here is the reality of my life on the Siphon:

**The Flesh and the Grease**
The heat here is a heavy, wet hand pressing against my back. I am naked save for a loincloth of cured hide and a harness of braided vine, but my skin is not bare. I am coated in a thick, pungent layer of grease mixed with the fine, gray dust of petrified root. It seals my pores, keeping the moisture in and the suffocating humidity out. My skin has the sheen of polished obsidian, tough and leathery to the touch.

My ears are gone—at least, the hearing parts are. I have carved plugs of dense, spongy fungi into my canals, sealing them shut. To hear with air-eardrums in the High Bloat is to go mad from the roar. Instead, I hear through my bones. The jaw-rattling scream of the Echo-Bats, the grinding thunder of the Drone-Hoppers migrating in the distance—these are not noises to me; they are textures felt in my teeth and the marrow of my shins.

**The Geometry of Survival**
I hang upside down, my ankles hooked into a "Lock-Knot," a complex macro-molecular weave that tightens under weight but releases with a specific flex of the calf muscle. Below me, the abyss yawns open, miles of empty green-gold air before it hits the root-choked floor.

I am inspecting the "Great Tether"—the primary anchor line that holds our cluster of woven shelters to the dead wood of the Shelf. The rope is as thick as a man’s torso, made from the sinewy fibers of the Pillar's own hanging vines.

I run my hands over the fibers. They are vibrating. *Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.*

It is the song of the Pillar. The Siphon is drinking. The moisture from the air is wicking through the bark, passing through the Dead Shelf, and traveling down the massive trunk. The vibration is so intense it makes my fingertips tingle. If the rhythm skips a beat, it means a crack is forming in the wood, and I must cut the line and re-splice us to a new sector before the shelf shears off.

**The Sky-Traffic**
Through the haze, I see them.

A cloud of Echo-Bats is rising from the canopy below. They are translucent blurs, catching the dim light, glowing like ghosts in the spore-thick air. They are not flying toward us; they are riding the thermal updraft generated by the heat radiating from the abyss.

I feel their approach before I see them clearly—a tickle in the back of my skull, a rising pitch in the vibration of the air. They pass us in a shrieking vortex. The wind pressure buffets my body, swinging me in my harness. I do not panic. I am an Anchor-Keeper. I am stone.

High above, I see a disturbance in the green-gold light. A shadow falls over us. A Drone-Hopper, a car-sized beast of chitin and clicking legs, has launched itself from a neighboring Pillar. It arcs through the sky, a clumsy projectile of biology. It misses the landing platform of the Shelf above us by a dozen feet.

I watch, detached, as it tumbles past me, its legs flailing, its carapace reflecting the diffuse light. It does not scream; it clicks. A Morse code of panic. It falls into the depths, becoming a speck, and then nothing. Down there, the Root-Weavers are already waking. They can smell the impact coming. They will have a feast tonight.

**The Approach of the Heavy Dew**
The pressure is dropping. I feel it in my joints—an ache that signals the coming of the Heavy Dew.

The air is thickening, turning from a gas to a suspension. The green light is fading, replaced by a bruised, heavy purple. The silence is not returning, but the pitch is changing. The roar of the insects is dampening as the air itself becomes heavy liquid.

I finish my inspection. The Tether is sound. The knots are swollen, ready to tighten as the fibers absorb the moisture.

I pull myself up, hand over hand, climbing the vertical surface of the Dead Shelf. My fingers are strong, used to crushing the bark of the Pillar to find water pockets. I reach the lip of the Shelf and haul my body over the edge, rolling onto the "safe" ground.

It is not safe. It is merely safer.

Around me, the other Scavengers are retreating into their woven pods. They look like wet, oily beetles scurrying into the hive. I nod to a fellow Anchor-Keeper. We do not speak. Speech is useless here. We touch foreheads—a vibration transfer. *Secure.*

I crawl into my own hammock, a pod of woven vines suspended over the drop. Inside, it is dark and smells of wet earth and old sweat. I wrap myself in the fibrous walls.

Outside, the world turns to liquid. The Heavy Dew falls. It is not a rain; it is a submersion. The entire universe becomes a roaring, rushing river of water.

I lie back, feeling the vibrations of the water hammering against the skin of my pod. I feel the Siphon groaning as it drinks its fill, a massive, living mountain gorging itself on the sky. I feel the distant, contented clicking of the Root-Weavers miles below, feasting on the fallen Hopper.

I am small. I am a knot on a thread on a hide on a god.

I close my eyes, and for a moment, I am not separate from the roar. I am part of the noise. I am the vibration.

I sleep.
