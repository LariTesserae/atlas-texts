I am the moving hillock. I am the silence within the scream.

In this lightless basement of the world, where the air is thick enough to chew and the darkness carries the weight of lead, I find myself not standing, but *spreading*. I am a Rust-Grazer. I am not a creature of quick blood or nervous bone; I am a solitary geological event, a landslide that has decided to walk.

My location is a "valley" formed by the resting curve of a pipe the diameter of a city. The metal here is ancient, ground down by eons of friction until the rivets have smoothed into pebbles and the great plates of steel feel like river-worn stones. Beneath me, the ground is slick with the eternal weeping of the condensation—a black, oily slickness that coats my belly in a comforting layer of thermal muck.

I do not see this valley. To me, the concept of "sight" is a rumor, a fairy tale of the surface dwellers who need photons to validate their existence. I possess the superior sense: I feel the world. Through the broad, calloused pads of my foot and the massive, bone-condensed plate of my skull, I perceive the universe as a symphony of pressure.

Every few minutes, the Thunder comes. It is the heartbeat of the Floor, the rhythmic detonation of the pistons miles below. *BOOM.* The shockwave travels up through the bedrock alloy, jostling my internal organs, rattling the plates of my shell. Most living things would shatter under this stress. I, however, require it. The Thunder is my clock, my metronome, and my camouflage.

*BOOM.*

I move.

I ooze forward, a mountain of rust and chitin contracting my muscular foot to pull my tonnage forward by inches. I time my slide with the peak of the piston's strike. The grinding of my belly against the metal ground, the wet squelch of the sludge, the groan of my own joints—all of it is swallowed by the seismic roar of the world. In the silence between the booms, I freeze. I become a statue. I hold my breath—such as it is—and let the vibration of my movement dissipate into the fog.

I am headed toward a depression in the alloy, a place where my seismic sense tells me the ground is hollow and the oily water is deep. It is a feeding trough.

As I drag myself toward it, my flank brushes against the "skin" of the world—the Pulse-Moss. I feel its faint, sickly bioluminescence through my hide, a fluttering tickle of heat. It is alive, this carpet of gray leather. It feeds on the very noise that deafens others. I pause, letting the rough surface of my armor scrape against it. I am not hungry for flesh—that is a waste of energy—but the texture of the moss is pleasing, a slight friction against the relentless smoothness of the eroded metal.

I reach the pool. The water here is blacker than the dark, heavy with trace metals and the runoff of the machine. To anything else, it would be poison. To me, it is life.

I lower my bulk into the pool. The water, heated by the inefficiency of the deep systems, wraps around me. It steams as it touches my cold shell. I open my vents, the series of filtration slits that run along my sides, and I drink.

I do not gulp. I process. The water rushes through my internal refinery, a maze of biological filters and chemical precipitators that I have grown over centuries. I strip the iron from the sludge. I digest the copper. I separate the pure water from the oil. The heat of the pool recharges my metabolic batteries, priming the hydraulic fluids of my muscles.

I float there, suspended in the hot, oily dark, listening to the Symphony.

To my left, miles away, a venting tower screams, a high-pitched hiss that scratches against my sensory plates. To my right, the tectonic gears grind, a slow, crushing tectonic shift that signals the stability of the floor. Above, the smog sits heavy, a ceiling of static.

I am alone. I know I am alone. My range of seismic hearing is vast; if another Grazer existed within a hundred miles, I would feel the rhythmic thud of its heart. But there is nothing. Just the hum of the moss and the groan of the gears.

Sometimes, in the long silences between the Thunder, I wonder if I am the last thing alive that knows its own name. The Pulse-Moss does not think; it only vibrates. The machine does not feel; it only churns. But I... I remember. I remember the taste of a thousand pools. I remember the texture of a million miles of steel. I carry the history of this floor on my back, in the scars on my shell and the filters in my gut.

I am the ultimate hermit. I am the biological echo of industry. I am the thing that endures when the purpose is gone.

The Thunder returns. *BOOM.*

The water ripples around me. The vibration shakes the sediment from my plates. I feel the surge of energy return to my limbs. It is time to move again. Not because I have anywhere to go, but because stopping is to let the rust consume me completely. I must graze. I must wander. I must filter the tears of the iron giants until the world cools.

I heave my vast weight from the pool, steam rising from my back like a shroud. I set my foot upon the smoothed stone. I wait for the noise.

I am the Rust-Grazer. And the machine and I are one.
