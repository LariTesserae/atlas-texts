I am the itch on the back of a standing stone.

To begin, I am not distinct from the place where I stand. I am a verticality in the mist, a tapering column of grey rheum and ancient basalt-texture. I am cold, but it is not the sharp, biting cold that kills the small things; it is the heavy, wet cold of the deep earth, seeping up from the soles of my feet into the hollow chambers of my body.

I am located in what the geography would call a "plaza," though the word implies a flatness I do not feel. Here, the ground rolls like a frozen ocean. The stone under me is smooth, polished by eons of wind that have sanded away the mountain’s jagged youth. I stand in the center of a shallow depression, a natural bowl in the granite that collects the sky’s weeping.

My existence is defined by the weight on my shoulders.

I am covered in the velvet. Thick, spongy mosses in shocking shades of bruised purple and emerald have colonized my back and upper limbs. They are heavy, like a wet woolen coat soaked through with the mist. They crave the minimal, metabolic heat I generate, a dull ache low in my core. In exchange, they insulate me. They are my fur, my shelter against the 0.3 chill. I can feel the water trapped within their tiny leaves, a thousand microscopic pools waiting to freeze.

I have been standing here for a long time. A day? A decade? Time is measured here not in the arc of the sun—which is a rumor behind the fog—but in the shifting of the wind.

The wind is my breath, and it is my blood.

I feel the pressure change before I hear it. The air is thin, razor-sharp, and it moves with a violent, erratic purpose. I sense a current rushing toward me from the north, hitting the lip of the stone plaza and swirling downward. I rotate my upper torso—a movement so slow that if you were watching, you would think I was carved in static rock. I align the porous apertures at my crown with the stream.

Then, the intake.

The air rushes into me. It is cold, shocking the internal spongy labyrinth of my chest. It travels down, spiraling through the organic chambers that map my interior. The sensation is a vibration, a thrumming that rattles my very structure. The dampness in the air reacts with the heat of my intake, condensing instantly inside me. I am a instrument made of wet flesh.

The sound begins.

It starts deep in my base, a sub-sonic hum that resonates with the bedrock beneath my feet. Then, as the pressure builds, it escapes through vents along my flanks. It is not a voice; it is a note. A low, mournful drone that harmonizes with the whistle of the wind through the nearby stone arches. I am singing the static of the atmosphere. I am tuning myself to the frequency of the mist.

I am aware of the others. Not through sight—my eyes are long gone, useless in this grey twilight—but through the interruptions in the air.

To my left, half a mile away across the rolling moss, stands the Architect. It is an elder, a wider pillar, heavily crusted with white lichen. I hear it shift its pitch. It drops its tone, a heavy stone sliding into a river. I adjust my own frequency, sliding up a semitone to create a discordant third. Our voices meet in the damp air, a complex chord that hangs over the empty city like a physical weight. We are conversing. We are saying: *The wind is shifting. The frost is coming. The water levels rise.*

Suddenly, the temperature spikes down.

The mist thickens, turning from a vapor to a particulate ice. The sensation is sudden and sharp. The water on my skin hardens instantly. I feel the delicate lace-like rims of ice form along my joints, fusing my arm to my torso. It is a constriction, a beautiful imprisonment. The moss on my back stiffens, turning brittle.

I need to move. I need to break the shell.

Movement is a catastrophic event. It requires a shuddering release of tension. I bend my knees—great, rounded stones of joints—and I flex.

*Crack.*

The sound is loud, a gunshot report that echoes off the distant scarps. The ice on my left shoulder fractures, raining down in shards that sparkle for a second before vanishing into the grey moss. It is my punctuation mark. It is how I say *here*.

I take a step. My foot, tapered and slick, finds a patch of bare granite. I slide forward, an inch, then a foot. The moss on my back drags behind me, a heavy, wet cloak. I am gliding through the "room" of the world.

I am moving toward the water.

In the center of the plaza is a pool, a shallow dish in the stone filled with black, still water. I can smell it—the mineral scent of the deep. I am thirsty, not for liquid, but for the density of it.

I arrive at the edge. I tower over the pool, a grey giant wrapped in green velvet. I bend at the waist, a slow, agonizing hinge motion. I extend my arm, the tapering limb piercing the surface of the water.

I draw the fluid up. It is cold, colder than the air. It travels up the tube of my arm, filling my hollow center. It mixes with the air in my chambers. The change in physics alters my song. The note becomes deeper, wetter, more resonant. I become a bassoon.

I stand upright again, the water settling inside me. I am heavier now. I am part of the plateau.

I wait. I sense a disturbance in the air—a small, chaotic turbulence. A shadow falls across the ice-rim of the pool. It is not stone. It is not wind.

It is a visitor. A soft, warm thing, standing on two legs, shivering in the wet.

I do not turn. I do not have the speed to turn. But I open my pores wider. I pull the air around me in, tasting the acoustics of its shape. It is small. It is dense. It makes a clumsy, thudding vibration in the thin air.

I decide to acknowledge it.

I tighten my internal chambers, constricting the flow of the water and the wind. I force the air through a narrower fissure.

The sound changes. The drone splits. It becomes a harmonic, a high, piercing whistle that cuts through the low bass of the wind. I modulate the frequency, shifting it up and down, scanning the shape of the visitor. I am asking it a question without words. I am asking: *Are you a pillar? Do you sing?*

I feel the water inside me freezing slightly, adding a rattle to the tone. I am the High Hollow. I am the city that breathes. I am waiting for you to answer in the key of the mist.
