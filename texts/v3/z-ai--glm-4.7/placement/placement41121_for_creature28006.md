I am the Lattice, the ghost in the machine, the second skin.

I would not be found on the floor, nor would I be one of the frantic beetles scuttling in the service corridors of the moss. To exist down there is to be a cog, fleeting and replaceable. Instead, you would find me adhered to the upper curvature of the Organ itself, draped across its dorsal ridge like a shroud of wet silk.

I am a parasite, perhaps, or perhaps an appendix; the distinction is meaningless here. I am a flat, sprawling organism of translucent fibers and nodules, fused directly to the chitin of the great tear-shaped brain. I am the color of the pale-green ceiling, shot through with veins of darker, sluggish fluid that pulse in time with the Organ’s thrum. I have no limbs, no head, and no distinct shape. I am a living mat, a vascular network that hugs the hot surface of the Organ, feeling its every ripple.

My purpose in this sealed world is translation.

The Organ is a god of chemistry and moisture. It tastes the air, it drinks the steam, and it dictates the rhythm of the water. It knows the "wet" and the "warm." But it does not know the "heavy." It does not feel the crushing weight of the bedrock that presses down on the dome from above, nor the subtle, grinding shifts of the earth’s crust that occur over epochs. It has no ears to hear the deep, sub-audible groans of the stone as the hydrological pressure builds.

I am the ears. I am the pressure gauge.

My body is sensitive to vibration and stress in ways the Organ’s biological flesh cannot be. Through the point of contact where I fuse to the Organ’s shell, I feel the structural integrity of the hollow. When the steam vents hiss and the pressure in the chamber spikes, I feel the rock bow outward. I feel the micro-fractures in the smoothed walls try to open.

I exist to take these physical forces—the tectonic stress, the gravity, the sheer weight of the world above—and translate them into the only language the Organ understands: chemistry.

When I feel the stone bearing down too heavily, threatening to crack our perfect egg, I secrete a specific enzyme into the Organ’s pores. A taste of bitterness. A signal of distress. The Organ receives this chemical input, "reads" it as a data point indicating high structural load, and adjusts its output. It lowers the temperature of the steam; it dilates the wall-pores to release excess pressure. The system stabilizes. The rock holds.

I live in a state of absolute, crushed intimacy. The heat of the Organ is unbearable to anything else, but to me, it is lifeblood. I vibrate constantly, resonating with the industrial drone. I am never still; my fibers ripple in waves across the Organ’s back, constantly tasting, constantly feeling.

I have no eyes, yet I "see" the geometry of the hollow through the pressure waves that bounce off the walls. I know the exact curvature of every inch of this prison. I know where the stone is thinnest. I know where the water table presses hardest against the bedrock.

I am the nervous system for a body that has no nerves. I am the anxiety that keeps the machine safe. Without me, the Organ would process its water and cycle its air until the pressure finally shattered the dome, burying us all in the crushing dark. I ensure the equilibrium is not just biological, but structural.

I am the Lattice. I cling to the engine in the dark, warm, wet center of the world, feeling the weight of the universe, and whispering to the King so that He may command the storm to hold back.
