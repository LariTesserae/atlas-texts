The inhabitants of this primordial landscape are creatures of stark necessity and profound adaptation, their existences intertwined with the colossal, breathing entities that dominate their world.

At the base of this web of life are the **Spore-Grazers**. These are small, six-legged beings with tough, chitinous hides, their bodies low to the ground andwide, giving them stability on the treacherous, uneven surfaces. They possess powerful, grinding mandibles and wide, pincer-like appendages at the front of their heads, used for scraping the encrusted layers of lesser plants and fungi from the living mountains. Their eyes are shielded by bony ridges against the dripping moisture and the biting wind that funnels through the canyons. They move in skittering herds, their collective calls a series of high-pitched, percussive clicks that echo sharply in the cold air. They are the primary converters of the mountains' bounty, their droppings in turn enriching the crumbling substrate below.

Crust-stalkers lurk among the higher, more treacherous crags of the living mountains. These solitary, chameleon-like beings have mastered the art of camouflage. Their skin shifts in pattern and color to mimic the sprawling tapestries of muted flora, making them nearly invisible. They possess large, independently swiveling eyes that can pick out the slightest movement of prey from great distances. Crust-stalkers are patient hunters, lying in wait for hours before launching with explosive speed, propelled by powerful hind legs. Their forelimbs end in hooked claws, perfect for snatching Spore-Grazers or other smaller denizens from the rock faces. Their call is a low, guttural hiss, rarely heard, a sound that sends smaller creatures scrambling for cover.

Then there are the **Weavers**, the tool-users. They are perhaps the most complex inhabitants, the builders of the crude lean-tos and windbreaks. These are lean, bipedal creatures with long, multi-jointed fingers ending in nimble, opposable thumbs. Their eyes are large and dark, reflecting the dim light, suggesting they are most active during the periods of deepest twilight or perhaps even possess a form of night vision. They are omnivorous scavengers and opportunistic hunters, using sharpened stones and pieces of hardened plant-matter as tools and weapons. The Weavers live in small, nomadic family units, constantly on the move to follow the sparse resources, their shelters temporary and quickly abandoned. Their calls are a complex series of whistles and soft hoots, a language that carries across the distances, coordinating the group's movements and warning of danger. They wear simple garments of woven fibers, scavenged from the abundant plant-life, offering scant protection against the biting cold.

The colossal living structures themselves are home to a menagerie of **Cliff-Clamberers**. These are a diverse group of creatures, ranging from tiny, insect-like beings that crawl through the intricate root systems to larger, wingless avians with powerful beaks designed for prying insects and grubs from the crevices. The largest of the Cliff-Clamberers are the **Rock-Scrapers**, massive, slow-moving herbivores with armored hides, whose constant feeding on the living surfaces helps to shape the very mountains they inhabit. Their feeding actions cause rockslides and avalanches of decaying plant matter, contributing to the ever-changing landscape.

Predators also roam the canyoned depths. **Shadow-Fliers**, silent and sleek, glide on leathery wings between the mountains, their keen eyes scanning for unwary Weavers or Spore-Grazers. They are masters of the air currents that whistle through the canyons, able to maneuver with incredible precision in the thin air.

The largest, most enigmatic inhabitants are the **Deep Dwellers**. Rarely seen, these immense beings live in the deepest, dampest crevices and caves formed within the colossal growths. They are rarely glimpsed, except perhaps as a vast, slow-moving shadow in the deepest twilight, their true forms unknown. Their presence is felt more than seen, a deep, subsonic rumble that vibrates through the ground, a constant counterpoint to the howling wind.

All these creatures live under the ever-present gaze of the **Sky-Watchers**, immense, immobile beings that perch on the highest peaks of the living mountains. They possess huge, receptive dishes that track the complex weather patterns, their discordant songs echoing for miles, a constant background hum to life in this strange, breathing world. No one knows their true nature or purpose, but they are as much a part of the landscape as the living mountains themselves.

These creatures, each shaped by and shaping their environment, exist in a delicate balance. Their lives are a testament to the enduring power of life, thriving in a world of scale and sparseness, dwarfed by the immensity of the living mountains and the vast, empty sky above.
