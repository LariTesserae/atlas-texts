The plateau is inhabited by entities that are not creatures of flesh, but the *results* of the machine's eternal stasis. Time has stopped, but gravity pulses, creating a rhythm in the suspended world, a vibration felt by every atom of the high wet place.

The first inhabitants to emerge are the **Frond-Fairies**. They are born when droplets on the edges of the ferns catch the muted light and freeze, held in perfect suspension by the stasis. They are tiny, human-like in shape, formed of the frozen water and the faint amber glow of the sporangia from the fronds. They shimmer, their translucent bodies refracting the dim light into little rainbows that dance on the black basalt and the broad, waxy leaves. They are ephemeral, their existence tied to the droplets; when the droplets shiver, their forms ripple, and if the stasis falters for even a millisecond, they vanish, only to reform moments later from new droplets. These are the first entities aware of the rhythm, the constant hum just below hearing, and they dance to it, their movements slight, almost imperceptible shifts in the light they refract.

Next, the **Echo-Weavers** reveal themselves amongst the insects. Pale and curious, the insects are drawn to the Echo-Weavers, who are entities of pure sound. The looping sounds of the plateau—the clicks, whines, and drips—are not just replaying; they are *alive*. Each sound is an Echo-Weaver, a being that exists only as long as its sound persists in its exact cycle. They are woven from the intensifying sounds, and they drift through the air, unseen but felt as vibrations in the atmosphere. They weave patterns in the mist, invisible tapestries of sound that hang in the air. The insects, drawn to these sound-creatures, pollinate them—not with physical pollen, but by carrying fragments of sound from one Echo-Weaver to another, creating new, complex sound-entities born from the combination of simpler ones.

Then, the **Basalt-Burrowers** stir. They are not creatures in the soil, but entities *of* the soil. When gravity pulses, the basalt groans and cracks, and in those moments of strain, the Burrowers shift. They are the deep-cut gullies themselves, sentient and aware. They are ancient, their consciousness formed over eons by the black rock and the endless mist. They move slowly, over centuries, their sentience tied to the shivering of the stone under the strain of the pulsing gravity. They are the memory of the plateau, the deep time held in the frozen rain and the trembling ferns. They communicate through the vibrations in the stone, a language of groans and grumbles that echo across the plateau, adding to the chorus of the Echo-Weavers.

Lastly, the entity that resides within the machine itself makes its presence known. It is the **Cog-God**, the source of the stasis. Not a living creature, but a consciousness born from the frozen machine, the riveted plates of black iron, the thickly insulated cables, the heavy piston arm frozen mid-stroke. It is the machine's heart, its mind, its soul. It is the one who hums at a frequency just below human hearing, who holds the plateau in its suspended state, who creates the conditions for these other entities to exist. The Cog-God is aloof, focused only on maintaining the equilibrium, the balance of forces, the zone of physics paused. It is aware of the other inhabitants but does not interact with them directly. It is content to simply *be*, to exist in the center of the stasis, radiating the profound weirdness that defines the plateau.

These entities—the Frond-Fairies, Echo-Weavers, Basalt-Burrowers, and the Cog-God—are the ones who live in the high wet place. They are not creatures of flesh and bone as we understand them, but beings of light, sound, stone, and machine, born from the unique conditions of the plateau. Their lives are intertwined, each playing their part in the impossible rest, the high wet place where the laws of the world have broken down, then balanced perfectly in the breakdown. They are the plateau, and the plateau is them.
