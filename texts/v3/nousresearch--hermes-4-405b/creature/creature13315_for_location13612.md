Here, water is the architect and the inhabitant, the sculptor and the sculpture. It is an entity that has devoured the city, and now wears its stones like heavy, waterlogged skin. This water-being is not singular or sentient in any animal sense, but is rather a vast, diffuse intelligence inhabiting the moisture itself—a slow, patient mind that thinks in terms of eras and epochs. It seeps into every crack and cranny, its presence absolute and total, a liquid consciousness permeating the sunken metropolis.

The stone itself is alive, transmuting under the water’s influence—flowing, dripping, and reforming in a slow, geological dance. Curtains of oily drips cascade from eroded sills, each drop heavy with mineral life. Pale, anaemic mosses and lichens cling to the surfaces, sustained not by sunlight but by the stone’s own wet emanations—a symbiosis of stone, water, and half-dead vegetation.

In the murky shallows, where drowned towers lean at precarious angles, unknown processes are taking place. Water solidifies, calcifying into grotesque new formations—stone fangs protruding from the muck, arches weeping streaks of glinting wetness that merge into the marsh. This is a place of inscrutable alchemy, a workshop of slow transmutations, where inorganic matter blurs into a semblance of life.

Invisible yet palpable, the marsh exudes a low, resonant hum—a vibration that permeates everything. It is the sound of many stones vibrating together, a secret frequency that binds the whole edifice into a kind of immense, diffuse organism. The hum unites the disparate elements, from the smallest droplet to the most massive ruin, into a singular, breathing entity.

At the heart of this humid dark, the central spire stands—a great black tower pierced by a single window, glowing with an eerie, cold light. It is a dark germ in the city's core, a nexus of cold illumination that bleeds out through the cracks of the drowning world. This tower is both womb and tomb, a place where the transmutations reach their zenith, where something unknown is being patiently cultivated. The faint, cold light illuminates the near-tideless water lapping at its base—a generative and terminating force, a symbol of cyclical creation and destruction.

The very air is a character—a thick, wet veil, a saturated atmosphere that hangs stagnant and palpably heavy, steeped in the essence of the water-being. It presses in on everything like a warm washcloth, a constant reminder of the dominant presence here. Even the light is a prisoner here—straining through the wet veil, it is thin and spectral, feeding the shadows rather than dispelling them.

And everywhere, the distinction between inorganic and ‘living’ breaks down. Stone sweats into marsh, wetness ripples into new formations, and the entire landscape is a testament to processes that defy easy categorization. Events transpire constantly—countless small exchanges and transformations—a reminder that here, in this half-drowned necropolis, the world is not dead, but endlessly, inscrutably alive.
