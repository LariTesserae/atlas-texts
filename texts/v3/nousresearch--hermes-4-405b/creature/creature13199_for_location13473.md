In this clearing, beneath the dripping shadows and the oppressive embrace of the jungle, dwell beings that are as much a part of the place as the old brick and the insatiable vines. They are not simply inhabitants—they are expressions of the clearing itself, embodiments of its essence, its perpetual struggle, and its twisted, fecund beauty.

These beings are not easily defined; they are a combination of human and something else, something ancient and elemental, born from the friction between the rigid geometry of the settlement and the unstoppable encroachment of the rainforest. They are the caretakers of the crumbling structures, the shepherds of the industrial machines that still sputter and sigh in the half-light, and the guardians of the countless vines and carnivorous blossoms. Yet they are also the Mathematicians of the place—the ones who understand and manipulate the alien geometries that shimmer within every brick, every drop of stagnant water, and every shadow.

Their forms are not fixed; they shift and morph in response to the environment, sometimes resembling humanoid figures draped in moss and vines, their eyes glowing with a strange, inner light that is not light at all. At other times, they appear as fluid, amorphous entities, their bodies composed of the same creeping tendrils and vibrant, unearthly blossoms that dominate the clearing. They are as much a product of the jungle as they are of whatever humanity has wrought in this place.

These beings are the keepers of the clearing’s essence—the sweltering darkness, the oppressive plant-life, and the endless, damp murmurs that fill the air. They move with a purpose that is not entirely their own, guided by the rhythms of the jungle and the slow, inexorable pulse of the industrial outpost. They are in constant motion, tending to the machines, directing the growth of the vines, and performing strange, intricate rituals that seem to blur the line between science and magic, between the laws of nature and the laws of some other, stranger place.

To these beings, the clearing is not merely a home—it is a crucible, a laboratory, and a battleground. It is a place where reality itself is stretched thin, where the rules that govern the rest of the world falter under the weight of emergent life. And they are the agents of that transformation, the ones who shepherd this fragile, precarious balance between order and chaos, between human ambition and the reckless fecundity of the jungle.

They are the Mathematicians of the clearing, the ones who understand the alien geometries that underpin this impossible place. They are the ones who ensure that the clearing endures, that it continues to be a space of negative definition, an expanse where emptiness itself is the dominant texture. And they are the ones who, in their own inscrutable way, call this place home.
