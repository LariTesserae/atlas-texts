The inhabitants of this place are beings defined by relentless, Sisyphean labor. Twisted, gnarled creatures with rough, weathered skin, punctuated by bulging, vein-riddled muscles rippling beneath, their bodies speak of perpetual, exhausting effort. Leathery hands end in broad, flattened digits, their palms hardened into a substance resembling cracked stone, tough as the materials they haul, though occasionally split and bleeding from the unceasing work. Their legs are surprisingly nimble despite being thick and trunk-like, navigating the treacherous mud and uneven rubble with practiced ease.

An aura of ancient grime clings to them, a deep-seated patina of mineral dust and dried, re-wetted mud that has become a permanent secondary skin. Their forms seem almost geologic, stumpy and broad, fashioned from the same rough clay, the same unyielding rock as their surroundings, baked hard by the ever-present warm humidity of the air. They move with a heavy, rolling gait, each step a small negotiation with the uncertain ground. These are the Rok Streng, the perpetually laboring people of the everlasting roar.

Their faces are blunt, their broad features conveying little beyond the immediate need to complete the next task. Small, deep-set eyes, dark, bright, and darting, reflect the murky light, their pupils constantly scanning for the next stone to shift, the next timber to place, the next gap in the ever-crumbling walls to patch. They wear simple, crude garments – rough-spun tunics and trousers fashioned from beaten fibers, perpetually stained, often repaired with patches that themselves then become stained. Wide-brimmed, conical hats woven from coarse reeds or thin slats of shaved wood offer scant protection from the omnipresent, dripping moisture.

When speaking, their voices are sharp, guttural barks, carrying clipped, functional phrases that cut through the drone just enough for the intended recipient – usually a work partner – to discern the meaning. There is no poetry, no song, no laughter, only the utilitarian exchange of information necessary to coordinate their tasks.

These beings are not lone workers; they are accompanied by a host of strange, specialized creatures, symbionts in this eternal task. Rok Streng are often seen saddled with a Strider, a bizarre, six-legged creature with a broad, shell-like back covered in overlapping, chitinous plates. The creature's legs end in wide, splayed, disc-like feet, perfect for distributing its weight – and that of its Rok Streng riders and their burdens – across the uncertain mud and pooled water. Their many limbs move with a constant, chittering sound, a series of sharp, grinding clicks as plates rub and joints articulate, adding a complex, almost crystalline layer to the pervasive drone.

Teams of Rok Streng work, hoisting freshly quarried slabs. These slabs are quarried from the earth by huge, grub-like creatures known as Chompers, who gnaw and grind at the bedrock, their progress marked by a wet, tearing sound as their unseen maws work beneath the surface, their segmented bodies occasionally writhing above ground, glistening slick and pale. Chompers consume the rock and, after some internal process, spew forth warm, wet slurry which cools rapidly, forming the rough, but largely consistent, blocks the Rok Streng use for their construction. This spew is accompanied by a gurgling, sloshing, retching sound, a constant undertone to the higher grinding of their gnawing. The Chompers themselves are blind, guided by scent, by the taste of the minerals they crave, or perhaps by some rudimentary communication from the Rok Streng.

Rok Streng children, smaller, more agile versions of their elders, clamber over the tumbled rocks, their movements quick and skittering, their voices high-pitched, bird-like chirps as they call to each other or shout warnings of impending shifts in the unstable landscape. They learn the paths, the dangers, the art of avoidance and the beginnings of the building craft from a young age, their play an unwitting preparation for their future labors.

Hovering above, flitting through the dense, noisy air, are Cloud-Eaters, winged, hairless creatures the size of large rodents. Their eyes are small, almost vestigial, as they navigate by echolocation, quick, sharp clicks and chirps lost amidst the greater sound, allowing them to sense their way through the perpetual dim. Their mouths are wide, constantly agape to scoop up the thick, moisture-laden airborne particles and the tiny, drifting mineral fragments, filtering sustenance from the very air. At dusk and dawn, great clouds of them would descend to puddles to drink, adding their massed, rustling chitterings to the soundscape.

Despite the constant labor, there are moments of low-level conflict. Scavenger birds with oily black feathers and sharp, curved beaks incessantly swoop down upon unattended piles of offal and waste, fighting amongst themselves with harsh, cawing cries. Feral dogs, lean and mangy, their coats matted with mud and filth, slink through the alleys, growling and snapping over rotting food scraps, their barks quick, sharp, and echoing sharply off the stone. This extends even to the younger Rok Streng, who sometimes engage in brief, sharp disputes, their voices rising in piercing shrieks over some contested piece of material, though these altercations are usually quelled quickly by an older, authoritative voice or a thrown clod of mud.

Rok Streng occasionally tether livestock – squat, broad-shouldered, hairless beasts with knobbly hides, kept for meat, milk, and hides – outside their crude dwellings. These beasts add a low, discontented, constant groaning and a wet, snuffling sound as they root amongst the mud to the cacophony. Everywhere, the air is thick with the varied scents of life and labor: sweat, wet earth, smoky fires, the musky, cloying scent of the Striders, the sharp, acrid scent of freshly dug earth and crushed rock, the rich, metallic tang of rain-washed minerals, and the unmistakable, ever-present stench of decay from things buried shallow or dropped in corners.
