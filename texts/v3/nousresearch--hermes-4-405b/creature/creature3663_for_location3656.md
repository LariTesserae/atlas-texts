The inhabitants of this endless metal basin could best be described as the unflinching, rust-blooded Children of the Giant Ceiling. They are a seething, silent, ever-present tide—their sheer numbers forming a thick, living veneer of ochre and glistening chrome over every rivet and water-slicked surface of this constructed world.

The dominant lifeforms are the megaworms, behemoth rust-colored flatworms as large as anacondas, their bodies segmented and seemingly composed of overlapping plates of tarnished metal. These colossal worms move with an eerie, silent fluidity, gliding over the weeping metal landscapes on an unseen film of mucous. With slow, deliberate undulations, they suction themselves to the iron plains, methodically grazing on the thin, rusty rain condensing constantly on every surface, or gnawing at the delicate, pale, salt-like crystal blooms that frost the edges of the rivulets—the only break from the world's overwhelming metallic rigidity.

Smaller kin to these megaworms are everywhere, from fist-sized, minnow-like versions that dart through the shallow, sheet-metal basins and innumerable rivulets, to fingernail-sized glints that shimmer in every puddle, all involved in a constant, silent consumption of condensation and mineral efflorescences. Microscopic versions, perhaps, are the unseen plankton of this iron sea, existing as motes of rust-blooded life within the perpetual damp.

Sharing this saturated world are living drill-bits, smaller insectile beings resembling miniature chrome augers. Tens of thousands of them skitter across the durasteel and sheet iron plains, moving in vast, coordinated herds. Each drill-bit possesses a set of spinning, ultra-sharp mandibles at its fore and tiny, multifaceted crimson eyes. They swarm over the landscape, feeding on minuscule flecks of metal and mineral deposits. Their passage is a low, constant scraping sound against the groans of the idle machines, a sound almost lost in the perpetual hum.

Above, adrift in the warm, humid air between the Giant Ceiling and the ground, slow, bulbous zeppelin-floaters patrol. These are living blimps, their bodies composed of rust-colored, semi-translucent gas sacs that swell and contract with a slow, rhythmic breath. They drift at the leisurely pace of continental clouds, trailing long, gelatinous, translucent feeding tubes. These tubes dangle into drainages and sump pools, siphoning the constant condensation and the microscopic life it carries. The largest of these majestic floaters stretch hundreds of feet from snout to the end of their trailing tendrils. They move with an imperceptible grace, riding unseen thermal currents, their forms casting vast, amorphous, and ever-shifting shadows over the landscape below. The mammoth creatures navigate the canyons of the giant, static machines as if those sleeping behemoths were just more inert terrain.

All these creatures share one profound trait: an apparent agelessness. They never seem to die or visibly decay in the conventional sense. Their vast numbers simply exist in a state of constant, dynamic equilibrium. The megaworms glide in endless, unchanging circular routes; the drill-bits skitter in patterns that never deviate; the floaters drift in lazy, fixed circuits. They swim, crawl, and fly through this mechanical purgatory in unyielding, predictable paths. Their populations teem, churn, and replenish, forming an ever-moving, crimson and chrome tide that shifts in ceaseless, silent patterns without ever disturbing the equilibrium of the machines or the iron world. Each of these beings is a permanent, essential, and unchanging component of this vast, warm, wet, and eternally idling engine of a world.
