The inhabitants of Hraza-Lor, a testament to human audacity and the will to survive in the face of nature's harshest laws, mirror the city's stark blend of brutality and ingenuity. They are the Kethra, a people whose very physiology and culture have been shaped by eking out an existence on the edge of oblivion.

Physically, the Kethra are to a large degree a response to their environment. Generations of living in a city where space is a premium have made them lithe and slender, their sinews conditioned for navigating narrow gantries and climbing the sheer surfaces that connect the myriad levels of their home. Their hands display an unusual articulation and grip strength, adept at manipulating the tools and equipment intrinsic to maintaining their vertical sanctuary and thriving in the gardens that provide sustenance. Their eyes are sharp, adapted to the oftentimes gloomy interiors of Hraza-Lor, yet they do not shun the bright lamps and artificial sunlight of the city's greenhouses.

Their attire is both practical and reflective of their society's values. Layered with tightly-woven fabrics that retain warmth and repel the constant moisture and industrial grime, their clothes are typically in shades of grey and rust, echoing the palette of their city. Durability is paramount; garments are designed with a multitude of utility pockets and reinforced seams, often patched and repurposed over time. Broad belts, built to accommodate tools, are ubiquitous – essential for a populace whose existence is entwined with maintaining the labyrinthine infrastructure that sustains them.

Yet in contrast to this utilitarianism, the Kethra exhibit a commendable cultural trait in adorning their clothing with intricate patterns, often stitched by hand. These patterns serve as a social and familial tapestry, chronicling lineages, affiliations within the city’s specialized guilds, and personal milestones. They weave their individual narratives and communal pride into their very appearance, highlighting a surprising aesthetic amidst the industrial severity.

Culturally, the Kethra are pragmatic above all else. Their life revolves around the continuous and meticulous upkeep of the city’s systems. They live by strict schedules, influenced by the perpetual artificial light cycle and the rhythmic pulse of the machines. Social structures are primarily framed around guilds – engineers, hydro-farmers, maintenance crews, miners – each responsible for the efficient operation of Hraza-Lor's vital components. Status is directly correlated with one's contribution to the broader community, fostering a sense of collective identity and responsibility. This shared purpose also manifests in communal meals and social gatherings where stories of infamous repairs, near-disasters, and hard-won innovations are told and retold, strengthening the bonds that ensure the city's survival.

While the Kethra are defined by their industriousness, that is not to say there is no room for leisure. Small public parks, often built within repurposed sectors, serve as communal areas where residents can momentarily escape the unrelenting sounds and demands of their industrious life. Individual balconies are often adorned with potted plants, personal hydroponic setups, or small-scale craft projects, providing a private niche for reflection. Gaming, with its focus on strategy and intellect rather than physical dominance, is particularly esteemed; complex board games or intricate puzzles are frequently played in their shared spaces.

A distinct aspect of the Kethra's culture is their veneration of foresight and precision exhibited by their ancestors. Those who first conceived and executed the original fortifications of Hraza-Lor, as well as subsequent generations who expanded and refined it, are held in the highest esteem. Design schematics of historical significance are sometimes displayed like art, and the ability to grasp the city’s complex systems or come up with innovative solutions is considered a hallmark of intelligence and wisdom.

As with all societies, there are those who rail against conformity. Within a culture that values collective effort so intensely, there are individuals who, either from strong aversion or simple temperament, drift to the periphery of Hraza-Lor’s societal structures. Some embrace a solitary existence as maintainers of the most remote and desolate sections of the city; others become artisans, repurposing discarded scrap into objects of adornment or utility; and a select few may abandon Hraza-Lor entirely, seeking an existence elsewhere – however perilous that may be.

The Kethra’s relationship with their environment is intimate yet adversarial. They construct their gardens and nurture life in the midst of industrial harshness, treating their water and warmth sources with an almost religious reverence. Every engineered component is imbued with a story of survival and ingenuity, and waste is anathema. They view their existence in Hraza-Lor as a testament to their antecedents' will to carve out a sanctuary amidst the clouded peaks – a defiant, humanity-driven affront to a world that may otherwise have forgotten them. The very act of living there is their identity, their home a vertical bulwark against the untamed wilds, reinforcing in their spirit a fierce independence and a resilience that defines the very essence of what it means to be Kethra. Their continued existence in this extraordinary place is the ultimate narrative of human ingenuity and the unyielding will to prevail.
