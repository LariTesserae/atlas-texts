To inhabit such a place - the Flooded Precinct, suspended on the abyssal flank, carved from the seamless polished obsidian, lit only by its internal phosphorescence - is to become an intrinsic part of its equilibrium. Not in the sense of being a visitor, or even a resident, but as a living manifestation of its physics: a being shaped by the perfection of that stillness, and the geometry of absolute dark.

What lives here, then, must be a creature of the stone and the cold blue luminance. Each specimen is formed of segments that, held up to the light, reveal the deep, elegant crystal structure of the obsidian-like material that makes up the entire city, suggesting they are a living, organic extension of the architecture. Their skin or carapace is polished smooth and hard, as seamless as the structures they inhabit, yet flexible at their joints. The beings are neither large nor small, simply perfectly scaled to the dimensions of the precinct, as if they were cast from the same geometric principles. 

The beings move through the black world with movements so slow that they are practically imperceptible—a slowness that echoes the slow deposit of mineral on the obsidian surfaces, a languid dance only matched by the drifted shadows of deep-sea giants that sometimes pass outside. When they pause, they become like statues, stalagmites of obsidian in the frozen moment of creation. They do not breathe in any conventional sense, but a slow, subtle pulsation of their crystalline skin suggests a form of internal respiration, almost like the rhythm of the faint, subaqueous light that annotates the darkness around them.

They see, perhaps, not with eyes but with their entire body, attuned to the almost imperceptible blue light and to variations in pressure or vibration in the water; living barometers in the heart of this watery cathedral, aware of its every quiver and sigh. Yet, despite the stillness in which they exist, they do sense and respond — to the mineral perfume breathing through the water, and to the presence of each other.

Communication, if it exists, occurs at a timescale so elongated that it would be meaningless to any observer, a silent language of the stone. Perhaps it is a slow dance of positioning and re-positioning among the angular courts, a shared languor absorbing the eternity of silence, a choreography governed by the slow pulse of the same phosphorescence swimming up through them and through the structures that give them form, as if sharing a single, dreaming breath.

They have no use for fast movements or aggressive interactions because there is nothing to hunt in the desolate expanse and nothing to flee. They are part of the quiet perfection of the built environment, lives defined by unsmiling vigilance, a meditative stillness that holds forever all that ever is.

And so, the inhabitants and the Flooded Precinct itself exist in a symbiotic stillness—a balanced world where physics achieves a perfect form. The beings guard the blue-lit night in its unending stability, each a deep, satisfied hum in the cold harmony of the dark, a part of the eternity itself. They are not intruders, not visitors, just an extension of the obsidian-like stone, a living symbol of the timeless repose—the watchmen of the abyssal equilibrium.
