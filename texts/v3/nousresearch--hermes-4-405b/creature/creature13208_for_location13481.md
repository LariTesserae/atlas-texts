From the luminous moss and steaming fissures, from burrows lined with metallic feathers and pools of shimmering, electric mud, they emerge. They are not a single people or species, but a riot of life—a spectrum of beings evolving too rapidly to settle into fixed forms, each adapted to thrive in a corner of this unstable world only to be remade when the ground shifts beneath them.

Some are pale, lithe creatures, almost humanoid in form but composed of glassy, translucent flesh shimmering with internal bioluminescence. They step lightly over shifting scree slopes, their toes splaying to grip the ever-moving earth, their slender fingers plucking iridescent beetles from the air to sustain their delicate, ever-regenerating metabolisms. They communicate with soft, choral humming and with the flashing of intricate patterns across their skin, sending shifting maps of light through the dim air to kinfolk clinging to distant cliffsides.

Others move through the dense, fleshy undergrowth and over steaming mudflats—humped, segmented beings with carapaces like smoky quartz. Their limbs terminate in clusters of flexible tendrils, some tipped with grinding rasps to harvest minerals from crystalline outcrops, others scoop the rich, mutating soil into mouths hidden beneath their shells. They trundle in communal lines, leaving trails of chemical signals that shimmer in the humid air, coordinating the endless construction of burrows that must be constantly re-dug as the land shifts. Within their armor, unseen bodies pulse with radiant heat, their life processes generating such warmth that steam rises from their joints when they rest.

In the deepest crevasses and beneath the translucent leaves of house-sized ferns lurk predators shaped by fierce, unstable niches. One such beast has a long, flexible neck supporting a head that is little more than a circular maw lined with crystalline teeth. Below this, a slug-like body, amorphous and glistening, propels it with undulating muscles, leaving a trail of viscous, faintly glowing slime. Its prey are the skittering arthropod-things that dart over every surface, and the great beast moves with deceptive speed, slamming its maw shut like a bear trap whenever it senses vibrations through the ground.

High above the predatory slugs and the rest of the crawling, digging, creeping life, the sky-jellies drift on thermals. These are vast, iridescent sacs filled with lighter-than-air gases, trailing clusters of stinging filaments that can paralyze the translucent, multi-winged fliers that flit between the floating islands of vegetation. The jellies are colonial beings, clusters of individuals merged into a collective, dirigible form—a living, drifting ecosystem. They pulse with soft light, absorbing the weak, scattered sunlight filtered through the pervasive fog, and when they die, their deflated bodies rain down onto the peaks, becoming fuel and fertilizer for the organisms that scavenge below.

Some of the inhabitants are barely corporeal at all—swirling vortexes of prismatic energy that dance along the cliff-edges where gravity weakens, or whispering entities of pure sound that echo from crevasse to crevasse, weaving ephemeral structures from solidified resonance. These beings seem less like life as we know it and more like manifestations of the broken physics that govern this place, temporary vortices of possibility given voice.

All these beings share a common trait: an existence predicated on adaptability, mutation, and constant change. Their forms flicker and shift—subtle alterations in color, texture, and even body structure occur as they move from one micro-environment to another. Their biology is hyper-accelerated, genes rewriting themselves in response to unseen cues. They live brief, intense lives, but their lineages are a tapestry of continuous, turbulent transformation—a living testament to the chaotic, fertile power surging beneath the newborn land's skin. The unnatural energy in the air permeates their flesh, drives their metabolisms, and twists their evolution into impossible, beautiful shapes, making them as much expressions of the land's will as creatures in their own right. They are the first tenants and the raw material of an unfinished world.
