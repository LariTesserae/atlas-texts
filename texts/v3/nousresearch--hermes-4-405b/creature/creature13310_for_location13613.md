This desolate, high plateau under a starless sky harbors a singular, colossal organism: an alien vegetable life that has overrun and transmogrified everything, its unbroken blanket of growth forming the very architecture of this miniature world. The primordial hyper-forest grips deep with immense roots, its slow-pulsing biology thrumming subsonically beneath a surface of cathedral-like structures—interlaced stems and trunks that form battlements, vaulted roofs, and corridors. Yet this ancient life is constantly churned in an industrial hellscape of its own making: autonomous machines, engines whining, grinders biting into the hot, living planks and beams, conveyor belts shifting chunks of freshly harvested timber toward processing zones alight with frantic sparks and electrified processes. The vegetation writhes slowly with this violation—sap boiling away, new growth surging back in an endless, storm-state cycle of consumption, regeneration, deconstruction, and creation. Though machines devour and rebuild relentlessly—foundries melting down plant-flesh, belts hauling limbs toward chippers roaring in hot darkness—the vast scale of this world swallows their roars into a brooding quiet, a silent bazaar teeming with anthill energy beneath a dark, hot sky. The only constants are the subsonic thrum of the forest’s deep biology and the relentless, chaotic change wrought by its own embedded industry. It is a landscape of near-absolute darkness, its edges sharp against the gloom, its light—dim emergency strobes, distant furnaces, flashes of electrified processes—flickering upon the hot, fused bones of its hard, hot, and sheer expanse, where trace gases sustain a life ceaselessly remade.
