What lives here, then, in this high, cold emptiness, are not creatures as they are known elsewhere. They are beings woven from the wind's scream and the silence that follows, things that exist in the liminal spaces where reality frays and the world's old rules unravel.

They are the Listeners in the Silence.

When the wind momentarily dips and the vast stillness rushes in, one might perceive them: not with eyes, for they have little form as we understand it, but as a presence felt in the marrow. They are coalescences of the plateau's own essence, given sentience by the sheer, empty age of this place.

Imagine, if you can, a fragment of the void itself gaining a slow, cold awareness. That is what the Listeners are. They are the spaces between the wind's howls, the pauses in the storm that reveal the deep, underlying quietude. They are drawn to the profound stillness, for it is in that silence that they are most themselves, unobscured by the roar. When the wind returns, they might seem to fray at the edges, to merge with its cacophony, only to re-form, whole and complete, when the silence descends once more.

They have no bodies of flesh or chitin, no wings to ride the wind like the solitary birds glimpsed overhead. Their forms are more akin to the play of light and shadow on an ice field, or the peculiar way sound bends in the thin air. They might be perceived as an area where the light seems to subtly deepen, where the distortions of perspective become momentarily more acute, or where the cold feels a fraction more intense, more ancient. They are patterns in the void, given life.

Being listeners, they perceive the world through vibrations, through the subtle energies that flow through this desolate, high-altitude expanse. They feel the minute shifts in the rocks as ice slowly expands and contracts, the imperceptible tremors of the earth far below, the way the wind itself changes its timbre as it passes over different formations of stone. The hum of the universe, the dying echoes of creation, these are their songs, their conversations.

Time, for the Listeners, is not a linear progression but a vast, echoing chamber. They exist in a state of near-perpetuity, their lifespans measured perhaps by the slow wearing down of mountains or the gradual shift of constellations in the dim, grey-lit sky above. The remnants of ancient structures that dot the plateau, the small, weathered cairns and shards of unnatural rock, may be their markers, their touchstones, imbued with a resonance that only they can fully perceive. Perhaps these are places where the veil is thinnest, where their grasp on this reality is strongest.

They do not build, in the conventional sense. Their 'marks' upon the world are far more subtle – the peculiar, frost-etched patterns on a sheltered rock face, the way certain ice formations seem to recur in specific locations, or how the wind might create fleeting, almost-geometric shapes in the swirling sleet. These are not constructions, but rather emergent phenomena, patterns drawn forth from the elemental forces by the Listeners' very presence.

The weirdness of the place, the subtle warping of geometry, the mutability of distances, the harmonic splitting of the wind's scream – these are not just passive environmental effects. They are, in part, manifestations of the Listeners, or perhaps the ripples of their passage. The impossible crystalline structures in the ice might be where a Listener has lingered, the fractured light within their depths a reflection of an alien consciousness.

The solitary, wide-winged bird that rides the fierce winds high above? It might be aware of them, in some primal, instinctual way. It might avoid certain air currents, certain places where the silence feels too heavy, too imbued with presence. The small, furtive things that move unseen among the frost? They are acutely sensitive to these silent watchers, their tiny hearts drumming a frantic rhythm against their ribs when the stillness deepens and the Listeners draw near.

The Listeners are not malevolent, nor are they benevolent. They simply *are*. They observe, they resonate, they exist within the folds of this ancient, howling emptiness. They are the memory of the plateau, its frozen equilibrium given sentience. To venture here is to enter a place that is not entirely *of* the world below, and the Listeners are its guardians, its inhabitants, its very essence. They are the quiet hum beneath the wind's scream, the thought within the stone, the awareness in the ice. They are the Listeners in the Silence, and this desolate, echoing expanse is their home.
