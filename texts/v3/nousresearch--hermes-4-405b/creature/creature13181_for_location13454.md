In this strange, liquid world, the kelp forests and ribbon-leafed alpine fields are not merely passive vegetation. They are colonies of a vast, interconnected, sessile organism - a planet-spanning macrophyte creature, its root structure twining deep into the submerged mountain rock. Individual fronds may die or be torn away by the currents, but the organism as a whole persists, ancient and sprawling. This is the source of the planet's "breath", a biological chorus: billions of tiny pores along the fronds and stems constantly releasing excess gases, a gaseous exhalation carried by the liquid currents, merging into a pervasive, low-frequency drone that permeates the aquatic atmosphere.

There are no animals in the sense one might expect, no free-swimming creatures navigating the ocean-sky or crawling on the seafloor. Instead, the prime sentience, the one responsible for the pale stone domes perched on the mountaintops, belongs to a different order of being entirely. They are the "Silvertides," vast, diffuse entities that exist as self-organized patterns of denser, silvery-viscous fluid, like oil clinging to itself in an ocean. When "concentrated" or "resting", they pool in the deepest, shadowed valleys, their forms slow, dark, and viscous, their light-absorbing qualities creating those regions of deceptive gloom and glittering, glass-dust sediment where their unique fluid-physics allow particles to settle in uncanny ways. They are not entirely separate from the environment; their silvery essence is merely a more organized, concentrated form of a trace element or peculiar molecule suspended throughout the planet's ocean-atmosphere, which gives the water its pearlescent sheen. In their quiescent state, they are the valleys' shadows and the water's shimmer, a dormant, planet-sized potential.

To move, to act, to *build*, the Silvertides don't swim or crawl. They *propagate* as coherent waves of themselves through the regular water. They ripple through the liquid atmosphere, their silvery essence concentrating and then dispersing, a wave of intelligence and physical presence that doesn't travel *through* the water so much as it temporarily reconfigures a section *of* it. It is through this wave-like extension that they shape the stone domes on the mountain shoulders. They don't use tools in the conventional sense. The "construction" is a result of their collective, fluid intelligence focusing their unique properties – perhaps manipulating pressure, subtle sonic frequencies from their passage that vibrate loose rock, or even some form of localized chemical dissolution and recrystallization. This patient, wave-form effort nudges and melds the pale stones over untold time. The structures are not shelters or dwellings; they are markers, nodes, perhaps resonators or focal points for a consciousness that is intrinsically part of the environment itself, solid evidence of their fluid presence and slow, deliberate thought.
