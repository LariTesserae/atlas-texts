The inhabitants of this submerged, embryonic city are beings of slow patience and deep, resonant connection to their environment. They are not built for speed or frantic activity, but for endurance and deliberate, almost geological processes of creation and adaptation.

Physically, they are often large, somewhat amorphous beings, their forms perhaps not fixed but subtly shifting, mirroring the slow dance of formation and dissolution around them. Their bodies might be composed of materials akin to the coral-stuff, the living rock, and the phosphorescent growths that constitute their dwellings – a chitinous integument over a more malleable, gel-like interior, allowing them to squeeze through tight crevices or endure the immense pressures. They move with a ponderous, deliberate grace, their limbs – if such discrete appendages exist – might be broad and paddle-like, or numerous and prehensile, like the roots of great, submerged trees, to steady themselves against slow currents and manipulate their primitive tools. Their eyes, if they possess them, are likely large, adapted to the dim, phosphorescent light, capable of perceiving the subtle shifts in the aqueous medium, the tiny variations in pressure, and the faintest glimmers of bioluminescence.

Their senses would be attuned to vibrations in the water, the thrum of the deep, the water's internal energies, and the stirrings of other creatures. Perhaps they communicate through slow modulations of bioluminescent patterns on their skin or the surfaces of their dwelling-structures, a language of shifting light and color, or through low-frequency vibrations, a subterranean song that travels far through the dense water. Their lives are measured in the growth rings of coral, the slow settling of sediment; generations might be born, live, and pass within the timeframe of a single, significant geologic event.

They would be cultivators rather than hunters, coaxing sustenance and material from the living rock, the symbiotic organisms that form their city, and the endless particles suspended in the water. Their technology is an extension of their biological being and the city's organic nature – tools grown rather than fashioned, harnessing slow currents and pressures, perhaps even the subtle energies of the water itself. Structures are not so much 'built' as encouraged to grow, a living architecture in constant, slow flux.

They are beings of immense resilience and quiet observation, attuned to the rhythms of a world in perpetual, slow-motion creation. Their culture, if it could be called such, would be one of profound integration with their environment, their art perhaps the city itself, ever-forming, ever-dissolving, a testament to the enduring power of life to find a foothold, to dream and breathe, even beneath an eternal, lukewarm sea. They are the stewards of this nascent, underwater world, living embodiments of patience and the slow, inexorable forces that shape worlds.
