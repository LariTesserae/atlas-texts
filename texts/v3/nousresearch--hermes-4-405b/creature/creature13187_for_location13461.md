There is something alive here in the wet shadows of the great stones. It is found in the cool, moist air that condenses in the hollowed ring; in the dripping, seeping water; and in the dim gray light filtering through cracks in the stone. At first, one might think that these beings are simply mosses or lichens, adorning the ancient megaliths with their deep greens, purples, and browns, sucking up the slow-water secret. But they are something more.

The inhabitants of this wet-stone ring are not shy; they are everywhere. They cling to the secret wet walls, clustered in patches of damp darkness. They are minuscule, like living mist--great clouds or swarms of beings so small and ephemeral that not one can be seen, only felt. They are not quite plankton, nor insects; they are instead a shifting fog of life, a living, teeming mass that exists in the humid air, in the rolling shadows of the giant megaliths.

These beings are soft-bodied and fragile, almost shapeless, barely taking on a form that is longer or wider than the head of a pin. Their existence is short-lived, their life cycle one of frenzied pulses of life and death. They appear almost to *rain down* from above, dying in startling numbers and collecting in pools around the bases of the stones. Most scuttle or writhe weakly along the dripping surfaces, feeding on the minute organic matter produced by the sparse, tough plants and dark moss. Some, too, are consumed by their own kind. The droplets of water that collect on the stones are alive with these forms, shimmering with movement.

Their world is constant, and they do not hide. When the air shifts or the light changes, they move: small clouds of them congregate in patches of moss, darkening the lichen. By their shifting, by their teeming, they give the stones a sense of motion, of damp life. The *sound* of the place, the wet hum of the grove, is not produced by any single being, but by all of them together. The stones are alive with the sound of water and the skittering of these beings in the dim, damp air.

The creatures of this hollowed room are always near, within the cold stillness of the stones, the damp and the dark. A vibration in the air, or the drop of a stone from above, or a fresh trickle of water will bring them scrabbling from their hidden places. They vanish into the dark crevices again as quickly as they appeared, leaving no sign of their presence other than the shimmering cloud in which they live. Their life is a constant, almost eternal pulse of the great hollow room.
