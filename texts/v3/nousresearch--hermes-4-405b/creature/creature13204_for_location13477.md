The inhabitants of this living city are ancient, too, their lineage stretching back as far as the primordial trees themselves, though their forms have changed beyond recognition over the millennia. They began as humble creatures, small and skittering, barely distinguishable from the myriad crawling things that inhabit any forest floor. But as the city grew around them, they grew with it, adapting and evolving to fill the niches that opened up between the roots and branches.

The denizen of the city is a composite creature, a being of many parts. At its core is a central nervous system, a pulsing mass of ganglia and neurons that resides in the heart of each major structure. From this core, tendrils of nerve-fiber stretch out to every corner of the city, threading through the bark of the trees, diving deep into the soil, insinuating themselves into every crevice and cranny. These are the eyes and ears of the city, the sensory apparatus that allows it to perceive and react to the world around it.

But the city is not just a single organism. It is a collective, a vast network of interconnected beings, each playing a crucial role in the functioning of the whole. Some tend to the infrastructure, their bodies adapted to shaping and molding the plant matter that forms the streets and bridges. Others maintain the great machines that dot the city, their forms fused with the whirring gears and humming engines, a perfect fusion of biology and technology.

The builders, if they can be called that, are not gone. They are the city, and the city is them. Over the millennia, the distinction between the inhabitants and their environment has blurred and then vanished altogether. They are the pulsing heart of the primordial trees, the skittering creatures that tend the vines and mosses, the thrumming engines that power the factories. They are the rain that falls from the sky, the damp air that hangs heavy between the branches, the very stone that forms the foundation of the plateau.

As the city grows and changes, so too do its inhabitants. New forms emerge from the ever-shifting landscape, strange chimeras born of the fusion of plant, animal, and machine. Some are little more than ambulatory clumps of vegetation, their bodies a riot of leaves and tendrils that creep and crawl across the city's surface. Others are vast, lumbering giants, their forms cobbled together from scraps of metal and slabs of bark, their minds a gestalt consciousness formed from the merger of a thousand skittering creatures.

These inhabitants move through the city with a strange, jerky grace, their bodies constantly shifting and reconfiguring to adapt to the ever-changing environment. They communicate not with words, but with pulses of light and sound, with the rustling of leaves and the groaning of wood, with the crackle of electricity and the thrum of engines.

The city is their world, their universe, a vast, living machine that they nurture and maintain with every fiber of their being. And as the city grows and changes, so too do they, evolving and adapting in an endless dance of creation and destruction, growth and decay. They are the living embodiment of the weirdness that permeates this place, a testament to the endless possibilities of life in all its strange and varied forms.
