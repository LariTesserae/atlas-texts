The denizens of this place are as strange and multifaceted as their environment, shaped and sustained by a seemingly sentient landscape that is both benevolent and suffocating in its embrace.

At the heart of this cathedral-like expanse, where the liquid ground yields most intimately to the rhythm of moving water and rising mists, dwell creatures most attuned to its shifting nature. They are not exactly beings of flesh and bone, but of the very Thing-Substance itself—slow to move, yes, ponderous in fact, yet ever-changing as they inhale and exhale the warm, moss-scented air. Their enormous, segmented bodies glisten with the same seamless quality as the vaulted walls around them, and when the room pulses with the rhythmic hum of its unseen engines, they pulse too, expanding and contracting like living bellows, their surfaces rippling with the slow, purposeful motion of the runnels and the drifting globes. They are ancient, these creatures, their memories etched into every worn groove of the floor, their thoughts expressed in the patterns of condensation and the trembling of the vines. As the runnels wear away the floor, so do their forms wear away and renew, century by century—never dying, never truly alive, but existing in a constant state of becoming. Their presence is a vibration beneath the drone of machinery, a silent heartbeat that underlies the cathedral’s clamor.

The walls of this place, too, are inhabited—by smaller, chittering beings that scurry and flit in the shadowed galleries carved by runnels and intricate folds of Thing-Substance. Their bodies are sleek and quick, each limb ending in a delicate, articulated claw, perfectly adapted for scaling the dew-slick surfaces. They are creatures of impulse and sudden light, drawn to the pinprick sparkle of condensed water and the ephemeral flutter of glowing moths. As they dart among the pillars and sculpted hollows, they leave behind faint, iridescent trails—slime or spoor that glows faintly yellow in the gloom, marking the paths of their ceaseless, skittering dance.

At the periphery, where tangled vines spill from cracks and fissures, other beings lurk. Their forms are armored and unyielding, slow to rise from the deep shadows but deliberate in their motion. Their carapaces resemble the enameled ceramic of the strange bundled pipes, patterned with concentric rings like the red-brown molds that spread in languid circles on the walls. They move with an air of ancient presence, their limbs—jointed in ways no natural creature’s should be—picking careful paths round the steaming vents. Occasionally one will pause beside a thick, wrist-thick flowering vine, reaching out with a delicate, pincer-like appendage to pluck a single luminous bloom or trace the intricate veins that spiral up its length. These armored beings are the gardeners of this place, for their touch makes the vines surge upward with sudden, almost painful swiftness, twisting round the nearest pillar or cracking open yet another fissure in the floor to drink up the trickling water that sustains them.

Other, even stranger forms of life thrive in the drifting mists and glowing globes. Mechanical birds flutter erratically near the hissing vents, their clockwork bodies whirring with a sound like spinning gears. Sparks fly from frayed wires in their intricately articulated wings as they circle and dart, occasionally landing amid the wet moss that carpets the floor. They nest among the roots of the vines, concealed by dark, steam-damp hollows, their movements hidden by the curling mists. These birds seem almost unaware of their own nature—part mechanical, part wild—and when they open their beaks to cry out, it is the same haunting, resonant hum that fills the vaulted air.

Higher still, where the pulsing globes drift lazily overhead and the unseen pistons beat their slow, eternal rhythm, delicate wings flutter among the soft, bioluminescent light. These are the moths—or perhaps something closer to living shreds of the same drifting luminescence—so nearly invisible that one might miss them entirely if not for the faint, ethereal trails they leave in the heavy air. Their wings are thin, almost translucent, and they circle the glowing globes in slow, languid spirals, drawn by an instinct that borders on devotion. Sometimes, when the light shifts suddenly and the harmony of the humming globes changes pitch, one will detach itself from its fellows and flit off into the gloom, circling a pillar or following the erratic path of one of the armored gardeners. Where they go or what they seek remains a mystery, though in their wake the vines seem always to surge with renewed vigor, and the dripping water trickles a little faster down the runnels worn into the floor.

Every living thing here is part of a slow, ancient cycle—a cycle of condensation and decay, growth and dissolution that mirrors the millennia of slow, deliberate erosion that have shaped this place. The cathedral is a living thing, in its way—a strange, symbiotic organism governed by half-understood laws and sustained by an unseen, unknowable intelligence. Its heart beats with the thrum of vast engines, its breath is the warm, mist-laden air, and its bones are the seamless, unyielding substance of the walls. Life here is not wild and untrammeled but cultivated, carefully tended by the armored gardeners and nourished by the drifting globes and the drifting light. It is a place of infinite mystery, where time seems as fluid and particulate as the shifting ground underfoot—a secret world suspended between mountain and sky, endlessly transforming and vibrating with the slow, ancient pulse of life.
