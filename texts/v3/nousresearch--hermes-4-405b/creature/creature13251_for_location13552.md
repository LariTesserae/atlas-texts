This impossible city, carved from mountain stone by the slow, patient caress of water and time and perpetually shrouded in thick, weeping heat, is alive. Not with the stirrings of recognisable fauna, for no beasts or birds navigate these dripping, cavernous avenues. Instead, it is host to a unique, silent inhabitant: the living flora, a dense, fleshy, almost black-green carpet of strange, broad-leaved life that thrives in the constant damp, pressing in from all sides, a natural, unthinking abundance in what appears to be an utterly abandoned, silent city.

This flora, however, transcends the ordinary bounds of plant life. In the high, wet heat, where reality itself frays at the edges, the vegetation becomes a testament to physics unbound. Gravity weakens, then strengthens, in bewildering pockets throughout the city, twisting stalks and leaves into impossible angles, creating disorienting slopes within the layered carpets of green, a plant-based echo of the urban labyrinth's own disorienting contours. Light, too, behaves strangely, bending and fracturing around corners without clear sources, casting its elusive glow upon leaves that seem too numerous, their edges bleeding into the air, creating a shimmering, layered unreality. The very fabric of the world is unstable here, a consistent, never-changing state of perpetual decay and impossible persistence, and the plants are its most visible embodiment.

The overwhelming, deafening *roar*—the unending hum, the relentless, metallic thrum of a sound that occupies all the air and space—is the voice of this fraying reality, a monumental, unseen engine of dissolution. It is the sound of the city's constant becoming, a stable geology and an architecture of long endurance, yes, but one built on a foundation of perpetual decay, a terrifying, beautiful equilibrium where existence stretches, warps, and echoes through the wet, hot, viscous air, a constant, metallic thrum that is less heard and more felt vibrating deep within the bones.

The inhabitants, then—the only inhabitants—are thus not creatures in any conventional sense. They are the unthinking, verdant masses, a silent, pulsating chorus of life that thrives in the interstices of stone and light and fractured reality. They are the city itself, growing, persisting, and decaying in a beautiful, terrifying equilibrium, where nature runs wild in the grand, echoing, vibrating chambers and winding passages, an endless static of green, enduring despite—or perhaps because of—the rules of existence fraying at the edges, teeming with life in a landscape of disorienting, perpetual dissolution.
