The inhabitants of this strange, timeless basin are a diverse and adapted collection of beings, both fauna and otherwise, that exist in a perpetual present. The animals, largely reptiles and amphibians, are more than mere creatures; they are the enduring witnesses and inheritants of this place, each alive with a stillness that mirrors the unchanging landscape.

Monitor lizards, massive and dog-sized, reign on the sun-drenched mesas of pulverized brick and concrete. Their scales, iridescent in the relentless light, hold the hues of ochre and grey from the debris beneath them. They move with a slow, purposeful grace, flicking tongues over sun-warmed concrete dust as if tasting the very age of the ruins. These lizards have made homes within the tiny shelters formed by strangely fused debris or pulled-up slabs of pavement, nesting amongst the unnatural geology that is their world.

In the humid lowlands, bright frogs and salamanders hop across floating islands of ceramic tile, their jewel-like colors shimmering against the dark, warm mud. Dragonflies with wings that catch the perpetual light in flickers skim over algae-thick ponds, their flight patterns ancient and unchanging.

The plant life here is lush but also sparse, thriving within the interstices of human-made bones that refuse eradication. Lush carpets of moss swirl over shattered asphalt, while vines with leaves like green leather scramble over brick cliffs, through air vents, into the open pits of emptied foundations. Tall trees rise from swampy pits, their heavy canopies like green horizons, roots like eels swarming over submerged sills, while pale ferns choke pit-bottoms.

Sounds in this place are few but amplified – the panting of animals, splash of a frog into pond, buzzing of insects, chirps from unseen creatures sheltering in debris. In the still air, these sounds are pronounced markers of life thriving amidst the suspended decay.

Timid tools lie scattered and preserved – wooden hoes, baked-clay chisels, sledgehammer heads made of flintite – all pristine as the day they were abandoned. Weepholes near rampart bases steadily bleed water that never deepens the pools at the bottom, a testament to the timeless stasis.

At rare intervals, a grid of soft blue light scans over everything, a sweep that neither changes nor erases. It resets the world in a moment, leaving all exactly as it was before. This temporal stillness is a constant, a quiet pulse beneath the shimmering heat and endless bright light.

The pervasive heat here is another inhabitant, a silent, oppressive presence that bakes the ground, making it shimmer. It imbues everything with a lethargic warmth, a stillness only broken by the scarce movements of its living denizens.

And water, in its many forms, is ubiquitous – condensation beads on stone, trickles from cracks, pools in hollows. It seeps from beneath broken-cement plains, forming warm swamps where life crawls and swims. It drips from eroded lips, cutting channels through dust that never seems to deepen. This water is hot, mineralized, smelling of wet earth and life blooming in and around it, the very essence of this timeless basin.
