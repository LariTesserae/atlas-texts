The Ring of Lament is tended by the **Silent**. I do not mean silence as the mere *absence* of sound—the dripping, the squelch of damp soil, the almost inaudible clicks and rustles of the stranger beings above your head, these things exist here. No, the true Silent are the ones who understand that *meaning* has withdrawn from the clamor of the lower world.

To call them primitive is to miss the bitter truth of the place. Let us call them the **Listened-To** instead, for that is their purpose and their burden. These are not the first who sought this Ring, not by centuries. Their ancestors were louder, like all who live lower down. Those others left only faint traces—scorch-marks on the cyclopean stone where desperate fires once blazed, the rust-stains of old, dumb iron wedged between the megaliths. These newcomers, the ones you see?
They are the ones who *learned*.

Observe: watch an old woman, her spine permanently bent into the shape of the Ring's damp embrace. She tends her shivering garden, yes—clearing strangler-vines from the pale fungi that are permitted to sprout just there, beside the slow-seeping water—but watch her, as she moves. See how her bare foot hovers above a stone, hangs there, then shifts, shifts again, before descending? She chooses her path as carefully as you or I might choose the words in a treaty of surrender. One misstep, one *unintended* sound... it is not fear, precisely. More a profound, bone-deep certainty: the small, sheltered things that cultivate the Ring would withdraw their tolerance as the eye withdraws from harsh light.

And what would be lost then? Speak to them and they would not answer, not in the tongue of the lower lands. Among themselves, they do not use words for such things as "pitcher plant" or "bioluminescent pad." Those are *things*. The meanings here are different. They will gesture to the slow pulse of a reed's light-bulb, or the particular tilt of a lurid, meat-colored bloom, and convey—or *seem* to convey—whole libraries of comprehension with the flick of a wrist. To call these motions a language is insufficient. They are more like the faint ripples that betray the passage of some vast, hidden shape beneath the surface of a too-still pond. They are... an *evaluation* of the things they see, an encoding of meaning *in* the thing itself, that other Silent can read as easily as you read this.

And the Ring itself, that watches and is watched? The Silent know it is not natural. They feel the cyclopean wall's immense age. It reminds them, in the perpetual twilight, that their little lives are fleeting sparks rising and vanishing. The surrounding silence holds that meaning for them—a meaning that binds them to the Ring, however tenuously. Their every motion is part of a slow, careful, unending dialogue with the Ring and its chorus of subtle sounds. Should the Ring withdraw its favor, all this—this small, hard-won existence in damp stone and frond—would be nothing but a hosanna to the void.
They suffer no delusions of permanence. They know, too, that beyond the wall lies the fall to the abyss, and the Ring watches that too, bleak-eyed and waiting.
The world is older and stranger than we know, and we have never known a particle, a title, of its true antiquity, its strange and terrible ways, and its haste to be gone.
And we are—*they* are—the dust it leaves in its passing.
