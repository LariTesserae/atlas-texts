It is not one kind of being but many that make up this place: a seething tapestry of life that thrums and churns and overflows.

First, the *architects*: small dark shapes that skitter and bustle. They wear no clothes, for their bodies are perfectly adapted to the humid environment—a hard carapace covers their torso and limbs, while a softer underbelly pulses with rhythmic life. Their minds, however, are sharp and quick as their movements; they communicate in a language of clicks and whistles and rapid gestures, constantly refining the living structures around them.

They shape the fungal matter as though it were clay: coaxing it to grow into arches and buttresses and dwellings, guiding its form with precise nudges and sonic pulses. A touch here swells a wall outward; a low thrumming note there narrows a passage. The settlement shifts and changes over time—walls grow thicker, new chambers bud from older ones, entire levels rise into being like mushrooms after rain.

Yet these architects are not the only inhabitants. The vast hollow teems with *wanderers*: iridescent insects as large as fists, great staring lizards clinging to fungal stalks, many-legged things that skitter under the pulsing ground cover. These creatures are drawn to the energy that thrums up from the earth—the same force that warps the air at the edges of vision and pools silence between the stalks.

The wanderers seem to possess a strange influence over the place. Where they gather, the vegetation thrives and swells; the light shimmers with extra radiance; the very air feels quickened, electric. Yet their presence is elusive—they flit through the shadows, their forms half-seen, always at the edges of perception.

Deeper still, within the dense undergrowth and shadowed alcoves, dwell the *keepers*. These are more than mere beasts or insects: they are vast undulating masses of flesh, composed of countless smaller organisms merged into one mind. Their bodies are like living mounds of wet earth or tumorous growths—the lines between individual and collective blurred beyond recognition.

It is the keepers who channel the energy that suffuses this place—the heat that thrums against the skin, the eerie luminescence to the mud. They are the source of the unsettling weirdness that pervades the hollow; the reason why shapes flow and reform at the edge of vision, why physics itself seems malleable as clay in certain areas.

These beings seem almost indifferent to the architects swarming around them. Yet in their slow generational dreaming, they have allowed this strange settlement to take root—perhaps even encourage its growth. For as the settlement expands, so too does the life-force that keeps them thriving. The two are locked in symbiotic embrace: the builders shaping the fungal flesh, the keepers channeling raw energy up from the depths.

And so the place breathes and pulses and lives—a cathedral of flesh, a monument to life's relentless ambition. The air hums with the sound of countless lives intertwined: architects, wanderers, keepers, all bound together in a turbulent dance older than stone itself.
