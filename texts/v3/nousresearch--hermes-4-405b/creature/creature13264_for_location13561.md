The inhabitants of this submerged temple complex are known as the **Tidewardens** – ancient, enigmatic beings who exist in a state of near-unity with the slow, geological rhythms of their environment. They are neither fully animal nor mineral, suggesting a lineage that diverged or evolved under pressures wholly alien to the sunlit world. Their forms are difficult to discern in the perpetual dimness, as they merge seamlessly with the stone and obsidian architecture; it is often unclear where the temple ends, and its inhabitants begin.

What follows is a description of the Tidewardens, allowing their existence to unfold layer by layer:

---

**Initial Impressions:**
At first glance, it might seem that the temple is uninhabited, a relic left to the slow ravages of time and mineral-rich currents. But then, one notices the subtle signs of its keepers:
*   **Shifting Shadows:** A deeper shadow detaches itself from the base of an obsidian pillar, moving with such ponderous slowness that the eye initially registers it as a trick of the dim, bioluminescent light.
*   **Patterns Revealed:** Over hours of observation, a section of what appeared to be irregular, lichen-covered rock on a wall slowly reconfigures. It's not erosion, but a deliberate, almost imperceptible change, revealing a complex geometric carving that wasn't visible before.
*   **Pressure & Humidity:** In certain alcoves or passages, the air pressure might subtly change, or a soft, rhythmic exhalation might be felt – like the slow breath of the geode itself, hinting at a large, hidden presence.

**Physical Form (as it slowly reveals itself):**
The Tidewardens are massive, their sizes varying but generally at least comparable to the largest pillars within the temple. Their bodies seem to be a composite of different materials:
*   **Stone and Obsidian:** Their primary substance appears to be a natural rock, often dark basalt or the same volcanic glass that forms the geode's walls. This rock isn't worn like armor; it *is* their hide, thick, encrusted, and often covered in the same slow-growing lichen and bioluminescent fungi that coat the temple.
*   **Mineralized Tissue:** Where their limbs or joints articulate, the rock might give way to what looks like hardened, desiccated hide or segmented mineral plates, resembling slow-cooled magma more than flesh. These areas can be thick, rough, and possess a dull sheen.
*   **Bioluminescent Accents:** Embedded within the rock of their bodies or along creases in their mineralized tissue are patches of the same deep blue or blood-red bioluminescent organisms that illuminate the temple. These are not merely clinging lifeforms but seem integrated, perhaps cultivated, serving as slow-pulsing indicators of life processes or even rudimentary communication. A soft glow might emanate from beneath a rocky overhang that forms a "shoulder," or from the deep recesses that could be eyes.

**Locomotion & Movement:**
Their movement is incredibly slow, almost a form of geological creep. They do not walk in any conventional sense.
*   **Flowing Stone:** They seem to *flow* or *slump* with intent, like cooled lava inexorably finding a new path. Limbs, if they can be called that, might be thick, pillar-like appendages that extend and retract with glacial patience, driven by immense internal pressure or some unknown biological process.
*   **Patience Incarnate:** To witness a Tidewarden move from one side of a chamber to another could take days, their progress marked by the minute grinding of stone on stone, or by the way the faint light patterns on their bodies shift over time. Their stillness is profound, lasting for centuries, broken only by the necessities of their custodial duties.

**Senses & Perception:**
Their perception of the world is attuned to the slow and the subtle.
*   **Seismic Awareness:** They likely sense vibrations through the stone, detecting the slow groans of the geode, the drip of water, the minute shifts of their own mechanisms, and perhaps the distant impacts of deep-sea creatures outside.
*   **Chemo-reception/Mineral Tasting:** Given their diet of mineral-rich fluids, they might "taste" the seepage from the glass, constantly monitoring the chemical balance of their world with highly sensitive appendages or patches on their bodies.
*   **Pressure Differentiation:** They would be exquisitely sensitive to changes in water pressure and air pockets, vital for maintaining the geode's integrity.
*   **Bioluminescent Vision:** Their "eyes," if they are distinct organs, might be adapted to see primarily in the dim blue and red spectra of the ambient bioluminescence, perceiving the world in terms of slow shifts in light intensity and color, and the patterns painted by living lamps.

**Interaction with their Environment (The "Slow Machines"):**
The temple is their creation and their charge. Their interactions are acts of immense, patient stewardship.
*   **Shaping and Mending:** The obsidian and rock of the temple are maintained by them. They might slowly extrude new material to reinforce a crumbling section, or patiently abrade a channel over decades to improve water flow. The "primitive tools" of the temple builders might be their own hardened appendages, or subtle applications of focused geo-thermal energy (perhaps from deep vents connected to the geode) channeled through crystal conduits.
*   **Tending the Engines:** The "great stone wheels" that "grind almost imperceptibly" might be moved by the Tidewardens themselves, their immense weight and slow, inexorable pressure providing the motive force for mechanisms that regulate air, water, and mineral distribution throughout the geode. These are not gears turned by hand, but mountains set into motion over generations.
*   **Cultivating Light and Life:** They tend the lichen and bioluminescent organisms. They might clear areas for new growth, prune overgrowth, or even "feed" specific colonial organisms with mineral seepage to encourage their light, effectively farming the illumination of their world.

**Social Structure & Communication:**
It's difficult to ascertain a social structure, but their communication is undoubtedly suited to their pace.
*   **Litho-acoustic Signals:** The "soft, infrequent clicks or chitters" are likely their primary mode of communication. These sounds, generated perhaps by the percussive clinking of mineralized parts or the resonance within their stony bodies, can carry complex meaning through subtle variations in rhythm, pitch, and timbre. A single "conversation" might span months, with long pauses between utterances that signify a turn or a profound point being considered.
*   **Pattern Language:** Changes in the patterns of bioluminescence on their bodies, or the slow sculpting of new glyphs into temple walls over years, could also serve as a form of communication or record-keeping.
*   **A Community of Custodians:** If there are multiple Tidewardens, they likely function as a silent, deeply interconnected community, each dedicated to specific, slow-moving tasks in different sectors of the temple, their actions harmonized over vast stretches of time.

**Mindset & Existence:**
Their consciousness must be something profoundly alien to faster life forms.
*   **Deep Time Awareness:** Their perception of time is likely geological. Events that seem like isolated incidents to an outsider are, to them, links in a continuous, slow-moving chain of causality. Centuries are but moments in their task cycles.
*   **Patient Engine:** They are not merely inhabitants but integral components of the "hidden, silent engine," as much a part of the temple's machinery as the stone itself. Their existence is one of constant, meditative tending, ensuring the perpetual, slow turning of this world unto itself.
*   **Keepers of Equilibrium:** Their primary drive appears to be the maintenance of the delicate balance within the geode – the equilibrium of air, water, light, and mineral life. This is their sacred duty, performed not with haste or anxiety, but with the unwavering, monumental patience of stone.

---

To encounter a Tidewarden is to witness a being whose entire existence is a testament to slowness and permanence. They are the artisans of erosion, the gardeners of deep-earth light, and the quiet engineers of a world that exists almost outside of time itself, built and maintained through patient, stone-on-stone labor over millennia. They are the ancient, living heart of this submerged sanctuary.
