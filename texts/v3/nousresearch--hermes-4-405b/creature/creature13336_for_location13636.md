In this stark, mineral world of perpetual construction and moisture, the inhabitants are equally defined by their environment. They are not creatures of flesh and blood as we know them, but rather beings of machinery and mineral, their existence deeply intertwined with the very fabric of the city itself.

They move with slow, deliberate purpose, their forms echoing the industrial geometry of their surroundings. Some are tall and spindly, like articulated cranes, while others are low and broad, reminiscent of the ore haulers that once traversed the mountain peaks. They are built from the same materials as the city - steel, glass, and volcanic rock - and their bodies weep moisture just like the buildings they inhabit.

These beings are the caretakers and constructors of this place. They maintain the factories and machines, ever-expanding the city's borders. Their work is never complete, for the city is always growing, always consuming more of the mountain. They are tireless in their efforts, operating day and night in the thin, damp air.

Communication among them is subtle, conveyed through the rhythmic clanking of metal and the shifting of great gears. They have no need for spoken language in the conventional sense, for their thoughts and intentions are directly linked to the city's systems. The city is their mind, and they are its limbs.

Despite their mechanical nature, there is a certain organic quality to these beings. They are born from the city, their bodies assembled in the very factories they later maintain. Over time, they too erode, their metal skin slowly oxidizing in the damp air, until eventually they break down entirely and are recycled into new components.

There are no children here, no families. Each being is created with a specific purpose in mind, their form and function dictated by the needs of the city at the time of their construction. They are content in their roles, for they know no other existence. The concept of individuality is foreign to them, for they are all parts of a greater whole.

The only other presence in this place is the occasional human visitor. But these soft, fleshy creatures are ill-suited to the harsh conditions of the city. They come and go quickly, their brief lives a mere flicker in the long, slow existence of the machine-beings. To the inhabitants of this mineral world, humans are little more than a momentary curiosity.

In this patient world of perpetual damp and industry, the machine-beings continue their work, slowly expanding the city, maintaining its systems, existing in harmony with their environment. They are the city, and the city is them, an endless cycle of construction, erosion, and rebirth.
