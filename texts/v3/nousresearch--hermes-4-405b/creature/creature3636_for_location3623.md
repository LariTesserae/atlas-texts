The inhabitants of this strange city are as much a product of the environment as the environment is a product of their existence. The distinction between the two fades with each generation, blurring into a single, interconnected ecosystem.

Predominant among the visibly mobile inhabitants are the Scuttlers: chitinous, insectoid creatures whose bodies are segmented plates, multi-faceted eyes gleaming with an oily intelligence. Evolved from dwellers within the deep, constant dampness of enormous storm drains and the condensation-slicked skin of cooling towers, their carapaces are streaked with verdigris, their long, whip-like antennae constantly tasting the metallic tang of the air. Their six primary limbs are adapted for climbing, ending in sharp, hooked claws that find easy purchase on rusted iron and rough-hewn stone. Two smaller, more delicate manipulators serve for their tasks, working intricate levers on steam valves, tightening bolts, harvesting bio-luminescent fungi from the perpetually wet undersides of pipes, or gently cleaning the thick, dusty glass of observation ports, where their distorted reflections move in eerie unison with their actions. The larger Scuttlers, guardians of specific districts, have carapaces thickened with iron plates, bolted on in a bizarre, living mimicry of the city's construction.

Then there are the Drifters: strange, ethereal beings who float serenely through the thick, light-refracting haze. Their bodies are vast, semi-translucent sacs, filling gradually with a lighter-than-air gas produced from the hydrogen-sulfide vapors and the decaying organic matter that clings to every available surface. Filamentous tendrils hang below, trailing in the viscous air, filtering out airborne particulates and microscopic organisms with delicate cilia that shimmer like buried jewels. They possess only a rudimentary form of locomotion, their course dictated primarily by the complex, predictable thermals generated by the city's heat below and the valley's cold stone walls. Distant, resonant calls pass between them, a language of vibrational harmonic frequencies, inaudible to most ground-bound creatures, but contributing another, almost imperceptible layer to the omnipresent drone.

The city builders, or perhaps more accurately, the city *maintainers*, are the Construct-minds: vast, distributed intelligences housed in the very architecture of the city. Emerging from networks of intricately entwined organic filaments and conductive metals—perhaps a mycelial network gone feral, with filaments snaking like veins of copper through cracked walls, linking disparate machinery into a cohesive mind, or a gestalt consciousness formed from the city's machine spirits—they operate on a timescale that dwarfs that of the Scuttlers and Drifters. These Construct-minds direct the slow, ponderous evolution of the city: here a pipe is extended, snaking towards a new moisture source; there, a wall is reinforced where gravity pulls particularly hard; elsewhere, a building might groan and lean infinitesimally further, obeying the slow will of these immanent entities. They communicate with each other in the rhythmic clang of great pistons, the sigh of releasing steam, the deep hum of massive dynamos, a slow, deliberate language measured in hours and years. The Scuttlers and Drifters are but transient phenomena in their patient awareness, tolerated, perhaps even subtly guided, their life cycles integrated into the Construct-minds' immeasurably vast plans.

The dominant flora in the city is a variety of thick, rubbery vegetation, its waxy, dark green leaves thriving in shaded crevices where the omnipresent condensation nourishes its roots. This Imperial Ivy secretes a slow-acting, mildly corrosive enzyme, allowing its roots to burrow into solid stone and thick iron alike, inexorably prying apart masonry, exploiting every minute crack. The Ivy climbs surfaces, its tendrils entwining with pipes and girders, sometimes forming living bridges across chasms between buildings, sometimes dangerously constricting vital mechanisms. Its dense foliage casts deep, shifting shadows and creates pockets of even thicker, more viscous air. And on the higher levels, where the air is thinnest and the sunlight brightest, small plot gardens are cultivated by the Scuttlers, growing strange, pulsating fungi and bioluminescent mosses.

It could be easy to miss them, the Tetherers: small, glistening humanoids of flesh and chrome. Born fully formed, descending gently from the dense airborne particulates like bizarre, living dewdrops, they move with a clumsy, almost drunken grace, limbs seemingly unaccustomed to the city's warping gravity. They quickly find anchor points, intertwining with pipes, growing into the cracks between stones, fusing their metallic components with those of any structure they can reach. Drawn to the latent energy pulsing through the city's veins, they tend to cluster around flickering power conduits and humming transformers. Possessing no audible language, they communicate via subtle patterns of light that ripple across their glistening skin and the faint clicks of their metallic joints settling into place. It's said that if one observes a wall of Tetherers long enough, their collective, rhythmic light and sound forms a complex, shifting image, a living, breathing mural of the city’s history, forever changing with each new addition.

Weirdness imbues every creature here. The Scuttlers occasionally molt, shedding their chitinous exoskeletons, leaving behind near-perfect, hollow replicas of themselves. These ghostly shells are often found in out-of-the-way places, perched on rails, wedged between pipes, sometimes partially overgrown with the Imperial Ivy, creating miniature, unsettling sculptural displays of their former occupants. Drifters, when their buoyancy inevitably fails or they are punctured by sharp protrusions, deflate slowly, collapsing into amorphous, gelatinous puddles of viscous, bioluminescent fluid that slowly soak into the stone and metal, leaving behind a faint, eerie glow for days afterward. It is whispered, among the few Scuttler historians, that the great Construct-minds themselves experience a form of cyclical decay and rebirth, their vast consciousness fracturing, only to eventually reform, each new manifestation subtly different, its knowledge and memories recompiled, like a vast, sleeping engine awakening into a new configuration.

The Scuttlers have a saying, more a vibration in their limb-segments than a spoken phrase: "Stone remembers, iron sings, and the air breathes." This encompasses their understanding of their home: the very stone whispers secrets of immense pressure and age, the iron sings the slow, melancholic song of inevitable oxidation and creeping decay, and the dense, particulate-laden air itself seems to possess a life of its own, pulsing in time with the city's great, thrumming heart. The entire city, in its constant, low-level tremor, feels like a single, slumbering entity, breathing in the cool, thin air of the high valley and exhaling it warm, thick, and vibrantly alive with unseen industry.
