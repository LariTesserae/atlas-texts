The beings who tend this place, who are indistinguishable from it, are the **Silth'garn**, the "Stone-Weavers" or "Moisture-Sculptors." They are not mere inhabitants; they are the crucible and the flame of this impossible sanctuary.

They appear at first as diminutive figures, no taller than a human child, perpetually slick with the moisture that beads upon everything. Their skin, if it can be called that, is a translucent, algae-green membrane, veined with traceries of gold and soft blue that pulse with a slow, internal luminescence. It is cool and damp to the touch, almost gelatinous. They wear no clothing; their bodies are living canvases, adorned with intricate, self-inflicted scars and patterns of hardened lichen that bloom in vibrant, unnatural hues. These patterns are not merely decorative; they are sigils of their purpose, their lineage within the living engine of this place.

Their faces are broad and flat, with wide, lipless mouths perpetually set in an expression of serene concentration. Their eyes are large, multifaceted like an insect's, but filled with a deep, ancient intelligence. They glow with a soft, internal light, reflecting the myriad colors of their domain. Small, membranous frills, like those of a prehistoric amphibian, surround their necks and joints, constantly absorbing moisture from the air and secreting a faintly shimmering, viscous film that aids their passage through the dense growths.

Their limbs are spindly but surprisingly strong, terminating in three or four long, slender digits. These digits are not tipped with claws or nails, but with delicate, porous pads that they press against the stone, wood, and fungal structures. In this way, they don't just sculpt, but *breathe* life into the materials, coaxing growth and form through a process that seems part metabolic exchange, part psionic communion. They can secrete enzymes, acids, and binding agents from these pads, shaping matter at a molecular level, or simply lay their hands upon a surface and cause the existing life within it – the fungi, the lichen, the moisture – to swell and rearrange itself into the desired structure.

The Silth'garn move with a slow, deliberate grace, their limbs flowing like kelp in a gentle current, yet with a precision that speaks of eons of practice. They are constant, tireless in their work. There is no rest, no sleep as we understand it, only a shifting of focus from one task to another, a perpetual cycle of creation and maintenance. They communicate not with words, but with a complex interplay of bioluminescent patterns that ripple across their skin, low, resonant hums that seem to emanate from deep within their chests, and subtle shifts in the ambient moisture – a pattern of drips that speeds up, a patch of air that momentarily clears. It’s a language of the environment itself, decipherable only by them.

They are not solitary. Though they often work individually, lost in their communion with a particular section of the living architecture, they are deeply connected. A wave of luminous color will spread from one Silth'garn to another, a signal that ripples through the entire chamber, and suddenly, a dozen of them will converge on a single point, their movements perfectly synchronized as they address some imperceptible imbalance or undertake a new phase of construction. They are a collective consciousness, not in a hive-mind sense, but in a way that their individual thoughts are like currents in the same ocean, shaped by the same tides and lunar pull.

Their awareness of intruders is not one of alarm, but of mild, detached curiosity. If you were to attempt to touch them or disrupt their work, their song would change – a low thrumming would intensify, the air around you would grow heavier, colder, and the intricate growths nearby might subtly shift to bar your passage or redirect your attention. They might not attack, but the living machine they maintain would register your disruption and begin to... process it.

The most striking thing about the Silth'garn, beyond their silent, rhythmic industry, is their connection to the subtle unravelling of reality within this place. As they work, their own forms exhibit the same delicate fraying. A worker might reach for a tool, and for an instant, their arm will stretch further than possible, or shimmer, becoming translucent, before solidifying. Pools of water they pass will momentarily reflect impossible geometries before settling. The light they emit sometimes casts shadows that don't align with physical objects. They don't seem to control this; it's a consequence of their being, an aura of bending reality that emanates from them as naturally as the scent of damp earth. They are gardeners tending a reality that is less a fixed construct and more a living, breathing, and ultimately *malleable* organism, and the laws of physics are but the trellis upon which they cultivate their visions.
