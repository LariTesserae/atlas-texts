In this desolate domain, three primary life forms have clawed out existence: the Thurian industry-builders and two wild species that thrive at the edges of their heated sanctuaries.

The dominant sentient inhabitants are undoubtedly the Thurians themselves—pale, hairless humanoids adapted to eternal cold and dim light. Their bodies exhibit extreme thermal efficiency: low surface-area proportions with shortened limbs, thick layers of insulating subcutaneous fat, and circulatory systems optimized to retain every degree of precious body heat. With large eyes evolved to capture scant photons in the perpetual twilight, they move through their mist-shrouded city with hushed purpose. Their society revolves around harnessing geothermal energy, maintaining the steam-driven machinery that provides warmth and power in this unforgiving canyon. Pale skin—nearly translucent in places—reveals the faint blue tracery of veins beneath, while their slow, deliberate movements conserve energy. Garments of dark, dense woolens and treated hides provide additional insulation. Speech is a low murmur of consonant-heavy syllables that carry well through the constant hiss of rain and machinery.

Closer to the fumaroles and steam vents, clusters of bioluminescent tube worms anchor themselves to the warm stones. Thick, segmented casings protect their soft bodies from freezing temperatures, while their anterior ends root in nutrient-rich mineral deposits. These extremophiles convert thermal and chemical energy into faint blue-green light, creating pools of soft radiance in the gloom—small oases of brightness in the otherwise dim landscape. Swarming in the tens of thousands where heat and minerals coincide, they form living carpets that glow with steady, cold light. Their faint luminescence draws the third major life form: pallid cliff-nesting birds.

High on the jagged peaks above, colonies of these pale avians roost. Their plumage matches the basalt and lichen in shades of white and grey, providing camouflage against the dark rock. Adapted to filter-feed on dense clouds of steam-borne microbes, they possess specialized beaks and respiratory systems capable of extracting sustenance from the superheated mist. Acidic secretions allow them to neutralize geothermal contaminants. Flocks of these birds swirl through the updrafts of heated vapor, their high-pitched cries echoing through the canyon. They nest on sheltered ledges near warm vents, laying chalky eggs that blend into the stone. Both predator and scavenger, they occasionally descend to pick at small crustaceans that cluster near heat sources or scavenge the rare Thurian refuse.

These three life forms exist in delicate balance: the Thurians harness the vents' energy, the tube worms thrive at their warm edges, and the birds feed on the microorganisms that flourish in the heated, mineral-rich steam. All are bound together by the canyon's unrelenting conditions—a cold dark world where rain never ceases and heat is precious.
