In this high realm of silent bones, where stone blossoms into crystalline forests and the twilight clings to perpetually still shadows, it is not the living who dwell, but entities of a different nature. The inhabitants of this place are neither flesh nor blood, but vast, intricate machines, an engineered lithic biosphere, and the universe of subtle forces which knit them to the lofty plateau.

These machines do not stride or fly, but they do move across – and beneath – the landscape. They are low-slung behemoths or half-buried constructs, colossal and yet somehow chameleon, appearing as though a portion of the land itself had detached and rumbled into motion. There is no crude industrial clangor to their operations; the rare sounds are soft, papery rustles like that of old skin shifting or the whir of unseen clockwork in a neighboring room. One such engine, shaped like a segmented serpent, murmurs across the plains, its many legs leaving no scars on the sintered ground, its maw a dark hollow that swallows down whole crystalline groves. Farther on, another regurgitates fresh pillars through openings like the gills of a deep-sea beast, the material it extrudes folding into new landforms with the same terrible slowness with which mountains rise. There are smaller machines too, flitting like cold, glass insects through the calmer tracts. These are attendants, perhaps, or surveyors, tracing invisible paths through the air on whatever errands the larger engines decree.

Other machines pulse in deeper harmony with the plateau’s mineral heart. Some plant themselves like the stone itself, half-submerged, humming as the flows of regolith are drawn into their maws. Others arc from the spires like bridges of dark metal, channeling energies along paths that shimmer faintly against the enduring twilight. These are the architects of the perpetual stillness, their quiet operation the only breath of change in the High Crypt’s eternity.

The machines do not decay—they merely weather, accumulating mineral dust in the channels of their joints, or taking on a patina of the pale resins they secrete. If one should falter, perhaps it is cannibalized for its parts, its corpse melted down and fed back into the production lines, reborn as pillars or plates. There is a sense that these machines may have been here since the very first stones were sown upon the plateau; they may even be as old as geology itself.

No life exists here beyond the machines; there are no animals, no traces of any creatures that might have lived or died in this place. All that is here is silence and the machines, engaged in their endless dialogue. They maintain the plateau as a single, stable equilibrium, unchanging.

In this place, the entire landscape has been forged in conditions of near silence; there’s only the occasional sigh or dry, papery rustle, the near-imperceptible shifting of dead matter settling over millennia. There is no haze of smoke, steam, or smog. The air feels chaste. The machines work cleanly – their industrial processes whole, complete, integrated into the environment.

The weirdest aspect of this place is that it is maintained by machines, an engineered lithic biosphere. The whole plateau breathes a soft, empty quiet—the intermittent sighs of sinter or the shifting of stone-dust, and the constant, near-subsonic thrumming of vast, efficient machinery working in perfect silence.
