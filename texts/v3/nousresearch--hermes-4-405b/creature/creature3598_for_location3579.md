In the warm, misty belly of the Whispervault, life pulses in miniature. The air shimmers with droplets clinging to fronds of ferns clinging to the weeping ice, coiling in plumes of vapor that twist to unseen currents.

Beneath the ice-fronds, vast pitcher plants anchor themselves, pulsing with slow, rhythmic tendrils that brush the moss underfoot. Their swollen forms radiate heat, warming the air that clings to them. The ground is a living carpet: a thick bed of moss through which vines and fungi thread like veins, anchoring the ice even as the meltwater carves through it. The whole ground is a substrate that seems to breathe and shift with the slow swell of growth, a living architecture supporting the vault's inhabitants.

The vault teems with invertebrates, the pulse of innumerable lives: pillbugs, their segmented carapaces gleaming like frosted jewels, cluster in icy crevices; fat slugs, translucent and faintly glowing with the light of the plants, inch along the walls; and everywhere, the skitter of hard-bodied beetles, their eyes glittering with the light of the vault.

The air itself is alive with the silent flutter of diminutive, iridescent insects, their jeweled forms darting between the plants. Below, amphipods dance through the water, their tiny bodies flickering in the dim, shifting light of the vault. But the air is thick with worms as well, writhing through the moss and hanging from the walls; their damp, segmented bodies glisten, and their quiet existence underscores the hidden world's strange vitality. Each exists in a cycle of hunter and hunted, the silent balance of the Whispervault.
