The Glass Metropolis is inhabited by an enigmatic race known only as the Thra'kai—a species that is neither wholly organic nor entirely synthetic, but rather a fusion of both. The Thra'kai are the creators and custodians of the city, their existence intrinsically bound to its crystalline structures.

They appear as tall, slender beings with multifaceted obsidian skin that mimics the city's glassy terrain. Their limbs are sharp and angular, much like the towers they inhabit, and they move with a silent, gliding grace. Their faces are devoid of recognizable features, bearing instead a smooth, reflective surface that mirrors the ever-changing play of light and water across the city. This strange visage is not a mask, but an innate trait—a manifestation of their deep connection to the Glass Metropolis.

The Thra'kai communicate not through audible speech, but via modulated vibrations transmitted directly into the crystalline structures, sending silent messages that resonate through the spires. These vibrations are felt rather than heard, a low thrumming that echoes the hum of the condensers and the shifting of the mountains beneath the city.

Their society is highly ordered and collectively oriented. Individual distinction is minimal; all Thra'kai contribute to the maintenance and operation of the city's vast water-harvesting systems. The rhythmic collection and evaporation of water is central to their lives, a sacred cycle they perpetuate with unwavering devotion. It is said that the Thra'kai are born from this very cycle—formed from the condensing vapor and hardened by the unforgiving sunlight, emerging fully formed from the glassy peaks.

Though they possess advanced technology, the Thra'kai live simply, their needs minimal and met by the city's self-sustaining mechanisms. Their architecture reflects their nature—precise, geometric, and integrated seamlessly with the natural terrain.

The moths drifting above are not separate creatures, but the Thra'kai in another form. These "sky-kin" shed their crystalline bodies periodically, taking flight to ride the thermal currents. While airborne, they carry out critical functions: dispersing mineral-rich vapors that maintain the city's ecosystem, surveying the ever-shifting landscape, and communion with the sun. Their enormous, iridescent wings are not just for flight but also serve as living prisms, refracting sunlight to nourish the structures below. When their aerial duties are complete, they descend, their bodies dissolving into shimmering mists that seep into the glass and merge once more with their earthbound counterparts.

The iridescent beetles that scuttle silently across the plains are the Thra'kai's "land-kin"—semi-autonomous extensions of their collective consciousness. Resilient and untiring, the land-kin tirelessly maintain the lower levels, clearing debris, repairing fissures, and ensuring the intricate runnels remain unobstructed. Their movements are choreographed in precise, mathematical patterns, adding to the city's eerie, otherworldly atmosphere.

The Thra'kai do not age or die in any conventional sense. Instead, they exist in a continuous state of transformation, shifting between solid, liquid, and gaseous states as necessary. Their crystalline bodies can incorporate new growth from the mineral-rich mists that perpetually bathe the city, making them as much a part of the Glass Metropolis as its spires and reservoirs.

Visitors to the Glass Metropolis are infrequent, but those who do ascend the treacherous peaks find themselves unacknowledged by the Thra'kai. The inhabitants neither welcome nor reject outsiders, treating them with the same detached indifference as any other natural phenomenon. To the Thra'kai, the city is a closed system—a perfect equilibrium of light, water, and crystalline form that exists for its own enigmatic purpose, without need or desire for interaction beyond its shimmering borders.
