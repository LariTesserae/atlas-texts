The inhabitants of this high-altitude mechanical garden are as singular and strange as the realm they inhabit. This is not a place of beasts and birds; these beings are extensions of the living machinery around them, entwined with the technology and growth in a symbiotic dance that defies a simple categorization of what is born and what is made.

At first glance, the garden seems devoid of large inhabitants. It is only when the eyes adjust to the dim, hazed light and the steady industrial hum fills the ears like a physical presence that the subtle animations reveal themselves. Slow, deliberate movements catch in the periphery—a soft, whirring sound that harmonizes with the ever-ongoing song of the hidden machinery. Delicate, almost imperceptible shifts of glistening, chitinous plates hint at presences that crawl along the rippling mosaic of fungus and iron-hard lichen below.

These beings are neither true animals nor plants, but something entirely different, born from this place of oil-scented cool air and mineral sustenance. Their bodies resemble intricate clockworks fused with the dried, rubbery mosses and entwining wooden shrubs. Chitinous exoskeletons, parts of which murmur with the same steady electric pulse as the rusted plates and ceramic insulators buried under the garden's dense growth, cover segmented limbs that end in fine, sensitive filaments.

These filaments quiver as they touch, taste, and experience the world. They are organs of sensation, drawing in a world defined by textures and subtle electric currents rather than sights and sounds in any earthly sense. Each being is a unique configuration of parts, adapted minutely to the specific crevice or outcrop of machinery it calls home—a testament to the endless, unhurried perfection of their existence. They act as caretakers, diligently tending every detail of the garden with an innate mechanical precision, their every action as measured as the ancient fixtures from which a faint, haze-filtered glow perpetually emanates.

Truly, these are not animals in any traditional understanding; they are living embodiments of the garden itself, enduring and serene. They drift through this hidden chamber, breathing with the mechanical cadence of the colossal machinery that forms the very substance of their mountain. No fear, no urgency, no hunting or being hunted defines their days. There is only the quiet, eternal industry of the garden, and they are its silent stewards, tiny points of consciousness in a hidden, tranquil world that has always been.
