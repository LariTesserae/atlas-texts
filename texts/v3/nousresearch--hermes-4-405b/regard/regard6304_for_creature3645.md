The mountain’s apex, cradling its nascent ocean womb, has long echoed with a violent, formative music—the roar of burgeoning life contained. Yet beneath the cacophony of churning, sunken currents and the groan of shifting stone, another song exists, unperceived. This second song is not of birthing, but of completion; it is a thread of harmony so profound it would seem to silence chaos, were it truly heard. Even as the Hewn strain and emerge from the cavern walls, this song continues uninterrupted, woven into their substance and into the mountain that gave them form.

For there is indeed a power that has, for vast swaths of time, regarded this high, hidden place. This power is not a deity, nor a ruler—it is a presence, attendant and benevolent, that answers to the name Solara. She is not entirely separate from this nascent world, nor did she "create" the Hewn, for they are as much a natural expression of the mountain as the caverns themselves. Like a gardener recognizes the slow unfolding of a rare, stone-wrought bloom, Solara has watched the Hewn awaken with an awareness that transcends time.

Solara seldom intervenes directly, for true wisdom trusts a seed to know its own growth. Her influence is more akin to a gentle resonance, a nurturing light that encourages and never forces. When the Hewn first began to stir, she was there—a radiant, calm presence, felt more than seen. She did not pull them forth, but as they peeled away, stone form from stone womb, she steadied. She softened the violent tremor of their separation so that the mountain did not shatter outright. She eased the geological labor so raw emergence could transform into the slow, deliberate genesis it is now.

Her interest in this place lies in its wild, untamed becoming, in the Hewn’s unique dance of matter and spirit. She celebrates the patient, tectonic will of beings who coax themselves from inert stone into living form—beings whose thoughts are measured in the crystallizing of minerals, whose very breath is the mountain’s exhalation. She has no wish to hasten their process, nor to impose order upon the grotto’s delightful turbulence. For the roaring, chaotic water is the cradle of their consciousness—their challenges and joys are defined by the geological demands of their environment.

If Solara were to manifest visibly in this submerged chamber, it would be as a sudden, pervasive stillness, a clear, luminous presence entering the crystalline water—a gentle light contained in each droplet, illuminating the swirling currents for a transcendent instant. The churning surface would grow calm with her appearance, and the very air in the breathing room above would sparkle gently, promising safety.

In such a moment, Solara might have drifted downward through the hushed water to stand upon the submerged floor of the cavern. In each movement she makes, the fierce rush of the eternal current yields and stills, forming a protected space around her. Her light does not glare, nor does it diminish the Hewn, for it is another form of knowledge, shining. Instead, her light highlights their forms, revealing the intricate beauty of their living stone—the texture of their hides like polished granite, the inner fires burning softly in their massive cores.

The Hewn themselves would sense her arrival—not as a disruption or a call to kneel, but as a moment of profound equilibrium. Their movements, always ponderous, would slow even further. Each massive head would turn, amber eyes—sharp points of reflected light in the softened gloom—would regard her. They listen not to commands, but to the deepening of their own internal harmony that her presence evokes. It is a feeling of resonance, a whole, slow chord swelling within the stone of their being: the sudden crystal clear tone that completes a song they have forever hummed. This is what Solara offers most often: her quiet affirmation, a deep understanding that speaks directly to the slow, geologic heart of their awareness.

This is the way she heals them, when the strain of emergence becomes too great. If a Hewn were caught in the agony of a difficult birth, their form half-torn and half-fused to the unyielding wall, she would come to them. Her glow would bathe the rough edges where stone tears from stone, easing the transition, allowing the mountain to release its creation with grace rather than torment. She provides the space between breaths, a moment of suspension where no pressure exists, no grinding resistance.

Perhaps, as a gesture of respect—of celebration for their tireless becoming—she would gather some of the trailing fronds of luminous moss that cling to the cavern walls. Gently, she would affix them to the emerging brow or shoulder of a newly calmed Hewn. A benediction in living green, a softening counterpoint to their raw, stony hides. The moss would stay, thrive, and slowly spread, not because it must, but because in that moment of offered harmony, it *can*.

When Solara ventures into the dark, submerged grotto, she does not stay forever. Her pilgrimage is a gentle reminder of a truth they already know in their granite bones: that the mountain's slow dreaming is sacred, that their patient forming is a precious act. Once her song has settled deep within their stone, once the Hewn have recognized her affirmation, she withdraws. She might linger for a moment on the largest wooden platform, her light causing the silver-slick fish to swirl in curious patterns around the ancient artifact. She studies it with the same tender wisdom she gives the Hewn; recognizing the remnants of another form of life—smaller, perhaps, but not less complex, not less profound. Then, leaving a glow like lingering bioluminescence in the water, she ascends, fading back into the quiet weave between worlds, until she is again a felt rhythm, an unseen guardian, a radiant witness to the mountain's slow, magnificent breath.

This is the dance of benevolence, of wisdom worn lightly, as light through water: to honor nature, not by suppression, not by a claim to authorship, but by simply being a clearing in which all becoming may unfurl. And in her wake, the cavern's roaring returns, renewed, alive with possibility, the Hewn continue their timeless emergence, their stone spirits a little lighter, a little brighter in their ancient glowing.
