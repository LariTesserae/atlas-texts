The air here is never dry. A dense, industrial city, built high among the peaks, seems to breathe moisture. Water beads relentlessly on metal and glass, collecting in grooves and crevices, weeping from condensation on cold pipes. For all this dampness, no plant life takes root — no mosses or lichen soften the hard edges of this place. Instead, it is a kingdom of mineral and machine, a stark architecture unfolding in the thin atmosphere of extreme altitude.

The temperature here is often hot, heat radiating from the factories and densely packed structures where engines thrum and unseen production churns. The city seems to be eternally under construction, a sprawling metropolis clinging to the mountainside like a precious ore. Its edges blur gradually into raw geology — cliffs of volcanic glass where the structures have not yet consumed the natural peak. It is a place of simultaneous assembly and erosion, a city still coming to form.

The light here is normal, yet the landscape itself is deeply strange. It extends far beyond the immediate gaze, a vast and open panorama of industrial complexity and emergent geology. Though the city is densely built, it feels eerily quiet — the sounds muffled as if by altitude, or perhaps simply swallowed by the sheer scale of the place. Life other than human presence is rare, the natural world here either long gone or never given the chance to arise, replaced by the industrial organism of the city.

It is a patient world, this city among the clouds. Change happens across long durations, hard edges giving way to new construction slowly, the cycle of condensation and rain a gradual rhythm. The rules of existence here are of constant assembly, perpetual dampness, a world where the heat of industry meets the cool, high-altitude air to create a place at once alien and purposeful.
