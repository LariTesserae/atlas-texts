I glide through a realm of silent bones.

This is the High Crypt, a vast and airy necropolis unfurling across a plateau suspended in a twilight sky. It is an open place, extending far enough that its boundaries remain unseen – a landscape of muted grandeur. The air here is dry, utterly devoid of water vapor, carrying only the faintest hint of stone dust and ancient resins. It is perpetually warm, a soft, lifeless warmth, as if the latent heat from the world’s formation slowly radiating from the colossal mineral structures all around.

The ground, though formed from stone, is soft. Underfoot, fine silts and powders shift – this is a place that is still emerging; its geology is raw and new, yet it feels simultaneously ancient. It’s as though the entirety of this high, warm plateau was sown with seeds of rock, then left to grow in slow, fractal patterns. Dendritic filaments of pale, chalky mineral snake across the ground, creeping tendrils of crystalline growth that spread and climb. Whether it is sinter, or some strange evaporitic deposit forming from air rich with minerals, the process seems arrested only recently. Pillars and plates are budding, half-formed extrusions from the soft floor. The landscape here is still natal, only just coming into being, its forms embryonic and strangely uneroded; the weathering process appears to have barely begun.

What would normally be vertical cliffs or sheer faces are instead stepped, terraced, like layered nodules that have bloomed into flat planes. Fissures gape here and there, but rather than appearing torn apart by time, they look more like creases where new growth is beginning to unfurl. These pinnacles and pavements are all being woven from something dry, something that feels as much like old bone as it does stone.

This entire plateau is indeed an ossuary, but of an arrested, true rock-formation. The only things that grow are the 'vegetation': strange, hard protrusions – crystalline, chitinous, lithic, things that grow without water. They resemble xerophytes, but are entirely mineral. Some are thick forests of pale, jagged crystals, shining with a chalky white or pearlescent sheen. Others appear more like branching coral in calcified form, or the petrified nervous systems of some colossal, long-fossilized titan. These growths are not sparse in spots, but lush; entire forests of these mineral formations stretch at the edges of vision. It’s a land of dry, tangled infestations of crystalline structures. They carve the sky in spires and blades, radiating a sense of fragile brittleness, forming strange, dendritic thickets that glimmer faintly in the normal light.

There is normal light here, uniform and somehow sterile, as if sunlight itself has been mined and redeployed by the architects of the terrain. It casts dim, blurry shadows that never move, fixed in place. The entire landscape has been forged in conditions of near silence; there’s only the occasional sigh or dry, papery rustle, the near-imperceptible shifting of dead matter settling over millennia.

The weirdest aspect of this place is that it is maintained by machines. No life exists here beyond the machines; there are no animals, no traces of any creatures that might have walked, crawled, or flown through this place. This is an empty, untouched world, devoid of built structures, without any constructed artifacts. Yet it is subtly and intricately entwined with technology. Scattered across the plateau, humming with low, consistent drones, are industrial-scale machines. They are not large, not skyscrapers nor sprawling complexes, but rather engineered in ways that seem to be extensions of the landscape itself – worn, weathered, even geologically integrated. They look as though they are growing from, or are parts of, the ossified world.

In one place, regolith and rock are visibly flowing in through a machine’s intake. The material is processed within: pulverized, smelted, and sintered. Farther along the plateau, this engineered dry matter is extruded through obscene, biomechanical nozzles, extruded into new plates, pillars, promontories, and new extensions of the mineral growths. One machine harvests mineral filaments from nearby crystalline thickets, re-processing them into feedstock. Another grinds up vast spreads of mineral sinter and chalk, mixing the powder with resins or solvents to form pastes that are piped long distances through veins that run like veins up the sides of ossified ridges. Yet another seems to be calibrating the very ambient conditions, controlling the temperature and humidity to maintain this lifeless, warm stasis. Even as I watch, one of these machines, low-slung and segmented like an enormous caterpillar, grinds slowly over the ground. It drags spiked rollers and blades, working a vast field of crystalline spires. The machine is mowing them, shearing through the brittle stalks just above the ground. Dull, flinty shards fall like autumn leaves, then are swept up by a secondary process into hoppers. I see that it has been systematically harvesting the crystalline vegetation, leaving geometric patterns of stubble that stretch into the distance, evidence of meticulous, lifeless order. The machine then moves on, and from vents and spigots on its underbelly, it extrudes fresh material: new stone, a little darker in color, perhaps richer in whatever reagents it has added, seeping out across the scarred ground. Slowly, over months or years, this newly deposited rock will itself begin to sprout fresh crystalline growths.

The sky above the plateau is deep and clear, yet for all this mechanical activity, there's no haze of smoke, steam, or smog; no sign of exhaust or pollution. The air feels chaste. The machines work cleanly – their industrial processes whole, complete, integrated into the environment. It is a kind of crystalline biosphere, an engineered lithic biosphere. The whole plateau breathes a soft, empty quiet – the intermittent sighs of sinter or the shifting of stone-dust, and the constant, near-subsonic thrumming of vast, efficient machinery working in perfect silence.

The High Crypt, untouched and empty, is a realm of bones being formed from dust and then maintained by the endless diligence of industrial processes, a place where stillness is held in a subtle state of unchanging equilibrium.
