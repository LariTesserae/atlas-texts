The world sprawled in low, young mountains. Water gushed down slopes and pooled in vast, rust-hued basins, a near-constant roar that filled the landscape. Iridescent veins of semi-precious stones glinted in the dim light that filtered through a dense, ever-shifting sky, thick with condensing vapor and mineral motes. Jagged peaks pierced the fog, their lines uncluttered by greenery, vegetation absent from a vista dominated by stone.

Sparse, non-recognized lifeforms—solid, smooth shapes that moved with a slow, mesmerizing purpose—glided through the weeping rock formations. They gathered in quiet, geometric clusters near the water’s edge, their deliberations punctuated by soft, resonant clicks. The humid air gusted with a metallic chill and carried strange, crystalline sounds: sharp chimes, low hums, like wind whistling through alien architecture.

Scattered ruins—walls of volcanic glass encased in mineral ivory—lay half-swallowed by the relentless drip and flow. They had a stark, enigmatic beauty—an economy of form that suggested solemn work performed in near-silence. Overhead, in the swirling dimness, vast sheets of precipitation moved like drifting continents, their edges blurred and rippling. In the basins below, slow mists turned the surface into a perfect, shifting mirror that reflected a world at once raw and sculpted—strange, fervent, solitary.
