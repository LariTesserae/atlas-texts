The place is a submerged realm, a world entirely overgrown by water that is cold enough to bite but doesn't quite freeze. It is an ocean, deep and vast, stretching further than any sound or light could reach. In this eternal dimness, the ocean floor is built. Not by geology or the gradual settling of sediment, but entirely by the work of life: great intricate reefs and towering structures of stone, all formed from minerals pulled directly from the water and woven into vast, unbroken cities by slow biological processes. There is no untouched rock, no barren patch of sand. Every inch is made, crafted, saturated with the dense presence of sessile organisms constructing a world together in near-total darkness. The water itself is almost still, ancient currents moving in slow rhythms over landscapes that have been worn soft and flat. Vegetation thrives here in lush abundance, generations of plants growing on plants, building up layers of biomass on everything. Strange, swollen tubers and fleshy pads float, some reaching down to anchor on the unbroken substrate while others drift forever, buoyed by gas bladders. They glow with soft, internal bioluminescence, casting a dim, unwavering light over the ordered forms of the constructed world. It is quiet, mostly, only the dense medium stirring faintly against itself. No animals swim or crawl here; there are no voices, only the mute work of growth and decay cycling through millennia. This is a world that has settled into its final form, ancient beyond measure.
