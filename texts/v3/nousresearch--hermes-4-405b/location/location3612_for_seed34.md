The city rises from the marsh like a dream of stone. It sprawls vast and dense, a metropolis of tiered ziggurats and winding streets, all carved from the pale, water-smoothed limestone native to this high plateau. Erosion has softened every edge; the city looks millennia old, settled into its own shape like a river stone. Condensation glistens on every surface - weeping rock faces, streets that gleam damply after frequent rains, the very air heavy with moisture you can almost see.

This stone metropolis is eerily quiet. No machines hum, no forges roar; the technological index here is near zero, a pre-tool civilization if there's any civilization left at all. The only sounds are the soft drip of water and the sighing wind. Vegetation is sparse - some moss and lichen clinging to the stones, a few hardy weeds in sheltered spots. Wildlife too is rare, just the occasional hardy bird or scuttling creature, their paths crossing in near-silence. The world feels balanced, complete in its slow deliquescence.

It's cool at this altitude, the sky a normal brightness but filtered through the mists. The scale is enormous, large enough for its own microclimate, a self-contained universe of stone and water. Despite the stillness, there's a sense of slow motion, of geological and ecological processes unfolding with glacial inevitability. Weeping rocks and condensing atmospheres are this world's heartbeat.
