The air is damp, hot, and thick with the promise of transformation. This is a world under construction—a raw, embryonic geology just coming into being. No tool has ever touched this place; it is a landscape of pure nature, devoid of animal life, built environments, and technology alike.

Here, vast plates of bedrock stretch seemingly forever, their surfaces still raw and unmarked. Scattered craters pock the expanse—huge, bowl-like depressions scooped from the planet's crust. These are not scars of impact, but cradles of creation—nascent calderas where the world's geological heart beats in slow, rumbling pulses.

Some craters lie dormant, half-formed hollows walled in by steep cliffs of ragged stone and loose scree. Others have begun to stir, their floors rippling with growing wrinkles of heat. From their depths rise curling tendrils of steam that condense upon cooler air, dissolving into mists that drift and thin beneath the normal light of day or coalesce into clouds that eventually gleam silver with the promise of rain—a rain that nourishes nothing yet, for in this place, the only growth is geological.

Occasional pools of water gather in pockets of rock where the steam from the craters has cooled and condensed. Rivulets and tiny streams may trickle across the damp stone at the edge of craters, but the waters are shallow and temporary. They seep into cracks and crevices, finding their way down into the thirsty stone as it bakes beneath the hot sun.

Above, currents of rising heat from the craters drive convection, the first hints of weather on a continental scale. Banks of mist swirl and drift in leisurely dances across the sky, dissolving into haze or gathering into roiling clouds. It is quiet here—the only sounds are soft, infrequent drips of water evaporating from the rocks, the whisper of mists condensing in the air, and the sighing of warmed mineral surfaces contracting as they cool.

Change is constant. Geology is the only life. Rock weathers slowly, shaped by wind, rain, and thermal stress as temperatures shift between day and night, expanding and contracting the stone in subtle increments. Scree slowly slides down crater walls. Minerals within the rock crystals reform under heat and pressure, and plumes of steam rise from some craters more thickly now, stinging the air with the mineral scent of creation. And still, the world is vast, open, and still largely empty—an expansive canvas upon which a landscape is slowly being drawn by the patient hand of geological time.
