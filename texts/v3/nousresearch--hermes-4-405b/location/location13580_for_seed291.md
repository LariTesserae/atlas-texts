The world here is a paradox. We stand in the Vale of Nine Rains, a vast, open valley ringed by mountains that weep a constant, cold drizzle. This is a turbid, turbulent place — storms roll in daily from the peaks, washing everything in abrupt downpours and coatings of freezing rain. The soil is black and perpetually damp, thick with the smell of wet stone and iron and something else, something sweet-rotten and mineral-sharp.

But atop this marshy, unstable, and cold place, men have hammered together an empire of steel and glass. Enormous factories and skyscrapers claw their way out of the bog, their foundations sunk deep into the valley's shivering bones. The city is an anthill of activity — steam chugs from vents, gears grind, and the whole settlement hums with electrified energy, all of it contained in this low-slung stretch between the mountains. The light here is normal, unremarkable, just the grey glare of daylight on an overcast day.

Vegetation is surprisingly lush. Vines twist around naked girders, leafy tendrils snaking through windows and clinging to brickwork. Trees spring from courtyards and squares in tight, explosive clusters, their roots punching through asphalt and iron. Even on the metal skeletons of buildings, green things creep and climb, taking back what they can from this manufactured world.

The air is thick with sound, though it's not loud — just the murmur of engines, the hum of pipes, the gentle mechanical song of the place. It's packed with life, too, this city. Every block is crammed with workshops and apartments, their windows glowing with internal light. Steam hisses from grates, and the fug of coal and metal is always on the air, mixed in with the valley's sharp dampness.

There is a strangeness here, though. This is not merely a city built in defiance of its environment — it's built in partnership with it. The rainwater is channeled through a labyrinth of pipes and cisterns, powering turbines and feeding great hissing engines. The cyclopean buildings are clad in metal that drips and rusts even as it's being laid. Everything is in flux, constantly churned and remade by the hands of thousands, by the encroaching green, by the relentless weather. Nothing is permanent, here. The world is alive with the sound of creation and destruction — factories rise and collapse, streets are paved and swallowed by muck, while the city's lifeblood pumps through pipes and wires that snake through the marsh like veins.

Nothing stands still. It is a city being born, a city that is also a machine, a city that is also a living thing.
