The landscape before you is a paradox of emptiness and life—a realm of towering, plant-formed cathedrals under a cool, damp sky. All around, colossal pillars and arches rise from the eroded surface, so ancient and worn by time that the ground beneath them has become nearly flat. But what astonishes most is the sheer *lack* of density: the landscape is vast and hollow between its mighty structures, an expanse of quiet grandeur that seems to stretch on forever.

Every surface of this world is utterly alive. Thick carpets of greenery smother the great pillars, thick vines hang in elegant draperies, and the walls of the arches are encrusted with mosses and deep greens, as if the very stone had become plant. Here, the landscape breathes—condensation glistens on leaves in the cool air, and an occasional gentle rain patters on the foliage, dripping from leafy fronds into the pools of standing water that dot the quiet ground below.

Despite its grandeur, this place is diminutive in scale, no more than a few acres at most—a compact and contained ecosystem. It feels like walking through an empty, sacred grove or a deserted hamlet reclaimed by nature. The only structures here are those grown by nature—there is no sign of human hands at work. And yet, the landscape is not quiet. The air hums with the clamor of life, the rustle of leaves, the drone of insects, the fluttering and singing of birds nesting within every crevice of the verdant architecture.

All is as it always has been here—the slow processes of growth and decay are in perfect balance, nature firmly in control, maintaining this incredibly stable, unchanging world. It is a land that is profoundly and deeply normal in its integrity—a vibrant, dynamic expression of ordinary life thriving in every molecule, every breath of air, every trickle of water.
