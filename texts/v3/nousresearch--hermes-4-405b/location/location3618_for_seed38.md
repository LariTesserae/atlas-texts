The world here is immense, a boundless expanse stretching almost to the edges of sight. As one takes it in, the vast scale becomes apparent - a horizon that curves faintly, hinting at planetary dimensions. This is a landscape of final forms, shaped by incomprehensible stretches of time into ancient, weathered states. Its high elevation places it far above any ocean or lowland plain, yet it remains saturated with moisture. The air is consistently humid and warm, a dim light filtering through the ever-present cloud cover.

Across this continental plateau is draped a dense lattice of plant life, so pervasive that it creates the very substance of the world. The vegetation isn't merely on the land - it *is* the land, forming an all-encompassing living mass. Roots and stems coalesce into a continuous, undulating surface. This botanical architecture supports an abundant fauna, countless small creatures that rustle and creep through the foliage. Yet for all this life, it is a mostly quiet world, filled more with natural sounds than any voices.

The landscape shows only faint traces of any prior structures or artificial alterations. Scattered, small remnants of unknown materials lie half-buried, their origins lost to time. There is no technology in sight, no devices or tools, nothing but purebiological processes at work. The entire system changes with almost imperceptible slowness, its rhythms and cycles unfolding across great stretches of time.

It is a realm defined by its pervasive moisture, the water constantly present at the surface or rising as vapors that shroud the distances in misty veils. In the dim light, everything takes on a dreamlike quality, the lines between land and sky blurring in the perpetual softness. The whole has an otherworldly feel, as if the rules that govern it are not quite those of our own reality. It is a place to lose oneself in wonder, to feel the deep agelessness that suffuses every part of it.
