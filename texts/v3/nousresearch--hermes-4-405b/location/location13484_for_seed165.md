The wind screams. That’s the first and constant fact here, a high, thin howl that carves its presence into the rarefied air. It rakes across an immense, open space—a high, cold plateau stretching far beyond sight under a dim, grey-lit sky. The air here is thin, so thin it seems the world is leaching away, leaving only the most essential fragments of itself: stone, ice, wind, and the vast, echoing void.

The elevation is extreme. Peaks loom in impossible distance, shapes suggesting height beyond reckoning, their summits veiled in perpetual cloud. The plateau floor isn't flat; it undulates in long, shallow swales and low ridges, all scoured by a cold that feels ancient, settled, and absolute. It’s a frozen equilibrium, a place where change seems alien. The rocks beneath are time-polished, their edges rounded by millennia of wind erosion, a landscape of settled forms weathered into a kind of stark, enduring harmony.

Moisture exists here only in pockets, in damp hollows where shadows linger longest, and in the form of hoarfrost that clings to the leeward side of stones. In sheltered crevices, ice gathers in slow, almost imperceptible accretions, meltless and still. Rain is an infrequent visitor, a brief, cold sleet that leaves the stones slick and the air sharp with the scent of wet rock and distant ice.

Vegetation is sparse—a few incredibly hardy lichens cling to the most sheltered angles of rock, pale grey and grey-green, their existence a testament to sheer, stubborn persistence. Perhaps in the deepest, coldest crannies, some form of slow-growing, ultra-resilient moss might find purchase, forming velvety patches that seem more like mineral deposits than living things.

The scale of the place is vast. When the wind momentarily dips, the silence rushes in to fill the space, a profound stillness that makes the occasional return of the wind’s howl feel like a physical blow. Yet even the wind is stable here—a constant, unvarying roar. There are no shifts in its force, no lulls beyond the briefest pauses; it is a sustained, pure note in the world's vast song.

Scattered across this emptiness are the faint marks of something that might once have been more. Small, weathered cairns—perhaps natural, perhaps not—stand as lonely sentinels. Occasionally, an unnatural angle of rock suggests a corner, a fragment of wall, the remains of structures so ancient and eroded their original forms are lost to time. These remnants are few, widely scattered, offering no clues beyond their own enigmatic presence.

There is no technology here. No devices, no artifacts beyond the stones themselves. The world feels stripped to its bones, a realm of pure, elemental forces. The light is dim, grey, and directionless, casting no strong shadows. It's like existing within a perpetual, cold twilight.

Wildlife is rare. Perhaps a solitary, wide-winged bird might be glimpsed far above, riding the fierce winds, its cry swallowed by the greater roar. Or perhaps something small and furtive moves among the rocks, unseen, its existence only hinted at by the faintest of disturbances in the patterns of frost.

And then there is the weirdness. This high, howling emptiness is where reality begins to fray. The geometry of the place feels subtly wrong. Distances seem mutable, shifting with a trick of the light or a moment's inattention. Sounds warp and twist; a whisper might carry impossibly far, or the scream of the wind might suddenly split into harmonic layers, tones that vibrate in the bones rather than the ears. Ice forms in impossible, crystalline structures that seem to hold fractured light within their depths. The sky itself might flicker, as if glimpsed through a flaw in an old recording, light momentarily stuttering or colors bleeding through the grey.

This landscape feels less like a place and more like an ancient, slow-collapsing echo of one. The rules here are not those of the world below. Gravity might weaken slightly in certain areas, unexplained shimmers might dance at the edge of vision, and the silence, when it comes, might carry the weight of something vast and incomprehensible listening. It is a place of frozen equilibrium and dynamic breakdown, a cold, empty, echoing expanse where the wind sings the song of the void.
