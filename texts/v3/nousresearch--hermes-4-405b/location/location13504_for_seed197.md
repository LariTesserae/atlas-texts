The Glade of Resonant Petrichor is a place the world seems continuously to forget. It sits low, cradled almost, within the gentle bow of forgotten hillsides that fade swiftly into an unhelpful distance. Its scale is intimate, like a single, perfect room somewhere in the arteries of a cavernous, derelict palace—a vast, hollow space possessing only the barest, concentrated essence of what once filled it. It feels like stumbling upon a stage still set for a long-finished play.

Little distinguishes the Glade's immediate borders. There’s no threshold, no visible rune or canopy gate. Instead, one simply becomes aware that they are *inside*, enveloped by an air that remembers water too completely. The temperature is a consistent, warm bath, the sort of air that leaves no part of you untouched. It presses down, close and comforting, against your skin, your throat, your lungs. Intermittently, a slow, heavy mist gathers, entire veils of moisture thick as unstitched silk, and in these moments, the light poor, distant, ungenerous while elsewhere beyond the glade’s unseen boundary brightness insistently glares. This dampness hangs like condensation on a windowpane, sluggish rivers of silver breathing down leaves and stems that remain persistently, stubbornly dry.

Almost nothing grows here. The Glade's single, defining feature is a solitary stand of colossal flora, a living amalgamation of impossible scale and density. This thing—it’s more than a tree, less than a forest—that dominates and defines the center. It fills every spatial dimension in its immediate vicinity. Bark the deep bruise-purple of storm clouds, thickly layered as if inwardly bleeding, rises in coiling, impossible columns from the dry earth, splintering into great black limbs and twigs like frozen lightning. Where the dizzying height yields to the heavy air, vast, broad leaves spread in sagging, overlapping layers. These leaves are thick and smooth, beaded with slick moisture that refuses to drip. They form a desperate, continuous canopy, an immense ceiling over the strange place. The few, withered tendrils of vine and root that have escaped the central biomass do not so much grow as fade into the still, heavy air, thinning into tatters and sighs of dangling, rootlike threads.

This ecology is a whisper held against the deafening reality of the Glade. It’s hard to tell where plant ends and structure begins; the massive, complicated florascape stands as plant-as-architecture, filling this small, hollowed-out space with hard, tensile surfaces of serrated leaf-edge and softened portals of broad frond-doors. Abundant fauna teem across these living surfaces. Armored, iridescent beetles, their shells like fragments of captured moonlight, creep in slow, deliberate parades across the spongy, porous surfaces of the leaves. Lizards patterned like shifting stains of moss and dew slip between knotted bark, their tongues flicking the humid air as they cling to high ridges where the light makes them glitter. Other things crawl, too, visible only as suggestions of motion, shadows with too many legs or wings that are simply wrong, patterns of movement that don't match any known, living anatomy.

No crafted devices exist here. Tools, metals, intricately designed things, these are impossible. The Glade is composed solely of soft earth and the overwhelming certainty of this one, vast living ecosystem. Whatever purpose or intention ever dwelled here, it came before hands could shape stone or hands could fashion tools from bone. Whether time or nature authored the Glade’s state or whether it was made by means yet undreamt, remains entirely uncertain, the place’s only explanation.

The air here thrums with a low, almost glutinous sound, a roar that presses against every surface, every pore, like the unyielding stridence of distant machinery or the gathering cacophony of an approaching storm. In its elusive center, a solitary and colossal phallic bloom curves upwards toward the leaf canopy, its lips just barely parted. From within, a universal, seething growl emanates—a saw-toothed static too rhythmic, harsh, and entirely without source or modulation to exist in any comprehensible reality.

Time here moves with an almost lazy, indifferent speed. Even when nothing seems to be *happening*, everything is in motion. The slow chase of lizard and beetle across the impossible bark continues in a soft tumult beneath the impossible symphony of the great bloom’s growl. High up in the interior spaces of the canopy, long, bright vines hang like viscous drips of sap, their glowing ends shifting and swirling with impossible, languid life.

Nothing ever changes. No-one leaves. Nothing stops. Nothing is added or subtracted. The Deer, seven skeletal figures posed as if caught mid-stride, are frozen on the soft ground beneath the impossible plant, fixed in an endless, unfinished act of graceful escape. Some have small, crystalline growths—like jagged flowers of fused bone—piercing their sides, breakable blossoms of persistent, iridescent blue that sprout from spines and tines. All are crowned with intricate antlers of carved, ash-brown bone, their spiraling shapes opening like fans to reveal entire miniature, self-contained scenes.

This is your inheritance, Kei. A place of mad wonders, teetering on the edge of the workable, an impossible question with no clear answer.
