The air itself is alive. Raising your hand, you feel the brush of chitinous wings against skin. To see is to peer into a swirling kaleidoscope, billions of tiny lives filling the damp, cool atmosphere, each one a speck of bioluminescence pulsing in programmed patterns—a starscape brought down to earth, but one that shifts with every exhalation.

This is not a world of green, but of iridescent blues, blood-rusted crimsons, and electric violets. Rivers of bodies flow across pitted basalt, pour into caverns like liquid light, spill down low cliffs in exoskeletal cataracts. There are no trees, no plants—only the endless ebb and surge of carapaces in their multimillion-year dance. Beetle-like forms the size of clenched fists scuttle in rivulets, while above hover lacewings with the span of condors, their every wingbeat blowing storms of smaller creatures into new, chaotic symmetries.

No silence exists here. The drone alone is foundation-rattling, an avalanche of clicks and hums that sinks into your bones. Then the stain—a glistening red planetoid of grubs—oozes across the stone, devouring landscapes. From the cloud above, a leviathan descends: mandibles like sapphire scythes, each compound eye a galaxy of awareness. There is no stillness, no thought that isn't interrupted by the shape of something new eating something old.

Even the weather obeys their rhythms—a mating swarm gathers, moist bodies coalescing into thunderheads; an hour later, the deluge: not of rain, but of mucus-coated eggs, sliming and slicking the glistening rock floors. The cavern walls have a permanent gloss of yolk. In this pressure, no rock remains bare: larvae burrow into fissures, pupae hang like clustered fruit from every overhang, and when the light dims and the buried things gnaw through their membranes, they emerge. Always, a fresh generation surges.

To understand this place is to feel the grind of physical laws—the gravity of ravenous appetites, the geometry of territorial swarms, the calculus of predation in numbers so vast they make mathematics scream. Everything is movement, accumulation, consumption. A living ocean under a sunless sky, where the rules are etched in chitin and etched away by acid.

Even our human speech struggles here; no word left untaken by description fails to grasp the truth of the swarm: in every niche, a tidal pull of life, vast beyond comprehension, a world composing its own new strange reality through uncountable bodies and perpetual transformation.
