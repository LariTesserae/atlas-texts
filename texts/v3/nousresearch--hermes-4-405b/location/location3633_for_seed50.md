The place that exists here is a hidden pocket at the top of the world, a cavernous space opening within the peak of an impossibly high mountain. It's submerged: the cold azure water of an ocean fills this cavern entirely, leaving only a small, V-shaped wedge of air at the very apex. This is the breathing room, felt rather than seen in the dark. The water churns violently everywhere else, a roaring, crashing turbulence that drowns out all other sound — yet it's contained, a furious embryonic sea within a single submerged room. The scale is disorienting; it feels like drowning at the summit of the world.

The cavern itself is raw, geologically unfinished. In places the walls are smooth basalt, freshly cooled, as if the mountain has only just risen from the sea floor. In other spots, raw, jagged edges of rock protrude, unfinished emitters fresh from the Earth's heart, still warm. Waterfalls cascade down the raw rock faces from cracks and fissures in the skull of the mountain. Mineral-rich water oozes from the walls. Everything is at a slant, the only true level the surface of the water beneath you.

This high grotto feels largely empty, a vast sunken chamber of cold water and dark promising stone. It should feel claustrophobic, submerged as it is, but instead it yawns like a nascent god's mouth opening for the first time. The sparse vegetation exists as clusters of pale, glowing moss clinging to the cavern walls just above the waterline, like stars emerging on a wet night. Strange fronds of some deep-sea plant, luminous and ragged as if chewed, drift slowly on the currents — sporadic moments of biology in the rock and roar.

A few scattered fragments speak of past habitation: the splintered edges of wooden scaffolding poke up from the dark water in a few small, isolated clusters, each forming a smaller platform of human scale, now claimed by the sea. A single primitive artifact rests on the largest cluster — a wedge of waterlogged, warped wood. From this height, you can see that almost nothing but the mountain's internal stone fills the volume. The air is cool with the spray and chill of high altitude, yet the light here is deceptively normal and pervasive, an enchanted, sourceless illumination that reveals the churning water and unfolding stone without glare. Rare creatures flicker in the depths: slick, silver things like the ghosts of fish, bleached eels writhing in the foam. All is movement and sound — deafening, relentless, a nascent world roaring into awareness.
