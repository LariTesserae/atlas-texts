The entrance is a narrow cleft in sunbaked rock, barely wide enough for a single person to slip through sideways. Beyond, the air cools rapidly, carrying with it the scent of damp earth, unknown blossoms, and a faint undercurrent of ozone. There is no water here, every surface dry to the touch, yet the space is lush with vegetation—strange, pale fungi cluster in honeycomb patterns on walls that feel unnaturally smooth, as if formed by some unseen pressure. Among them, ferns with brittle, glass-like fronds reach upwards, and orb-like seed pods hang from delicate, trailing vines, glowing faintly with an internal bioluminescence. The air is warm, still, and heavy with the constant noise—a low, resonant hum that vibrates through the rock underfoot. This hum is punctuated by sharp, chittering calls and the skittering of unseen insects, though sometimes larger rustles imply the movement of larger creatures well-hidden among the fungal growths.

This place is a pocket, a hidden micro-environment buried just beneath the surface. Its features are sharp and distinct, the fungal shelves and columns appearing almost freshly formed, as if the environment itself is perpetually new. The vegetation sprouts in scattered clumps and isolated colonies, leaving vast stretches of the smooth, featureless walls and floor bare, amplifying the sense of space despite the close quarters. Little artifacts, primitive and crudely made, appear with surprising frequency: sharpened stones, pieces of obsidian worked into scrapers or spear points, remnants of fiber-woven mats, or an occasional shallow carving. They are few enough to seem sparse, scattered tokens of an intermittent presence that has passed through or briefly sheltered here. Light filters down from narrow cracks or fissures high above in the cavern's ceiling, dim and diffuse, encouraging the weird bioluminescence of the plant life. Tiny, six-legged insectoids flit through the air. They produce the loud hum, their wings moving so fast they blur into translucency. Larger, beetle-like things with iridescent carapaces scuttle over and under the mushrooms, while something—which might be a very large, slow-moving insect, or a small, reclusive vertebrate, with a pale, segmented body and multiple sets of stalked eyes—occasionally shifts amid the densest clusters of vegetation. Despite the life and the sound, the environment is strangely static—no wind stirs here, the air is utterly still, and the glowing plants seem locked in a perpetual twilight. It feels as if time has paused in this small pocket, everything held in an unchanging equilibrium.
