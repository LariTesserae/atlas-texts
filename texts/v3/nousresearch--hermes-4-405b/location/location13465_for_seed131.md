The space reveals itself only slowly: a cramped, dripping, hidden pocket within what must be an infinitely larger structure. There is no unmaking here—it is all crafted, industrially assembled, though in a style and scale that seems less built than grown, or more accurately, *cured* into this final, permanent form. Water is omnipresent: perpetually weeping from joints in the flat, nearly featureless ceiling, beading on every smooth, hard surface, and pooling in hollows worn deep into the floor. The air is warm, heavy with condensation, and thick with a perpetual, deafening thrumming—the sound of enormous, unseen machinery in constant, static operation.

The thick, wet, utterly empty space expands—a single room, a cavern of industry—isolated from the whole. Dense moss blankets every surface: the damp-mottled floor, the towering featureless walls, the softly dripping pipes of some immense, forgotten factory system overhead. In the low light of unseen sources, the cavern feels like a hidden pool within a machine. The thrumming of vast, indifferent engines vibrates through the walls, through the floor, through the lush growth. The air is thick and damp—as if exhaled from some deep, active, industrial furnace.

This is a place of ancient equilibrium: a hidden pocket buried within an unthinkably vast complex, a space that has been eroded to a near-flatness over uncounted eons. It is a made place, constructed with deliberate hands. The space is nearly entirely empty—a void filled only with the lush vegetation and dripping water. The thrumming of distant industrial engines forms a constant, monotonous backdrop.
