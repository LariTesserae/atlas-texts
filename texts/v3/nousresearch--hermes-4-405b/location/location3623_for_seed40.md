The landscape begins as an immense, high-altitude valley, its stone walls rising sharp and freshly carved, young in geological age. Within this vast, open basin, the air is thin, cool, carrying the scent of intermittent moisture – of condensation forming on cold surfaces and the recent memory of high, thin rain. From the valley floor and clinging like dense moss to the walls rises a city, no trace of the natural world remaining beneath it. Every surface is quarried stone and cast iron, slate and riveted steel, steam-polished copper and thick panes of faintly greenish, slightly warped glass.

This city isn't merely built; it is constructed, with an industrial purpose running through every level, every towering structure bolted to the cliff faces, every blocky edifice erupting from the valley floor. Walkways rib through the structures like conjugated veins; external elevators, pistons groaning with the slow release of pressurized steam, crawl up the buildings' sides. Pipes snake everywhere, sighing with internal flow, their surfaces often beading with moisture that gathers in the constant, deep hum of the place. That hum is the dominant sound, the very atmosphere vibrating, punctuated only by the slow, rhythmic clang of massive, unseen mechanisms and the distant hiss of steam valves releasing pressure in timed, languid bursts. It’s not a silent city, but a deeply resonant one, each sound amplified and distorted by the structures, bouncing back from the valley walls to create a low, omnipresent drone that absorbs sharp noises, leaving only echoes and the slow heartbeat of industry.

Light pours down, painfully bright, refracting through the omnipresent haze of condensed vapor and exhaust – an industrial fug that turns the high-altitude sunlight into something thick, almost tangible. This dense atmosphere seems to press in, carrying the scents of hot metal, ozone, and a faint, underlying dampness, the air heavy with suspended particulates that glitter and swirl, catching the light. It feels as if the air itself is a physical substance, thick enough to slow movement through it, dense enough to distort vision.

Despite the overwhelming presence of iron, stone, and the persistent hum of engines, life is abundant here. It scales the structures, clings to girders, fills the gaps where the constant moisture condenses and drips. Thick, rubbery plants with waxy, dark green leaves grow in riotous profusion from every crevice, their roots wedging into masonry, their fronds brushing against steam vents. Strange, multi-limbed creatures, chitinous and glistening, scuttle across metallic surfaces, climbing the ever-present pipes, adhering to walls. Others drift through the air like living dirigibles, their translucent bodies filled with lighter-than-air gases, filtering nutrients from the dense miasma. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their calls subsumed into the ambient drone, adding another layer to the omnipresent sound.

Weirdness permeates this place. Gravity seems mutable, shifting in pockets, stronger in some streets than others, making some climb feel strangely easy while descent is perilous. Perspective itself warps at the edges of vision; structures seem to lean at impossible angles when not observed directly, straightening when one turns to face them. Puddles of condensed vapor on rust-stained platforms reflect nothing but swirling, impossible geometries, glimpses of fractured, other places. The edges of the city itself, especially where the sheer cliffs meet the sky, blur and shimmer, the line between solid structure and the thin, cold air beyond dissolving into a rippling distortion. Clocks, where they exist, each run to their own time, some hands sweeping backwards, others vibrating in place, their faces flickering. Reality here isn't a fixed state, but a slow, viscous fluid, its rules bending, stretching, and occasionally, snapping. A sense of patient, inevitable decay pervades, a slow entropy. Structures groan under their own weight or some internal stress, shedding flakes of rust that drift like metallic snow. Vines visibly tighten their grip on iron supports, and the glass in the ancient, multi-paned windows flows, thickening at the base over decades. It’s a place of immense age, locked in a slow-motion unraveling, where change is a constant, creeping force, inevitable and inexorable.
