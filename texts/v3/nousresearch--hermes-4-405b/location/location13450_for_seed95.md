The air here is warm and thick, a constant weight that seems to press the world into itself. It’s a high place, baked over eons into something almost flat: an ancient, elevated plain where the ground isn’t hard rock but layers of fine, dense sediment, compressed by time into a surface that drinks the infrequent rain greedily. When the storms do come, water pools in shallow depressions, filling the air with the scent of damp stone and awakened dust; afterward, those same pools vanish as if they never were, leaving only darker stains on the thirsty earth.

Vegetation here is sparse—just the occasional cluster of dry, spiny shrubs that cling stubbornly to the edges of old, eroded channels, their roots digging deep for whatever moisture they can find. Yet despite the meager plant life, the plain teems with a dazzling abundance of animals. They move in slow, overlapping processions: herds of immense, lumbering creatures whose broad, flat feet barely stir the dust as they pass. Their hides are thick and leathery, deeply creased with patterns that resemble the cracked earth beneath them. Flocks of flightless birds with iridescent feathers dart between the larger animals, pecking at the ground or riding atop the vast backs of the herd-beasts. The sheer density of life is staggering—countless eyes watching from every direction, a tapestry of motion and sound that seems to stretch unbroken to the distant horizon.

Amid this overwhelming throng, the traces of something older punctuate the landscape: weathered pillars of stone that stand at odd angles, their surfaces so worn by time that whatever markings they might have once bore are now little more than faint smudges against the grain. These remnants do not look built, exactly—more like the last surviving fragments of some immense, naturally occurring structure, perhaps the mineralized bones of creatures far larger than any that now walk the plain.

The light here is ordinary—a warm, buttery sun illuminating the scene with no particular drama. Yet there is an uncanny quality to the place nonetheless. The way the herds move, their slow, unhurried pace… it feels deliberate, as if the entire ecosystem moves with a single, collective purpose. The silence is profound—a low thrum of shifting bodies and the occasional rustle of dry vegetation, but next to nothing in the way of cries or calls. When the animals do speak, it’s in whispered rasps and low, guttural moans that carry strangely far in the dense, heavy air.

The whole place feels steeped in deep time—erosion etched into every surface, the slow rhythm of life playing out as it must have for millennia. And yet, for all its age, the plain feels strangely mutable… as if the slow, deliberate movements of its countless inhabitants could, given enough epochs, reshape the world entirely.
