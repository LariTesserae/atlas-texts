The darkness here isn't merely the absence of light—it feels heavy, almost tactile, as though the air itself is thick with it. Sound, too, is muffled: the region holds a profound, almost expectant quiet, broken rarely and briefly. But where light fails and sound falters, other senses heighten—or perhaps something else perceives what you cannot.

At the edge of perception, the world begins to bleed and warp. The ground underfoot—often slick, sometimes pooling with shallow, cloudy water from the perpetually saturated earth—occasionally pulses with a deep, resonant hum. The sparse vegetation seems to shiver not with wind, but with an unseen energy, casting faint, shimmering outlines that resolve into nothing before your eyes can fully focus. This is a place where reality frays at the edges.

This strangeness is woven into the very fabric of a landscape defined by its openness and low elevation. Vast, flat expanses stretch out in the darkness, punctuated by scattered heaps of debris and the occasional angular silhouette of abandoned structures. Near these ruins, the air smells of ozone and oil—a faint, mineral tang that carries over the damp earth-scent and the occasional whiff of unseen stagnant water trapped in depressions. Patches of coarse, hardy moss and the occasional defiant reed cluster—who knows what strange sustenance roots them in this place?—cling to the wet, almost slick ground, sparse yet tenacious. Footsteps squelch softly, and the irregular drip of condensation punctuates the low, omnipresent hum.

Among these relics of industry—half-submerged machines, skeletal frameworks reclaimed by corrosion and patches of wetland—among all this, the fauna is surprisingly abundant. The darkness teems with life: slick amphibians with eyes that occasionally catch the low, ambient glow near the few functional electrical apparitions; small, hard-shelled insects that skitter through the dense, still air with surprising weight; and silent, winged shapes that glide with unnerving grace through the humid dark, sometimes colliding with unseen barriers that ripple like heat haze. Their movements are languid, unhurried; time itself feels slow here, as though the gradual processes of decay and transformation have been stretched into patient cycles that mark epochs rather than days.
