The place is a pocket of damp cold nestled low within the city's intricate fabric, a single ruined courtyard barely large enough to echo with footsteps or prayer. Crumbling walls of nanocrete, their surfaces subtly self-repairing even now, rise around a space perpetually slick with condensation; water beads on every edge, slicks the pale stones underfoot where they are not already submerged beneath still, reflective puddles. Somehow this dampness never seems to lessen, a quiet persistence against the cold that leaches warmth from your fingertips the moment you step inside.

Vegetation is sparse here — just a few skeletal ferns clinging to the cracks where wall meets broken flagstone, pale and tough and intricately veined with circuits that drink the cold water, process the meager light filtering down from whatever sliver of sky remains visible above the encroaching rooftops and galleries that press close around this space. The air feels thick, close, not only with moisture but with density: too many structures layered too tightly, too many unseen functions humming just below the threshold of hearing. It is a place of near-silence, only the drip of water, the soft *shhhh* of your own breath frosting the air as you move.

For it is also a place of emerging erosion: the nano-repair buckling against some deeper law, the very stones beginning to lose their sharp edges not to weather but to a kind of… conceptual wear, as if reality itself is rubbing thin here. Frost blooms in fractal patterns across the walls, spirals eating into the material, and where the frost is thickest, the world seems to… double. A flicker at the edge of vision. The ferns ripple as if their image were projected on water. At times the cold puddles hold reflections that do not match the ruined grandeur of the space above them: glimpses of deserts, of cityscapes turned inside-out, of geometries that cannot exist. The air thrums with something you feel in your teeth, a frequency that resonates deep in the bones of the place, as if the pavement, the walls, the very idea of enclosure is singing a single, subsonic note.

This is not a wild place — it is pervasively built, a remnant of the city folding in on itself, architecture that once held meaning reconfiguring into near-nonsense. The rules that bind it seem subtly other; step through the wrong archway when the light hits a particular angle, when the frost achieves a certain density, and you might find that distance no longer behaves, that gravity has gentle currents, that time flows thick and slow. It is a pocket of intricate collapse: hidden, bounded, densely packed with the ghosts of purpose, resolutely urban… yet the laws of that urbanity are fraying. Whatever built this place once commanded forces beyond current physics, and their fingerprints persist in the warp of local reality, slow and cold and damp and teeming with the quiet hum of the utterly, beautifully, terrifyingly strange.
