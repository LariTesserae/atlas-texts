The ocean is everywhere, a hot and restless presence. Its surface churns with a perpetual storm—rain lashes down, not in drops but in a crushing wall of water, merging seamlessly with the heaving swells. Below, in the turbid depths where a submerged city lies, the water presses in with an almost solid weight. This city is an open expanse, no longer tightly contained but sprawling into a region-spanbing necropolis, yet simultaneously dense with its own decaying presence. Its structures are scattered, like isolated bones in a vast grave, eroded into fresh, sharp angles that speak of violent formation rather than age. Only fragments remain: skeletal foundations and the jagged outlines of industrial complexes, now hollowed by time and the endlessly shifting water. The dim light filtering through the turbulent surface casts everything in shifting, nebulous gloom, though here and there the ruins glow with the sputtering remnants of broken machinery, still radiating a strange industrial heat. These machines thrum and clatter, their engines engaged in a futile struggle against the inevitable, but the overwhelming sound is the ceaseless roar of the ocean's rage and the groan of fabric tearing as reality itself frays. For this flooded realm is no stable place. The laws of physics are unravelling, and the world is in constant, chaotic turmoil. The very matter of the drowned factories and the water that envelops them squirms and mutates, foundations dissolve and reform in impossible geometries, and fleeting geometries of light—ghostly, sourceless—flicker in the dense, suffocating murk. It is a realm devoid of life, a storm-tossed mausoleum where the only presences are the ruins, the broken technology, and the violent, ceaseless transformation of everything into some grotesque other, all under the crushing, aquatic sky. Yet, despite the absence of life, there is a strange vitality here, a hyperactive energy that seethes through the water and the dissolving structures—a bazaar of annihilation where the noise of the world ending is matched only by the sound of its constant, turbulent rebirth.
