The place is a subterranean fissure, a narrow canyon sunk deep below the surface. It is small — the size of a great hall — its walls separated by perhaps twenty paces at the widest point. Two or three thousand feet above, a crack in the earth’s surface admits a torrent of light — a vertical river of near-daylight that floods the space with illumination so intense it bleaches color from the upper half of the canyon. Even at the bottoms of the walls, far below where the sun’s direct rays reach, the air glows.

The heat is the heat of sun on stone, warmth radiated back from the canyon walls. The air is still and carries the scent of warmed minerals and something else — the faint, sharp tang of ozone, as if a distant storm has passed or is gathering. Here, in this deep place, the atmosphere is thick, humid, filled with the slow exhalations of stone that has been breathing its trapped moisture into the light for millennia. Drops of water collect on the undersides of overhangs and fall in slow, irregular patterns — some strike rock and shatter into mist, others land in shallow pools that gather in the fissure’s floor. This is the only water in the void: no streams, no rivers, only condensation, as if the canyon itself sweats.

Vegetation is sparse. Small colonies of bioluminescent fungi cling to the lower walls at the edges of the light, their caps glowing with a soft blue or green luminescence visible even in the bright air. A few stunted, pale ferns grow in crevices where moisture seeps from the rock. Their fronds are thin, almost transparent, adapted to the intense light and heat.

The walls of the canyon are a palimpsest of geological time — layers of sediment compressed into sandstone and limestone, intruded by veins of granite and basalt. They are dry and weathered, sculpted by the slow action of heat, light, and dripping water into fluted columns, deep grooves, and overhanging ledges. Some are streaked with the iron-red stains of rust, others with the greenish-blue of copper. The erosion is not violent but patient, a settling into form over eons. The air is filled with the products of this slow decay: tiny flakes of stone, the dust of ages, a mineral grit that coats every surface and hangs in the still air.

Above, the sky — the ragged tear in the earth that admits the light — is a thin, raw blue. It is too bright to see anything beyond the fissure’s upper edges. The light fills the canyon with a sense of verticality, as though the space is tilted on end. The air is quiet — the only sounds are the slow drip of water, the scrape of stone on stone as a pebble falls from high above, the dry rustle of the wind far above the fissure’s mouth. These are infrequent sounds, each one distinct, each one amplifying the vast silences between.

The fauna is abundant but hidden — creatures of rock and shadow adapted to the light-saturated verticality. Small, pale lizards with translucent skin that pulses with internal light scuttle across the walls. They move slowly, deliberately, each movement a cautious calculation against the bright backdrop. In the deeper shadows, where overhangs create pockets of relative dark, clusters of chitinous, crab-like creatures clamber over the stones. Their carapaces are intricately patterned — geometric mosaics of black and white that scatter and confuse the light. They are scavengers, feeding on fallen insects, mineral deposits, the occasional carcass of a light-drunk bird that tumbles into the canyon. Their movements are precise, economical, as if conserving energy in the heat. They use simple tools — sharpened stones to pry crustaceans from crevices, strips of fibrous plant matter woven into crude nets to catch falling debris.

The rules of this world are the rules of light and shadow, of heat and slow decay. Nothing here is built — no structures, no artifacts — only what the world itself provides. The tech level is primitive, hand-tools only, made of stone and bone and fiber. Nothing is made of metal; nothing is fabricated. The light is the governing force — it defines where life can exist, shapes the behavior of every creature, dictates the slow unfolding of geological time. Change happens over long durations: the gradual deposition of dust on the canyon floor, the slow shift of stones, the growth and decay of the sparse vegetation.

This place is a pocket of emptiness in the earth — a near-void filled with light and slow time. It is strange, alien, a place where the ordinary rules of life are suspended, where the sun burns even underground.
