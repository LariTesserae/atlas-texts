The darkness is total, a velvet blackness that swallows all sight, but the air pulses with life, thick with the hum of countless tiny engines and the rustle and slither of unseen creatures. The ground underfoot is surprisingly soft — a wet, yielding surface, spongy and peppered with shallow puddles that occasionally splash as something moves through them. The warmth of the place hangs heavy in the air, stagnant and humid, condensing on uncovered skin and the dripping surfaces of porous rock walls that feel impossibly ancient, smoothed and hollowed over epochs into shallow, cavernous concavities. This is a hamlet-scale grove, borders defined by the weeping rocks and the damp earth underfoot.

The ruins of an unfathomable technology are scattered here and there, jutting from the soft ground like jagged teeth or half-buried monoliths, their edges unnervingly sharp against the damp, eroded surfaces. Their material is unknown, glimmering faintly with a dark iridescent light that gives away nothing of its origins. The sounds are a strange symphony of whirring, clicking, and distant, mournful calls that echo eerily in the near-void, the vast emptiness between the small pockets of life. It feels like you're listening to the creak and groan of some unfathomable machine, a slow, gradual process of change that takes ages to complete. And then, almost imperceptibly at first, the ground shifts—a slow, seismic pulse that vibrates through everything. It is a world still settling into its final form, a landscape defined by the quiet mourning of the oldest ruins and the profound stillness between the hidden lives that teem all around.
