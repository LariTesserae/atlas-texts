The heat is the first thing you notice — a thick, wet heat that presses against your skin as if the air itself is condensing. This is a high place, perched near the roof of the world, and yet water is everywhere, weeping from the stone faces of the cliffs that tower above and pooling in shallow depressions across the rocky terrain. Mist curls and writhes, clinging to the ground, and you can see the ghostly outlines of buildings scattered through the haze — sleek, impossibly slender structures that vanish and reappear as the fog shifts. They are the color of rain, blending into the landscape until only their occasional sharp edges give them away. These are not the works of any familiar technology; they feel alien, as though they have grown from the mountain itself.

Vegetation is abundant, a riot of strange ferns and broad-leafed plants that glisten with moisture. Orchids cling to crevices, their roots finding purchase in the perpetually damp stone. The air hums with life — the drone of unseen insects, the cries of birds that flit through the mist like living shards of stained glass. Larger creatures move through the greenery, their eyes glinting from the shadows between the dense foliage, their forms indistinct but undeniably present. The ground is uneven, slick with water and thick moss that swallows sound. Beneath your feet, you can feel the slow pulse of the mountain, the way the stone has settled into its final form after eons of erosion. Time here feels stretched, languid, and the landscape seems to change only with the gradual turning of seasons you cannot name.

This is a world that holds its secrets close, a place where the familiar rules of nature feel somehow askew. The light is normal enough, but it filters through the mist in strange ways, casting long, distorted shadows. The air shimmers with heat, even as water drips from every surface. It is a place of contradictions, of impossible balances held in delicate tension. Strange and wonderful, unsettling and serene — it invites you to linger, to lose yourself in its deep, wet heart.
