The scale is regional, a vast cavern city that unfolds in interconnected chambers and tunnels, some broad enough to hold fog banks, others narrowing into barely traversable fissures. It’s all below surface - perhaps only a few dozen meters deep, but in an endless horizontal sprawl. The air is cold, carrying a mineral chill from the stone that forms every surface here. Water is everywhere: it trickles down the cavern walls in thousands of tiny rivulets, pools in shallow depressions, and gathers in whole subterranean lakes where the terrain dips. Condensation drips constantly from the ceilings, a gentle percussion that echoes through the otherwise silent spaces.

The vegetation is lush but alien, adapted to near-darkness. Thick moss carpets every horizontal surface, glowing faintly with bioluminescent greens and blues. Fungal growths erupt from cracks in the stone – some bulbous and soft, others spiky and brittle. Vines hang in thick curtains from the cavern roofs, some bearing strange fruit that glimmer like jewels in the gloom. It’s a young ecosystem, still actively shaping itself; you can see where new rivulets are carving paths through the moss, where rockfalls have created fresh niches for fungi to colonize.

This isn't a built environment in any conventional sense, yet it has an urban density. The caverns are saturated with life – every crevice holds some plant or animal, every pool teems with amphibious creatures. Gelatinous filters cling to the walls, strands of luminous algae drift in the still air, and colonies of mushroom-like structures rise in clusters that resemble crude dwellings. The creatures that move through this space are as densely packed as residents in a crowded city: blind fish glide through the waterways, pale frogs croak in the damp shadows, and six-legged predators with glowing eyes stalk silently through the fungal forests.

Despite the constant activity, the soundscape is muted. Water drips echoing from distant chambers, the occasional skittering of small creatures, the low thrum of biological processes – respiration of giant fungi, digestive gurgles of unseen organisms. There are no machines, no tools, no signs of anything we would recognize as technology. But rules govern this place: water flows downward, light blooms where moisture collects, predators follow the scent trails of prey. It’s an active ecosystem in motion, cycling moisture and nutrients in endless loops, carving its own landscapes through patient erosion, and filling every possible niche with pulsing, glowing, breathing life.
