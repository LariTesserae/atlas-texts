The place arrives as a fever-dream of green on the edge of vision, down where the rules of the world above begin to fray. I descend into a cavern whose ceiling is invisible, the walls swelling into the dim distance, the entire space suffused with a hazy, sourceless light that bleaches the air to the pale gold of dawn. The air is thick, dense with the exhalations of an empire of plants. The heat is a physical presence, a humid embrace that even the occasional drip of moisture cannot dispel. The air is saturated with the scents of growing things: damp soil, new leaves, the tang of sap and the musk of ancient roots. It fills the throat, the nostrils. It is the only thing to breathe.

The vegetation here is a raw throat-song of chlorophyll. It is a single note struck and held. Everywhere, soft-bodied ferns unfurl from every surface, floor and wall and even the spaces where stalactites hang, their tips perpetually weeping into clear pools that form in hollows worn by time. The plants here are woven together, a tapestry of green that covers the stone completely. Great pulsating mosses spread in carpets thick enough to lose a hand in. The place is damp; the plants glisten with condensation that gathers in droplets on the tips of leaves, at the bases of stems, in the whorls of bark on the few thin trees that stretch upward, their branches draped with epiphytes and heavy, weeping fronds. They feed on the moisture in the air, on themselves. They do not need the earth.

This place is a cathedral of becoming. The stone walls weep a slow cream of dissolved rock, new-made earth that gathers in crevices and hollows. Stalactites descend from above like crooked teeth; from below, stalagmites begin their ascent, thrusting up like the prongs of an anchor from some drowned ship. Pools of water glint in the hollows, their surfaces unruffled, their depths hidden. They hum low, a sound pitched below hearing, a vibration felt in the soles of the feet. It is the sound of the earth dreaming itself into being. The stone is soft, porous, new. It is raw geology, still forming.

The plantgrowth is so dense it almost chokes the space. Fronds and stems press close, brushing against my skin as I pass. The air is thick with spores, dancing in the pale light. The place glows with its own damp life. The light filters through the canopy, a green-gold haze that turns everything to dream. The green grows in every direction, a perpetual becoming, a slow swallowing of everything that is not itself.

Scattered among the growth are the faint marks of previous explorers. A rusted lantern, half-buried in moss. A series of holes in a wall, as if someone hammered a piton there. A broken length of rope. A pale spoor of something that passed this way, a long while ago. But this place erases and rewrites itself. It grows back over itself, a patient, vegetable hand smoothing over the marks of intrusion. The technology here is the technology of the sapling and the mushroom, of the creeping vine and the unfurling fern. It is the technology of roots boring into stone. There is no metal or glass down here, only the soft mechanics of damp and decay and endless rebirth.

The light is everywhere, a diffuse glow that fills the cavern. It bleeds through the lush canopy, painting the air a pale gold. The heat is tropical, thick and wet and languid. It presses down with the weight of a damp hand. This place is a regional space, a vast hollow in the earth, a hidden valley in miniature. It is a self-contained world, a pocket of damp-green heat that breathes to its own rhythm.

Animal life is rare. A flash of iridescent wing in the green gloom, a beetle bigger than a hand slowly making its way up a moss-slick wall. The low thrum of hidden things, the chitter of unseen insects. It is a quiet world. There is little to break the silence but the drip of water, the hum of new roots, the rustle of fronds in some unfelt current of air. The sound is low, a breath held in the chest. A whisper at the edge of hearing. The silence grows exponentially. It is the silence of held breath, of waiting, of something about to wake.

The weirdness is subtle. A sense of being watched from the green, of the plants slowly turning their faces toward the sound of your footsteps. Things grow in spirals here, in fractals, in patterns that repeat until they fill the space. Everything is connected. Everything is part of the same great, dreaming green. There is no sense of time here. No decay, only a constant becoming. No seasons, only the endless hum of growth. The plants never wither. The stones never crack. The pools never empty. The world is stuck in a fever-dream of emergence, a perpetual state of becoming that never quite arrives at being.

There are no cycles here. No rises and falls. No beginnings and endings. This place is its own event horizon, a place where the rules of the world have begun to soften and stretch. The only rule is growth, a slow and patient accretion that swallows everything in the end. It is a dense, rich, humid prison of green. A world unto itself. A place that absorbs all things into itself, that grows and grows until it has swallowed the whole of the earth. It is a hidden world, a green dream that will never wake.
