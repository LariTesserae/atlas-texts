The place exists precariously, perched at an extreme elevation. It is a meticulously constructed cathedral-scale space, a single room seemingly grown and sculpted, not merely assembled. The materials used are impossible to discern as stone or metal or glass; perhaps a seamless, almost liquid Thing-Substance, poured and molded into vaults hundreds of feet high, unfurling with groined ribs and baffled, whispering galleries. Over millennia of slow, deliberate engineering, it has eroded into its current state: deep runnels worn into the floor and along the sculpted walls, carved by persistent trickles of condensed water. The air is warm, thick, and hangs heavy, carried and continually transformed throughout the room by an unobtrusive system of ducts, a lifting fog that resembles white, cottony mold in slow-motion drift around the trembling lights.

Yet, in such a cavernous space, there is an overwhelming sense of emptiness. Here, density unravels. A liquid ground underfoot shifts and hisses, both particulate and flowing. The supporting pillars are vast, irregular, and appear corroded, as if the air itself wore them down. Warm, moist mists rise from hidden culverts in the floor, illuminated by softly pulsing, bioluminescent globes that drift lazily through the air like sleepy fireflies, emitting a low, organ-like hum of complex harmonies. Other lights, smaller and more erratic, race and flit along the high ribs and among the pillars, occasionally winking out, leaving only the unvarying, white-lit bioluminescence to glow wherever it settles. Perhaps they are fragments of the large globes, or another species entirely.

Vegetation flourishes here, nourished by the constant, controlled moisture and the rich, reprocessed air. Thick moss carpets every surface, glistening and wet. Red-brown molds spread in concentric rings, patterned like feathers in stone, while deep green lichens clump on the pillars and in the mist-damp runnels. Flowering vines, thick as wrists, push their way out of fissures in the floor, reaching for the dim light high above. Bright flowers, some yellow, some red, nod gently where they grow thickest, and walls of tangled, snaking roots press into the hollows, their grasping tendrils reaching around and into the room. The strange, bundled pipes that sometimes follow the roots look ancient, made of stony, enameled ceramic looped with wires and set with sensors, occasionally emitting warm fog.

Incredibly, this is all built, a total, fully constructed environment with no natural substrate visible beneath the Thing-Substance. The impression is less of a grounded structure and more of a vast, self-contained machine that might walk if it wished, or detach from whatever mountainside it is anchored to and drift quietly into space. The technology is industrial but oddly biological, half-engineered, half-grown. Steam-venting pipes and dripping faucets feed the condensation that trickles across the floor, collecting into deep, channel-like runnels that follow grooves in the smoothed floor. These runnels lead to sculpted culverts hidden in shadowed corners, where the water slowly drains away to be recycled or repurposed elsewhere in the structure, endlessly recycled.

Fauna is abundant, suited to the protected, isolated, vertical territory of the room. Quick, squirrel-like creatures skitter and dart along high ledges, perhaps drawn by the distant, intermittent flashes of light. Solitary things, like armored land-crabs, move slowly and deliberately through the dense foliage, picking paths around the hissing, steaming vents. Small, mechanical birds flutter and hop in the vines, their clockwork bodies whirring softly, occasionally emitting sparks from worn electrical connections. Butterflies or moths, almost invisible except for their shadowed wings, flutter and glide around the softly glowing globes, drawn to the vegetable light.

Weirdness permeates every detail, from the seamless, flowing material of the walls to the hauntingly alive quality of the shifting, hissing floor. The place possesses a strange, unaccountable unfinishedness, as if abandoned in mid-sentence, half-artifact and half-creature. The sound here is a constant, deafening roar; the drone of powerful, hidden engines vibrates the floor, punctuated by the hiss of venting pressure and the resonant echo of vast, distant pistons. The gears of some vast, unknowable machinery clank and groan in the background, and thick, wet streams of slurry and lubricant pulse through the bundled, veined pipes, making the air heavy with the scent of damp moss and a faint, metallic tang. Every slow process in this place feels ancient and immense; the runnels wear slowly at the floor, the vines inch up the pillars, the steam condenses and pools and drains on a slow, patient cycle.

This landscape, this manufactured valley, might be only a single, hollowed-out interior, a self-contained room in a larger, industrial complex burrowed into the mountain's heart or spilled down its flanks. Or it might hang suspended, a surreal cathedral of pipes and growth, all sealed in a vast, hollow, bell-like chamber. To enter this place, one might step through a half-hidden, irregularly shaped opening in a rock face or a vast, riveted bulkhead, revealing the sudden and breathtaking scale of the room.
