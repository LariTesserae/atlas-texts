The place reveals itself as a hidden pocket cradled at the summit of a young, sharply featured mountain range — a constructed chamber, its integrity maintained by ancient, riveted plates buckling slightly as the fresh mountain shifts beneath its foundations. The sheer scale of this secret place humbles the mind, yet it remains intimately contained, its boundaries undetectable, as though it has always been there or was always meant to be. Inside, the air churns with activity, echoing the mountain's recent emergence, and is thick with the low thrum of industrial machinery and the pervasive roar of a massive underground waterfall.

A perpetual veil of mist, condensing in the cool atmosphere, hangs in the air. This environment drips from rivulets tracing the sharp, freshly hewn contours of the space, collecting in clear pools and marshy verges at the edges of a central, artificial plain. The engineered ground is fragile, uneven — yet the sparse, struggling pockets of vegetation that cling where the water saturates the thin soil are deliberate, their design intrinsic to whatever purpose this place was made for long ago.

Every stone, every curve of what could have been a natural cave, is touched by the hand of an engineer, an architect; nothing is left to chance, nothing is wild or native. The constructed aspect of the world extends even to the feel of the air — the water near the surface, the coolness, and the constant erosion of the engineered surfaces are all carefully calibrated to support a hidden process long since forgotten. The deafening sound of the environment — the roar of the falls, the groan of massive engines, the ringing of the machinery that works nonstop — is an imposing, enveloping force. It is as though the mountain itself is alive, pulsing with a rhythm whose purpose has been lost, its inner workings audible in the dim, filtered light that seeps in from hidden vents or through translucent plates, casting long, dynamic shadows that hint at the unseen, active cycles within.
