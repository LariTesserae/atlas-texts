The plateau sprawls beneath a sky the color of wet slate. This is the Roof of Rain, a vast upland of dark, waterlogged peat and mudstone. Fractured basalt plates jut through the soft earth like the prows of buried ships, slick with constant moisture. Mist condenses on every surface, gathering in hollows and fissures, weeping down stone faces in thin, continuous sheets. Pools of standing water gather in the low places, reflecting the perpetually overcast heavens.

Yet life thrives here. Tough, fleshy vegetation clothes the plateau, a quilt of deep greens and coppery browns. Succulent shrubs carpet the boggy earth, their thick, rubbery leaves streaked with mineral deposits. Bulbous fungi nestle in sheltered spots, emitting a soft, damp glow that does little to pierce the gloom. Dense copses of tall, salt-tolerant reeds line the marshy depressions, rattling softly with each gust of the chill, wet air.

Strangest of all, the light here pulses with a faint, internal thrum. Everywhere one looks, the landscape is alive with mechanical motion. Armored vines, studded with whirring gears, slowly constrict around stones. Insectile constructs of glinting brass and tarnished silver trundle across the mudflats, harvesting the succulent leaves and reed stalks. These automatons are part of the ecosystem, native to the Roof of Rain. They move in silence, save for the rhythmic click of their gears, the occasional rasp of a joint, the spatter of displaced mud. Their purpose, efficiency, and industry are intrinsic - an instinct, not a program.

The air is cool and thick with moisture, spiced with the tang of metal, decaying vegetation, and an ozone-scent of building storms. Dense banks of fog roll in and thin unpredictably. Sounds are muted, muffled by the wet air and thick vegetation. The rustle of reeds and the occasional, mechanical skittering of the local fauna are the only disturbances of the quiet. Under it all, the soft, constant patter of weeping water is the heartbeat of this world.

This is an ancient place, weathered and settled. The erosion here is creative, carving gullies, pools, and softening the sharp edges of the basalt. The landscape seems to breathe water. There is a deep balance here, a rough equilibrium between the biomechanical fauna and the hardy flora. Despite its harshness and strangeness, the Roof of Rain has an air of permanence and stillness, enduring through the slow, continuous cycles of mist, rain, and seeping water.
