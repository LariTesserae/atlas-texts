The high valley, a place of recent upheaval, carries the scent of displaced earth and damp stone even now. Rain falls intermittently, never heavy but frequent enough to keep the air cool and the rock faces slick with condensation, trickling with veils of water that feed the mosses and ferns clustering in crevices. These pockets of lushness cling to the sharp, uneroded cliffs and crumble into the loosely scattered undergrowth below, where hardy shrubs and saplings take root in the thin, newly settled soil.

Though appearing young geologically, bearing the raw scars of its recent emergence from whatever cataclysm lifted it high, this place is far from empty. A settlement sprawls across a raised plateau within the valley, structured yet ancient-seeming; built from dark stone quarried from the cliffs. Multi-tiered buildings, their features softened by moss and lichen, rise in dense clusters, connected by winding stone paths and stairs carved directly into the bedrock. This is a primitive urbanity, crafted not with grand machinery but with hand tools, an echo of a civilization intimately acquainted with stone and the patient art of building.

Despite the density of the settlement, a sense of space prevails—courtyards, plazas, and gaps between structures give way to sudden glimpses of the open valley beyond, where the terrain is allowed to remain its raw, sparse self. Wildlife abounds here, drawn to the green pockets and the water that gathers in basins and channels throughout. Birds nest on ledges and in the thatched or hide-covered roofs of the buildings; small mammals scurry through the shrubs. Sounds are muted—dripping water, the rustle of wind through sparse foliage, distant bird calls—rarely interrupted by the hushed, deliberate movements of the inhabitants, who seem to communicate without disturbance.

Strangely, the dynamic forces of nature—erosion, decay, overgrowth—seem suspended, as if the valley and its settlement are locked in a permanent state of recently completed formation. New structures are added, but the old do not crumble; the landscape is always damp but never floods, the vegetation thrives but never overwhelms. Footprints linger in soft earth without washing away in the next shower, and tools remain where they are set down, untouched by time's usual wear. Everything exists in a quiet, unchanging equilibrium, an urban outpost in a landscape paused in perpetual, moist afternoon light.
