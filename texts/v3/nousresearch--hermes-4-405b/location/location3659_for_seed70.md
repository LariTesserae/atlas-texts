The landscape emerges from a vast, blue-green paradox. An immense mountain range rears here, freshly risen from the sea floor—a young, sharp-peaked archipelago of volcanic rock and raw basalt, its slopes draped in the thick, kelplike forests that thrive at these depths. Attraction for you, dear reader, must be strong if your eyes catch such a vista. The highest peaks in this drowned cordillera, a region-sized expanse teeming with life, reach nearly to the distant surface; through the undulations of translucent pressure, a soft, water-filtered glow descends, casting slanting rays that dance amid towering amber fronds and emerald vines.

This submerged world is lushly overgrown, yet it remains unpeopled by any residents you might call fauna. Fish and the larger swimmers are rare things in this place—only the trees and lesser creatures live here in numbers. Schools of minuscule fry, glowing softly like stars against the deep, weave pathways through a maze of narrow canyons. These are the channels that time has barely begun to carve into the new-born slopes. Other things besides fish drift here too: vast flocks of translucent moon jellies, their gelatinous bells pulsing with slow intent as they ride the lazy currents.

The almost-liquid atmosphere is warm, dense with brine and the hum of unseen tides. If one listens closely enough, a sound resembling lapping drifts amid the deadening quiet. Perhaps it is an echo of the waves far above, or perhaps the breathing of the forest itself. As for the trees, they grow thick and close, their trunks riddled with holes that house the glowing fry. Roots twist into sinuous braids that anchor each great column to the next, bearded in grey with symbiotic worm-things that curl and release in slow-time pulses to draw nutrients from the water into root and branch.

This place has never known the built environment or anything resembling technology, existing before the advent of tools. Its beauty is a strange and subtle one—wild, alien, vast with the dimensions of the deep. Every level of the landscape reveals new wonders: luminous anemones that wave from every crevice like slow, sentient hands, and glass-crabs that walk among them like amber jewels. These creatures represent the ecosystem's dominant life—small, beautiful, and many. Though change here is slow, its paths are patient. You sense that great ages must pass before erosion and growth will merge and mute these dramatic contours into something more rounded. Until that distant day, the peaks remain sharp and the flora continues its endless, unhurried conquest of darkness.
