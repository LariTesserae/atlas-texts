The place is submerged and vast, a sprawling underwater cityscape of colossal scale. It's a fully constructed environment where every structure, every surface, every intricate detail is meticulously crafted, as if the entire realm had been dreamt into existence and then poured whole from some planet-sized mold.

Though underwater, the light remains steady and even, illuminating immense glass and steel towers that claw their way into the deep blue distance. These impossibly tall structures seem to brush the limits of sight itself, their sharp, fresh edges testament to recent creation rather than long erosion. All around, gargantuan factories—complete with pounding engines and intricate networks of piping—churn with a relentless, aquatic rhythm, processing resources unseen and fueling processes unknown. The constant, low thrum of their activity carries clearly through the chilled waters, punctuated only by the occasional shriek of metal straining under unimaginable pressures.

Despite the sheer size and complexity of this artificial wilderness, individual presences are few and far between. Isolated figures, mechanical or otherwise, drift slowly through the cool, liquid atmosphere, their movements precise and odd amidst the near-void. Sparse, bioluminescent vegetation grows in isolated patches here and there—strange, sparse fronds that reach toward the filtered light from above. But the true life here is in the abundant fauna: shadowy schools of segmented creatures that weave purposeful paths among the behemoth structures, and solitary, gliding leviathans that patrol the wide causeways.

Here, multiple processes unfold on every scale, from the grinding of massive gears to the swift currents sweeping through the manufactured avenues. The whole environment feels alive in a mechanical sense, as if the very water were a medium for its immense, internal clockwork.
