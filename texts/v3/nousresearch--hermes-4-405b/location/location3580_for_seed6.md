Turbid currents bear me through the silted, interior sea — an expanse not of any world's brine, but of sediment carried from some deeply convulsive aquifer into this plateau-ringed caldera. Submerged and vast, the basin brims with cloudy water, its light a diffuse, wavering gold descending from the surface high above.

Through the murk emerge the city's uppermost structures: vast, terraced platforms of poured concrete, sheathed in verdant marine growth. Dense carpets of algae and churning bacterial blooms coat every surface, rendering the original elevations into indistinguishable slopes. Pylons and support columns, obscured by the lush growth, merge into verdant pillars rising from the seafloor. Light filtering down from an unseen sky illuminates the watery canopy, revealing schools of filter-feeding fish and drifting colonies of bioluminescent invertebrates — the only animal life in this sealed, self-sustaining biosphere.

Near the very bottom, the sediment gives way to a cavern system carved by thermal vents, tunnels lined with strange, glassy filaments that pulse with faint, synchronized light. Thermal differentials drive slow currents through the passages, carrying mineral plumes that stain the water in gradients of rust and indigo. The vents exhale warm, effervescent water into the surrounding cold, their heat creating shimmering mirages where the two flows meet. In places, the caverns open into flooded atriums, abandoned halls of an industrial complex half-swallowed by sediment. Rusted machinery looms in the gloom, overgrown with aquatic vegetation that has infiltrated through breaches in the substrate.

This sunken city, once a thriving industrial complex, has been transformed by the slow, patient forces of erosion and submergence. Its structures have become habitats for an ecology of silence and gradual growth, a world where everything is composed and ordered, yet utterly alien. The constant, low-frequency hum of geothermal activity vibrates through the water, a soundscape of pressurized equilibrium. It is a place of waiting, of slow dissolution and renewal, where time itself seems suspended in the green murk.
