The air hangs heavy and still deep within the earth, a subterranean chamber sweltering with heat. Moisture beads and drips from every surface; condensation paints the curving stone ceiling far above. Below the rough, grasping tangle of roots and dangling luminous mosses, the ground is dotted with small, still pools gathered in rock hollows, their mirror surfaces broken only by an occasional ripple as something stirs within.

This place is a natural cavern, weathered over uncounted ages, its dimensions creating a kind of natural cathedral. Rock walls arch and bend, their shapes sculpted by slow water and the persistent push of unseen surfaces. Stalactites like stone teeth descend from the ceiling; some meet their matching pillars rising from the cavern floor. Faint mineral veins thread through the rock, catching what little light exists, and across these walls a few scattered, almost faded handprints can be glimpsed — some in ochre, some in ash, as if left by ghosts who once walked here.

But the sense of human touch is false, as this is a place without tools, without any mark of the made. There is only life, and the stone. Life is abundant here, thick as the humid air. The cavern floor is overgrown with strange ferns and broad, veined leaves, and the air thrums with the unplaceable, low drone of unseen creatures — a sound so deep in the ears it feels like the cave's own slow breath. By the dim glow of the bioluminescent mosses scattered here and there like fallen stars, movement is frequent; insects crawl across the damp stone while small animals dart between the shadows and roots.

The atmosphere is dim, the silence broken only by the soft plink of droplets into pools, the whisper of something shifting in hidden places. It is the quiet of condensation falling, of plants growing, of roots creeping through stone, even as the cave's ecosystem appears in constant, unseen motion — a place of slow growth and sudden hunger, of quiet living becoming.
