The air hums here, thick with evaporating rain and the green exhalations of a million billion living things. This place is a sunken hollow no larger than a cathedral's ambit, a bowl-shaped depression with edges crumbling into soft mud and fern. It is perpetually wet — a garden after downpours — where water slicks every surface and drips from arching fronds, bleeding from fissures in the encircling earth. Mist rises at dawn, slow and pearlescent.

Within this small low place, a primordial stillness has settled deep into the rock and mud, leaching the volcanic fury from ancient stone — the erosion here is utter, flattening all to humic substrate. But in this exhaustion of the mineral world, life has risen. The entire hollow is choked, utterly dominated by a single kind of vegetation: colossal fungal structures, their rubbery stalks thick as tree trunks but far taller, rising in a loose scatter like a sparse stand of redwoods. Between them, however, flourishes a lower story of seething, restless forms: bulbous shelves of green flesh, pulsing fans of some leathery growth, translucent veils draping between stalks. It's a dense tapestry woven from countless interlocking lifeforms, each plant swelling against its neighbor so tightly that no soil shows between — a pure plant ecosystem, an architecture of vegetation. The forms suggest ancient, deep growth, but also a restless churning.

It is warm here, the air thrumming with the respiration of this whole living mass and thick with the croaking, chittering, buzzing of its inhabitants — a rainforest contained, a singular biome. Strange, jewel-bright creatures flit and crawl; iridescent insects as large as fists hum past great staring lizards clinging to fungal bark; many-legged things skitter under the pulsing surface of the ground cover itself, which seems to ripple and breathe asleep. Abundant and strange, a jewel box of alien forms.

Scattered amongst this teeming life are structures, nestled at the base of huge stalks or clinging to their middles — dwellings, ramparts, towers. They are small in footprint but densely built up, stacked vertically into as many as four or five distinct levels, each level a dense cluster of interlocking rooms and passages. They have a rough-hewn, ramshackle look, yet also an undeniable sophistication: arches curve with impossible slender grace, tapered buttresses spiral up load-bearing stalks. The material is the same plant flesh as the ground and the great towers — clearly shaped and grown, not quarried or cut. This is a living architecture, a settlement of fungal stone.

Within these wart-like cities movement never ceases. The hollow teems with activity: small dark shapes bustle around the settlements and across the open spaces, clambering over the bulging green substrate, climbing the living stalks, darting like insects across precarious sky-bridges of sinewy rope that stretch between the highest spires. The air vibrates with their constant industry, a sound like wind in reeds or the low thrum of engines idling, a low roar of gathered voices and clattering labor. It's a bazaar, an overstimulated hive, yet the ambient light is ordinary — it simply reveals this scene, without drama. Just daylight.

The rules here are not the rules of wind and sun and erosion. The technology that built these fungal cities is beyond modern understanding — the dwellings seem to grow and heal and change shape, guided by the will or need of their inhabitants, who move through their living walls like phantoms, unimpeded. Physics here must be malleable, or the city-builders have access to engineering that manipulates forces not yet named. The city is built from living things, shaped as though clay, into a dense warren of interlocking dwellings within a larger sparse framework of titanic growths — and it's all made of flesh, not stone.

In the mud at the base of the great stalks, in the shadowed spaces between the smaller growths, the silence pools like water. These dim alcoves are thick with hanging vegetation, dripping water, and the skittering of small creatures. From these shadows the unsettling sense of *weirdness* radiates — some faint luminescence to the mud, a heat to the air that thrums against the skin. The edges of vision twist here; shapes flow and reform as you look away. The place breathes. The living walls pulse. There is a sense of immense energy constrained, the constant low sound of the place a veil over something vast and active just out of sight. It is turbulent, unstable, ever-changing. In this small sunken place, ancient stone has surrendered to the endless ambition of life.
