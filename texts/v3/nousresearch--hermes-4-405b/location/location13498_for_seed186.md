The place is a vast verticality, an immense cliff face towering into near-total darkness. Here, nestled against impossible heights, an ecosystem thrives in cold, damp secrecy. Vines — thick, ancient, and gnarled — cascade down the stone in a lush green curtain, fingers brushing the void below. Water weeps from the rock, a thousand tiny rivulets that gleam in the scarce light like veins of liquid silver, their drips and trickles the only soft punctuation in a silence as heavy as the stone itself.

Moss, thick and spongy, carpets every ledge and crevice, iridescent patterns swirling in its surfaces like a dream of colors too alien for any sunlit world. Air moves in slow, chilling currents, carrying scents of wet earth and something metallic, something sharp, a taint from further up.

This cliff is alive. Nocturnal creatures stir in the leafy shadows — faint skitterings, the beat of leathery wings, eyes like cold jewels glinting in the dark as they search for unseen prey. They are many, pushing the cold silence with their quiet urgency, a predator-prey dance played out on this sheer stage. The stones themselves seem to exude cold; they are dark, damp, time-smoothed, their edges rounded by millennia of erosion, but also etched with strange, almost crystalline structures, obsidian veins that run through the rock like frozen lightning.

The scale of this place is immense, a wall of rock and life stretching upwards into a black sky where no stars show. It feels open, yet untouchably remote from any world below. It exists in near-isolation, empty except for the life clinging to its face. No hand of civilization has ever touched it; no paths, no buildings. It is pristine in its extreme wildness.

Yet the weirdness deepens. In the far, vaulted distance above, where the cliff vanishes into pure dark, a strange, faint pulse of light throbs — cold, blue, rhythmic. It illuminates vast shapes moving slowly beyond the haze, shapes of riveted steel and glass tubes glowing from within, creaking tangles of pipes that snake in and out of the solid rock as if the mountain itself were part of some colossal, dormant engine. The air carries low vibrations, the deep, resonant hum of massive machinery idling in the black, echoes of a vast, subterranean industry hidden within the peak. It is this slow-beating heart that feeds the strangeness below, the source of the water, the cold, and the subtle metallic tang in the air. A world in itself, active and alien, balanced on the edge of wilderness and some unknowable design.
