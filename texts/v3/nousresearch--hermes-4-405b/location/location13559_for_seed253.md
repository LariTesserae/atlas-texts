The darkness is near absolute here, at the scale's upper bound — an eternal water column suspended at inconceivable elevations, isolated not merely by height but by its sheer impossibility. There are no currents, no tides — the water hangs in the void, a colossal suspended sphere maintained by forces beyond reckoning. It is cool, not freezing, a liquid that might have been pure once but now carries faint salts and minerals left as its only impurities.

This place was built — every molecule is placed, held, by design. It is a constructed sphere of infinite water, a featureless aquatic prison, utterly devoid of the usual currents and flows. No natural land exists, no seabed to anchor anything. Yet life persists, adapted and abundant, filling the emptiness with vibrant presence. The vegetation is the foundation of everything — thick, rootless forests of fleshy plants that spiral endlessly through the darkness. They grow in tight fractal spirals, looping through the water in infinite, gravity-defying paths. These vast plant-structures twist through the dark, weaving through each other in a dance that never repeats yet never collides. They are solid enough to support entire ecosystems but porous enough to let the cold water pass through, tangle, and gather.

And the fauna — endless schools of blind, luminous fish drift through the plant lattices, existing in sprawling communities. Their soft movements are the only sound, a constant, low-frequency chorus that echoes through the water. Occasionally, larger predators glide silently between the spirals, creatures whose true forms are impossible to discern in the endless gloom; they are shadows that flow around the light, unseen hunters existing just beyond the edge of perception. All life here is built for darkness: the fish have never known true light, their forms adapted to an existence of endless shadow, guided only by the faint pulses of their own bodies and the subtle currents generated by the drifting plants.

The only consistent illumination comes from within — the fish generate their own dim, ethereal glows, their bodies casting faint, shifting patterns that dance across the plants and each other. This self-made light is weak but constant, transforming the darkness into something richer, deeper — a vast, shadowed space punctuated by living stars. The scale is immense, the plant structures extending past any immediate perception, continuing into unseen distances as though they might fill the entire sphere. Despite their density, the plant formations maintain wide spaces between presences, leaving vast, lightless regions of empty water. Only the occasional cluster of fish or slow, drifting predator breaks the silence, filling it instead with the quiet song of their passage.

This world is timeless. Volcanoes do not erupt. Earthquakes are impossible — there is no land to shake. The sphere is changeless, existing in a state of perfect equilibrium, frozen in a moment that never began and will never end. It is unusual, almost impossibly artificial yet utterly natural in the lives it supports — an engineered impossibility that became a home.
