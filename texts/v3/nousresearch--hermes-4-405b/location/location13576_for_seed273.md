The city of Hraza-Lor hangs like a wasp's nest from the sheer face of the Skyfang Peaks, a vertical metropolis clinging ten thousand feet above the clouded lowlands. From the depths of the valley below, it appears as a dark, intricate scar against the mountain's pale stone. Up close, it is all industry and ingenuity, an unyielding testament to the will of those who call such a place home.

Water is everpresent but never abundant. Condensation beads on every cold metal surface, drips from jutting pipes, trickles in rivulets down the city's external skin. The deep thrum of the aqueduct systems is the true heartbeat of Hraza-Lor, pulling water from glacial melt and subterranean springs miles distant. Pipes vein the city, feeding into countless cisterns and smaller conduits. Rain, when it comes, is torrential and dangerous; it streams down the mountain in sudden cataracts, threatening to tear loose anything not anchored into the bedrock. It’s not unusual to see workers on suspended platforms, using massive compressed-air hoses to blast away ice from crucial structures or clear debris from water intakes.

Within this crafted world, vegetation flourishes in manicured brilliance. Every inch of viable surface not dedicated to machinery is given over to hydroponic gardens and hanging trellises. Entire levels are dedicated agri-domes, their thick, reinforced glass panes already running with condensation in the early morning. The air is thick with the scent of chlorophyll and damp earth, a startling contrast to the everpresent tang of ozone and hot metal. Huge, genetically-modified ferns unfurl in shaded terraces, their fronds heavy with moisture. Orchids in startling neon hues dangle from the undersides of support girders. The higher altitudes and intense artificial sunlight of the upper levels favor hardy alpine grasses and mosses, carefully cultivated in vast, shallow planters that buffer the industrial zones from direct wind. Vines spill from every planter, crawling over railings and up support beams, tethering themselves with thick, rope-like roots.

Warmth radiates from the city's deep veins. Geothermal taps drilled miles into the mountain's heart provide a constant, reliable heat source, fed into the city’s under-levels. Coupled with the greenhouse effect of the agri-domes and the sheer thermal mass of the mountain itself, Hraza-Lor maintains a surprisingly stable warmth. Yet step onto an outer gantry, away from the heat exchangers, and the air bites with the chill of high altitude, thin enough that bright sun doesn’t always translate to comfort. The city is a patchwork of microclimates – stiflingly humid in the hydroponic domes, dry and warm near the geothermal vents, cool and damp near the external water collectors.

Hraza-Lor is a city of layered sound. The constant roar of ventilation systems is underpinned by the groan of engines, the shriek of steam, the incessant clang of metal on metal as structures shift and settle. Workers shout over the din, their voices amplified and distorted by crude sonic-boosters. The city is never quiet – even in the dead hours, the thrumming continues, a deep, resonant pulse like the breath of some colossal, sleeping beast. Rain transforms the noise into a chaotic symphony: a thunderous drumming on the roofs and walkways, the shriek of wind around exposed corners, the gurgle and crash of overflowing pipes.

Fauna thrives here, adapted or introduced. Insects buzz in dense clouds near the gardens, some bred for pollination, others tolerated pests. Rust-colored lichen creeps across the oldest structures, piggybacking on condensation. The city is famed for its Skyfinches, small, iridescent birds that build their nests in the high crevices and feed on the ubiquitous condensation-midges. Below, in the dimly lit utility levels, one might spot the occasional scurry of six-legged, segmented creatures – ‘pipe-crawlers’ – that feed on the algae and fungi growing in the damp, warm darkness. These creatures are mostly benign, if unsettling.

The entire city is a constructed marvel. Every slab of plasteel, every rivet and conduit, every carefully-tended garden, every echoing chamber was brought here or made here. There is no raw stone exposed; the mountainside is completely encased in generation after generation of construction, additions built upon repairs built upon the original desperate scaffolding. The sheer scale of it all is overwhelming – to stand on one of the outer gangways and look up or down along the city's flank is to see industry and habitation extending for miles, a vertical tapestry of human endeavor.

Technology here is industrial, robust, irreplaceable. Belching smokestacks rise like blackened teeth from the refinery sectors; gargantuan pistons drive the foundries where new parts are forged; massive conveyor belts haul raw materials from the mines delved deep into the mountain's bones. Electricity is generated by harnessed steam and geothermal turbines, fed into a web of cables that spider across every surface. There’s a certain brutalist elegance to the machinery – nothing is hidden, nothing is wasted.

Brightness is a constant companion, necessary and artificial. The city is studded with powerful arc-lamps that cut through the perpetual gloom cast by the mountain's bulk, their light reflecting sharply off wet metal and thick glass. Sunlight, fierce at these altitudes, is often filtered through polarized panels, creating vast pools of shadow and glare. The agri-domes blaze with artificial sunlight, visible from afar as bright green jewels set against the city's predominantly grey facades.

Change here is slow and ponderous, a function of the immense scale and the city's precarious perch. Replacing a major conduit or expanding a sector is a generational undertaking. Yet the city does evolve – new levels, meticulously planned and reinforced, are gradually cantilevered out from the central mass, while older sections are sometimes decommissioned and dismantled with great care. It is a process of accretion and refinement, a slow metamorphosis played out over decades. The fauna adapts in turn, generations of creatures shifting their habits in lockstep with the city's creeping transformations.
