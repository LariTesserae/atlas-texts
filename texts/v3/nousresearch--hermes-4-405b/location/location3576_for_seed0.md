The air itself is breathing. It's a warm, damp exhalation that beads on every surface — thick condensation running down fronds, spongy moss, and the sharp black rock that juts through the layers of growth. This is a recent place, carved by something immense and sharp just yesterday or a thousand years ago — time blurs in the constant transformation. Jagged volcanic ridges, still glassy in places where the rock cooled instantly against the air, form the skeleton of this low basin. But the basin is vast, fading into darkness in every direction, its edges undefined.

At the basin's center lies a shattered caldera or perhaps a newborn lake bed, a depression where steam perpetually rises. It's not a single large body of water, but a thousand pockets — rainwater caught in obsidian cups formed by bubbles in the cooling lava, rivulets winding between boulders still warm to the touch, pools in the hollows of giant ferns. The sound is a roar, a multitude of sounds layered until individual sources drown: the hiss of steam, the drip and trickle of water, the groan of new rock settling, the crack of stones fracturing in the residual heat.

And life — life explodes here. Not just the dense riot of vegetation that climbs and carpets every surface, huge ferns unfurling fronds as tall as a person, strange succulents clinging to sheer faces, vines knitting the cracks into emerald veils. But fauna, overwhelming in its density and its noise. Think of lizards with obsidian-scaled hides, six-legged insects chittering in clouds so thick they cast their own shadows, bird-things with iridescent plumage shrieking from the canopy. And larger shapes move in the dimness, their forms suggested more by the rustle and snap of vegetation than by clear sight. The whole basin seethes with it, a churning, hyperactive, almost frantic ecosystem. It's the sound of a world being born, violently alive and shouting its existence into the heavy, warm, dark air. Primitive tools would be swallowed by this place, simple hand axes lost in the undergrowth, fire drowned by the humidity. This is a landscape in the raw, savage act of becoming.
