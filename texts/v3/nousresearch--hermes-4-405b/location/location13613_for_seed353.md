The air here burns, thick and desiccated, shimmering faintly even in the near-absolute darkness; any liquid water vanished epochs ago. What remains is a high plateau — the wind-scoured crown of some unimaginably ancient mountain eroded down to its hard, hot bones over geological ages, now settled into its final, near-flattened form. Scale is open, vast: this place extends for kilometers, a miniature world under a starless, lightless sky. No animals stir. Instead, the world-surface is packed utterly dense with a singular organism — a colossal, hot, slow-breathing vegetable life that has overrun and transmogrified everything.

Vegetation is total here: the substrate of the world, its architecture and air. The plateau is covered — more accurately, *composed* of — an unbroken, rolling, peaking, plunging blanket of what must be plant life, though it is alien, synthesizing matter from the hot dark and presumably whatever trace gases remain in the atmosphere. It is a single titanic growth, fibrous yet hard as ironwood, its grotesque forms looking like some cathedral of vegetable life, interlaced stems and roots and trunks grown into battlements, vaulted roofs, corridors, all merged into itself, creating immense vaulted halls and dizzying drops below, paths winding above pits that drop away into blackness. Everything is plant-as-architecture: the substance fills the space completely. It is huge beyond comprehension, a primordial hyper-forest, living, slow-pulsing, immense roots gripping deep, but all formed, annealed, and fused. The air vibrates with the deep, subsonic thrum of its massive biology.

And it is *changing*: with no animals, the strange, industrial-scale fabricated systems are continuously, turbulently remodeling this plant-flesh reality. autonomous machines, scaled for production are embedded within, constantly at work: engines whine as grinders bite into the hot planks and beams of living wood, conveyor belts shift chunks of freshly harvested timber toward processing zones where the air shudders and glows with frantic, unseen industry. Sparks fountaining in the gloom. It is a turbines-and-engines landscape, silicon and steel interfaced directly with cellulose and sap, everything fully constructed, a made-environment. The light is almost nil — just dim emergency strobes flickering on machines, the hot red glow of some distant furnace, the occasional arc flash of electrified processes. Sounds are sparse but deep: he occasional groan as a circular saw bites, the hiss of steam pipes, the crash of a felled section of the growth, the constant thrum of power systems. The living plant writhes slowly with this violation, sap boiling away, new growth surging back where the machines have finished their work — an endless, unstable, storm-state cycle of consumption, regeneration, deconstruction, creation. It is deeply aged, yet everything is dynamic, chaotic change, instability given form. An industrial hellscape wrought entirely from hyperactive, living wood.

Nothing here is stable. The machines relentlessly devour and rebuild: a great foundry melts down plant-flesh, casts it into new shapes. vast belts haul limbs and planks toward chippers and grinders that roar and spark in the hot darkness; the air shudders with vibrations. Sparks fountaining in the gloom. The air shudders with the deep, subsonic thrum of its massive biology. Yet this world is also quiet: the sounds of industry echo, but the vast scale swallows them into a vast, brooding quiet. Wind sometimes whispers through roots and fronds. It is a silent bazaar, an anthill-energy world made of total plant-architecture under a dark, hot sky.
