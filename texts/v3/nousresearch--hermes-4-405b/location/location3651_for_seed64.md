The place is a high-altitude garden, hidden from any open sky and folded into the inner spaces of some colossal machinery that forms the very substance of its mountain. Cool air, stale with the scent of oil and electricity, fills this pocket world, a space barely large enough to hold its creation. Though incredibly lush in its strange way, the flush of growth here is a dry, mineral-rich affair—thick, rubbery mosses cling to every surface, their deep greens turned dusky by the faint, hazed light filtering down from ancient electric fixtures above, while knotted, woody shrubs twist up from crevices, their unseen roots drinking from something other than water. No true animals live here among these unchanging forms, yet sound saturates the air: a loud industrial hum that has been the world's only song since time immemorial, steady and ever-ongoing. The ground is not earth but a rippling, spontaneous mosaic of fungus shelves and iron-hard lichen, all packed tight together with no space between, not one speck of bare stone visible beneath. Everywhere there are faint marks of industry—rusted plates, ceramic insulators peering out from beneath thick growth, occasionally a flashing diode still living on some deep circuit. It's a world in a state of perfect, frozen equilibrium, its every strange surface weathered and settled into forms that have always been, a hidden chamber breathing with the mechanical pulse of a long-forgotten purpose.
