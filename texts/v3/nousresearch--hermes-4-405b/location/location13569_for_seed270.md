The air rings with electric whines, steam hisses, and clattering reverberations. You stand within a cavity whose walls have a sharp, crystalline geometry; they lean inward, meeting in a vaulted ceiling. The mineral surfaces are gray, finely-grained, and unexpectedly warm beneath your hand—heat radiates outward, while the chamber's atmosphere hangs at a perpetual coolness. No plantlife softens this space, not even lichen. This pocket exists sealed away from any water cycle; dust is mineral only, never damp.

It's a single room, hardly larger than a workshop, yet space feels sparse—a room defined by its contents. Devices and machinery crowd every angle, sculpted at strange diagonals, stretching from floor to ceiling in Escherian layers. Each mechanism is a dense construction of stacked bulges and angular projections; you might reach out to touch one, only to find another piece of equipment occupying the space you thought was empty—a cascade of manufactured forms rendered in dark, non-reflective materials that drink the chamber's utilitarian light.

Further machinery is visible through an open archway: the floor of the adjoining space is higher, forming a natural dais, and its surfaces are no less crowded—components nestle under, between, above one another, forming impromptu shelves and tunnels of technology. Here industriousness has mineralized into something permanent, slumbering, and omnipresent.

Although no human presence is evident, life thrives here in abundance—and it is fast, insistent, diverse.

Vast insectile colonies—carapaces iridescent, but mottled with neutral tones to match these unpainted walls—cluster across machine casings, surging fanlik.e over control panels, antennae flicking in ceaseless murmur. They pour in rippling streams from a fracture in the floor where a maintenance hatch hangs open—march in legions across coiled and sleeping cabling—then vanish into an access duct that leads into the ceiling's shadow. Their collective motion creates deep rustling undertones—a wet rasping, like many chitinous limbs drawn perpetually across stone. Atop a logic chassis, a larger specimen raises itself, the jointed architecture of its body borrowing symmetry from the metal frame it stands on.

A different sound weaves between the clatter of long-disused moving parts: an electric cracking that builds to a bzzt—as though something should spark, but cannot here—a sharp repellent noise that echoes from one corner. You follow the sound to a shelf jutting from the wall, where a line of transparent jars stand in military order—a research project abandoned mid-catalog. Each jar is filled to bursting with small, skittering life that seems to share some common biology: a pulsing carapace, a rattle like bone on bone. Their legs or chelicerae drum against glass walls—each tiny specimen is framed by isolated crumbles of substrate, by deserted glittering stones. Dozens of arachnoid bodies cluster in each sealed container—yet between each jar lie several inches of undisturbed emptiness, gaps that feel planned, deliberate—a sorting or categorization in progress.

Industry defines the air: dry metal—a hint of machine oil that feels ancient—a faint and almost sweet trace of decay. The very geometry of the room feels ancient beyond comprehension. You run a hand along a machine housing—where the metal curves, it has worn away over epochs into smooth cusps and valleys; grates whose honeycombs were once sharply octagonal show softening edges. Surfaces do not lie flush; metal components have settled into new relationships—warped, contracted, or bowed very slightly over unfathomable durations.

Time itself has bowed to these presences. Yet each living thing seems to flit through this landscape—a blur of ceaseless industry. The whole room pulses—a whirlpool of perpetual biological and mechanical motion framed within these oldest stones.
