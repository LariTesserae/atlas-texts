The City of Frozen Moss:

From the outside, the place is a low, eroded mound in a cold, dry landscape. Only a single, narrow fissure at its base hints at anything within. Passing through this crack requires crawling over a floor of dark, wet stone, through the only passage that remains unfilled by the growth. The entrance is perpetually damp, slick with condensation that drips from moss-thickened stone overhead.

Inside, you find yourself in a small, sunken city—a subterranean pocket no more than a hundred yards across, its limits defined by steep, impassable walls of crumbling, moss-blanketed rock. High above, hidden from any outside view, a natural ceiling of crystal admits daylight that floods the space with a startling, diamond-bright glare. The light refracts through prisms of ice that cling to the cavern walls even in what passes for summer here, casting fractured rainbows across the city below.

A cold climate persists inside this cavern. Temperatures hover just above freezing—an over-chilled dampness that encourages condensation to bead on the surface of the stone, the ever-present mosses, and the outsides of dwellings. Steam rises faintly from the warmer ground of the cavern floor, mingling with the omnipresent mist.

A dense and lavish carpet of moss in countless shades of green—emerald to near-black—clothes every surface, obliterating the underlying stone. Lush ferns sprout from the moist soil between structures. In the few remaining open spaces, a few low, hardy shrubs take root. Thick vines cling to walls or hang in ropy masses. The scents of wet stone and vegetation mingle in the air. And yet, for all this life, silence reigns—a hush broken only by the slow drip of water from the cavern ceiling, echoing slightly in the still, cold air, or the faint patter of falling moisture.

Between and upon these mounds of moss, buildings cluster—low dwellings of crudely worked stone or dark, heavy timbers. They are set close together, their outer walls softened by thick growth. The city feels overgrown, its spaces saturated; walkways are narrow, barely an arm's width in places, forcing movement to be slow, careful, single file.

Though the buildings are simple, their construction speaks of long settlement; this is a place whose patterns were set lifetimes ago, and now persists in a settled, unchanging equilibrium. There are no open spaces, no sudden ruptures or new growths. The whole pocket has the feel of something long weathered, long inhabited, long lived-in—a place whose form is established, and in balance. It might have been there for centuries, sunk within itself and its damp, luminous world.

The people here are small and quiet, clothed in hide and fur that mingles with the moss and stone. Bearded faces, pale as the crystal light, peer from low doorways. They go about their lives with minimal speech, their movements economical, as if the silence of the city has seeped into their bones. When sound does occur, it is quiet—a soft footstep, the gentle thud of a wooden bowl set down. Their technology is simple, hand tools of bone and polished stone visible in their work. They climb ladders of rough-hewn timber that lead to higher dwellings, or lower to chambers that are little more than caves in the stone beneath the city. There is no sense of rush or progress here; life follows rhythms as slow and steady as the drip of water on mossy stone.

The fauna, too, is abundant in its own quiet way—dark, moss-backed beetles scurrying soundlessly along damp walls; pale, near-translucent spiders spinning thick webs in the corners of dwellings; blind, pale earthworms enriching the dark soil beneath the moss. A few small birds with muted plumage nest in the hanging vines, flitting silently from perch to perch. Their calls, when they come, are high, thin, and brief.

It is a strange place, an anomaly in the wider dry world—a pocket of overgrown, saturated life buried within stone, hidden away, illuminated by crystal and ruled by cold and moist silence; an ancient, unchanging urban space, tightly packed and secret, a city of moss flourishing in a cold, bright, hidden chamber.
