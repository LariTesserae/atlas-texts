The air here is thin and perpetually cold, whistling through the vast canyons formed by colossal, living structures. These are not rocks, not earth, but immense, slow-breathing organisms that have grown mountains of themselves, their surfaces encrusted with layers of lesser plants that crawl and bloom in sprawling tapestries of muted colors. The light is faint, a perpetual twilight that deepens in the narrow passages between the colossal growths, where dampness hangs heavy in the air and condenses on every surface. Occasionally, water drips from the high niches where ice has formed.

In the immense spaces between these living mountains, the ground is almost bare, a crumbling substrate of decaying organic matter from which fragile, rootlike structures erupt. Here and there, small, crude shelters have been erected from scavenged plant matter, simple lean-tos and windbreaks that suggest the presence of tool-using fauna. They seem temporary, inadequate against the raw elements, yet a testament to survival in this place of scale and sparseness.

Fauna is abundant, though often small and adapted to clambering over the vast, uneven surfaces. Their calls echo loudly in the cold air, a constant chorus under the moaning of the wind through the high, plant-choked canyons. Predator and prey move through the dim light, their interactions part of the ever-shifting ecology here. The entire landscape seems to breathe, to grow and decay at an almost visible pace.

The rules of physics feel stretched; gravity is strong, the air thin, and the horizon distant. The scale is such that the weather moves in visible, intricate patterns, clouds snagging on the peaks of plant-mountains and rain falling in localized, icy bursts. Yet despite the abundance of life, there is a profound emptiness, a sense of vastness that dwarfs even the immense living structures.
