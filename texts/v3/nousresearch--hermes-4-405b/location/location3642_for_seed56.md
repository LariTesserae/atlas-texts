The space is a low cavern, its folds and pockets intergrown with a dense symbiotic organism—a plant, yes, but one grown to architectural scale. Light comes from the slow weeping walls: a post-mineral substance has replaced the rock, soft under the touch, exuding cold radiance and bitterly cold water. This seepage has allowed life to take total hold, the aboveground plants and sessile cave fauna working together to shape their environment. The cavern’s small scale—little larger than a large room—amplifies the abundant sounds of a population adjusted to the frigidity: high chatters and low hums of life, the intricate clicks of mutualist arthropods roaming the plant-architecture.

This place is a nursery, seeded by some diffuse intelligence weaving advanced biotech into cave-ecosystem processes. Light is dim enough to allow perception of the slow actions within the growth medium, which support plant and animal. Structures self-repair with proteins extruded by resident fungi; moisture hovers in the cool air like a breath, condensing where the plant-mass grows most thick. The entire system is dynamic yet slow—a century between great pulses or shifts in temperature—and the deep roots of strangeness are just emerging as the cavern’s final form settles. No artificial structures exist here, only the untouched material of post-geology and the living calibrated to exist there. This is the cold womb where something new slowly spins itself from the fabric of an older world.
