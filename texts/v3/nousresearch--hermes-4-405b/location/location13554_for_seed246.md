The place is high, impossibly high—a cathedral-scale scoop out of the sheer face of a mountain that itself seems part of the sky's foundation. Here, altitude bleeds into air so thin it hums, a constant, lowoiced thrumming that vibrates through the bones of the world. It is an ancient hollow, worn smooth over epochs into a near-flattened crater. The ground is a single continuous surface, polished to an eerily unblemished sheen, wet with a perpetual dampness. This dampness gathers in shallow, mirror-like pools that never seem to overflow.

There is no loose stone, no gravel—only this singular, sealed expanse. It is empty, hollowed by millennia, a vast and unsettling vacancy. Yet into this void, there grows a lush profusion of mosses and lichens: they are the only life here, spreading in thick, velvet carpets over every inch of the polished ground and up the sheer walls of rock. They are not just green but deep emerald, rich crimson, and startling cobalt, their colors almost violent in the otherwise stark emptiness. Their textures are thick, fleshy, and damp—constantly weeping tiny droplets of moisture that bead and cling to their surfaces before falling to join the pools below.

The air is cold—bitterly cold—and utterly still. It hangs heavy and immutable in the hollow, a trapped pocket of frigid sky. The light that falls from above is clear and sharp, clean and precise. It illuminates the damp sheen of the polished stone and the vibrant hues of the mosses with an almost clinical precision, etching each detail in vivid relief. There are no shadows; the light is everywhere, uniform, and yet in its very perfection it deepens the unsettling quality of the place.

The only sounds are the deep, resonant hum that permeates the air and the slow, rhythmic drip of moisture from the weeping mosses. These drips fall with a quiet, almost imperceptible plink into the shallow pools, a constant, steady percussion beneath the deeper hum. There are no other signs of life—no birds, no insects, nothing that breathes or moves or breaks the humming quiet. It is a living tableau frozen in time, a damp and moss-choked emptiness that is both ancient and perpetually, eerily alive.
