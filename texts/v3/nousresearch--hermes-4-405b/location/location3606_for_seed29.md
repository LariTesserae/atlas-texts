The first thing is the heat. It's a dense, baking stillness that feels like someone left a kiln door open. The air itself seems thick with radiant energy, every surface warm enough to raise blisters. This place is hidden – a pocket world nestled at the peak of some immense mountain range, or maybe embedded deep within a geothermal labyrinth. You can't see the sky. The ceiling is oppressive and close, an uneven cradle of dark rock still cooling into its final shape. Freshly-baked stone feels porous, like pumice but still glowing faintly from the fires that made it. It's too new for erosion; geological processes are just beginning here.

What little light exists comes from the rocks themselves – a deep, sullen red radiance seeping through cracks in the low ceiling. Hot embers glow like watchful eyes in the walls, the only illumination beyond the industrial processes churning below. Deep within this pocket, sunk into the very stone, throbs machinery. They're not factories for building things, but raw crystalline engines – geometries of pulsating light and metal sunk into the living rock, crusted with minerals as if half-formed or half-melted. It's industrial but feels oddly elemental, like the world's own pulsing heart given metallic form. Thick, hot wind gusts from grated vents where these subterranean engines breathe – a dry, oven-blast that carries the sharp smell of ozone and fused earth.

This small cavern is packed. Not with artifacts or human-made structures, but with life. Every surface crawls with fauna adapted to this primal forge. Things like armored insects – iridescent beetles whose carapaces shimmer in the ember-light, TICK-TICK-TICKING as they skitter across hot stone. There are centipedes here too: fist-sized, with obsidian-black segments that gleam wetly, coiling around fissures and vents. The sound is immense for such a contained space – the shrill SCRAPE-SCRITCH of chitinous bodies, the RUSTLE-RATTLE of legions in motion, the deep SUBSONIC PULSE of buried engines. It's loud, yet static – a continuous, roaring backdrop accompanying slow, inevitable processes. Geology doesn't rage here; it seeps, shifts, transforms with vast patience, cradling its thrumming machines and the creatures that swarm across it.
