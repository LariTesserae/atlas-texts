The Hole is a sunken arena, a circular depression roughly a hundred meters across, surrounded by crumbling tenements of packed, damp stone under a perpetually twilit, ash-hazed sky. Steam perpetually billows from cracks in the ground where hot mineral springs simmer close to the surface, and condensation weeps down the walls of drab, soot-encrusted apartment buildings huddled around the Hole’s inner slopes. Buildings tower like slumped giants, some leaning precariously over cobbled streets that wind around the Hole’s bowl-like rim and down into the middle. Moss and hardy, dark-leafed vines cling to stonework, feeding on the humid air and moisture left by the steam. Rusted iron pipes snake alongside streets and jut from upper floors, spewing more vapor or dripping warm liquid, adding to the pervasive wetness. Inside and on rooftops of these sparse, hulking structures, human figures in stained coveralls labor at huge, grimy machines: throbbing pumps, thundering pistons, and grinding gear-trains, some large enough to fill entire rooms. These engines run through complex networks of pipes and belts strung between buildings, shuddering as they work, attended by quiet, focused workers who communicate in curt, functional shouts. Though no animals inhabit this self-contained district, the Hole pulses with relentless mechanical activity: hissing steam, rumbling conveyors, banging pipes, and deep infrastructure tremors form a continuous, overwhelming din. During shifts, the loud clamor inside factories adds another layer: clanging metal, rhythmic grinding, and witnessed stood, holding valves of the perpetually functioning systems that circulate steam, drain fluid, or process unknown materials in this forgotten, toiling settlement.
