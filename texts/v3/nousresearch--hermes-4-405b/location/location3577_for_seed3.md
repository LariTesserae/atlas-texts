I glide through the dim air of this world, and it welcomes me. A cool mist brushes my skin, and strange sounds echo from distant peaks. The atmosphere is thick with moisture — a pervasive, buoyant dampness that beads on every surface and fills the deepest canyons with soft fog. The air tastes like rain on the edge of becoming, and beyond the haze, impossibly tall mountains pierce the sky. They are jagged and raw, as if freshly torn from the planet’s bones — sheer cliffs and unstable slopes bleeding mineral-rich rivers. These immense formations rise up and up, vanishing into the low clouds, and in the vast open spaces between them, something new and improbable is taking root.

Humanity has found a foothold here. Settlements cling like lichen to the slopes of these infant mountains — dense clusters of buildings that spill across ridges and valleys. There’s an urban energy to them: streets and bridges layered over themselves, sheets of glass and steel holding back the wilderness. Furnaces burn in the mist, casting long shadows — factories and foundries that breathe steam into the cool air. I hear the rhythmic thrum of engines below, the clang of metal on metal echoing between the peaks, the low roar of machinery doing patient, relentless work.

There are few trees — just scrappy, wind-bent bushes and contorted pines — but the land is overflowing with lush, improbable life. Moss drips from every crevice, thick and silver-green, and the rocks are furred with dark lichen that thrives in the damp. Vines as thick as your arm snake up cliffs and around lampposts, leaves drooping under their own weight. This is a place where nature and industry intertwine — where ferns erupt from broken asphalt, and water-slicked pipes become trellises for creeping, emerald growth. Fauna is everywhere — thick, glossy beetles with iridescent shells scuttle between cobbles, schools of silver fish dart through sunken alleys flooded by glacial melt, and more. I glimpse taller shapes in the mist: angular creatures stalking the high ridges, their forms rendered hazy and uncertain by the fog.

And yet… there’s an instability to this reality. Certain buildings flicker at the edge of vision, as if half-synthetic, half-dreamt. Shadows move in ways that defy perspective, and the mountains in the distance seem to shift — a slow, tectonic breathing. Gravity is a suggestion here, the distinction between solid and ethereal porous. Perhaps the mist is not merely water, but something more: the visible edge of a liminal space where hard edges dissolve, and the ordinary rules of matter grow soft and yielding. The air thrums with the low, resonant harmonics of the factories, but beneath that, I catch stranger sounds: whistling that seems to come from nowhere, sudden cracks of static, a deep vibration that hums up from the earth. These are the sounds of a world still becoming — slow, patient, alive, and unutterably vast. Time stretches here. The mountains erode grain by grain; the mist condenses at the speed of honey. And through it all, that fog clings — a veil of cool, damp uncertainty.
