The world here is submerged, a sunken cityscape swallowed by a vast freshwater sea. Great stone structures—walls, towers, palisades, courtyards—rise from the soft, low-lying plain, smothered in a warm, buoyant darkness that deepens with every fathom. This is a place of still, liquid night, older than tools, lit only by faint bioluminescence that drifts like phantom dust in the water. The currents here are slow and thick with time.

The water itself is a dense presence, full of unseen things, pressing in on the drowned architecture. It is not ocean saline but the accumulated weight of slow rivers that drowned this place long ago, now settled into a deep, quiet equilibrium. The stone beneath is eroded, edges smoothed to soft contours by centuries of subtle currents, its surfaces draped in a sparse scatter of pale, feather-like algae and the anchor-lines of great colonies.

This is no dead world. Fish swarm in massive, fluid schools along the causeways and through empty windows—darting, shifting, ever-present. Crustaceans scuttle across dark walls and paths, their hard shells rasping softly. Silt shifts in unseen flows. Bioluminescent jellyfish drift like slow lanterns through colonnades. Whales move like silent mountains through the deeper avenues, their calls resonating through the water. The whole sunken metropolis thrums with quiet, dense life—a complex, active ecology thriving in the drowned world.

The rules are those of weight and pressure, of buoyancy and current. Sound is muted, absorbed by the water; the loudest noise is the groan of shifting structures, the click of chitinous legs, the rush of a great beast moving past. Light is scarce, a ghostly twilight filtered through layers of water or cast by living things. It is a world where gravity is softened, where up and down become less certain, and the ruined geometry looms unexpectedly from the dark. The water is the only atmosphere, and it is everywhere, all-encompassing. Prey darts away from unseen hunters; currents deposit fresh silt over ancient stone; life consumes life in an endless, slow dance. This city breathes water. It is not dead, but transformed, dreaming in its liquid cradle. The sheer scale of the place is evident even in the gloom—vast plazas where fish swirl in the upper stories, residential blocks collapsing into reefs, all of it interconnected by drowned streets and submerged staircases. It feels like visiting the slow, deep thoughts of a very old world.
