The landscape before you is a monstrous, unending industrial complex. Every inch is built, the ground made of corroded alloy plates and ancient, riveted girders, slick with condensation. Here and there, water pools gather in dents and rivulets, bleeding into the vast, open space, draining into the steel innards below. This water is the lifeblood of the machine-city, seeping up from unknown depths, and constantly dripping, weeping from the joints of titanic pipes overhead.

The air is cool, thick with the scents of oil, rust, and ammonia. It shivers with a low-frequency thrum — a mechanical throb so deep it feels like the metal world’s own heartbeat. Then, the soundscape explodes into a deafening cacophony: steam valves blast open nearby with engine-force roars; huge gears grind against each other with an arthritic grinding of metal on metal; unseen processes churn in the deep distance, a relentless arrhythmic clanking that never ends.

This is an ancient built world, deeply weathered by its own processes. Great structures loom in the industrial twilight — skeletal frames of factories, ductwork ridged like exposed ribs. Everything here has been here for millennia, settled into a state of heavy permanence. The few lights...lights? There are cracked panels here and there, their glow failing to push back the all-encompassing industrial gloom. The vast majority of illumination bleeds down from immense, rust-stained pipelines running across the distant ceiling.

Vegetation is sparse. It clings stubbornly to the edges of the light, to the moisture-rich shadows where dripping rustwater gathers: thick stands of black, rubbery reeds rooted in oily puddles; fungus clinging in unexpected crevices; lichen encrusting the corroded metal. This is the only life that thrives here. There are no animals, no beasts. No birds, no insects. The world here is built for machines, from machines, and by machines.

And the machines...they are strange. Most seem to have no operators, or no apparent makers. Some mechanisms operate with a rhythm that seems almost organic, yet they are composed of metal, steam, and gears. One towering apparatus nearby groans with the sound of immense turbines turning, but its product is only condensation, only the sound. Another machine seems to be engaged in a relentless, mechanical excavation—or perhaps an endless burial? It’s difficult to tell in the dim, orange gloom. The scale is overwhelming. This is not a single factory; it is a city of factories, a landscape of intricate, ancient mechanisms, each with its own impossible purpose. The rules here are not natural laws. What is built is what is real. The material has a voice, a vast, deafening voice, that speaks through every grinding gear. Water flows, condenses, and drips—but only to be put to work in these technological processes. Every mechanism operates irreversibly toward its own logic, its own weird function.
