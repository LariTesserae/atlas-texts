The landscape emerges: a vast, near-empty basin high on a nascent mountain range. Ice-cold winds scour rock and frost, carrying veils of freezing rain and sleet that turn the air itself into an ever-present, cutting damp. Peering through the dim, cloud-diffused light, one can see the mountainsides still raw with tectonic violence, freshly thrust upward and only beginning to erode. Glacier-smoothed outcrops, precariously balanced talus slopes, and jagged ridges have not yet been worn to gentle profiles; their edges are sharp and their forms embryonic. Water clings and condenses everywhere—in crystalline films sheeting over dark stone, in weeping fissures that never dry, in meltwater pools hallucinatory in their clarity. The thinness of atmosphere at such height leaches warmth away, leaving a perpetual, marrow-deep chill.

Sparse, hardy life finds purchase on these margins. Hardy lichens crusting exposed rock face the biting wind, while mosses, dark emerald and deep russet, cling in sheltered lee sides of boulders and within crevices where meltwater trickles. Low, gnarled alpine shrubs, most no taller than one's shins and twisted by the constant wind, cling tenaciously to pockets of gravelly soil. Their silvery-gray or deep green leaves are small, waxy, often curled to preserve moisture. Perhaps there are alpine forget-me-nots, tiny and vividly blue, or the papery bells of mountain avens dotting sheltered nooks. This ragged biome is interrupted, shattered—great swathes of bare, scoured rock sweep across the slopes between these hard-won colonies of vegetation.

The cold that rules here is intense, perpetual, the chill biting through to the bone, and the thinness of the atmosphere at this altitude allows sunlight to fall weakly upon the stark terrain. Sound is almost entirely absent—a profound stillness. Little beyond the occasional hiss of sleet or the groaning creak of stone expanses as they settle into themselves, or the lonely, haunting wind whistling over high passes. The wind itself carries a constant taste of mineral frost and distant ice.

Astonishingly, amid the cold and desolation, fauna abounds. Herds of sturdy, shaggy mountain ibex cling to cliffs and watchful, pale-feathered ptarmigan scurry through the rocks, almost vanishing against patches of persistent snow. Keen-eyed, sleek-feathered falcons ride the updrafts, scanning for movement below, and in the deepest mountain tarns, strange, pale fish dart beneath the ice, their forms barely visible. This high, remote corner of the world is a region of constant motion: the slow grind of geology reshaping raw stone, the freeze-thaw cycles widening cracks, and the constant push of the wind over slopes of scree and talus. It's a place where erosion has barely begun its long work, where everything is coming into being.
