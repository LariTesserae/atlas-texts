The place is a pocket dimension of flooded city, submerged deep within an engineered water-world. This isn't a sunken ruin — the city was built to be here, its foundations fused with bedrock at the bottom of a deep, cold ocean trench. The architecture is strange, sharp-edged and geometric but designed expressly to be part of the endless crushing dark, the buildings creating canyons and grottoes where city lights still glow with a pale, lonely luminescence. The water is intensely packed with motion and suspended particles, ocean currents sweeping around towers and pushing through empty plazas, a turbulence you can feel in your bones, an immense unseen weight pressing down.

Despite being fully submerged, the place possesses a sense of enclosure — a hidden space within the greater abyss. The water itself is a medium of transport and industry. The tech here manipulates physics in ways that feel both advanced and ancient, as if the city had evolved here over eons, the processes of erosion and growth guided by intelligences long vanished but still humming in the strange portals and conduits that channel the currents, building and eroding the structures in slow, uninterrupted cycles. No animals live here, no fish roam the corridors. The city itself is the only inhabitant, a single vast nervous system of stone and metal.

It is cool, perpetually damp, the chill leaching into your bones even through protective gear. The only light comes from the city itself, the luminous stripes and nodes embedded in the architecture providing a false daylight in the perpetual midnight of the deeps. The air, if it can be called that, is thick and heavy, saturated with moisture, the taste of salt and minerals sharp on your tongue.

The sounds here are loud, a symphony of shifting water and groaning structures, the city itself vibrating in response to the ceaseless motion of the ocean outside. The weirdness of the place resonates in the alien geometries of the architecture, the unseen processes that govern the slow transformation from stone to multiplex structures of the city. It's a place in constant motion, the currents a living, breathing force that shapes and reshapes the flooded streets and plazas, an ecosystem of physics rather than biology.
