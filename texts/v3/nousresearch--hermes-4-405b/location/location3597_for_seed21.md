The world unfolds here as a high, wet grove, perched like an emerald in the cradle of a young, sharp-featured mountain range still puckered with the labor of its recent emergence. Scale is intimate: perhaps a hundred paces across, ringed by sheer, freshly-broken cliffs that weep condensation in silver threads. An ancient technology beyond our modern understanding mingles seamlessly with the environment, expressed in solitary crystalline structures—none larger than a cottage—that stand half-swallowed by burgeoning jungle. These artifacts hum with a barely sub-audible vibration, resonating in the bones more than the ear, their angles strangely precise against the mountain's raw, angular teeth yet not unharmonious with the local stone.

Heat washes over the area in thick waves, but respite comes from water constantly dripping, flowing, and pooling. The jungle is lush and bursting with life, yet curiously sparse—palms and broad-leaved shrubs erupt from every crack in the dark, rain-slickened rock, but they stand widely spaced like deliberate plantings in an ornamental garden rather than a monoculture. Empty spaces yawn between the stalks and fronds, filled only with shimmering veils of humidity and the low, wet calls of infrequently glimpsed creatures. These animals, rare and often unseen, add their quiet chorus to the constant percussion of water: the soft drumming of heavy raindrops on broad leaves, the slow splash from over-flowing pools, the ceaseless trickle of condensation down a thousand rock faces. Water pools in shallow depressions, reflecting the sky in myriad glittering mirrors before seeping into the porous, newly-exposed rock.

The grove is active: clouds scud by overhead, Forever altering the play of light through the trees. The Intense sunlight filters through the canopy, creating living patterns of shadow that shift as the leaves stir in a hot, thin breeze high above. Pools slowly gather and drain, roots visibly grow and shift, and the crystal structures themselves seem to subtly alter—perhaps absorbing light or moisture in a slow, deliberate rhythm. Strange flowers speckle the landscape, their iridescent petals occasionally shifting colors like oil on water, and the ground is a tapestry of mosses and ferns that seem to mourn the passing of an older, stranger world only recently born. There are undercurrents of deep time here, in the resilient green life and the silent, watchful technological artifacts—whispering of isolation, secret experiments, or sacred gardens where the boundaries between engineered and evolved blur into something wholly new and alien.
