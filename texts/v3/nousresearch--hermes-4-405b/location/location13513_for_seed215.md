In this place, massive crags and ranges rise out of an endless plane, their slopes young, still sloughing off sharp-edged, perfectly-shaped geometric stones. Across the crust of this world, cities stretch out over the hard terrain in grids of force and field. Skyscrapers of shimmering crystal, geodesic domes, and towers of impossible geometries are scattered across the land, each miles apart but connected by strands of light, energy coursing between them like rivers on a map. The cities hum with advanced tech, visions and fogs condensing on glowing structures and exotic materials. From the skyscrapers, water cascades in rivulets down their crystalline sides, collecting in pools at their bases. The cities seem unfinished, some still half-buried, their foundations dug deep into the rock, others just extruding from fabrication farms on the outskirts. The towering cities provide warmth for settled colonies living in the cracks. Their carefully shaped fields are quilted in bright greens and blues, small farms. Each of the great spires rises farther than the last, punching through the atmosphere into orbit, becoming rings, stations, vessels floating in the night above the clouds. Cold mists persist in the shadows, even though the near distance basks in the fierce light of the sky. The air is alive with the calls of an array of fauna. Birds, reptiles, many-legged things dart through the skies and run across the ground. Plasma conduits miles across are submerged in the earth, and the static hums of machinery whir through the very bones of the place; sciences and minds beyond human reach, strange satellites, constructs - watching perches for passing god-minds. In the heights above, strange fabrics unfurl like wings or sails on the great spires. The air is sharp and cold, smelling of frost and ozone. The sky is full of weather, and the light is cold, sharp, crystalline, never stopping the cold, just revealing it. Rains come in short bursts, feeding into the marshes. In the rare moments when the cities pause, the world reveals its indigenous sounds: wind, storms, constant geological activity, the invisible hum of the strange and distant.
