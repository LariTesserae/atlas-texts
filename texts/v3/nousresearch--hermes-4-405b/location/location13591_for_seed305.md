The land is a vast plateau, stretching toward distant horizons so jagged they might be mountain ranges or crystal growths a thousand miles tall. At this high elevation, the air should be thin, but the entire plateau is drenched in a cold, dense atmosphere, heavy with the scent of ozone and petrichor. There are no buildings, no made roads, no artifacts of intelligence beyond the landscape itself. Even so, everything here thrums with a deep, internal industry. The sound isn't mechanical or artificial — it's the groaning of geology, the deep thrum of vast, slow-moving processes, the roar of distant wind across innumerable canyons.

Moisture is intermittent here, arriving as condensation that beads on enormous, sharp-edged rock formations. These are young rocks, recently emerged from the core of this place, still sharp and angular, like shattered glass the size of skyscrapers. They erupt in clusters and ridges, and in every crack and crevice between them, life thrives. Here, the vegetation is lush, spilling out in vast, dense carpets of deep green moss and lichen thick enough to bury a person. Strange, cold-hardy plants cling to vertical surfaces, their broad leaves beaded with condensation that glitters in the intense, bright light. The entire area glows; the sky is perpetually washed in brilliant auroras, hanging close to the earth like luminous fog.

Fauna here is overwhelming. In every direction, around every rock, life crawls and buzzes and roars. Insects the size of small birds flit through the frozen air, their carapaces glinting like metal. Dense flocks of birds or something like them darken the sky in patches, their cries sharp and bright in the cold. Heavy, slow breathing holds a deep rhythm in the mist, as thick-bodied mammals or something stranger wade through fields of moss and tangled, frost-rimed brambles. They move languidly, conserving energy in the biting cold even as they navigate the saturated density of this place. There is no space here unoccupied by life or its processes, no empty patch of ground or quiet corner of air. Every inch pulses with existence, a saturated density of presence.

This is a slow, patient world. Seasons pass like dreams across these vast plains. The slow creep of glaciers (for there are glaciers, white at the high edges of the plateaus) inches forward over centuries. Erosion grinds stone into sand with imperceptible patience, carving new valleys with the weight of millennia. Yet even in its slowness, the place is irrevocably alive — a colossal engine of biological intensity and geological upheaval, running on a timescale all its own. The weirdness of it all is the strangest part, a deep sense that every rock and plant and creature is part of something more, threads in an ecological tapestry unfathomably vast and ancient. The land seems to breathe, to pulse with cold light, to exist in a state of permanent, vibrant becoming.
