The light here is dim because you are below the surface, where a riparian forest of colossal trees has bled down into a subterranean canyon. The forest canopy far above blocks most sun from reaching this low place, and you are beneath a second canopy: mature roots and seeping cisterns of moss, a damp total vegetation of root and drooping vine that has filled this sunken city with its own soft, wet life. The city was built in a narrow river canyon, a settlement-scale warren of layered architecture pressed between water and high walls, now a humid ruin. Rusted iron pipes jut through root systems; gears and checkerboard walkways, the remains of a mechanized municipal infrastructure, groan as the plants deform them, choking gears with flowers, splitting stone with thirsty roots. Water drips and pools, feeding the overwhelming, deafening population of insects and larger, stranger fauna that have infested the warm dark: the air shudders with the noise of life, the carnal symphony of a predator-prey ecosystem in active motion, a pure density of living things. It's wildly strange, the ruin and the flora grown indistinguishable, a single dripping mass of warm, breathing stone.
