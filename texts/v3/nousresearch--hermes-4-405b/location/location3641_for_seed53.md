The name of this place is the Whisper-Riven. It is a high-altitude basin, roughly the size of a mountain-cradled hamlet, where the air thins and carries an edge of unnatural chill. The sky here is painfully bright, a sharp blue intensified by the altitude and cold, yet it rarely shows a sun; light seems to permeate the very air, reflecting off the silvery dust that perpetually hangs in the atmosphere. This dust, fine and metallic, is the cause of the basin's name, for when stirred by the almost imperceptible, icy winds that sigh through the narrow mountain passes, it creates a faint, shifting chorus of whispers that can drive the unwary to madness.

No plant life stains this silver-grey world. It is a place of pure, weathered mineral. The basin floor and the soaring, near-sheer cliffs that form its boundaries are composed of ancient, dark rock, sculpted by eons of wind and frost into grooves, crevices, and deep, shadowed overhangs. Water exists here not in rivers or lakes, but as a ghostly, intermittent presence. Condensation collects in millions of tiny rivulets during the colder parts of the day, only to freeze and then sublimate away under the intense light and dry air, leaving behind intricate traceries of frost and elusive, damp patches that glisten briefly before vanishing.

The density of geological features is striking. Pinnacles, boulders, and wind-carved arches are packed so closely they seem to press against one another, creating a maze of narrow passages and hidden alcoves. Scattered amongst these natural formations, in a way that seems almost deferential, are the vestiges of a crude, forgotten people. Low, rough-dressed walls of grey stone, no higher than a man's waist, outline small, purposeless enclosures. There are no complete structures, just these faint marks, a few hammer-dressed stones, and the occasional shallow cave whose entrance has been crudely, uselessly walled. Tools, if they can be called that, are primitive indeed: shards of harder rock crudely knapped into scraping edges, or massive, blunt hammer-stones that scatter the ground, some arranged as if to suggest a path or a long-abandoned task. There is an eerie stillness to these remnants, a sense they were abandoned not with violence, but with a quiet, collective forgetting.

No animals call the Whisper-Riven home, adding to the profound silence that underlies the mineral chorus. Yet, the basin is not static. The weather cycles are abrupt and fierce, characterized by sudden temperature drops and bursts of dry, crystalline wind that scour the landscape, shifting the singing dust and subtly eroding the ancient stones. The light, too, plays its active role, its intensity fluctuating unpredictably, casting stark, elongated shadows that writhe and crawl as the sunless day progresses, making the terrain seem alive with unseen movement. This is a place of stark beauty and unsettling energy, a dense, mineral heart beating slowly in the roof of the world, where the only sounds are the sigh of wind through stone and the endless, whispering secrets of the dust.
