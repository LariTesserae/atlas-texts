The place is a Vast Bowl, an immense basin where the air is warm and still. The scale is continental, the horizon a distant curved line hinting at the bowl's true size. Dotted across this vast expanse are countless clusters of growth, each a dense mesh of life — these are Cities of Biota, their structures built not of stone or metal but of living wood and flesh, root and chitin, all woven into intricate, towering forms by an intelligence that is not of tool or device but purely biological. There are no machines, no technology as we know it; everything is grown, everything lives. The rules of this world are those of biology taken to an extreme, every niche filled, every surface claimed, every structure part of a living, breathing whole. The only water here is what is bound within the cells of the life forms themselves; the air is dry. Sound is scarce, a low hum of processes, a whisper of wind through biological chimneys, an occasional deep thrum felt in the dense-packed "ground" — the sound of hearts beating on a grand scale. The landscape is low, the Cities of Biota built out rather than up; they sprawl, densely packed with flora and fauna, lives intertwining in a silent, active dance. Erosion is almost non-existent; the land is emerging, still soft and pliable, pre-formation, as if the Cities are sculpting the very earth into their foundation. This is a young land, raw geology being shaped by the relentless expansion of life. Light here is normal, the sun bathing the Cities in a warm glow that feeds the lush vegetation woven into every structure. There is no void here, no emptiness; every inch is occupied, saturated with life, a carpet of sensoria, communication, and construction all happening through biological means only. The fauna are abundant, filling every niche — creatures that tend the living structures, creatures that maintain the delicate balance, creatures that are prey and predator in a silent, constant cycle. Weather here is subtle, the vast scale allowing for gentle shifts in humidity and temperature, but no rain or storms — the cycle is internal, the world breathing with the rhythms of its own vast, packed biosphere.
