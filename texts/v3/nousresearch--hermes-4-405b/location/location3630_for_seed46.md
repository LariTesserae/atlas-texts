The Gulch of Irresistible Emergence

Here in the Gulch, high-elevation desolation collides with embryonic fecundity. The light is normal, midday — which also means the heat is intense, almost oppressive. And there's no water here, not a drop — a hollow baked to desiccation, rock and dust bleached pale beneath an uncaring sun.

But the footing is strange. This narrow dry wash keeps getting resurfaced from within. Underfoot, pebbles press, insistent and precise. They are gray, seamless, machine-made — not here and there, but everywhere, pressing up through the pale dust like seeds seeking sun, clicking lightly as they shift against their neighbors, a sparse but constant sound that never rises or falls, a substrate emerging relentlessly beneath you, almost geologic in its patience.

Sometimes a rough-edged slab of warm metal heaves up through the pebbles, raw-blanked and mill-hot, glowing faintly. Scattered, sparse eruptions of industrial fallout, still smelling of ozone and hot alloy. Then silence, except for the pebbles' patient clicking. Immense factory fanfares seem to loom just out of perception, felt more than heard.

Then the floral infection sets in. Cabbage-plants emerge from cracks between pebbles, thick meaty leaves, iridescent-purple-and-gold, elephant-ear frills. But they sprout big hole-punch patterns, deliberate-looking absences, smooth circular voids excised straight through the leaves, revealing nothing — just pristine slices of absence itself.

Lush ferns fork upward in a slow-motion paroxysm, growing visibly yet glacially. Their broad fronds are eye-achingly vibrant, visual static as if freshly faxed, shimmering in pixelated intensity. They cast deep, solid rectangular shadows — that slide sideways, complacent as sundials, when nothing else moves.

These plants seem to thrive on emptiness; some even extrude planar nothingness from their tips, glossy blotting-pads of erased reality that slowly delaminate from the world. The air around them carries a low, velvet numbness.

There are no animals. No insects. No water, no rivers, no sea. Just this dry, sun-baked gulch high in some deserted place, where industry and flora bloom from the fabric of the world itself, emerging slow and sparse and irrefutable, unmaking and remaking the world patient molecule by patient molecule, while distant engines dream their cadences just beyond the veil.
