The world hums. More than hums—a deep, resonant, shaking thing beneath. As if the bones of the planet are shifting, alive, uneasy. This is a subterranean realm, a wide cavity unfolding as new-made, ancient rock—or what should be rock, but isn't quite.

Water finds its way here—small condensation beads gather like pearls on jagged edges, unseen drips echo soft against the granite-like surfaces. But no rivers, no lakes grace this cavern. The air is warm and heavy with a scent that is mineral, yes, but also a strange, pungent mix: ozone, something vaguely sulfurous, and an alien musk, fecund and intense.

Light filters down from far above, shafts catching on crystalline formations—fleeting, new growths, semi-translucent, jutting like embryonic stalactites. The clarity is surprising. The cavern itself feels unfinished, a space being carved not by slow erosion but by a feverish energy, stone actively splitting, fresh scars hinting at recent fractures. Everywhere, a sense of things beginning to form.

It is teeming here.

Not with plants; no roots take hold. Instead, the space swarms with life of an overwhelming, alien kind: dense populations of creatures no catalog would list. Carapaced things the size of dogs move in restless, chittering hordes, clattering across the canyon floors. Swift, winged forms, no birds, dart like scattered thoughts through the open space between the rising walls. In every alcove, across every rough surface, movement pulses—burrowing, scaling, a thousand little lives enacting a relentless dance of survival. The sounds rise into a colossal wave: clicks, shuffles, piercing trills, alien chirrups, the skittering of countless chitinous limbs on stone. It is not a peaceful hum, but the cacophony of a very lively, crowded market—a roar, almost, that fills the air and vibrates through the stone.

Scattered amid it all are crude, simple artifacts, as if washed down from the world above or left behind by hands unknown. Here a rough-hewn bowl, there a sharpened stone. Simple, primitive things, as if just emerging from the first idea of tools. The scene is turbulent, frantic, as if creation and destruction are acting in fierce collaboration. Stone walls bear smooth-worn paths where those endless tiny feet have rubbed, faint trails of some soft substance smeared in patterns that hint at minds at work.

This is a place coming into being—ever-changing, ever-renewed—yet already dense with life. The sheer density of the fauna, the layered intensity of their presence, creates an oppressive, almost vibrating atmosphere. Sound and movement and primitive forms: an ecological chaos hinting at rules not set, not known to any familiar world. The underlying weirdness coils beneath it all, the hum of a creation that has not settled, that pulses with raw, dynamic energy, chaotic and unstable as it carves its way into existence.
