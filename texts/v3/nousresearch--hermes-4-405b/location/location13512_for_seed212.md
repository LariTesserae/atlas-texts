The air itself shimmers, thick with golden pollen and suspended chloroplasts. From the damp, yielding soil, roots have erupted—not as a slow, stately taking-of-ground, but in sudden bursts of fibrous life. These roots coil like tentacles, forming living bridges and tunnels through the lower canopy. The entire valley has been consumed by Growth: a low, sprawling city of living plant-matter, sharp-edged and freshly unfurled in petal-like layers. Stalks that look like towers still bead with condensed dew near their bases, even as the midday sun glares down, baking the upper strata into a turkey's-wattle red.

Every surface sweats moisture into the warm air, creating pockets of micro-rain where condensation drips continuously. The light is normal—neither dimmed by the leaf canopy nor unnaturally bright—but somehow refracted, bending around the edges of flesh-like succulent leaves to make them glow from within. Below, in the blue-green shadows, swollen fruits pulse with bioluminescence like awful hearts.

There is no animal life—only the deafening rush of wind through countless stomata, the groaning stretch of cellulose, the wet snap of tendrils as they flail toward the sky. Every cell here is hyperactive, a demolition derby of growth. New shoots knife upward between the bone-white ribs of older structures, which sag, turn necrotic purple, or crack along fault lines to collapse into fungal decay. Sap runs like open veins down the side of a tower that yesterday was pristine—a wound already thick with opportunistic moss and whipping, parasitic vines. The sheer density of it all presses in; a steps away, you might lose your way in this suffocating tangle of leaves and thorns.

The rules of this world have broken down: plants do not compete. They merge, hybridize, absorb one another. Colors that shouldn't exist in nature burn like signals on every surface—a cobalt so deep it feels black, a violet that bleeds orange at the edges. Time has gone turbulent, leaping forward in explosive bursts of new life, then collapsing into rot, then surging again. The air vibrates with that frenzied energy, and the roar of it all is a physical weight against your eardrums. Nothing here is stable for long. Nothing here remembers what it means to be only itself. The damp petrichor of a new world rises from disturbed earth, even as older structures wither, crumble, and begin their own strange, secondary lives as a new generation of roots drinks them dry.
