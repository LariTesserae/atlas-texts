The darkness here is not quite absolute. It’s a velvet, violet gloom, heavy and cloying, thick enough to feel like a pressure against the skin. The air is warm, holding at a steady temperature that suggests a deep, planetary heat source—or perhaps no source at all, simply a primal condition of the void.

This place is deathly dry and new. Crystalline structures emerge raw from the void as if pulled from some slow, massive womb. They pierce the gloom in spears and ragged sheets—obsidian black facets, deep translucent blues, cloudy greens, rough uncut jewel tones that catch the faint, evenly distributed ambient light and fracture it into fleeting, violet-tinged rainbows. The light itself is a paradox: a dim, ethereal glow from no discernible source, only barely skirting the edge of total darkness.

The terrain builds itself rapidly. Jagged slabs of something like volcanic glass—only colder, heavier, holding a sullen inner radiance—slide forth to form shelves and platforms. The vastness here is cleaved by towering crystalline masses emerging too fast to remain still. The ground beneath is a shifting field of raw geology, dense and deeply shadowed. Elevation is extreme and dynamic: new formations heave up from below with a low, grinding rumble as others collapse without sound. Fracture lines appear spontaneously, racing across crystalline faces before they split apart—or connect in impossible geometries.

Scattered across this terrain, faint marks and partial remains hint at attempts at structure. Sharp-edged, unidentifiable shards—perhaps small, abandoned constructs or the husks of long-extinct, unrecognizable life—lie half-buried in the swirling dust of creation. There are no plants, no water, no animals—the only movement aside from the terrain itself is the slow drift of coarse particulate through the violet air. The low, constant sound of fracture and collapse is the only break in the stillness.

This place is defined by its instability. Crystalline formations manifest, mutate, and dissolve in a turbulent, Solaris-like churn. The air itself thrums with a deep, subsonic frequency. Yet within the chaos, patterns begin to emerge: a fractal geometry in the formation of crystalline abstractions, the suggestion of some underlying, alien intelligence pulling itself up from the void. It’s a world actively and turbulently creating itself from nothing—and tearing itself apart just as quickly in an endless, chaotic, embryonic dance.
