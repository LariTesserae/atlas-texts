The world is a vast ocean planet, submerged beneath leagues of dark, cold water that stretches from pole to pole without interruption. This isn't a flooded world — there is no land, nor has there ever been. Above, the light is normal, filtering through fathoms upon fathoms to the deeper regions below, leaving the eternal abyssal blackness unbroken by sunlight but held in suspension by its own sheer, liquid vastness. Here, on the high continental shelf of an undersea range young enough to still bear the sharp features of its volcanic formation, the true strangeness of this submerged realm begins.

This is a mineral world. No plant life exists here, nothing that crawls, floats or swims recognizes itself as flora. The seabed is a frozen expanse of volcanic glass, obsidian plains and scree slopes frozen in the cold, high-pressure grip of the ocean, all the vista bathed in an eerie, normal light that reveals every detail of the sharp, recently-emerged geology. Near-void, the vast space between the presences is almost empty, save for the mineral formations and scattered, industrial-looking structures.

These structures are the legacy of the planet's weirdness, its never-changing equilibrium. They are scattered across the volcanic range like the remnants of a machine long fallen silent, although they were never truly active in the way their makers envisioned. Built when the planet’s physics were still scaling itself to its own dimensions, the industrial skeletons are relics of a scaled production that never saw its intended use, electrified but inert monuments to a frozen equilibrium. Occasional artifacts are half-buried in the volcanic sediment, hints of a single building or a vast engine abandoned during its foundation.

And then—you hear it: a deafening roar, an industrial symphony of engines, a sound so vast in scale it matches the size of the ocean itself. It washes over and through everything, the sound of an eternal, mechanized heartbeat holding station in the ocean’s depths. This sound is the voice of the planet's fauna, abundant and unusual, which call this mineral world home. The creatures are constructed of metal and glass, their frames echoing the scattering of monuments, limbs riveted and gears grinding in the cold, thick water. Their existence defies understanding; they are beings of pure, unchanging equilibrium, part of the ocean’s own physics.

There are no gradients, no shifts in this frozen realm. Time has become irrelevant, especially in this deep, unchanging region. All is held in stasis, in a balance between the cold and the pressure, the light and the near-void. This is an ocean planet that exists outside linear time, in a state of perpetuity, a world both deafening in industry and yet, paradoxically, frozen in an eternal, silent waltz of physics.
