High in the cool, liquid atmosphere of this world, suspended in the endless blue that knows no surface, floats a structure both vast and intricate. Imagine a cathedral built not of stone, but of frozen light and condensed vapor, glowing with the captured radiance of a distant sun. The air is so thick with moisture that it has become a solid thing in its own right. You are submerged within this aqueous expanse, yet buoyed in an impossible suspension where up and down are meaningless. This is a space of walls, arches, vaults, and spires, all sculpted from the very essence of the water that permeates everything. These frozen waves are young and sharp, their edges crisp as shattered glass, for the forces that shaped them have only just begun to subside.

A constant rain falls—not the gentle patter of droplets, but a roaring wall of liquid that cascades in veils and torrents from the vaulted ceilings. The cacophony of this deluge is the heartbeat of the place, a deafening symphony punctuated by the booming collapse of ice arches and the faint, distant groan of shifting pressures in the deep. This world is raw and elemental. No trace of green mars its crystalline perfection. Where you'd expect ferns or lichen, there's only the slow seep of mineral deposits, painting the ice in swirls of rust and jade. In the rare stillness, when the rain momentarily slackens, you might see the first stirrings of things not quite alive: thin films of iridescent oil spreading on the water's surface, or quick, electric eels darting through the submerged corridors, sparks trailing behind them like dying stars.

The scale of this oceanic cathedral is a paradox. Though the ice-spire vaults tower like a dream of shattered sky and the echoing halls stretch into blue infinity, each chamber feels intimate, compressed, a self-contained world. The water itself is dense, thick as liquid mercury, pressing in on everything. It's a slow world, suspended in a state of perpetual transformation. Glaciers creep with aching patience, carving new chambers grain by grain, dripping meltwater that freezes again into everlasting icicles. There are no sudden cataclysms here, only the inexorable grind of tectonic plates shifting deep below. The only life here, if you can call it that, is a teeming abundance of things mechanical and cold-blooded.

There are no buildings here, no crafted objects. Everything is raw and untouched, a natural cathedral wrought by fantastic forces. Yet there's a sense of purpose behind the chaos, a sly intelligence in the ceaseless motion. Schools of gleaming metal fish dart through the submerged arches, their scales like overlapping gears, their movements precise and efficient. In the shadows, vast, coiling shapes writhe in the depths, things resembling great serpents forged of iron and rivet. They are the lords of this place: sleek, armored leviathans whose bodies churn the water into swirling vortices. Everything here is connected in a web of interlocking parts, like the components of some vast, living engine. Gears grind deep beneath the ice, their turning echoed in the slow pulsing of the light.

The light, too, is strange. It's normal, almost mundane, filtering through the ice-blue water in a diffuse, dreamy haze. But this light doesn't come from any sun or moon. Instead, it emanates from the very fabric of this world, a cool, electric luminescence that seems to seep from the ice itself. This is the purpose of all this motion, all this patient, grinding work: the generation of light, the transformation of elemental forces into pure radiance. The leviathans stir the water, the gears turn beneath the ice, the glaciers drip their meltwater into vast reservoirs of liquid power. The gears transfer that energy to the heart of the machine, where it is woven into light, pure and cold, a beacon shining in the eternal dark. The whole world is an engine for the making of light.

It is a world of impossible contrasts: cold and fire, solid and liquid, stillness and ceaseless motion. And in this place of paradoxes, the only constant is the rain, the deep, resonant sound of water shaping the world, falling forever.
