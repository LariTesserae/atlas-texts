The Citadel of Whispers emerges from the salt-white sands of the Empty Quarter, a hidden fortress of stone and vine. It is pocket-scale, tucked into a vast desert where water cannot exist—its stones seem to drink dryness, exhaling only heat. Here, at land's low elevation, the air shimmers, baked by a relentless sun. Light floods every crack and alleyway with a harsh, white glare. Yet within the Citadel's labyrinthine walls, a paradox blooms: dense, lush vegetation erupts from every crevice. Thick green vines cascade down towers; their tendrils snake across cobblestones. There is no water, yet these leafy giants thrive—feeding on sunlight alone, perhaps, or some sorcery in the stone.

This is an ancient place of primitive construction: the walls fit without mortar, hand-hewn and set in place by unknown hands. Moss-curtains hang thick and heavy from old timbers; ivy ropes bind stairwells; ferns spread in damp corners where shadows gather. One might expect such dense foliage to hold abundant fauna, but life here walks softly—if at all. Only the occasional needle-fast beetle darts between leaves. Even birds are absent. Instead, the wind speaks for all. It roars through the Citadel's open roofs and broken parapets—a constant, deafening hurricane. It whips the vines into frenzy; they writhe like serpents and lash against the stone. Creaking timbers sing above a groaning chorus of shifting weight. Pops and snaps ring out as old wood splits. Here, the very bones of this world are in motion, powdered by time into a fine grit that turns every surface into a mosaic of sand and stone.

A strange rule governs the temperature: heat rules outside, but deep within the Citadel’s heart, a cool breath flows—perhaps from underground aquifers of some alien liquid, or cold veins buried deep in the planet's crust. These temporal shifts between swelter and chill breathe life through the fortress walls. Each night brings dew-slick surfaces that shimmer under the stars; each dawn heats them dry once more. This cycle etches its own strange erosion into every surface—a history written in cracks and salt stains.
