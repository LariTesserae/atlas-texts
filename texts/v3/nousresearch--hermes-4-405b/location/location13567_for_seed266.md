The air is warm and thin, carrying the scent of heated metal, sun-warmed stone, and a faint ozone tang. Within the towering canyon walls, a network of impossible structures clings to the rock faces and spans the void on impossibly slender suspension bridges. These are the homes and factories of a species whose biology is as alien as their architecture – part organic, part constructed, their energy drawn directly from the thermoelectric currents in the rock or harvested from the light that pierces the atmospheric roof.

This city, if ‘city’ is the right word, is built vertically into the chasm’s faces rather than spread across its floor. Great honeycombed bulks, pierced by windows like distant stars, rest directly against the cliff walls as if seared into place, their surfaces scarred by the canyons’ eons. Gossamer-thin elevated walkways hang suspended, shifting slightly as unseen masses move across them; closer examination reveals crystalline filaments, grown rather than spun, their internal geometries humming with slow power. This is built habitat – dense with intention, layered in construction both recent and deeply ancient – but the urban fabric itself is sparse, with vast stretches of bare rock face between settled zones. Movement is patient, reflecting the slow thermal currents and deliberate energies that power this place.

Life here is deserving of the word ‘abundant’, but it is a life that flutters on iridescent vanes in the updrafts, or scuttles in complex clockwork colonies within the fissures and access tunnels that lace the living rock. Their forms are built as much as grown, intricate and many-limbed, sometimes meshing seamlessly into the humming relays and power conduits that stud every surface. No greenery mars the severe mineral beauty of this place; the native biologies construct themselves from the raw materials of the world itself, evolving their components like living alloys and ceramics. Many possess intricate photoreceptive structures, feeding on the abundant light that illuminates the chasm depths in ever-shifting mosaics. The sounds here are intimate despite the scale – the deep thrum of geothermal engines, the drip of condensate from a million cooling surfaces, the faint clicks and whirrs of living industry carried on the constant whispers of wind finding its way through turrets and bridges.

The entire vertiginous landscape feels constructed, carefully balanced by beings who have shaped their habitat through the long, slow application of formidable technology. Engines and processes, thermal and mechanical, are everywhere present yet almost entirely silent, integrated into the living rock and built fabric as traces of heat or subtle vibration rather than dissonance. The whole forms a vast, deep, warm canyon world governed by the slow timescales of geology and the patient, unending cycles of the sun. Here, the separation between the built and the biological, between the inanimate and the living, has long since ceased to hold meaning; the world is entirely of a piece, a colossal urban engine humming within the deep bones of a mineral world.
