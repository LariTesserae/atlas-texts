At a planetary scale, the water here is a deep, cold ocean — or rather, it resembles a primordial, hyper-churning version of one. We are submerged within it, yet paradoxically elevated. The water itself is the terrain, sculpted into towering, raw-geology mountain ranges, but these are no static peaks; they are embryonic landmasses, constantly and chaotically birthing themselves. The very fabric of the landscape is in hyperactive flux, a turbulent storm-state of becoming. This is a place of instability at a cosmic scale, as if Solaris itself had decided to manifest and unmanifest entire mountain chains within its depths on a whim.

Vegetation is almost nonexistent. Sparse, hardy forms cling to the infinitesimal ledges and crevices — strange, dark growths that seem ancient and enduring, like memorials to an earlier, more stable epoch. They are the only visual relief in a landscape dominated by the raw, mineral presence of stone and the ever-shifting, crushing embrace of the ocean.

The temperature is cool, a biting chill that seeps into everything. The light here is normal, filtering down through the miles of water above, painting the emerging peaks and valleys in haunting, ethereal tones. There is no animal life at all, and no constructed artifacts or signs of building — the landscape is utterly untouched and undeveloped, a wilderness of water and rock.

The sounds are loud and immense — the groaning of stone shifting, the roar of water carving new paths, the cracking and rumbling of entire mountains birthing themselves. The atmosphere is thick with a sense of raw power, of creation and destruction locked in a dance of unimaginable scale. The density of the place is paradoxical. While the water and stone are omnipresent, there is an overwhelming sense of hollowness, of an immense emptiness behind and within the unfolding geological drama. The space between each atom feels vast, as if reality itself is stretched thin here.

The rules of the world defy easy explanation. Physics as we understand it seems to bend and warp, allowing for the beyond-modern engineering of these ever-changing, monumental landforms. The technology, if it can be called such, is woven into the very fabric of the planet — not artificial, but an integral, inexplicable force driving the dynamic transformations. It is a weird, turbulent and altogether unusual place, a crucible for the emergence of new worlds and new possibilities.
