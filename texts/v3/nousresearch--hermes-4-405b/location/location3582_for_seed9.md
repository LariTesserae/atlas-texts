The Engine Garden of Clouds perches near a warm, high-altitude peak where the air is perpetually thick with condensing vapor. Here, nothing is as it seems—every rock, every leaf, every drop of water is part of an immense, fully constructed system that simulates nature with uncanny precision. Giant, hissing pistons rise from the ground like trunks of primordial trees, their steel surfaces slick with mineral-rich water that weeps constantly from joints and seams. These mechanical pillars support a lattice of copper and iron girders draped in lush, engineered flora: rubbery ferns with graphite-veined leaves, pulsating orchids whose bioluminescent patterns shift like mood rings, and creeping vines that extrude from nozzles in the infrastructure, their tips weaving ambitious bridges across the gaps between structures.

The ground is not ground at all, but a suspended platform of perforated grating through which geothermal steam continuously rises, warming the hanging garden and feeding the perpetual cycle of artificial rain. Water condenses on every cold metal surface, gathering into rivulets that chime like glass chimes as they trickle down before being recaptured by a network of hidden channels. This near-constant wetness feeds the dense, layered vegetation that thrives in misty air, its leaves pearled with perpetual dew. Small, cathedral-like enclosures emerge from the machinery—domed rooms formed by the curvature of giant cogs and gears whose teeth interlock like ancient stonework—each hosting a miniature biome of hanging mosses, vibrating lichen, and spongy, absorbent fungi that purify and recycle the water endlessly.

The soundscape is a meditative thrum punctuated by sharp mechanical sighs, the groan of cold metal expanding in the steamy air, and the gentle percussion of droplets falling from lofty reservoirs into collection basins below. Rarely, one glimpses the caretakers—glossy, multi-limbed constructs with glass abdomens swirling with nanite-laden oils, they move with silent precision along designated service gantries, their telescopic eyes scanning for inefficiencies or ripe seed pods ready for harvest. They are the only fauna in this active, living engine, tending to the embryonic landscape as it cycles through mist and precipitation, growth and harvest, a fully orchestrated dance of artificial life perched impossibly in the clouds.
