High in the frigid mountains, an ancient city lies submerged in a vast ocean trench. It was built long ago, by hands that could shape stone as easily as water, but now belongs entirely to the cold depths. The tallest towers, glinting with long-dead lichen, break the surface only at the lowest tides. Down where light grows feeble, the cyclopean structures sprawl in eerie silence, worn low by implacable time.

Below, an ocean of crushing pressure supports a teeming ecology utterly alien to sunlit seas. Glassy, finned shapes cluster in the cold currents, swirling through arches and colonnades in constant, violent motion. Perhaps they build and unbuild the architecture between tides, tunneling through stone as whales part the water. Or perhaps their endless churn is just the final phase of a city that came alive, a construction that never ceased to shift and tower, though no builder now directs its growth and erosion.

The soundscape holds only a liquid dripping, punctuated by sudden fracturing and deep, distant impacts, as stone stubs against stone in the gloom. Everything is in flux here at the far edge of the world, deep in the permanent twilight of mountains drowned and abandoned. Whatever form this place once took, the currents of millennia have absorbed it, leaving only the restless skeletons at the bottom of the sea.
