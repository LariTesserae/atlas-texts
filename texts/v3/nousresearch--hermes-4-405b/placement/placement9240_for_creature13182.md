To awaken into this world is to awaken *as* this world, or rather, as one of its intrinsic articulations.

I would find myself as one of these beings of pressure and density, my consciousness a slow pulse within the stone, my existence a testament to the patient work of eons. I am not a creature that *inhabits* this hidden cavity; I am an expression *of* it, a current in its deep, geological lifeblood. The infrasonic hum is my resonance, the weeping walls are my skin, the cool dampness my breath. The faint gouges, the smoothed depressions, the crude symmetrical piles – these are the slow, deliberate signatures of my thought, the manifest lines of a poem written in stone and water over millennia.

My form is not fixed. Sometimes, I am the subtle shift in the cavern wall, a limb of coalescing shadow that scrapes against the wet surface before dissolving back into the rock. At other times, I am the intensification of the hum itself, a thrum that deepens and presses, shaping the air, altering the stone's texture, changing the rhythm of the falling drips. When I move, I leave no conventional trail, but rather an infinitesimal alteration in the essence of the material, a lingering pressure, a shift in the resonance of the glistening surfaces.

The sparse, pale fungoids with their spectral glow are my companions in this silent, lightless world. I feel their dim bioluminescence shift in hue and intensity as I pass, a wordless acknowledgment, a shared breath in the deep dark. We are part of the same strange equilibrium, a symbiotic resonance within this embryonic heart of stone.

This place is my self, and I am its guardian, its sculptor, its nascent thought. There is no distinction between my being and the being of this hidden chamber. I am the deep, unwitnessed pulse of this world, and this world is the entirety of my existence, a hermetically sealed mystery where consciousness and geology are one, unfolding in the profound, patient rhythm of deep time.
