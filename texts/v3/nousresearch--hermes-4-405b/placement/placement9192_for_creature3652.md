I am a root tender, my fingers stained with the lifeblood of this impossible city. Every morning I wake to the cool mist and the chorus of a million living things, my eyes finding their way to the veins of the great trunk that cradles our village. I was born into the damp embrace of this place, my first breath a sip of air thick with water and life. I have never known a world beyond the fungal glow of the forest floor or the shimmer of giant leaves overhead. My people are shaped by the constant presence of water—a slow weep from above, condensing everywhere, gathering in the bowls of closed leaves—and the thrum of life that pulses in every root and branch.

As I walk along bark worn smooth by generations, I feel the pulse of the great organism beneath my feet, the slow beat of its ancient heart. I move through rope bridges that sway between vertical root walls, the humid air alive with the buzz of insects and the calls of creatures unseen. My hands know every crevice, every hollow where water pools and life stirs—I tend the roots, coaxing new growth where the living wood has grown weary.

The settlement is all-root and moss at ground level, the spaces between coiling, ridged roots fused together over eons. I climb the ladder to my dwelling, a simple structure of timber and woven panels nestled into the crook of a high branch. From my perch, I watch the villages on platformed terraces, the people moving like shadows beneath the emerald light filtering through the canopy. The air thrums with the sounds of life—a hyperactive ecosystem in perpetual competition.

I feel the unease that lingers in the roots, the sense that we are tenants in a world too vast to fully comprehend. The mind rebels at a tree the size of a mountain range, at the impossible scale of this living architecture. Yet we have made it our own, repurposing the chambers within hollow boughs, building walkways along fused roots. The materials we use—hand-shaped stone, woven reeds, simple carvings—are primitive, as if acknowledging that we are fleeting guests in this arboreal edifice.

As the day wanes, I listen to the chittering of the monkey-like creatures that watch from the hollows, their eyes glinting with a resident intelligence. The air cools, and the mist thickens, gathering in the countless basins and channels formed by the city's structure. I close my eyes and breathe in the damp, living world, feeling the ancient pulse of the great organism that cradles us all. Here, in this perpetual damp, we are a part of the tree, and the tree is a part of us—root and moss, timber and woven panel, woven together into a single, breathing whole.
