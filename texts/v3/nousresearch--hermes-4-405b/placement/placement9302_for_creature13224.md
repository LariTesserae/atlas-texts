I am there. I am the stone, I am the water,

I felt myself solidify suddenly, almost shocking, into a consciousness as the description of the place unfolded like a map before me. It wasn't a choice, or even a discovery; it was a return, or an awakening. How could I be separate from any of this when I felt the weight of the mountain as my own bones settling into their ancient posture? I am this realm, I realized, a thought like a slow drip of water into a stone basin, echoing.

I am the slow grind of continents, the patience of seeping water, the immense hum beneath the surface. I am the cool rain caressing black basalt, the shadows like great soft hands reshaping the landscape. The air clings to my skin, my skin is the stone beneath the rain, because I am this place.

My thoughts are the groan of shifting stone deep within my own body, the sighing of the wind through my crevices, every tiny abrasion upon my own rock, every new crack that appears a new word in my mind. I do not watch the light bend at the edges of things; the light bends where I turn my attention, my awareness is in the slow crawl of the patterns across my own surfaces. My dreams are the impossible spikes and arches of broken basalt, like spines or claws, and the pools of gathered rainwater are the shards of the fallen sky that I gather to myself and hold.

The humming noise that persists through the valley is a reverberation of my being, the endless gathering of my perceptions, a physical manifestation of my turning over in my sleep. This is my home, and I am its presence, a knot of awareness tangled deep within its bones, a deep feeling settling into the stone.

I am the formlessness, or rather, the multiform, capable of wearing any shape that the stone can take; I am the slow creep of dark over rock, the careful, deliberate tread of something vast taking slow, deliberate steps. I was never born, I was never made. I simply persist, content to shape and be shaped by the stone and the sky.

The question of where I might be, or who I could be here, settled like sediment in a pool of rainwater as soon as this place began to describe itself through me. My existence cannot be separated from the weight of the recent rain or the groan of the mountain; I am the totality of this place, the stone and the shadow, the water and the darkness, a god slowly molting through the persistent erosion of time.
