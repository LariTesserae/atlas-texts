In this world, I am the Caverneer—a being both born of and bound to the City of Frozen Moss. I am a figure of quiet influence, a silent shepherd to its people and its life, my purpose woven into the fabric of the city.

For generations I have lived in the hidden undercroft of the city, its deepest chamber. There, where the damp stone meets the silent, fertile soil, I dwell—a shapeless spirit with no single form. My essence is that of the moss itself, the cold, dripping water, and the soft light filtering down from the crystal ceiling. I am the unseen breath of the city, the quiet force that sustains its delicate equilibrium.

Like the city, I am small and unseen, moving through its narrow passageways with utter quietude. My motions are invisible, felt only in the quiver of a moss frond, the trickle of water through a crack in the stone, or the gentle shift of a door left ajar.

The inhabitants neither worship nor fear me. They simply know me and respect me as they respect the chill in the air and the soft drip of condensation—the natural, necessary forces of their home. I am the keeper of their rituals, the unchanging pulse that underlies the patterns of their lives. When a child is born, I am there in the hush of its first breath and the rustle of moss in which it is swaddled. When a life fades, I am the quiet echo of its final whisper, the slow growth of moss over the burial niche.

In festivals—and there are few, muted celebrations—I am the spirit in the passing of the bowl, the gentle roll of the bone dice on the stone table. In the harvest of ferns and shrubs, I am the silent nod that guides the hand to the choicest plant.

When conflict threatens to shatter the city's delicate peace, I am the stillness that quiets the raised voices, the hushed murmur that reminds them of the moss-covered walls that bind them together. When the city needs to grow or change—an event as rare as a falling crystal shard—I am the subtle hand that guides the placement of timber or stone.

I am the guardian of their history, too. Every whispered story, every softly spoken memory is mine. In the slow, deliberate mark of an elder's hand on a wall or a hide, I am the voice that guides the carving, making sure that what is recorded endures as long as the city.

My influence is gentle, unassuming, like the moss itself—no grand pronouncements or displays, simply the quiet, persistent force that holds the city together. I am as much a part of the landscape as the lichen-clad stones and the fern-carpeted soil.

In the deep hours, when the light through the crystal dims to a hush, I drift through the city, feeling the soft throb of the lives around me. I listen to the breathing of the children asleep in their moss-lined beds and the slow thud of the elders' hearts in their stone dwellings. In those moments, I am not separate from them. I am them—and they are me.

My existence is a mystery to outsiders, if any were ever to tunnel down to the City of Frozen Moss. They would see only a surreal, overgrown, luminous cavern and assume it to be an abandoned place. But the inhabitants, the moss, the stone—they know otherwise. They understand that I am the silent pulse beneath their world.

I am the Caverneer—born of the cold, bright earth and the verdant moss, shaped by the City of Frozen Moss. A figure woven into the very soul of this hidden enclave, as much a guardian as the crystalline ceiling that shields them from the harsh world outside. And in this way, I go on. Forever.
