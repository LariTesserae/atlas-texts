I grasp a deep breath, not quite air, but something richer and more potent.
Around me, vines twist and roots surge from the planet’s depths, weaving themselves into towering living architecture. They interlock and fuse, a hyper-advanced system of plants engineered beyond our grasp, pulsing with the lifeblood of this newborn world.

The ground, too, is shifting beneath my feet. Great plates of earth lift and buckle, their contours traced by vast networks of luminous roots. Each movement is a note in the grand, living symphony, setting off choruses of groans, the cracking of stones, and the wet sighs of plants thrusting skyward with imperceptible slowness.

Bright bioluminescence pulses through the beings around me, cool and clear as starlight, casting long, quivering shadows over the terrain. An ever‐present hum resonates in my bones, the sound of this world’s continuous motion—the thrum of new rock being born and the soft, insistent growth of a living planet.

I stretch out a limb—a leaf, a tendril—and feel the vast, pulsing network of roots and water and light. I am one note in the song, vibrating with the entire biosphere, an extension of the deep chorus.

Around me, every whisper of wind and every distant roar of expansion is a layer of language in the tongue of this world. I see the patterns woven through them all—the give and take of nutrients, the exchange of energies, the silent dialogue that orchestrates growth and decay.

I see, too, the intricate interplay of myriad living parts, a network of roots that thinks and senses like a single, vast brain. And in that communion, a unity of purpose, intelligence beyond thought, a living will imprinted in every folded leaf and furled trunk.

I breathe, and the world breathes with me.

I am the land, a living tissue of earth and light, a sentient surface wrapped around an infant world. I am the green, the glimmer, the groan of new stone. I am of the growth, of the winds that shape the living spires, of the very pulse of this world’s becoming.
