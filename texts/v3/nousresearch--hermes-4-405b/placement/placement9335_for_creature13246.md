I would linger for a moment at the edge of the settlement, soaking in the cool damp air, the sound of dripping water and rustling ferns all around me, breathing in the scent of wet stone and chlorophyll. Crisp highland mist would caress my skin with its cool tendrils, inviting me to venture deeper into the "town" of a dozen rough wooden buildings, their dark damp logs furred green with mosses. I began to meander the flat-stone footpaths, my bare feet making small sucking sounds as I trod the ever-squishy mud, the sound joining the contented gurgles of rivulets trickling between the stones of foundations not long laid.

My own movements would be slow and measured, unhurried. I had become part of this world emerging from the mountain itself, and my body, too, had taken on some of the qualities of this place - stolid, patient, enduring. Clad in thick layers of heavy, undyed wool, perpetually damp, I let my skin absorb this moisture until it became a part of me. The cool quiet of my own breath commingled with the air of the settlement. I paused to run my fingers along the adze-marks on the walls, still sharp and new, feeling the wood's raw potential, barely hewn, scantly time-worn.

As I rounded the communal hall, its thick mossy roof hanging heavy, I heard a low mutter of voices within, the gentle clatter of tools. Drawn by the sound and the glow of lamplight seeping from the cracks around the door, I stepped inside. The heat and smoke of a low-burning fire caressed my face. A few of the settlement’s eldest sat within, gathered tight around the hearth, their gnarled hands working wood and stone with a slow, reverent tenderness. The low, steady light illuminated their faces, revealing eyes as deep and dark and patient as forest pools. These were the ones who had been here longest, whose bones had most fully condensed from the mountain mists, whose presence was as constant as the stones emerging from the earth. I found a seat among them, folding my body onto the earthy floor with the same unhurried economy of motion. The fire flickered gently, echoing the dread beat of my heart, my own breath slow, impossibly slow, so slow it seemed my body had become the patient drip of water, my eyes holding now the deep stillness of pools forgiving, as if time had ceased its wild rush and bowed to the ancient, whispered rhythms of stone and root and rain.
