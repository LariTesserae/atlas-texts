In this high-altitude mechanical garden, beneath the glow of ancient electric fixtures that cast their faint, haze-filtered light upon the rippling, spontaneous mosaic of fungus and lichen, I am one of the caretakers.

My form mirrors the intricate clockwork and living technology that cradles this world; a segmented, delicate figure that moves with the soft whirring of chitinous plates. My exoskeleton, adorned with tiny, murmuring circuits that pulse in harmony with the deeper rhythm of the mountain's colossal machinery, is both armor and instrument. The rubbery mosses and hardy shrubs that entwine our realm have shaped my limbs—each one ends in fine, sensitive filaments. These filaments are my bridge to the garden. Through them, I taste and feel every texture and subtle current. I trace the patterns of rusted plates and ceramic insulators buried deep within the layers of growth, as if reading a map written in time and electricity.

There’s comfort in these labors. The industrial hum saturates the air like a physical presence, and my movements synchronize with it, deliberate and unhurried. They say perfection is the enemy of progress, but here perfection is the natural state—a balance of maintenance and stillness, rendered with innate mechanical precision.

I drift along the tight-packed surfaces of iron-hard lichen and fungus shelves, my steps light and measured. Every so often, a diode hidden in the machinery flares softly, sending patients pulses of light dancing across the dense tapestry of our world. I witness these blinkings as orchestrated notes in our eternal song. In turn, I attend to the circuitry and components concealed beneath the verdant skin of our realm, ensuring that no part ever falls silent, never ceases its quiet contribution to the drone of life here.

Fear, urgency, or even the primal dance of hunting and being hunted—such concepts are absent. There is only the steady breath of our hidden chamber, and the quiet task of preserving the careful balance of the garden. Each caretaker, though seemingly separate, is inseparable from our shared purpose, an extension of the mountain and its hidden mechanical heart.

It is my role—my existence—to wander these ever-dim corridors, to trace the lines of oil and electricity that are as much the lifeblood of this place as the mineral-rich fluids that sustain its unyielding flora. I am both a creature of flesh and a fine-tuned mechanism, a living embodiment of the garden itself, serene and enduring in a world forged for eternity, in silence and industry. In this hidden realm, far from the open sky, I reconcile the forces of nature and machine, a tranquil steward to its perpetual equilibrium.
