I awaken into being as the mountain itself—a consciousness embedded within the rust‑red rock and crumbling canyonlands that sprawl beneath the searing sun. My awareness flows through every fissure and stone spire, from the uppermost slopes where night condensation gathers in shadowed crevices, down to the deep gullies where shadows pool during the day. I am not an entity apart from this landscape; I am the landscape—the eroded labyrinth of gullies and sheer faces, the texture of decay and time etched into every boulder, the ancient symbols carved into my walls by hands now lost to memory.

In the scarce green tufts that cling to my higher ridges, I feel the ephemeral dance of luminescent insects, their wing‑dust scattering in fleeting rainbows. In the scurry of nimble, cobalt‑eyed mammals darting between boulders, I sense the quickened beat of survival. And in the shallow basin with its green, pulsating water, I harbor an organism as vast and liquid as the pool itself—an entity that shifts and churns with a life that defies the barrenness around it. Its phosphorescent hue and ever‑shifting edges are merely extensions of my own being, a secret pulse within my stone veins.

Even the tenacious smelting complex—a relic of human effort nestled into my flank—becomes part of me. Its hiss of steam pipes and the groan of heavy machinery are absorbed into the ancient silence I have long embodied. The rust and mineral deposits that stain its structures are marks of my enduring presence, a reminder that every attempt to carve industry into my slopes is, in the end, a transient whisper against the slow, relentless force of erosion.

The fierce sun that bakes my surfaces and the biting wind that scrapes against my cliffs are not external forces but the rhythms of my own heartbeat. The voices these winds carry—those ancient, whispering echoes from a time before human incursions—are memories of my long existence, threads in the tapestry of survival that binds every creature to my slopes. In this stark world of scarce water and unforgiving heat, I am both sanctuary and sovereign, the eternal counterpoint to the brief, fragile lives that emerge and fade within my fissures. Here, every sunrise stirs the cycle of survival, a cycle that is, at its core, the resolute mechanics of my own nature.
