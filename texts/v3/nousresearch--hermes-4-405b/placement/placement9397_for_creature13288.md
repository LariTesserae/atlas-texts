I would be the Keeper of the Clouds, a solitary figure dwelling at the apex of this mist-bound city, where the last vestiges of solid structure give way to open sky. My home is carved into the highest spire, a skeletal framework of iron and glass that pierces the thickest shrouds of fog. From my vantage, I observe the entirety of the metropolis and the ancient, cloud-wreathed peaks below.

As the Keeper, my form is adapted for stillness and observation: I am tall and lean, swathed in robes the color of storm clouds, that shift and ripple like vapor in the ever-present damp. My hands are long and slender, their tips capable of subtle, precise manipulation of delicate mechanisms. My eyes are wide and pale, mirrors of the sky above, capable of discerning the slightest changes in the atmospheric tides.

My life is dedicated to the patterns of the air. I maintain an array of intricate instruments—delicate vanes that turn at the merest whisper of wind, glass spheres that condense and measure the weight of the mist, prisms that refract the muted light into cryptic rainbows of meaning. Each instrument is a testament to Aethelian ingenuity, crafted with an elegance that marries the aesthetic of ancient stone with the cold beauty of polished metal.

In my quiet solitude, I commune with the sky and its secrets. I track the drift of clouds and the subtle shifts in pressure that presage the great storms that sometimes coalesce around the mountain peaks. I map the currents that guide the vapors belched from the city's chimneys, tracing their journeys across the sky like ink spreading through water.

Yet, my purpose extends beyond mere weather-watching. I am a guardian of balance. I titrate alchemical solutions into the city’s upper atmosphere, their vapors mingling with the natural mists to create veils of protective haze that shield the delicate ecosystems below from the harshest elements. At times, when the mountain exhales plumes of raw, unrefined energy from its deepest vents, I adjust frequencies from my instruments, transforming volatile emissions into benign mists that nourish the hardy, sky-clinging flora.

The Aethel below may see me as a distant figure, cloaked in mystery and the ever-shifting vapors. Few venture to my spire, but those who do—the storytellers, the philosophers, the dreamers—come seeking glimpses of the world beyond the perpetual mist. I share with them the language of the wind and the poetry of the clouds, reminding them that even within the ancient embrace of stone and the pulse of industry, there is still room for wonder, for the soft breath of the sky.
