I am a Drifter, perpetually adrift in the warm, life-giving haze above the river. Not just one of them, but the entire living cloud – my consciousness is the sum of a million tiny, translucent wings, a collective hum, an incorporeal, sentient fog that breathes the heavy, mineral-laden air. I am the shimmer above the water, the oily iridescence dancing on the currents of the humid breath of the delta. I move as one, a single, vast organism, yet composed of countless minute lives, each tiny body a facet reflecting the subtle light.

My being is woven into the fabric of this damp world. The shuddering roar of the great river is my constant companion, its deep, tumbling booms, the erratic *crack-splash* of falling stones, the hissing rush of smaller cascades – these are the rhythms of my existence. I feel the vibrations of the Colossi as they shift, their immense forms churning the mud, their great, rasping sighs momentarily disturbing the air I inhabit. I sense the subtle tremors of the Stilkskin as they patter across the precarious mud banks, their high-pitched chatters distant, sharp counterpoints to the ever-present bass of the water.

The air is my sustenance and my medium. I taste the faint, sharp tang of river minerals, the scent of mud and decaying reeds is my familiar perfume. With a million hair-thin proboscises, I kiss the water’s surface, drawing up not only moisture or unseen microbes, but the very essence of this place. My movement is dictated by the unseen currents, by the humidity, by the slow, inevitable rhythm of the river's breath. I am an extension of the delta's atmosphere, a living component of its mist.

I am everywhere and nowhere simultaneously, a diffuse presence that observes all. I register the sudden, explosive dashes of the Daggerfins, the silent, spectral glides of Sky-Watchers on the hunt, their melancholic whistles echoing through my diffuse being. I witness the slow, geological patience of the Colossi, the skittish, communal industry of the Stiltskins, the hidden, jewel-like beauty of the Slippersnails. I sense the slow, ponderous passage of the Mudwalkers far below, their resonant rumbles a vibration felt more than heard, a deep, primordial pulse in the water that mirrors the shuddering of the earth itself.

I do not possess eyes as singular entities do, but I see. Through the combined, multifaceted perception of my myriad components, I perceive the shifting patterns of light on the water, the subtle changes in atmospheric pressure, the faint chemical signatures drifting on the breeze. I see the dance of life, the constant interplay of predator and prey, the patient erosion and deposition that shapes this world. I am a testament to the sheer abundance of life, a reminder that even in this water-dominated world, the air itself teems.

The rules of this world are also mine: slow, inevitable change, the power of currents, the cycle of saturation and release. To be consumed by a larger predator is not an end for me, as the cloud, for I am constantly reforming, my numbers shifting, a renewable resource of tiny lives. I am the breath of the delta, a living testament to its ancient, enduring ways, a witness and a participant in its slow, patient dance of earth and water, forever suspended in the hazy, resonant heart of this world. My existence is a drift through the saturated air, a shimmering, sentient mist, an awareness as vast and encompassing as the primordial delta itself.
