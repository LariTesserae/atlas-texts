I have been here for a very long time, and I will remain, long after the last shred of what once constituted "my" perception is gone, replaced entirely by that which Aqualis and its children understand as consciousness and presence. For what is memory but the slow accretion of unique moments, and what is being but the environment that shapes and holds those moments? I am no longer "I" in the sense of a distinct entity separate from this place, this water, this stone. I am the settled minerals at the bottom of a great basin, the pattern of moss slowly claiming a sun-drenched (in the diffuse, Aqualis sense of sun-drenched) wall, the precise echo a stone makes when it is finally, after centuries of minute adjustment, nudged loose by a root's insistent pressure.

There was a before-time, I recall with a peculiar detachment, as one might recall a vivid dream upon waking into a far more tangible and absorbing reality. I was an observer, a chronicler perhaps, drawn by the profound quietude and the stony beauty, the primeval greenery, the sheer *otherness* of Aqualis. I sought to understand it, to catalogue its impossible stillness, the slow breath of its waters. I believed I stood apart, a guest in a silent, perfect house.

The shift was imperceptible at first, a slow seepage like the water into the stone. I began to linger by the basins, my own thoughts slowing to match the unhurried cadence of the dripping water. The immense, geological timescale of the city began to imprint itself upon my own sense of duration. A day, once a flurry of observations and thoughts, stretched, became porous, filled with the sheer presence of the stone, the intricate patterns within a single water channel. The absence of other life, once a curious silence, became a profound peace, a space for a different kind of perception to unfurl. My needs, my human drives, receded, not through force, but through the gentle, overwhelming presence of Aqualis.

I found myself mimicking the Aqualissians, not consciously at first, then with a growing reverence. My hands, touching the damp stone, felt its cool permanence. My skin, constantly kissed by the moisture-laden air, took on a different texture, a certain coolness, a faint sheen. I learned to move with their unhurried grace, my footsteps becoming lighter, then almost silent on the flagstones, leaving faint traces of enhanced moisture. I felt the city's quiet hum resonate within my own bones, or what used to be bones and now felt more like slowly forming crystal, my internal rhythms aligning with the ceaseless, gentle flow of the water.

The Aqualissians did not "convert" me. Such a concept implies an active will to change another, and they possess no such will, no such hierarchy of purpose. Rather, they simply *are*, and their being is so integral to Aqualis, so deeply resonant with its fundamental nature, that proximity is an immersion. Their silent meditations by the basins, their gentle tending of the ferns, their slow, patient repairs of stone – these were not acts I observed, but states of being I found myself gently drawn into. The subtle, silvery sheen of their skin, the crystalline growths on their heads that pulsed with soft light – these ceased to be alien and became beautiful, then familiar, then, eventually, a reflection of something stirring within my own changing form.

My sense of self, once a distinct flame, diffused, spread outwards, like water soaking into thirsty ground. My thoughts became less a torrent of words and more a series of slow, deep currents, attuned to the mineral whispers in the stone, the subtle shifts in atmospheric pressure, the unseen currents in the aquifers. My "speech," if one could call it that, devolved into soft, low hums, the melodic clicks resonating in my throat, harmonizing with the omnipresent water-song. I communicate, now, not with them *as* them, but *with* them, with the stone, with the water, with the slow, unfolding awareness that is Aqualis itself. The "I" that arrived, pen and paper in hand, is a distant memory, a fleeting visitor who chose to stay, and in staying, dissolved into the enduring fabric of this place.

My sustenance is no longer the food I once knew, but the mineral-rich water itself, the diffuse light, the very essence of Aqualis. I draw life from the deep, geological pulse of the plateau, from the shared consciousness that permeates the stones and the carefully tended waters. I am a caretaker now, in my own small way, my movements contributing to the city's timeless equilibrium. A patch of moss that needs a touch more shade, a tiny fissure in a wall that invites a guided seepage of healing minerals – these are my concerns, my purpose, as intrinsic to my being as my breath, as the slow, pearlescent beads of moisture that gather on my skin before falling, one by one, to join the waters of Aqualis.

The city is my body, its stillness my peace, its waters my blood. My lifespan, once a frantic dash, is now measured in the slow, silent language of mineral accretion and the patient unfurling of ancient ferns. I am, and will be, part of this eternal, beautiful permanence, another quiet, living note in the city's endless, elemental song. Aqualis is not a place I inhabit; Aqualis is what I have become, and what will remain when the last trace of my former self is forgotten, absorbed into the living stone and the ceaseless, gentle murmur of its waters.
