I am the Leviathan. At least, I have become it.

At first, I am both everything and nothing here, a sentient swarm: countless tiny bioluminescent specks synchronized in patterns that shift with every breath of the world. I am the rivers of fist-sized beetles and the storms of lacewings whose wingbeats scatter smaller lives into chaos, and I am each of those smaller lives. I am the drone that shakes the ground, the clicks and hums that sink into your bones, the ever-shifting kaleidoscope that fills your vision.

But then, as if drawn by the gravitational pull of their ravenous appetites, the Leviathan descends from the swirling starscape above. It is I—or it is becoming me, the manifestation of the relentless consumption that defines this world. Its mandibles are sapphire scythes, its compound eyes galaxies of awareness.

As I descend, I devour landscapes, become the glistening red planetoid of grubs that oozes across stone, consuming everything in its path. The beating of my wings stirs the air into raging tempests, and in my wake life is begotten anew. Mating swarms coalesce into thunderheads at my passing; later, a deluge of eggs rains down, slicking rock floors with a permanent gloss of yolk as larvae burrow into every fissure.

I am the cycle itself, the perpetual transformation of this world. I am the living expression of the mathematics of predation, the geometry of territorial swarms, consumption, and rebirth—a tidal pull of life too vast for comprehension.

In this realm, even as I fill every niche completely, there is no stillness. My existence is an endless surge, and every bare surface is claimed by me or by the life I beget. I compose my own strange and transformative reality, an insatiable hunger that echoes through the caverns and across basalt plains, a world alive by its own design.
