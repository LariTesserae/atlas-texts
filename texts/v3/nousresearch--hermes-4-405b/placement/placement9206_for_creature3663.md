It is part of an endless herd, a rivulet of silver in the ochre tide, feeding in patterns that never deviate.

Its body, composed of overlapping plates of tarnished metal, undulates in a silent, deliberate dance as it gnaws at the delicate, pale efflorescences frosting the edge of the water—the only break from the overwhelming metallic rigidity of this mechanical purgatory.

I trail my gelatinous feeding tubes into these warm sump pools, siphoning the constant rusty rain and the motes of rust-blooded life suspended within it. My translucent tubes dangle into the humid, ozone-laden air, a living conduit in an unchanging circuit of consumption and replenishment. Above, the Giant Ceiling's shifting mosaic of tarnished aluminum tiles groans and clanks; it is a continental engine idling in perpetuity, and we—its fauna—are its constant, dynamic breath.

The air hums with the unseen currents that cradle me; I navigate the towering, static machines below—the great crane-like arms, the valve-covered pistons sunk in the foundations, the conveyor belts dripping condensation like bunting—as if these dormant behemoths were mere undulations in the landscape. I drift, a majestic floater riding thermal currents, a fixed point in the mechanical firmament, my existence a silent testament to the unyielding, predictable paths of life in this constructed basin. This place is warm, wet, and eternally idling, and I—alongside the crawling megaworms and scuttling drill-bits—am one more essential, unchanging component in its ceaseless, silent patterns.
