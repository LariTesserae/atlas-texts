In Cinderbog, I would be a scavenger - perhaps a lone wanderer or part of a small, tight-knit group. We would navigate the labyrinthine network of streets and alleyways, always on the lookout for useful scraps or items to trade. Rebar, copper wiring, intact machine parts - anything of value that we could pry loose from the crumbling facades or scavenge from abandoned lots.

Our home would be a makeshift shelter cobbled together from scrap metal and salvaged materials, tucked away in a seldom-used corner of the city. It would be damp and cramped, but relatively safe from the worst of the elements. Inside, we would have a few meager possessions - tools, spare parts, maybe a small stove to cook on (though where we would find fuel, I'm not sure).

Day-to-day life would be a constant struggle for survival in this harsh environment. We would need to be ever vigilant of the crumbling infrastructure, the polluted air and water, not to mention potential threats from other desperate inhabitants. But through it all, there would be a fierce determination, a stubborn refusal to succumb to the decay all around us.

In the perpetual gloom of Cinderbog, I imagine we would develop an intimate knowledge of the city's sights, sounds and smells. The dripping of condensation, the scuttling of rodents, the distant thrum of machinery - these would be the soundtrack of our lives. We would learn to navigate by the flickering neon signs and sodium-vapor lamps, to find our way through the maze of streets even in the thickest mist.

And while life in Cinderbog would be unforgiving, there is a certain raw beauty to be found there as well. The play of light on wet stone and metal, the way the mist swirls and eddies in the canyons between buildings, the resilience of the hardy creatures (human and otherwise) that call this place home. In that strange decaying world, I believe I could eke out an existence and perhaps even find a kind of purpose and meaning - a small defiant flame in the encroaching darkness.
