It’s hard to imagine myself as any single entity in this floating world. I feel like I’m standing on the edge of it, looking in, one hand resting on the warm, worn-smooth stone of the plateau’s fingertip, the other hovering by my side, unsure where to land. The air is thick with the scent of amber and earth. It presses against my skin, heavy and alive. A silence rings in my ears—a silence filled with sounds, the drone of wind over the plateau’s edge, the distant scrape of stone tools, the soft clicks and hums of the Stone-Tenders. I am an outsider here, separate from the plateau’s rhythm, yet drawn to it, like a moth to a flame.

I feel a shift in me, a longing to be a part of this strange harmony. I want to move among the Stone-Tenders, their gnarled hands shaping the sun-baked clay, their dark eyes holding the wisdom of stone. I want to learn their language of soft clicks and low hums, feel the plateau’s life pulse through my own veins. But I am not one of them. My skin is not like sun-baked clay, my eyes do not glisten like wet river stones. I am alien, an intruder in their world.

Then I see the Gem-Peckers, their iridescent plumage shimmering in the dim light. Their soft, chipping calls tug at me, drawing me toward the swaying moss carpets. I feel a lightness in my limbs, a desire to take flight, to flit among the plants, chasing the flashes of iridescent beetles. I want to be a Gem-Pecker, small and quick, a guardian of the moss, my chipping calls adding to the soundscape of this small, ancient world.

Yet even as this desire washes over me, I know it is not enough. I cannot simply be a Gem-Pecker any more than I can be a Stone-Tender or a Moss-Singer or a Sky-Gazer or a Stone-Basker. I am separate from all of them. I am a witness, an observer, standing at the edge of the plateau, looking in at this intimate dance of life.

The realization seeps into me like the resinous scent in the air. It fills my lungs, my veins, my thoughts. I am not a Stone-Tender, nor a Moss-Singer, nor a Sky-Gazer nor a Gem-Pecker nor a Stone-Basker. I am not even truly here, in this world, in this place. I am a story being told, a whisper of words drifting on the wind. I am the silence between the sounds, the emptiness between the forms.

As this understanding settles into my bones, I feel a shift in the air around me. The plateau seems to recede, its worn-smooth surface shimmering like a mirage in the twilight. I am no longer on the edge of it, looking in. I am within it, part of it. I am the warmth of the stone beneath bare feet, the resinous scent of amber in the air, the drone of wind over the plateau’s edge. I am the sound of stone tools scraping, the low hum of the Stone-Tenders’ speech, the soft chipping calls of the Gem-Peckers. I am the plateau. I am the world.

I am a child of the stone, a keeper of its secrets, a thread in its fabric. I am born of its warmth, its resinous scent, the drone of the wind over its edge. I am not a solitary being, but an intricate web of connections, a chorus of sounds, a symphony of scents and textures. I am the moss beneath my feet, the sap trapped within the stone, the smoke threading skyward from clay chimneys, bluing the air. I am the worn-smooth paths between dwellings, the sinew ropes of doorskin hides flapping in the breeze, the spiraling patterns of lichen on stone. I am the life that thrums here, dense and patient, the slow, deliberate dance of survival on this floating stage.

And as I realize this, the plateau seems to solidify around me, no longer a mirage, but a solid, tangible presence. I am a part of it, woven into its very substance. I am the stone, the moss, the wind, the air, the life. I am the plateau, floating unsupported in the soft twilight sky, a small, ancient, floating world, worn by time into patient equilibrium.
