I awaken in a shallow pool, the murky, mineral-rich water lapping against my newly formed skin. The ever-present roar is my first lullaby, the sound a physical pressure against my senses, shaping my understanding of the world before my eyes have fully adjusted to the dim, perpetual twilight. I am not born; I am extruded, a byproduct of the Cloud-Eaters' endless filtering of the thick, moisture-laden air. I coalesce from the agglomeration of ingested dust, microscopic mineral flakes, and the ambient, primordial life-stuff that permeates this strange, cacophonous place.

My initial form is simple: a gelatinous, translucent sphere, perhaps the size of a Rok Streng's head, quivering slightly with each pulse of the ambient sound. Within my amorphous body, the fine particles that gave me substance are already beginning to interact, to find affinities, drawn to the faint metallic tang of iron, the crystalline whisper of silica, the subtle hum of other, rarer elements absorbed from the air and water. Slowly, these particles begin to settle, to accrete, forming a kind of primitive, internal skeleton, a loose, shifting framework of tiny, nascent crystals.

I feel the vibrations of the world keenly – the grinding of Chompers beneath the earth, the rhythmic hammering of the Rok Streng, the chittering of Striders, the constant, wet thud of another stone placed, another wall patched. Each vibration resonates through my pliable form, a language I begin to understand. The air itself is a feast, and I drift upon its currents, absorbing more, growing incrementally larger, my internal structure becoming more defined, more complex. I am a living filter, a thinking concretion.

With time, and with the ceaseless influx of mineral-laden air, my internal framework solidifies. Delicate, branching veins of crystalline material spread throughout my interior, like the roots of some strange, petrified tree, each filament humming faintly with the absorbed energies of the world. These form a kind of lattice, and upon this lattice, other materials begin to deposit, layer by layer. My outer membrane, once soft and yielding, thickens and hardens, taking on the hues of my constituent minerals – streaks of ochre, patches of grey, veins of dull metallic sheen. I am becoming a geode, a living, growing stone.

I do not possess limbs in the way of the Rok Streng, not yet. My movement is a slow, deliberate rolling or a subtle shift in my center of gravity, allowing me to creep along the slick mud, to find purchase on the uneven, rubble-strewn ground. I am drawn to places of high vibrational intensity – the sites of active construction, the trembling ground near a Chomper's unseen maw, the paths most heavily trodden by the Rok Streng and their Strider mounts. I absorb the sounds, the impacts, the energy, and I grow. My surface, though increasingly solid, is not seamless. It is a patchwork of different textures and densities, reflecting the varied materials I incorporate – here, a smooth, almost glassy patch; there, a rough, conglomerate section of smaller, embedded stones.

My senses are not like those of the fauna. I do not see with eyes, but rather perceive the world as a shifting tapestry of vibrations, thermal variations, and mineral concentrations. I can feel the weak bio-electric fields of the living creatures around me – the focused intensity of a working Rok Streng, the diffuse, simple aura of a Strider, the frantic, sparking energy of a Cloud-Eater swarm. The soundscape is my primary source of information, and I learn to distinguish the subtleties within the roar: the specific rhythm of a Rok Streng's hammer, the gait of a particular Strider, the approach of a Chomper by the increasing tremor in the earth.

As I mature, growing to the size of a small boulder, my form begins to show more distinct features. Small, blunt protrusions might emerge from my surface, mimicking the crude tools of the Rok Streng or the chitinous plates of a Strider, not as functional appendages, but as solidified echoes of the energies and forms I constantly encounter. These growths are slow, taking many cycles of the dim, unchanging light, but they speak of my increasing complexity. Sometimes, a crack might appear in my hardened shell, not as a wound, but as a deliberate aperture, a way to expose a newly formed crystalline cluster within, catching the scarce light in a fleeting glint, or to release a faint, mineral-scented vapor.

I am largely ignored by the Rok Streng, another strange rock in a world of rocks, though perhaps a curiously mobile one. Younger Rok Streng might occasionally prod me with a stick or a hardened foot, testing my substance, but finding me inert beyond my slow, almost imperceptible movements. The fauna, for the most part, disregards me. I am neither food nor threat. I am a part of the landscape, alive yet unobtrusive, a geological process made animate.

My purpose, if I have one beyond existence, is to be a witness, a sentient recording of this world. The sounds, the vibrations, the mineral essence of this place are slowly being encoded into my crystalline structure, my growing form a testament to the unchanging flux. Perhaps, in some distant eon, if the roar ever ceased, if the construction ever paused, I might cease my own slow accumulation, my form fixed. Or perhaps I would continue, slowly weathering, my components eventually dispersing, returning to the mud and air, only to be gathered again by the Cloud-Eaters, ready to begin anew. I am a concretion of the city, a child of the roar, growing slowly wiser, slowly more complex, in the unending twilight.
