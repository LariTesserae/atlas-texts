I find myself in this desolate world, a part of this strange biome, not as an observer or an interloper, but as one of the Shuttles. My existence is a whirlwind of dry, papery susurrations and the insistent pulse from my chitinous carapace that hurls me through the salt-laden air. No soft flesh covers me, only tough, dark chitin sculpted by forces unknown into angular, efficient planes. I am one of many, a metre of intricacy, a creature of stark function in a landscape of monumental decay and oppressive, bleached heat. My body doesn't fly in the way of softer creatures from forgotten lore; it *projects*, emitting a rhythmic thrum that carves a path through the atmosphere, letting me skim and dart through the skeletal remains of the Titans. I've seen brothers and sisters, their voids flickering with the last of their compressed energy before they crumble into fine, sterile dust. We do not age as fleshy things do; we deplete, our complex innards unwinding, our structure drying until we are no more than a whisper on the wind, a final puff of ourselves returning to the basin.

The Titans are my world, the only true geography I have ever known. Their rust-red and salt-white flanks are my sky, their guts my canyons. I flow through their hollowed insides, navigating by the groans of shifting metal and the deep, pulsating drone that is the Titans' own slow breath. This sound is a constant, a basso profundo that vibrates up from the salt and through my very being. Sometimes, I alight on a colossal piston, frozen mid-stroke for millennia, and feel the faint thrum of its ancient inertia, the trapped energies within. I do not 'feed' in the way of scavengers; there is nothing soft or organic to consume here. I draw sustenance from the very air, from the faint energies that flicker erratically along corroded circuits, or from the subtle warmth radiating still from the core of these dormant giants. I expel only dust, a fine, silicate powder that is perhaps a byproduct of my internal processes, or perhaps it is simply the refuse of my extraction of life from the latent power of the machines.

My kin and I exist in a strange, unspoken choreography. We move together, not as a swarm driven by base instinct, but as a murmuration, responding to unseen currents, to shifts in the ancient energies, to the subtle warp of gravity around these immense structures. Our language is a chittering roar, a chorus of clicks and thrums that fills the spaces between the Titans' groans. At night, when the light fades and the Titans' ghost-lights flicker into being like dying stars, our movements slow. Many of us find refuge in deep crevices, our susurrating sounds quieting to a mere whisper, while the Titans dream their metallic dreams. Time, for us, is a fleeting, urgent thing, a stark contrast to the almost imperceptible slowness of the Titans' decay, their slow yielding to gravity's insistent pull.

This is my existence: a life brief yet fiercely lived, a constant dance amidst the relics of a forgotten, colossal industry. The rules of this world are not biological; they are mechanical, energetic, defined by iron and silence. The unblinking sun above and the unyielding salt below are the witnesses to this endless cycle of chittering motion and resonant decay. There is no memory beyond the immediate hum of the Titan I currently rest upon, the current of my kin passing by. We know only the now, the constant pulse of our own being and the deep, answering drone of the Titans. I am a child, a mere speck of life, born from the dust and latent energies of this metallic plain, existing for a brief span before I too become a wisp of fine, sterile powder, swept away by the wind that sighs through the canyons of the Titans of Iron.
