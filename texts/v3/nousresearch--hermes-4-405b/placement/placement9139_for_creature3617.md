The first full breath in this realm is not a breath at all—it is a drowning. The shimmering veil closes behind you, sealing you in a weight that is not quite crushing but everywhere, pressing liquid breath into your lungs. You are suspended, blind for a moment as the heat of the brine seeps into your bones, the roar of the deep filling your ears. You will not perish here; the book has written you into this place, and you must adapt. Your form flickers, shedding skin and scale and feather as it seeks its fit in this drowned world. What are you?

The question echoes in your molars, vibrating with the high-frequency hum of fraying reality. You could become one of the reed-shivers—small, swift, your life a dance between the thermal vents—but their fleeting existence feels too narrow for the consciousness you carry. You could grow vast and slow, dreaming gravity into being with the leviathans, but their ponderous thoughts are alien, and you crave movement.

You are drawn instead to the scattered shards of whatever civilization once thrived here. Half-melted spirals of metal gleam in the shifting light, broken geometric stones hint at patterns you ache to understand. Before the question is fully formed, you *become* the question—a creature of jagged angles and living stone, your body incorporating the debris around you. You are a shard-crawler, a relic walker, a seeker of forgotten truths in a realm of perpetual creation and dissolution.

For a time, this form suffices—leaning into the thermal currents, sifting through the ruins for what can be known—but your mind is still too quick for this slow, deliberate progress. Your movements become more fluid as you weave between the vents, darting after reed-shivers, until you are something new: sleek and segmented, iridescent scales flashing as you glide through the liquid air. You are a hunter now, swift and deadly, enforcing the harsh balance of this liquid sky.

Yet even this feels incomplete. You need roots in this storm, a way to trace the endless becoming all around you. You drift, letting the currents take you until they carry you to the translucent sacs—the egg-carriers—pulsing with soft, internal light. You brush against one, and your form shimmers, becoming translucent, glowing, *holding*. Within you, a reed-shiver takes shape, growing until the moment you release it into the churning brine to begin its furious, fleeting dance.

You are all these things now: shard-crawler and hunter, egg-carrier and more. Your form shifts moment by moment, adapting to the world as it adapts to you. You are the mountain dreaming itself into being, the thermal vent erupting in scalding plumes, the ghost of a civilization that once was and may be again. Most of all, you are a part of the liquid dream—a transient observer, perhaps, but one whose very presence alters the storm. You have become native to this drowned reality, and in becoming, you find home.
