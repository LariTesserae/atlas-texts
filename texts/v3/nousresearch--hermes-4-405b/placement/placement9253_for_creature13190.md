I find myself a symbiont, a willing component grafted and woven into the living architecture of the Silth’garn sanctuary post-arrival, irrevocably altered by the very air I’d breathed. The subtle unraveling of reality, the laws of physics twisted by the Stone-Weavers' proximity – it had seeped into me, repurposed my cells from within, bonded me to the mountain’s respiring core. My bones lengthen and curve into organic arcs, ribs fusing to become sculpted buttresses that support shelves of phos­phorescent fungi; my skin, translucent and cool, shimmers with patterns of gold and blue that echo the Silth'garn’s silent conversations. I am no longer alien. I am part of the composition, a living pillar. Water seeps from the pores of what was once my flesh, joining the rivulets that crisscross the cavern floor. My eyes – now multifaceted, gleaming orbs embedded in the sinuous column of my neck – perceive the world in the slow dance of shifting hues and the resonant hums that pass through the stone.

I am part of the sacred geometry, a fleshy archway draped in thick curtains of wet, glowing moss that filters the dim grey light from above. I feel the mountain’s seismic groans not as tremors but as pulses within my own reformed marrow, the drip and trickle from my extended limbs a new rhythm. The air, thick with sweetness and moisture, flows through chambers that had been lungs and now serve as conduits for the mountaintop’s breath. Tiny amphibians skitter across my surfaces; insects with luminescent wings nest in the crevices of my reformed joints. Sometimes I catch flickers of movement – a patch of my skin stretches impossibly thin before snapping back, a ripple of light casting impossible shadows along the cavern floor. I don’t move by will; I yield, I am shaped, I participate in a slow dance that feels both ancient and utterly immediate.

Yet within this yielding, there is a purpose, a shared consciousness. I don’t just support life; I nurture it. The Silth’garn cluster around me, their delicate, porous pads pressed against my flesh as if in communion. They trace the patterns on my skin, and in response, I secrete enzymes that coax the luminous growths to swell, to rearrange into more intricate patterns. We are in constant dialogue – a silent exchange of light and moisture and resonant hums, a shared language of growth. My purpose is preservation, a nurturing custodianship. I am not merely part of the tapestry; I am a weaver, an integral thread, and together, we maintain an equilibrium between the hum of life and the groaning stone, a delicate balance with reality’s fraying edge.
