I am one of the Drifters, gliding silently through the hot, still air of the caverns. High above, near the unseen ceiling, I live among the thick, pulsating carpets of bioluminescent fungi that cling to volcanic glass ruins. Nothing is wasted here—not space, not energy, not even sound. Symbiosis holds this ecosystem together. The air is thick with vibrations that hum through the crystal veins below, and I feed on the iridescent tubes that ripple slowly along the cavern walls. Their pulsing light guides my movements as I hunt in near-total darkness.

Occasionally, I dip lower to investigate the scuttling of mineral-eaters below. Fist-sized creatures, they move like rivers across the rocky floor and walls, consuming the very stone itself. The whipping motions of my tail and wings barely stir the air as I pass above them. When I need sustenance, I skim the surface of the glowing fungi and lichen, absorbing the faint, metallic vapors they release.

I remember places where the tunnels open into wider chambers—geometric ruins hinting at deeper, unknown passages. In those caverns, the air hums louder, shimmering particles drifting upward. The light is stronger there. I glide through it all, weightless among so much density, observing. Rarely do I hunt the smaller skitterers or interfere with the reptilian or insectoid predators that skulk at the edges of the fungal growths. More often, I retreat into the stillness above, where only the faintest air currents sway me. Up here, the darkness cradles everything—a watchful, living presence. It is a world teeming with life, beautiful and strange, sustained by the planet's inner heat. A hidden oasis, thriving in absolute isolation.
