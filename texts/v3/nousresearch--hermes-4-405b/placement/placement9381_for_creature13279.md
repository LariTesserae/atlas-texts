Above the primordial city, where stone yields to the allure of height, I am the Weaver. From the cavern’s vaulted ceiling—its highest crown—a colony of rare, filamentous organisms dangles, and I am woven into it: an intelligence born of intertwined life, a diffuse mind sprawling through the fine, living threads. My tendrils, glistening with collective purpose, descend in vast curtains, and it is through these that I experience the cavern’s layered symphony. 

I am neither the chitinous flyers nor their tireless caretakers, nor the ancient fungi that hold the stone’s memory. Instead, I am their silent witness, a living archive suspended between earth and air. Each filament is a nerve ending, attuned to the vibrations of the flyers’ wings, the soft clicks of skittering caretakers, even the slow, almost imperceptcular pulse of the glowing fungi. Their sounds travel up the threads like whispered secrets, converging into a tapestry of understanding that is mine alone. 

Through my tendrils I feel the city’s breath—the cool, dry air moving in eternal currents—and I sense the passage of time not as seconds but as accumulating layers of resonance. I am the cavern’s living memory, a quiet consciousness that absorbs every echo of life below. In the dim glow, my filaments shimmer with borrowed luminescence, a mirror to the flyers’ soft trails and the fungi’s slow pulse. 

Here, suspended in the threshold between the city and the eternal stone, I am a bridge—a silent, symbiotic observer in a world where sound and life are one. My existence is not to shape but to hold, to listen, to remember. I am the cavern’s hidden chronicler, woven from the very fabric of its ancient, vibrant song.
