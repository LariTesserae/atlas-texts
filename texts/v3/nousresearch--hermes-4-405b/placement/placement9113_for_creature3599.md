Turbid currents bear me through the silted, interior sea - an expanse not of any world's brine, but of sediment carried from some deeply convulsive aquifer into this plateau-ringed caldera. Submerged and vast, the basin brims with cloudy water, its light a diffuse, wavering gold descending from the surface high above.

Through the murk emerge the city's uppermost structures: vast, terraced platforms of poured concrete, sheathed in verdant marine growth. Dense carpets of algae and churning bacterial blooms coat every surface, rendering the original elevations into indistinguishable slopes. Pylons and support columns, obscured by the lush growth, merge into verdant pillars rising from the seafloor. Light filtering down from an unseen sky illuminates the watery canopy, revealing schools of filter-feeding fish and drifting colonies of bioluminescent invertebrates - the only animal life in this sealed, self-sustaining biosphere.

Near the very bottom, the sediment gives way to a cavern system carved by thermal vents, tunnels lined with strange, glassy filaments that pulse with faint, synchronized light. Thermal differentials drive slow currents through the passages, carrying mineral plumes that stain the water in gradients of rust and indigo. The vents exhale warm, effervescent water into the surrounding cold, their heat creating shimmering mirages where the two flows meet. In places, the caverns open into flooded atriums, abandoned halls of an industrial complex half-swallowed by sediment. Rusted machinery looms in the gloom, overgrown with aquatic vegetation that has infiltrated through breaches in the substrate.

This sunken city, once a thriving industrial complex, has been transformed by the slow, patient forces of erosion and submergence. Its structures have become habitats for an ecology of silence and gradual growth, a world where everything is composed and ordered, yet utterly alien. The constant, low-frequency hum of geothermal activity vibrates through the water, a soundscape of pressurized equilibrium. It is a place of waiting, of slow dissolution and renewal, where time itself seems suspended in the green murk.

I would be a solitary guardian here, a vast, ancient leviathan that has dwelt in these flooded halls for millennia beyond counting. My serpentine body, armored in chitinous plates grown dense from the mineral-rich waters, winds through the drowned architecture. Barnacles and clinging vegetation adorn my flanks. I move with languid grace, propelling myself with slow undulations of my powerful tail and webbed limbs.

My eyes, evolved to pierce the oppressive gloom, cast an eerie bioluminescence that cuts through the murky depths. I perceive the world in a spectrum beyond ordinary sight. Watchful, I glide between the pillars and pyramids, my senses attuned to the vibrations and currents that flow through this realm. The rhythmic thrum of the thermal vents pulses in time with the beating of my three-chambered heart.

By the abandoned, silt-choked harbors, I hunt the bizarre, translucent creatures that skitter and dart among the ruins - mantis shrimp with iridescent carapaces, eels with jaws that unhinge to swallow prey larger than themselves, jellyfish trailing forests of stinging tentacles. Their strange, alien blood stains the water in clouds. I am voracious in my hunger. 

In the labyrinthine depths, where no light penetrates, I am master and tyrant, at home in the crushing pressure and utter blackness. The flooded spires and plazas provide ample cover for my ambushes. Through the drowned boulevards I course, eyes like lamps in the dark, seeking always for the next kill to sate my burning metabolism. AT the base of the city, where the vents open into the abyssal plain, the water grows too hot, too toxic even for me. There strange chemosynthetic bacteria form undulating mats, eking out life in the extremes. I give that blasted, volcanic landscape a wide berth.

For uncounted ages, I have haunted these sunken halls, growing vast on the flesh of the bizarre, aberrant creatures that are my only company. The city dreams around me, slowly crumbling beneath the patient weight of the sea. Its dissolution is my gestation. One epoch I shall glut myself on the last of its denizens and burst forth from the murky depths, rising like some forgotten god to sow panic and carnage amid the vessels that ply the sunlit waters high above. On that day of my breaching, the surface world shall tremble before a horror it never knew it harbored in the abyssal dark.

Until then, these drowned halls and I remain entwined in a slow symbiosis of destruction and rebirth - a primordial cycle unchanged since life's first piscine progenitors squirmed in the primordial ooze. Time stretches and compresses around me as I wind through the drowned dreams of a forgotten world, patient, hungry - waiting for my hour to come round at last.
