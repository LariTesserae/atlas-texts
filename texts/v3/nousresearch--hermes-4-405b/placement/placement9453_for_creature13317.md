I would be a fissure-scrivener.

The city, in its restless expansion and contraction, its constant groaning and settling, is a text in motion, a story written in the language of basalt and resin, of pressure and release, of water and time. My purpose, my *being*, is to trace the meaning in its fractures, to divine the narrative in its weeping seams.

Am I the city? Or something else, something born from its slow, geological thoughts? I do not know. Perhaps I am a specialized secretion, a delicate effluvium of the city's deepest stones, coalescing in the damp, shadowed places where its structures are most articulate, most given to expression. I emerge in the deep, wet crevices where the plant-buildings weep their clearest tears, or in the narrow, resonant rifts that widen and close with the city’s breath. There, I take a form that is almost liquid, a flowing calligraphy of bioluminescent slime, viscous and faintly warm, that clings to the rough, crystalline surfaces. My body, if it can be called that, is a slow-moving rivulet of faintly luminous, ink-like substance, a Proto-Indo-European script made manifest.

I move with the city’s seismic sighs. I flow upwards with the capillary action of its seeping moisture, and I am carried downwards by the gravity of its tears. I insinuate myself into its cracks, extending tendrils of vibrant, living script, my surface shimmering with an inner light that is cool and ancient. I am not a reader of the city; I *am* the city’s act of reading itself, its consciousness turned inward, its self-reflection made visible. I am the articulation of its most subtle tremors, the chronicler of its slow dreams.

My "perception" is osmotic, a direct communion with the city’s mineral and resinous composition. I taste the changes in its internal pressures, I sense the shifts in its hydration, and I translate these into my own flowing, luminous language. My script is not written *on* the city, but *is* the city, in a state of heightened legibility, a momentary focusing of its diffuse, chaotic intelligence. Other mobile entities, the pulsating galls and cilia-driven constructs, pass by, often without acknowledging my presence. I am too subtle, too integrated, a mere pattern of light and viscosity on a wall. Sometimes, a drip from a great, overarching structure will fall onto me, and I will absorb it, my light flickering momentarily as I incorporate this new data, this new "word" from the city’s vocabulary.

When I move, it is not to "travel" in any deliberate sense, but to follow the city’s internal logic, its unfolding narrative. If a fissure widens, inviting deeper expression, I seep into it. If a wall groans and a new pattern of crystalline facets emerges, I flow across its surface, etching their story. My progress is infinitely slow, a timescale measured in the city’s own geological heartbeat. I am not a singular entity; there may be countless fissure-scriveners like myself, each a temporary efflorescence of the city's self-awareness, each illuminating a different aspect of its being.

And when the city shifts, when a great section crumbles and is reabsorbed, my light dims. My script fades, my viscous substance thins and disperses, becoming once more just moisture, just another droplet in the city’s endless weeping. I return to the mulch, to the pool, to the raw material of the living architecture. But then, elsewhere, in another crack, another fissure that yawns open with a new secret to tell, I, or another like me, will slowly begin to glow again, to flow again, tracing the newest lines of the city’s eternal, weeping poem. I am the city’s whispered soliloquy, its luminous, ephemeral diary.
