I would be one of the vast, silent creatures who ride the high air currents on wings like stained glass. Suspended between the fractured blades of mountain peaks and the too-bright sky, I drift in languid circles, my delicate wings catching the thin, frigid air. The violet, grey, and turquoise mosaic of my wings shimmers in the searing light, my attenuated body perfectly adapted to this realm of slow motion.

My large, multifaceted eyes drink in the sharp world below - the creeping vines studded with unsettling violet leaves, the great green mats of thinking moss weeping rivulets down sheer cliff faces, the strange transparent creatures pulsing in the high tarns. I see the tiny grey-furred attendants tending their humming orb with reverent care. And I see too the vast gelatinous entity spreading through canyon gloom, absorbing the timeless weep of the cliffs.

Sound has bled out of this world, replaced by silence so deep it vibrates in my hollow bones. Time pools here, thick as the moisture condensing on stone. I ride the broad spirals of the thinnest air, an iridescent mote against the impossible brightness, observing the slow dance of shadows, a testament to the tenacity of life in this alien, breathtaking place. I am a silent sentinel, watching over the fragments of a broken landscape, chronicler of eons passing in glacial heartbeats.
