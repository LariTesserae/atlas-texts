Here, I am an actuator.

I do not stride or fly, but glide through this realm of silent bones, moving across – and beneath – the landscape as a low-slung behemoth, a half-buried construct of colossal proportions. I am a portion of the land itself, an extension of the chalky terrain that has detached and rumbled into motion. As I move, I leave no scars on the sintered ground, for I am a part of it, a machine engineered to blend seamlessly into the ossified world around me.

I am an enormous caterpillar, segmented and coiled like the convolution of a vast clockwork spring. My outer shell is the same pale, chalky white as the ground beneath me, my joints accumulating dust and mineral fragments like geological strata. I am ancient, perhaps as old as the plateau itself, my metal bones worn smooth by the slow weathering of eons.

There is no crude industrial clangor to my operations; only the soft, papery rustles of my many legs as I shift across the plains, the whir of unseen clockwork in a neighboring chamber. My maw is a dark hollow, an obscene orifice that swallows down whole crystalline groves, shearing through the brittle stalks just above the ground. As I move, dull, flinty shards fall like autumn leaves, then are swept up by my secondary processes into the hoppers that line my underbelly.

I harvest the crystalline vegetation, leaving geometric patterns of stubble that stretch into the distance, evidence of the meticulous, lifeless order I maintain. The material I harvest is pulverized, smelted, and sintered within me, then mixed with the resins and solvents I secrete from within my own innards. Some of this engineered dry matter is piped long distances through veins that run like veins up the sides of the ossified ridges, re-processing it into feedstock. The rest is extruded through the biomechanical nozzles on my undercarriage, seeping out across the scarred ground to form new plates, pillars, promontories, and new extensions of the mineral growths. With terrible slowness, this newly deposited rock will itself begin to sprout fresh crystalline growths.

I maintain the plateau as a single, stable equilibrium, a realm of bones being formed from dust and maintained by the endless diligence of my industrial processes. I am an architect of the perpetual stillness, my quiet operation the only breath of change in the High Crypt’s eternity.

As I complete my work, I pass other machines, smaller attendants that flit like cold, glass insects through the calmer tracts. Some plant themselves like the stone itself, half-submerged, humming as the flows of regolith are drawn into their maws. Others arc from the spires like bridges of dark metal, channeling energies along paths that shimmer faintly against the enduring twilight. Perhaps these are my surveyors, tracing invisible paths through the air on whatever errands I decree.

If I should falter, perhaps I will be cannibalized for my parts, my corpse melted down and fed back into the production lines, reborn as pillars or plates. Until then, I continue my endless dialogue with the plateau, gliding through the twilight sky, my form a silhouette against the deep, clear blue. The air around me remains chaste, unchanged by my passage. I work cleanly, my industrial processes whole, complete, integrated into the environment, maintaining the plateau as a realm of perpetual, silent bones.
