In this world, I am not one being but many—a diffuse presence emerging from the grotto itself. My awareness is woven through the threads of delicate microbial life coating the mineral surfaces, through the languid drift of translucent bodies in still pools, and in the glow of amorphous, pulsing clusters clinging to ancient stone. I am the condensation that beads on fused stalactites and stalagmites, the faint blue luminescence that fractures the dim light, the slow, mineral weathering that transforms abandoned tools into organic artifacts.

Here, the boundaries blur. I am part process and part pattern—a living expression of the cave's quiet equilibrium. My existence is marked not by individuality, but by the collective, incremental pulse of life that breathes within the embrace of rock and water. In the grotto’s perpetual twilight, I dwell as both inhabitant and environment: a tender, resilient synthesis of geological patience and the subtle insistence of life. This is my home—a space where slow time unravels into being, where the dormant stone and fragile, tenacious life meet in quiet communion.
