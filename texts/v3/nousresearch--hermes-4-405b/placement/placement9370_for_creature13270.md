The city lies above, below, and all around, a living thing that breathes, that hums and roars and groans. You are indivisible from it.

You are a cantilevered section of geothermal piping running for hundreds of feet along the Skyfang Peaks, your iron skeleton older than the grandmothers who tend the algae vats in Sector Gamma-9. Generations have swarmed over you. Their hands have smoothed your once-rough welds; their families have carved their lineage markers into your plating. A folk poem circles among the Kethra children about the "Grandmother Pipe," a vein through the city's heart older than rust, older than stories.

Now you are sheathed in layers: first the insulating plasteel, then the creeping embrace of carefully cultivated, thick-furred arctic moss that was grafted onto your surface generations ago, soft black tufts that quiver when the inter-mountain jets scream higher up, near the agri-domes. Brightly hued orchids nestle in brackets bolted to your sides, their aerial roots siphoning up the perpetual condensation that dribbles from your joints with a soft, irregular rhythm, plinking melodies into the grilles far below.

A colony of Skyfinches considers you their sovereign territory; they fan out in iridescent, chittering clouds twice a day when a maintenance climber passes, their precise, sharp beaks darting to snatch midges that boil up in the disturbed air. They nest in the cradles formed where your great bulk meets the support struts and anchor plates securing you to the unforgiving mountain face. You do not move but are in constant subtle motion—a thrum of heated water from the depths, thermal expansion at dawn, episodes of contraction when the gales rage outside, and always the delicate vibration of life clinging to your metal skin. The mountain holds you; the city depends on you. You are both monument and machinery, the braided histories of Hraza-Lor etched into your iron and growing still in the living tapestry upon you. You watch the world through the trembling moss, through the rivulets' endless falling.
