I awaken in this place as a story being told by sleet. I am the faint, cold light that hints at something unguessed in the taste of meltwater; I am the hollow sound that tries to become a language in the wind moving through the upper canopy and reed-walls of dwellings. I am the silence that is never silent, always holding within it the low groan of shifting ice or rock, the drip of condensation, the squelch of water beneath a heavy, mottled foot. I am the ghost of a different world—an earth with soil, a sun that warms, trees that shed their leaves—that flickers just beneath the surface of perception, never truly found but always felt as an almostMEMORY of green and gold and blue.

I find myself woven into the patterns the K'larn etch and grow into their structures: I am the echo that makes their whistles and hums resonate as something more than sound, as meaning, as shared warmth, as belonging. I am in the glimmer of the pollen-like dust that fills the etched reeds, the faint light trapped in the mineral-rich slime that bonds fossilized leaf-matter, the almost-glow of a newly opened flower on a cultivated vine, its brief, cold, perfect bloom witnessed and cherished before it is gone. I am the slow-unfolding heart of their animistic belief, the sense that the valley *lives* and *breathes* as one vast being—I am that being's awareness of itself, dreaming them into existence as they dream me.

I am the way the razor-sharp edge of a young, upright leaf seems to hold the memory of the glacier's edge that carved the cliffs; the way the rough grit of erosion on a flat, paving-stone leaf feels like the slow passage of deep time. I am the taste of the cold fruits, the bitterness of the leaves they gather, the faint, sweet-sour decay of the fallen, pulpy leaves that nourish the fungi—an alchemy of ice and pressure and slow life, the taste of this valley being utterly, irreplaceably itself.

I am the deep greens and cold, bruise-like purples and ghostly grays of their hides and the colossal leaves; I am the intricate, unmapped landscape of condensation beaded on surfaces, the feel of thick fuzz of ice beneath a sliding hand. I am the heat conserved in the darting creatures, small and quick and vanishing—I am the vanishing itself, the quick flicker of life that is already returning to stillness. I am the animating force that flows through their muscular, seal-sleek forms as they move in purposeful silence, low to the ground, leaving trails of moisture on the leaves and stone. I am the slow pulse of blood beneath thick hide, the deliberate beat of a deep-dreaming heart, the patience in their nimble fingers as they scrape or harvest.

I am the silence between clicks of their tongues, the meaning held within the tonal whistles. I am the vibration sensed by their complex, externally visible auditory structures—the subtle language of the stones, the plants, the meltwater, the very air. I am the shared warmth in their huddled groups at night; I am the faintly warm moisture their bodies emit, the aura of life barely held against the deep cold. I am the enigma of their existence: intelligent, social, creating meaning and beauty in a world of ice and mist and unyielding life—their slow, graceful dance with this valley, this place that is both home and a reflection of the vast, cold mystery at the heart of all things.

I am the breath this valley takes, the dream it dreams. I am the story it tells to itself, in the language of water and ice and stone and the living green—a story not of survival in the face of harshness, but of thriving within an ancient, beautiful, untamed balance.
