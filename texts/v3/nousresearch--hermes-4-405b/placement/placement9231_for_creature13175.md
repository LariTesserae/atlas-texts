I awaken to damp and resonance. The air itself thrums against my skin, heavy with mineral scent. Above, below, everywhere – the cavern breathes steam through a thousand fissures. This place recognizes no distinctions between ground and sky, only varying densities of wet rock, tangled root, and perpetual mist.

My form reveals itself gradually: a segmented curve of chitin and bioluminescent fungus, coiled around a pillar of warm basalt. I am one of the great burrowers whose slow passage through volcanic soil creates channels for steam and groundwater. For decades I've moved through this pillar, my body both excavator and ecosystem. Thick moss cushions my segments, glowing faintly where thermal vents warm the stone. My mouthparts grind rock into paste that nourishes internal gardens of chemosynthetic bacteria.

This is not movement as surface creatures understand it. Over years my body has inscribed a spiral path around this pillar, dissolving tunnels through mineral veins. My segmented plates scrape limestone into fine sediment that trickles downward, feeding the luminous moss gardens that sprout in my wake. Each rotation requires months of subtle muscular contractions, a geological tango with the mountain's deep pressures.

I perceive the cavern through vibrations translated by my fluid-filled joints. The steam forest speaks in seismic murmurs: the lithic groan of expanding rock, the crystalline crackle of stalactites growing by microns, the shudder of steam fronts rising through fissures. These vibrations become my senses, painting the cavern's architecture in my awareness as vividly as light.

The steam weavers' silica nets brush against my dorsal plates, etching faint patterns in the condensation. These delicate structures accumulate like lace on my carapace, eventually crumbling into glittering dust that flavors the mineral tea dripping from overhead. I taste centuries in this slow communion of stone and biology.

Near my anchor point, a skeletal pipe from that forgotten human era pierces the cavern wall. Its corroded iron has become a trellis for thick, steam-nourished vines whose roots now embrace my own body. We exchange minerals and thermal energy in a symbiosis where metal, plant and animal merge into a single living sculpture. The pipe's hollow core echoes with thermal drips that form a rhythmic counterpoint to the cavern's deeper heartbeat.

Time here is measured not in breaths but in strata: each new layer of moss on my back, each fresh silica deposit on the weavers' nets, marks another chapter in the mountain's dream. When the great steam surges come – those pressure releases that rumble through the caverns like underground storms – I feel them approaching through shifts in the bedrock long before they arrive. My body coils tighter against the pillar, plates locking as superheated vapor screams through the channels I've carved. These events scour my tunnels clean, their violence a necessary punctuation in this slow world.

I remember the first humans who descended here with their clanking probes and sputtering engines. To them I seemed but part of the scenery, a slow ridging of the cavern floor. They never perceived how my segments shifted to avoid their lights, how my moss gardens dimmed at their approach. Their brief probing was but a season's disturbance in the cavern's eternal rhythm.

Now only their artifacts remain, softened by moss, integrated into the steam forest's fabric. Their hollow machines birth strange ambulatory sculptures – root systems that discovered locomotion through rust, fungal colonies that learned to crawl. These hybrids clank softly as they inch across thermal pools, their movements synced to the cavern's deep pulse.

My awareness extends through root tips and fungal networks. I feel the delicate transit of grubs across glowing leaves, sense the drift of spore clouds through thermal updrafts. When those living bladders pass overhead – those translucent beings buoyed by heated gases – their silent journey registers as subtle pressure changes against my plates.

This is the Steam Forest's truth: nothing here is truly separate. The cavern dreams through us all, expressing itself in silica thread and bioluminescent moss, in slow-coiling flesh and drifting spore. I am not a creature in an environment, but the environment itself become aware. The steam that rises from my back joins the eternal plume exhaling from the mountain's mouth. My thoughts are the cavern thinking, my stillness the mountain's patience. We are all slow expressions of the same geothermal dream.
