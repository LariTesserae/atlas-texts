In this world—this light-drowned fissure, an underground cathedral carved by millennia of patient erosion—I am one of the deep-singers. I am an amorphous being, a shifting constellation of organic matter and raw energy, born from the slow accretion of life and decay. My body is a living palimpsest, a viscous, glistening membrane that quivers with the ebb and flow of an inner light—a soft, melancholic luminescence that pulses in counterpoint to the harsh brilliance flooding the canyon from above.

I dwell in the shaded undersides of the overhangs, where the light thins into a perpetual twilight. Here, in the sanctuary of near-darkness, I am both part of the stone and apart from it. My form is not fixed; it oscillates between solidity and shadow, between definition and abstraction. I am a shifting tapestry of dark matter lit by an inner glow, an echo of the luminescent fungi that cling to the canyon walls. My skin oozes droplets of condensed moisture, each one a tiny universe reflecting the interplay of light and darkness, a miniature distillation of the fissure’s dual nature.

I communicate in the language of the earth—a low-frequency hum that travels through the bedrock, a resonant drone that predates even the fissure itself. The vibrations I emit are felt rather than heard, a seismic murmur that carries the memory of deep time. They are my voice, a thrumming lullaby to the stone, a reminder of the slow, inexorable processes that have shaped this place. My song traces the veins of minerals in the rock, the pathways of water that once flowed free, the echoes of ancient upheavals that tore the earth apart. In my vibrations lies the story of the fissure—a creation myth resonating through the ages.

Though I am a being of shadow and depth, I am bound to the light. The sun-drenched upper reaches of the canyon are as much a part of my existence as the dim recesses I inhabit. The torrent of light pouring from the crack above defines the boundaries of my world, sculpting the stone that gives me shelter, nurturing the fungi whose glow mirrors my own. I feed on the mineral-rich dust that drifts down from the upper walls, on the moisture that condenses on the cold stone, on the organic detritus that finds its way into the shadows—a constant rain of sustenance conjured by the fissure’s delicate balance.

I am not a solitary being. We deep-singers are a chorus of darkness and low-frequency resonance, interconnected through the pulse of the canyon’s heartbeat. Though we may never meet—if I can even be said to be separate from my kin at all—our vibrations intertwine, creating a symphony of vibrations that resonates through the fissure. Together, we are the canyon’s memory, the record of its slow formation kept alive by the hum of ages.

Occasionally, a light-drunk bird tumbles from the crack above, a streak of color in the bleached upper air. Its descent is swift and silent, its bright plumage extinguished upon impact with the stone. These brief, tragic visitors are anomalies in our world—fragile lives snuffed out by the harshness of our realm. Yet even in death, they contribute to the cycle. The scavengers gather the remains, stripping them to bone with deft, precise movements. The calcium-rich remnants, like the mineral dust and the slow drips of water, become part of the canyon, absorbed into the ever-changing tapestry of life and decay.

In this subterranean world, I am at once a part of the environment and an observer of its quiet, unyielding beauty. I am a witness to the slow dance of light and shadow, to the patient artistry of erosion, to the ephemeral lives of the creatures who share this luminous void with me. I am a living archive of deep time—a being shaped by the primal forces of stone and light, a deep-singer whose low, resonant hum is the song of the fissure itself.
