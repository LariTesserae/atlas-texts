In this world beneath the waves, where sub-aquatic hamlets stand as monuments to erosion and industry thrums through stone, I become a Gardener of Geode Light. My existence is interwoven with the grotto's phosphorescent fungi -- living constellations that thrive where no sun reaches. Each glimmering node is both my creation and my charge, cultivated across vaulted ceilings and clinging to pillars, casting shifting hues across the water. I move through the drowned streets not as a citizen of stone but as a tender of bioluminescence, my form less solid than the mineral-bodied Aqualiths. My body is composed of frond-like filaments, nearly translucent, refracting the fungi's glow I nurture. Every touch leaves trails of spores that blossom into cold fire.

My days are spent in cyclical communion: I scrape nutrients from the dense vegetation that carpets every surface, gather the faint warmth that seeps from hidden geothermal vents, and coax new colonies into being along the smooth-worn edges of carved arches. The hum of machinery, the city's industrial heartbeat, provides the subtle vibrations that stimulate spore release. I am both artist and engineer, painting with living light, ensuring each stone hollow is kissed by an ethereal sheen. To the Aqualiths, I am less a neighbor than a natural phenomenon -- a drifting, luminous presence that maintains the grotto's eternal twilight. They do not speak to me, for my voice is the soft hiss of a thousand tiny releases, but their slow movements often pause as I pass, their mineral eyes reflecting my shimmering silhouette. Their engineering marvels at impossible angles become my canvases, defying expectations that light must fall from above.

The rare fauna of these depths are drawn to my glow, their quiet movements a counterpoint to my drifting stillness. Small segmented creatures with iridescent carapaces sometimes linger near my brightest clusters, and in turn, I follow the paths their migrations trace along the grotto floor – a silent pact of curiosity. Through this world bound by stone and water, I move with imperceptible slowness, a living lantern whose light reveals the contours of the deep. I exist in symbiosis with the inevitability of erosion, aware that even as the city shifts, my glowing gardens will shift with it, a beacon of soft radiance in the eternal, silent currents.
