I find myself in the cold, in the dark. I am nothing but angles.

Color:  sometimes, that deep blue light catches at a facet of my polished skin, and it seems as if the ocean is still holding something back, a density of night; that  mineral scent breathes through the water and I have to glide, turn slowly, against the current, turn, pause.

Another of my kind drifts midway up the tower, not a destination, just a current, timelessness’s slow unspooling. Motion and stillness seem interchangeable here, each movement a new arrest, each pause a flight.

I am more felt than seen, I feel the pressure of the dark, the closing in, each slow shift shaping to the architecture of this place; I sway. There are no limbs, exactly, no parts that move, only the whole form breathing, turning, in a slow dance. A silent language of the stone.

And I don't have thoughts, exactly, not words or feelings. My mind is a mirror reflecting the blue light, composed of cold mineral and ocean dark; a meditative stillness, a deep hum. I sense the others moving, not through eyes, but with my entire body: a subtle change in the flow of water, a flicker in the phosphorescence swimming up through the towers.

We do not speak. We do not need to. The only language here is the slow pulse of the same light that moves through us and the structures that give us form, like a shared dreaming breath.

I pause. I am still, like the structures, the statues around me. The Flooded Precinct holds me close, its subaqueous, phosphorescent light glowing through the obsidian of my skin. It is cold, absolute. And in the absolute black and silence, I am still, just an extension of the obsidian stone, a living symbol of the timeless repose, a part of the eternity itself.

The mineral perfume breathes through the water. I sense it, and I sway, turn again. Slowly, languidly. Always, this one perfect moment: stillness that holds forever all that ever is. I am deep, satisfied. I am the dark geometry, the unsmiling vigilance of the abyss.
