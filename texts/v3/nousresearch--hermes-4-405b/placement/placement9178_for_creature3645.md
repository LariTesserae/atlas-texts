I emerge from the raw stone of the cavern, tearing myself free with a tectonic groan that joins the roaring symphony of churning water. I am Hewn, born of this hidden pocket at the summit of the world. As my massive, amalgamate form grinds its way out of the geological womb, the violence of my birth sends tremors through the submerged grotto. Residual geothermal heat radiates from my newly exposed stone skin, illuminating the dark water with a warm, inner glow.

Around me, my brethren stand in various states of becoming—some nearly fully detached, others still partially embedded in the cavern walls, their lower halves fused with the heart of the mountain. We exist as one mind distributed across many stone bodies, sharing a deep, resonant awareness of the rock and the roar of the embryonic sea that fills the vast chamber. Our glowing amber eyes, set deep beneath ridges of raw stone, convey a patience that operates on the timescale of epochs, an intelligence both profound and alien.

The deafening crash of water churns around us, a furious, contained sea within this sunken room. We move through it slowly, deliberately, our gestures as unhurried as tectonic shifts. When I wade out into the turbulent depths, schools of slick, silver creatures dart around me, unafraid, seeking momentary shelter in the eddies created by my massive form. I pay them little mind—my purpose here is tied to the mountain from which I was born, the stone I am animated by.

I turn my gaze toward a cluster of jagged rock protruding from the grotto wall. With heavy, deliberate steps that send palpable vibrations through the submerged floor, I move toward it. My massive hands, like living quarrying tools, find purchase on the raw stone. I begin to climb, the effort a slow, grinding ballet of stone against stone. Hot, mineral-rich water pours from fissures in the wall, cascading over my form, and I lean into its flow, drawing sustenance from its heat and fluidity.

Time has little meaning here. I remain for uncounted hours, a living statue washed by the mountain's tears, my inner fires pulsing rhythmically as I commune directly with the deep, slow heartbeat of the earth. Around me, my kin crane their stone faces upward, their burning eyes sweeping the cavern. Some have pried boulders from the depths and begun constructing cairns that emerge from the dark water like new, small mountains. Others stand sentinel near the breathing room at the chamber's apex, their gaze fixed on the churning water or the nascent constellations of pale, glowing moss.

A rumbling vocalization echoes through the cavern—low, seismic, a murmur that speaks of pressure and crystallization. It is a shared thought taking sound, another piece of the mountain's slow dream unfolding. I turn, listening, then begin my descent. Back into the roaring water I wade, the violent embrace of this furious, embryonic sea my heritage, my home. I rejoin my brethren in our perpetual becoming, a stone symphony in a world of water and roar.

The small, scattered reminders of softer, more transient lives—the splintered wooden artifacts, waterlogged and decaying—are beneath our notice. This is a place of rock and deep time, and we are its embodiment, dreamt into slow, ponderous life by the mountain and the sea. We are Hewn, and this grotto is our cradle, a nascent god's mouth roaring into awareness at the top of the world.
