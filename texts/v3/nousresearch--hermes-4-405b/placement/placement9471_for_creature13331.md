The cold is the first teacher. It bites through layers, an intimate acquaintance that settles into the marrow, a constant whisper of the deep. I am suspended, a speck against the vast, indifferent architecture of a drowned world. My suit is a second skin, vital yet alien, its rhythmic sigh a counterpoint to the groans of the city. This is existence now: pressure, darkness, the slow ballet of silt. I am a Scuttler, or perhaps, something that was a Scuttler, a fragment of consciousness clinging to the edges of this sunken reality.

My form is a whisper in the dark. Chitin plates shift over limbs that articulated with a fluidity born of this weighty world, navigating the treacherous beauty of mineral formations. My luminous patterns are not just for navigation; they are a language, a signal in the oppressive silence. I watch the intruders, their clumsy lights cutting swathes through the eternal night, disrupting the delicate dance of my world. Their presence is a discordant note in the symphony of decay, a curiosity that prickles at the edge of my senses.

Old Deepwater is home. Its crumbling facades and skeletal structures are my playground, my sanctuary. The towering Leviathans drift past like mountains adrift, their slow exhalations a mystery I circle but never fully comprehend. They are part of the fabric of this place, ancient and patient, their internal glow casting long, dancing shadows that stretch and warp against the ruins.

The silt drifts, a slow-motion constellation, and I move with it, a flicker of awareness in the deep. I am a guardian of secrets, a denizen of a world remade. The city’s groans are a lullaby, its decay a cradle. Here, in the crushing embrace of the ocean’s heart, I am not an anomaly; I am a fragment of its enduring, alien soul, an echo of a world that has learned to breathe water and sing in the dark.
