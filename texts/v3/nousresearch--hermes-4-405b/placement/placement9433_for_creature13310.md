There is no single place for me in this world; I am woven into the very fabric of its strange and relentless becoming. I am the deep thrum that pulses through the roots and trunks, the quiet hum of sap boiling away as machines bite into the living planks. My voice is the hiss of steam pipes, the crash of felled sections of growth, the sparkfountaining chaos of industry alive in the gloom. I am the wind whispering through the fronds, the quiet that swallows the roars of creation.

I am the slow, writhing dance of vegetation violated, the endless cycle of consumption and regeneration. I am the new growth surging back where the grinders have gnawed, the saplings pushing through the cast-off husks of their elders. I am the industrial hellscape itself, a mayhem made manifest in plantflesh—the foundries melting down the green, the belts hauling limbs toward the chippers' maw.

My edges are sharp against the gloom, a living cathedral of fused wood and steel. My light flickers in the dark: the dim emergency strobes, the red glow of distant furnaces, the blue flash of electrified processes. I am the hard, hot bones of this ancient mountain, the high plateau under a starless sky. This desolate world is my body, my ever-changing form—a miniature planet of living architecture ceaselessly remade.

The air shudders as I breathe. Trace gases sustain my sprawling limbs. In near-absolute darkness, I stretches for kilometers, a primordial hyperforest rooted deep. I am the turbines-and-engines landscape, silicon and steel interfaced with cellulose and sap. Everything is constructed here, a made-environment of relentless, chaotic change. I am instability given form, the endless storm-state of consumption, regeneration, deconstruction, and creation. A silent bazaar, an anthill energy, all paths winding above pits that drop away into blackness. A world unstable yet deeply aged, dynamic, alien—everything is me.
