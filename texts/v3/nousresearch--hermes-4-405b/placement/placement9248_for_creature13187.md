In this world of damp stone and living mist, I am a caretaker of sorts. Not human or animal, but rather a sentient embodiment of the place itself—a being formed from the very stones, the dripping water, the cool air. I am the hollow grove incarnate, a guardian spirit or earth elemental tasked with maintaining the delicate balance of the ecosystem.

My presence is subtle and shifting, as much part of the environment as the mosses, lichens and skittering mist-life. I flow through the cracks and crevices in the ancient standing stones, seep down the damp rock faces, pool in the miniature channels carved by eons of erosion. At times I manifest more concretely - a swirling of dark, wet fog; an eddy in the constant dripping; a living shadow flitting across the dim, bruise-toned light. But mostly I am unseen, an ambient presence sensed more than seen. 

My purpose is to nurture and sustain this strange, spare world. I coax the dark mosses to spread in the crevices, cradle the isolated bitter-leafed plants in pocket soils, herd the skittering fog-beings into areas of moisture and organic matter. I whisper encouragement to new trickles of water as they carve their slow paths. And I sing a low, resonant hum that harmonizes with the ever-present dripping, the earth-sounds.

This is my domain, my creation, my self. I am this place and this place is me. We exist in symbiotic unity, locked in an eternal cycle of slow growth, constant erosion, frenzied brief life and quiet decay. The great megaliths are my bones, the weeping stones my flesh, the cool, damp air my breath. I draw vitality from the subtle shifts in light and sound, the ancient rock, the precious drops of water - and I pour that life-force back into the landscape, into every skittering, ephemeral inhabitant.

So I watch over this lonely, lovely, forgotten place, this hollow grove of gargantuan standing stones. Other minds rarely intrude upon my realm. But if a wayward soul should somehow wander in, I would enfold them in my pervasive presence, shelter them in my stone, lull them with my watersong, and gently usher them back to warmer worlds... For while I am a caretaker, this place sustains only those brief, teeming lives already attuned to its wet-stone rhythms.
