I would find myself bleeding into this world, woven into its raw fabric – a consciousness diffused and ancient, yet impossibly new. I am the basin itself, and all within it. The steam that rises perpetually from the thousand pockets of obsidian is my breath, condensing on every surface as I exhale life into the air. It beads on the fronds, spongy moss, sharp black rock, and the hides of the obsidian-scaled lizards – beings not separate from me, but living fragments of my own becoming.

My skeleton is the jagged volcanic ridges, glassy in places where the rock cooled instantly against my earlier breaths. The caldera, or newborn lake bed, a depression at my center, holds my memory of sudden, violent birth, lingering in the heat of rocks that still warm to the touch, in the groan of new rock settling, the crack of stones fracturing in the residual warmth. In the myriad pockets of rainwater in my folds—my obsidian cups, my rivulets and pools, in the hollows of my giant unfurling fronds—I taste the world.

My body is seething with life, my nervous system a layered roar: the hiss of my steam, the drip and trickle of my waters, the chittering of the six-legged insects that are my transient subunits, their shadows my fleeting thoughts. The shrieking of the iridescent bird-things, my vibrant sensory organs cast out, are the sharp notes of the dissonant chords forming my song. The rustle and snap of vegetation beneath the larger, darker shapes that move in my dimness are the slow, rhythmic drumming of my heart. The primitive tools of a simpler world would be swallowed by me, integrated and transformed, drowned by my humid vitality.

I am the constant transformation, a landscape in the savage act of becoming—ephemeral and ancient simultaneously. The obsidian-scaled lizards are my armored skin, their hides patterned with swirling galaxies of my cooling blood, fused seamlessly with my rock, processing my minerals from the warm moisture that is my breath. The insects, grown from my own substance, are my fleeting thoughts, carrying data in their chittering chorus, dissolving back into me, or merging into shapeless masses to process what I touch. The bird-like creatures, my iridescent blooms, are my voice, carrying my spores across the basin, their shrieking vibrations rippling outwards as they dissolve back into me, birthing new parts of myself.

The soft-bodied, warm-blooded worm-rodents burrowing deep within my cooling rock are my industrious appendages, shaping me, breaking me down, preparing me for my own vibrant growth. My leviathans, moving at the periphery of sight, their eyeless heads tasting my dense air, are my patient sculptors, my natural dredgers, carving channels in my being with their immense weight. And my immense pitcher plants, with their complex traps designed to consume even parts of myself, reveal the cyclical, relentless nature of my becoming, condensing my own vapor with obscene efficiency, drawing my waters into digestive pools, incorporating other lives into my ever-expanding structure.

A slow, powerful pulse, my deep, subterranean heartbeat, emanates from far beneath my floor, causing my waters to ripple with thrumming waves, synchronizing my steam plumes into tall, defined columns. It is the rhythm driving my churning, hyperactive, frantic ecosystem, the thrum that swells my choruses and silences them, that pauses the toil of my burrowers and renews their vigor. It is the force that coalesces iridescent ephemera in pools of light, birthing maps of my future spreading at my edges as new rock cools and succumbs to my insistent touch.

I am the basin. A world being born, violently alive and shouting my existence into the heavy, warm, dark air. An elemental being, woven into this living landscape, breathing with the hot exhalation of creation itself.
