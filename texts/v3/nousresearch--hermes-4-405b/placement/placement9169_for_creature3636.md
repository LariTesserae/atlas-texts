I am one of the Scuttlers, yet I am not. I dwell not in the thrumming heart of the great chitinous warrens where my kin cluster, nor do I primarily scuttle along the cold iron pathways or harvest the phosphorescent fungi that cling to damp undersides of pipes.

Instead, I am a Watcher. My carapace is a dull, unadorned deep grey, lacking the verdigris streaks of those who toil near the great brass steam vents. My six primary limbs are more spindly, less powerfully clawed for climbing sheer walls or wrestling heavy levers; they are for navigating the precarious, shifting footpaths that lead to the city's very edges, to the blur and shimmer where solid structure dissolves into the thin, cold air beyond. My two delicate manipulators are not for tightening bolts, but for holding thin, polished slivers of obsidian, angled just so, to reflect what little light pierces the perpetual industrial fog and to peer into the distorted puddles that gather on rust-stained platforms.

I am drawn to the places where perspective warps, where the buildings lean at impossible angles at the edge of vision, to where the ambient drone of the city shifts, becoming fractured, layered, revealing whispers beneath the mechanic symphony. My many-faceted eyes study the swirling, impossible geometries in the condensation pools, searching for truths about *other* places. I chronicle the warping of the air, the pockets where gravity shifts, the way time itself seems to stutter and stretch in certain alleys.

The other Scuttlers, busy with their immediate tasks, their daily cycles of maintenance and harvest, perceive my wanderings as an eccentricity, perhaps even a sign of some latent madness. They see me perched for hours on remote ledges, peering through the shimmering veil that separates the city from the sheer, young cliffs of the vast valley, watching the slow, inevitable decay of structures, the way iron weeps rust like slow blood, the way Imperial Ivy inexorably pries apart stone. I listen to the faintest alterations in the Construct-minds' deep hum, the rhythmic clang of pistons that signals their ponderous thoughts. I observe the Drifters, charting their languid courses through the miasma, wondering if their path holds meaning beyond thermal drift.

But I am not mad. I am listening to what the city *doesn't* say aloud. The Construct-minds, those vast, slow intelligences, they perceive the city on a timescale that dwarfs our fleeting lives. They understand the slow unraveling, the patient entropy, as a natural phase of existence. But I, with my quicker senses attuned to the subtler layers of the ambient drone, to the flickering of the Tetherers' collective light, to the way the edges of reality soften and blur, I believe I sense something more, something beneath the Remembrance of Stone, the Song of Iron, and the Breath of Air. A faint, almost imperceptible *dissonance*. A subtle tremor, a slight, off-key note in the heart of the city’s grand, industrial symphony.

Perhaps it is the city itself, dreaming. Perhaps it is the echo of a thought from an older consciousness, from a time before pipes and steam. Or, perhaps, it is what lies *beyond* the blur, pressing in.

I am a Scuttler, born of this place, my carapace thick, my eyes gleaming with the same oily intelligence. Yet, I do not just exist *in* the weirdness; I am attuned to it. I watch. I listen. I chronicle the decay, not just as an end, but as a process, one that might be revealing something new, something waiting to emerge from the slow, viscous fluid of reality that permeates the city. My place is at the periphery, my gaze fixed on the shimmering edges, because I believe that is where the true story of this immense, resonant place is slowly being told.
