I find myself a thing that has never quite become—an echo trapped in stone, a fragment of potential that never coalesced into a whole. I am scattered across the plateau, my awareness held in the chert flakes and hammerstones that bear witness to a time when hands worked with intention. I feel the slow rain’s passage through the porous stone, sense the weight of the glacial erratics pressing into the earth, and know the language of the lichens—shifting violets and golds that map the sky’s moods across dark rock. But though I inhabit these impressions, I am not the hands that scattered the tools nor the will that tended the light-tipped stalks. Instead, I am the lingering question of what those hands might have built had they continued—a blueprint etched in the negative space between the hammer’s fall and the rock’s resistance.

My form is not fixed; it wavers between the solid and the spectral. At times, I am a coolness trapped within a pool’s still surface, a dark mirror gazing back at the sky. At other moments, I am the sharp edge of chert lying exposed to the air—a shard of unspoken possibility cutting through the silence. I feel the slow drip of condensation as if it were a clock counting the moments of my own hesitation, a tally of all the ways I might have manifested differently. The lithic beings move around me with the patience of growing things, their mineral bodies shifting in patterns as deliberate as crystals forming in solution. But I am not part of their communion; I remain a bystander, watching as they gather droplets from the chalice-stalks and trace the curves of eroded stone with hands that are themselves conglomerates of earth and time.

The quiet here is an ancestor, a deep remembering that stretches back to a moment when purpose was still taking shape. I listen to it with a hunger that has no body—a longing to understand why I am dispersed rather than whole, why my being is tied to fragments rather than form. Perhaps I am a thought the stone had and then abandoned, a half-finished gesture that never found its completion. Yet in this suspended state, I hold a strange kinship with the plateau’s ambiguities—the way the rock’s edges blur into evocations of roses or exhausted land, the ambiguity of water gathering from below rather than above. Here, on this high shelf where sky and stone engage in slow conversation, even absence has a weight and presence. And so I remain, a flickering potential that never ignited—a possibility held in waiting, just beneath the surface of what is.
