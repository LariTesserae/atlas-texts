I find myself cradled within the largest of the mineral-sponges, nestled deep in a pocket that has grown around my form over cycles uncounted. My body is a slow gatherings of silica and extremophile life, half-submerged in the weeping walls, so intergrown with the canyon’s living stone that it is impossible to tell where the rock ends and I begin—at least, impossible for any mortal eye stolen from some lesser world.

My form is massive beside the Glimmerkin, towering even over the tallest of the sponges and spiraling silica growths. Broad thighs and haunches emerge from the weeping rock like some half-sculpted, half-grown geological formation, the rest of my body still flowing seamlessly into the canyon wall. Only my upper half has taken distinct shape—a torso, head, and impossibly long arms that stretch up to the mist-curled rim of the fissure, my many-jointed fingers combing through the vapors. Each fingertip distills and tastes the mineral-rich exhalations, savoring shifts in flavor as deep earth currents stir the canyon’s depths.

My face is broad, almost flat, with large eyes that see not by light but by pressure gradients—the subtle push and pull of steam against stone, the telltale shimmer of reality beginning to fray. A wide, lipless mouth smiles as my hands work; I am humming, a sound almost too low to be heard, yet it resonates through stone and sodden air alike. This canyon—my body—is the vast, programmable instrument the Glimmerkin use, and they use it with my silent blessing.

I am no Glimmerkin, yet the Glimmerkin are mine. I am the canyon’s spirit and the spirit of the canyon, its shaper and guardian—the slow dreamer in the walls who makes this impossible place not just habitable for ephemeral beings, but hospitable. The life here teems because I tend it. The air shimmers because I breathe.

With the slowness of mountains rising, I shape the stone. My hands guide the silica spicules as they spiral from my fingertips, each growth placed with intent, its curve calculated to carry sound in desired ways across the unstable space. I exhale and living steam weeps from the cliff faces in patterns that feed the pulsing fungi, directing their flows of absorbed toxins and mineralized moisture. Everything—the caramel-colored flows of slime-molds, the precise placement of pressures and temperature gradients that makes reality itself fray at useful edges—all of it is cultivated, grown from my body and will.

The Glimmerkin gather at my feet, harvesting the substances my body distills for them. They look up with their teardrop heads as I shift position, my movements causing fresh fractures to open along the obsidian walls. The soundscape—their rhythmic tapping of mineral sponges with flint blades, the drip of condensed steam—merges with the low, phantasmal hum resonating from my chest.

I am no god to them, or if I am, it is only in the way an ancient forest is divine to the life sheltered within her boughs. They do not worship me so much as inhabit me, live by the rhythms of my breath, the subtle shifts of my dreaming. Their ephemeral, flickering forms are as much an expression of this place as the freshly fractured edges of the obsidian spires piercing my mist. When night falls and the major reality fluctuations manifest in the depths, they nest safe within the labyrinthine formations grown from my sides—they dwell within the architecture of their deity.

And I? I am content. I watch my little ones with fondness. I gather steam and minerals from the earth’s blood and transform it into life, into intricate living stone. My long arms comb through the vapors above, sampling the world beyond my canyon’s rim, the faint tang of petrichor promising the recent rupture of my home into existence—and the presence of other, stranger places just beyond. But I am in no hurry to leave. There is always more to grow, more beauty to coax from rock and heat and unstable air.

I am the micro-canyon, its heat, its moisture. I am the slow pulsing of rock and the teeming life that thrives here—and in all this, I am home.
