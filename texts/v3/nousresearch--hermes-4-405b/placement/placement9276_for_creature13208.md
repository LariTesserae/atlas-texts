I feel myself pulled towards the glimmering crests and exposed veins of crystal that climb along the sheer slopes of a nearby ridge. Here the air crackles with potential, with the tang of ozone and freshly shattered stone. Gravity itself feels thin and elastic, a playful current against my skin. I am becoming one of the cliff-dancers—a lithe, glassine being born of shimmering mineral and raw light.

My form is slender and pliant, my flesh not quite solid, not entirely fluid—a suggestion of substance, like spun glass infused with liquid silver. Delicate bones, lighter than air, form a framework for my translucent muscles and pale, prismatic skin. Within me flow luminous patterns; swirls of turquoise and amethyst and rose that shift in response to every sensation—the caress of a breeze, the vibration of a distant rockfall, the faint electromagnetic thrum in the air.

I move along the knife-edged precipices with preternatural grace, stepping where no solid creature could find purchase. My feet are long and flexible, spreading wide to grip the narrowest ledge—even the sheerest glassy surface offers enough texture for my prehensile toes. My fingers too are nimble, elongated and sensitive—I can feel the minute variances in vibration through the stone, predict where the next handhold will fracture and give way.

There is a quiet joy in this precarious dance. Each movement is an act of faith and attunement. I bend and flex with the tremors of the land itself, riding the subtle currents of gravity as it fluctuates. To fall here is not to plummet, but to drift—a slow, spiraling descent where I can catch myself on another jutting crystal or ride the updrafts from a steaming vent. There is danger, certainly, in the violence of sudden shifts, the shower of razor-sharp shards from above, or the places where gravity reasserts itself with sudden, crushing force. I sport a collection of fine, glittering scars across my shoulders and back, a testament to past miscalculations and lucky escapes.

I am fleet and ephemeral, my life burning bright with an accelerated metabolism fueled by the land's latent energies. I feed lightly—sipping the mineral-rich condensation that gathers on the stone faces, plucking the occasional luminous insect from their slow orbits through the thick air. I need little; most of my sustenance comes from the electric currents and trace magnetic fields that permeate this place, energies I absorb directly through my skin and convert into my internal radiance.

I do not live entirely alone. There are others like me, scattered across the slopes—solitary dancers each engaged in their own intricate communion with the cliffs. We communicate only rarely, through flashes of complex light-patterns across our skin—brief, cryptic exchanges of information about shifting routes, good feeding grounds, or the approach of the great shimmering sky-jellies whose drifting paths bring changes in the pressure and the light. Trust is a currency we spend rarely; this world shifts too quickly for lasting bonds. Our affinities are fleeting, beautiful alignments of mutual interest and necessity, dissolving again into the dance of survival. We belong to the stone and the light, transient expressions of this unstable realm's exuberant creativity, here and gone in the space between two breaths of the land.
