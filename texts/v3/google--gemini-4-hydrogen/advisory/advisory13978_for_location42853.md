Visiting this world is deceptive. At first glance, it doesn’t look like a place that wants to kill you—there are no roaring predators, no blizzards, no lethal radiation, and no collapsing ruins. Instead, it feels like stepping into the basement of a continent-sized power plant that has been running smoothly for a million years. 

That quiet stability is the trap. Everything here has reached a permanent, unforgiving equilibrium, and an ordinary human body is not built to survive in it for long without deliberate preparation. Here is how you should prepare before stepping onto the grates.

---

### 1. Beat the Wet-Bulb Trap (Thermal & Hydration Prep)
The single greatest immediate threat here is the air itself. Hovering in the mid-thirties Celsius at a perpetual 100% humidity (the dew point), this atmosphere is at or near the lethal **wet-bulb threshold** for humans. Because the air is already saturated with moisture, your sweat will not evaporate. Your body’s primary cooling system is completely useless here.

* **Bring Active Cooling:** Passive cooling (cotton shirts, fans, misting bottles) will do nothing. Wear a phase-change cooling vest (ice or paraffin packs) or a battery-powered liquid-cooled undergarment beneath your outer layer. Know your exact thermal timer—when your cooling packs melt or your batteries run low, you *must* leave.
* **Carry All Your Water:** You are surrounded by water, and none of it is potable. The shallow basins and dripping condensate are rich in dissolved minerals, chemical scale, and trace leachates from oil-cooled machinery and copper conduits—plus the waste of millions of animals. Boiling it won’t make it safe; you would need heavy reverse-osmosis or distillation gear to remove the chemical load. Pack more water and electrolytes than you think you need, because you will sweat profusely even while failing to cool down.

### 2. Dress for a Live, Wet Grid
This is a continent of uninsulated industrial geometry drenched in conductive, mineral-rich moisture. The power grid is live, dense, and humming at 60 hertz.

* **Dielectric, Slip-Resistant Footwear:** Your boots are your lifeline. They must be electrical-hazard (EH) rated—thick, non-conductive rubber or composite soles with zero exposed metal shanks or eyelets. The treads need to grip wet, slimy perforated steel catwalks and submerged cast-iron plates. 
* **Puncture and Abrasion Protection:** Nothing here has weathered. Every bevel, expansion joint, and drainage grate is as razor-sharp as the day it was cast. Combined with the needle-like proboscises of the grazers and the rasping radulae of the mollusks, thin athletic wear will tear quickly. Wear tough, smooth-surfaced synthetic workwear (tightly woven ripstop or coated nylon) that is difficult for creatures to grip or rasp through, and sealed at the ankles and wrists.
* **Keep Your Hands to Yourself:** Wear insulated, waterproof work gloves, and carry a non-contact voltage detector. Never grab an overhead busbar to steady yourself, never lean against a humming rectifier housing, and do not wade into the basins near submerged copper conduits without checking for stray voltage. Even if a surface isn't electrified, it will be coated in slick chemical slime or clinging organisms.

### 3. Navigate the Living Circuit
There are no plants, no soil, and no hiding places—just animals packed into every cubic inch of space. Because there is nowhere to flee, nothing here has a panic response. The creatures will not charge you in anger, nor will they scatter when you approach. Their danger lies in their sheer, indifferent density and the fact that they treat *everything* as part of the industrial substrate.

* **Don’t Become Furniture:** If you sit still on a catwalk or lean against a radiator bank for too long, the local fauna may treat you as just another warm, mineral-sweating fixture. Molluscan sheets may slowly drape onto your boots; sump-polyps may test your ankles; swarms of papery flyers may settle on your shoulders if your gear emits any electromagnetic hum. Keep moving, and check your legs and pack regularly.
* **Respect the Grazers:** The heron-sized, six-limbed grazers move in slow, ceremonial herds and won’t bother you if you give them space. However, their carapaces are ceramic-hard and their needle proboscises are meant for spearing mantle-slugs and probing metal grates. Give herds the right-of-way on narrow catwalks, and keep your eyes open—their only warning sound is the faint *tick* of their feet on the iron.
* **Don’t Camp on the Floor:** You cannot pitch a standard tent on wet, crowded iron plates or hang a hammock from power cables covered in vibrating flyers. If you must rest, you need a short, controlled break on a cleared section of catwalk using a waterproof insulated pad, or better yet, keep your visits short enough that you never need to sleep here.

### 4. Protect Your Senses and Gear
The environment is subtly hostile to human perception and standard equipment.

* **Eye Protection (With Anti-Fog):** The sky is an endless, flat, white glare, amplified by millions of wet, reflective surfaces. Bring polarized, UV-blocking sunglasses or goggles. Crucially, treat the lenses with heavy-duty anti-fog coating *before* you arrive; the moment you step into 100% dew-point air, untreated lenses will blind you with condensation.
* **Waterproof Everything:** Anything that isn't sealed will weep. Paper notebooks will turn to pulp; unsealed electronics will corrode; food will spoil. Use IP67/IP68 waterproof cases, dry bags, and silica gel packets for anything sensitive.
* **Navigation Without Landmarks:** The horizon is a ruler-straight line of identical cooling baffles and transformers. There is no sun angle to track time or direction, and the pervasive 60-cycle electromagnetic field will render a standard magnetic compass useless. You will lose your bearings in ten minutes. Bring a physical tether, bright non-conductive flagging tape to tie to catwalk railings (chalk will wash away in the condensate), or an inertial dead-reckoning tracker and laser rangefinder to keep track of your entry point.
* **Manage the Hum:** The 60-hertz vibration will rattle in your teeth and the soles of your boots, and the eerie, breathless silence between the dripping water and ticking feet can feel deeply claustrophobic. Bring foam earplugs if the hum grates on your nerves—though you will still feel it in your jaw—and set strict mechanical or vibrating watch alarms so you don't lose track of time in a world with no day or night.

### 5. First Aid & Exit Protocol
Pack a compact trauma and decontamination kit: strong antiseptics (any cut exposed to the tea-colored basin water or animal slime will infect rapidly), heavy tweezers or a plastic scraper for removing clinging mollusks or polyps cleanly, and burn/shock treatment.

Above all, treat this world like a deep-sea dive or a spacewalk: **enter with a clear objective, watch your thermal and hydration clocks, don't touch the machinery, and leave before your cooling gear runs dry.** Everything here has been in balance for eons—it will not notice you, and it will not make room for you.
