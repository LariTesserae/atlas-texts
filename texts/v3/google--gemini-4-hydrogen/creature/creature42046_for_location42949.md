At first, it seems nothing could live here, because nothing lives the way animals do. The avenues hold no footsteps, no wingbeats, no scuttling in the moss. Yet a city of porous towers, fluted aqueducts, and sunken plazas is not uninhabited; its residents simply do not cross the street.

You notice them first as a kind of precision inside the roar. The valley’s chord—that deafening, teeth-vibrating wall of wind and falling water—is too steady to be mere accident, and the geometric lattices frozen on the surface of the plaza pools are too crisp to be the work of stone alone. Look closely at the places where the city’s rules slip: along the lips of the fourth-tier viaducts where cascades bend smoothly backward, inside the throats of the hollow towers, and along the wet margins of the sunken basins.

Wedged into the travertine and braided into the calcified root-buttresses are tall, narrow, hollow-bodied forms. At a glance they pass for pale, mineral-encrusted trunks or natural swellings of lime, slick with the same condensed water that weeps down every wall. They have no eyes, no jaws, no jointed limbs for walking or grasping. Instead, their pale, porous hides are pierced by neat rows of narrow slits and flared siphons, like the finger-holes of flutes or the spiracles of giant, stationary larvae. From their lower flanks, bundles of fine, translucent filaments trail directly into the fast-moving currents of the aqueducts.

Nothing moves on its own, and neither do they. They do not breathe in and out; no chest expands, no gill-cover pulses. Rather, the scalding updrafts and the crystal-clear water pass straight through them in a continuous, laminar rush. Each one is tuned to the valley’s unvarying chord the way a struck fork is tuned to its twin. Driven by the screaming air from the cloud-floor below and the weight of the water above, they vibrate at a frequency so high and so even that to the eye they appear utterly still, ringed only by a faint, permanent halo of atomized mist.

This motionless hum is their entire metabolism. Because nothing here rots, sheds, or grows, they neither feed nor excrete; they are closed circulatory loops, taking in mineral-heavy steam and fast water, channeling the energy through their resonant chambers, and returning it unchanged to the air and the cisterns. They do not age because they never accumulate waste or wear. As long as the sun hangs at its pale zenith and the heat rises from the abyss, they persist in the exact same state of turgid, humming fullness as the emerald moss around them.

Yet what clings to the upper terraces is only half of what lives there.

Walk—or stand—beside one of the sunken plazas and look down into a standing pool. The water does not mirror the sky; it shows the glistening, wet undersides of the root-arches, suspended over an endless, sunlit drop. And hanging from those inverted arches, trailing their pale, hair-fine filaments straight down into the rising thermals miles above the clouds, are the rest of them.

Whether these are separate individuals or the lower halves of the ones wedged into the streets above is impossible to say, for the city’s geometry folds around them. Up on the sunlit terraces, the air immediately surrounding their porous flanks is so dense with superheated, vibrating vapor that it acts as a lens, bending the noon glare slightly backward—which is why the colonnades beside them cast crisp shadows that lean toward the sun rather than away from it.

If they possess a mind, it is nothing like the sequential thoughts of creatures that move through time. A thought requires a before and an after, a swelling and a fading, and here nothing swells or fades by a single decibel. Instead, their awareness—if it can be called that—is purely spatial and unanimous, distributed across the entire valley through the vibration in the stone and the water. When a droplet falls in a sunken plaza and locks into a geometric lattice, the pressure of that ring is felt instantaneously by the filaments hanging over the abyss three ridges away.

They do not guard the city, and they do not tend it with hands. They *are* the tension that holds it together. Without their resonant bodies wedged into the aquifers and the wind-pipes, the falling water would plunge straight into the clouds below instead of curving back into the upper cisterns, the ripples would wash away, the roots would rot, and the valley would drain into silence. Locked in their scalding, deafening equilibrium, they simply hold the note—keeping the city, and themselves, forever at noon.
