At first, you think the gliders and the fiberglass lichen are the whole of it—the last, thin coat of varnish on a world that has worn down to its bones. The Deck is too bright, too dry, too acoustically dead to harbor anything with weight or ambition.

Then you notice that a patch of the floor has moved.

Nothing breaks the horizon. Nothing rises above the knee-high arcs of the cooling fins. Yet fifty paces away, where the glare pools thick and white-gold over a sunken grid of frosted lenses, a cluster of chalky hexagonal plates shifts by an inch, then another, sliding with a faint, dry *shhh* of ceramic against ceramic.

Whatever lives here in earnest has learned the first law of the Deck: do not argue with the light, and do not trust the air to carry you.

***

The smallest of the true dwellers never see the sky at all. They live inside the flaky pastry of the delaminated tiles, in the millimeter-high gaps between the crystalline wafers and the honeycombed baffles.

They are **baffle-slips**—flat, translucent things shaped like torn scraps of parchment, no thicker than a fingernail and pale as the silver-white dust that drifts into their crevices. They have no eyes and no hardened shells; the stacked ceramic plates above them are shell enough. They move by rippling their edges in slow, peristaltic waves, grazing on the microscopic crusts of oxidized bronze and the brief, pre-dawn film of hygroscopic salt-dew that condenses in the deep seams. If you pry up a loose hexagonal tile with a boot heel, you will not hear them scatter. You will only see a dozen pale, papery shapes vanish into the hair-thin conduits beneath, leaving behind tiny, clean-swept tracks in the metal powder.

Above them, out on the open plain where the dust-devils spin without a whisper, move the **jar-walkers**. They are no larger than a closed fist: three slender, needle-thin legs of vitrified glass—natural insulators—supporting a globular abdomen wound tight with fine, tarnished copper filaments they have harvested from eroded sub-layers. They are scavengers of weather. When a tall, silver pillar of electrostatic dust sways past, shedding its silent blue sparks into the floor, the jar-walkers stride into its wake on their glass stilts. They raise their copper bodies toward the charge, soaking up the static until their filaments hum with a faint, sub-audible vibration, storing the voltage in internal bladders lined with salty fluid. That stored charge is their currency of survival: with it, they can split carbon from the dry air to mend their shells, or discharge a sudden, lethal arc into a diamond glider that has dropped flat against the tiles to hide.

***

But the true inheritors of the Deck are larger, quieter, and far stranger. A traveler who spends enough days crossing the cooling fins might come to call them the **Seam-Keepers**, though they have no name for themselves that could ever be spoken aloud.

At midday, when the white-gold glare shrinks every shadow to a razor-edged smudge, a Seam-Keeper is nearly impossible to spot. It hunkers down in the shallow depressions between the cooling fins, pulling its limbs tight beneath a broad, low-slung mantle of overlapping hexagonal plates. Those plates are not scavenged; they are grown. The creature consumes the pulverized glass and abraded metamaterials of the floor, grinding them in a heavy, mineral gizzard and extruding the sintered paste through pores along its back, where the relentless heat bakes it into a chalky, high-albedo armor identical to the Deck itself. Even its posture mimics the terrain—the dorsal ridge of its mantle is fluted with stubby, rounded crests that match the worn-down cooling fins around it.

Only when the sky tilts toward late afternoon, and the thermal tide begins to ripple across the substrate, do they unfold.

A Seam-Keeper stands no higher than your knee, though its body is as wide as a low table—an articulated, flattened oval supported by eight slender, multi-jointed legs sheathed in smooth ceramic. The tips of its feet are padded with dense mats of carbon-felt grown from the air, so that when it steps cleanly into the narrow gaps between the cooling fins, it makes no sound at all. Not even the dry *tock* of a dropped tool. You do not hear one approaching across the plain; you only feel a sudden, faint prickle in the hairs on your wrists as its electrostatic field brushes yours, or notice that the silver dust beside your boot has stood up on end in stiff, trembling ridges.

Along the shadowed rim of its mantle sit a dozen narrow optical slits, each shaded by a tiny, movable louver of frosted quartz that tilts open and shut in response to the glare. Behind the mantle trail two long, hair-thin antennae of tarnished silver, sweeping lightly across the tiles behind it like trailing wires, tasting the stray currents leaking from buried field-coils and listening to the sub-audible hum that sits behind the eyes.

***

In a world that swallows sound, the Seam-Keepers have built a civilization out of light, pressure, and charge.

They do not speak into the dead air. When two of them meet at the edge of a sunken optical basin, they lower their heavy ventral plates flat against the warm ceramic and *knock*—sharp, coded pulses driven straight into the substrate through a hard, mallet-like nodule on their undersides. Through the solid, sintered floor, those vibrations travel for hundreds of yards without losing their shape, bypassing the silent air entirely. To a human standing nearby, it feels only like a strange, rhythmic tingling in the soles of your boots, as if the machine beneath you has developed a sudden, purposeful stutter.

Up close, they use light. Beneath their chalky outer armor, the Seam-Keepers carry rows of electroluminescent organs tuned to the exact violet and amber wavelengths of the Deck’s buried waveguides. When they gather in the shallow, saucer-like basins where the outer plating has eroded away, they tilt their mantles to shade the blinding sky and flash rapid, silent patterns across one another’s flanks—a flickering conversation of amber and violet that looks, from a distance, like a nest of broken consoles exchanging handshakes.

And they are obsessed with the waveguides.

No one knows whether the Seam-Keepers evolved from the Deck’s own ancient, autonomous maintenance systems—flesh-and-mineral descendants of repair drones that went feral eons ago and forgot their makers—or whether they are native organisms that simply grew into the shape of the machine. Either way, the Deck’s autonomic reflexes are their seasons, their music, and their religion.

When a wave of thermal motion approaches from the horizon, millions of flush-mounted louvers tilting open in unison to vent trapped heat, the Seam-Keepers move with it. They follow the opening louvers like herds following a rainfront, using hooked, diamond-tipped foreclaws to clear away jammed silica grit and dead fiberglass lichen that might keep a vent from sealing. When a buried waveguide dims—choked by blown glass flour or severed by the slow shifting of the plates—a dozen Seam-Keepers will gather around the dead lens. They will spend days there, patiently scraping the frosted quartz clean, tilting broken shards of mirror-tile to focus the overhead glare into the dark node, or even shedding pieces of their own copper-veined under-shells to bridge a broken circuit.

They do not know they are tending the hull of a forgotten vessel. They only know that when the amber and violet pulses run strong beneath the glass, the sub-audible hum in their antennae is steady and clear; and when a section goes dark, the silence becomes a physical ache.

***

In the broad, sunken basins—the places where the Deck has worn down to its tangled latticework of wire-thin reeds—you can see their handiwork, though at first glance it looks like nothing more than scattered rubble.

The Seam-Keepers are architects of shadow and charge. They drag loose hexagonal tiles and shards of vitrified glass into vast, flat, spiraling mosaics across the basin floors. Nothing is stacked high enough to catch the wind or break the horizon. Instead, every shard is angled with mathematical precision to trap the slightest whisper of morning chill, creating micro-climates where the pre-dawn salt-dew lingers for an hour longer than it should, and where the brittle, pale-olive lichen grows thick enough to graze. Other arrangements act as electrostatic lenses, gathering the stray charge from leaking field-coils and focusing it into a single, shimmering patch of dust where the Seam-Keepers come to rest, basking in the dry tingle like lizards on a warm stone.

To cross one of their basins is to walk through a cathedral built for senses you do not possess—a library written in angles of reflection and gradients of static.

***

Even here, in this flat, bleached stillness, there are things the Seam-Keepers fear.

They avoid the scarred trenches—those ancient, wind-smoothed maintenance tracks where the outer plating is thin and unstable—and they step with agonizing care over delaminated tiles that rattle or give slightly under their felt pads.

Down there, threaded through the dark, cramped sub-layers of conduits and baffles, live the **braids**.

A braid is rarely seen whole. It is a long, ribbon-like predator made of tangled, tarnished alloy fibers and pale, sinewy muscle, entirely blind and deaf, navigating the sub-layers by following the heat of living bodies through the ceramic above. It does not hunt out in the glare. It waits beneath a loose hexagonal plate, coiled around a leaking field-coil, soaking up charge like a battery.

When a jar-walker—or a distracted Seam-Keeper, or a careless traveler—steps onto the tile above it, the slight shift in pressure trips its ambush. A single, whip-thin strand of braided metal snaps up through the narrow seam between the plates. It does not bite; it *grounds*, discharging a violent, silent arc of blue static that stops a nervous system in a fraction of a second. If the prey is small, the braid drags it down, piece by piece, into the flaky darkness of the sub-layers. If it is too large to pull through the seam, the braid simply anchors itself to the carcass and siphons its moisture and residual charge through hollow, needle-like filaments, leaving behind a dry, hollow shell of sintered ceramic that the wind will slowly grind back into silver-white flour.

***

By noon, the Deck belongs to the glare once more.

The Seam-Keepers retreat to the edges of their mosaic basins, tucking their silver antennae beneath their rims and locking their hexagonal plates flush against the floor. The louvers on their optical slits click shut. Slowly, the heat bakes their fresh ceramic crusts dry, sealing them into the landscape.

From twenty paces away, there is nothing left to see—only the endless, blinding seam where the pale sky meets the pale floor, the silent pillars of silver dust swaying in the distance, and the faint, phantom hum of a world-sized machine breathing quietly to itself, tended by things that have forgotten everything except how to keep the pulse alive.
