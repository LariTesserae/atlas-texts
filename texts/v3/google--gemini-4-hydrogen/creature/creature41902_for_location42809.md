At first, you assume the city is abandoned. A place this hollow, this stripped of walls and floors and shelter, looks like a monument left behind after the living things have moved on—a wind-harp playing to an empty room.

It takes an hour of standing on the jagged basalt, your teeth aching from the sub-bass, before you realize that the noise is not entirely weather.

Every few minutes, a single thread of the high, shrieking harmonic snaps off—not faded by a lull in the mist, but *cut*, clean as a plucked string stopped by a thumb. A moment later, the ribbon of translucent alloy arcing overhead ripples, and the faint shimmer of its transit current thickens into a bright, traveling knot. Something is riding it. Not *on* the ribbon, which is no wider than a fingernail, but suspended an inch beneath it, carried forward in the wake of the field like a seed in a riptide.

When it slows and drops down toward the spire nearest you, you finally see what lives here.

At a glance, you mistake it for a torn piece of the aero-flora—a tangle of green-black moss and pale membrane snagged on a pylon. Then it unfolds.

It is roughly the height of a tall human, though it weighs almost nothing, built on the same impossible rules of tension and negative space as the city itself. There is no solid torso, no heavy column of spine. Instead, it is an articulated truss of matte-black spars—slender as knitting needles, hollow, and faintly pitted like volcanic glass—joined together by knots of pale, glistening ligament. Between these spars are stretched vast, folded sails of translucent, pearlescent membrane, thin as gold leaf and veined with fine dark filaments.

It has no eyes, and no face in any sense you recognize. Where a head might sit, the upper spars converge into a delicate, crown-like basket of bone-white cartilage, packed with nested tuning-forks and feathery, constantly trembling cilia.

It never touches the ground. To a creature built of taut skin and hollow needles, the raw, unweathered stone below is a bed of knives. Instead, its long, multi-jointed limbs end in three-pronged hooks that never quite close on the metamaterial cables; they grip the invisible field-sheaths surrounding the wires, sliding along them with a frictionless, eerie grace. When it wants to cross the open air between two cathedral-sized rings, it doesn’t flap like the manta-creatures. It simply flares its membranes, catches the rising thermal, and *steps* from one standing-wave node to the next—finding purchase in the pockets of dense, vibrating air where the city’s hum locks into stillness.

Once you know what to look for, you see them everywhere, hanging like strange, geometric lanterns in the slate twilight.

They are the tuners of the valley, and to them, the dim, empty air is not empty at all. Having no eyes, they see by the city’s roar. Every manta’s metallic click, every strider’s scurry along a wire, every bead of warm mist condensing on a bladder-vine sends a ripple through the acoustic architecture of the basin. The inhabitants read those ripples through their feathered crowns and the taut tympana of their wings. Where you see a miles-wide void of bruised-indigo haze, they perceive a blindingly crisp, three-dimensional tapestry of pressure, pitch, and phase.

Because shouting is useless here, they speak with the city itself.

Watch two of them hanging half a kilometer apart on opposite sides of a hollow geometric shell. One presses a hooked limb against a tension-cable and *damps* it, swallowing a slice of the ambient chord; the other flares a single wing-fold, deflecting a warm updraft so that it strikes the shell’s rim at a sharper angle. They are carving words out of the noise—not adding sound to the storm, which would be lost in the din, but *sculpting* it. A conversation is a temporary architecture of interference patterns, rippling across a district in complex, braided chords that make the fluid in your inner ear slosh in rhythm.

And when two of them want privacy—a rare, intimate thing in a world where every vibration is public—they descend into the shelter of a smaller ring, pull their spars tight, and flare their membranes in exact opposition. Phase-cancellation. For a radius of three feet around them, the deafening roar of the valley simply vanishes, eaten by geometry. Inside that tiny, fragile bubble of dead silence, they press their cartilage crowns together, speaking through direct bone-conduction—a rapid, clicking purr that no other ear in the basin can overhear.

You begin to understand, too, why the stone beneath your boots is so young.

Old rock is soft. Weathered stone, rounded by rain and carpeted in soil, absorbs sound; it muffles a room. Freshly fractured mineral rings like a bell. The valley isn’t raw by accident. Every few cycles, when the mist clears and the thermal drafts run dry, a dozen of the inhabitants gather along the upper tiers of the city, linking their limbs to the great transit ribbons to form a living, parabolic array. They focus the basin’s scattered sub-bass into a single, downward lance of pressure—and shatter a fresh shelf of the valley floor, peeling back the dulled crust to expose new, razor-edged terraces of dark glass. They are acoustic gardeners, keeping the ground young so the valley stays bright.

Their lives are woven into the city’s ecology with the same unsentimental precision.

They don’t eat heavy food; their hollow frames couldn’t bear the weight of a full stomach. Instead, they feed as the aero-flora feeds, drifting through the lukewarm mist with their damp, velvety membranes spread wide to absorb airborne spores and mineral dust. When they need water, they don’t climb down to the mirror-flat pools in the sharp creases of the stone; they dangle from the lowest cables like spiders and unspool hair-thin, hollow proboscises down into the crevices, drinking without ever risking a foot on the glass.

They tend the gossamer moss-nets with long, serrated spurs, pruning away the heavy, waterlogged tangles before the added weight can pull a metamaterial filament out of pitch. They herd the drifting clouds of bioluminescent jellies into the hollow cathedral-shells—not for the sickly amber light, which means nothing to eyeless things, but because the jellies’ gas-bladders hold a dense, sluggish vapor. When a tuner punctures a dozen jellies inside a resonant shell, the pitch of the entire structure drops an octave, turning a harsh, shrieking chamber into a warm, booming hearth.

Around these lowered shells, they gather in clusters during the heavy mists. Not for fire—fire would thin the air and ruin the acoustics—but for the physical warmth of compressed sound. They furl their sails tightly against their spars and hang inside the booming interference-nodes, letting the intense vibration scrub the condensation and clinging parasites from their joints.

Even their young are born into the air. In the center of the smallest, most tightly tuned rings, you can see clusters of silver-veined seed-pods hanging motionless in mid-air, anchored to nothing at all—cradled in the standing-wave nodes like corks in a whirlpool. They rock gently in the hum, fed by the mist and shaped by a constant, low lullaby-chord that the adults maintain on the surrounding wires, slowly crystallizing their first hollow spars out of the mineral dust carried up on the warm drafts.

Only one thing breaks their poise.

Every so often, a sudden ripple of panic—a sharp, dissonant *slap* of damped cables—races through a district. The tuners furl their sails instantly, collapsing into tight, needle-like bundles pressed flat against the pylons, and go utterly still.

Overhead, gliding down through the miles of empty twilight, comes one of the eyeless predators—the sleek, heavy-skulled hunters with dish-shaped ears.

To you, it is just another animal in the gloom. To the inhabitants, it is a nightmare. It flies without whistling vents, without disturbing a single standing wave, stealing through the roaring air in a pocket of near-silence. In a world where everything that exists is made of noise, a creature that makes no sound is a moving hole in reality—a blind spot with teeth, listening for the one faint, panicked tremor of a spar against a wire.

When the predator passes, vanishing back into the indigo haze, the city holds its breath for a long, agonizing moment. Then a single tuner uncurls a wing-tip, touches a filament, and sends a low, tentative hum down the line. Another answers. Within seconds, the chords weave back together, the mist thickens along the sharp stone below, and the invisible city resumes its endless, deafening song.
