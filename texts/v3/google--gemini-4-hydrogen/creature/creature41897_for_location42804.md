If you stood in the middle of those stepped, roaring alleys—supposing anything of flesh and water could survive a breath of that kiln-hot air—you would swear the city was empty. There are no prints in the grey ash-drifts. Nothing scurries behind the shattered leaves when a gust slams through the doorways. The grey pumice rooms sit open and bare, their corners choked only with dust and the blackened stalks of tallow-reeds.

You would have to look up, into the soot-darkened undersides of the cantilevered balconies, or lean close to the screaming mouths of the wall-jars, to realize that the city is not abandoned at all. It has simply been inherited by something that has no use for floors.

At first glance, they look like part of the builders’ decaying handiwork—thick, frayed hanks of spun-mineral cord wedged into the mortar, or gnarled roots of resin-soaked fiber clinging to the ribs of the cavern. They are neither animal nor plant, belonging to no lineage that has ever known water. Where a beast would have bone, they have brittle, translucent silica; where a beast would have blood, they run on molten wax.

A full-grown one is roughly the length of a human forearm, though supple enough to coil twice inside a storage jar or stretch flat along a mortar-groove. Its body is a dense, braided tube of grey mineral-wool, saturated to the core with hot amber tallow so that it gleams with a dull, greasy warmth in the dimness. Along its back, it wears overlapping shingles of glass-hard silica, harvested from the dry avalanches of shed leaves in the streets and cemented onto its fibrous hide with cooling resin. Beneath, where it presses against the stone, lies a thick brush of fine, heat-proof bristles that grip the porous pumice with a dry, rasping tenacity.

They have no eyes—there is nothing here worth seeing in the thin needles of daylight—and no mouths that chew. Instead, the forward end of each creature tapers into a crown of three or four rigid, hollow silica quills, like fine glass needles or the tines of a tuning fork.

To watch them move is to watch something closer to a slow-creeping wick than a crawling beast. They never descend to the dusty alleys, where the loose ash offers no purchase and the drifting leaf-shards would slice their bristled undersides. Instead, they own the vertical world and the ceiling-planes. They inch along the undersides of the cantilevered balconies, thread themselves through the frayed lattice of the mineral-fiber awnings, and bridge the gaps between terraces on lines of their own spun silicate silk. When a violent thermal surge roars up from the floor-vents, they flatten themselves into the recessed joints of the brickwork or slip headfirst into the hollow clay jars set into the walls.

It is in those jars, and in the deafening voice of the city, that they truly live.

In a world without light or water, sound is the medium of space. The cavern is a hollow bell of stone, and the thermal vents below are its lungs. To an outsider, the roaring wind, the hooting jars, the snapping awnings, and the grinding stone vanes would be an unbearable, chaotic din. To the inhabitants, that noise is a map, a weather report, and a collective tongue—and they spend their long, hot lives tending it.

They are compulsive, blind musicians. Using pellets of baked ash and dabs of thick, slow-cooling wax, they constantly alter the mouths of the clay storage jars. One jar is narrowed with a fluted lip of hardened resin so that it shrieks a sharp, piercing note when the updraft hits; another is half-plugged with ash-mortar to drop its resonance into a low, chest-rattling thrum. Up on the balcony corners, they creep onto the spinning stone vanes, dripping hot tallow from their own saturated hides into the dry ceramic sockets to make certain vanes spin faster and sing higher, while wedging splinters of broken silica leaf into others to force them into a stuttering, clacking rhythm.

They do this not out of whim, but out of hunger. 

When the afternoon heat peaks and the swollen seed-pods on the upper balconies split open with sharp, echoing *cracks*, clouds of glittering golden spores are flung out into the central void. Left alone, the violent updrafts would whip that fertile dust straight up and out through the ceiling fissures. But by subtly shifting the pitch and rhythm of the perimeter walls, the inhabitants shape the air itself. Overlapping chords roll out from the hooting jars across the chasm, creating pockets of still, swirling eddy-currents along the lower ledges. Caught in those acoustic dead-zones, the golden spores settle in thick, rich drifts onto the terraced stairs—right where the copper-colored spore-felt grows thickest, and where the inhabitants can graze.

When they need fuel, they inch into the windowless alleys where the blackened-amber tallow-reeds crowd together. Pressing their hollow silica quills into the segmented stalks, they siphon the lead-hot liquid sap straight into their braided cores, replenishing the wax that keeps their fibers supple and their inner chemistry burning. For salts and sulfur, they flatten themselves against the copper spore-felt on the lower steps, wicking the metallic haze directly through their bristled undersides.

Even their birth is bound to the cavern’s violent breath. When one grows heavy with mineral salts and thick with old wax, it climbs to the shadowy upper ledges, away from the sharpest drafts. There, behind the clattering curtains of silica fronds, it weaves a tight, spherical cocoon of spun-mineral cord and seals it with glowing resin—looking, for all the world, like just another translucent sac swelling along the stone. Inside, it dissolves and re-braids itself into a dozen pale, finger-length knots of fiber.

They wait in the dark until the heat crests and the upper seed-pods begin to burst. The sharp percussive shockwaves of the splitting pods rattle the balconies and crack the hardened wax cocoons open. Out spill the young—glistening with fresh tallow, their tiny glass quills already trembling—immediately hooking into the pumice mortar and tuning themselves to the roar.

And when the great thermal surge finally ebbs, and the cavern draws a long, shuddering breath before the next cycle begins, a deep, lingering chord hums through the curving ribs of the city. In that brief, kiln-warm hush, hundreds of braided forms press their silica quills flat against the stone. They do not hear it as an empty ruin. Through the dying vibration of the pumice, they feel the exact weight of every brick, the tension of every awning, and the slow, resinous pulse of every other living wick clinging to the dark across the void.
