At first, you think the chamber’s only minds are the blind, frantic appetites in the water. Then your eyes adjust to the strobing blue-white flashes, and you realize that some of the static arcs are too neat.

They aren’t jumping at random across the weeping rock. On the far wall, just above the splash of the basin, thin, deliberate trails of dull copper flakes and shattered pale quartz have been pressed into the wet seams, bridging the gaps between the magnetite veins. They lead upward, like faint, sparking veins of their own, toward a dry-ish hollow tucked beneath the jagged vault.

And the *HISS*—when you stop bracing against the *THUMP* and actually listen to the air screaming out of the hairline vents, you notice that the vents around that upper hollow don’t scream. They hum. Someone, or something, has wedged tiny splinters of quartz and the empty, calcified shells of tube-worms into the cracks, tuning the escaping pressure into a low, three-note chord that rises and dies every four seconds.

In the next flash of blue light, you see what lives there.

At first it looks like a hump of wet peat or a particularly thick cushion of the dark-emerald liverwort. It is crouched on a steaming mat of moss that has been deliberately wired into a weeping copper seam with braided, mineral-crusted roots. The mat is actually warm; thin wisps of vapor curl off it into the biting air, and the creature’s long, folded limbs are pressed deep into the greenery to drink the heat.

When the *THUMP* hits, it unfolds—just a fraction, timed so precisely to the deafening impact of the basalt slabs that your eyes almost miss the shift.

It is roughly the size of a curled-up lynx, though when a narrow forearm reaches out to adjust a copper flake, the limb seems impossibly long and multi-jointed, built for squeezing through flooded, razor-edged cracks. Its skin is hairless and slick with a heavy, insulating oil, mottled the color of wet slate and pale tallow, stained a dull verdigris along the shoulders and knuckles where it has pressed itself against the raw copper seams.

It has no eyes—only two smooth, slightly sunken membranes beneath a narrow, wedge-shaped brow, faint and milky in the gray blade of daylight. Instead of sight, it wears a sparse fringe of fine, dark filaments along its jawline and the backs of its wrists. Even in the brief silence between beats, those filaments never stop trembling, tasting the ozone and reading every invisible ripple in the chamber’s electric field.

It isn’t native to this room; nothing could be, in rock this freshly torn. It came up from the older, drowned labyrinth beneath the peat, following the surging groundwater when the earth settled and split open this new, raw pocket. Down in the deep aquifer, it would have lived in total, muffled dark. Up here, it has found something astonishing: an open-air foundry where stone grinds on stone, where the currents aren’t damped by endless water, and where the cold air itself crackles with stray power.

It has made itself the keeper of the circuit.

Watch it through three beats of the chamber, and you see how completely it has surrendered to the four-second clock.

On the *THUMP*, it strikes. One long, iron-nailed hand darts down into the shallow pool below its ledge, snatching a pale salamander that drifted too close to a grounding point—the creature’s movement completely swallowed by the roar of the bedrock. During the *HISS*, it retreats to its warm moss, cracking the salamander’s spine with flat, heavy back teeth, or using the edge of a loose quartz splinter to pry the pyrite-crusted shell off a grazer it caught an hour ago. In the brief, breathless pause before the next surge, it sits utterly still, its ribs expanding in a single, slow draw that matches the pressure building under the floor.

Its hands are never idle for long. With patient, calloused fingers, it weeds the dead, blackened patches out of its liverwort bed and presses fresh, wet fronds onto the warm seams, cultivating its own living blanket. It collects the brightest flakes of native copper washed out by the groundwater and lays them in careful paths to guide the static arcs away from its sleeping hollow and down into the pools—herding the blind amphipods toward the grounding points so the salamanders will follow, and bringing the hunt right to the base of its wall.

And it knows you are here.

To a creature that reads the world through electricity, you are not a shadow in the dim light; you are a massive, wet, clumsy disruption standing in the middle of the circuit, bending the field around your boots.

As the next *THUMP* rattles your teeth, the creature turns its smooth, eyeless face directly toward you. Its jaw filaments flare wide, quivering in the ozone-thick air. It doesn’t hiss or bare its teeth. Instead, during the screaming rush of the *HISS*, it reaches out one long, green-stained finger and nudges a single wet flake of copper across a weeping magnetite seam.

A thin, blue-white arc snaps across the basin, grounding into the pool at your feet, and a sharp, unmistakable tingle shoots straight up through the soles of your boots—a quiet, measured tap on the wire, just to see how you answer.
