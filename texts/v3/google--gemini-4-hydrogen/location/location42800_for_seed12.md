Miles above the lightless abyssal plains, a broad volcanic tableland has thrust itself upward—not high enough to break the surface, yet high enough to sit in the warm, dim upper reaches of a vast, stagnant sea. Here, the water is the temperature of a long-drawn bath, heavy with dissolved silica and the faint, metallic tang of fresh stone. Looking up offers no sky, only a bruised-amber ceiling of thick, drifting spore-clouds and the tangled undersides of floating root-mats far above, filtering the daylight down to a permanent, murky twilight.

The plateau itself is geologically newborn. Nothing here has been rounded by time or current. The terrain is a sharp, stepped valley of raw, unweathered basalt and glassy obsidian lobes that have cooled so recently their edges can still slice skin. In the deeper creases of the rock, pale mineral crusts precipitate directly out of the warm water, forming brittle, crystalline shelves that look like frozen foam. Here and there, a seam in the valley floor still oozes a sluggish, heat-shimmering bead of stone, cooling silently in the dimness with an occasional, muted *tick* as the glass fractures from within.

Out of this sharp, un-eroded cradle grows a dense, layered jungle that knows nothing of wind. Vegetation thrives in the steady warmth, thickening into motionless curtains that fill the water column from the jagged floor up into the gloom. Broad, leathery fronds—the color of bruised plums, wet slate, and pale bone—hang vertically, anchored by tough, fibrous holdfasts that wedge into the fresh cracks of the basalt. Interspersed among them are clusters of translucent, amber-veined gas bladders, some the size of a fist, others as large as a hut, tugging gently upward on their braided stems like tethered moons.

Across the breadth of the valley, woven directly into this suspended forest and anchored to the raw stone steps below, lies a sprawling, multi-tiered settlement. It is an urban landscape built entirely without mortar, saws, or smelted iron. Every structure is a patient exercise in wedging, lashing, and counterweight. On the lower terraces, flat plates of unworked volcanic slate are dry-stacked into dense, honeycombed retaining walls and low, windowless chambers, their corners bound tight with soaked and shrunken sinew. Above them, the town takes to the water column itself. Thick cables braided from stripped kelp-fiber span the valley between the sharp rock ridges, supporting hanging neighborhoods of woven reed-pods, netted platforms, and inverted dome-tents stitched from oiled bladder-skins. These upper tiers do not rest on pillars; they are buoyed from above by harvested clusters of living gas-bladders, hovering in the dim water like a dense, tangled canopy of lanterns.

The tools of this place are simple and intimate: chipped obsidian knives, bone needles, stone hammers, and the occasional rare wedge of native copper hammered cold from a raw vein. Because the water softens weight, massive slabs of stone can be maneuvered into place with levers of dense, waterlogged root-wood and patient, collective effort. Under the stitched bladder-domes, rising warm gas from the cooling rock seams collects in trapped, shimmering pockets—dry, humid spaces where fibers are cured, cordage is braided, and small, slow crafts are worked away from the drift of the water.

For all its architectural density, the valley feels hushed and strangely uncrowded. Wild fauna is scarce; there are no darting schools of silver fish, no chattering reefs. Only rarely does a pale, slow-moving grazer drift through the hanging gardens, scraping mineral film from the stone with a rasp-like mouth before vanishing into the amber haze. Even the builders themselves are seldom seen in numbers, moving with an unhurried, deliberate economy that matches the pace of the water.

Sound travels strangely here—muffled, low, and distant. The loudest noise in an entire afternoon might be the dull *clack* of a stone-knapper shaping a fresh blade on a lower terrace, the faint *creak* of a braided fiber bridge shifting under a load, or the soft, resonant *bloop* of a warm gas bubble breaking free from a rock seam and beginning its long, slow ascent toward the unseen surface. Everything in this drowned highland operates on a patient clock: the rock still emerging from beneath, the moss creeping millimeter by millimeter over razor-sharp glass, and the suspended city swaying almost imperceptibly in the warm, dim, eternal stillness.
