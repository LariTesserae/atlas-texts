The basin lies low and flat, a broad, shallow depression stretching for miles under a clear, ordinary sky. The light is bright and warm, the kind of steady, unfiltered daylight that makes every edge crisp. Yet the air holds no moisture—not even the memory of humidity. Nothing here is damp, nothing is muddy, and nothing rots.

The ground beneath the flora is young and sharp, as if the crust split open recently and never felt a drop of rain or a scouring wind. There is no soil, only raw, freshly cleaved slabs of dark basaltic glass, pale silica-slate, and jagged ridges of dry mineral no higher than a waist. Every fracture has a razor edge. In the narrow crevices between the slabs, dry, talc-fine mineral dust sits undisturbed, never blowing away because the warm air is entirely still.

Out of these bone-dry cracks, the vegetation rises in a dense, layered, near-touching tangle. It is lush in a way that has nothing to do with water. Broad, overlapping fans of translucent mica-leaf—thin as hammered gold and warm to the touch—spread out in tight canopies, veined with fine, copper-colored filaments that hum faintly when sunlight hits them. Beneath them, shoulder-high thickets of hollow, spun-silica reeds stand packed so closely they form walls of pale jade and rust, while the sharp stone below is carpeted in thick, cushiony mats of dry, fibrous growth that feels like warm, static-charged felt. Nothing wilts. The plants run on a solid-state chemistry, drinking the bright light directly through microscopic lattice structures grown into their leaves, turning photons and trace mineral dust into rigid, papery tissue.

There are no walls, no roads, no towers, no carved stones—nothing built or placed by hand. Yet the landscape itself behaves like an instrument whose casing was never made, or whose engineering lives entirely beneath the scale of sight. If you drop a pinch of mineral dust, it does not scatter; it drifts down along invisible, mathematically exact contour lines, pausing for a heartbeat in mid-air before settling into the crevices. The fractures in the bedrock repeat in subtle, non-repeating quasi-crystalline patterns that hold the entire valley in a locked thermal equilibrium. It is always this warm, always this bright, always this still.

Because the equilibrium is so complete, the fractures in reality do not flicker or roar; they simply sit there, settled into the scenery like furniture.

A stand of pale-jade reeds a hundred yards away clearly overlaps the jagged silhouette of a ridge a mile behind it, yet both are sharp and solid under the same noon light. Shadows cast by the dense thickets lie at slightly divergent angles—not because there are two suns, but because light takes a quietly crooked path around certain clusters of stone. In the deeper tangles, two separate groves of glassy-stemmed shrubs occupy the exact same patch of ground; their branches pass cleanly through one another’s volume without touching, like two solid projections layered in the same air. High in the papery canopy, a single dry leaf that has snapped from its stem rests motionless three inches *above* its branch, caught in a small, permanent pocket where gravity points sideways.

The valley is mostly quiet. There is no wind to rustle the mica-leaves, no water to trickle, no insects to drone in the heat. The silence is thick and dry, broken only now and then by the sharp, brittle *tink* of a silica seed-pod splitting from internal tension, or the faint, sub-audible thrumming you can feel in your teeth if you press your palm flat against the warm bedrock.

Animals are rare, and those that live here move with the careful economy of things trying not to wake a sleeping room. Every so often, a pale, multi-jointed lizard with translucent, heat-radiating frills slips through the overlapping thickets, or an articulated, dry-plated beetle picks its way across the razor-sharp slate, grazing on the static charge of the fibrous moss. When one of them crosses a spatial seam between the rocks, its body briefly stretches like a reflection in still glass—appearing for an instant in two places at once—before snapping back into a single, unbothered form on the other side, vanishing into the bright, unmoving, impossible green.
