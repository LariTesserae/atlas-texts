Four paces across, three paces high, and sealed beneath a crust of unweathered basalt, the pocket looks at first like a quarry seam cracked open at high noon.

There is no ceiling gap, no skylight, no shaft leading upward. Yet the air inside is fierce with light—clean, white, shadowless daylight that seems to inhabit the volume of the room rather than travel from a source. You can look directly at the sharpest corners of the rock and find no dark crease, no recessed gloom. The illumination belongs to the stone itself, dispersed at a scale too fine for any lens or grain to betray: a quiet, ambient photonic engineering woven into the bedrock long before this particular fracture ever opened, leaving no seams, no filaments, and no trace of craft.

Because the fracture is young, every surface is abrupt and unforgiving. The walls are raw, stepped planes of pale silicate and dark, glassy stone, their edges still keen enough to nick skin. Nothing has been smoothed by wind or tumbled by current. In the center of the floor, a shallow, jagged basin holds clear water no deeper than an ankle.

The heat is immediate and heavy, pressing against the lungs like the air inside a kiln just after the bricks are drawn. Yet the rock is wet. Water weeps steadily from hair-thin fissures high on the walls, gathering in thick, trembling beads that refuse to behave quite like ordinary condensation. Driven by an invisible, frictionless pull through the mineral lattice, the moisture creeps sideways along razor-sharp ledges and clings in taut, glassy sheets to vertical faces before finally breaking free.

When a drop falls onto the hotter lower shelves, it flattens with a brief, soft *tiss* and vanishes into a shimmer of vapor. When it falls into the central basin, it lands with a single, bright *plink* that carries cleanly across the small space. Otherwise, the pocket is mostly quiet—just the rare, sharp *tick* of fresh stone adjusting to the heat, and the faint, steady hum of a closed weather cycle turning over in miniature: weeping, pooling, rising as invisible vapor, and condensing again along the upper seams.

No plants grow here. There is no moss on the wettest ledges, no fern or root or green stain. In the dampest, slightly cooler crevices near the water’s edge, a sparse, chalky-orange film clings to the stone—half mineral deposit, half simple, heat-loving microbe, scattered thinly enough that most of the rock remains bare and bright.

If you stay still and let your eyes adjust to the glare, you may spot one or two of the pocket’s only other inhabitants. Small, flat-bodied, pale-amber arthropods—blind, segmented, and no longer than a thumbnail—move unhurriedly along the submerged lip of the basin, grazing on the thin orange film. Occasionally one darts sideways across the warm water, its shadowless silhouette crisp against the white stone floor, before tucking itself back beneath an overhang.

There is nothing eerie about the place, for all the impossible physics keeping its light ablaze and its water circulating beneath tons of rock. It has the plain, matter-of-fact presence of a sun-baked puddle on a summer ledge—just a small, hot, brilliantly lit room of fresh stone, quietly working through its own tight rhythm, undisturbed and unvisited.
