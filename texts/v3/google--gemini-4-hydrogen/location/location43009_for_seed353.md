Up here, the air is thin and dry enough to crack the skin, yet it holds a heavy, oven-like heat that never breaks. You are high on a broad, mountain-rimmed shelf, miles across, though you cannot see the sky or the distant peaks. Overhead, a seamless, lightless ceiling of interlocking charcoal-barked plates seals the entire valley in a permanent, stifling dark.

There is no stone beneath your feet, no dirt, and no water—not a drop of dew, not a trace of dampness in the air. Everything is plant, and everything is built. 

For miles in every direction, the basin is packed wall-to-wall with an ancient, industrial forest-city that has long since forgotten the difference between growing and being constructed. The oldest parts of the complex—the massive retaining terraces, the sunken press-yards, the broad, terraced ridges that divide the valley into districts—have been worn down by centuries of scorching, dust-laden drafts. They are settled into smooth, almost geological forms: flattened mesas of petrified heartwood and wind-planed, vitrified resin, striated like sandstone and stained black with age. 

Yet wedged into every crevice, spanning every canyon between the ancient hulks, and stacked in dense, claustrophobic tiers from the floor to the dark canopy, the newer works are hyperactively alive. There is no empty space. Every cubic yard is occupied by rectilinear lattices of cured cane, hexagonal boiler-bulbs, taut vine-belting, and miles of swollen, oil-slicked phloem-pipes. 

The darkness is nearly absolute, broken only by the dull, ember-red glow of overheated friction-bearings, the occasional sharp blue snap of static discharge along copper-veined taproots, and the faint, feverish amber of resin-vats simmering deep inside translucent gourd-wood housings. By that dim, shifting light, you can see that the whole valley is in constant, restless motion.

Huge flywheels grown from dense, iron-hard burl-wood spin silently on oiled pith-axles. Reciprocating pistons of fibrous stem-matter slide smoothly in lubricated bark sleeves, pumping thick, volatile mineral oils and dry, pulverized seed-meal through elevated conduits. Overhead, suspended conveyor-vines twitch and haul baskets of carbonized husks across the dark, their pathways crossing and recrossing in a dense, automated web. Nothing here uses metal; nothing here uses water. The machinery runs on geothermal heat drawn up through deep, heat-shielded taproots, static electricity harvested from the dry wind, and the sheer, pressurized tension of resinous sap.

Despite the relentless, anthill-like industry, the valley is strangely quiet. The thin, high-altitude air carries sound poorly, and every wall, pipe, and strut is wrapped in thick, fibrous matting of deadened pith and root-felt. The clatter of a normal factory is replaced by a soft, pervasive hush—a dry, rhythmic *shush-shush* of wood sliding on oiled wood, the rapid, insect-like *ticking* of wooden pressure-valves, and a low, felt-muffled thrum that vibrates through the soles of your boots.

And the stillness is deceptive, because the place is deeply unstable. The heat and mechanical stress are too high; the system is constantly tearing itself apart and rebuilding on the fly. A sudden, muffled *crack* echoes from a nearby tier as a dry-rotted gantry buckles under the torque of a drive-belt, dropping a tangle of pipes into the lower yards. Before the dust has even settled, fast-growing weaver-vines surge out of the surrounding walls like pale, blind tendrils. Within minutes, they are wrapping the wreckage, splicing severed belts, sealing ruptured oil-ducts with quick-hardening gum, and scaffolding new, rectilinear frames over the rubble.

Dry, silent whirlwinds of thermal air corkscrew through the narrow gaps between the towers, carrying thick clouds of ash, carbon-fluff, and millions of papery, winged seeds. There are no birds to catch them, no beetles to bore into the wood, no rats in the conduits. Animal life has no purchase in this parched, airless-feeling heat. There is only the vast, blind, botanical engine—ancient, eroded, and furiously busy in the dark, endlessly consuming its own wreckage to keep the wheels turning.
