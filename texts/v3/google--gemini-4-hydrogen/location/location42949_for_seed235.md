Miles above a featureless floor of white cloud, a city hangs wedged between jagged limestone needles and sheer, terraced precipices. It is open to the sky—vast enough to span an entire high-altitude valley, with avenues, stepped plazas, and multi-tiered bridges stretching across chasms until they blur into the heat-shimmer of the far ridges.

The sun is bright, clear, and ordinary, hanging in a pale blue zenith, yet the air here is scalding. Heat radiates from the pale stone and rises in dense, invisible plumes from the abyssal drop-offs below, trapping the whole basin in a permanent, breathless sauna. Everything is slick. Moisture condenses directly out of the heavy air onto every vertical surface, so the walls weep continuously in thin, glittering sheets. Shallow water sheets across the paved terraces, and broad, fluted aqueducts carry fast-moving, crystal-clear currents between the upper tiers.

There is no metal here, no mortar, no mark of a chisel or saw. The city was not built so much as accreted—a dense, layered metropolis of calcified root-buttresses, fused woody lattices, and mineral-encrusted travertine. Colonnades of pale, ribbed trunks rise three and four stories high, their upper branches knitting together into vaulted arcades and load-bearing arches. Between them, thick carpets of emerald moss, broad waxy leaves, and trailing curtains of epiphytes crowd every balcony and stairwell, pressing in until the streets feel narrow and saturated with green. The vegetation is impossibly lush, turgid with water, yet nothing drops a dead leaf. There is no rot, no mulch, no new shoots unfurling. Every frond and vine sits in a state of perfected, unchanging fullness.

Nothing moves on its own. No birds ride the rising thermals; no insects skim the standing pools; no lizards cling to the hot, wet stone. The avenues are entirely empty of animal life, yet the place is deafening.

The noise is a physical weight—a continuous, unvarying roar that vibrates in the teeth and behind the sternum. Thousands of cascades plunge off the sheer outer edges of the terraces, their water hitting lower basins and exploding into fine, hot mist. Worse are the towers themselves: hollow, porous, and pierced by millions of narrow flutes and windows, they act as colossal organ pipes for the superheated updrafts screaming up from the cloud-floor below. The entire valley hums in a dense, multi-layered chord of rushing water and resonant wind—a wall of sound that never swells, never breaks, and never drops by a single decibel.

If you watch the water long enough, the rules of the valley begin to slip. A waterfall pouring off a fourth-tier viaduct does not vanish into the abyss; halfway down, the white ribbon bends smoothly backward, curving upward against the cliff face to empty into a cistern two stories *above* its starting point. In the sunken plazas, ripples from falling droplets freeze mid-ring, locking into crisp geometric lattices on the surface of the water before simply ceasing to be, replaced by the next droplet falling in the exact same spot. 

The geometry of the city holds together only from a distance. Up close, stairways climb toward a bridge that passes underneath the street you just left, and colonnades cast crisp, midday shadows that lean slightly toward the sun rather than away from it. Where the standing pools mirror the sky, they do not show the pale blue zenith or the bright sun; they show the undersides of the root-arches, glistening and wet, suspended over an endless, sunlit drop.

Nothing ages here. The stone is worn smooth and rounded along the water channels—settled into an ancient, established balance—and it will wear no further. The heat does not peak or cool. The light does not shift. Locked in a roaring, steaming, noon-lit equilibrium, the city simply persists, pouring its water in closed loops through its empty, overgrown streets forever.
