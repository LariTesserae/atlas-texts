I would find myself neither buried in the dead silt of the foundations nor drifting in the freezing dark above the chimneys, but suspended in the middle height of the city—tucked into the underside of a calcified arch where two towers lean together across a narrow alley.

There is no light here, and I have no eyes to miss it. My world begins where my skin meets the water, and where my weightless body meets the stone.

I am a **Listener-Squid**.

At rest, I am barely separate from the city at all. My mantle is draped over the ribbed underside of the buttress, and by flexing thousands of tiny muscular papillae across my back, I have raised my skin into ridges and pits that match the calcified mucus beneath me. When a stray eddy curls up from the avenue, it washes over my flanks as if I were just another knuckle of old aragonite. To the lateral line of a passing fish, or the sweeping bristles of a crab, I am simply part of the wall.

From this perch, the city is not a thing to be seen; it is a thing that hums.

My head is framed by two broad, dish-shaped pits where eyes might once have been, and through them the darkness resolves into a map of motion and weight. Every creature here writes its shape into the water. Ten stories below, on the smoothed limestone plaza, I can feel the dry, rhythmic *shhh-tick* of Silt-Coins gliding through the flocculent dust, their fringed edges sweeping the floor for fallen scraps. Closer, woven into the porous mortar-joints of the tower beside me, a pair of Thread-Eels have met in a shared crack. They do not bite; instead, I feel the stone beneath my tentacles begin to buzz—a rapid, high-frequency shudder as they press their slick flanks together, arguing in vibration until the smaller one backs down into the dark.

And everywhere, constant as blood, is the *thrum* of the Masons.

During the ebb, when the thermal vents below quiet down and the water cools by a fraction of a degree, the Masons withdraw into their spiraled shells. Even then, they never truly stop. I feel the steady, velvety pulse of water being drawn through hundreds of crescent siphons along the tower walls, vibrating up through the fused limestone like a slow, collective heartbeat.

Every few minutes, a different kind of pressure rolls down from above—a heavy, sub-audible *whoomp* that flattens the eddies and presses me gently firmer against the arch. It is the wake of the Abyssal Manta, circling endlessly in the boundary layer above the fluted chimneys. It never comes down into the streets, and I will never touch it, yet its passing reaches all the way to the silt-drifts, lifting a slow veil of dust from the ledges before letting it settle back down.

I wait through the ebb, anchored by two arms to the rough stone, letting the other six hang loose in the current to taste the water. It tastes of old lime, faint sulfur, and the clean, metallic mineral weight of the deep ocean.

Then, the city’s breath changes.

I feel it first in the stone—a deep, low tremor rising from the basalt bed, traveling up through ten stories of discarded carapace and petrified shell into the skin of my mantle. A moment later, the thermal plume hits from below.

The water warms instantly, growing thick and syrupy with dissolved calcium and sulfur. The Masons along the towers unfurl, their pale, pleated bodies stretching out from their alcoves into the rising heat. I can feel the sudden shift in the current as hundreds of fleshy hoods open, drinking the mineral-rich surge and weeping fresh, pearlescent cement onto the rims of their shells.

Above the plazas, the Plume-Shrimp boil out of the crevices. To my dish-shaped pits, they are a sudden, rustling static—tens of thousands of rice-sized bodies treading water in the warm updraft, combing the current until the entire avenue hums with their tiny, frantic legs.

The surge wakes the hunters, too. Below me, a gaunt Vane-Fish drifts into the alley, letting the warm current carry it past the alcoves with its jaws unhinged. I cannot see the pale-blue spark it flashes from the gland beneath its chin, but I feel the result a fraction of a second later: a sharp, panicked *twitch* in the silt three yards down, followed by the sudden, violent displacement of water as the Vane-Fish snaps its trap shut.

I do not use sparks. I only listen.

Unclenching my grip on the arch, I let the rising warmth lift me off the stone, smoothing my skin into glass so the water slips cleanly past my flanks. I hang motionless in the throat of the alley, reading the complex geometry of the plume—the steady *thrum* of the Masons, the static of the shrimp, the slight, rhythmic *tick* of a crab’s claw against a lower ledge.

Near the mortar-joint beside me, a Thread-Eel has drifted too far out of its tunnel, its feathery barbels tasting the rich updraft for stray shrimp. I feel the tiny electrical flutter of its snout, the slow, sinuous wave of its ribbon-like body in the warm current.

I do not jet forward. I simply uncoil two long tentacles into the dark, guided by the displaced water, and pluck it cleanly from the edge of the plume.

By the time I drift back to the underside of my arch, the thermal tide is already beginning to ebb. The water cools; the silt begins its slow, silent descent onto the ledges; the Masons pull back into their spiral anchors. I press my mantle once more against the ribbed limestone, raise my skin into ridges that match the stone, and settle back into the quiet, tireless dark.
