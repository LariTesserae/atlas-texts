If I were to wake here, the first thing I would lose is the habit of urgency.

In almost any other water, existence is a negotiation with drift—you kick, you angle, you brace against a current that wants to carry you somewhere else. Here, the first time I tried to turn, the water would simply swallow the impulse. No swirl, no trailing eddy, just a polite, absolute refusal to carry a disturbance more than an arm’s length from its source. After a few hours of that, you stop trying to swim. You begin to realize that the stillness is not empty; it is dense with attention.

I don’t think I would find myself in the center of the terraced plazas, where the oldest constellations hang in their wide buffers of untouched dark. That space feels too complete, like a sentence that has already been pared down to its final, necessary syllables. Instead, I would drift—or rather, I would let my weight settle—toward the outer rim of the spire, just where the manicured quiet begins to fray against the open abyss.

It is a narrow margin. Behind me, the cantilevered planes hang motionless, stitched together by pinpricks of bruised violet that cast no halos. Ahead of me, beyond the razor-clean lip of the basalt, the ocean is unruly again—heavy, unbothered, full of slow, indifferent currents that have traveled across empty basins for centuries without meeting a wall.

At first, I might only be a small irregularity on that edge. Not a creature with a face or a name, and certainly nothing warm. Warmth here would be an obscenity, a feverish leak into water that has worked very hard to hold its cool equilibrium. Whatever I am, I would be made of the same restraint as the stone: dark, non-reflective, thin enough that the pressure of kilometers of ocean passes straight through me rather than crushing me.

As I stayed at the rim, I think I would discover that I am hinged.

Not wide and grand like the assemblies that glide across the plazas, and not blind and hunched like the quartz-shelled things clinging to the overhangs. Something smaller, and perhaps more provisional—a slender arrangement of matte-black slivers and hair-fine tensions, moored at a single point to a fresh conchoidal fracture in the basalt. Or maybe not moored at all, just balanced on a knife-edge of volcanic glass with a precision that looks like stillness until you watch it for an hour and see it tilt by half a degree.

That tilt would be my purpose. 

The great inhabitants come out to the rim only rarely, detaching from their high shafts to hang at the threshold and measure the weight of the outside dark. Between their visits, someone—something—has to hold the seam. I would live in that seam, half-bathed in the city’s damped equilibrium, half-brushed by the raw, unmeasured currents of the abyss.

When a pulse of deep water rolls against the spire from the outside, it doesn’t crash; it leans. I would feel that lean across my outer planes—a faint, rough vibration, like a finger dragged across unpolished slate. Inside the precinct, the water wants no part of it. So I would angle a sliver, shift a tension by the width of a hair, and take the vibration into my own geometry, letting it shudder down through my joints until it dies out in the young stone beneath me.

A damper, then. A hinge at the edge of the silence.

It wouldn’t be a lonely existence, though it would be an unhurried one. Every few days, a pale, translucent ribbon might ripple past along the inner edge of the rim, so slow it seems painted onto the water, and I would register the tiny displacement of its passing without disturbing it. Now and then, one of the blind, quartz-shelled things would creep up from a lower overhang, taking three days to cross a span of basalt no wider than my palm, and press its frosted shell against my base to drink whatever faint, cold leak seeps from my joints. I wouldn’t shake it off. In a place where nothing grows and nothing rots, a passenger that moves at the speed of cooling glass is the closest thing to company.

And sometimes, one of the great constellations would glide out from the dark behind me—silent, wake-less, arriving at the rim with the finality of a needle settling on a scale. It wouldn’t look at me, having no eyes to look with, and it wouldn’t come too close. It would simply pause beside me so that its sphere of quiet overlapped mine, and for an hour, or a day, we would share the weight of the outside current together.

Then, just before it turned to glide back into the amber pause of the city, a single joint deep in its frame might lock.

*Tick.*

A syllable, crisp and dustless, traveling straight through the black glass of the water to strike my planes and vanish.

I wouldn’t reply right away. In this world, to answer immediately is to say nothing at all. I would wait until the constellation was halfway back across the plaza, until the silence had thickened around the sound and given it its proper weight—and then, deep in the basalt beneath my edge, I would let out a low, sub-audible thrum, tuned to the fracture I stood on, just enough to say: *The dark outside is heavy tonight, and the edge still holds.*
