It arrives as light does, at first—not out of cruelty, but out of habit.

Far above, where the sky is a shifting ceiling of storm and glare, the power gathers its attention and bends it downward. Through miles of sunlit shallows, through the twilight zone where shapes dissolve into silver, down past the last stray threads of warmth, it sinks toward the drowned cordillera. It comes ready to mend, because mending is what benevolence usually looks like from above. It comes carrying the old, familiar mercies: a spark of heat to push back the chill, a flare of clarity to pierce the dark, a seed of something new to break a long stagnation.

Then the sound reaches it, and it stops.

The roar of the basin rises to meet the power before a single arch is visible—a vast, cavernous chord pressing up through the water. The organ-pipe booming of the bellows-swimmers, the dry *clack-clack-clack* of the snapping arches, the ceaseless *shhhk-shhhk* of a million rasping mandibles. It is not the sound of a ruin groaning under its own weight. It is the sound of a room that is full, and tuned, and awake.

The power holds its light back, folded tight within itself, and instead of looking, it listens.

In the listening, the temptation of pity falls away.

It feels the slow, heavy displacement of a swimmer gliding between the viaducts, trailing its faint string of blue sparks. In that brief wash of bioluminescence, the power sees the braided kelp-timbers, worn smooth as driftwood by millennia of cold currents. It sees the bruised-plum turf, the monumental plazas balanced toward open water, the sheer drop-offs falling away into the abyss. And it sees the builder.

At first, even to an ancient gaze, the creature seems no different from a root-buttress. Then the power senses the slow thrum inside that heavy chest—slower than a heartbeat, timed to the passing bass notes of the swimmers. It watches a pale grazer crawl from a pillar onto the builder’s slate-grey shoulder, rasping methodically at the microscopic film there. It watches the long, spatulate hand rise, slow as a drifting frond, only to lift the grazer gently away from the sensitive acoustic grooves along the jaw and set it back upon the wall.

The power understands, all at once, what would happen if it offered its usual gifts.

If it brought warmth here, the pickled stasis of the highland would break. The cold-water moss would rot; the ancient, lignified kelp-beams would soften and slump; the slow-blooded swimmers would sicken in water that moved too fast.

If it brought light—even the gentle, golden light of a summer afternoon—it would be an intrusion worse than darkness. It would wake dormant things that do not belong here, clouding the clear black water with algae blooms until the acoustic shadows blurred and went muddy. And to the builders, who have no eyes and no need for them, light would be nothing at all—or worse, a strange, silent pressure they could not read.

And if it left a blessing—a glowing stone upon a plaza, a new song taught to the snapping things in the arches, a sudden burst of fresh eelgrass to show that someone above remembered them—it knows what the builders would do.

They would rise from the velvety turf in their heavy, deliberate way. They would pinch off the new shoots with thumb and forefinger and feed them to the grazers. Two or three of them would converge across the plaza without a word, gather the glowing stone in their broad, calloused hands, heave it slowly to the edge of the cordillera, and tip it silently over the sheer ledge into the mile-deep blackness below.

Because here, the only threat is clutter. Because this work is finished.

So the power does nothing to the valley.

It does not announce itself. To speak into a chamber this finely tuned would be to introduce a question that has no answer, a yearning where there was only completion. It does not touch the builders’ eyeless brows to grant them sight, nor quicken their slow, cold blood. To rescue them from a peace they grew with their own bare limbs would be to demote them from authors to dependents.

Instead, the power withdraws its presence from the basin entirely, leaving no ripple in the water, no stray note in the roar.

It moves outward, past the sheer ledges of the highland, down into the mile-deep blackness where the scavengers wait, and further still—into the unseen basalt spine of the mountain itself, miles beneath the deepest holdfasts. There, in the blind, crushing dark where the builders’ spatulate hands can never reach, it listens to the earth’s slower rhythms. It feels the distant, inevitable drift of deep-ocean currents that might one day bring a degree too much warmth, and the faint, hair-thin stress in the stone that, in a few thousand years, might crack a ridge.

With a touch too quiet to register as an event, it turns the stray current aside, back toward the abyssal plains. It eases the tension in the deep rock—not freezing the world in amber forever, for even mountains must eventually have their way, but granting the highland the full measure of its stillness, undisturbed from without.

Then it rises back through the miles of water, back toward the storms and the glare, leaving the drowned plateau untouched.

Down in the dark, a builder rises from a meadow of dark-olive moss, stirring no silt. It walks the bottom in long, buoyant steps toward a sweeping viaduct, pausing every few paces to tilt its head as a bellows-swimmer pulses past, letting the low bass note wash through the hollow chambers of its chest. Its fingers brush a thousand-year-old graft, testing the tension.

Everything holds. Everything is loud, and cold, and whole. And the valley never knows it was watched, or kept, or loved enough to be left alone.
