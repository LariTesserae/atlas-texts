To look upon such a place without disturbing it requires the first discipline of wisdom: to arrive without weight.

A power that came here wrapped in light would blind nothing—the shelf’s largest inhabitant has no eyes—but light carries heat, and here a single degree is the hinge upon which the world turns. A power that came with wind would scatter the ghostly ribbons of vapor before they could reach the overhangs, starving the basins. Even a whisper of awe, if it stirred the air, would chill the weeping veins too soon.

So the attention settles over the terrace the way the afternoon shadow does: slowly, taking the exact temperature of the basalt, asking nothing of the stone.

It watches the small, closed loop turn.

It hears the *tock* of heavy drops falling from the crags, the dry *tick* of the silica reeds flexing in the rising thermals, the sub-audible hum of the matte-gray disc vibrating through the basin floor. It sees the thumb-sized amphibian pulsing quietly along a submerged ledge, and the pale crustacean scraping its slow, patient harvest from a bone-white strut.

And when the shadows lengthen by that single, critical fraction of a degree, it watches the slumped buttress near the back wall uncoil with the sound of wet slate sliding against slate.

A lesser benevolence—one that knew only how to pity, and not how to see—might be moved to immediate mercy.

It would look at the freshly fractured basalt, sharp enough to shear flesh, and want to smooth the ledges into comfortable slopes. Yet as the creature steps out onto the open shelf, the watching power sees the six slender limbs ending in cloudy-white needles of grown glass, and hears the delicate, deliberate *click* as each point finds its exact pocket between the blades. To soften the stone would be to unmake the foot.

It would look at the broad, eyeless cowl and want to grant it sight, so the creature might know the bright, haze-softened void beyond the cliff-lip. Yet it watches the spider-silk cilia trembling in the rising steam, reading the weight of the air, the pulse of the heat, the distant hum of the disc—a map far truer than anything painted in glare and shadow.

It would look at the creature nudging fallen reeds from a mineral vein with its spade-like forelimb, and want to widen the channels so the water might never choke, or summon a gentle rain so the creature would not have to wait at the center of the terrace, tongue extended, catching leaping pearls of distilled water one by one with a soft *tink*. Yet the power sees that tending the veins is not a burden laid upon the creature; it is the creature’s own shape, stretched outward to the walls of the shelf. To automate its care would be to sever it from its own body.

And most of all, a merely kind power might look at the two hundred paces of stark, glistening rock and feel the ache of solitude. It might think to shape a second plated form from the wet basalt, to give the slow ship a companion on the stone.

But wisdom measures the terrace honestly. It counts the sparse islands of translucent bladder-moss, slow to swell and slower to mend. It measures the rate at which the matte-gray disc beads and purifies the simmering water, one trembling sphere at a time. It looks toward the cliff-lip, at the three bone-white struts leaning inward over empty air, and measures the small, ash-dry circle beneath them—just wide enough for one heavy, plated belly to settle out of the rotting night-damp.

A second creature would not be a gift. It would be a winter.

So the power stays its hand. It does not touch the ancient, humming geometries to explain them or improve them; whatever hands set them here are gone, and the creature has already translated them into the only language it needs—one for clean water, one for dry sleep. It does not ward the mountain against future ages, nor freeze this young, unfinished rock in amber. Everything that lives here is built for a world that is still shifting, still weeping, still sharp.

Instead, the power remains simply as witness until the last of the direct sunlight slips over the western rim.

It watches the creature lift itself from the simmering basin, *click, click, click*, and step carefully between the bone-white struts into the bent, thick light. It hears the long, wet *hiss* of spent steam as the plated belly settles onto the dry basalt, the bladder-mosses tightening on its back, the milky oil curing into a thin, protective skin.

When the cowl tucks down and the trembling threads go still, the creature is once more indistinguishable from the volcanic dark.

The power withdraws as quietly as it came—leaving not a footprint, not a blessing etched in light, not a single altered drop of water. Leaving only the loop, unbroken, waiting in the one dry place on the mountain for the sun to return.
