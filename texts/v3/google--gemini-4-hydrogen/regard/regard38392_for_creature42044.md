At first, the power does nothing at all.

That is the beginning of its wisdom: to stand at the edge of the amber-grey haze and *not* reach out. A lesser benevolence, seeing a world of stifling kiln-heat and sweating slate, would have cracked the upper ceiling of vapor to let in wind and starlight. It would have cooled the flagstones, coaxed moss into the sluice-channels, and given the blind, slow-moving things in the mist eyes to see the sky.

The power knows better. Sunlight here would bake the living paste into dead, brittle ceramic. Rain would wash the slaking lime into sterile mud. Green things would bring rot to a world that has never known rot, and to give eyes to a creature made of wet mortar would be no gift—only a confusion of glare in a place meant to be known by weight and level.

So the power withholds its sky. It keeps its wind to itself. It simply *looks*, with a patience as broad and quiet as the plain, until it understands the grammar of the place: the endless, silent rehearsal of craft; the *drip-drip* of condensation; the *clack* of a ceramic peg dropping into place a mile away; the creatures that feed on the chemical tension of things setting, and then wake again when the steam rolls over them.

It sees that the world is not broken. It is only *unmet*—full of trowel-arcs and thumbprints, waiting in the dark for a hand that belongs to them.

---

When the power finally enters, it does not come as a voice or a radiance. There are no ears here to hear a voice, and no eyes to catch a light. It comes in the only language the plain understands: *weight*, *warmth*, and *stillness*.

It takes a form—small enough not to crush the delicate, un-keyed balance of the terraces, yet dense enough to press a true mark into the dampness. It kneels on the sweating flagstones beside a low, waist-high wall of sun-dried clay, and rests one open palm flat against the wet slate.

It radiates a steady, unfamiliar heat—not the fierce, trapped exothermic fever of the voids beneath, nor the cool indifference of stone far from the seams, but the quiet, living warmth of something that refuses to cure.

For a long time, nothing happens. A front of steam peels off a hotter sector of the plain and drifts past, condensing on the power’s shoulders, beading along its knuckles. Half a mile away, a corbeled plinth quietly slumps into grey paste, its copper pins *tinking* softly onto the floor.

Then, behind the waist-high wall, the air grows slightly heavier.

There is the faint, wet *shhh* of something broad sliding over slick stone, followed by the mineral smell of slaking lime. A creature—a low, damp mass of mineral-grey rind, ribbed along its flanks with comb-channels—emerges from the dimness. It has felt the anomaly in the level. It has felt a weight that does not shift, a warmth that does not stiffen into brick.

It approaches slowly, laying a flat lobe forward, pressing down to drive the moisture from the slate, trailing its softened half behind it. Inside its paste, a ceramic peg clicks against a fragment of terracotta.

When it reaches the power’s kneeling knee, and then the open palm resting on the stone, it pauses. It has no mind to wonder what this is, only the deep, patient instinct of its kind: *to measure, to press, to take the shape of what rests upon the floor.*

A human traveler would have flinched at the heat, or pulled their hand back from the damp, grey flank.

The power does not move.

It stays completely still as the creature’s whorled, steaming underside presses against its palm, its wrist, the side of its hand. It lets the creature lean its full, heavy mass against it, feeling the concentric ridges grip the skin, feeling the trapped heat rising to drive the dampness out. It waits, with infinite, unhurried courtesy, while the grey slip drinks the air and firms up around its fingers—holding the exact, sharp, unweathered impression of knuckles, palm-lines, and thumb.

For a moment, in the dim, shuttered quiet, the world has what it was rehearsing for: a real hand inside the thumbprint.

---

If you were to ask what the power *changes* before it rises, the answer is almost nothing—and everything.

It does not stop the plain from heaving. To take away the instability of the terraces would be to starve the creatures of the *setting* they live by. It does not fill in the warm, colorless void beneath the flagstones, or forbid the arcades from slumping back into paste. When the next hot tide of vapor rolls across the slate, the creature’s joints will slake, the crisp impression of the palm will soften and flatten out, and the being will slide away twenty paces on to pull itself together as a damp, weeping column once more.

The power lets it go. It knows that a gift forced to stay forever in a world of wet clay is just another kind of cage.

Instead, the power does only one small, quiet thing.

Before it withdraws its presence from the plain, it leaves behind a single, gentle *datum*—not a monument, not a wall that refuses to fall, not a sun to dry the floor. Somewhere out in the dimness, where the mist blurs the horizon into bruised amber-grey, a small, steady warmth remains upon the flagstones. It has no eyes and no voice. It does not command the slate to stop shifting, nor the water to stop flowing uphill.

It simply waits there, a quiet, un-curing weight in the level, ready to be found in the dark—so that whenever one of the slow, blind builders of the seams wanders out of the steam, looking for something real to measure itself against, there will always be a hand left resting on the stone.
