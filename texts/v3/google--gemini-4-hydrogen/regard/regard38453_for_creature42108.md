At first, from a distance, it looks like a place in agony.

A benevolent power, passing over the high ridges where the air is thin and cold, would be drawn here by the sheer *noise* of it—by the anomaly of a valley radiating heat like an open kiln beneath a clean, indifferent sky. Looking down, it would see no birds, no soft moss, no quiet pools. It would hear only the rifle-shot *crack* of snapped vines and the low, booming *whump* of swollen cisterns bursting across the flats. It would see towers frantically knitting themselves together out of raw sap and bone-hard reed, only to be pried apart from within by dark, feverish swellings wedged into their joints.

The first impulse of kindness is almost always to soothe.

A lesser mercy would reach down at once. It would lay a cool hand over the steaming terraces to lower the fever. It would soften the brittle, over-tensioned cellulose so the towers would bend in the mountain wind instead of shattering. Most of all, it would pluck the olive-brown knots from the flying buttresses, thinking them cruel parasites bleeding the architecture to death. It would try to quiet the deafening groans, to turn a kiln into a meadow.

A *wise* power pauses before it touches.

Before changing a single reed, it would press its attention against the taut, olive-ochre drumhead of the floor and listen—not to the violence of the sound, but to what the sound is *doing*. It would trace the faint, high-pitched hum inside a tap-knot, feeling the surge of boiled sugars, and follow that pressure past the breaking point.

It would watch the tower crash into the ankle-deep water. It would watch the tough, shock-absorbing husk split cleanly along its seams, and follow the glassy, hook-rimmed seekers as the hot flood drags them down through the slatted floor into the muffled dark below.

And there, in the warm, echoing under-lattice, it would see the truth of the place.

It would see the seekers unfold into vast, pale-ochre veils, wrapping silently around the jagged wreckage of the fallen spire. It would watch them dissolve the raw, sap-soaked cellulose without rot, without sourness or stink, stripping the ruin down to clean, bare fiber so the creeping runners above can immediately weave it back into the living floor.

Nothing is wasted. Nothing festers. The knot that breaks the tower and the veil that cleans the dark are the same creature; the violence above is the engine that feeds the mercy below. If the knots were removed, the towers would still overheat and split under the scalding sun, or else grow stagnant and choke—and without the veils to clear the debris, the dark channels beneath the plateau would clog with dead, fermenting reed until the whole taut skin poisoned itself and collapsed. If the heat were cooled, the high leaf-sails would stop weeping mist, and the hydraulic pulse that builds the cathedrals would die.

Still, a benevolent power would check one thing more. It would reach gently into the humming rind of the tap-knot, into the drifting membrane of the veil, and into the feverish ribs of the towers, searching for the one thing that would justify interference: *suffering*. It would listen for a nerve, a fear of falling, a trapped awareness enduring the heat and the impact.

It would find none. There are no eyes here to dread the collapse, no lungs to gasp in the scalding air, no minds to mistake tension for pain. There is only physics, chemistry, and a blind, faultless geometry of becoming and unmaking.

So the wise and benevolent power would do nothing.

It would withdraw its hand before a single drop of mist was chilled or a single reed was softened. It would recognize that peace does not always look like stillness, and that beauty does not always ask to be gentle. To impose quiet on this plateau would not be healing; it would be erasure.

If it left any mark at all, it would be only an unseen stillness at the edges of the valley—a quiet decision to let the kiln stay hot, to let the towers reach and snap, to let the dark veils go on cleaning the ruin in peace, unbothered by pity.
