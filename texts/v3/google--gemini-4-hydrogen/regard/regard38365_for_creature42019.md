The first thing it did was learn how not to look.

A lesser kindness, arriving at the lip of the root-cliffs, might have mistaken the basin for something broken. It is a natural error: to see a bridge that measures twenty paces one way and forty the other, to hear the splash of a water-drop before the drop has even parted from its leaf, and to reach out a hand to smooth the seam flat. To see creatures with milky, veiled eyes and pale membranes stretched tight between their ribs, living inside a roar thick enough to bruise, and to think: *Here is a place in pain. Let me quiet the wind. Let me straighten the walls.*

The power had arrived with that same readiness to mend. It rested its attention along the rim of the bowl—not as a beam of light, which would have cast too sharp a shadow on the walkways, but as something closer to the warm mist that drifts down over the roots every few hours.

It listened to the surf of sound. It watched the needle-winged clouds boil through the sunlit voids, trailing their shimmering afterimages. It saw the gliders vanish into solid bark and burst from cisterns three tiers down.

And then it waited. It did not touch the reeds. It did not hush the frogs. It simply stayed still on the rim, long enough for its vast, patient sight to adjust past the surface clamor and notice the *hands*.

It saw the three-ply splices in the vines. It saw the calloused, pared thresholds of the gourd-chambers, and the neat collars of rotting fronds stacked to feed the pale new shoots. It watched a keeper step sideways out of a wind-rippled leaf-wall, cross a span in twelve unhurried strides without once looking down at the void, and press a waxy palm against an oak-thick reed.

The power felt the two tiny taps travel—not through the deafening air, where they would have been swallowed whole, but through the *slip*, arriving crisp and dry at a water-viaduct three tiers up a fraction of a second before the keeper’s claw ever struck the bark.

In that moment, the power understood.

If it straightened the geometry of the basin, the deeper streets behind the leaf-walls—those quiet, green-lit galleries where hundreds of keepers moved in peace—would collapse into the small, cramped bowl of the cliffs. If it mended the echoes so that sound obeyed the ordinary clock, the keepers would lose their language; they would be stranded inside the organ-drone, deaf and mute in a world that could no longer carry a whisper ahead of the roar. Even the milky veils over their eyes were not a blindness to be cured, but a grace: the exact softness needed to cross a bridge without pinning it down.

Nothing here was wounded. The fray was not a scar; it was the loom.

So the power did the hardest thing for any benevolence to learn: it kept its hands off the work.

It did not quiet a single reed. It did not straighten a single shadow. It let the drop fall after the splash, and let the glider hunt through the solid trunk, and let the keeper mute its own small pocket of air around a vibrating frond.

Instead, the power turned its attention outward, away from the bowl and toward the wide, ordinary world beyond the tangled root-cliffs.

There, space held still. There, sound followed footsteps, and iron was sharp, and travelers often carried a fierce, impatient need to map what they saw and fix what did not match their maps. The basin had survived for ages in its own equilibrium, tucked into the earth, but the power could see how fragile that seam was to an outside eye—how a single boots-on-stone certainty, staring too directly at the braided bridges, could stiffen the air and bruise the resonance.

Very gently, the power reached out—not to the basin, but to the rim of earth around it.

It did not raise a wall, for walls invite climbing. It did not hide the bowl in darkness, for the keepers needed their ordinary noon sun to warm the dampness into mist. Instead, it borrowed a trick from the keepers themselves. It laid a slight, sideways tilt over the outer edges of the root-cliffs—the same soft habit of glance that lets an eye slide past the edge of a void without locking it stiff.

From that day on, anyone wandering the world beyond with haste in their stride, or tools in their pack, or a mind full of straight lines, would walk right past the depression without ever quite noticing the hollow in the ground. Their gaze would slip smoothly from one side of the ancient roots to the other, as if the space between were only a trick of afternoon shadow, and the muffled organ-drone beneath their boots only the wind in their own ears. Only someone who already knew how to step lightly—and how to listen without demanding an answer—would ever find the lip of the basin.

The work took no effort, and left no mark. No reed bent; no amphibian paused in its chorus.

Before it withdrew its attention, the power lingered for one heartbeat more on the damp balcony. A keeper was passing by, its chest-membranes fluttering softly as it registered the strange, quiet stillness the power’s presence made in the warm air—a disruption no heavier than a cool breeze before rain.

The power did not speak. To speak into the air would only add to the surf, and to tap the reeds in a language it had not grown up inside would be an intrusion. Instead, it simply softened its own presence until it vanished into the mist, leaving the basin exactly as it had found it: loud, frayed, impossible, and whole.

Two seconds after it was gone, a single drop of condensation detached from a high, waxy leaf, and fell cleanly into a cistern below.
