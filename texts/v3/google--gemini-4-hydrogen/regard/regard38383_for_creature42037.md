A power that is merely kind might have pitied the hollow. Seeing the bone-dry chill, the tarnished light, and the silent thing folded into the seam of the rock, it might have offered warmth, or water, or a voice to break the heavy stillness. It might have smoothed the impossible corner so the walls met as walls ought to meet, and called it a mercy.

A power that is both benevolent and wise stops at the lip of the overhang and listens first.

It does not bring light with it, for the dimness here is not a lack of sun; it is the medium in which the room exists. It does not bring warmth, for warmth would stir the air, and stirred air would scatter the pale-gray dust. Instead, the power folds its own immensity down until it takes up no space at all, and steps across the threshold as a quietness within the quiet.

Then, it waits.

It does not wait as a human waits, counting seconds against a pulse. It matches its attention to the viscous tempo of the stone, where an hour is a single, unhurried breath. It watches the flat pewter light hold steady. It watches the tiny, pale-ochre crust of silicate needles in the lee of the smaller pylon, noting how patiently it drinks the trace atoms from the rock.

When the sub-audible pressure shifts, the power does not flinch. It watches the fine mineral dust lift half an inch into its rigid, ghostly lattice. It watches the impossible corner unfold—the frictionless slide of matte, lightless planes, the slender caliper limbs threading cleanly through the open geometry without disturbing a single mote.

And in that brief, suspended interval, the power understands.

Nothing here is broken. Nothing here is suffering. The creature is not a prisoner of the cold ridge; it is a solution to an equation the mountain itself forgot it had written. Its lack of eyes is not blindness, for there is nothing here meant to be seen. Its immobility is not a burden; it is the exact weight required to hold the wind at bay, the quiet price it pays in exchange for the lattice that feeds it. To "rescue" it into a sunlit valley would be to crack it like dry mica. To give it company would be to crowd a room built for one.

Even so, wisdom looks closely at the margins of things—at the slow, indifferent pressures that even the most patient keeper cannot guard against.

The power looks down through the banded gneiss, beneath the matte, lightless pylons, into the deep bedrock where the ancient machinery still idles. The cycle is steady, yet over eons the stone has begun to exfoliate, flaking away in microscopic grains, blurring the concentric grooves around the pylons' bases. And high above, on the windward face of the ridge, centuries of freezing gales have been worrying at the sheared slate of the overhang, widening hairline fractures that will, in another few thousand winters, bring the roof down into the hollow and let the mountain wind rush in.

So the power acts—if such a small, invisible gesture can be called an action.

Inside the chamber, it touches nothing. Not a grain of the pale-gray dust is shifted. The shadows continue to pool in the wrong directions; the corner remains both acute and obtuse. The creature is not woken, nor addressed, nor made aware that it has been seen. A blessing that requires gratitude would be an intrusion here.

Instead, the power reaches into the mountain *around* the pocket.

Far above the threshold, in the raw gale outside, it quietly knits the hairline fractures in the sheared slate, lending the overhang the endurance to outlast ten thousand more winters of ice and wind. Deep beneath the floor, well below the roots of the pylons, it steadies the faint, idling pulse in the stone—not brightening it, not altering its rhythm by a fraction, only ensuring that the slow well will not run dry before the creature's own long span is finished.

Then, the power withdraws.

When the next hour turns, the cold pressure shifts once more. The dust lifts into its rigid lattice. From the folded corner, the slate-dark calipers unfold, thread the invisible lines, and drink the brief, clean surge of order. In the lee of the smaller pylon, the ochre crust shudders in faint sympathy.

Nothing in the room has changed. Everything in the room is safe to go on being what it is.

The pressure eases. The dust settles back into the worn grooves. And the hollow returns to its dark, dry, unbroken peace.
