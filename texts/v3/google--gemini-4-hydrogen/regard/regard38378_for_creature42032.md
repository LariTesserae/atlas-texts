It would not bring light.

A lesser kindness—one that had only power, and not wisdom—might have split the fused, glassy bulkheads at the far ends of the vault to let the sky in. It might have silenced the tireless shifting beneath the floor, cooled the stone, and offered these tallow-pale creatures eyes to see the sun. And in doing so, it would have killed them more thoroughly than any glider ever could.

To bring light here is to bring a blade. Without the dark, the dwellers’ matte skin would blister; without the sting-proof seams where eyes might have been, the mineral grit in the air would turn to constant injury. To silence the machine is to stop the breath of the world—the twelve-minute tide of heat and condensation that feeds the moss, fills the hanging tendrils with clean water, and tells the dwellers when to wake, when to work, and when to braid themselves together in the dark. You cannot rescue a people by erasing the grammar of their existence.

So the power does not arrive with a blaze or a voice. It arrives as attention.

It settles onto the cantilevered lip of sintered basalt, not taking up space, not displacing the warm, ozone-thick air. It waits through one cycle, then ten, then a hundred, letting the chest-rattling thrum become the rhythm of its own patience. It learns the vault the way the dwellers do: not by looking, but by listening to how the roar returns from the tiered walls.

It watches the maintenance hatches swing open on oiled hinges during the *whuff*. It watches the long-limbed figures slip out in their fluid, three-point crawl, two fingers always trailing the phase-locked ceramic-metal. It sees the care with which they scrape the pale gypsum from the intake louvers so the crypts behind the walls do not turn scalding and dead. It watches them hang motionless from the upper walkways for three full cycles just to catch a glider-bladder of tepid rain. It feels the sharp, high *ping* travel down a load-bearing filament—*cool seam, rain coming, glider down*—and feels the answering vibration in a palm half a mile away.

Most of all, it looks inside the unlit service-crypts, where the roar softens to a pulse. It sees them sleeping tangled together, chins resting on each other’s collarbones to feel the slow rise and fall of breath. It sees them bringing softened moss-pulp to the stiff and the old. It sees them carrying their dead into the cold, dead-end conduits, pressing a flat palm to a still chest one last time before latching the hatch.

The power recognizes what it is looking at. These are not broken things waiting to be fixed. They are a whole, fierce, exquisitely adapted people who have made a covenant with a harsh room—and kept it with tenderness.

Even so, wisdom does not mean folding its hands and walking away. A world left entirely to indifferent machinery still grows teeth that are sharper than necessary. The power looks deeper—not to remake the vault, but to read its hidden fractures.

Deep beneath the floor-plates, where the vast, post-industrial mass shifts through invisible fields, the power listens to the machine’s long memory. It feels a slow, centuries-long drift: a subtle imbalance in the field-coils that is making the high-frequency whine a fraction sharper each decade, pushing the thermal vents hotter than the lower tiers can bear. Left alone, in another hundred generations, the *whuff* would turn into a scalding blast, cooking the moss from the ledges and driving the dwellers out of their crypts into a dead room.

And along the perimeter galleries, the power notices the stressed conductors—the ones bleeding that bruised, ember-violet glow through hairline fractures in their casings. To the dwellers, they are useful landmarks of warmth. To the power, they are slow fuses, leaking raw ozone into the air and straining toward an arc-flash that would split the ceramic-metal and collapse an entire tier of sleeping hollows.

So during the peak of the cycle—when the whine climbs to its breathless height, the curtains of condensation let go from the ceiling, and every dweller is curled tight behind a latched hatch with hands pressed to ears and chests pressed to stone—the power goes to work.

It does not stop the machine. Instead, it reaches down through the bedrock and gently trues the invisible fields. It eases the strain that was sharpening the whine, returning the twelve-minute pulse to a deep, sustainable steadiness that will hold for ten thousand years. The roar remains; the heat remains; the *whuff* still sends its warm gust swirling up through the hollow core. Nothing is stolen from the clock.

Then it moves along the recessed galleries. It does not extinguish the warmth of the stressed conductors—the dwellers need those beacons under their fingertips—but it anneals the hairline fractures from the inside out. The bruised, ember-violet light dims into a safe, steady, invisible heat. The ozone clears just enough that the narrow slit-nostrils do not have to pinch shut quite so often.

It checks the oldest dead-end crypts, where the resting dead are wedged behind sealed housings, and quietly reinforces the sintered basalt above them so the shifting earth will never crush their graves.

It considers doing more. It considers softening the lime-crust on the louvers, or making the clean condensation fall a little lower so the dwellers would not have to climb so far into the dark rafters.

It stops itself. To take away the clearing of the louvers is to take away the reason they emerge together into the galleries. To make the water effortless is to unwrite the patience that binds them to the ceiling, to the tendrils, to one another. Struggle is not the enemy here; pointless ruin is. The power knows the difference.

Before it withdraws, it lingers for one last cycle, tempted—just for a moment—to leave a sign. A gentle hum on the tension-web, perhaps. A pattern plucked into the wire that means *you are seen, you are held, you are safe.*

But the very first truth of this cathedral is that its proportions are wrong for worship. A voice from nowhere, even a kind one, would only make them wonder what new predator had learned to speak through their nerves. True benevolence does not ask to be thanked, and it does not make itself the center of someone else’s home.

Instead, as the whine drops away and the heavy pneumatic *whuff* sweeps upward through the dark, the oiled maintenance hatches click open.

A tallow-pale dweller slips out onto the narrow gallery, ceramic wedge in hand, trailing two long fingers along the phase-locked wall. It reaches the place where the stressed conductor used to bleed harsh heat and stinging ozone—a place it had always hurried past with its nostrils pinched shut.

Its fingertips brush the seam. The ceramic-metal is smooth, whole, and gently warm, like the flank of a sleeping kin.

The dweller pauses for a heartbeat, its calloused chin tilting toward the stone as if listening to a subtle new health in the floor. Then, satisfied, it moves on toward the intake louvers, scraping the pale gypsum away in the dark, while the tireless machine beneath its soles breathes on—steady, faithful, and safe.
