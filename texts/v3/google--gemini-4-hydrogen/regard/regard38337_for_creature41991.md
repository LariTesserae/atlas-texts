When the power turned its attention toward the sunken hollow, it did not arrive as light.

Light would have been a violence here. It would have bleached the bruised-violet flicker of the lampposts, dried the weeping salt along the ledges, and startled the blind amphibians from the girders into the hundred-foot drop. Instead, the power entered the way dampness enters old timber: without wind, without sound, settling into the cool, cellulose-scented dark until it occupied the empty air as quietly as the void itself.

For a long while, it simply listened.

It felt the sub-audible thrum in the vascular cables beneath the floorboards. It watched a droplet of condensation detach from a pipe, drift sideways at a gentle angle, and vanish into the rim of a spinning brass flywheel. It followed the young tenants down on the lowest walkways, standing at the soft, wet meristematic tips of the unfinished streets with lengths of copper wire coiled in their hands, waiting for the opposite ledge to inch across the dark.

Any lesser benevolence—any kindness that mistook itself for competence—would have begun at once to mend what looked broken.

The power felt the shape of those impulses, and weighed each one against what it saw.

*It is dark here,* a hurried mercy might have thought, and hung a sun beneath the ribbed vault of roots. Yet the tenants did not stumble. They tilted their faces *away* from the violet arcs and read the hollow through the pressure in their teeth, the drift of chill air against the pale hairs of their knuckles, the dry clockwork *tick* of their own jaws. To give them sight as the outer world knew it would be to deafen them in a different sense, drowning out the tiny click of the relays and the quiet language of foreheads pressed together on narrow bridges.

*It is unfinished,* an impatient wisdom might have said, and knit the growing streets together in a single breath, closing the gaps, capping the pipes that ended twenty feet out in empty air, straightening the staircase that deposited its climbers upside down on the underside of their own landing. Yet the power watched a pair of tenants hunched beside a copper flange, adjusting a brass valve by a hair’s breadth, waiting for a falling drop to miss its cistern—and saw the deep, unhurried satisfaction in the nod they shared afterward. There was nowhere finished to arrive at. The *inching* was the point. To complete the town would be to stop its heart.

*They are in danger,* a protective hand might have decided, and swept the wrong-jointed predators from the catwalks, or pried the long-sleeping tenants out of the gall-walls where their shoulders had widened into bark and their fingertips had threaded into the floorboards. Yet when a clicking hunter trotted past, the tenants flattened themselves against a pillar and coarsened into the texture of bracket fungus without fear, only a calm, practiced stillness. And in the deep tenements, the living leaned their ribs against the fused sleepers to listen to the hum of the relays, hanging their damp cambium coats on hardened elbows without grief. Nothing here rotted. Nothing was wasted. To cut the sleepers free would be the only true wound this place had ever known.

So the power touched none of them.

It did not untwist the gravity in the alleys of half-rendered brick. It did not wake the sleepers, or splice the copper wires, or answer the needles spinning lazily behind unmarked glass. It did not even hush the predators, or tell the ash-pale moths to stay moths instead of stiffening into levers and clasps.

Instead, the power turned its attention outward—to the shell of fused roots that roofed the hollow, and to whatever lay beyond it.

It thickened the vault by the quietest margin. Not enough to seal off the slow seepage of mineral water that fed the salt ledges, nor enough to change the dampness of the air, but enough that no harsh sun, no drying wind, no iron-shod curiosity from the outer world could easily break through and mistake this place for a ruin in need of clearing. It steadied the outer earth around the basin so the cathedral of empty dark would not collapse before its roads had finished growing—even if that took ten thousand cycles of the central boiler-house.

Then, before it withdrew, the power lingered for one more moment inside the suspended boiler-house at the center of the void.

A tenant was standing by a pipe that ended twenty feet out in nothingness, tapping the iron lightly with a knuckle and tilting their jaw to wait for an echo that never quite came back.

The power did not send back a voice, or a miracle, or a blaze of violet heat. It only let the empty air beyond the pipe hold its stillness a fraction more cleanly—so that the tiny, faint vibration of the tap returned, barely audible, like the click of a distant relay somewhere across the dark, acknowledging that the line was open.

The tenant paused, listened to the pressure in their teeth, and seemed satisfied.

And the power drew back, leaving no footprint on the wet bark, no disturbance among the hanging beads of water—only the cool, hushed dark, and all the time in the world.
