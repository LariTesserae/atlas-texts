First, it would have to learn how to listen without ears.

A power accustomed to wetter, louder worlds might arrive here and mistake the quiet for an absence—might think the pale daylight was merely waiting for clouds, the salt-pans waiting for rivers, the tall, dry-skinned walkers waiting to be rescued from a mistake of geology. An impatient mercy would reach out before it understood, and in a single afternoon of rain, undo ten million years of patience.

So the wise power does nothing at all, at first. It simply settles its attention onto the vitrified stone, as lightly as cooling air, and lets the world explain itself.

It watches the three-kilometer wall of ochre dust tear itself apart in total silence, and understands that the silence is not emptiness; it is the price of keeping the sky clean enough for light to travel fifty miles unimpeded. It follows the invisible shears rising from the abyssal floor, and sees how the gliders read those lethal currents with their paper-thin wings, banking across the gulf without a single wasted beat. It peers into the horizontal flutes of the basalt fins, where the chaff-plates lock their hooked limbs into microscopic pits and plaster themselves in metallic dust, and into the shadow-notches, where the rime-borers wait in the millimeter between ice and vapor.

It sees, too, the temptation of every easy gift.

*What if I brought water?* it asks itself. And immediately it sees the cracked white plates of the salt-pans dissolving into choking brine, the armored gale-rollers drowning where they lie wedged in the crust, the wire-stalks rotting at the root. It sees the walkers’ salt-baffled vents swelling shut in the damp, their waxy blood turning sluggish, their cured-membrane frost-bladders mildewing in their hands. To give them rain would be to flood their house and call it hospitality.

*What if I thickened the air, so they might hear the storm and speak with their throats?* And it sees the gliders plummeting like wet parchment, their weightless geometry ruined; it sees the silent shears turning into screaming hurricanes that would rip the chaff-plates straight off the stone.

*What if I shut down the broken machines?* It looks at the matte-grey needles, the pearlescent ring, the sunken cylinder venting ionized dust, the drifting polyhedra dragging ribbons of grit. They are relics of an older, vanished hubris, running without a governor, their physics worn thin. Yet along the ring’s fractured ends, the corona-mosses bloom in cold violet sparks whenever the dust-front charges the sky. Beneath the polyhedra, the eddy-hangers dangle their static-stiffened silk, having never known solid ground. And the walkers themselves have woven the cylinder’s vents into their calendar, reading the failing machines the way other peoples read the moon. To erase the wreckage would be to starve the life that has rooted in its cracks.

So the power withholds its hands from the sky, from the frost, from the wind, and from the bodies of the ones who walk the ridges. It recognizes what an unwise benevolence never does: that scarcity is not the same as poverty, and that a world honed to the bone is not a broken world. It is a finished thing, austere and exact, where every living creature has earned its place on the rock by knowing how to keep still when the air goes mad.

Yet, looking closer at the ungoverned engines buried in the haze, the power notices one thing that is not part of the world’s hard balance. Deep beneath the salt-pans, in the roots of the sunken cylinder and the buried anchors of the drifting polyhedra, the ancient energies are not merely leaking—they are fraying toward a sudden, meaningless rupture. Left entirely alone, a century or ten from now, the governorless core will not simply fade; it will tear open the continental ribs, boil the last frost-notches dry in an hour, and leave nothing on the basalt fins to hold on.

Here, and only here, the power acts—and so quietly that nothing on the surface will ever know a hand passed near.

It does not repair the machines, nor restore them to whatever purpose their builders intended. It does not quiet the shears, nor stop the polyhedra from wobbling too close to the spires and scraping their passengers loose. It simply slips an invisible, patient limit beneath the failing physics—a deeper steadiness, like a thumb resting lightly on a spinning flywheel so it cannot shatter its housing. The cylinder will still vent its shimmering plume; the ring will still hum against the hairs of a forearm; the corona-mosses will still spark in the dark notches. The world will remain as dangerous, as unforgiving, and as glorious as it was before. Only the senseless end is quietly turned aside.

Then, before it withdraws, the power turns its attention back to the high shelf of pale stone, where a single walker stands thirty paces off, stave planted, electroscope-filaments lying flat against its hood.

The walker has sent a single, questioning *tick* through the rock.

The power knows better than to answer in the sky. In a world where light across the gulf is language, a sudden blaze of glory would be a deafening shout, an assault on eyes built for clear, pale daylight. Nor does it trouble the air with a voice that has nowhere to carry.

Instead, it gathers just enough quiet weight to press the vitrified stone—no blinding shape, no wasteful warmth, only a still, unmistakable presence resting on the same ridge—and taps back.

*Tick. Tick-tick. Pause. Tick.*

A dry, deliberate rhythm, traveling through the pale rock into the soles of the boots, up the shins, into the bones of the jaw. It carries no command, no promise of an easier world, no offer to carry the walker’s weight. It says only what can be said truthfully across such a gulf:

*I am here on the rock with you. I see how well you hold on.*

Then the weight lifts from the stone. Across the abyss, a three-kilometer wall of ochre dust collapses in total silence. A glider banks sharply through the clear daylight, reading an updraft no eye can see. And the walker on the ridge stands still for a long moment, listening through its heels to the quiet world, before turning back toward the shadow-notches with its frost-bladder and its stave.
