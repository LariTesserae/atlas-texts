If a power that is both benevolent and wise turns its gaze downward—past the last roots that remember rain, through the tight, jagged seam in the young basalt—its first instinct might be pity.

That is the hazard of any kindness born under a sun.

From above, the chamber looks like a place of deprivation. It is small, airless to any lung that needs moisture, and louder than a storm that never breaks. Nothing grows green. Nothing softens. The people who crouch in the troughs of the floor are dry as cured vellum, lidless-eyed, pressing their horned skulls against hollow gourds in a room where even the dead do not get to lie down and rot. A lesser mercy, seeing this, would reach in at once to *mend* it: to crack the ceiling for a shaft of daylight, to hush the shrieking fissures, to send a single, merciful thread of cool water trickling down the stone.

Wisdom is what stays the hand before the mercy lands.

So the power pauses at the seam. It does not even step inside, for it knows that a single foreign body—a stray displacement of air, a cool shadow falling across the warm slate—would scatter the pale Chladni ridges on the floor and knock the *parchment-drifters* out of their quiet nodes. Instead, it folds its attention into the geometry of the chamber and *listens*.

It listens long enough to learn the grammar of the place.

It watches a slight, amber-skinned figure kneel beside a hairline vent, press a fluted temple to the vibrating basalt, and tap a splinter of stone into the slit with a bead of warm pitch. It sees the *parchment-drifters* shiver and glide an inch to the left, chasing the new stillness. It watches the dust-lines sharpen. It follows another dweller to a tripod of vine-wood and bone, and—slipping its awareness into the hollow of the scraped seed-pod—hears what they hear: not noise, not torment, but a single, pure harmonic braided inside the roar like a silver wire pulled from ore.

And then the power understands.

Nothing here is enduring a punishment. Nothing here is waiting to be rescued.

To bring water into this hall would not be a blessing; it would be a blight. Moisture would rot the silica-stiffened *bladder-ferns*, dissolve the needle-sharp anhydrite reeds that sing the room into shape, and crack the resin-oiled skin of the dwellers. To quiet the vents would not bring peace; it would collapse the sky the *drifters* hang from, blind the horn-plates that read the room's pressure, and leave the people groping in a dead, flat void. Even to pour in sunlight would bleach the bruised-amber glow their lidless eyes were made to drink.

Their dryness is not thirst. Their roar is not chaos. Their stillness is not stagnation—it is an instrument they are playing from the inside.

So the power does nothing to the chamber. Not a grain of mineral powder is stirred. Not a single white tally is added to the central slab, where they keep their own careful clock against eternity.

Instead, the power turns its attention outward, to the things the chamber-dwellers cannot reach.

Above the pocket, in the dark, damp strata between the surface soil and the young basalt, water is always patient. Roots of surface trees feel blindly downward along hairline faults, and rain gathers in unseen pockets, waiting for the rock to shift by a fraction of an inch. Left alone, a single seep would eventually find that jagged seam—and one drop of surface water, falling into a kiln that has never known damp, would be the beginning of the end.

The power does not build a wall of alien stone, nor does it starve the roots above. With infinite gentleness, it coaxes the creeping moisture aside, guiding the slow seepage along older, deeper channels that bypass the basalt pocket altogether. It encourages the stressed rock around the outer shell to settle without cracking, knitting no foreign magic into the stone—only helping the mountain keep its own seal.

Farther down, beneath the floor where the non-aqueous gas rises from the deep crust, it checks the throat of the vents. It does not freeze them into unnatural permanence, for even a world has its own span and its own rhythm; it only eases a slow-forming mineral pinch deep in the rock that would have choked the chamber’s breath before its time.

Then, for one quiet moment before it withdraws, the power rests its attention in the exact center of the room—not altering the standing wave, not adding a note, only matching its stillness to the stillness of the nodes, the way two dwellers press their horned temples together in a narrow passage to share the hum of their chests.

The *parchment-drifters* do not stir. The chimes of anhydrite shimmer in the high register. At the central slab, a figure kneels, draws a shard of glass-stone across the basalt with a brief, crisp screech, and adds one more white mark beside the others.

The power departs, leaving no fingerprint on the dust, no echo in the horn-plates, no sign that the room was ever beheld—only the quiet assurance that, for a long while yet, the chamber may keep the middle of its own breath.
