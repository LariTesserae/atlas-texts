At first, the power’s impulse was to mend.

It had come down through the unseen hundreds of feet of water drawn by a faint, rhythmic disturbance in the dark—something too deliberate to be tide, too patient to be a dying thing’s thrash. Arriving at the edge of the trough, it met the cold like a closed fist, and the murk like a shutter drawn against the sky.

From the vantage of a power that knew sunlit shallows, warm currents, and the easy bloom of coral, the basin looked like an oversight. A place left unfinished, or punished. It felt the relentless, claustrophobic press of the glacial flour, the sandblasting eddies, the blind weight of the cobbles grinding one another toward dust.

And so, for the space of a long breath, it considered what it could do.

It could still the hyper-dense currents spilling off the shelves. It could let the suspended shell-dust and mineral silt settle, once and for all, until the bruised slate-grey cleared to a clean, blue transparency and light reached the floor. It could soften the grind, warm the water by a few merciful degrees, and open the sealed, translucent skin over those shallow eye-pits so the creatures below might finally see the valley they inhabited.

It reached toward the water—not with hands, for it had none, but with the quiet, effortless willingness that unmakes storms.

Then it paused. Before changing a single grain of silt, it leaned closer, and listened.

Not with ears, and not with sight, because sight died at arm’s length here. It let its attention sink into the broth the way the valley demanded: through pressure, through salt-taste, through the low, muffled vibration of stone against stone.

It felt a grazer unhook from the cobble-lock.

It watched—if watching is the word for knowing the shape of a thing in the dark—as the heavy, armored body hauled itself an inch forward, wedging a blunt claw into a seam before the eddy could pry it loose. It heard the quiet, muddy cough as the creature purged its gill-combs of compacted silt. It sensed the ballast-stones turning slowly inside the thick-walled gut: basalt, iron-nodule, and among them, a notched river-stone and a snapped bone hook, still being polished in the dark.

The power followed the creature’s senses outward through the slit-like pores along its jaw. It tasted what the grazer tasted: the faint, sharp bitterness of an octopus tucked behind a flint shield; the distant, salt-sweetness of a shredded kelp frond half a trench away; the sudden, dropping chill that meant a squall was gathering on the shelf.

And then it heard the *tock*.

A pause.

*Tock-tock.*

The power held itself still. In the miles-wide gloom, where scent was torn to ribbons and light had no meaning, that flat, muted note traveled straight. It carried no plea, no complaint, no hunger for rescue. It carried only the exact, stubborn geometry of presence: *Here. Mine. Wait.*

When the squall finally poured down from the unseen edges, the power did not part it. It stayed with the grazers in the deepest dishes of the bedrock, feeling the water press them harder into the floor, feeling the cobble-fields shudder and creep, feeling a rolling boulder strike a curved back-plate with a dull *tock* and bounce away into the blind dark. Beneath the weight, motionless as iron, the creatures were not suffering. They were holding.

And when the squall passed, the power slipped lower still—down past the wedged cobbles that sealed a clutch of silt-crusted eggs, down through the pitch-black labyrinth *beneath* the floor, into seams no wider than a coin.

There, in the quiet dark under the grinding world, it found the young. Pale as peeled willow, soft-skinned, threading the tight mud-choked passages where the currents could never reach, grazing on bacterial slime and shell-dust.

Understanding arrived then, cold and clear as the water itself.

To clear the silt would be to strip the young of their veil and invite the swift, sharp-eyed hunters of the upper water down into a nursery built for the blind. To warm the basin would rot the slow, dense broth that fed them. To still the currents would suffocate the seams beneath the stones, turning the labyrinth into a stagnant grave. Even to soften the grind—even to spare them the rolling boulders and the heavy, choking dust—would be to unmake the very law that had shaped their armor, their ballast, their patience, and the clean, unmistakable grammar of their *tock*.

Mercy, the power realized, is not always an opening of the sky. Sometimes mercy is a closed door that keeps the roof from falling in.

It considered leaving a small gift—a richer seam of clams, a gentler eddy, a single answering knock in the dark to keep a lonely grazer company.

It refused itself that, too. An answering knock would only send a creature hauling across the gravel toward a mate that did not exist. A sudden abundance would unbalance the trough. The valley did not need a caretaker; it had been tending itself for eons, grinding flint and bone and bedrock into the exact weight required to endure.

So the benevolent and wise power did nothing.

It drew back its warmth before a single degree of the cold could be lost. It gathered up its light before a single fleck of glacial flour could be startled by it. It left the bruised grey intact, the currents chaotic, the stones wedged tight against one another.

Only for a moment, before it withdrew from the basin altogether, it lingered in the quiet interval between two squalls—not to bless, not to mend, not to be known, but simply to witness a world that needed nothing from it.

Down on the floor, a heavy-backed grazer wedged its blunt claws into a fresh lock of cobbles, drank a slow gulp of the thick, grit-heavy water, and waited for the next tide of dark.

*Tock.*

A pause.

*Tock-tock.*
