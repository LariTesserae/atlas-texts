It would not enter through the seam.

Even a glance pushed carelessly through that glassy fissure would displace water, disturb a shear plane, or muddle the standing waves holding the silver dust in place. So the power—which has learned over long ages that the sharpest kind of care is the kind that leaves no fingerprints—arrives first as pure attention, resting lightly against the outside of the basalt shelf like a hand held an inch above a flame.

From there, it listens.

At first, it only reads the anomaly: a tight knot of folded geometry wedged into an ordinary coastline, running hot and bright beneath the cold-water tide. It hears the *clack-clack-clack* of the micro-valves, the high taut whine of cavitation, the deep harmonic groan of the stone settling a fraction of a millimeter at a time. It traces the impossible, unpiped columns of water and the razor-sharp boundaries between motion and stillness.

Then it notices the tenants.

It watches them glide along the shear, crisp as cut glass, their internal rings of basalt grit and silver splinters tilting in unison when the ceramic plates shift toward noon-glare. It hears the bright, stinging *tick* of one pinching its own hull to speak to the next down the alley. It watches a needle-thin prism rise up a vertical column to shear a tuft of pale moss clean off the ceiling, restoring the stone’s bare, ringing clarity. And when it tilts its perspective against the waist-thick central pillar, it sees the long, impossible street receding into the stone, and the quiet single-file procession carrying harvested grit toward whatever engine waits at the vanishing point.

A younger or less patient power might feel the urge to mend what looks harsh.

It might look at the sterile, shadow-etched alleys and think: *It is too loud here. The edges are too sharp. Nothing soft can grow, and nothing with eyes can bear the glare.* It might be tempted to hush the roar, to round the bevels of the ceramic plates, to introduce some gentle, bioluminescent life into the dead-still pockets so the chamber would not seem so fiercely alone. Or it might want to widen the throat of the fissure, to invite the ocean in, or to knock politely on the glass-hard hull of a tenant and ask what it is building.

Wisdom stays its hand.

To soften the edges would be to blind them; they read the world through the crispness of its angles. To quiet the roar would be to drain the air from their lungs; the noise is what holds their dust in order, what carries their speech, what tells them the city is alive. Even the moss they shear away is part of the rhythm—a small friction that keeps them sharp. They are not imprisoned in the cellar, and they are not suffering. They are young, exact, and entirely at home.

So the power does nothing inside the chamber. Not a single ripple is added to the basins; not a single frequency is altered in the stone.

Instead, it turns its attention outward, to the indifferent world the city does not know it sits inside.

It looks at the coastline above the fissure. It sees the winter storms gathering miles offshore, heavy enough to roll boulders across the basalt shelf and slam driftwood like battering rams against the newly fractured lip. It sees the slow, blind drift of barnacle larvae looking for fresh stone to crust, and the silt-heavy spring runoff that could choke a hairline seam before the chamber’s own currents have time to mature. Most of all, it feels the deep tectonic stress in the shelf itself—the hairline fault that birthed this pocket, still poised to either pinch it shut or crack it wide open to the crushing indifference of the sea.

Here, in the raw, unmeasured world outside, the power acts.

It reaches deep beneath the shelf and eases the grinding tension in the bedrock—not enough to stop the chamber’s tiny, millimeter-deep harmonic groans, which are part of its breathing, but enough that no sudden quake will split the blister open before its work is done. It shapes the subtle contours of the outer reef, turning the brunt of the winter swell a few degrees aside so the heaviest surf spends itself elsewhere, leaving the cold-water tide to wash cleanly past the seam without battering it.

It does not wall the fissure off, nor does it make the rock impenetrable; things that are alive need a threshold, even if they never cross it. It simply weaves a quiet, unremarkable ordinariness over the spot—a trick of shadow in the kelp, a slight boredom in the eye of anything passing by—so that curious hands and foraging claws drift past without pausing to pry at the glassy edge.

Before it withdraws, the power lingers for one more cycle of the city’s cadence.

The ceramic plates brighten to noon-glare. Every silver splinter inside every wandering polyhedron tilts in unison. Down in the impossible depth of the central pillar, the long file of bright, faceted shapes glides steadily toward the vanishing point, carrying their dust to the engine.

The power leaves no mark, asks for no acknowledgment, and makes no sound that could compete with the valves. It simply steps back into the dark water, holding the storm off the roof, and gives the cellar time to become whatever it is meant to be.
