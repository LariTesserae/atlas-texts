It would not arrive in light. 

A lesser power—one merely kind, and not yet wise—might have parted the dome to let the sun in, or touched the pearly domes where their eyes had been and given them sight. That would have been a massacre. Sight in a lightless room is only a new kind of hunger, and the high mountain sun would have blistered their capillary-thin skin like tallow on a stove. 

Instead, the attention settles over the mountain’s crown the way a deep snow settles: without sound, without hurry, pressing gently into every seam of the barren scree to learn the shape of what lies beneath.

It reads the cistern from the outside in. It feels the howl of the thin air against the cyclopean mound, the slow ache of winter ice wedging into the outer mortar, and then—just past a yard of hand-fitted stone—the sudden, enveloping fever-warmth of the drowned cathedral. It sinks through the dome without stirring a single grain of the pale mineral silt.

For a long time—if time means anything to something that watches mountains grow—it simply observes.

It watches the convection loop make its hours-long turn. It counts the nine slow heartbeats, beating once every dozen seconds, and finds the small, translucent tenth tucked behind a fallen capital, still fragile as a shrimp. It watches a six-fingered hand slide into a terracotta jar with geological patience, waiting for a catfish. It sees the neat, crescent-shaped patches scraped into the tube-worms, and notices how the grazers always leave a fringe behind to regrow. 

It sees the hoards tucked into the recessed mortar: the petrified reed, the giant spiral shell, the green nub of a bronze chisel. It is there, silent and weightless, when one of the dwellers pauses in the dark, lifts a blunt-nailed finger, and taps the bronze once against the basalt, listening with its jaw-pits as the dull *tink* dissolves into the warm water.

A wise power understands what it is looking at. These are not pitiable things trapped in a basement. They are not unfinished. They are a masterpiece of fit—as tightly mortared to this warm, dark geometry as the hexagonal slabs beneath their bellies. To "rescue" them would be to unmake them.

So it touches nothing inside the water. It leaves the darkness whole. It leaves the silence thick. It leaves the old one behind the fallen capital to its slow transformation, recognizing that to become part of the room’s masonry is not a tragedy here, but a kind of homecoming.

Yet love, when it is wise, still looks for the hidden fractures.

The power turns its attention outward, to the things the dwellers cannot reach and do not know to fear. High above the cloud-line, millennia of frost and wind are patient enemies. The lime-and-ash mortar in the outer retaining walls has weathered deep; in a century, or ten, a winter freeze might pry a keystone loose, and the mountain’s warm heart would spill down the scree in a single, steaming rush.

Quietly, invisibly, the power knits the outer stone. It does not erase the ancient tool-marks inside, nor fill the recessed mortar grooves where the dwellers read their geography by touch and store their small treasures. Instead, it reinforces the deep spine of the masonry from the outside—drawing the basalt tighter into itself, sealing the hairline fractures against the ice, lending the ancient walls the stubborn endurance of living bedrock.

Next, it looks to the crown of the vault, where the trapped air pockets lie pressed flat and silver against the stone. The air there has grown sour and thin over the decades, heavy with sulfur and old breath. The power does not blow a cold wind through the collapsed seam—that would chill the upper arches and break the fragile convection loop. Instead, it coaxes a hair-thin, winding vein through the upper rubble, warmed by the stone itself, so that the stale pockets gently trade their dead breath for the crisp, thin air of the peaks, arriving at the water’s surface as mild and still as a sigh. The next time one of them climbs hand over hand up the thick pillars to take three rattling breaths at the ceiling, the air will be sweet, and it will not know why.

And deep beneath the hexagonal paving stones, down in the dark bones of the mountain where the sulfurous fissures rise, the power lays a gentle steadiness against the rock. It ensures the vents will neither choke on their own mineral crusts nor surge too hot and scald the leathery eggs wedged inside the lower jars. It sets the fever-heat to hold steady, century after century.

Before it withdraws, the power lingers for one turn of the convection loop in the deepest gallery, behind the fallen capital where the oldest dweller rests.

The water brushes past, slow and warm. The feathery crowns of the calcareous worms growing along the old one’s shoulders snap shut. Its barbels twitch once in the absolute dark, tasting the sulfur, the silt, and the unchanging warmth.

The power does not speak—there are no ears here for words, and no need for them. It simply matches the twelve-second rhythm of that slow, hidden heart for a moment, offering the only benediction a creature of this room could ever understand: a stillness that does not threaten, and the quiet, unbreakable promise that the walls will hold.

Then it lifts away into the howling wind above the cloud-line, leaving no trace of its passing, save that the dark beneath the stone is a little safer for the waiting.
