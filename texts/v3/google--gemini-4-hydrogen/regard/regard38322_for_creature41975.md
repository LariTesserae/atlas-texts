It notices the snow first.

Miles above the thunderheads, where the air is too thin to carry much besides light and cold, a faint, continuous drift of gold-colored mica-scale is spilling out into the wind. It catches the sun like dust shaken from an old rug, trailing away from the flank of the spire before it scatters and vanishes into the blue.

A power that is both benevolent and wise does not hurry past a thing like that. It follows the bright trail back to its source, easing down through the freezing glare until it hovers outside the bulging quartz oculus, where the stone casing of the ancient tower is warm to the touch.

Through the thick lens, it sees the little world inside.

It watches for the span of many valve-strikes. It feels the low, grinding *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the brass impeller vibrating through the quartz and into the air. It counts the forty seconds, hears the anvil *clack* near the dome, and watches the silver column of superheated bubbles tear upward and shatter. It sees the copper-bellied skates peel off the mustard tiles, the blind thread-eels snap through the shrimp-clouds, and the heavy shadow uncoil from the hammock of the catwalk.

It watches the keeper work.

It sees the three pairs of feathery gill-slits opening and closing in strict time with the engine below. It watches the long, effortless seal-strokes to the north wall, the horn-thick thumbnail clearing calcium from the fluted rims of the organ-barnacles. When the keeper’s throat swells and that low, rattling, bronze-colored fourth note threads itself into the barnacles’ dissonant chord, the power listens with everything it has—not with ears, which would be useless here, but with the same full-body stillness the keeper uses.

And then it watches the keeper swim to the western wall, kneel on the rounded lip of the bronze sluice, clear the drain with one hooked finger, and rise to press both palms and the curve of its forehead against the glass.

Through the quartz, their gazes meet—though the keeper only sees the thinned, blinding blue of the upper sky and the white tops of the clouds far below.

In that quiet span between one *clack* and the next, a lesser power might be moved to pity, and pity might tempt it to ruin.

It would be so easy to shatter the quartz, to drain the bath-warm water, to reshape those feathery gill-slits into lungs and give the pale skull a tongue and a name. It would be easy to lift the keeper out of its thirty-foot cistern and set it down in the vast, rolling oceans far beneath the cloud-deck, or to fashion a second creature just like it and drop it through the dome so the catwalk would never hold only one shadow.

The power weighs all of these, and in its wisdom, refuses every one.

An ocean has no walls to keep clean. Its currents do not run in a forty-second circle; its silences have no brass impeller to time a heartbeat to. Set loose in the endless water below, this being—shaped by the gyre as surely as the river-smooth bolt-heads—would only wander until it starved for the *thrum*. A voice would only give it words for a loneliness it does not feel. And a second keeper would crowd the sagging catwalk, leaving half the kelp unharvested, half the barnacles untended, breaking the quiet, solitary covenant between the room and its steward.

The keeper is not a prisoner. It is the heart of the chamber, and the chamber is the skin around its bones.

So the power does not break the glass. It does not even ripple the water.

Instead, it turns its attention downward, sinking silently along the outer flank of the ancient spire, plunging through the wool of the thunderheads and miles of storm-darkened stone into the unseen deep where the boiler-core sleeps.

Here, no light ever reaches. Here is only heat, pressure, and the slow, patient fatigue of ten-thousand-year-old metal. The power lays its unseen hands against the casing of the great shaft and listens. It finds what the keeper—for all its scraping and harvesting—can never reach: a hairline wear in the bronze bearing of the impeller, slowly widening toward the century when the shaft would seize; a crust of mineral scale choking a deep feeder-pipe, slowly starving the forty-second surge; a quiet thinning in the riveted iron ribs that hold the domed ceiling against the sky.

The power mends them.

It does not make them new. To make them new would be to erase the river-pebble smoothness the room has spent millennia earning; it would change the pitch of the *thrum*, and the keeper wakes when the rhythm changes. Instead, the power knits the tired metal whole from the inside out, leaving every rounded lip, every glassy crust of rust, every familiar vibration untouched—only ensuring that the warm water will keep rising, the valve will keep striking its forty-second *clack*, and the brass impeller will turn without faltering for ten thousand years more.

Then it rises again, out of the weather, up through the freezing blue, back to the western wall.

The keeper is still there, palms and forehead pressed to the warm side of the quartz, milky eyelids half-lowered against the glare, looking out into the impossibly vast, still, blue cistern with nothing at all to keep clean.

The power lingers just outside the glass for the span of three valve-strikes. It does not knock. It does not show itself. It simply stands watch on the far side of the lens, honoring the quiet of a world that needs nothing added to it, letting the faint snow of gold mica-scale drift past its fingers and out into the sky.

Deep in the floor-grate, the impeller grinds. Overhead, the corroded valve *clacks* like an anvil.

The keeper pushes off the glass and slides effortlessly back into the bright, loud, endless circle of its home—never knowing it was visited, only that the rhythm held, as it always has, and always will.
