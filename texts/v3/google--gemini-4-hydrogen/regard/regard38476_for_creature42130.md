It does not break the glass.

A power that is merely strong might have parted the hundred meters of basalt like a curtain, letting the thin, freezing sky rush in to cool the furnace. A power that is merely kind might have pitied the small, eyeless things clinging to the sweating seams, and lifted them out into some softer, sunlit valley—where they would have dried to husks in an hour, starved of sulfur, salt, and the invisible rails of the air.

Instead, the power narrows itself. It slips through a hairline fracture at the crown of the spire, riding a sliver of bruised violet light into the thick, shimmering hush. It does not displace a single molecule of the hot air. It does not stir the amber haze below.

For a long time, it simply attends.

It watches a bead of condensation swell on the upper dome, detach, and fall through the cathedral dark, hissing softly as it strikes a warm ceramic roof. It feels the slow, heavy turnover of the thermal cycle rising from the core. It watches an entire cantilevered neighborhood drift three centimeters sideways on silent bearings, adjusting its angle to the rising warmth.

And it watches the drifters.

It sees one detach from a damp seam—matte charcoal above, pale pleated vellum below—and hang weightless in the shimmering void, buoyed by its own trapped heat and the city’s unseen field-lines. It hears the dry *click-tink, click-tink* of silica rasps clearing mineral blooms from the stone, keeping the city’s joints from seizing without ever knowing what a joint is. It watches them tuck themselves like dark shutters into the impossible, folded corners of the windowless rooms, the only things in the blister that fit the bent geometry of the builders.

A lesser benevolence would ache to change this. It would want to give the drifters eyes, so they might see the ember-glow and the bruised violet slivers of the sky. It would want to give them voices, or cool the stifling air, or finish the city’s long, humming equation so the walls could finally rest.

Wisdom stays its hand.

Eyes would be a cruelty here, where there is nothing to read and nowhere else to go; the drifters already see the only things that matter, through heat and tremor and the pull of the nodes. To cool the air would collapse their bladders. To silence the city would turn off the field-lines that hold them aloft, sending them all at once into the abyss below, with no updraft left to catch them.

They are not prisoners in a ruin. They are the breath of an organ, and the organ is their sky.

Still, the power does not leave empty-handed, nor does it leave them entirely untouched.

First, it lays its perception against the outer shell of the blister—the hundred meters of young basalt and fresh obsidian, pushed up fast by deep violence and still settling against the cold of the upper dark. Here and there, the extreme gradient has scored hidden stresses into the stone: faults that might, in a season or a century, split the chamber open prematurely and vent its banked warmth into the dead air above. Without altering the mountain’s nature, without denying it the slow, inevitable cooling that all stone owes to time, the power eases the sharpest of those tensions. The rock settles by less than a hair’s breadth. Nothing inside hears it. The blister will have its full, natural span.

Next, the power turns its attention to the city itself—the quiet, self-regulating calculation running at the roof of the world, with no one left to read its answers. It does not touch the ceramic nodes or interrupt the drift of the balconies. It simply listens. It follows the brightening and fading of the pale membranes, and the slow, rippling cadence passing along the flanks of the drifters in reply. It reads the equation the way one listens to a song sung in an empty room: not to take it, not to use it, only to receive it, so that the long, hot labor of the spire is not spoken into nothingness.

And at the last, it looks at a single drifter hanging upside down beside a pale node, waiting for a droplet of water to pause and slide along an invisible curve.

Across the void, a membrane brightens—a faint, cold-white pulse signaling some small shift in the hidden work. The drifter answers, thin slits along its underside flickering with a ghost of light onto the wet obsidian beneath it.

Before the cadence can fade, the power lets a shimmer of cold-white—no brighter than the creature’s own, no warmer than a reflection—wake along the dark stone beside it, passing the pulse onward to the next tier.

Just one beat. Not a command, not a revelation, not a rescue. Only a quiet *I am here* in the only grammar the blister knows.

Then the power withdraws through the hairline fracture, leaving the chamber as it found it: stifling, dim, and whole.

Inside, a droplet of water trembles in mid-air and begins to slide toward a slotted intake. A tendril flicks out, too fast to see, and draws it in. Above, along the sweating seam where the city meets the stone, a small, dry sound begins again.

*Click-tink. Click-tink.*
