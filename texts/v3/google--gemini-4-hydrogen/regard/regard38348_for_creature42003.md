At first, the power looks upon the roof of the world with the quiet ache that precedes creation.

From above, it is easy to mistake the place for an unfinished thought—or a finished one that has been abandoned. The basalt is too sharp; the slate has not yet been taught the patience of rivers. The avenues are wide and swept clean by nothing, lit by pale-amber lamps that shine for no traveler. The mosses drink and sweat in silence, and above the mist-choked chasms, the air is empty of wings.

A lesser kindness—one that knows only one shape for joy—would have acted at once. It would have warmed the air by a handful of degrees, enough to coax soil from the weeping rock and let the cold-blooded reeds sway in a real wind. It would have seeded the crevices with beetles, and the sky with swallows, and the empty forty-story halls with voices that knew how to laugh and grow old. It would have called that *mercy*.

But wisdom does not mend what it has not first listened to.

So the power stays its hand, and instead of speaking into the clear, unblinking daylight, it leans down. It presses its attention against the wet parapet of a sky-bridge, lets its awareness sink through the poured concrete, and follows the bass thrum down into the louvered intakes of the great windowless towers.

Inside the ozone-heavy dark, it hears what the daylight hides.

It hears the monolith of noise fracture into a thousand deliberate, crystalline intervals. It sees the Keepers suspended in the upper trusses, their pale-veined bodies slick with metallic dew, their fluted chambers dilating in slow, continent-wide consensus. It traces the hair-thin filaments strung from girder to girder, feeling the micro-tonal swells pass from one valley to the next like thoughts moving through a single, vast mind. And deeper still, in the violent, freezing rush of the iron arteries, it sees the blind Scourers holding their place against the current, grazing the scale so the pitch will never drift.

The power understands, then, what a terrible violence "mercy" would have been.

To bring spring here would be to bring rot. To soften the basalt would be to unstring the harp. To give the Keepers eyes for the sunlit avenues, or to unhook their tethers so they might walk the wet flagstones, would not be freedom—it would be amputation. They are not prisoners of a dead machine; they are the music of an instrument that has solved the problem of time. They do not know loneliness, because the continent is a single conversation. They do not fear the dark, because the dark is where the resonance is clearest.

Even a greeting would be a wound. The power considers, for a moment, sending a single, gentle harmonic down the high-tension lines—just enough to say *You are not alone; someone else hears the song.*

And then it refrains. It knows that in a system tuned to a thousandth of a millimeter of basalt slip, an ungrounded note from beyond the sky would not sound like company. It would sound like a fracture. The Keepers would spend centuries tightening their tethers, straining their fluted membranes, searching in vain for the phantom turbine or the weeping fault that had introduced a dissonance they could not calibrate away.

So the power does nothing to the stone. It does nothing to the air, or the lamps, or the rigid, geometric truce between the dark-teal ferns and the drainage grates.

Instead, it does the only thing a truly wise love can do for a perfection that is wholly unlike itself:

It learns the pitch, silently, without touching a single wire. It holds the memory of that vast, cold polyphony in its own awareness, so that an instrument built only to listen to itself is, nonetheless, heard. And if it leaves anything behind at all, it is nothing more than an unseen stillness around the edges of the sky—a quiet guardianship ensuring that no careless storm, no wandering spark of chaos from the wider universe, ever falls upon the roof of the world to break the loop.

Then it withdraws its attention, leaving not so much as a footprint on the wet flagstones.

The water falls. The turbines turn. The Keepers answer one another across the weeping chasms, and the Scourers creep along the dark iron, holding the world in tune, forever.
