At first, seeing as outsiders see, the power feels only pity.

It stands above the sun-blistered shelf, feeling the faint hum through the salt-crust, and steps down through the jagged lip into the white glare. The roar strikes it like a physical wall. The dryness pulls at the air like a drawn bow. Looking along the six-tiered perimeter—at the narrow-eyed figures sealed in plant-wax, at the scarred folds where ears ought to be, at the mouths perpetually shut—its first, fierce instinct is to mend.

It is so easy, for a power that loves what lives, to mistake difference for suffering.

A single gesture would summon cloud-cover over the desert, cooling the selenite dome until the blinding glare softened to pearl. Another would quiet the northern wind-throats, letting the roaring vault settle into mercy and silence. A third would coax a thin, clear spring from the deep rock beneath the salt-floor—just enough to wet the dry throats, to wash the chalk from their eyes, to soften the brittle lichen.

The power’s hand rises. The air above the dome begins to cool.

Then, because it is wise as well as kind, it pauses—and looks closer.

It traces the path that one drop of water would take. It sees the clear trickle seeping into the dry-stacked salt-brick retaining walls, and watches them slump into gray paste. It sees the chalky gypsum terraces soften, the bone-white plaster of crushed shell and plant-wax swell and peel. It sees the amber lichen—which has forgotten rain for ten thousand years—choke and blacken with rot. It sees the decades-old cakes of chaff and lichen-meal bloom with sudden, poisonous mold, and the blind gliders tumble from the wall-drafts, their papery wings sodden and heavy.

It traces the path of silence. If the wind-throats hush, the standing waves in the center collapse. The copper wires coiled around wrists and jawbones go dead against the skin, and the city loses its clock, its barometer, its pulse. The bladder-plants never hear the noon shriek that tells them when to burst. The tightrope walkers lose the hum that steadies their soles. And up in the northern crevices, the drilled femurs of the grandmothers fall mute in the dark.

What the power mistook for a wound was an instrument. What it mistook for deprivation was exactness.

So the power lowers its hand. It lets the cloud disperse over the outer flats, unspent.

Instead, it stays for one full turning of the sun, and learns how to listen without ears.

At noon, when the selenite snaps with heat and the chamber’s pitch climbs to a white-hot shriek, it watches the inhabitants lean out from their terraces with braided nets, catching the glittering snow of chaff-scales before the central void can tear them apart. It sees the black soot-stained palms flashing crisp, bird-like signs through the glare. It rests a weightless attention against a taut sinew handrail and feels the rapid, stammering triplet of a climber’s news vibrating through the cord.

In the dim quiet of a pith-lined alcove, it watches a mother press her forehead to her child’s wrapped skull, humming low in her throat—not into the useless air, but straight through the bone. Nothing in this room is lonely. Nothing here is starved of speech.

At dusk, as the crust cools and the pitch drops back down into the belly of the rock, the power climbs to the highest ledges near the northern wind-throats. It watches an elder sit motionless with a notched bone rod extended into the current, gently brushing gypsum dust from the blind head of a resting glider. Nearby, wedged into a crevice beside the hollow stalks, a pale husk wears slowly down into white powder, while beside it, a drilled long-bone catches the evening draft and sings.

Now the power knows what must be done.

It steps back up through the jagged lip, out onto the empty salt-flats under the first stars. It walks the wide circumference of the buried dome, measuring the thinness of the translucent selenite roof beneath the drifting grit.

It does not thicken the crystal, lest the morning light grow dim and the bladder-plants starve. It does not touch the wind-throats, nor smooth the sharp gypsum fins that make the chamber’s chord. It leaves the dwellers to their dry alchemy, their copper wire, their hard-won truce with the void.

Instead, it turns its attention outward, to the only thing that could truly undo them: the blind carelessness of the wider world.

Along the outer rim of the blister, where the crust is fragile enough to break beneath a heavy boot or a stray caravan’s wheel, the power lays a quiet, subtle persuasion into the desert itself. It tilts the wind-blown dunes a fraction to the east, so that wandering travelers naturally skirt the shelf. It flattens the heat-mirage above the jagged lip, making the buckled scar look like nothing at all—just one more meaningless ripple of salt in an endless plain. It softens the tremor of the ground just beyond the dome’s edge, so that only those who already know how to listen with their soles could ever feel it humming.

Then, before it departs, the power sits for a long moment on the cooling gypsum crust above the northern rim. It presses its palm flat against the stone, and lets the long, low, deafening hum of the hidden city rise up through its own bones—grandmothers and gliders, copper and salt, all singing together in the dark—and simply keeps them in mind, untouched, whole, and free to remain exactly what they are.
