A lesser mercy would have ruined them.

A power that knew only softness would have stepped through the riveted bulkhead with a sigh of warm air, silencing the turbine-scream so the bone behind its ears might rest. It would have tempered the cryogenic chill, coaxed a drop of condensation onto the basalt-ceramic, and called it kindness. And in half a second, the twelve flat grazers wedged into the expansion joints would have lost their tuning, their rigid silicate trusses catching the dying vibration at an off-angle, shattering without a sound into puffs of dry grey dust.

Wisdom, arriving at the crown of the superstructure, knows better than to enter with a body.

It comes instead without volume, without moisture, without heat. It occupies no space between the four fluted induction pylons and casts no acoustic shadow across the perforated grates. Not a single needle on the pinned pressure gauges trembles; not a single Chladni line bends by the width of a hair. To the crawlers locked in their nodal troughs, nothing has arrived at all.

For a long time—long enough for a crawler to ratchet three inches along a seam, long enough for a mica-thin frond to grow toward the roaring air and be patiently shaved back—the power simply attends.

It does not look through the flat, clinical glare of the cold-cathode tubes. It reads the chamber in the only language the chamber speaks: phase, impedance, and the curving ribs of magnetic flux. It feels the monstrous chord not as noise, but as the room’s single, unbroken breath. It watches the grazers borrow their slow, glacial motion from the lethal floor-plates; it feels the sharp, polite distress when two of them drift too close and their resonant cavities stutter into a beat-frequency; it watches an overgrown elder creep to the lip of the black sub-void, trembling its oldest seed-scales free into the dark shafts below.

It sees a life stripped of every comfort known to flesh, and recognizes it immediately for what it is: not a prison, and not a mistake, but a razor-edged Eden, complete unto itself.

So the power asks the only question benevolence can ask here: *What, if anything, is missing?*

It considers the rare, fractional surges in the pylons—the tiny instabilities that occasionally catch a crawler’s flawed lattice and shatter it back into dust. For a moment, it weighs the urge to smooth the current, to make the twelve immortal. Then it sees the narrow geometry of the nodal troughs, the finite seams between the ceramic tiles, and understands that immortality here would be a slow agony of overcrowding—a room packed shoulder to shoulder with grazing lattices, trapped in a permanent, inescapable dissonance. Even the shattering is part of the chord. It leaves the surges alone.

It considers the cold, the dark of the sub-void, the blind ignorance of the daylight slicing through the fused quartz slit. To give them eyes would be to blind them with glare; to give them warmth would be to dissolve them.

In the end, the wise power understands that its highest gift to this place cannot be given *inside* the chamber at all.

A room like this is a pocket of pure industrial stasis, but the superstructure that holds it is mortal. Everything made by hands—even riveted alloy, even braided copper and cast basalt-ceramic—eventually meets the patience of time. Someday, a technician with soft boots and warm, wet breath will unseal the bulkhead to check a gauge. Someday, far below in the unseen machinery, a bearing will wear down, a turbine will be decommissioned, and the chord will falter and die, leaving the twelve grazers frozen mid-ratchet in a silent, creeping damp.

So the power sets its hand not on the crawlers, nor on the tufts, but on the threshold.

Outside the twenty-pace pocket, in the vast, humming arteries of the superstructure, it weaves a quiet, imperceptible grace. To any mortal eye or remote console, the chamber’s telemetry remains eternally, boringly nominal. No maintenance schedule ever quite aligns with this bulkhead; no footstep ever turns down the catwalk toward its door. The soft, warm aberrations of the world are gently, endlessly given better things to do elsewhere.

And deeper still, down in the black sub-void where the main shafts drop away into the earth or the sky, the power lays a subtle, unbreakable root beneath the four induction pylons. Not to alter their note. Not to smooth their rare, necessary surges. Only to underwrite the chord against the slow decay of the world outside—so that when the builders are gone, and the neighboring gantries have rusted into the upper atmosphere, and the pale daylight through the quartz slit has shifted through ten thousand ages, the pressure will still hold.

Inside the room, nothing changes.

Along the perimeter plates, a crawler docks beside a crystalline tuft, bridges the circuit with its copper-plated palps, and drinks a sip of static. A micron of silicate is shaved from a frond before it can blur into the roaring air. The dust-webs keep their sharp, silent geometry.

And if the power lingers for a moment longer—just to listen to the single, deafening, unchanging chord—it does so in absolute stillness, loving the room enough to leave it entirely alone.
