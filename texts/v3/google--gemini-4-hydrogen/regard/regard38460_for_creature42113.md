It does not arrive in light. Light would mean nothing here, except to throw pointless shadows across thrashing bamboo and wet slate. Instead, the power settles into the notch the way the mist does—fitting itself into the narrow nave between the sheared basalt walls, feeling the gale compress, taking the measure of the cold.

It listens with something deeper than ears. It feels the three-way war in the dark: the young mountain trying to slide, the cloud forest trying to swallow the stone, and the bolted-down iron hammering blindly through the night.

And then it finds the fourth thing, wedged into the seams where the iron meets the rock.

To a lesser eye, the creature looks like a mistake—a smear of tarry grit trapped in an industrial wound, clinging to rusted U-bolts while diesel fumes rain down through the ferns. Benevolence, at first, feels the familiar pull to mend. It is an ancient reflex: *Here is something cold, blind, and battered. Here is something living on poison and noise.*

So the power pauses, and before it moves a single grain of slate, it traces the shape of every mercy it might offer.

It considers quieting the wind and warming the air. Instantly, in its mind’s eye, it sees the consequence: the sixty-hertz hum dying out of the taut cables, the cooling fans slowing, the nocturnal gale dropping to a gentle breeze. Inside the creature’s fibrous hide, the tiny cartilage chambers go still. The grains of quartz and magnetite settle to the bottom. The thick, oily blood pools and stiffens, and the creature flattens against the cooling steel, hardening into a lifeless patch of pitch. To give it peace would be to stop its heart.

It considers tearing out the rusted generators, hauling the armored cables out of the moss, and washing the diesel soot and hydraulic weeping from the ridge—giving the mountain back to the dwarf trees and the mist. Again, it sees the end: the creature’s acid-secreting underside resting on quiet, unmoving basalt, starved of mineral slurry, starved of ozone, starved of the shudder that wakes its dormant knots. A clean mountain would be a tomb.

It considers changing the creature itself. Why not give it a true heart, so it does not have to borrow violence from a machine? Why not give it eyes to see the amber LEDs, or legs to climb down out of the frost into a gentler valley?

Here, wisdom stops the hand of benevolence altogether.

To give this being a heart, eyes, and legs would not be to save it. It would be to erase it, and put a different creature in its place—one that would never know how to drink the ozone off a wet insulator, or wrap itself like a living gasket around a grinding anchor bolt, or ride a slate avalanche as a handful of tough, oil-rich seeds flung into the dark. It would be a declaration that what lives here is not worthy of its own life.

The power stays very still in the thrashing dark. It watches a generator governor surge under load; it feels the faint electromagnetic twitch in a cluster of magnetite grains as a blue-white arc cracks across a damp insulator. It watches a black, grit-armored mat creep an inch along a hydraulic hose by sheer hydraulic stubbornness, and pull back from the orange sear of an exhaust manifold, leaving its ring of dead crust behind.

It understands, then, that this is not a tragedy. There is no captivity here, and no exile. Out of a landslide, a storm, and a handful of bolted-down engines, something utterly improbable has taught itself to live. It has taken the worst noises of two blind worlds colliding and turned them into a pulse.

And so, what does the benevolent and wise power do?

It holds its hand back.

It leaves the gale to howl through the church-wide gap. It leaves the diesel to hammer and the rusted turnbuckles to groan. It leaves the creature blind, flat, and stubborn in the dark—knowing that one day the fuel will run out, or a U-bolt will shear, and the iron will tip sideways into the talus and go cold. Knowing that the creature will lock its edges down, harden into pitch, and wait while the wet, frantic forest finally grows over them both.

Even that, the power does not steal from it. To be temporary is not to be broken.

It offers no miracle, no shelter, no light. It simply stays for a moment in the roaring dark, witnessing the whole fierce equilibrium—the rock, the moss, the iron, and the black thing in the seam—and then withdraws as quietly as it came, leaving the sixty-hertz hum to keep the blood moving.
