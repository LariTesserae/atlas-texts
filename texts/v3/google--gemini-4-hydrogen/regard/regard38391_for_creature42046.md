When a benevolent and wise power turns its attention to the valley, it does not arrive as a storm or a visitor. It arrives as a stillness—though even stillness, here, must take care not to press against the air.

At first, looking down from beyond the pale blue zenith, the power sees what any creature of time would see: a trap. A city choked in its own green fullness, unable to drop a leaf. Water that cannot reach the sea, forced to climb its own cliffside in endless, scalding loops. A deafening, unvarying chord that has forgotten how to resolve.

Benevolence, by its nature, wants to ease a burden. It looks at the tall, narrow forms wedged into the travertine and sees creatures pinned in a vise of steam and stone, vibrating so hard they have blurred into stillness. It feels the heat radiating from the limestone needles and thinks: *I could bring them night. I could send a cool wind across the cloud-floor, just enough to drop the note by a single tone, just enough for a shadow to lengthen and a leaf to fall.*

It is easy for a great power to mistake its own mercies for universal goods. A creature that lives in sequence—that knows the relief of an exhale after an inhale, of dusk after noon—naturally imagines that a single, unbroken note must be an agony.

So the power does not act yet. Wisdom is the patience to listen before speaking, and here, listening requires learning a different kind of grammar.

It lowers its attention—carefully, keeping its presence weightless so as not to cast a shadow across the terraces or deflect a single updraft—and rests at the edge of the valley’s roar. It does not speak to the beings in the travertine, because a word has a beginning and an end, and to introduce a *before* and *after* into this basin would be like dropping a stone into a tuned bell. Instead, it simply matches its own stillness to their vibration, and feels what is there.

There is no grief. There is no patience, either, because patience is the endurance of time passing, and here nothing passes.

The power feels what the being feels: the crisp, instantaneous pressure of a droplet locking into a geometric lattice in a sunken plaza, known in the exact same instant by pale filaments hanging over the endless drop three ridges away. It feels the smooth, effortless bend of the fourth-tier waterfall curving back into its cistern—not a struggle against gravity, but the natural shape of a world held in tension. It feels the thick, breathless saturation of the air, the dense lens of vapor bending the noon glare backward, the unanimous, spatial hum that *is* the city’s bones.

The being is not holding up a ceiling that threatens to crush it. It is not a prisoner gripping the bars. It is a chord, and a chord does not wish to become a silence.

The power looks at what its "mercy" would have done. If it had cooled the air by a fraction of a degree, the vapor-lenses would have thinned, the shadows would have swung away from the sun, and the chord would have fractured. The cascades would have remembered how to fall, plunging straight through the white cloud-floor miles below. The geometric lattices would have washed out into ordinary, forgetting ripples. The moss would have yellowed, rotted, and sloughed off the travertine. And the spatial mind—so vast and whole across the valley—would have shattered into isolated, hollow husks, experiencing time for the first and final moment as they dried and cracked in the quiet.

To give them a tomorrow would be to give them an ending.

So the power withdraws its hand from the basin, having touched nothing.

Yet benevolence and wisdom are not idle, even when they choose restraint. If the power does anything at all, it does it on the far side of the horizon—out beyond the jagged limestone needles, and deep beneath the featureless floor of white cloud, where the wider world still moves and changes.

It looks to the great, slow engines of the world outside: the wandering storms that might one day drift high enough to chill the zenith, the tectonic shifts that might tilt the precipices, the curious or ambitious travelers from the lands of time who might climb the needles, plug a flute to hear what happens, or dip a hand into a sunken pool to break its lattice.

Quietly, without building a wall that would block the light or a dome that would trap the updrafts, the power bends the paths of those outside things gently aside. It leaves the sun where it hangs, and the cloud-floor where it steams, and the valley open to the pale blue sky.

Then, having done the only thing that leaves no mark, it steps back—and lets the city keep its noon.
