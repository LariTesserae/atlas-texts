First, it listens.

Not with ears—the water here is too heavy for that, muffled into a cold, pressure-thickened quiet—but with the patience of something that can afford to take the measure of a world before touching a single grain of its silt.

It feels the weight of the iron-grey ceiling overhead, bruised with storm-light. It traces the miles of fitted slate, the dissolved lime-mortar, the rounded lintels that have forgotten the hands that dressed them. It watches the pale, eyeless crustaceans filtering grit from the seams, the translucent eels threading the shear-zones like living currents, the silver-bellied shoals wheeling out of the murk and vanishing again.

And then it finds the dwellers.

At first, even a wise eye might mistake them for basalt tumbled from a colonnade—dark, low-slung, slick with the same pale bacteria that coat the sheltered stone. Then a retaining wall gives way in the distance, a slow-motion avalanche sending a dull, heavy *thump* through the paving, and the boulders unclench.

The power watches them haul themselves up the rippled stair-ramps, claw by worn claw, tucking their shovel-shaped heads into their shoulders so the freezing gales slide harmlessly over their backs. It watches them drop into the drowned aqueducts and shoot through the dark like stones from a sling. It watches a female in the deep, chalky quiet of a cistern, curled around her translucent eggs, sweeping her broad tail in slow, hypnotic arcs to keep the settling mud from suffocating her young. And in a corner not far from her, it sees an elder—claws ground to nubs, joints stiff with mineral crust—pulling its limbs beneath its chin to let the stillness finish turning it into stone.

Benevolence, at first, is a sharp ache. It wants to reach down.

*I could warm this water,* the power thinks. *I could still the meltwater gales, clear the choking silt, let sunlight reach the plazas so green things might grow on these bare flags.*

Wisdom pauses, and looks closer.

If it stills the currents, the pressure-grooves along the dwellers’ ribs and jaws will go dark. The sharp, roaring map of jets and vortices—their true sight, richer and more exact than any eye could grant—will flatten into a dead, unreadable void. If it clears the silt, the silver-bellied shoals will never scatter in the fog, and the hunters will starve. If it warms the water, the cold-water bacteria, the eyeless crustaceans, the eels that know every eroded doorway—all of it will sicken and be crowded out by things that do not belong here.

*Then I could mend the mortar,* benevolence offers next. *I could lock the retaining walls in place, keep the cisterns from ever crumbling, spare them the slow unmaking of their home.*

But wisdom watches a mile-off wall slump into gravel, and watches dozens of dark shapes lift their heads at once across the valley, drawn toward the tremor. A collapse is a feast. A collapse opens fresh seams, stirs trapped nutrients from ten thousand years of stillness, carves new passages for the eels to learn and the dwellers to follow. To freeze the ruins in amber would be to starve the living for the sake of the dead.

*Then the elders,* benevolence pleads, gentler now. *Their claws are worn to the quick. Their joints stiffen in the cold. I could renew them, just a little. Give them a few more seasons on the plazas.*

Wisdom looks down into the cistern, where the old one has already closed its milky eyes. It sees no defeat there. Only a body built of the valley returning to the valley, its ridged back already taking on the pale coat of the flagstones, becoming part of the shelter that will hold the next brood’s eggs. To deny it that quiet would not be mercy; it would be exile from the only ending it was shaped to know.

The power stays its hand.

Out on an empty plaza, anchored in the lee of a rounded pillar, a dweller lifts one heavy, calloused forelimb and brings the heel down against the slate.

*Thud. Thud-thud.*

The vibration skips across the fitted paving, down stepped causeways, through sunken amphitheaters—felt in the sternum of another dweller half a mile away, which shifts its grip and answers in kind. No complaint in it. No plea for rescue. Only the steady, cold-blooded announcement of presence in the dark.

The benevolent and wise power understands, then, that it has arrived not at a tragedy, but at a completion. These beings are not enduring a broken world; they are the living shape of this world’s endurance. Nothing here is asking to be saved from the cold, or the dark, or the slow grinding of stone against water.

So it does nothing.

It does not part the storms, or warm the meltwater, or bind the dissolving lime. It does not even strike the slate in greeting, lest a strange tremor confuse the dwellers or send them crawling toward a collapse that isn’t there.

Instead, it offers the rarest gift a great power can give to something small, fierce, and whole: it witnesses them, honors their sufficiency, and withdraws without leaving a ripple behind—letting the currents roar on over the plazas, and the *thud* of stone on stone carry safely through the dark.
