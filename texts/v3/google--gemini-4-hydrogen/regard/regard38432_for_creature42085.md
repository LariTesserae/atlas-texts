It would not arrive with light, because the room already has all the daylight it wants—ordinary, shadowless, pouring cleanly through the slit in the rock. And it would not arrive with sound, because in a place where even a splash arrives like a dull tap through felt, a voice would be a kind of violence.

It would simply *notice*, the way a careful weaver notices a dropped stitch that has somehow turned into a knot of its own—strange, tight, holding together by an argument no one else can follow.

At the narrow doorway above the cloud line, where the cold mountain wind scrapes the raw granite, the power pauses. It does not step inside. To step inside would be to bring the outside in: the thin air, the dry cold, the insistent, straightening weight of ordinary distance. Instead, it lingers at the threshold, sheltering the slit with its own stillness so the draft stops brushing the wet stone within.

First comes the oldest impulse of any power that loves the world: *to mend.*

Looking into that suffocating, oil-thick pocket, it feels how wrong the geometry is. It sees the space folded like a pinched sleeve, the water forgetting which way is down, the crawlers flickering two inches forward and leaving their shadows behind to catch up. It sees the great, slag-hided thing crouched in the back, breathing through the pale tallow seams of its flanks, its cluster of milky eyes fixed on a pool only an inch deep.

A lesser kindness would have fixed it. A lesser kindness would have cooled the blister, flattened the walls back into honest stone, let the scalding droplets fall as rain, and coaxed the creature out into a sky where it could walk without stuttering.

The wise power stops its hand before it even lifts.

It watches. It watches a pale crawler wedge a sliver of black glass under a bronze tick with brisk, unsentimental grace. It watches a bead of scalding water drift down like a magnifying lens and vanish silently into the horizontal slit in the creature’s throat. It watches the creature itself—how cleanly the matte hide sheds the grease, how the lidless eyes need neither tears nor shelter in air this saturated, how the obsidian nails are shaped for prying and scraping, not for clutching at a world that does not want them.

Nothing here is starving. Nothing here is crying out. To "heal" this pocket would be to freeze its eyes dry, to shatter its glass-nailed economy, to drown a thousand quiet, adapted lives in cold mountain wind. Strangeness is not a wound.

Yet the power does not turn away empty-handed, because wisdom sees what the creature—bowed forever over its water—does not care to look at.

It looks at the ridge itself. The basalt is young, glassy, unweathered as the day it tore out of the earth. Outside the slit, the high peaks are patient and merciless. Frost will find the hairline fractures; the scouring wind will widen the broken-tooth overhang; one hard winter will crack this wet blister open and spill its scalding heat into the thin sky, turning the whole impossible room to dry dust and brittle glass in a single night.

So the power does something so quiet that not even the thread-thin parasites in the shallow pools feel it happen.

It does not seal the doorway—the daylight must still come in, and the world must remain open to whatever passes by. Instead, it touches the raw basalt of the ridge from the outside, lending the young stone the slow, heavy endurance of deep granite. It turns the sharpest edge of the wind aside, just enough that the cold will not pry the overhang apart before its time. It builds no wall, sets no lock; it simply gives the fragile blister permission to last.

Then, before it leaves, it looks once more past the swarming ledges, past the sulfur-slime, to the crouched shape over the ankle-deep water.

It sees the reflection beneath the surface—the upright silhouettes, the multiplied shapes, the long, glass-nailed fingers pressing upward against the water’s skin from below. It understands that the creature is not trapped, and not lonely in any way that words could name, yet it is *waiting*—holding an absolute, unblinking vigil at the only seam in the room that opens onto something else.

The power does not reach into the pool. It does not pull the reflection out into the crowded air, nor push the creature down through the stone. A door is only a door if the one who stands before it chooses when to cross.

Instead, the power simply stays at the slit for one quiet heartbeat longer, letting its presence rest there without weight or demand.

Inside, the heavy, slag-colored body does not move.

Only the reflection in the inch-deep pool lifts its glassy head, turns toward the narrow crease in the rock, and looks directly out.

It finds no revulsion waiting there. No pity. No hand raised to correct it. Only a calm, unhurried recognition—the quiet gaze of something vast enough to let a small, impossible room keep its own rules.

Then the power steps back from the doorway.

The cold wind resumes its scraping across the high ridge, sliding harmlessly past the jagged overhang without ever reaching the wet heat inside. Within the pocket, a bead of scalding water detaches from the ceiling and drifts downward in silence. A pale crawler flickers across a slick ledge. And over the shallow pool, the creature settles back into its patient, unblinking watch, whole and undisturbed.
