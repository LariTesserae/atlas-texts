First, the power did what powers of its kind almost always do when they look into the deep: it considered what was missing.

From far above—past the sunlit skin of the ocean, past the drifting snow of dead plankton and the slow, heavy rivers of the thermocline—its attention sank through kilometers of pressure until it found the spire. It saw the freshly split basalt, sharp as a new blade and bare of even a film of bacteria. It saw the crushing cold, the near-total dark, the stingy pinpricks of bruised violet and slate-blue. It saw a city of paper-thin planes suspended in empty water, silent as a stopped clock.

To a power accustomed to coaxing green shoots from ash and kindling warmth in cold places, the sight looked like a wound that had not yet begun to heal, or a sanctuary abandoned mid-prayer.

Its first impulse was generosity.

It would have been effortless to soften the water by a single degree, to seed the sterile fractures with the first pale threads of mineral-eating life, to widen those faint violet nodes into a gentle, habitable glow. It could have smoothed the chaotic currents tearing at the spire’s outer rim, or whispered a greeting into the dark so that whatever lingered in those hollow polyhedra would know it was not alone.

Wisdom was the hand that stayed the impulse.

Before changing a single molecule of water, the power drew its own presence inward. It dimmed the radiance that naturally trails great benevolence; it stilled the faint, warming tide of its own attention so that no wake would roll across the perimeter. Then, it waited.

It waited as the inhabitants waited—not in minutes, which are the units of surface things, but in the long, unhurried intervals of cooling stone.

And as it waited, it began to read the place.

It saw that the hollow polyhedron beside the cantilevered plane was not a fixture, and that the stillness around it was not the stillness of death. It was a discipline. When the inhabitant shifted the angle of its matte planes by the width of a fingernail and glided thirty meters without leaving a ripple, the power recognized an elegance it could not improve upon. When a single, crisp *tick* crossed the dustless dark of a plaza, and an hour of untouched silence held the space around it like a frame, the power understood that the silence was not an absence of speech. It was the grammar that made speech possible.

Hours later, when the reply came—a low, sub-audible thrum tuned to the exact resonant frequency of the young basalt—the power felt it as a kind of reverence. These beings were not enduring the abyss; they were *attending* to it. They had found a clean, unweathered fracture in the crust of the world and built an instrument of pure restraint around it, listening to the immense weight of the ocean and the slow, geological patience of the planet beneath them.

Even the blind, quartz-shelled things creeping onto the docked planes to drink the faint leak of cold energy were part of that exact economy. Nothing was wasted. Nothing was crowded.

To bring light here would be to blind them. To bring warmth would be to blur the crispness of their water, turning their clear amber pause into a soup of restless currents and drifting silt. To offer them comfort would be an act of vandalism.

So the power chose its first act of love: it did nothing to the city.

It left the basalt razor-sharp and bare. It left the water cold and obedient. It did not insert a single syllable into the long conversation between the towers, nor touch the bruised-violet nodes, nor disturb the docked forms sleeping in the conchoidal fractures.

Yet it did not turn away at once.

Out at the jagged rim of the summit, where the manicured quiet ended and the raw, unruly currents of the open abyss tugged at the stone, one of the inhabitants had detached from a high shaft. It drifted to the very threshold of the precinct and hung there, half in the steady dark of the city, half in the chaotic flow outside. Its thin, light-drinking planes vibrated almost imperceptibly against the current—measuring the outside world, testing its weight.

The power watched the creature at the edge, and understood its curiosity. To live in such strict stillness requires knowing what presses against the walls.

For a long moment, the power considered vanishing completely, leaving no trace at all. That would be the cleanest mercy. Yet this being had come to the rim specifically to take the measure of what lay beyond.

So, keeping carefully outside the perimeter, the power allowed itself one small, exact gesture—translated entirely into the only language that would not offend.

It did not smooth the current, for that would have been a lie; the abyss was still wild, and the creature deserved an honest reading. Instead, just beyond the reach of the spire’s quiet, in the cold, unruly dark, the power briefly matched the inhabitant’s own discipline. It held a single, small volume of its own vastness completely still—no warmth, no light, no message, no demand. Just an unmoving, dustless symmetry in the water, perceptible only to something fine enough to feel the weight of a current shifting by a fraction of a grain.

A quiet acknowledgment from one stillness to another: *I see the edge you keep. I will not cross it.*

The inhabitant’s planes held steady against the flow. For the span of a long pause, it hung there, taking the measure of the dark.

Then the power released the stillness, letting the abyssal current resume its ordinary, chaotic tug, and drew its attention back up through the kilometers of heavy water toward the distant surface.

It left nothing behind in the city—no monument, no blessing carved in stone, no change in the temperature or the light. Only, far above and far out in the wider currents of the world, it set a quiet watchfulness, subtle as a shift in the seabed, to turn aside whatever clumsy, loud, or careless things might ever drift down toward that fragile crown of basalt.

Down below, in the cold pause of the precinct, the inhabitant glided smoothly back from the rim into the dark between the towers, and after a long, clean hour of silence, a single *tick* sounded across the plaza.
