The first thing a wise power does, upon finding a place like this, is dim itself.

To arrive here blazing with light, or radiating the warm, frantic energy of the outer universe, would be an act of violence. A single careless degree of heat would quench the superconducting veins of the kelp-curtains, shatter the delicate arithmetic of the supercooled drips, and turn the cathedral’s hum into the hiss of scalded sap. Benevolence, here, begins with restraint.

So the power folds its brilliance away. It sheds its own haste—the swift, star-spanning tempo of its usual thoughts—and steps down, octave by octave, into the cavern’s clock. It slows until the swell of a single droplet on a graphite frond feels like the gathering of a season; until the three-second starburst of needle-ice in a puddle is a long, graceful ballet for the waking thread-worms; until the six-minute breath of a bladder-bellied boomer is a steady, comforting tide.

Only then does it listen.

It does not listen as a conqueror or a scientist, looking for flaws to mend or secrets to harvest. It listens the way a musician listens to an unfamiliar instrument being played in the dark. It traces the transformer-drone of the root-mat, the mechanical *clack-clack-clack* of the frosted-glass climbers, the dull bell-chime of drips striking their discarded husks. It watches a drape-leech uncurl with agonizing patience to drink from a locked limb, and feels no urge to play savior; it watches a hunched pod-breaker rasp warmth from a stolen metallic seed to keep its single egg above freezing, and recognizes the fierce, quiet tenderness of the theft. Nothing here is cruel, and nothing is wasted. Everything is simply keeping the bargain of the cold.

And at the center of the weave, threaded through the oldest braided trunks, it finds the lattice-weaver.

To a fast mind, the weaver would seem inert, a mere tangle of pale-violet nerve-filaments. Down here, in the true tempo of the dark, the power feels the weaver’s thoughts moving—vast, deliberate currents drifting from one side of the chamber to the other over the span of hours. It feels the weaver’s quiet satisfaction as a climber clears rime-ice from a light-bending bud; its subtle, coaxing pinch on a plant-bladder to tune the room’s resonance; its blind, patient curiosity as it nudges the root-tips outward against the raw basalt.

The power looks *with* the weaver, and then it looks *beyond* it—out through the fractured stone walls, into the deep, unlit crust of the planet.

That is where wisdom becomes action.

The cavern is embryonic, still wedging itself open, millimeter by millimeter. And the lattice-weaver, guided only by the softness of the rock immediately against its roots, is coaxing the eastern root-mat toward a promising seam of fractured basalt. It cannot know what lies forty feet behind that seam. The power can. Waiting there in the dark is a pressurized vein of geothermal brine—ancient, sulfurous, and scalding hot. If the roots split that seam, even decades from now, the sanctuary will be flooded in minutes. The cold will break, the copper-tufted slugs will boil in their puddles, and the cathedral will die before it has finished its first verse.

A lesser power might erect a wall of gleaming, impossible metal to block the vein, leaving an alien scar in a place where nothing was ever built.

Instead, this power works in the cavern’s own grammar.

Over the span of a single boomer’s exhale, it reaches gently into the stone ahead of the eastern roots. It does not blast or shatter; it simply *persuades* the geology. Along the path toward the scalding brine, it coaxes the basalt to anneal—drawing the microscopic fractures shut, pressing the stone into a dense, glassy, unyielding shield that the patient turgor of the roots will taste, find barren, and naturally turn away from. At the same time, along the western wall, it breathes a hairline weakness into a deep, sprawling stratum of cold, iron-rich rock—a vast, quiet shelf of stone that will feed the seam-pressers for ten thousand years and give the cathedral room to grow safely away from the planet’s fire.

Then, the power looks *upward*, through miles of crust toward the surface and the distant, restless future. It knows what happens when fast, warm-blooded, tool-making things discover room-temperature superconductors growing feral in the dark. They do not bring patience; they bring drills, and cages, and light.

So it drapes a gentle indifference over the pocket. Not a barrier—just a slight, seamless bend in the way vibrations travel through the overlying rock, a subtle dullness to any future scan or seismic echo, so that to the hungry world above, this cathedral will always read as nothing more than solid, uninteresting stone.

Before it withdraws, the power lingers for one more cycle of the mist.

It knows the lattice-weaver cannot comprehend a god, nor would it want to. It has no use for worship, and no words for gratitude. Yet the power is benevolent, and kindness sometimes asks for a small, unnecessary grace—simply to let a living thing know it is not entirely alone in the dark.

It waits until the weaver begins a slow, sweeping thought—a pulse of current travelling from the central trunks out toward the perimeter, guiding the next *ping* of splitting stone. Gently, without altering the voltage by a fraction enough to singe a single root-hair, the power lays a matching harmonic alongside it.

It is not a command, and not a message. It is just a chord—a deep, cold, resonant *yes* that ripples through the organ-pipe bladders, sets the needle-ice shivering in the pools, and makes the copper tufts of a hundred blind slugs flare together in a brief, silent constellation of violet sparks.

The lattice-weaver pauses. Across the span of two hours, its distributed mind turns the strange, warm-less chord over, tasting its vast, gentle steadiness. Then, satisfied, it folds the memory into the hum of its own veins and goes back to nudging the roots toward the western stone.

The power steps back, returning to its own swift, burning stars, leaving the cathedral exactly as it found it: freezing, deafening, unfinished, and safe to become whatever slow miracle it was meant to be.
