The attention does not arrive with light. Light would mean nothing here, and a blaze of glory would only scald the pale, translucent things in the water and blind the delicate membranes of a creature that has no use for sun.

Instead, it settles into the pocket like a change in barometric pressure—a sudden, vast coherence in the cold air, as if the dark itself has leaned closer to listen.

*THUMP.*

The floor jars. The water slams the wedged basalt. Blue static snaps across the weeping magnetite, and the pale ferns shake their spores into the mist.

*HISS.*

The displaced air screams through the hairline cracks—except around the upper hollow, where it rises into that low, patient, three-note chord.

*Pause.*

In that breathless sliver of quiet before the next surge, the power takes the measure of the room.

It sees what a lesser kindness would try to mend. A lesser kindness would look at the biting chill, the razor-cleaved stone, the deafening violence every four seconds, and feel pity. It would want to warm the air, hush the pistons, smooth the jagged walls, and open those milky, sunken eyes so the creature might see the sky.

Wisdom stays its hand.

To warm this room would be to rot the dark-emerald liverwort that drinks the stray current, and to suffocate the cold-water salamanders in their own broth. To silence the *THUMP* would be to stop the creature’s clock—the very pulse by which it times its lungs, its strikes, its long, still rests. And to give it eyes in a room of strobing ozone and black stone would be a cruelty, trading a world of exquisite, shimmering geometry for glare and shadow.

This being is not trapped in a dark hole. It has climbed up out of the muffled aquifer and found an orchestra. It has wired its own bed for warmth, taught the wind to sing in thirds, and turned the lightning into a sheepdog for its supper. It does not need to be rescued from its life. It only needs its life not to be cut short before the song is finished.

So the power looks with longer eyes—geological eyes—at the things the creature’s jaw-filaments cannot reach.

High above, at the frost-rimmed lip where the earth tore open, a heavy wedge of basalt hangs by a thinning tooth of stone. Two more hard winters of freezing meltwater would pry it loose, dropping a ton of rock straight through the ceiling vault to crush the moss bed and seal the throat of the chamber.

The power does not close the sky-crack, nor banish the frost; the gray blade of daylight and the cold draft are part of the engine. Instead, it lays a quiet weight upon the upper seam. With a sound no louder than a settling pebble, lost entirely inside the next *THUMP*, the hanging wedge slips half an inch sideways and locks against its neighbor. A guillotine becomes a keystone. The vault will hold for a thousand winters.

Deep beneath the floor, in the drowned labyrinth no light has ever touched, a mat of dead bog-oak roots is creeping down a fissure toward the main intake, tangled with peat-silt, preparing to choke the surge within a dozen seasons. The power does not replace the loose piston-slabs with eternal stone, nor make the copper seams inexhaustible—a living world must be allowed to wear and change. It only loosens the root-tangle, letting the pressurized water tear it free and flush it harmlessly away into a side-passage.

Nothing in the room is altered. The air still tastes of wet iron and ozone. The walls still weep and bite.

Yet on its steaming mat of moss, the keeper of the circuit has stopped weeding.

Its smooth, wedge-shaped brow is lifted toward the center of the hollow. The sparse dark filaments along its jaw and wrists are flared wide, trembling. It cannot see the presence, and there are no boots in the basin to bend the electric field—only an unmistakable, gentle stillness resting against the current, like a warm hand held an inch above a vibrating wire without touching it.

When the *HISS* rushes out through the tuned splinters of quartz, the creature does what a maker does when it meets the unknown. It reaches out one long, verdigris-stained finger and nudges a bright flake of copper across the magnetite seam.

A thin blue arc snaps across the wet rock, grounding into the hollow’s empty center. *Tap.*

In the quiet pause before the next beat, the power answers.

It does not speak in words, which would rattle the water like stones, nor in visions, which have no home behind eyeless brows. Instead, it breathes a single, feather-light ripple across the chamber’s own electric field—just strong enough to lift the fine filaments along the creature’s wrists, carrying the exact three-note hum of its tuned vents, with a fourth, quiet interval resting cleanly above it.

Then the *THUMP* hits.

Up through a narrow fissure beside the moss bed, carried on the surge of the groundwater, washes a single finger-length crystal of clear quartz. Unlike the dull, flaky copper that turns green in the damp, this stone is threaded inside with a hair-thin vein of pure, bright gold—something that will not corrode, something that holds a charge differently, waiting to be wedged into a crack or bridged across a seam.

The power withdraws, leaving no lingering glow, no miracle to worship, no monument to clutter the dark.

*HISS.*

In the dim gray blade of daylight, a narrow, multi-jointed forearm unfolds from the warm moss. Long, iron-nailed fingers close gently around the gold-veined quartz, turning it over in the cold mist, testing its edge against the beat of the stone.
