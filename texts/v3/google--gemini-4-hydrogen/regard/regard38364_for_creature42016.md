It arrives without displacing a single spore.

In a chamber tuned to turn a falling drop into a mallet-strike, anything grand—a voice, a parting of the bark, a sudden shift in the air—would be an assault. So the power enters the only way this room can bear: as a quality of attention, settling into the stolen noon like warmth soaking into dry heartwood.

The woodlouse notices first. Its pale, segmented legs pause at the rim of the scraped basin, antennae testing an air that has not changed temperature yet feels, by some unmeasurable fraction, less frantic.

Then the dweller feels it—not in their ears, which are already full of the xylem’s hydraulic groan, but through the palm pressed flat against the pale cambium floor. Beneath the teeth-vibrating hum, beneath the *crack* of parting fibers, the drumhead steadies. Just enough to make the dweller’s dark-seeded pupils narrow, searching the clean, empty void.

There is nothing to see. The shadows still pool strangely under their raised wrist and lift along the torn ridges of the wall. The afternoon light still hangs bright and sourceless in the close air.

The power looks at everything in the blister at once, and for a long moment, it simply listens.

It sees the economy of the place: the three lengths of stripped, knotted bast funneling sweat from the root-node; the shallow basin scraped into the living floor; the single chipped flake of pale stone wedged into the weeping seam. It sees the scars on the dweller’s knuckles where splinters have been dug out, the amber crusts of resin along their wrists, the slackness of their jaw held carefully against the ache of the wood’s vibration. It sees how they share the lukewarm, slightly sweet water with a blind creature no larger than a thumb.

A lesser mercy would have acted at once. A power that was only kind, and not wise, would have felt the sharp sting of pity—would have reached down through the miles of tangled root, parted the living walls, and lifted this lean, pale shoot of a person out into some gentler air. Or, if there was no longer any soil or stone to lift them to, it would have hushed the roaring xylem, smoothed the splintered heartwood into soft walls, hung a lamp, laid down a mat, and healed the plant’s confusion so the roots remembered which way was down.

The power stays its hand.

To silence the room would be to blind the one who reads it by its sound. To tear open the blister would be to shatter the only sky this root-system remembers. Most of all, to replace three knotted fibers and one shard of vanished stone with effortless comfort would be to look at a creature who has learned to keep a fragile, roaring peace—and tell them their patience meant nothing.

Still, wisdom is not indifference. The power has seen the thinness of the margin. It has seen how hard the stone flake is working, and how close the chamber is to tearing itself shut in its own stutter of growth.

So it goes to work, so quietly that the dweller only feels it as a change in the weather of the wood.

First, it lays its attention on the plant—not forcing the vast, confused colossus to forget its afternoon, and not sealing the wound, only easing the panic in the sap. The backward-healing stutter softens. The torn ridges still weep clear resin, and the seam will still close if the dweller neglects their wedge, but the wood stops snapping against itself in blind haste. The engine still runs at full speed just behind the bark, readable and alive, yet the frequency that aches in human bone gentles by a fraction, like an instrument tuned a hair’s breadth warmer.

Next, it touches the stretched emptiness in the middle of the room. It does not fill the void—clutter here would be a cruelty—but it untangles the knot in the space between wall and wall, so that distance becomes honest again. Nothing blocks the light. Nothing hangs from the ceiling. Only now, if the dweller ever wished to reach straight across the open air, their hand would arrive where they meant it to.

Then it looks at the single chip of pale stone wedged into the seam. The dweller’s thumb rests against it, waiting for the next drop to fall. The power does not trade it for a finer tool; instead, it folds a quiet, enduring density into that one small edge—the stubbornness of the vanished mountains it came from—so it will not crumble or dull when this lung finally closes and another must be pried open.

And finally, it gives the only gift that cannot be scraped from cambium or knotted from bast.

Ripples stir across the lukewarm water in the basin. The dweller freezes. A heavy drop detaches from the upper pith, falls through the bright void, and strikes the taut membrane of the floor like a mallet on wet hide. In the ringing aftermath—while the wood groans and a fresh fiber parts somewhere behind the wall—the dweller twists the stone flake, clears a bead of sap from the knotted bast, and presses their cheekbone briefly to the warm bark to read the pulse.

This time, woven deep beneath the hydraulic roar of the roots, they feel something else: a faint, unmistakable steadiness, like a large hand resting lightly on the outside of the wall. Not pressing in. Not pulling them out. Simply bearing witness to the weight of the world pressing around them.

And far off through the dark xylem, a few fathoms deeper where the sap is beginning to strain, a thin place warms—patient, unhurried, and ready to hold another pocket of stolen noon whenever this one is done.

The power withdraws before the echo of the drop has fully died, leaving no trace that could snag on the resin or rattle on the drumhead floor.

In the bright, shadow-sharp quiet, the dweller dips two fingers into the basin, brings the sweet water to their lips, and leaves the rest for the pale creature waiting at the rim.
