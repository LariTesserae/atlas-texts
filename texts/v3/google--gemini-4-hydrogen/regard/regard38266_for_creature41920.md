At first, looking down from the upper world, the power mistook the basin for a wound.

From above, it looked like nothing so much as a place the planet had forgotten to heal: a jagged, raw-edged scar dropped deep into the crust, trapping white glare and motionless heat. The power—which had mended shattered coastlines, coaxed forests out of ash, and taught dry riverbeds to remember rain—felt the familiar, ache-deep stir of mercy. It began, as it always did, to gather the elements of repair.

A breath of cool wind, first, to break the stagnant heat. A veil of high cloud to soften the punishing glare. A slow, steady rain to wash the razor-sharp quartz into gentler curves, to carry silt down the cliffs, to fill the crevices with clear, cold water.

Then, before it loosed a single breeze, the power leaned closer and listened.

It did not descend as light or air; it simply laid its attention against the rim of the scar and let the basin speak in its own register. For a long while, there was nothing at all. Then, through the motionless heat, the smallest sounds began to arrive: the faint, glassy *tick* of a taut leaf expanding; the single, heavy *plink* of a drop falling from a paddle-tip into a cupped stem below.

The power lowered its attention further, beneath the chartreuse haze of the canopy, down to the springy, woven mat of canes and creepers. And there it found them.

It saw the lean, chalk-dusted figure resting upside-down in the luminous dark beneath the roots, its wide, padded palm pressed flat against a hollow stem. It saw the second eyelid, frosted as sea-glass, drawn calmly against the bounced glare. It saw what the creature was doing—not enduring a punishment, not waiting for rescue, but *listening* to the miles-wide drumhead of its world.

And in a single, clear flash of understanding, the power saw what its mercy would have done.

A breeze, however gentle, would have been a catastrophe. It would have roared across the hollow canes, blinding the creature’s ears to the *tick* of the leaves and the *plink* of the water. It would have toppled the delicate quartz cairns—their maps, their tremor-bells—sending sharp shards clattering across the silent floor. The tall, jade-still stems, having grown for generations without ever learning to bend against lateral force, would have snapped at their bases.

Rain would have been worse. It would have flooded the narrow under-mat where they slept and breathed, washed the protective white chalk from their matte skin, and turned the raw, un-eroded mineral seams into shifting mud that would tear the pale roots loose from the stone. Even a kindness as small as shade, held too long over the rim, would have broken the delicate engine of heat and humidity that beaded water on the undersides of leaves.

To save this place from its harshness would be to unmake it. These beings had not been abandoned in a broken world; they had become the quietest possible expression of a complete one.

So the power uncurled its hand from the sky. It let the clouds it had begun to gather drift elsewhere, toward lands that knew how to drink. It let the upper-world winds keep their course, skimming straight over the jagged lip of the basin without dipping down.

Still, benevolence does not easily walk away empty-handed, and wisdom looks for the seam where care can enter without weight.

The power turned its attention back to the rim—the one place where the basin met the chaos of the world above. Slowly, unhurriedly, it walked the miles-long perimeter of the scar. Where a hairline fracture in the upper cliff threatened to loosen a slab of stone that would one day plunge through the living mat, the power settled the rock deeper into its bed. Where the roots of a fierce, fast-spreading upper-world thorn were creeping toward the edge, ready to drop seeds into the pit and choke the slow, waxy flora below, the power gently turned them back toward the open plain. It wove a quiet permanence into the lip of the basin, ensuring the winds would always pass over, and the landslides would always pass by.

It did nothing to the glare. It did nothing to the heat. It left the quartz sharp and the water scarce.

Before it withdrew, the power lingered one last moment above the chartreuse haze. Deep beneath the woven floor, a lone wanderer was crawling slowly along the roots toward a new seam of moisture. Half a mile away, another pressed its palm to a fluted cane.

*Knock.*

A pause, long enough for the heat to settle, for a single drop of condensation to gather and fall.

*Knock. Knock.*

The power did not answer. It knew better than to speak into a silence that was already whole. It simply stepped back from the rim, and left them to their bright, unmoving peace.
