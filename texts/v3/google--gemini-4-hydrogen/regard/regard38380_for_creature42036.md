It is drawn first by the rising air—a quiet, steady column of warmth lifting forty feet out of the earth into a cooler sky, carrying the faint, sweet scent of wet chalk and nothing else.

The power—old enough to know the difference between stillness and emptiness, and gentle enough not to confuse its own comfort with another’s good—stops at the rim of the oculus. It does not lean over. Even a shadow, falling across the bowl out of season, would interrupt the pendulum of light by which the room keeps time. Instead, it lets its attention settle downward like the dust, weightless and unseen, until it rests among the pale curves of the gypsum.

At first, it looks for what is broken. That is the oldest habit of mercy.

It sees the glare ricocheting from the selenite crystals until there is nowhere for an eye to rest. It sees the salt crusting in the creases of leathery elbows, the bitter pith of the rosettes, the flint flakes dulled and abandoned in the grit. It sees an old one, too stiff now to follow the retreating shade, sitting motionless in its limestone pocket while the afternoon heat tightens its hide against its ribs.

A lesser kindness would have reached in at once.

It would have drawn a veil of cloud across the oculus to cool the kiln—and so broken the night’s exhalation, starving the silver-gray moss of its morning dew until the walnut-sized eggs baked dry in their seams.

It would have cracked the deep rock to let a spring well up in the center of the floor—and so dissolved the soft gypsum, drowned the translucent mites that keep the creatures’ joints from splitting, and turned a patient geometry of millimeter grooves into mud.

It would have thinned the milky membranes over their eyes, or softened their horn-padded thumbs, or whispered to them how to stack stones into a shelter, how to carry water, how to remember a name—and in a single afternoon, it would have invented hurry, and hoarding, and the weight of yesterday.

It would have lifted the dying one out of the midday sun—and stolen its slow return to the chalk, leaving the mites hungry and the wall missing its next thin coat of lime.

The power considers all of these gifts, and sets each one aside, the way a hand lowers a stone it had meant to throw.

Looking closer, it sees that nothing here is asking to be rescued. The opalescent lids already turn the glare into a bearable, silvered dimness. The slow, reptilian hearts are tuned to the exact measure of a dew-drop. The silence is not an absence of song; it is the room they live inside, wide enough to hold the *tink* of flint and the *plip* of water without crowding either one. Even the drying bones in the corner are not a tragedy—only the chamber finishing a sentence it began a hundred years ago.

So inside the bowl, the power does nothing.

It does not cool the air by a fraction of a degree. It does not sharpen a single flake of flint, or nudge a drop of condensation toward a waiting chin. It withholds its own presence completely, knowing that a miracle in a place like this would be indistinguishable from a wound.

Only above, forty feet up, where the fast and careless world runs along the grass, does it linger for a moment.

The rim of the oculus is fragile—a thin lip of limestone hidden in dry scrub. The power does not build a wall around it, nor seal it from the sky; the chamber still needs the summer cloudbursts, the stray swifts that misjudge the updraft, the owl pellets that rain down dry femurs for next season’s scrapers. Instead, it lays a quiet, almost imperceptible bend in the ground a few paces back from the lip. Nothing dramatic—just the subtle tilt of soil and root that makes heavy hooves angle away, that turns a wandering wheel, a brushfire’s edge, or the idle curiosity of iron a few yards to the left or right.

No one passing above would ever notice they had been steered. They would only feel, perhaps, a brief inclination toward easier ground.

Then the power withdraws its attention, leaving the sky-hole open to the sun.

Forty feet below, in the warm, shadowless glare, a pale figure shifts half an inch to follow the cooling stone. Its three-fingered hand closes around a splinter of bird-bone.

*Tink.*

Three slow breaths.

*Tink.*
