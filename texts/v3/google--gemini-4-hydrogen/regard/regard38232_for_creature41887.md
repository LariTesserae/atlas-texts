It would not arrive as a voice from the sky, nor as a pillar of warmth, for anything loud or sudden would be a kind of violence here. Its attention would settle over the gravel plain the way sea-fog settles: without weight, without shadow, taking the shape of the ground.

At first, looking down over the lip of the bowl, even a benevolent power might feel the old, familiar ache to mend.

It would see small, round-shouldered figures wrapped in damp wool and scraped pelts, their fingers red-knuckled from freezing water, their pupils pinned tight against the white bounce of the selenite. It would see them sleeping heaped together in low, heatless rooms, chewing raw tubers and salted fat, never speaking a word. Power, by its nature, wants to answer scarcity with abundance. The first impulse would be to open a hand and give: a hearth that burns without smoke; a softer, amber sun; a spring of warm water bubbling up through the chalk floor; a loosening of the throat so they might sing.

And then wisdom would look a second time, and stay its hand.

It would trace the path of a single drop of condensed air sliding down a carved groove—not falling, never splashing—into a trough where red-gilled newts hang suspended in exact stillness. It would feel how a single degree of warmth would rot the cushion-moss, wake mold in the dry-stone chinks, spoil the cold-sweetened berries, and suffocate the newts in stale, clouded water. It would see that smoke would linger forever in this windless pit, and that a voice raised between these close, pale walls would strike like a dropped stone.

It would watch two figures meet on a stone-slab bridge, turn sideways, and press the backs of their hands lightly against each other’s ribs. It would feel the three slow heartbeats measured through a wool-wrapped wrist. And it would understand that silence here is not an absence, any more than the cold is a wound. These people have not been trapped in a well; they have pared away everything that breaks, rots, or blows away, until nothing is left between them and the world but stone, moss, water, and touch. To "save" them from this would be to shatter the only vessel strong enough to hold them.

So the power would touch nothing inside the rim.

It would not widen the alleys, or brighten the dim rooms behind the split gypsum panes, or lift the perpetual, patient squint from their eyes. It would leave the snails to their algae, the voles to their warm nests behind the rawhide linings, the owl-plumed birds to their silent glides beneath the liverwort eaves.

Instead, if it did anything at all, it would act the way the people themselves act along the moisture grooves—lifting a snail gently from bare stone to thick algae, without disturbing the film of water.

It would set a quiet seal around the lip of the hollow—not a wall, and nothing that could be seen, only a steadying of the chalk bedrock and the rim’s wind-line—so that whatever violent thaws, salt gales, or shifting climates pass across the gravel plain in the centuries to come, the bowl’s own small weather would hold its balance. The air inside would stay just shy of freezing; the glare would stay even; the slow condensation would not fail.

And on the rare mornings when the sea-fog rolls in thick over the plain and two or three bundled figures climb the dished steps to scavenge what they cannot grow—when they walk out blind into the windless grey toward the tideline—the power would not walk beside them or speak to them. It would simply hold the fog steady a little longer, keep the gales back beyond the horizon until they had turned for home, and see to it that the tide had left what was needed within reach: a sound piece of driftwood not yet waterlogged, a length of bone, a seal-carcass untouched by gulls.

They would never know why the fog felt so forgiving, or why the driftwood lay so cleanly above the wrack-line. They would gather what they came for, slip back over the chalk lip before the wind rose, and descend into the bright, damp silence.

And the power, having seen that the cisterns were clear and the newts steady, would withdraw as quietly as it came—leaving them to their own slow pace, unbroken and unimproved.
