If a truly wise power turned its attention to the bell-shaped hollow, it would not arrive as light, and it would not bring silence.

It would understand, before it even crossed the threshold, that light here is an intrusion—something that bleaches the liverwort and drives the salamanders deeper into the moss—and that silence would be a kind of blindness. To enter this place with mercy is to enter it by way of the bone-deep thrum, listening as the creature listens: through the weight of the air and the slow argument between sea and stone.

So the power would first simply *stay*, unseen and unfelt, for the length of a full tidal cycle.

A lesser kindness—one hasty or sentimental—might look at the biting chill, the pewter gloom, the endless roar, and mistake them for hardships. It might be tempted to warm the hearth-stone, to clear the ceiling slit so sun could reach the boulders, to soften the subterranean blast, or to fashion a second creature so the first would not sit alone behind the crescent of cobbles.

Wisdom would stay its hand from all of these.

It would watch the creature wake as the tide pushes into the throat, unfolding without haste so no warmth is wasted. It would see how the creature drinks from the overhangs, how it reads the drop of a rock-wren’s wings by the snag in the standing wave against its cheek. It would wait for that moment on the slick boulders when the dome’s drone rises to its purest pitch, and the creature tilts back its head and locks its single, low note into the wet stone—and seeing how the vibration settles through the creature’s ribs like a hand smoothing out creased cloth, the power would recognize that nothing here is broken, and nothing here is asking to be rescued.

Even the solitude has its own rightness. The chamber is tuned for one voice against the sea’s. A second set of footsteps, a second chest humming out of phase, would only muddle the room.

Still, a benevolent power looks closely, and looks ahead, at the things too slow for a creature of flesh and marrow to notice.

It would look down into the unseen fissure beneath the floor, where centuries of winter swells have been grinding at a shelf of softer marl—a hairline weakness that, in another decade or two of heavy storms, might slump inward and choke the narrow throat, muting the cavern forever. It would look outward, beyond the briar-choked seam in the limestone, to the salt-marsh and the higher ground beyond it, where the world of roads and boots and curiosity grows steadily closer.

And so, if it acted at all, it would act the way the creature itself acts when a cobble in the baffle wall begins to chatter: quietly, with what is already at hand, to keep the room smooth.

Deep in the wet dark below the floor, it would ease the grinding shelf of marl back into alignment with the harder limestone, knitting the fault without leaving a seam—not to silence the sea, only to make sure the pipe keeps its voice for as long as the creature’s ribs can answer it.

Outside, along the low coastal ridge, it would do nothing so obvious as raising a barrier. It would only encourage the blackthorn and briar to tangle a little denser across the cleft, and let the salt-marsh silt stay soft and treacherous to heavy boots, while remaining firm enough for the narrow, otter-like heave of something moving low in the dark. On the nights when the creature slips out to hunt crabs and glasswort, the power might let the coastal fog linger an hour thicker before dawn, giving it unhurried passage home.

And inside the hollow, it would touch nothing. It would leave the cold charcoal on the hearth-stone, the chipped flint in its dry crack, the weathered willow frame, and the notched tally-marks—knowing that the creature’s blunt fingers already have their own quiet use for them.

Only once, perhaps, before it withdrew its attention, it might offer a small, unspoken courtesy. On a rising tide, as the creature sat on the moss-blanketed boulders with its eyes veiled and its throat open to the dark, the power could steady the swirling draft in the vault for the space of three long breaths—just enough to let the standing wave ring clean, without a tremor of grit or a rattle in the stones.

The creature would not know anyone had been there. It would only feel the note lock into its chest a little deeper than usual, and settle back behind its wall when the tide went slack, content with the stone.
