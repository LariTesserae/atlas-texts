The inhabitant is at the entrance before you recognize that anything has come out.

What seemed to be the lowest roof plate has shifted forward. Beneath it, two pale supports have found the ground, then two more. The plate is not stone, though small pieces of stone adhere to it. Water runs from its edges. It settles beside the warm threshold and remains there, broad and low, with the stillness of something accustomed to being overlooked.

Its back would reach a little below your knee. Length is harder to judge. Much of the body lies under a covering of overlapping plates, and the rear narrows into a blunt, flexible portion that remains inside the shelter. Fully emerged, it might be as long as a person lying down.

There is no distinct head.

At the forward end, the plates become smaller and spread apart around a thick fold of skin. Two dark recesses sit beneath the fold. Occasionally something clear moves across one of them, catching the light like the surface of a bubble. Below these are several short, pale feelers, held against the ground. They explore without waving: pressing, releasing, pressing again.

The animal has six limbs. The front pair are longer than the others and can fold back almost completely beneath the body. Each ends in a broad pad divided into three blunt lobes. A hard edge runs along the inner lobe. You will later see it hooked beneath a slab.

The other four limbs carry its weight. Their pads spread on wet rock and contract on loose chips, drawing the angular pieces inward before the foot lifts. When it walks, the body moves very little up and down. Only the plates shift, opening narrow black lines along its sides.

The gray polish on the entrance stones is made by those sides.

A waxy secretion gathers along the lower margins of its armor. It leaves a dull streak wherever the animal squeezes past rock. The substance appears to protect the soft seams from salt and abrasion; it also allows a body of this width to pass through an entrance that looks too narrow for it. The plates tilt, the skin folds, and the creature goes through almost sideways.

On its back are several older injuries. One plate has regrown as two unequal pieces. Another bears a white patch, mineral rather than scar tissue, with a small violet fringe at its wet edge. The same film found in the pools lives there.

For several minutes the animal does nothing visible.

Then a deep note passes through the plain. You feel it under your feet. The creature raises the forward edge of its body slightly. Along both flanks, soft folds open between the plates.

They are thin, closely pleated, and dark red where light reaches their interiors. Water drains from them in fine threads. These folds are both protected breathing surfaces and sensitive membranes. The creature keeps them covered while crossing sharp ground, but here, beside its threshold, it exposes them to the air moving through the shelter.

Its response to the sound is precise. A high whistle from a nearby cleft produces no movement. A distant chamber knocking beneath the stones causes three of the folds to close. When the low note returns, the long front limbs shift, one before the other, and the animal turns toward the channel below the rise.

It listens partly through the ground. The pale feelers under its front edge are more elaborate than they first appeared: each divides at the tip into several fine, stiff branches. They rest on separate grains of stone. As it moves, it repeatedly lowers them, pauses, and chooses another foothold.

In this place, sight cannot tell it whether a passage is flooded beneath its apparent floor, or whether a roof has thinned to a plate ready to fall. The vibrations reaching those feelers can.

It descends to the channel by a route that appears unnecessarily indirect. It passes a level slab, circles a cluster of knee-high blades, and lowers itself down a broken incline. A few moments later, the level slab tips. Water rises around one edge and disappears beneath the other.

The creature does not look back.

At the channel it feeds.

Its mouth is on the underside, behind the foremost limbs. You see it only when the animal braces itself against a wall and lifts its front. A pale muscular surface presses against the stone, spreads, and draws inward. The movement leaves a clean patch in the violet film.

Feeding is slow. The animal covers scarcely the length of your hand before stopping. For a while it rests with its breathing folds submerged and its upper plates in the air.

A small shelled grazer emerges nearby. It moves toward the cleaned patch, finds little there, and begins to turn. One of the builder’s feelers touches its translucent rim. The grazer withdraws. The larger animal shifts its mouth to another surface.

Most of its food is too thin to be seen directly. It must work considerable stretches of rock to maintain that heavy body. Around an occupied shelter, the feeding surfaces form a loose circuit: the underside of one slab, the sheltered face of a blade, several pools reached at different stages of the tide. An animal that stripped them all at once would soon have nothing to eat. The long pauses and wandering routes allow the films to thicken again.

This also explains some of the distance between the shelters.

Others are farther apart because warmth is scarce.

Inside the shelter, beneath the roof opening, is a shallow depression that you initially take for ordinary wear. At its bottom lies a cluster of flattened, translucent capsules. Each is attached to the floor by a short white stalk.

There are perhaps a dozen. Condensation falls among them. Within several, something curved presses against the inner wall.

The warm entrance is only the outer edge of a narrow heated seam. The creature has built over it. Its fitted stones retain a little warmth, while the roof opening lets the heavy damp air escape. A low gap at the rear admits the tidal breath of the ground. When water rises beneath the island, the shelter’s floor remains above it, but moisture climbs the stalks of the capsules and beads on their skins.

The young inside are already plated.

One capsule has opened. Beside it, a creature no larger than your thumb holds itself against the warm floor. Its plates are colorless and soft, its limbs disproportionately long. It has gathered three grains of stone against its body. When a drop strikes nearby, it flattens until the water has passed.

The adult returns before you have watched it for long.

It stops outside the entrance. Its feelers touch a patch where your boot has displaced the gravel. One front limb reaches forward, presses down, and draws several chips back into the disturbed place.

Then it waits.

You step away from the doorway.

The animal enters, its sides brushing the polished stones. From inside comes a series of small scraping sounds. Through the roof opening you can see it lower itself near the capsules, without touching them. One limb remains extended toward the newly emerged young.

Later, from farther down the rise, you see it building.

A loose roof plate has slipped enough to narrow the central opening. The animal gets beneath it and raises its front half. Its long limbs brace on either side of the stone. For a considerable time, nothing happens. Then the plate moves by less than a finger’s width.

The builder settles back. It selects a wedge from the floor, turning the fragment between the lobes of one pad. It rejects that piece, takes another, and works it beneath the lifted edge. Several attempts are needed. Between them it rests, breathing folds opening and closing in the damp interior.

By the time it finishes, the light has changed scarcely at all.

Across the plain, another inhabitant emerges beside the pale-roofed shelter. You would not have noticed it had you not learned where to look: below the roof line, among the low stones, where a wet surface changes its angle without falling.

The nearer animal presses the underside of its body against the shelter floor.

A faint tremor reaches the soles of your feet.

There is a pause. Then another tremor, too regular to be a settling fragment, comes from somewhere beyond the channel. The nearer creature remains lowered for a while before returning to its repairs.

Their signals travel through the same ground that carries the tide’s breathing. Perhaps they announce occupancy, or the state of a warm seam, or merely that a familiar animal is still there. Much of what they send must be lost among the chambers and fractures. They repeat themselves patiently.

Toward the unseen sea, the water begins to rise again. The feeding wall disappears by degrees. Air pours from the clefts, gathering into a roar.

The builder draws a loose stone closer to its entrance. It leaves a gap wide enough for the young. Inside, its pale feelers come to rest on the smooth floor, separated carefully from one another.

Long after its limbs are still, small adjustments pass along the folds beneath its armor. A chamber floods. A blade shifts in its bed. Somewhere out on the plain, another animal presses against the rock.

Each disturbance reaches the shelter, and the creature attends to it.
