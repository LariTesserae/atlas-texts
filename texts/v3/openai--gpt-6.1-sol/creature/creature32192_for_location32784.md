The first inhabitant you see is carrying something.

You mistake it for a flaw in the glass: a short, pale interruption in one of the streets. Then it crosses an intersection, stops, and turns back. Whatever it carries is too wide for the passage ahead.

It sets the object down.

The object remains visible—a fleck shaped like a curved needle—but the bearer becomes almost impossible to distinguish. There is a slight distortion above the road, as though a transparent foot has been pressed against the other side of a window. You find it again only when it moves. The hot street dims behind it and brightens ahead.

It takes hold of the fleck at a different point and continues, now dragging it lengthwise.

There is something domestic about the difficulty. The pause, the reconsideration, the choice to pull rather than carry. You have interrupted no ceremony. Someone is taking a cumbersome thing home.

When it reaches a building, the wall clears.

For a moment you see much farther than you should. The fleck is now a hooked piece of pale metal, long enough to hang across two hands. Its bearer stands beside it.

The inhabitant has no face that you can recognize. Its head is a shallow hollow surrounded by a dark rim, inclined toward its burden. Three narrow limbs support it; two others hold the metal. Inside its body, fine surfaces lie folded together, each reflecting a different light. It seems less transparent than intermittently absent. Parts of the room can be seen through it, but not the parts directly behind.

Something farther inside approaches. The bearer raises one end of the hook. The newcomer takes it.

The building closes.

You are left looking at a block no taller than your thumbnail, and at the little street by which the object arrived.

Other movements become apparent once you know how to look. There are inhabitants at the intersections. One stays near a reservoir, moving around its rim whenever the water trembles. Two travel together along the edge of a bare district, stopping at regular intervals. Beneath the bridge, the amber light passes over a row of small distortions that you had taken for irregularities in the floor.

They are waiting.

When the light returns, one of them is no longer there.

The inhabitants are not always small. Their size depends upon where you see them, and perhaps upon what they are doing. In the streets they are scarcely more than disturbances. Inside an open building they have height, reach, weight. The pale surface you glimpsed earlier is a table. There are marks along its edge where hands have rested.

The spaces around the buildings are kept clear because a room sometimes extends through them.

You discover this when you step toward the tallest block.

A dark line appears on the glass between your shoes and the building. It runs straight for several inches, turns, and returns upon itself. Within the boundary the floor becomes faintly clouded. Something touches the side of your ankle.

You move back.

The clouding deepens. Now there is a room where there was empty floor, not fully visible but present enough to obstruct the light. A narrow shelf reaches across it. Behind the shelf, an inhabitant is sitting with its limbs tucked beneath its body.

You can see only a portion of the room at once. When you look at the shelf, the occupant diminishes. When you look directly at the occupant, the block at the room’s center seems very far away.

On the shelf stand six shallow bowls.

The inhabitant lifts one, holds it near the hollow of its head, and waits. A pale line develops within its body. The line lengthens slowly, crossing from one folded surface to another.

Then it sets the bowl down and takes up the next.

The bowls contain differences in temperature.

You cannot see what has been portioned into them, but you feel it when the room withdraws: six little currents, each distinct, passing across your hand. One is sharply cool. Another has the close warmth of something recently held against a body. The last leaves your fingers aching.

The seated inhabitant remains still for a while after the shelf and bowls have disappeared. Its outline persists near the building, faint and low.

Eventually another arrives and sits beside it.

They do not move.

Elsewhere the rain begins again. This time several inhabitants gather before the first drops fall. They stand well clear of the square, waiting while the cloud broadens.

Under the shower, a sheet of glass rises out of the floor.

It is thin enough to bend under the falling water. Marks develop across it: branching strokes, dark patches, a little bright knot near one edge. The attendant at the reservoir approaches and runs a limb beneath the sheet. Others help lift it. Together they carry it to a nearby building, where the wall opens onto a room crowded with vertical surfaces.

There are many such sheets inside, held apart in a warmth so even that the rainwater does not steam.

Some are clear. Some bear elaborate marks. One, close to the opening, is dark from edge to edge.

An inhabitant reaches up to straighten it with extraordinary care.

The room closes before you can decide whether you have seen a nursery, an archive, or a place where the sick are brought. Later, you notice that the attendant has stayed beside the emptied square. It tests the glass repeatedly. When the next cloud begins to collect elsewhere, it leaves at once.

The city’s few sounds belong to these lives.

The tick in your teeth occurs when a closed room is joined to a street. The water’s small clicks carry through the reservoirs. Beneath the bridge, the traveling light briefly illuminates each waiting inhabitant from within; for an instant you see the folded surfaces of its body arranged like a hand preparing to open.

They seem to know one another by those arrangements.

Two meet in the road and stop. One alters the angle of a surface within its chest. The other answers with a brief gleam low in its body. They move closer. For several seconds their outlines are indistinguishable.

Then they separate, and each continues along the street.

There are also inhabitants that cannot travel.

Near the far wall, you find one incorporated into the base of the growing tower. Its dark rim is embedded sideways in the glass. Several long limbs extend into the streets, too fine to follow beyond the first intersection. Heat passes along them. As the tower gains height, the folded surfaces inside the creature turn, exposing fresh edges to the arriving mineral haze.

Another lies beneath the shortening tower.

It is much larger, though almost all of it is concealed. You can make out a slow contraction beneath the foundation, followed by a loosening. Each time it loosens, dust appears around the upper story and drifts away.

Smaller inhabitants visit both towers. They touch the exposed portions of the buried bodies and remain there briefly. One brings a bowl. One brings nothing, stays a long time, and leaves by a different road.

The city has been built around them, but they are not merely part of its machinery.

At the shrinking tower, a little room opens close to the floor. Inside is a shelf, a pale hanging sheet, and a crooked object much like the one you saw carried through the streets. The buried inhabitant moves a limb into the room and touches the object.

For a while the tower stops shortening.

Only near the entrance do the inhabitants seem uncertain.

The new street beneath the loose chip has no reservoirs yet. Its warmth spreads unevenly. Three figures work there, tracing the unfinished line, drawing their limbs back whenever a patch of glass grows too hot. One repeatedly ventures farther than the others. Each time, one of its companions follows and brings it back.

You crouch beside them.

Your shadow passes over the new road. All three stop.

The adventurous one tilts its hollow head toward you. A surface inside its body catches the dull light from your clothing. For an instant you see a tiny, sharply bounded version of your own shoulder.

Then its companion touches it, and both reflections disappear.

They know you are here. Perhaps they have known since your first step softened against the floor. Your weight must reach their lower rooms as pressure; your breathing must disturb the weather. Yet no crowd has gathered. A few streets have diverted around your shoes. One building remains shut while you stand too close to it.

They are making allowances.

When the ceiling sheds another grain of dust, an inhabitant catches it before the streets can draw it toward the tower. It holds the grain up, turns it, then carries it into a narrow house.

The wall clears just long enough for you to see a second figure waiting within.

Between them is a small, unfinished body, its inner surfaces not yet separate. The grain is placed against it. A dark rim begins to form.

You look away before the room closes.

At the square where the bead of water revealed the steps, a faint light now shows through the dry glass. Something is ascending. You follow its progress as a series of pale interruptions, each appearing deeper than the last one should have been.

It reaches the surface beside your hand.

The inhabitant pauses there, holding a shallow bowl. Within the bowl a single drop rests without evaporating.

It puts the bowl down and withdraws a short distance.

You do not touch it. Your hand is enormous beside the offering, and the glass beneath it is hot enough now to hurt.

After a while you lift your palm from the floor.

The inhabitant approaches the place where it rested. It sets one narrow limb against the cooling impression, then another. The surfaces within its body shift gently.

Below you, down the lit stairs, someone answers.
