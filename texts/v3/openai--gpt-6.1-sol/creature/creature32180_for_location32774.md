The animal beneath the stair is not hiding from you. It is waiting for the stair.

Two of its feet are on the underside of a tread; the other four are spread against the wall. Between them its body is drawn thin enough that the stone shows through. You can distinguish it chiefly by a slight interruption in the surface: a patch where the dim light does not quite behave as it should.

When the tread thickens, the animal shifts a foot. When the wall draws away, it lets its body stretch.

It has been there long enough to accommodate several changes of shape. One leg is much longer than the others. Another has folded twice at joints you cannot see until it moves. There is no fixed outline to remember it by.

The mouth is underneath, close to the middle. What looked like a head is only the end that happened to move first.

After feeding from the plant, it remains swollen for a while. A cloudy yellow bead travels slowly through its body, dividing at each leg. By the time the last trace reaches the feet, the animal has become almost transparent again.

You begin to find others.

There is one inside a recessed threshold, fitted around the angle so closely that you first take it for a slight thickening of the stone. A smaller one rests behind a black stem. In a roofless court, something pale bridges a narrow crack at floor level. It withdraws when your shadow passes over it, but only after a delay, and only far enough to expose the crack.

They are not numerous. Looking carefully makes the settlement seem inhabited, then looking more carefully makes it seem nearly empty again.

Most rooms contain none.

The occupied rooms have a peculiar cleanliness. Dust settles everywhere except in a few narrow strips: under a sill, around the base of an arch, along the shaded side of a floor. The strips are not paths. They end abruptly, or double back into places too shallow to shelter anything thicker than a sheet.

An animal has passed there with its whole underside against the mineral.

It moves by releasing one part of itself while the rest remains attached. A foot travels forward. The body follows in a shallow crease. Then another foot advances, sometimes in a different direction. Crossing a handspan can take several minutes, but it can turn inside an angle without first withdrawing.

There is no patter. The pads part from the wall without a sound.

When the air pressure changes, all six feet tighten at once. The body lifts very slightly from the stone, making a low dome. Afterward it flattens again.

You notice this in two animals on opposite sides of a court. They respond together, though neither could see the other.

The larger inhabitants are harder to recognize.

In a chamber near the tower, a pale film lies across the upper corner. Its edges follow the junction of two walls and the ceiling. It looks like a thin residue left by growth, except that one edge is free.

For a long time nothing happens.

Then the chamber narrows.

The movement is too slight to see directly. You become aware of it because the free edge of the film wrinkles. A fold travels across it, gathers near the corner, and opens again. Something underneath changes position.

This animal is larger than your spread hand. Its legs are still there, but they have broadened and become part of the surface holding it up. Each pad covers a separate patch of stone. Between them hangs the body, loose and translucent, with a small thickening at its center.

It has grown into the corner without entering the wall.

Below it, the mineral is faintly yellow.

A black plant could not reach that seam. The animal can. One of its broad feet rests on the newest surface, while the others remain on older stone. At intervals, a small cloud passes from that foot toward the thickening in its body.

The little animals take what the plants have gathered. This one no longer needs to wait for a plant.

It does not move while you watch. But the corner is changing, and it has room to change with it.

The four empty skins at the threshold belong to animals somewhere between these sizes. Each skin has six delicate extensions folded beneath it. Once opened, it would cover considerably more ground than its crumpled shape suggests.

There is no discarded head.

The slit along the back is wide at one end and almost closed at the other. The animal seems to have left through it gradually, freeing one pad after another while keeping some purchase on the stone.

One skin still has two feet adhering to the threshold. Fresh material has risen around them.

Elsewhere, you find a skin partly embedded in a wall. Only its central membrane remains exposed. It trembles when you lean close enough to warm the air, then lies still.

The floor will take these back too.

Near the tower, the animals are smaller. One rests in the mouth of a vertical chamber, a pale fleck beside the yellow light below. Another is pressed into a depression scarcely wider than a grain of the angular material in the basin.

There are no plants here.

The smallest creatures have no distinct legs yet. Their margins are thickened at six points, and when they shift, those points hold while the middle draws inward. You see one cross the soft band at the tower’s base. It leaves no footprint. The impression under its body slowly fills as it advances.

Whether they begin there or merely find their way there, you cannot tell.

The tower offers new surfaces continuously. It also offers fewer open corners. As its upper lip bends inward, the mouths of the narrow chambers lengthen into slots. A creature feeding inside one will eventually have to retreat upward, descend toward the buried glow, or remain as the opening closes.

You find no bodies.

That does not settle what happens.

In the older rooms, the broad animals rest on walls already thinning from within. Their bodies are duller there, less like clear skin than a film lifted from cooled milk. Sometimes an edge has released. Sometimes two.

One has attached itself across what used to be a lintel. The center of the stone is nearly gone. The animal’s middle hangs in the opening, unsupported, while its feet hold to the thicker ends.

For an hour it remains like that.

When you return, one foot has reached the side of the neighboring doorway. The rest of it is drawn toward that small new anchorage. A long crease divides its body, and for the first time you can see how much of the creature has been stored in folds.

It may get across before the lintel disappears.

You sit nearby until the changing pressure fills your ears. The animal tightens, releases, and advances another foot.

Later, in the new chamber beside the doorway that no longer admits your shoulders, you find a resident already in place.

It is small. Its six feet surround a patch of yellow at the base of the wall. The untouched floor extends almost to it, but immediately beneath its body there is a clean interruption in the dust.

You crouch in the opening.

The animal does not withdraw. Your presence shades the fresh surface, and after a while it loosens its hold enough to settle more fully against the stone.

As the chamber grows cooler around you, a faint yellow cloud begins to move through it.
