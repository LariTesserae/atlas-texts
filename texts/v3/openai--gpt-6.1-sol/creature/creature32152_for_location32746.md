The first thing you might notice is that one of the droplets beneath the pipe’s lowest elbow does not fall.

It lengthens when the pumps accelerate. It draws itself closer to the metal when the duct shudders. Other droplets gather beside it, swell, and let go. This one remains, clear at its lower edge and clouded where it touches the pipe, as though a little construction dust has entered the water.

From the doorway, that is all it is.

Closer, you can see that it has fastened itself to the underside of the elbow at several points. Between those points its body hangs in a shallow pouch. There is no definite division between its body and the wetness covering it. A film runs over both, collects at the lowest part, and drops away. For an instant afterward, something remains exposed: pale, finely creased, about the size of a folded fingernail.

Then the next film arrives.

It lives there.

It is small enough to have passed through a louver without anyone noticing, but that does not establish where it came from. It might have arrived in something larger: a damp packing sleeve, the hollow of a bracket, a length of pipe awaiting installation. It might have been carried upward with the first water. There is nothing in the court old enough to tell you.

Its color is difficult to hold in mind. Against the pipe it looks gray. Over the blue floor it takes on a faint bluish cast, except for two narrow lines inside it that remain the color of unbleached thread. These run nearly the length of the body, turn together near one end, and disappear into a denser patch. Whether they are nerves, vessels, or something it has swallowed cannot be seen.

It has no visible eyes.

The end it uses to investigate is slightly broader than the other. When extended, it divides into three short margins, each of which can flatten independently against a surface. They are less like fingers than the corners of a damp label being pressed down.

Most of the time it stays folded.

It unfolds just before the overhead line clears itself.

The warning is in the metal: a change in tension, then the beginning of the shudder. While the grass still stands upright and the floor is still only wet, it withdraws the hanging portion of its body. Its attachment points spread. What looked like a drop becomes a thin, irregular patch fitted tightly to the elbow’s underside.

The flush passes below it.

Spray reaches it, but the main sheet cannot. For those several seconds it is almost indistinguishable from the pipe’s coating. Only one edge moves, lifting and closing repeatedly, testing the water that runs around the curve.

Afterward, it waits.

The grass has begun to rise again before it releases its hold.

It comes down by lengthening itself along the back of the pipe bracket. The movement is slow but not hesitant. Each small part secures itself before the part behind it lets go. On the vertical metal its body is narrow. At the foot of the bracket it spreads until it is broad enough to cross a seam in the resin.

The seam is a substantial obstacle. It lifts its investigating end onto the ridge, settles there, then gathers the rest of itself over. A thin trail remains behind it, visible only where the yellow doorway light catches it.

It usually goes to the moss.

There it works along the edge of the green patch, pressing its broad end into the grit behind the washer. It takes very little. A particle disappears; a dark fiber is shifted; once, a paint flake rides briefly on its back before falling away. The moss does not visibly diminish. Whatever it is finding may be beneath the moss rather than part of it.

Sometimes it remains there through an entire interval between flushes. The bracket interrupts the water sufficiently for it to hold on, though its body flattens when the heavy runoff begins. The green fibers move over it. After the water subsides, it emerges carrying a grain of grit against its underside, then leaves that grain a little nearer the washer.

There is no sign that it tends the patch. Its feeding and sheltering simply leave some of the loose material caught more firmly than before.

It does not cross the hand-sized pool behind the sealant.

On reaching the pale bank, it raises its front against the slope and stops. Water touches its lower surface. The two thread-colored lines inside it contract. After a while it turns back.

The steel shaving continues to move in the pool without being approached.

Its journeys are short. From the elbow to the washer. From the washer to a depression in the blue resin where fine sediment settles after a flush. Occasionally to the base of the wall, near the grass, though it never climbs a blade. The drain remains beyond the routes it follows.

A boot placed in its path makes it contract at once. The pressure comes through the deck before the sole lands. It becomes a low bead beside a seam and stays there long after the boot has gone.

Speech produces no comparable response. Neither does a light aimed at it from the door. It will continue feeding under the beam, its small translucent margins closing against the floor.

A cleaner would probably take it for softened sealant. The rag would catch it, the grit, and most of the moss together. There would be no sound audible above the machinery, and very little left to examine.

For now, it returns to the elbow.

Once attached, it loosens the middle of its body and allows it to hang again. Water gathers over it. Its outline enlarges, blurs, becomes an ordinary pendant drop.

Below it, the court clears. The last branching streams reach the drain. The grass lifts a little farther. A bead beneath a ladder rung falls, followed by another.

Under the elbow, the one that stays grows heavy with water and lets the water go.
