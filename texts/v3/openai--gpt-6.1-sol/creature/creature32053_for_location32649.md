The ants live here in a way that makes it difficult to say where their bodies end and their dwelling begins. A worker emerges with a grain held in her jaws, and for a moment the grain is part of her outline. She sets it down beside an entrance. Now it belongs to the slope, and the next ant must climb over it.

They are small, dark ants, their legs fine enough to disappear against the earth. In the green light, their bodies seem nearly black; under the brief yellow opening in the fern, there is brown in them, and a little shine at the joints. Their antennae are almost always moving. They touch a crumb, the root, the face of another worker. Two meet on the narrow route between entrances, pause with their heads together, then separate without either having visibly made room.

Most carry nothing when they come up. They investigate what has changed.

A wet place has dried. A piece of bark has tilted. Something with a soft body has passed beneath the leaf and left a trace. One worker reaches the water basin and tests its edge, leaning forward until her mouth touches the surface. Another goes around it. Their routes overlap, diverge, and become routes again through repetition.

Below, the colony is less tidy than the four round holes suggest. Its chambers follow the material that will permit them: a softened seam in the root, the underside of the fallen leaf, a pocket among fragments that have not yet collapsed. Some walls are earth; some are wood still firm enough to hold. Passageways narrow where a fiber crosses them.

There are workers down there whose bodies rarely receive even the green light. They attend the young, pale and folded, carried when necessary in the jaws that also carry soil. A larva is lifted gently, turned, laid against others. When dampness enters a chamber, the nursery moves. For a while, a passage fills with workers carrying the colony’s softest bodies past one another.

Deeper in the sheltered space is a larger ant, the queen. The courtyard gives no sign of her. She is surrounded by touch: antennae along her sides, workers approaching her mouth, eggs gathered from beside her. Her place remains dark when sunlight falls across the clearing.

The ants are the most visible inhabitants, but much of the hollow is occupied by things they pass without disturbing.

The springtails have the damp margins. Among the moss stems, their small pale bodies can look like moving flecks of light. One stops on a leaf tip, works its mouth against the surface, then drops out of sight. Disturbed, it vanishes with a jump too quick for the eye to follow. It lands somewhere nearby, in another fold of the same moist world.

The mite on the wood is slower. Its translucence makes it seem scarcely separate from the dampness beneath it. Up close, it has legs, a mouth, a definite front. It enters a crack that looks too narrow to admit an animal.

Under the seed plate lives something that feeds inward. It is known first by the hollow it leaves, then perhaps by a pale curve glimpsed when the plate shifts. The shelter and the meal are the same object. As it eats, its ceiling thins.

The spider keeps a different part of the courtyard. Its body rests against the root while its feet remain in contact with the web. When an ant turns away, the spider stays still. Later, a smaller insect catches on a strand near the curled leaf. The movement reaches the spider through its legs. It advances into the gap, briefly becoming distinct from the bark.

There are inhabitants too slight to appear even as flecks. Fungal threads run through the fallen leaf and into the pale wood, entering softened walls, spreading around harder fibers. Bacteria gather in films of moisture. Together with the animals that chew and scrape, they loosen what still has the shape of a leaf, a seed, a branch.

By afternoon, the visible residents have shifted toward cover. The fly has gone. The mite is inside the wood. Springtails move beneath the moss rather than over it. At the entrances, ants continue to arrive with pieces held high enough to clear the ground.

One worker brings up a dark crumb and tries it against the edge of a mound. It rolls back toward her. She takes it again, carries it a little farther, and presses it into a gap beside a pale chip.

Then she turns and goes below.
