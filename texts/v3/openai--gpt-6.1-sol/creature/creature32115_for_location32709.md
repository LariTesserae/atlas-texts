At first you take it for another fold in the wall.

It occupies the shaded side of a chamber, a little above the ledge. Its back is green where the light passes through it, cream-colored where the flesh is thick. Several long ribs converge beneath a narrow head. The head rests sideways against the wall, and one arm hangs down with its fingertips in the moisture gathering along the floor.

Then the fingertips move.

They move separately, with long intervals between them. Each touches the floor, presses, and lifts. The motions leave five small depressions that fill with water. Farther down the chamber, a seam in the wall opens enough to release a trickle.

The creature turns its head toward the sound.

Its face is small for its body. There are two eyes, dark and deeply set, protected by transparent lids that slide from the bottom upward. Between them lies a ridge with the soft shine of a new stem. Below that, an opening puckers and relaxes. It might be a mouth. Around its edges are short white filaments, so fine that you see them only when they catch a droplet.

It has been aware of you for some time.

When you shift your weight, its eyes follow the darkening footprint. When you breathe, the filaments around its mouth incline toward you. Your face seems to interest it less than the small alterations your body makes in the room.

You have brought shade, pressure, warmth, and a little salt.

These are things it can examine.

***

Standing takes it a while.

First it draws its fingertips together. Then its back loosens from the wall, one rib at a time, with the faint wet sound of a leaf being peeled from another leaf. Its limbs unfold farther than you expect. What appeared to be an elbow proves to be one of several places at which the arm can bend.

It is taller than a person when fully extended, although it seldom extends fully. Across open ground it travels low, supported on two feet and the knuckles of one hand. The other hand stays lifted, its fingers loosely gathered beneath its chest.

Its feet are broad and almost transparent at the edges. Under pressure they spread into shallow cups. Before each step, the rim of the cup releases in a slow wave from heel to toe. It leaves no lasting print.

The body is neither leaf-thin nor heavily fleshed. There is substance around the shoulders and at the base of the limbs, enough to suggest strength. Elsewhere its surfaces lie close together. You can sometimes see water moving inside it, a pale line advancing through a forearm, dividing at the wrist.

On its left side is a narrow brown mark.

This is the first thing in the valley you have seen that looks old.

The mark crosses three ribs and interrupts their regular spacing. When the creature bends, those ribs move together while the others separate. It accommodates the stiffness without hesitation, lowering one shoulder, placing a hand farther out. Whatever happened there has become part of how it walks.

***

There are others, though you will not see many.

One lies along the upper surface of an arch, its limbs fitted into the grooves. Another stands in a distant pool for most of an afternoon. At sunset, two small ones emerge through a doorway you had thought too narrow to serve any purpose.

The small ones are not simply miniature versions of the first. Their bodies are compact, their fingers disproportionately long. They walk upright with a quick, cautious gait, stopping frequently to press their palms against the floor. Along their backs are tightly folded membranes that the larger creatures lack.

One pauses beside a channel and opens a membrane a little.

Its companion reaches over and presses it shut.

They remain there together until water collects around their ankles. Then they continue across the terrace, shoulder touching shoulder.

You cannot tell whether they are young. Size seems to have something to do with where they live. The creature on the arch has flattened feet and a broad underside; the one in the pool carries a fringe of slow-moving filaments down both legs. The chamber-dweller has those long fingers and a back shaped to rest between the wall’s ribs.

None looks finished in quite the same way.

***

They eat at the water’s edge.

A resident kneels beside a channel and dips the filaments of its mouth into the current. Beneath its chin, a pouch gradually rounds. The process may take an hour. During that time its eyes close, but one hand stays spread against the bed, feeling the force of the water.

Sometimes it takes one of the tethered nodules into its palm.

It turns the nodule without breaking the stalk, holds it beneath its mouth, then returns it to the same hollow. Afterward the hard surface is cloudy instead of clear. By the next day it shines again.

They also spend long periods with portions of their bodies pressed against the valley.

A forearm lies inside a trough. A foot is inserted into a soft cleft at the base of a tower. The narrow end of a head rests within an unopened fold. Around the point of contact, their outlines become difficult to distinguish from the surrounding tissue.

They always pull free eventually.

For a few moments afterward, the place retains the shape of what rested there.

***

The chamber-dweller accompanies you without staying close.

You cross a plain; it moves along the edge of a channel some distance away. You stop beneath a tower; later you notice its pale fingers around an opening above you.

Once, when you sit, it approaches and puts its raised hand on the ledge beside yours.

The fingers open.

Between them stretches a fine membrane, nearly colorless. Water beads across it in an orderly row. The creature tilts its hand, and the beads run together, making a low, brief sound.

From somewhere inside the hill comes an answering vibration.

It repeats the gesture, more slowly.

You put your hand flat against the ledge. The vibration reaches your palm. The creature watches you, then settles beside you with its feet drawn up beneath its body.

You remain there until the chamber’s shadow reaches the far wall.

***

What they attend to is often too small for you to notice beforehand.

A channel lip has begun to curl. One presses it down and waits.

Two leaves no longer meet evenly at a seam. A resident lies across them, distributing its weight with extraordinary care.

A thin partition sounds rougher than its neighbors. Several creatures gather on opposite sides. They touch it at different heights while the water strikes. Gradually the roughness diminishes. One withdraws a hand, listens, and replaces it lower down.

Their work leaves no new structure that you can point to. Yet they have preferences. The chamber-dweller returns repeatedly to a particular ringing conduit. It rests its cheek against the stem and listens even after the others have gone.

Sometimes it makes the note change.

Sometimes it simply stays.

***

At the circular wall in the central basin, you find it standing knee-deep in reflection.

It has placed both hands on the inward-rolled edge. Its shoulders rise and fall slowly. Under its feet the broad leaves flex, sending small ripples outward.

You wait for the wall to open.

Instead, the creature lifts one hand and turns it palm upward.

The tender edge rests across its fingers. A little water runs from the fold, down its wrist, and into the basin. With its other hand it smooths the green surface, following the curve as far as it can reach.

Then it lets go.

The wall holds exactly as it did before.

The creature wades toward you. For once it comes close enough that you can see the cloudy lines within its eyes, the brown scar along its side, a single white filament bent outward from its mouth.

It touches the water beside your foot.

When you move, it withdraws its fingers and watches the ripples travel. They cross the basin, meet the circular wall, and return broken into smaller waves.

The creature waits until those have passed too.

Then, slowly, it sets its hand down again.
