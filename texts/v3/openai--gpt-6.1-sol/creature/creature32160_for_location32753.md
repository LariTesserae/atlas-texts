What lives here is easiest to see where the new crust meets the water. It occupies neither side of that boundary for long.

At first it looks like a flaw in the deposit: a narrow, greyish seam beneath a projecting lip. The seam widens near the outlet, branches around a grain of gravel, and comes together again. There is no obvious head. There are places where it is thicker, but these move. A swelling that was under the pipe in the morning may be beside the ladder foot by afternoon, with the intervening seam scarcely altered.

Its body is flat enough to fit between two layers of mineral that appear to touch. Held up to the light, a piece of it would have the color of water in which a nail has been left. In the basin it is almost invisible. The amber of the submerged crust conceals it; the white of the dry crust covers it. Only at the growing edges does it expose a little of itself, a wet line that withdraws more slowly than a shadow.

It is not as large as the hollow. Not yet. It extends beneath two of the islands and along the terrace below the pipe, with a thinner part reaching toward the steps. There are gaps between these portions. Some are linked by threads across the floor. Others may have become separate animals, though nothing in their behavior makes this certain.

The places that look like damage are often its openings.

Under one island’s sharp edge is a row of shallow notches, each scarcely wider than the point of a pencil. Warm water enters these. Farther back, beneath the crust, it passes over folded tissue so thin that individual folds collapse when the pumps hesitate. The animal takes very little from each passage of water. It has a great deal of time, and water keeps passing.

Its nourishment is in the difference between one seep and another. The rusty water from the bank supplies something the clear water under the steps does not. The discharge from the pipe arrives with traces of oil and metal too slight to make a visible film. Across the animal’s inner folds live smaller things, crowded into soft brown patches. They turn these traces into what it can use. Where the patches are fullest, the body has a faint granular appearance, like damp leather seen through glass.

The white mineral is not its flesh. It lays the mineral over itself, or encourages the water to lay it there. Beneath that covering it remains soft.

This gives it a way to live on a floor that is continually replacing itself. As one passage closes, it opens another along the edge. As a roof thickens, it slips out from beneath it. Behind it are abandoned chambers, very low and perfectly fitted to the body it had a week earlier. Breaking an apparently solid piece of crust sometimes reveals a wet, smooth cavity inside, with nothing occupying it.

Most of its movements are smaller than the movements of the water.

A rim gathers a drop. Before the drop falls, something under the rim draws back. Water reaches a notch, pauses, and then enters. Near the pipe, a bubble travels beneath the surface and stops against an edge; the edge lifts just enough to let it through.

There is one part that moves more openly. It lies in the strip of permanent shade at the far end, over the coated brick fragment. Here a narrow flap emerges from beneath the translucent deposit. It is the same reddish grey as the buried body, but its margin is clear. The flap rests across the slow current and catches what comes down from the steps: fragments of softened leaf, a little road grit, the occasional strand of grass.

It does not retain everything. Grains too large to pass beneath it are eased to one side. A flake of rust may remain against it for hours, gradually losing its ragged outline. Then the flap releases what is left.

A person leaning close might see that edge lift and mistake it for the effect of a current. The next movement would be less easy to explain. The flap closes before a boot comes down on the upper step.

It knows disturbance first through the ground. The pumps provide a continuous vibration against which other pressures are distinct. A ladder rung struck by a tool, a bucket placed on the shelf, a foot shifting weight above the water: each arrives through the brick or the newly hardened floor. When someone enters the hollow, the exposed margins contract. Water continues to pass through the notches, but more slowly. The little flap in the shade disappears.

This withdrawal leaves the basin looking slightly cleaner. No one who comes to check the drain has noticed that.

Rain forces a different response. The animal cannot withdraw from water that reaches every part of it. Instead it closes its finer openings and thickens. For an hour after heavy runoff, small pale bulges can be seen under the lips of the islands. Its outer tissue has swollen with the cooler, diluted water. It waits in this condition until the warm seep has displaced the rain.

Sometimes a portion does not recover. It turns cloudy, detaches, and lies against the floor. The surviving body seals the break. Mineral soon covers the lost part, preserving its shallow outline as faithfully as it preserves a stone.

The undated mark on the wall is within reach of its thinnest extension. There, under the waterline, a thread runs upward through a mortar joint. It has found warmth inside the masonry. It has not found enough water to follow it far.

For now, its strongest growth is toward the drain.

The remaining slots draw water over a crowded row of folds. Here the body is darker and less sheltered, fastened to the underside of the grille. The current keeps it supplied. It must also resist that current, and its attachments leave small, bare circles on the iron where the white deposit cannot settle.

At irregular intervals, one of the folds tightens at its base. A short length separates. It is almost transparent, curled around a fleck of brown.

The detached piece holds to the grille for a while. Then its hold loosens, and the drain takes it.

Elsewhere below the pumping house, beyond the reach of daylight, warm water runs through old pipes. Some joints leak. Some chambers are quiet between cycles. The little piece passes through several places where it cannot live before it catches against one where it can.

In the hollow, the margin under the pipe opens again. A drop swells on the lowest point of a rim. The soft body moves a little farther beneath the edge it is building.
