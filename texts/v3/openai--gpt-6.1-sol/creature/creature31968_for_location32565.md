The pale creature under the porch has worn a place for itself between a root and the lowest step.

It is not a nest. There is nothing gathered there, nothing brought from elsewhere. One side of the root has simply become smooth, and the dust beneath it has a narrow trough where the creature lowers its body. When it is resting, five of its feet remain on the ground. The sixth touches the underside of the step.

It has been resting for most of the morning.

Its body is translucent at the joints and opaque elsewhere, the color of a shell that has lain a long time out of sunlight. Along its back are seven shallow ridges. Dust collects between them, but never on their crests; each crest trembles too finely for a grain to stay.

The head is difficult to distinguish from the body until it moves. Then a narrow front plate lifts, exposing two dark pits and, below them, a mouth folded into three parts. There are no antennae. There is a pair of small, blunt projections beside the mouth, which it presses against whatever it is about to bite.

Its feet are more elaborate than the rest of it seems to require. Each ends in a split pad surrounded by several hooked filaments. On plaster, the pads spread. On a mineral leaf, the filaments close around the edge, separately, testing their holds. It can hang beneath a leaf in a draft that lifts the entire vine from its roof.

It cannot cross the central square at noon.

It has tried. A little way out, the bright air lifts its body and turns it sideways, and the sound comes through the ground in too many directions at once. It crouches, spreading all six feet until its belly nearly touches the paving. Then it retreats along the exact line by which it came. There are three shallow scratches at the edge of the square where it has done this repeatedly.

The square is not part of its range. The porch, two walls, the underside of a covered passage, and a garden above a house with a broken chimney are enough.

At a change in the humming, it lifts the foot from the step.

For a moment it holds that foot in the air. Then it puts it down beside another, turns, and climbs the root.

Its movements are quick, but separated by long pauses. It crosses a hand’s breadth of wall and stops. It places one foot against a plugged pipe and stops again. Sometimes it waits so long that dust begins to settle on its head. Then a shutter shifts somewhere above, and it immediately passes a doorway it would not approach a moment before.

The roar is not an undifferentiated thing to it. A note rising through the wall means that a passage is about to become hot. A clatter beneath a particular eave precedes the lash of loose foliage. A low interruption in the humming means that one of the garden’s heavy stems has moved and that, for a little while, there will be room behind it.

Some changes it feels through its feet. Others move the ridges of its back. A few make the plates around its mouth open slightly, although it is nowhere near food.

It reaches the roof by going through the house.

The room below has a shelf, a sloping clay channel, and a small object lying on its side beneath the window. The creature has never touched the object. It has crossed the shelf often enough to leave a clean line through the dust. At the upper end of the channel, where white roots have cracked the plaster, it squeezes through a gap narrower than its body. Its back ridges flatten one after another.

Above, the leaves are opening their seams.

The red combs stand out underneath them, delicate and evenly spaced. The creature avoids these. It goes to a joint on an older stem, where an amber bead has collected in the shade.

First it braces four feet. Then it touches the bead with the projections beside its mouth.

It waits.

The bead is hard in the morning. By this hour its outer layer has begun to yield. The creature bites, closing the three parts of its mouth in a sequence that leaves a small square missing from the deposit. It holds the fragment while something pale works behind its front plate. A dark gold color gradually appears in the first translucent joint of its body.

It takes another bite. Then a third.

Enough amber remains to feed it many times, but it leaves. The stem has begun to tighten, and the leaf above it is turning into the sun. Before the new draft reaches the roof, the creature is already on the wall.

There is another of its kind beyond the kiln.

That one is larger and almost yellow. One of its rear feet ends without filaments. It travels around the same post each afternoon, descending to feed where a root has emerged from a fault, then climbing until it is level with the top of the kiln door.

Their ranges meet at a sheltered angle of masonry.

On some days neither goes there. On others, one arrives and waits with two feet against the wall. When both are present, they remain apart. The smaller creature lowers its body. The larger shifts its weight forward. Their back ridges tremble at different rates.

This can continue for a long time.

Once, the smaller one approached while the kiln gave out its descending whistle. It touched the larger creature’s damaged foot with a front pad. The larger withdrew the foot, then put it down again. Neither moved until the whistle ended.

There is no mark on the wall to show where this happened.

Under the porch, however, there are four small plates fastened to the root above the resting place. They resemble flakes shed from the creature’s back. Three are empty and have split lengthwise. The fourth is whole.

Inside it, something shifts when the afternoon humming deepens.

The creature does not bring it amber. It sometimes presses its mouth against the edge, without biting, and remains there while the plate quivers. At other times it ignores it for days.

The small thing within has more limbs than it will keep. They lie folded against one another, scarcely thicker than the white fibers of the root. When the plate finally opens, it will emerge into still air under the step. Its first surface will be the root; its second, the worn plaster. There is food close by, though no clear path to it yet.

For now the plate remains shut.

The creature returns from the garden as the western streets begin to quiet. At the workbench it stops beside the repaired mouthpiece. A thin sound is escaping through it, stirred by air from a crack behind the bench.

It puts one foot on the lip.

The pitch changes.

It lifts the foot. The former pitch returns.

For several moments it repeats this, with longer pauses each time. Then the draft alters, the mouthpiece falls silent, and the creature passes beneath the bench, beside the knife in its fitted groove.

By evening it is back under the porch. Five feet settle into the dust. The sixth rises to the underside of the step.

Above the root, the unopened plate trembles once, very lightly.
