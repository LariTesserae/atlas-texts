What lives here is still learning the difference between one surface and another.

It has found the mortar first. The broad faces of the tiles are too smooth, too exposed to the sweep of water, but a joint offers small protections: a pore, a shallow depression, the underside of a projecting grain. There, something carried in from the roof has caught and remained. It has divided without moving away. A second cell has stayed beside the first, and then others, until their attachment has become a place.

From above, this is only the green thread beneath the step. Close to it, the thread is less definite. Its margins are loose and nearly colorless. Fine strands extend beyond what the eye recognizes as growth; between them is a clear, clinging substance that holds dust and makes the mortar faintly slippery. The green is the visible part of an occupation already spreading beyond its edges.

There is no head to it, no leading end. One length lies in the light while another rests in the step’s shadow. The current lifts a loose filament, folds it back, lets it stretch downstream. The base stays where it has fastened. Its motion belongs to the water, but its remaining belongs to itself.

The patch behind the spout lives differently. It receives more disturbance and less direct light. Water strikes nearby, breaks into spray, runs over the plaster in thin, unequal paths. Growth gathers in the sheltered places between those paths. A trowel ridge only barely visible from the coping is enough to make a lee. The thumbprint holds moisture in its hollow after the surrounding wall has begun to dry.

Nothing intended as shelter was built here. Nevertheless, there are shelters.

At the overflow, the faint slickness is another beginning. Much of it has no visible green at all. It is a film so thin that the plaster still appears bare beneath it, inhabited by minute lives that arrived separately and now persist together. Some use the light. Others make use of what the water brings: traces from the roof, softened dust, the scant remains of things that settled in the channel.

The basin is generous with warmth and stingy with food. A great deal passes through; very little stays.

So the living patches keep what they can. A particle catches in a filament. A trace of organic matter adheres to the film. Water flows past the same patch all afternoon, bringing something too small to see, carrying something else away. Growth proceeds by additions that would not register during a single visit.

A surge can undo part of it. The thread beneath the step stretches farther than usual; one pale extremity tears loose. For a moment it travels freely over the tiles, turning in the circulation. It may reach another joint. It may go over the notch and disappear into the drain. What remains attached is shorter, but alive.

These inhabitants do not yet make the chamber look old. They make it look almost clean.

Their hold is slight enough that every mark of construction remains legible. The sand in the plaster shows through them. The mortar retains its pale color. Even where they have begun to thicken, there is no soft bed, no dark mat, no accumulated layer concealing the work beneath. They occupy the room at the scale of its imperfections.

At midday, bubbles catch briefly among the green strands. Some have been swept there by the falling water; some may have formed against the growth itself. They shine, swell, and release. Their departure leaves no obvious vacancy.

Later, when the light withdraws, the patches become harder to distinguish. They remain in the warmth of the steps and wall, attached to the same few grains. The current continues to draw their loose edges outward and lay them down again.

For now, whoever leans over the coping will see a room containing water, with a little green in two or three places.

Beneath the second step, those places are becoming larger.
