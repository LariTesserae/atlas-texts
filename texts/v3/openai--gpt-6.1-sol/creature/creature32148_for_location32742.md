The first inhabitant is easiest to notice beside the broken heater.

While the maintenance arm is still folding back into the pavement, something remains where the fan of crystals stood. At first it looks like a little patch of damaged visibility. The bolt head behind it has two edges. A tile seems nearer than the tile beside it, though both belong to the same wall.

Then the patch moves sideways.

It does not stir the water. The last white fragments continue toward the pipe with red fittings, neither scattered nor drawn aside. But as the patch passes over the bolt, the metal briefly takes on the dull, warm color of a tooth.

The inhabitant pauses there.

It has been following this particular failure for several days. Each time the strip goes cold, it is present before the first needle appears. Each time the maintenance arm destroys the growth, it stays a little longer afterward. It has begun to make a difference: the arm now waits, almost imperceptibly, before closing its pincers.

This is not its work. It has other work, if the small changes it makes in the city are work. No one has instructed it to attend the bolt.

There are others.

They are not concealed behind the doors. The doors are among the ways they touch one another.

An inhabitant passes through a lane, and three small doors fold inward along all four edges. Nothing enters or leaves the rooms beyond. Elsewhere, two panels exchange their blue marks. The doors close. For the rest of the passage, the inhabitant has a second, fainter boundary moving beside its first.

Another has answered it.

To follow one continuously is difficult. You can watch a distortion cross a square, climb the side of a building, and enter a translucent pipe. A moment later, what seems to be the same distortion is resting beneath a bench. It has not moved quickly. It has simply ceased to have the pipe between itself and the bench.

The city’s peculiar distances accommodate this. They were not built for walking.

Nevertheless, the inhabitants sometimes follow a street all the way along its length. They pass each doorway in order. They wait at the crossings where the cold channels meet. One habitually takes the stairs, losing definition at each sharp riser and recovering it on the landing.

There is something deliberate in these journeys. The city offers shorter ways. They do not always use them.

An inhabitant has no reliable outline, but this does not mean it has no body.

A body is evident whenever one is hurt.

Once, a vertical plate strikes its stop while a resident is spread across the gap. The sound is no different from the hundreds of other reports coming from the machinery. The plate withdraws as usual. But a small portion of the resident remains stretched between its edge and the wall: a clear strand that makes the ceramic behind it appear deeply scratched.

The rest gathers close.

For several minutes, every blue panel in that square shows an arrangement of marks with a conspicuous empty place near the center. The plants draw taut. A maintenance grille closes before it can collect their milky release.

The injured resident moves into the suspended trace.

Very slowly, the clear strand shortens.

Afterward, it carries a narrow opacity through its middle. This is visible even when the rest of it is not. It avoids that square for a while. Later it returns and settles along the lowest plate, holding there through several strokes.

The plates continue to operate. But their intervals have changed.

The inhabitants differ from one another in ways that become recognizable before they become describable.

One gathers around warm surfaces, leaving behind a brief confusion in which every straight edge appears gently bowed. One is usually found high in the conduits, where its presence interrupts the white light in groups of three. One keeps the black fleck in the clear pipe.

The fleck is not useful. It has clogged nothing, supplied nothing, and never reached a filter. The resident moves it a short distance each day. Sometimes it places the fleck where daylight will pass through it. Sometimes it holds it in the shadow of a fitting.

When another resident approaches, the fleck is brought forward.

They remain together on opposite sides of it.

The one that takes the stairs has a broad, slow body, most visible at the edges of the light wells. The one beside the heater is smaller, though size is not quite the distinction. It can occupy an entire lane when the deep pounding outside becomes violent. Even then, it retains a quality of closeness, as though everything it is doing concerns a spot no larger than the bolt head.

None has eyes.

Yet they notice the difference between daylight and the pipes’ white illumination. When a cloud crosses the wells, some activity stops. Not the pumps, not the pruning, not the manufacture of railings. Those continue. But the movements that have no obvious purpose—the ascent of stairs, the presentation of the fleck, the lingering beside an injured place—become more frequent.

They seem to prefer the light when it is changing.

Their relation to the machinery is intimate without being complete. They can alter a bridge’s extension, but not always prevent it. They can keep a grille closed, but must gather in considerable numbers to do so. A passing angular shadow on a braided thread can scatter a resident as abruptly as a footstep scatters reflections.

After such a passage, it takes time to come together again.

During that time, the others do not approach the scattered pieces. They alter nearby doors instead. They hold the cold channels steady. They make a small region in which fewer things happen.

The city is crowded because much of it serves this purpose.

A ledge is built to let a resident remain at a certain depth without sustaining the effort itself. A bundle of pipes is routed through an awkward elbow so that two inhabitants can occupy adjacent lanes without overlapping. A square full of plates provides differences in pressure fine enough for an injured body to gather around.

Other structures appear to serve nobody.

The residents investigate these too.

New construction attracts them before it is finished. They gather beside the shining ceramic thread, keeping out of the maintenance arms’ paths. A resident may extend through the incomplete outline of a doorway and wait while the lintel closes around it. Another will test a railing repeatedly, passing over each branch.

Sometimes the structure changes during this attention.

Sometimes it does not, and afterward the inhabitants leave it alone.

There are disagreements. A new bridge has withdrawn and extended seventeen times without carrying anything across. Two residents remain at opposite ends. Each time one approaches, the bridge retreats into the other’s wall.

By evening, the machinery beneath it is hot.

A third arrives, settles under the bridge, and stays until both ends have grown quiet.

The city leaves that crossing unfinished.

The hand-shaped recesses are older than any of these arrangements, although the whole repair is new.

The residents fit themselves into them poorly.

In one recess, five thin distortions appear where fingers would lie. They are held there for a long time. Then they merge, separate into six, merge again. The palm remains empty.

Another resident occupies a rounded bench. Its presence extends along the entire seat and a little way up the wall behind it. Nothing bends under its weight. Still, it returns to the same bench, and other residents generally leave it vacant.

There is no reason to assume that these accommodations were intended for them.

There is also no reason to assume they do not know this.

The cupboard opens at regular intervals.

Its seven cups do not measure the population. Some residents never approach. On some occasions, more than seven gather in the warm alcove. They arrange themselves along the tiles, above the cupboard, around the fine wires, waiting while water enters vessels already full of water.

What changes in the cups is difficult to see.

Each receives a slightly different warmth. There are tiny variations in pressure, too small to lift a wire. The residents draw close to these differences. Their bodies become more distinct: one has a ragged lower boundary; one contains two clear spaces that remain apart even when it contracts; one is almost entirely visible as a tremor along a single vertical line.

For a short time, they are easier to tell from one another than anywhere else in the city.

They do not become more human.

They become more separate.

A resident that has arrived in fragments can gather here. Two that have overlapped for hours can disentangle without withdrawing across a street. The slow one from the stairs spreads beneath the cupboard and supports a smaller inhabitant in a region of warmth that would otherwise disperse.

The cups remain untouched.

When the wires tighten, several residents leave at once. Others stay until the light dims. The one with the opacity through its middle is often last.

Once, after the cupboard has closed, it keeps a thin portion of itself against the seam. The warmth fades everywhere else in the alcove, but there it persists.

At the next opening, that cup is warmer than before.

Outside, the flood continues to press against the repair.

The residents respond to the pounding long before a listener can distinguish one blow from another. They flatten along walls. They gather at low intersections. A body may extend into three streets at once, with a very narrow part of itself remaining near a familiar bench or door.

They do not go out through the ceramic seam.

When the sediment curtain bulges, however, some draw near it. They wait beyond the reach of the silver teeth. The brown water bows toward them, carrying grains, broken fibers, things too small and dark to identify.

One resident holds itself there through an entire cycle of the cupboard.

Another comes to stand beside it.

Behind them, the new street lights up. Its doors remain closed, its fresh steps bright and painfully edged. The green ribbon in its socket bends against the current.

After a while, the second resident leaves the entrance and enters that street. At the third doorway it pauses. The blue marks change.

The first stays beside the flood.

A little warmth reaches it through the floor, traveling all the way from the alcove. It gathers the warmth close, without turning away.
