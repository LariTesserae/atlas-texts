The inhabitant is easiest to see just after the pillar clicks.

For a little while, something stands beside the hollow square. It is narrow, darker than the substrate, and taller than the pillar by roughly the length of a forearm. Its upper end turns toward the small doorway. Below that, its outline becomes difficult to follow. One part appears to rest on the floor; another rests farther down, though there is no depression beneath it.

When the seam in the wall turns blue, the figure disappears. In its place are three short lengths of silver, a pale crescent, and a patch of black surface that seems more worn than the surrounding ground.

These are not remains. Nor has it gone elsewhere. The blue interval makes the basin solid in a way that prevents its parts from appearing together.

It waits until the seam darkens before continuing whatever it was doing.

***

Most of its body lies along a direction opened by the cracked dish.

From the basin floor, this body is glimpsed in pieces. A joint might show beneath an overhang, apparently embedded in the wall. A thin plate appears briefly between two terraces. Something resembling the underside of a wrist occupies the hollow square in the pillar without obstructing the view through it.

To the inhabitant, these parts are close together.

It can touch the doorway and the far side of the basin at once. Reaching a grain directly beneath it is more difficult. For this it must lower itself through several small adjustments, each of which takes time and leaves some other part of its body unsupported. It does not undertake such movements casually.

Its surface is neither skin nor shell. It consists of close-fitting plates with edges so finely divided that they look soft until they move. Along a bend, the edges separate and expose a darker material, smooth and faintly lustrous. No moisture gathers there. The plates close again without a sound.

On the side nearest the entrance, a patch has been repaired.

The repair is old, but younger than the rest of the body. Its plates are broader and slightly uneven. One carries the dull violet sheen sometimes visible on the basin walls. Another is white. When the inhabitant bends, this pale plate catches against its neighbor, and the whole movement pauses until it has been eased free.

It has learned to begin certain motions earlier to allow for this.

***

There is no head in the usual sense.

The upper portion opens into a shallow arrangement of folds, sheltered beneath an overhanging plate. Within them are several clear surfaces. They reflect nothing recognizable. A person looking into one would see the entrance from outside the mountain; into another, the underside of the suspended fragments; into a third, a place in the basin that has not yet become accessible.

These surfaces are sensitive to differences in distance.

The inhabitant can feel a terrace approaching the floor. It can detect the slow closing of the dish long before a measuring instrument could distinguish the change. When someone stands in the entrance, it becomes aware of the new weight of proximity, although the person may remain invisible to it until they move.

It has a poorer sense of boundaries.

A hand held still may be perceived as part of the wall. A lamp set on the ground becomes difficult to separate from the region it illuminates. The inhabitant would inspect a visitor by making small changes around them: inclining a pin, loosening a grain, drawing the light a little farther beneath an overhang.

If the visitor responds, it makes a place for that response.

This is how it recognizes something alive.

***

It spends much of its time near the doorway.

The room beyond is small from every direction available to a human body. To the inhabitant it is deep enough to lie down in, though not deep enough to turn over.

Inside are shallow recesses worn into the floor. Some fit parts of its body. Others no longer do. Against the back wall is a low ridge it has built from individual grains carried in over a very long period. Each grain occupies a position chosen with care. Together they keep the room from tilting toward the seventh step.

It rests with its clearer surfaces folded inward.

During rest, attraction along the eastern terraces weakens. A few lines of dust stop moving. The pillar continues to click, but the inhabitant does not answer every time. Occasionally a disturbance reaches it through the threshold, and one narrow part of its body extends into the basin.

It checks the three pins.

Then it withdraws.

There are intervals when it remains inside for so long that the doorway seems genuinely empty. Dust approaches the threshold but does not cross. The inhabitant keeps that boundary even in sleep, with the same slight effort by which another creature might keep its mouth closed.

***

It takes nourishment from the slow displacement of the basin.

Where two moving lines of dust meet, a small amount of the place’s unevenness becomes available. The inhabitant lowers a fine, jointed extremity to the ridge and holds it there. For several hours nothing seems to happen. Then a few grains settle, and one of its dark inner surfaces becomes less opaque.

It does not consume the dust. Afterward, the ridge remains.

Its feeding leaves the particles more securely beside one another. Over time this produces the rounded terrace edges and the broad, untouched intervals between the moving lines. Parts of the landscape have passed through its attention many times.

Other regions it avoids.

Below the band of reflected light is a patch of grit where the grains lean apart. Nothing is visibly wrong with it. The inhabitant will take most of a day to pass around that patch rather than extend itself across it.

Once, something lived there.

The surface that turned the light was part of its body. The inhabitant still makes room for it when moving beneath the overhang.

***

It has no reliable memory of arrival.

There are earlier arrangements of the basin that it remembers clearly: the pins upright; the dish whole; the pillar’s hollow broad enough for two of its folded parts to pass through together. It remembers a lower rim and more steps. Some led downward.

Its memories are kept in the shallow pits on its body.

When it wishes to recall something, it draws a fine edge across a group of them. The edge catches differently in each hollow. A sequence of minute resistances brings back an arrangement, sometimes with enough force to make the present basin seem wrong.

Age has softened these pits.

Several memories now return together. The completion of the doorway cannot be separated from a long blue interval. A journey toward the entrance brings with it the sensation of a plate breaking. It remembers having stood beside another inhabitant, but the features of that body have merged with the geometry of a terrace.

It spends increasing amounts of time over one small group of marks.

There is almost no resistance left there.

***

The footprint belongs to it, although the foot that made it does not.

For a brief period, long ago, the basin admitted a different kind of contact. The inhabitant gathered itself around that possibility. Its lower parts became heavy. The substrate supported them in a single direction. It experienced the whole of its weight arriving somewhere at once.

The print records the first step.

Before it could take another, the basin altered. The forward portion of its body entered the direction now held open by the dish. The part that had touched the floor unfolded and ceased to have an underside.

It remembers the contact with unusual clarity.

Sometimes it comes close to the footprint and lowers a joint toward the sharp front edge. It never quite touches it. The posture is awkward and costs it more than feeding restores.

Afterward, it rests.

***

It does not maintain the whole mechanism. Much of what the pillar does is beyond its understanding.

It knows a few adjustments: how to keep the doorway accessible, how to prevent the pins’ meeting shadows from slipping too far upward, how to lift dust from the crack without drawing the two halves closer together. It learned these through attempts whose consequences are still present in the basin.

One of the fragments overhead was once a tool.

Another was part of its body.

It can still feel that part faintly. The sensation comes from a great height and has become simpler over time. First there was strain, then cold, then only a recurring pressure along an edge.

The inhabitant no longer reaches toward it. Reaching made it smaller.

***

Recently, it has begun spending time at the entrance.

Getting there requires a succession of movements that are difficult to see as travel. Its pale repaired plate appears beside the pillar, then near the eastern wall, then briefly in the hollow of the square. Finally a dark joint comes to rest against the ceramic lip.

It stays behind the poised grain.

Outside, weather moves over the mountain. Changes in warmth pass through the casing. Sometimes an animal’s foot dislodges grit far below. Sometimes nothing nearby happens, and the pillar clicks anyway.

The inhabitant waits through all of this.

It has begun to make a recess on the inner face of the lip, just large enough to shelter the grain. The work proceeds slowly. Each time it presses against the ceramic, something farther inside its body must loosen.

When the recess is finished, the grain will have somewhere to settle without falling.

The inhabitant checks its depth, withdraws a little, and rests.
