The one who comes for the yellow beads lives farther back than the hollow has yet made room for.

At present, its dwelling is a crease behind the western wall, narrow enough that it must turn its head sideways to pass through. Tomorrow the crease may be a pocket, or solid stone, or part of the chamber. It has lived in all three conditions. When the mineral closes around it, it becomes very still and waits for water to find another way through.

It is not large. Stretched out, it would fit along the handle of the seed-shell spoon. But it seldom stretches out. Its resting shape is a folded one: knees against the underside of its jaw, arms laid along its legs, long hands clasped behind its neck. Thus arranged, it resembles a scrap of pale root caught in a crack.

There is nothing pale about it when it moves.

A flush passes through its skin, showing the course of vessels that are ordinarily invisible. For an instant it is red-brown, nearly the color of the swollen wedges. Then it grips the stone, and the color drains away. This happens at every exertion. Crossing the chamber, it flickers from foothold to foothold.

Its fingers are long because every surface here is liable to change between reaching and arriving. The fingers spread, touch, and bend wherever they find purchase. A joint may appear halfway along one and disappear after the grip is released. The nails, however, are definite: short, dark, blunt at the ends. They leave the deliberate scratches.

Its head is broad behind the eyes and very narrow in front. There is a mouth, but you would have difficulty deciding where. It opens along the lower edge of the face when the creature presses itself against a surface. Food is taken directly from stone: the minute things that settle on a fresh membrane during the few breaths before it whitens, the soft green scum underneath the jointed plants, sometimes a piece of the membrane itself.

It is careful not to eat the six rooted strands. Once, one of them came loose. The creature held it against the wall for most of an afternoon, with both hands, until new mineral gathered around its base.

The strand now grows crookedly. It lifts last after each passage of the current.

The creature has no gills that flutter. Nothing on it could survive that way. Water enters through close-set holes beneath its arms and leaves through a slit along its back. Inside, there is an arrangement of chambers that can be closed one after another. When the current turns, the creature shuts them and lets the violence carry it a little way before opening them again.

It does not hear the roar as a continuous sound.

Each collapsing bubble arrives through its bones as a separate contact. Most are disregarded. A few tell it that a membrane has thickened, that a projection has become hollow underneath, that the cup on the mound is about to overflow with new stone. It rests one hand on the floor and sorts these impacts by where they began.

The sharpest ones hurt. You can see this. Its fingers close, and the skin above its eyes draws tight.

There is an old injury along one side of its face: a smooth depression where the skin has healed against something that was pressing there. It cannot turn that eye fully toward the entrance. When it checks the wooden wedges, it presents the other side of its head.

It made the spoon.

The shell was carried up from a place far below the cloud line, where seeds fall into streams and soften without immediately becoming anything else. The creature split it with a flake of newly hardened mineral. It used the thicker half for the bowl and patiently wore the other into a handle.

The groove took longer. The wall kept growing over it.

For several returns, the creature would find the spoon trapped beneath a thin white covering. It cut away the covering, deepened the groove, and put the spoon back. Eventually it found a place where the stone added itself mostly around the tool rather than across it. The fit you see is partly workmanship and partly the result of having persisted.

The bark screen was harder to bring.

Wet bark is nearly weightless here, but an unfastened length of it catches the current and fills the chamber with motion. The creature came through the entrance wrapped in its own weaving. It had to crawl beneath the tilted blade to unwrap itself, anchoring each freed strip before releasing the next.

One corner is still knotted incorrectly. Under strain, that knot tightens in a way that makes it difficult to undo. The creature has picked at it often. It has not replaced it.

For all its patience, it sometimes leaves work unfinished.

A broken peg rests behind the white knob. There are five little scratches on the mound where a different screen was meant to be attached. Near the entrance, a pit has been started and abandoned because water struck too strongly inside it. These failures are already disappearing. The creature occasionally feels for them after they are gone.

The yellow beads are taken only when they can be lifted whole.

It approaches the depression from below, holding the spoon backward so that the bowl trails behind its wrist. There it waits through the surges. If a bead gathers, the creature places two fingers on either side of it without touching it, then brings the spoon beneath.

Sometimes the bead divides.

Then the creature withdraws the spoon and waits. The divided portions disappear into the water, and there is nothing to take that day.

With a whole bead in the shell, it moves slowly toward the entrance. It does not grip with both hands now. One hand and both feet must do the work. At the wedges, it turns sideways, pushes the spoon through first, and follows.

Outside, it cannot remain uncovered for long. Its skin loses its suppleness in the air. The fingers settle into fixed joints. Its back slit seals, then begins to gape uselessly.

It shelters beneath the overhang in a fold of wet bark, where a little water persists even in freezing weather. The four yellow stones lie there on their cord. They keep that small fold from freezing solid.

There used to be five.

The empty place on the cord is apparent: two knots with a length of fiber between them, stained yellow. The creature touches this length whenever it adds a bead. It draws the fiber through its fingers as though checking for damage, although there is no damage to find.

Beneath the bark lives a second creature.

This one is scarcely longer than a grain of barley. Its hands are short. Its back has not opened. Most of its body is clear, except for a dark mass behind the eyes and a fine yellow line running down each side.

It remains curled around one of the stones.

The larger creature brings water to it in the spoon and lets the water pass over its body. Then it empties the spoon, returns through the seam, and fills it again. On calm days this takes little time. On days when the current runs outward, it may spend an hour gaining the entrance.

The small one has begun to unclasp a hand.

Sometimes it catches a fiber of the bark. Sometimes the larger creature offers a finger, and the little hand closes around the nail. Neither moves until the grip opens of itself.

The yellow line is becoming fainter. Water now enters beneath the small creature’s arms, though it does not yet pass all the way through. When it does, the larger one will carry it inside.

For this reason, the new wall matters.

The larger creature was on the ridge when the mineral sheet crossed the chamber and fixed itself to the opposite stone. It returns near evening with the empty spoon and stops at the unfamiliar surface.

First it touches the edge. Then it presses its cheek against the wall. The separate shocks reach it from the water beyond: hard, numerous, coming from surfaces it has never touched.

It searches along the bottom until it finds a gap no wider than its wrist.

The gap is thickening.

For a while it simply holds it open with its hand. White gathers against the fingers. When it pulls them free, five shallow channels remain, already narrowing.

It goes to the entrance and removes the smallest wooden wedge.

The other two creak under the added strain. It stays beside them long enough to be sure they will hold, then carries the wedge across the chamber and drives it into the new gap. The tethered stone knocks against the floor.

Beyond the wall is a narrow pocket sheltered from the main current. Daylight enters it through the translucent roof. Along its floor, a membrane has begun to gather, still clear, still bending.

The creature passes one arm through. It feels the floor, the roof, the place where the current enters and turns aside. Then it withdraws and settles beside the opening.

For the rest of the evening it keeps the gap clear with its nails.

Outside, under the bark, the small hand closes and opens around a fiber. Inside, a little pile of white curls gathers against the creature’s feet. Each time the stone presses inward, it cuts it back.
