At first, what comes out of the passage is indistinguishable from the water carrying it.

There is a thickening in the brightness, a place where the floor seems nearer than it should. It passes beneath the racing surface without breaking it. At the basin’s lip, where the water folds down toward the center of the room, several pale lengths separate from the flow and fasten to the stone.

The surge goes on. The pale lengths stay.

They are jointed, though the joints are difficult to find. Each bends through a succession of small angles rather than at a knee or elbow. Their undersides spread against the rock in narrow, scalloped strips. The current lifts their loose edges, showing a rose-gray lining, then presses them flat again.

A larger shape draws itself over the basin’s rim.

It is about as long as a person lying down, much flatter, and too broad through the middle to fit the passage from which it has apparently emerged. Only when it pauses do you see how much of it is folded. Its body consists of overlapping lobes, each edged with a thin mineral sheath. These slide over one another as it moves. Water runs between them and emerges through openings underneath.

Its back has the color of wet cream stone. There are darker veins in it, and small irregular pits full of sediment. One lobe bears the beginnings of the same crust that rims the ledges. Another is clean and translucent, with a slow contraction visible inside.

It has been lying still somewhere for a long time. It has also been growing.

Six of the jointed lengths hold it above the rock. Two more remain folded beneath its front, finer than the others and tipped with clusters of short, blunt fingers. What seemed to be a trailing ribbon of water is another part of the body: a thin membrane, nearly transparent, spread between its rear limbs. The membrane streams downstream while the animal advances across the current.

Nothing resembling a head lifts to look around.

Instead, a seam opens along its forward edge. From the seam emerge several soft stalks, no longer than your thumb. They incline separately: one toward the arch, one toward the waterfall, two toward the entrance. Their tips are dull and porous. Spray collects on them without beading.

The stalk directed toward you remains extended after the others withdraw.

The animal crosses a channel that would take your legs out from under you. Its feet do not grip harder when the pressure increases. They release in sequence, letting the body swing sideways, then attach farther along. The movement is economical and surprisingly loose. For a moment it hangs beneath an overhang, upside down, with the whole torrent passing over its belly.

From there, in the upward light, its underside is visible.

The belly is dark. Not black, but the deep brown of the coating around the fronds. Thin bands of paler tissue divide it into chambers. Those chambers swell independently, making shallow bulges that travel from the rear toward the front. Along either side runs a row of small openings with flexible lips. Some are shut. Some admit water. A few release fine bubbles that cling to the animal until the next pulse tears them away.

It waits under the overhang through the strongest part of the surge.

Then it turns one forelimb upward and touches a hollow in the stone.

The touch is brief. It moves on.

Once you have seen this, you begin noticing other hollows: small depressions on the sheltered sides of pillars, at the entrances to channels, beneath the lips of basins. They are smoother than the surrounding rock. Some lie far above the present water level. Others are accessible only from below.

The inhabitant touches three of them on its way to the arch.

At the dark ceramic, it folds itself into the narrow space between the feet. Its back is now almost submerged. The suspended thread passes through the open seam at its front and rests against something inside.

The arch clicks.

The creature closes around the thread.

For several seconds, its body becomes less opaque. Light reveals branching structures within it, packed closely through the central lobes and spreading more sparsely into the limbs. There is no single bright organ, no obvious center. The illumination moves through the branches unevenly, stopping at thickened knots before continuing.

One of the old plates in the arch’s inner edge clears a little.

The animal holds still until the next click. Then it withdraws, taking nothing visible with it.

Its fine forelimbs unfold.

One reaches into an empty rectangular recess. The fingers explore the corners, scrape along the back, and pull free a curl of whitish deposit. The other limb catches the curl before the water can carry it away. Both hands bring it beneath the body.

A moment later, the deposit is gone.

It works through several recesses this way. At one, the fingers stop. The animal shifts its weight, and a narrow flap lifts near the center of its back. Beneath it lies a cloudy rectangle with rounded corners.

The rectangle comes away slowly. A clear strand stretches between it and the exposed tissue, thins, and breaks.

Held between the small hands, the new plate looks soft. The animal presses it into the recess, works each edge down, and keeps one palm against it while another surge passes through the arch. When the hand lifts, the plate has darkened to match the others.

There is a raw-looking patch on the animal’s back where it lay.

The flap closes over it.

You cannot tell whether this is repair, feeding, or some obligation it has carried out so often that the distinction has disappeared. It gives no sign of satisfaction. It simply moves to the next recess.

Only afterward does it approach the dishes.

Its body settles beside them, shedding water in a dozen small streams. The two sediment-filled dishes receive a quick examination. It probes their contents and removes a dark pellet from one, rolling it between its fingers before placing it on the stone.

At the clean dish, it lowers its front seam over the water lens.

The lens rises to meet it.

For a while they remain joined by a short, clear neck. The fine limbs brace against the dish’s rim. Under the mineral sheaths, the creature’s body makes a slow expanding movement, then another. The lens dwindles, although it never disappears.

When the seam lifts away, the lens settles back into its shallow curve. The animal stays beside it longer than it stayed at the arch. Its limbs relax. One of the sensory stalks droops until its tip touches the dish.

This appears to be rest.

The chamber quiets enough for you to hear water draining from the high seams in the wall.

Then the stalk lifts.

The inhabitant turns toward the stairs.

It does not come directly to you. It approaches by a route of sheltered channels, pauses beneath the metal staples, and reaches up to touch the lowest one. Mineral scale cracks beneath its fingers. The exposed metal catches the light.

At the foot of the stairs, it stops again.

Now there is only a narrow channel between you. Up close, the pale sheaths are less like stone than like worn teeth. Their edges have been polished by movement against one another. Small things live in the pits on its back: dark filaments, a pair of translucent specks that withdraw when its skin contracts. Along the healed margin of one lobe, several deep scratches run parallel. Something once caught it there, or it forced itself through a passage before it was wide enough.

The stalks emerge toward your feet.

Warm water drips from your clothes. Each drop striking the shelf produces a tiny adjustment in their angle. When you shift your weight, all of them incline toward the point where your sole presses the stone.

It waits.

You stand still.

One of its finer hands reaches across the channel and touches the edge of your shelf. It moves along the rough surface until it finds a loose pebble, lifts it, and places it farther back, near the wall.

Then it places another beside it.

There are already several there, half-hidden in the dimness above the floor’s light. They form a shallow, uneven line across the shelf. On the stairward side, the stone is dry. Beyond the line, a faint sheen of water has begun to gather.

The animal withdraws its hand.

A low knock passes through the floor.

It leaves before the channel rises, folding beneath the stair and flattening itself into a crevice you had taken for a dark vein. The last visible part is one porous stalk, held just outside the opening.

Across the chamber, the arch clicks twice.

Water strikes the shelf, covers the place where the creature’s hand rested, and stops short of the pebbles.

From inside the crevice comes a small answering click, too soft to hear until the torrent has passed.
