At first it is one of the grains that has not settled properly.

It rests at an angle beside a pale crystal, dark side uppermost, with a little ash caught along its edge. When the floor trembles, it trembles with the floor. When a stronger vibration makes the loose dust slip, it holds its place.

Then two legs unfold from beneath it.

It is a mite, broad through the middle and scarcely longer than the crystal beside it. Its back is covered by a hard, brown-black shield. At the entrance that shield would shine; here it has only the dull finish of the surrounding stone. Dust has collected in the shallow grooves where its plates meet. Some of that dust has traveled farther than the animal ever will.

The legs are lighter than the body. Their joints look almost transparent. They lift it just clear of the ground, high enough that its underside need not touch every surface its feet encounter. The front pair reach ahead, pause, withdraw, reach again. Before the body enters a space, these have already examined it.

One foot touches the crystal. Another finds the ash beneath its lip. The mite lowers itself into the gap.

For several moments it appears to have vanished. Then its rear legs follow.

Its home is not the whole seam. It occupies a few connected hollows beneath a lifted skin of basalt, with a cooler route toward the entrance and a deeper route that ends in heat. Between them lies a shelf where the temperature changes less abruptly. There is room on that shelf for the mite to turn around, though only if it draws two legs close beneath its body.

It has crossed this shelf many times. A faint track curves around a splinter too tall to climb. In one place the track divides: the shorter branch leads over a thin flake that was tolerable yesterday. Today the mite approaches it, touches it, and takes the longer way.

There is no visible sign explaining the choice.

Beneath the flake, warm gas has begun to pass through a new opening.

The mite does not know the opening is new. It knows the contact at its foot, the air against its body, the need to withdraw. Its familiarity with the place consists of such encounters, repeated and altered. A route remains a route only as long as it can be traveled.

Near the broken moth wing it stops.

The wing is enormous at this scale, a sloping sheet with ribs beneath it and a ragged edge lodged against the stone. Under that edge, finer debris has accumulated: scales, ash, broken particles of tissue. Other mites have worked here. A pale one moves slowly through the sheltered material, its soft body narrowing as it enters a space between two ribs.

The dark mite touches the debris with its mouthparts. It stays for a while.

Around it, the seam continues its almost invisible traffic. A springtail passes below the wing, following the underside rather than the upper surface. Something smaller moves inside the silver-gray heap of scales. Farther along, the spider shifts in its hollow, drawing one leg back from the dusty web.

The mite eventually leaves by a different gap.

Its shield scrapes the roof. A grain catches at the joint of a rear leg, travels with it for several steps, and falls into another hollow. That is one of the changes made here today.

There are others. Food becomes smaller pieces. Droppings lodge between crystals. A dead body loses the firmness that once held open a space beneath it. The inhabitants rearrange the floor by amounts too slight to see from outside, but sufficient to obstruct a passage or expose something edible.

The machine below changes load.

A pulse passes through the shelf. The mite stops with its front legs extended. Nearby, one beetle also pauses, wedged halfway beneath the windblown debris. The spider remains where it is.

The next pulse comes, then another. After a while the mite resumes moving. Whatever the vibration means, it has not made this particular passage impassable.

Later, when the machinery stops altogether, the mite is beneath the lifted basalt skin. Its legs maintain their separate contacts with the floor. For a short interval there is only wind, and then the small click of a flake moving somewhere beyond its hollow.

It stays still until a springtail brushes past.

At the back of the seam, the fresh crack gives off its steady heat. The mite does not approach the opening. It joins several others among the stones farther out, where the warmth has weakened and the narrow spaces still retain something the moving air would carry away.

They do not form a cluster. Each has found its own place: beneath a chip, alongside a crystal, in the shadow of an ash grain. Between them are distances small enough for their legs to span, yet none touches another.

The dark mite settles with its shield just below the roof. Its legs fold, but not completely. Two remain extended into the passage.

Above it, the stone is cooling slowly. Below it, the mountain begins to hum again.
