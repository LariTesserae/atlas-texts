At first it is only a place where the leaves do not lift.

The gust turns the foliage all around it, showing pale undersides, shaking droplets loose. But beneath the horizontal tree, a small patch stays dark. There is weight there, spread thinly through the branches. When the wind passes, that weight withdraws from one twig and settles onto another. The released twig rises.

It lives inside the thickness of the forest, not on the ground beneath it. The ground is too uncertain. A root may hold for years and then come away with the stone it grips. The creature rests across several supports at once: part of itself on the fallen trunk, part in the fork of a shrub, one narrow limb reaching uphill into the exposed roots. It does not require any one of these to remain where it is.

Its body is longer than the space it occupies. Folded among the branches, it might be taken for a bundle of wet bark. There is a narrow chest, a belly drawn close to the spine, and a head that can tuck so far backward that the neck disappears. The limbs are difficult to count. Some of what seem to be limbs are lengths of tail, or loose folds of the coat hanging from an elbow. But when it moves, four hands can be distinguished, opening and closing at different heights.

Each hand has two long central fingers and shorter fingers set around them. It can encompass a thin branch without squeezing it. On rock, the fingers flatten, bringing ridged pads against the surface; in moss, they spread and find the bark underneath. Its nails are short and dark. They make almost no sound.

The coat is not thick enough to explain how it survives the cold. Close to the skin, the hairs are fine and densely packed. Above them grow longer, flattened hairs, gray at the roots and nearly transparent at their ends. Water travels outward along these and hangs clear of the body. When the creature is still, the droplets make it look threaded into the surrounding leaves.

There are bare places beneath its wrists and along the underside of its jaw. It presses these against whatever holds it.

Tonight, one wrist rests on the tipped tree. The other touches a root emerging from the pale wall. Its chin lies against a branch no thicker than a thumb. Through these contacts it feels three different versions of the wind.

The tree turns slowly under load. The root trembles in brief, close shivers. The small branch repeats every collision in the crown above.

The creature waits.

It has eyes, large but deeply recessed, with lids that close from the sides. Against the fresh stone, they catch a little light. They are not looking into the valley. They follow the hanging root where it enters the torn earth.

That root used to be underfoot.

Before the break, the creature’s sleeping place was farther uphill, inside a hollow screened by fern stems. The hollow is still there, but its lower half is now open to the air. A dark bowl of compressed leaves can be seen among the dangling roots. A few hairs remain caught along its rim.

It has not returned to collect anything.

When the grit begins to fall, it lifts its chin. Its fingers stay in place. A pebble strikes the buried slab; another comes loose. By the time the second stone reaches the branches, the creature has drawn its belly upward, allowing it to pass through the space where its body was.

Afterward it extends one hand into that space and feels the branch again.

It is not fearless. Fear moves through it locally. A wrist stiffens. The skin at its throat tightens. One hind hand closes hard enough to whiten the branch beneath its pads. Then the tension passes, leaving the rest of the body free to act.

It feeds during such pauses.

Along the sheltered side of the fallen tree, the bark has loosened without drying. The creature works a nail beneath an edge and raises it slightly. Its muzzle enters the gap. There is a quick movement of the tongue, then another. It takes the pale larvae living there and leaves the bark almost where it was.

Farther along, it bites through a softened fern stem and chews the inner tissue. The jaws move slowly. Its ears, small and nearly hidden in the coat, turn independently while it eats.

The sound from the valley troubles them.

It is too regular to ignore and too distant to locate precisely. At intervals, the creature holds still with its mouth slightly open. The metallic pulse reaches the notch, weakens, returns. Its ears angle toward the drop, then toward the stone wall, where the sound is reflected.

There is no answering movement in the wood beneath its hands.

Eventually it resumes chewing.

It has done this many nights. The machinery has become something it must check and then set aside. But each time the wind brings the note close, its ears turn again.

The stronger gust arrives while it is reaching for a cluster of leaves.

Its body lowers immediately. The long coat lies flat. Two hands draw inward; the other two keep their holds. For a moment it hangs beneath the tree with its back almost touching the root mat, sheltered by the wood rather than the foliage.

The tree twists.

This time something changes in the uphill root. The creature feels it before the creak becomes audible. One hand opens. The released root swings away, taking a small clot of soil with it.

The creature drops the length of its arm and catches a shrub below.

There is no leap, no scrambling. Its weight passes through the remaining holds in sequence. The shrub bends sharply. Leaves brush the pale wall. For an instant the animal is stretched across the new gap, its belly exposed to the cold air rising from the valley.

Then it folds itself into the branches again.

A little earth continues falling behind it.

Now it is farther from the stone and nearer the outer edge. There is less shelter here. Rain strikes the long hairs of its back. Its eyes close, but its hands remain open against the wood.

It cannot sleep yet.

When the weather eases, it noses into a crotch between two trunks. It turns once, slowly, fitting the length of its body into the available curves. One hind hand stays extended toward the fallen tree. Another grips the living shrub. Its tail lies across both.

This will be its bed for as long as these things remain together.

Below it, beyond the leaves, the machinery continues. Above it, soil loosens around an exposed root. The creature rests its bare jaw against the wood and keeps one eye open until the next gust has passed.
