The people have soles as pale as the animals’ bellies. When one sits with their feet turned outward, you can see fine lines across the skin, some branching, some running straight from heel to toe. Powder has settled into them. The same powder lies in the seams of the plateau, and it is difficult to tell whether the lines were made by walking or whether the stone has fitted itself to the feet.

They are not all old. There are children, although you may spend several days mistaking them for small adults. A child stands under the chest of a tall animal whose legs bend in unfamiliar places. One hand rests against a tendon. The other holds a pebble with a hole through it.

At intervals the child draws the pebble a little way along the tendon, stops, and listens.

You hear nothing. The child waits with the absorbed patience of someone doing a thing that can be done incorrectly.

The animal’s head is too high to examine from beneath it. From a shelf some distance away, you can see that its face is small, almost unfinished, with two soft nostrils and eyes close together. It seems poorly equipped to oversee so much body. Nevertheless, it knows where the child is. A narrow fold of skin descends from its breast and shelters the top of the child’s head without touching it.

Elsewhere, such folds lie empty.

The six-legged creatures are among the easiest to approach. Their weight is distributed unevenly: four legs hold up the trunk, while the remaining pair, shorter and set close together, rest on the ground as lightly as fingers. Their hides have the faint grain of weathered wood. What appear to be stripes are shallow creases, each containing a different shade of dust.

One has a mouth beneath its body. You discover this when it lifts the smaller pair of feet and reveals lips pressed around a smooth stone.

It keeps the stone there without chewing. The stone is darker than anything nearby.

If you kneel, the animal lowers itself slightly, making the underside easier to see. This takes a long time. During the movement, its upper body remains at precisely the same height against the horizon.

There are animals whose anatomy becomes clearer with observation and others whose anatomy becomes less so. A shell-backed creature has a seam around its circumference, apparently the meeting of two halves. Follow the seam with your eyes and it returns to its starting point on the inside of the shell. You can see this quite plainly. It gives you a headache only when you try to remember it afterward.

A small woolless animal carries three young against its side. They are attached by their mouths, or by narrow feet resembling mouths. Their eyes are open. The parent’s eyes are shut, but underneath the thin lids something moves in separate directions.

One of the young watches you until you leave. Much later, looking down into a distant basin, you see the same small eye looking up. You recognize the pale notch in its lower lid. The parent is still on the shelf behind you.

The people seem able to recognize individuals of kinds you cannot reliably distinguish. A woman with a broad, flat face goes among the creatures in a depression, touching some and passing others. She holds a length of braided fiber with seven loose ends. At one animal she lays an end across the neck. At another she slips a strand beneath a blunt horn. None of the strands is tied.

By the time she reaches the far side, the braid has come apart. Each separate length lies on an animal, and she has nothing in her hands.

The next time you see her, she is braiding.

Her clothing has been repaired so often that there is little room left for hide between the stitches. Some patches are smooth and nearly transparent. Others carry short fur growing in a direction that makes them uncomfortable to look at. Along one sleeve, six small toes remain. They curl gently against her wrist.

You do not see the people eat. There are bowls, and there are mouths capable of eating, and in one shelter a flat stone bears the shallow pits left by repeated grinding. But the bowls are empty or turned over. A man holds a piece of pale root between his teeth while working with both hands. Whenever you pass, the root protrudes by the same amount. It has tooth marks at the end.

His work is easier to follow. He is smoothing the edge of a bone.

The edge is already smooth. He rubs it with another bone, pauses, tests it against the pad of his thumb, and begins again. Beside his knee is a pile of fine curls. They are not bone shavings. Each has a little dark tip, and from time to time one uncurls far enough to show legs.

He pushes them aside carefully.

Some of the plateau’s smallest inhabitants live close to the people. They gather where clothing touches skin, beneath the rims of bowls, inside the holes of beads. There is a kind that resembles a pale seed with articulated edges. Another consists mostly of two feet and a membrane stretched between them. When disturbed, it folds itself along a crease already present in the air.

The buildings accommodate them. The central post of the slab shelter has several polished hollows near its base. One is large enough for a cheek. Another could receive the curved back of a thumb-sized creature. Both are occupied when you look inside.

The person resting their cheek against the wood has their eyes open. The little creature’s eyes are closed.

You begin to notice such arrangements throughout the plateau: a shoulder beneath a lip of stone, a horn against a person’s palm, a tiny body nestled in the gap between two teeth. The fit is exact, but there is no pressure. A person can withdraw their hand. An animal can raise its head. For a while afterward, the empty space retains the shape of what has left it.

The people are attentive to these spaces.

One kneels beside an unoccupied sleeping hollow and lays a strip of hide across it. They lift the strip, inspect it, and lay it down again at a slightly different angle. On the underside are rows of short cuts. On the upper side, where the hide has crossed the hollow, a damp-looking mark appears.

The mark is shaped like an ear.

The person sees you watching and turns the hide over before setting it on their knees.

They do speak, though seldom within your hearing. Their voices are quite ordinary: low, sometimes rough, sometimes unexpectedly young. Once you hear two people exchanging a few words beside a wall. One laughs briefly through the nose. The other looks down at their hands.

You are relieved by the sound, more than you expected to be.

Later, passing the same wall from another direction, you hear the laugh again. The two people are standing farther apart. Neither has a hand raised, but the sound seems to come from the small interval between the fingers of one of them.

They do not beckon you away. They let you stop and listen.

Near the rim lives a person with a cloudy patch in one eye. They have made a place to sit between three animals, two facing inward and one outward. The inward-facing pair have low heads and heavy lips. The outward-facing one has no visible head at all; its back rises toward the drop, and a line of small breathing holes runs along the exposed ridge.

There is just enough room for another person.

If you sit, the animals do not shift. You feel the coolness of one flank through your clothes. After a time, the cloudy-eyed person reaches across and rests two fingers on your wrist.

Their fingers are warm.

They keep them there until you become aware that your pulse and the breathing holes are taking turns. Not matching: taking turns. A beat beneath the skin, then a slight opening along the animal’s back. Sometimes there is a long interval. The person’s fingers do not tighten.

When they release you, they inspect the place on your wrist as if a small mark might have been left.

Farther along the rim, the largest inhabitants stand where walking becomes difficult to judge. They have immense, rounded limbs, plates thick enough to resemble separate ledges, lashes pale as salt. One has an old injury behind its foreleg. The flesh has closed around it in smooth folds. Inside the folds, sheltered from the slight coolness of the air, grows a miniature tree.

Its canopy is no wider than a hand. Beneath the beast, among the feet of the animals arranged along its shadow, lies the shade of a forest.

You can sit there. The stone stays cool, but the light has the greenish dimness of deep foliage. Once, while seated, you hear something fall through branches overhead. Nothing reaches the ground.

The beast’s eye is open. You had taken it for a hollow in the plate above you. A clear membrane passes over it so slowly that its leading edge appears at first to be a flaw.

Behind that membrane, the eye turns toward the place where you are sitting.

Around you, the smaller animals keep their positions. A blunt horn occupies a notch of shadow. A narrow creature lies beside it with its forefeet extended. Between those feet is an empty space, about the size of your hand.

You rest your hand there.

After a while, something underneath the stone touches one finger.
