At first it looks like one of the loose stones beside the wall: a low, cream-colored body with a darker underside, its back rounded but not quite smooth. It is about the size of a curled cat. Fine gray lines cross its surface, irregular enough to resemble the seams in the limestone.

Then it lifts its head, and a little clay falls from beneath its chin.

Its head is broad, carried close to the ground. There is no distinct neck. Two eyes sit well apart beneath folds of thick skin, their brown almost the brown of the pool. They are clear and moist, though so little of each eye is exposed that they seldom catch the light. Between them, the face slopes toward a wide, soft mouth.

The rest of its body is less stony than it first appeared. Its back has thickened into overlapping pads, pale on top and gray at their edges. Beneath these, the skin is pliant, faintly wrinkled, warm to the touch. Four short limbs fold close against its sides. Each foot ends in several blunt, spreading toes, with a small dark nail set well back from the tip. They look better suited to pressing against rock than digging into it.

When it stands, it gains very little height.

It lives in the shallow recess behind the northern shoulder, where the damp seam disappears around a bend. The entrance is wider than its body but low enough that its back brushes the stone. Inside, the clay has been pressed into a smooth, shallow bed. There are a few flakes from its skin, thin and translucent at the edges, and a stone that has been pushed aside often enough to leave a short groove.

It comes out while the hollow is still warm.

For a while it remains near the opening, head raised just above its forefeet. Its sides move with its breathing. The motion is slight but surprisingly quick beside everything else about it.

Then it crosses to the seam.

Its walk is deliberate, though not feeble. One foot is placed, its toes spread, and the weight follows. On bare limestone it makes almost no sound. On clay there is a faint tackiness as a foot lifts, audible only nearby. Its belly clears the ground except where the floor rises over an embedded stone.

At the wall it turns its head sideways and lays its mouth against the darkened limestone. A narrow tongue passes along the seam. It works a few inches at a time, pausing with its lips still against the rock. Whether it is drinking or taking something from the thin film there is difficult to tell. Afterward, moisture remains in the folds around its mouth.

It does not make the rounds of every wet place. The small depression several yards from the pool may remain untouched for days. Sometimes it drinks from the pool’s clay edge, lowering its face until its chin darkens. It withdraws without shaking itself. A drop gathers beneath its mouth and falls onto its forefoot.

The worn step is another place it uses. Its body fits across the lowered center, with its forefeet resting on the floor inside the rectangle. From there it can see the gap at the far end and most of the open ground. It settles heavily, then adjusts one hind foot. Its eyes remain open.

The masonry seems to interest it only as a surface. It rubs one cheek along a fitted corner, passing slowly enough that the skin bunches ahead of the stone. On the shaded side it sometimes mouths the edge of the moss. A small clean patch marks where it has cropped it, but the surrounding growth is undisturbed.

There are other signs of its life, once one knows where to look: a pale smear on the stone beneath the overhang; tiny dark pellets tucked under a shelf; a crescent-shaped impression in clay that is not quite a footprint. Near its recess, one rounded rock has a polished side at the height of its shoulder.

It can move quickly. If something comes through the gap, its head turns first. The feet draw underneath it, and for a moment its body looks taller and narrower. A few swift steps carry it behind the limestone shoulder. There is no scramble, only a brief dry brushing of its back against the entrance.

Later, an eye becomes visible in the recess.

If nothing approaches, it comes out again. It may stop halfway to the step and rest beside the solitary stone near the center. There the muted light shows the fine texture of its back: pores, old scratches, small patches darker than the rest. A fly lands on it. One fold of skin contracts, and the fly leaves.

When a drop falls into the pool, it sometimes turns toward the sound. It holds that position for a little while, then lowers its chin onto its feet.
