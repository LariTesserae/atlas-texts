The first thing the power did was nothing that could be distinguished from the mountain.

It listened through a season of closed ice.

It followed the streams without straightening them. It entered the gardens without making them flower. At the house facing the basin, it watched the bowl carried out and carried back, and did not put anything into it.

There were many things it could have put there.

But it did not yet know what the bowl was for.

It learned that the same stone could be a burden on one crossing and a necessary part of someone on the next. That a cord almost lost inside its covering of roots could still be handled with exactness. That the inhabitants sometimes abandoned useful pieces and carried awkward ones a long way.

It learned to tell an empty shelter from a shelter whose occupant was dispersed through it.

This took longer.

In one hut, what appeared to be a loose shutter was part of the inhabitant resting along the wall. In another, three pebbles below a bench belonged to no one. The power left both undisturbed until it knew the difference.

Under the fallen slab, the thread continued to rise through the drilled stone.

The power settled there.

When the others came, they found nothing new to look at. But the silt beside the two wooden members no longer lay entirely still. A little water moved beneath the slab, too little to scour it, enough to carry away what the rising thread released.

The visitors remained longer than usual.

One brought a pebble and placed it at the edge. The power did not move the pebble closer. Another passed a swollen cord beneath the projecting wood, waited, and drew it back.

For a long time, nothing followed.

Then the wood turned against the cord.

The movement was slight. It happened again when the visitor returned.

Only then did the power begin to make room.

It did not lift the slab. The old stream continued across its upper face, rounded the same corner, and fell from the same lip. Beneath it, grains of sediment loosened one by one. They traveled out through a narrow opening on the downhill side.

Over many visits, the opening widened.

A hollow appeared between the wooden members. The drilled stone rose far enough to clear its bed, then settled again. The power stopped removing sediment.

There was now space to leave.

For the rest of that cold season, the inhabitant stayed beneath the slab.

Sometimes a visitor sat at the opening. Sometimes the drilled stone moved nearer, and a little circulating water joined the two bodies. Once, a pebble was passed inside and later returned.

During the thaw, one wooden member came out.

It lay on the slope until the next visitor arrived. Together they brought the drilled stone through the opening. The second member remained under the slab.

No one went back for it.

The power kept the opening clear.

***

Elsewhere, its work was smaller.

At a bare stretch of road where many worked pieces had been lost, warmth began to reach the paving through an existing crack. It was scarcely enough to change the water. Low growth spread from stone to stone over several years.

The power waited to see whether the inhabitants used it.

At first they crossed as before, holding close to the old patches and losing pieces in the long intervals between them. Then a small one paused in the new growth. Its loop of fiber caught among the leaves. Its three pebbles rested apart without drifting away.

When it moved on, it took all three.

Others began to stop there.

The place became neither a grove nor a shelter. It was a stretch of road where a body could loosen without being lost. Shed pieces lay among the roots. Some were collected. Some remained.

The power made no more warmth than the crack could bear.

It left the dark basins dark. It left the heights cold, and did not introduce mouths among the leaves.

At the thaw, however, one seam in the ice stayed open a little longer.

Not wide enough to admit anything from above. Wide enough for the inhabitants beneath it to see the colors of one another’s stones.

They gathered there until the light began to narrow. One with a black slab along its back climbed down first. A smaller body followed in the current it displaced.

The power watched them descend.

The following year, it opened the same seam.

***

At the house, it still waited.

The bowl had a repaired place beneath its worn rim. The repair was old enough that growth had entered the join. When the inhabitant held it at the threshold, one wooden member always supported that side.

The power had considered making the bowl sound.

It left the repair alone.

A visitor came down the climbing road during the next thaw. It carried a short piece of cord close to its hollow and stopped outside the curtain of leaves.

The current was weak. The leaves did not rise.

Inside, the inhabitant remained spread around the niche.

The power moved a little water through the rooted branch.

The curtain lifted.

The bowl came to the threshold. The visitor entered, slowly, with the cord held clear of its outer frame. For a while the two rested together in the swept strip.

Then the visitor extended the cord.

The inhabitant touched it with the member that supported the bowl.

Nothing passed between them. The visitor withdrew the cord and settled beside the doorway. The bowl remained out longer than it usually did.

Afterward, the power allowed the leaves to descend.

It did not hold the doorway open for the rest of the season. It had seen enough to know that the curtain also sheltered the house, and that not every arrival was an invitation.

But when someone waited outside, the leaves stirred.

Sometimes the inhabitant gathered.

Sometimes it did not.

***

Years passed before the power was given anything.

It was waiting beside the road, in the small current that moved through the new resting place. An inhabitant stopped there carrying a white stone, two dark ones, and a length of wood with a rounded end.

From close to its hollow, it drew out a wooden peg.

The peg had once fitted something. One end was polished by use; the other was split.

The inhabitant placed it in the current where the power waited.

Then it moved away.

The power kept the peg there. It held the water gently enough that nothing rubbed against the split end, and allowed a fine strand of growth to secure it to the road.

Later, the inhabitant returned.

It paused beside the peg, touched it, and continued toward the pass.

The next time it came, there were two others with it.

Beyond the ridge, a loose stone knocked against another stone. Water crossed the drowned saddle. In the house, the bowl rested in its niche.

And along the old road, there was now somewhere else to stop.
