The power did not put its face to the seam.

A face would have darkened the entrance. A breath would have overturned the dust. Even the gentlest arrival, made at the wrong size, would have been a catastrophe.

It attended instead to the coolness of the stone.

There were differences within that coolness: the deep, steady cold beneath the nursery flake; the shallower chill of the socket near the entrance; a place beside the copper tube where warmth descended through the metal and ended in a patch no larger than a seed. The inhabitants used these differences. A few degrees, a little shelter, the condition of a film beneath a foot: these were not details added to their world. They were much of the world.

The power stayed until the engines stopped.

It stayed through the change of air, the emergence onto the copper tube, the cleaning of feelers. It followed one pale animal without making that animal conspicuous to anything else.

This one had lost a hind foot.

The healed end of the leg touched down with the others, but found nothing. On level stone the absence mattered little. At the retaining clip it mattered more. The animal crossed by using its feelers against the underside, making a third point of contact while its remaining feet changed their hold.

The power could have given the foot back.

It did.

There was no flash. During the animal’s next shedding, a folded limb came free with the others. The animal stood while its covering hardened. Later it cleaned the new foot, drawing it toward its mouthparts with precisely the same attention it gave the old ones.

For a time the power did nothing else.

It wanted to know what a repaired foot meant here. Whether the animal would travel farther, whether it would feed differently, whether the repair would merely deliver it sooner to the waiting hunter beside the quartz.

The animal took a different route on its next circuit. At the clip it went over rather than under. It found a scrap of food lodged against the metal, fed, and returned to the nursery flake.

That was enough to begin with.

***

Above the chamber, the bead of oil was growing.

Its underside curved around a reflection of the inspection grille. Through that small bright image the power could see the pale bodies moving below.

It did not make the oil disappear. It had watched long enough to know what depended on its arrival: not only the animals that fed at its edges, but the organisms in their guts, the creatures that ate their droppings, the beetles, the narrow hunters, the inhabitants of shed skins.

Nor did it decide that, because the oil sustained them, every manner of its arrival must be left alone.

On the underside of the stair was a loose filament from an old belt. It hung within a hair’s breadth of the bead.

The power brought the two into contact.

Oil traveled down the filament, slowly enough to gather dust along the way. At its lower end it touched a rough iron scale resting against the limestone. The scale drank poorly, but spread what it received across a broad, pitted surface. By the time the oil reached the floor, it was a film rather than a pool.

An adult tested the margin and began to feed.

Later a newly emerged animal crossed it. Its feet darkened. It paused, lifted one, then another, and continued.

There would still be accidents. There were other leaks, other beads, and surfaces the power had not yet learned. It attended to those in turn. Where a safe alteration was possible, it made one. Where it did not understand the consequences, it watched longer.

Under the stair, the old landing place slowly lost its greasy shine.

***

The first human change came three weeks later.

A maintenance worker knelt beside the staircase to retrieve a washer. He had a tube of sealing compound in his pocket. The cracked ribbon along the entrance caught his eye; he pulled the tube out.

Nothing commanded him.

But when he lifted his torch, its beam fell through the seam instead of against the wall. He saw movement on the limestone. He lowered his head.

The pale animals retreated. The beetles did not retreat as far. Behind them, beneath a flake of stone, something small was pulling itself free of its old covering.

The worker remained kneeling until his leg hurt.

He had sealed a dozen gaps that morning. This one he left open.

Later he returned with a small mirror and a better light. He did not turn the flake over. He could see enough along its edge to know that turning it would do damage.

On the inspection sheet he wrote:

*Occupied rock cavity beneath stairs. Do not fill.*

It was not a magnificent sentence. It did not need to be.

The power watched him underline it.

***

The hardest work concerned the factory’s future.

The lubricant would eventually be replaced. Someone would find a cleaner compound, or a cheaper one, or one that lasted longer in the heat. The engines might stop altogether. A place could survive a thousand small disturbances and then be lost to a decision made in a room whose windows faced away from it.

The power did not keep the machinery running for the animals.

Instead, it began to loosen their dependence on it.

In a hollow beside the rear wall, among the organisms already living on old residues, there were some that could use food other than oil. They had little opportunity. The chamber was dry; nourishment arrived chiefly from one direction and in one form.

Through a crack behind the grille, the power let in a trace of what the outside air carried: pollen, fragments of dead leaves, the minute organic dust that had previously caught on a ledge farther up the wall. It admitted no flood of rain, no growth that would lift the nursery flake. The change was small and gradual.

For many days it seemed to accomplish nothing.

Then the surface of the rear drift changed. Threads too fine for an unaided eye joined one particle to another. A mite fed there. The pale animals followed, bringing their gut companions with them.

The power watched the young especially.

Some failed to thrive on the new food. It adjusted the mixture, preserving the old supply while the new one became usable. It worked across generations, not demanding that a single small body bear the cost of the transition.

The amber chambers remained amber. The nursery remained dirty.

But when a joint upstairs was repaired and the yellow stain stopped widening, the chamber did not go hungry.

***

The hunters still waited beside the quartz.

The power had not overlooked them. It followed their hunger as carefully as it followed the struggles of a young animal caught in oil. It could not help one by simply forgetting the other.

So this work took longer.

It found ways to leave edible matter where a hunter could encounter it, fresh enough to be accepted, sufficient to let a passing body pass. Some took it. Others required movements, scents, conditions the power had yet to understand. It learned.

There was no announcement that the chamber had been perfected. Each morning brought something else into view.

Beneath the nursery flake, an egg failed to open. Near the stud, dust obstructed a passage. A beetle lay on its back with its legs reaching for a surface just beyond them.

The power eased the dust aside.

It set a grain of limestone within reach of the beetle.

And when the six bars of noon lay across the floor, it was still there: attending to a world in which very small differences could be the whole difference, and making them.
