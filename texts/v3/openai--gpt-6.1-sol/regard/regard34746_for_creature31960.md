The power first notices the foot that does not open.

Seven feet are attached to the mountain. The eighth is held beneath the body, folded so tightly that its membrane has become a pale seam. It is an old injury. The hard organ inside it is intact, but the tissue around it has healed unevenly, and spreading the foot pulls at a scar that runs well up the limb.

The stay has adapted. It rests lower on the saddle than another animal of its size would rest. One of its other limbs reaches across the damaged one to touch a shelf below. When the current strengthens, that limb takes most of the strain.

For thirty-one years, this has been sufficient.

The power does not take sufficiency for ease. Neither does it assume that the animal wants a different body.

It attends for a long time.

There is much to distinguish. The stay draws a foot inward when a hunter passes below it. It draws the same foot inward when a bearing in the nearest pumping house begins to wear. The movements look alike, but the changes within the body are not alike. One is a brief redistribution of attention. The other persists through feeding, through rest, through the slow circulation of material along the damaged limb.

The bearing is eleven kilometers away.

The power follows the vibration back through the rock.

Inside the pumping house, a ring of metal has begun to shed particles too small for the filters to catch. A crew will discover the damage in six months. They will stop the pump, replace the bearing, and restart it. During the stoppage, several thousand animals will change their feeding positions. Most will recover. Some will not.

The power could hold the bearing together.

Before doing so, it follows the pipes downward.

They descend through basalt into water that has not seen daylight since the sea first covered the range. There are dissolved substances here that would accumulate without circulation. There are living things along the bore walls, and others on the buried plains, and still others so small that the distinction between a body and its surroundings is difficult to make.

The reason for the pumps has been forgotten by the crews. It has not ceased to exist.

The power leaves them turning.

It keeps the failing bearing round until the next scheduled overhaul. It does not make it indestructible. On the inspection gauge, the wear becomes unusually easy to see.

An engineer checks the reading twice, then orders a replacement.

Nothing is announced.

On the saddle, the stay eases its extended limb by a fraction.

The great sound continues.

The power does not bring silence to the mountain.

Silence would be an event larger than an earthquake. It would remove the steady references by which the animals distinguish a moving body from a stationary cliff. It would change where food collects. It might be pleasant to the people in their suspended bunks, but that is not enough reason to impose it on everything else.

Instead, the power listens to what the roar is doing to particular bodies.

There are frequencies that pass through the stay without harm. There are others it uses constantly. And there is a narrow band, generated by the worn bearing, that tightens the scar around the closed foot.

That band has already diminished.

The remaining injury is older than the machine fault.

For this, the power approaches through the stone.

Beneath the folded foot, on the underside of the saddle, a small patch of basalt becomes slightly more yielding. Not soft enough to break. Not warm enough to alter the animals in the neighboring crack. It gives under pressure by less than the thickness of a shell.

The stay does not notice immediately.

Its feeding ribbon is far down a fissure. White material moves inward through the body. A transparent swimmer passes above, and three eyes along the rim open toward the change in light.

Hours later, the damaged limb uncurls.

It touches the altered patch with the outside of its foot.

The rock yields.

The limb withdraws.

The power waits.

By the following day, the foot is resting there again. Its membrane remains closed, but the limb no longer has to be held beneath the body. The stay can put some of its weight down without stretching the scar.

For a week, that is all that happens.

Then the animal shifts two other attachments. Its body rises a little from the saddle. The feeding ribbon enters a second fissure it has not been able to reach for years.

The power makes no further change.

*

Repair begins only when the foot starts to open of its own accord.

The power assists what is already happening. Material arrives through the limb’s narrow channels. New tissue forms at the edge of the old scar. It is the stay’s tissue, made from what the stay has eaten. The power helps it settle without the distortion that has made each previous attempt fail.

This takes months.

There are days when the foot closes and remains closed. During those days the power does nothing to it. A hunter settles on the cliff below. The stay moves its body around the far side of the saddle and keeps the injured limb tucked away for twelve days.

The yielding patch remains where it was.

When the animal returns, it uses the patch before putting down its feeding ribbon.

By then, the power has attended to the saddle’s other inhabitants. It has learned how far the stay feeds into each crack, which animals escape the hooks, which colonies recover quickly, which require years. Its first thought of filling the nearby fissures with food would have crowded out much of what already lived there.

It leaves the food to grow as it has grown.

The stay still has to feed. It still withdraws when threatened. The great distances remain great, and crossing them still requires nearly a day without the reassurance of stone.

But one foot hurts less.

That matters across a life measured partly in the time between movements.

*

At the station, a juvenile fastens itself to the inside of a removable cover.

The power notices this too.

It could carry the animal elsewhere, but there are good feeding places behind the housing, and the juvenile has chosen one. Moving it to a place that looks safer would mean choosing on the basis of less knowledge than the animal already possesses.

So the power attends to the people.

A mechanic loosening the cover feels a slight resistance before the last bolt comes free. She stops and shines a lamp through the gap.

There is the pale thread.

She calls for a stand.

The cover is set down beside the casing, at the same height and angle. Through the rest of the shift, the animal remains stretched between the two. By morning it has transferred its final foot.

The mechanic adds a note to the work procedure. There were older notes, but they were kept in a different manual, and not everyone had seen them.

Now the instruction is on the first page.

Over the following years, the power makes itself known mostly in opportunities to notice: a tremor that can be felt before a seal fails; a clear view of an animal behind a grille; enough time to set a tool down and look again. It does not choose what the crews do with every discovery.

Often they do well.

Sometimes they need to be shown twice.

*

The old stay eventually leaves the saddle.

There is no ceremony. The feeding ribbon folds away. Six feet release in succession. The seventh holds while the body takes the current.

The eighth—the repaired foot—is the last to open.

For a few moments the animal rests its sensory organ against the yielding stone. It registers the pumps, the returning sound, the small movements in the neighboring cracks.

Then it lifts.

The power does not guide the crossing.

Halfway over the valley, the stay opens one foot to correct its course. It is the foot that had been closed for thirty-one years. Its membrane spreads into a broad, thin oval, catches the water, and folds again.

Near the far ridge, that limb reaches ahead of the others.

The animal finds stone.

By the time its last foot has settled, its feeding ribbon is already unfolding.

The power remains attentive to both ridges. On the abandoned saddle, the yielding patch slowly hardens. It would be a poor foothold for some other animal, and the stay no longer needs it there.

Nothing fills the old footprint. At its center, the island of mineral glaze remains silver.

Beyond the valley, on the other mountain, a new oval begins to clear.
