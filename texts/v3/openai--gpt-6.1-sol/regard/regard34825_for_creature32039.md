The power first makes itself small enough to wait in the passage.

It has no need of a body, but takes one because a body can be approached or avoided. It chooses the shape of a loose scale, pale on one side, dark on the other, and settles near the wax mark.

For several seasons it does nothing that the burrower can distinguish from weather.

It feels the warmth of the abandoned hollow. It learns the difference between the tower pulses that pass through the floor and the small, irregular pressures of an animal dreaming. It watches the resident test each wall before sleeping, though the walls have scarcely changed. It learns which foot is held clear of the ground after a long journey.

The burrower sometimes pushes the scale aside.

The power lets itself be pushed.

***

There are things it could give at once.

It could fill the hollow with water. It could still the towers and make a silence large enough to hear a breath. It could soften the walls, warm every passage, keep the green fronds alive forever above the old animal’s grave.

But water would undo the chemistry that holds these bodies together. Silence would remove the pulses by which the resident knows where it is. Soft walls would close differently. Constant warmth would erase the gradients it follows home.

The power leaves those gifts unmade.

Instead it attends to the foot.

One contact pad has a sliver of conducting fiber buried beneath its hard rim. The wound is sealed over. Every time the animal braces against a narrowing passage, the fiber bends against living tissue.

During sleep, the power eases it outward by less than the width of a mite.

It takes eleven nights.

The burrower wakes one morning and presses all its feet to the floor. It stays that way for a little while, receiving the country.

Then it goes to feed.

***

The plateau is harder to understand.

There is no single place where its experience gathers. A wound at the rim changes growth far inland. A tower slowed by frost shifts pressure through chambers that have never admitted daylight. Some responses take moments. Others take the lifetime of an animal.

The power follows them without hurrying.

It finds that the plant already distinguishes many kinds of contact. A grazer’s rasp brings one response, a sleeping burrower’s weight another. The scrape of a dead vane calls forth roots. Repeated movement in a useful passage encourages the walls to round themselves and remain apart.

But a frightened animal pushing against a closing seam can resemble damage that must be sealed.

The harder it struggles, the faster the fibers gather.

Here the power stays for a long time.

***

The resident is far from its hollow when the great chamber opens.

For two days the deep pulses have been changing. It has moved upward, as others have, and waits beside an old scar with its limbs spread. Small crawlers cluster under the rear of its shell.

The opening comes. Air pulls past. The scar flexes beneath its feet.

When the pressure falls again, the burrower begins its descent.

At the third junction, it finds a passage it knows, warmer than the others. It tests the entrance, advances, and presses its jaw to the wall.

Behind it, a membrane folds.

Ahead, the warm band goes cold.

It turns, but there is no room to turn completely. It braces six limbs, then eight. Clear secretion forms along the walls and hardens against its shields.

The power could lift the continent.

Instead it holds a space beneath the animal’s jaw, just large enough for the sensory combs to remain free.

The burrower sends three short presses into the wall.

There is no answer.

It sends them again.

The power carries the pattern into the tissue that is closing around it. It does not carry the force of the struggling limbs. It carries the intervals: press, release, press, release, press.

The wall hesitates.

That hesitation belongs to the plant. In other places, rhythmic contact accompanies animals maintaining useful channels. Growth there slows, divides, forms rounded edges.

The power brings those places into relation with this one.

A fold loosens. One limb comes free.

The burrower backs out slowly, stopping whenever the secretion catches. The small creatures beneath its shell emerge with it, though not all of them.

At the junction it rests for a long time.

The power remains in the membrane until the passage has settled into a shape that will stay open.

***

It does not make every closure harmless.

Some chambers must contract. Some outlets must open even when creatures are near them. The plant has its own necessities, and the power has learned enough to take them seriously.

But there is room for another response before the final sealing.

In a few places at first, then along the conducting bands, living walls begin to hold their tension for a little longer when they receive the brief, repeated pattern. Pressure escapes through a narrow slit. A trapped animal gains time to withdraw.

Sometimes no animal withdraws. Sometimes the passage closes anyway.

The change spreads unevenly. Burrowers encounter it, survive it, leave marks near it. Young ones follow adults through seams that would once have shut too quickly.

The power watches for costs. Where the delay strains a conduit, it helps the plant grow a supporting rib. Where a slit drains too much warmth, it narrows the slit.

Above, the crowns continue turning.

***

The resident returns to its hollow.

It touches the wax mark, renews it, and settles into the depression left by the larger body. The pale scale lies near the entrance.

This time the animal draws it inward and wedges it beneath one edge of its shell. There is a slight unevenness there, a place where the sleeping cushion has grown too high.

The scale makes the fit better.

The power stays.

Years pass. The resident enlarges the chamber. Living patches gather on its armor. Its marks overlap the old marks until the entrance carries a thick, polished stain.

When it begins to rise less often, the power attends closely.

It keeps the front of the hollow clear enough for the animal to leave. It loosens new fibers that would fasten the shell to the wall, but leaves the soft contacts through which warmth arrives. The burrower still makes journeys. Each is shorter than the last.

Eventually, it tests the entrance and turns back.

Nothing bars its way.

The power follows it to the resting place.

There are visitors in those last seasons: a narrow surface animal, two juveniles, an old traveler that announces itself before entering. They touch the floor, feed along the wall, shelter beside the resident. The power preserves no moment. It lets each arrive and pass.

On the last night, the burrower’s jaw rests against warm tissue. Its feet still receive the tower pulses. The pale scale supports the edge of its shell.

Its limbs relax one after another.

Only then does the power cease loosening the fibers.

***

Above the chamber, green breaks through the pale crust.

The flying creatures find it. Mites gather beneath the fronds. Far below, the wall grows slowly around the shell, and a smaller animal enters the passage.

It tests the broad depression.

It sleeps there.

Near the entrance it leaves a fresh wax mark, low on the wall, beneath the layered marks of those who came before.

The power has gone back into the country. There are other feet to examine, other closing passages, responses that take centuries to understand.

But in this hollow it leaves the scale.

The new resident uses it to brace a crack.
