The power arrives without making a dry place for itself.

Water holds around its feet. A crab beneath a raised plate keeps its comb extended. Along the nearest conduit, the snails continue into the narrow stripes left by other snails.

For a long time, there is no way to distinguish its arrival from another interval in the life of the plain.

It does not begin with the gauge.

The needle is steady, and the amber lamps are steady, but these report only what they have been built to report. The power watches the animal holding one foot above the water. It watches the black creature working beneath the sole, the minute adjustments of the lifted leg, the moment when the larger animal wants to put its foot down.

The smaller creature finishes before that moment becomes painful.

This happens often. Not always.

The power attends to the exceptions.

It learns the difference between a fish waiting for a warm place and a fish unable to reach one. Between a wing folded in sleep and a wing held closed because opening hurts. Between the stillness of a creature content inside its drainage slot and the stillness of one for which the slot has become too narrow.

None of these differences is visible on the gauge.

There are few such difficulties. The place is extraordinarily well made. Its provisions extend into scales of need that most makers would never have noticed. But the inhabitants are not identical to their provisions, and they keep becoming slightly different from what the provisions expect.

A crab grows.

A sleeping animal chooses not to wake when the movement of wings reaches it.

One of the long bodies in the deep shafts begins to favor a particular turn, though the shorter route is equally warm.

The power spends enough time here to learn which irregularities need help and which are simply lives.

Then, beneath a plate far from any observation platform, it changes the shape of one drainage slot.

Only a little. Less than the width of the crab’s smallest claw.

The crab comes out backward, tests the lip with its wedge, and goes in again. Later, its companion joins it. There is room for both to turn.

No lamp changes color.

***

The power follows the disappearances.

It does not assume that an absent snail has died. Some travel farther than their temporary trails suggest. Some descend through couplings into a second country on the undersides of the pipes. Some remain in one sheltered place for so long that the rhythm of an observer’s visits cannot catch them awake.

Others do die.

The power stays with those.

It learns how sensation leaves the dense foot, how the pads loosen, how the last awareness sometimes remains in a body whose other work has finished. Where that lingering is peaceful, it leaves it peaceful. Where it becomes distress, it eases the distress.

It does not take the shell away from the smaller animals that will shelter beneath it. It does not prevent the body from returning what other bodies need.

Nor does it forbid every risk. The creatures in the drops continue to fall, and the fish continue to turn upward. The power learns what happens to the pale bodies inside each kind of bead, rather than deciding from the sight of a closing mouth.

There is much to learn.

Benevolence gives it a reason to remain. Wisdom keeps it from hurrying.

***

When it comes to you, you are sitting beside a basin.

You have begun to recognize the ivory animal with the curved ridge. It is farther away today. Another stands where it stood before, and you have discovered that you mind this, though you cannot quite say why.

The power sits at the edge of the plate.

Its weight sends a shallow movement through the water. A fish lifts, lets the movement pass beneath it, and settles.

You know what has come, without knowing its name.

It does not ask whether the place is beautiful. You could answer that too quickly.

It asks whether there is anything you have been unable to do here.

You look across the basin. Beyond it, a walkway passes between exchanger banks and disappears into warm vapor.

“I haven’t found the end.”

“Would you like to?”

You consider the question.

“I don’t know whether I want an end. I want to know that walking could matter.”

The power waits while you find the rest.

“Not to everything,” you say. “Just somewhere.”

It does not make the plain smaller. It does not place a mountain on the horizon or extinguish the ceiling to show you unfamiliar stars. Those things might be welcome, but it has not yet learned that they would be welcome to you, or to the countless eyes accustomed to this light.

Instead, it asks where you have wanted to go.

You tell it about the curved flank passing across the lowest light in the inspection shaft.

The next day, you find a seat beside that shaft.

It is not a throne or a vehicle. It has a low rail, a surface warm enough for resting hands, and a control that stops its descent when you release it. Beneath it, lights come on slowly, at wavelengths the inhabitants below do not mind.

You can go down.

You can also remain at the top.

For several days you remain at the top.

The power does not interpret this as ingratitude.

***

Some of what it does is smaller still.

A handrail no longer heals over the shallow notch you made to recognize a turning.

The repair system keeps the metal sound beneath it. The mark itself remains.

At the next junction, you make another.

Elsewhere, a route number acquires a second line of information: not merely where the walkway leads, but what can be found there. A district of very young snails. A place where several kinds of animals sleep together. An opening from which you can watch the slow bodies below without descending.

The signs do not promise that the creatures will be present.

You learn to arrive and find them gone.

You learn other ways of recognizing a place.

***

The power leaves most things alone.

The clouds keep their level undersides. The ivory animals drink through their dark bristles. Heat passes into wings and out through fine, wet holes.

It makes no requirement that the inhabitants become adventurous. A fish may occupy the same warm patch for the rest of its life, if that patch continues to satisfy it. The crabs may tend one another long after there is anything left to clean.

But when a winged animal begins, repeatedly, to climb beyond the last exchanger fin, the power notices.

Beyond that fin is an open interval crossed by a hot current. The animal reaches toward it, withdraws, and returns another day.

The power gives the current a cooler margin.

Nothing pushes the animal across.

One morning it unfolds both wings. Water brightens in their perforations. It holds there for a while, feeling the air.

Then it goes.

***

You eventually descend into the shaft.

Far below the plain, you stop beside an opening large enough to admit a house. A pale body rests beyond it. Its short limbs are folded against a surface ribbed with conduits.

For a while you see no eye.

Then a fold near the curved flank opens, and something looks back at you.

You sit there until your hands have relaxed on the rail.

Above, water continues to bead beneath the observation platform. The fish are choosing which drops to take. A crab turns easily in its widened slot.

The power is still present, though not always beside you. There are places where it is listening, places where it is helping, long expanses where it has found nothing it ought to change.

Down here, the pale creature shifts.

It extends one short limb into the passage and rests it near your seat.

You leave your hand where it is.

Neither of you has to come closer yet.
