The power does not begin by stopping the pumps.

It attends through one full inspection, then through a night, then through the change in the ground when a weak sun reaches the eastern bank. It follows the water down to the oldest tissue and outward to the places where a thread has recently failed. It distinguishes the creature from the moss without separating them. At some points, separation would mean tearing both.

It finds no central place to address.

There is no hidden head beneath the shed, no thought moving among the flattened sacs. There are differences of pressure, degrees of wetness, small exchanges across membranes. There is a living continuity that can lose a kilometer of itself and persist in a fold beneath a root.

The power does not gather that continuity into a person. It does not give the creature a voice with which to ask for things it has never needed to name.

Instead, it learns what happens when the water falls.

At the northern end, the strand against the buried root has enough moisture to thicken but not enough to go farther. A short distance beyond it lies tissue that still lives, separated during the cutting of a channel two winters earlier. Between the two is peat that freezes readily because it has become too dry. In summer it admits air. Each season closes the gap in a different way.

The power stays there for a while.

Then it attends to the person who checks the gauges.

He comes down the ramp with one shoulder raised against the wind. Before opening the shed, he looks at the outlet. He knows the sound of the engines well enough to notice an alteration while still on the road. His hearing has suffered from them. His wages have not been large.

He has been told that another section will be cut when the weather improves.

The power follows this prospect beyond the basin: into an office, through a set of figures, into the homes of people who depend on work continuing. It follows the drainage channel too. The water does not cease to matter when it leaves the peat. Farther down, it passes a culvert and a field and the foundations of houses.

There is room to change the basin. There is not room to act as though the basin were alone.

During the next inspection, nothing breaks.

The service door does not jam. An engine does not catch fire. The man is not made frightened of a place he has walked for years.

But he notices the gray pad beneath the step.

He has seen it before without quite seeing it. This time, after his weight comes off the metal, he watches the pad slowly fill. He crouches. Through his glove, he touches its edge.

The tissue withdraws.

He leaves it where it is.

Later, when he mentions it, someone listens. The first photograph is poor. The second shows the fine ridges. A visitor comes with containers and then, after looking at the trench wall, puts most of them away.

The power helps attention hold. It does not make the evidence larger than it is.

No one immediately understands the creature. For a time, it is given several incompatible names. There is an argument about whether the material found in distant compartments belongs to one organism or many. The argument continues, but work on the proposed new cut pauses while people examine the ground.

That pause matters before any name does.

Money is found for a different kind of work. The man who checks the gauges learns to measure water levels in places the old inspection route never reached. Others help shore the unsafe crossing, remove fuel that could leak, and take away machinery whose weight is no longer serving anything.

The pumps remain in use.

At first, only a small compartment is allowed to become wetter. The rise is watched through a thaw and a freeze. Some tissue dies along a margin where it had grown accustomed to air. Other tissue opens in seams that have been closed for years.

The people learn to wait between changes.

The power could keep every exposed fringe alive. It does not. A fringe lives by occupying a particular place, and places change. What it prevents is the repeated destruction of whole reaches before there has been time to understand what is there.

It makes no promise that the creature will remain as it is tonight.

By the following winter, one engine runs less often. The other has been enclosed so that the shed no longer casts quite so much noise across the ground. There are intervals in which the bird can be heard.

Within the peat, the difference is more complicated.

Some sacs loosen around the pipe supports. Strands that once carried a strong mechanical rhythm receive little but the movement of water. For several days, a patch beneath the abandoned platform draws inward. Then it extends along the damp grain of a board.

The creature does not miss the engines in the manner of a person missing a familiar sound. Nor does their absence mean nothing to it. Its growth has been shaped by them. Gradually, it grows otherwise.

Farther north, water remains in the seam beyond the bent root.

The advancing strand rests there through the cold. During the thaw it puts out a pale extension, finer than a root hair. The extension crosses a cavity and meets the separated tissue on the other side.

For a while the contact is slight. Fluid moves through it, stops, moves again.

By late summer, the join has thickened.

There is no announcement beneath the moss. Above it, the basin still looks broad and low and mostly empty. The banks retain their cuts. A truck roof passes along the rim.

At the service step, the gray pad fills after the inspector has gone. He no longer puts his full weight on that corner.

The power attends to this too.
