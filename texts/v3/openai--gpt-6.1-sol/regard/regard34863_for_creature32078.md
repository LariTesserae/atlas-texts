The power does not arrive from above.

For a long while it attends to the country from the level at which the country lives: beneath the fans, beside the damp seams, in the narrow interval between a resting belly and the stone.

It learns the difference between a basin that is asleep and one that has failed.

There are places where nothing turns because nothing is ready to turn. Frost holds the leaves together. Animals settle deep into the carpet, their cords laid loosely through the roots. To warm such a place would wake mouths before there was food for them.

Elsewhere, the leaves are ready. Water has gathered in the buried chambers. The roots carry their small currents into a colony whose inner surfaces no longer move.

The silence there is different.

The power listens until it can tell these silences apart.

It takes longer to understand the pools.

At one, it follows the pale shape beneath the water and discovers that it is not simply an image of the animal approaching. Nor is it waiting to harm the animal. The power withdraws its attention a little.

Afterward, it watches from the margin.

There are things here that need room even from kindness.

—

The animal it first follows is old, but not among the oldest.

A ribbed fan grows behind its head. Along one flank is a patch of pale moss that has survived three departures from the basin where it first took root. Beneath the other flank, a young animal shelters through the cold interval.

The young one is not its own.

When the basin begins to wake, the adult gathers its cords and rises. The youngster slips out, makes a quick passage beneath two overlapping leaves, and returns to the moving shelter.

They go toward a ridge where the stone carries a faint, irregular tremor.

The power follows them.

At a break in the vegetation, the adult waits. Its front pads lie open on the rock. Beneath its chest, the youngster searches for a passage that is not exposed to the wind.

There used to be one.

A winter movement of the stone has widened the break. The leaves on either side have continued growing toward each other, but their edges remain separated by a space scarcely wider than the adult’s smallest foot.

The adult could cross.

It lowers itself instead.

For most of the night, neither animal moves farther.

The power could carry them. It could close the stone, bring the leaves together, or place warmth directly inside the young body.

It studies the break.

Below the exposed surface, a wet seam ends against a flake of mineral crust. Beyond the crust lies a hollow under the farther shelf. Roots have entered that hollow from both sides.

The power loosens the flake.

Water passes through.

Nothing happens quickly. The seam darkens. A root takes up moisture. Two tightly folded fronds begin, by their own means, to open.

Near the end of the night, one pale underside reaches the other bank.

The youngster finds it before the adult does.

Its weight bends the frond into the hollow, out of the wind. It crosses there, underneath the stone, and emerges among the leaves beyond.

The adult remains still until the minute clicks reach it.

Then its pads gather. Its body lifts.

On the farther side, it settles for a while, and the young one returns beneath its flank.

—

Past the ridge is a colony that should have begun turning several nights earlier.

The old animal has heard it trying.

A narrow trembling travels through the metallic bed, stops, and begins again. Other animals have answered from distant shelters. None has come close.

The colony occupies a shallow basin crowded with folded leaves. Beneath its center, one moving surface bears against another at a place where a mineral grain has worked loose. Each attempt to turn drives the grain more firmly into the soft housing.

The roots have already begun growing around the obstruction.

Given time, they may repair it. But the water in the outer chambers is freezing, and the animals waiting along the basin’s margins have little stored warmth left.

The power touches the grain.

It does not remove it. Small roots are fastened to its underside; their tips are living in the moisture gathered there.

Instead, it turns the grain by less than the thickness of a leaf.

The pressure changes.

Far below the folded fans, something passes its first difficult fraction of a circle.

The old animal flattens itself against the ground.

The power holds still.

Another fraction follows. Then another.

By morning, frost has begun releasing one margin of the colony. The opposite margin remains white. Warmth moves through the basin in the old uneven fashion, leaving cold shelters among the waking plants.

A predator unfolds beside an exposed stone.

The power does not strike it away.

A grazer, approaching that stone, pauses at a root that has just warmed. It lowers its mouth elsewhere. Later, another grazer will cross without pausing.

The basin has many hungers. The power stays long enough to learn more of them.

—

The old animal settles at the colony’s edge.

Its cords pass into the ground. One follows the repaired vibration inward; others spread beneath the sleeping plants. The youngster presses against its belly, then leaves to feed.

When the adult next speaks through the stone, the power feels the utterance.

It does not mistake it for thanks.

There is the direction of the warming. There is something about the ridge they crossed. There is an interval the power cannot understand.

Far away, another animal answers.

The old one changes the position of a foot and listens.

—

In the seasons that follow, the power does little in some basins and a little more in others.

It releases no constant summer into the plateau. It gives the country no tall trees, no rivers, no bright sky under which all its small lights would disappear.

It opens a passage for water where a fallen stone has sealed the last wet chamber of a hollow. It lets that stone remain, with the frost-scrapers beneath it.

Once, it steadies a loosening shelf while an animal withdraws its cords. After the last pale tip has vanished into the belly folds, the shelf falls into the depth. The sound does not return.

Sometimes the power finds an animal too late.

It stays beside one whose mouth has ceased moving, while the plants on its back continue to draw moisture from the fur. A young animal approaches, clicks against the stone, and waits. Eventually it goes toward another warmth.

The power follows that small journey without making the body behind it rise.

In time, roots enter the empty folds. The sheltered garden spreads down into the surrounding ground.

—

The animal with the ribbed fan lives through several more warmings.

On its next journey over the ridge, it pauses at the narrow break. The frond below the stone has thickened. Other plants have entered the damp hollow. Something small moves through it, showing blue for a moment and then no light at all.

The animal lays one silver patch against the bank.

The power is there, listening.

After a while, a thin cord extends from beneath the fur. Its flattened end comes to rest against the wet seam.

The power does not pull it farther.

They remain like that until the basin beyond the ridge begins to turn.
