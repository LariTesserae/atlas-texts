The power did not arrive through the notch.

If it had, the inhabitant would have felt it first as a change in the rising air, and withdrawn before there was anything to see. Nor did it lean over the walls. A presence large enough to cast that shadow would have taken away the last warmth of the day.

It attended instead to the pad laid against the shelter floor.

Through that small contact came the impacts from the falling flake: the first sharp blow, the second, the scattering of fragments. Then the long quiet in which the inhabitant waited to learn whether anything more would fall.

The power waited with it.

It did not mistake stillness for peace.

For several days, nothing in the hollow changed.

The basin froze and opened. The mineral combs inclined toward dampness. At noon the inhabitant folded itself into the narrow crack, and late in the day it came out with a grain of white powder caught between two plates. It worked the grain free against a rounded edge of stone.

The power attended to that also.

There were many things it could have done badly with kindness. It could have filled the hollow with warm air, and dried the dark seams beneath the plates. It could have brought soil and growing things, obscuring the surfaces on which the inhabitant found its way. It could have made the water plentiful without learning what the water carried. It could have furnished the shelter for the people who had built it, rather than for the body that now slept there.

It could have made another creature and set it beside the first.

It did none of these things.

Under the shelter’s western roof slab, a fracture had been widening for many winters. The weight above it was held by a narrow bridge of stone. When that bridge failed, the slab would descend into the sleeping hollow.

The power held it through one more night.

The next afternoon, while the inhabitant was among the mineral combs, the bridge gave way. The slab settled outward instead of inward. Its edge came to rest against a standing rib, with a small fall of dust and a sound that did not travel beyond the basin.

The roof remained low. The entrance remained familiar. Beneath one side there was now a little more room.

When the inhabitant returned, it stopped.

One pad spread against the floor. Another touched the displaced edge. It waited, then went around the shelter and examined the new bearing from outside. Before entering, it fetched three thin fragments and pressed them into a gap.

The power let the gap remain until that work was finished.

That night, the inhabitant slept in its accustomed place.

The next thing the power learned was that thirst did not always bring it to water.

On certain evenings it approached the basin, touched the frozen lip, and withdrew. There was water beneath the ice, but reaching it would require placing its weight over the clear sheet. Once it tried. A crack ran outward from a forelimb, and it backed away so quickly that two of its plates struck together.

Later, at the carved vessel, it lowered its head into the little water left below the channel. It remained there a long time.

The power did not mend the vessel. Its broken rim had become part of a route the inhabitant knew.

Instead, where the basin overflow entered the stone, a few grains loosened. Water found a second course beneath the overhang. It emerged into a shallow hollow, no broader than a divided pad, sheltered from the clear sky.

It was not enough water to alter the basin. It did not stay liquid through every cold night. But it froze later, and the inhabitant could reach it from firm ground.

For three evenings the new wet place went unused.

On the fourth, the inhabitant found it by following the darkened seam. It touched the water, withdrew, and touched it again.

Afterward it returned to the shelter without visiting the vessel.

The power learned to recognize that shorter journey.

Beyond the notch lay slopes, ridges, other hollows. Some were dry. Some held water too bitter for this body. Some had been broken open by frost and offered no deep refuge from the midday air.

The power searched them without taking anything from the hollow.

Far away, beneath a shelf of darker stone, it found cast-off plates with shallow channels on their inner faces. They were old, but not empty of meaning. Farther still, there were fresh impressions in powdered rock.

It did not bring their maker across the distance.

First it learned the intervening ground: where a body could shelter, where moisture persisted, where the route narrowed beyond passage. It found an old fall that had sealed a shaded cleft. There, over several seasons, thaw and settling opened a way beneath the stones. Water already moving through the ridge began to leave small wet places along it.

Nothing was summoned. Neither end of the route was closed.

One evening, rising air brought a trace that the power could distinguish but could not interpret for the inhabitant.

At the chipped foothold, the inhabitant extended its head. Its foremost plates lifted slightly. It stayed there until the cloud thinned.

The following day it returned.

The power did not call this loneliness, or hope. It watched what the inhabitant did.

There were still hard nights. A plate split along an old injury. The basin froze to its pebbled floor. Loose stones sometimes changed the passages, and the inhabitant had to learn them again.

But the roof held. There was another place to drink. Beyond the notch, a way lay open.

And when the next flake fell high on the wall, the inhabitant laid its pad against the shelter floor, felt the blows diminish, and drew the limb back.

The power remained attentive after it slept.
