The first thing the power does is wait.

Not because the hands in the water can wait. They cannot. The mineral threads are already whitening around the resident’s fingers, and something beneath them is beating its folded wings against a space that grows smaller with every beat.

But the power waits long enough to learn which movement is rescue.

Then the stone softens beneath the resident’s left hand.

Only there. Only to the depth of the trapped animal.

The resident pulls, expecting resistance, and nearly falls backward when the rim opens. A shrimp springs against its chest, drops into the pool, and vanishes under a ledge.

For a moment the resident holds its hands above the water.

The posts continue singing. Across the basin, a ridge rises another finger’s width. Nothing else has stopped.

It puts one hand down again.

Under the palm, the stone remains soft.

***

The power has noticed a great many things.

The white mouths need their openings to remain above water, but not every opening is occupied. The gray mat needs the stone to lift it, though not so quickly that its roots dry. The birds need the seams to open. The animals in the seams need time to leave.

The resident needs food, including some of those animals.

There is no arrangement in which everything here can be kept exactly as it is.

For a while the power listens to the machinery beneath the door. It follows the knocking down through warm chambers, through joints whose surfaces have never seen the sky. The work is larger than the basin. Loads pass through it. Heat is carried elsewhere. Some of the immense shapes moving behind the cloud depend upon it, although from the shelter nothing of that dependence can be seen.

Stopping the work would not be a small kindness.

So the power does not stop it.

It finds, instead, that the machinery can wait between one instruction and the next.

There are pauses already. They are very short.

It makes one longer.

***

In the morning, the lost tool lies beside the cup.

Its white handle is bent. Fine black scratches run along the shaft where the stone held it. It has not been replaced with something splendid or unfamiliar.

The resident touches the handle.

Then it touches the place in the ground where the last white tip disappeared.

Then the handle again.

It takes the tool out into the basin and pushes it into a shallow pool.

Nothing happens.

It tries a second place, nearer the posts. Still nothing.

Only when the note begins does the tool answer. A faint vibration runs into the resident’s hand. The line opens as it always did, but this time there is a short space between the opening and the next upward thrust of stone.

The animals cross.

The resident pulls the tool free.

It comes easily.

For the rest of that morning, the resident carries it even while drinking.

***

The next difficulty is beneath the shelter.

During the rain, water gathers in the wall gap. The young with broad rear feet climbs above it. Two others press against the curled one, whose limbs barely move.

The adult arrives with the cup, discovers the water already there, and begins taking it out.

Its fingers are poorly shaped for this. Each handful loses most of itself before reaching the edge.

The power could lift the young into another place.

Instead, a narrow groove opens under the gap.

Water travels along it and drops onto the stone outside. The opening is too small for any of the young to fall through.

The adult touches the groove from end to end. It brings one of the metal pins and lays it across the outlet. Water catches against the pin, hesitates, then passes beneath.

The resident removes the pin.

Later, it puts a piece of cord there.

The cord stays wet. The inner wall begins to dry.

That change is left to stand.

***

The curled young one does not unfold.

The power attends to it without brightening the shelter or calling the adult away from the pools. There is a small constriction beneath its chest plates. Each attempt to breathe draws the plates inward and tightens it.

This is not the stillness of an animal resting.

The constriction loosens.

The young one’s chest opens, closes, opens again. Its rear limbs push feebly against the others. One clear plate lifts and settles.

There is no sudden growth. No promise that it will become the largest or strongest.

When the adult returns, the little creature has moved half its own length.

The resident lowers its mouth to the gap.

This time there is a sound: a thin, uneven pulse, almost below hearing. The young answer against the panel. You feel it if you rest a finger there.

The power listens from farther away.

***

For several days, help arrives only where the resident is already working.

A breathing hole holds its lip above a rising pool.

A seam stays open through one more rush of translucent bodies.

When the adult braces itself to pull the tool free, the surrounding stone gives before the white handle bends.

The resident becomes quicker. It crosses the basin with the tool lifted clear of the water, stops less often to test the ground, sleeps for longer beneath the shelter.

But it still goes out whenever the posts sing.

The power notices this too.

On the sixth evening, before the note begins, a little tongue of dark metal emerges beside the shelter’s lowest panel. It is broad enough for the resident’s divided fingers. Its surface is dull, without markings.

When pressed, it moves down.

The posts fall quiet.

The resident withdraws its hand.

The note resumes.

It presses again.

Out in the basin, a pool stops filling with mineral threads. Water continues dripping. Birds continue hunting. Beneath the door, the machine knocks at a slower rhythm.

The resident holds the tongue down for a long time.

Then it releases it and runs to the pool.

There is something beneath the cloudy surface. This time it has time to find the rim, choose where to open it, and draw the animal out without tearing its folded wings.

By morning, the resident has wedged one of its metal pins beneath the tongue.

The basin is still.

The power leaves it still.

***

It takes most of the day for the resident to remove the pin.

During that time, a few things happen that do not happen often.

The birds settle on the ridge and clean their broad brown wings. The long predator leaves the gray mat to hunt beside a different pool. The young with the broad feet comes down from the gap.

The adult catches it at first.

Later it lets it stand on the shelter floor, with one hand laid between it and the open ground.

The young presses its belly to the stone.

There is very little to hear.

It raises its head.

The adult lowers its own head beside it. For some time they remain that way, two different sizes of the same listening posture.

In the late light, the resident goes to the metal tongue and removes the pin.

The posts begin softly.

***

The power does not make the basin safe all at once.

It is still high above the cloud. The predator’s jaws still close. Rain still changes the shape of the pools, and an animal can still make a poor choice of shelter.

But the machinery learns to notice occupied hollows before filling them. Its warning note comes earlier. The pauses become long enough for a small body to cross a line.

If the resident is asleep, growth can proceed around the shelter without requiring its hands in every threatened pool.

If it presses the tongue, the work waits.

The cup remains a cup. The folded seat still fits badly.

One evening the resident opens the door and listens to the knocking below. It taps twice, pauses, taps once.

This time there is an answer.

Not a voice. Not an explanation.

Two knocks, a pause, another.

The resident stays at the threshold until the stale air has dried the plates along its brow. Then it closes the door and returns to the shelter.

It does not leave any root on the red floor.

***

After the next rain, the ground begins to lift.

The resident comes out carrying the white-handled tool. Behind it, the largest young one makes its way down from the shelter, stopping at each change in the surface.

The adult turns, waits, and lowers a hand.

The young steps over it.

At the first pool, they both kneel.

The adult places its fingers against the rim. The young places its much smaller fingers beside them.

Beneath the cloudy mineral growth, something moves.

The stone gives slowly. Water enters the opening. A flat animal slips out, runs between their hands, and disappears into a seam.

Above them, a bird opens its wings.

The resident watches the seam for another moment. Then it sits back on its broad rear feet, leaving the pool open.

The next note from the posts is still some way off.
