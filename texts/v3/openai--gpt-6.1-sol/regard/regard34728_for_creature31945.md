The attention arrives without light.

There is already light enough for what lives here. More would send the swimmers deeper into the mud and close the mouths along the exposed banks. Warmth, too, must be measured carefully. A little would release the ice from a sensory fringe. A little more would take the feeding difference out of a seam and starve the gray felt gathered there.

For a long while, nothing changes.

The newly legged creature feeds at the edge of the empty shell. Its mouth turns three times, stops, turns once in the other direction. Beneath its body, one leg has not hardened properly. When it bears weight, the joint folds sideways.

The creature compensates. It has other legs.

The attention follows the compensation down through the stone.

Below the shell is a narrow passage carrying water into the bank. Below that, a plate moves in its socket. The movement loads a vein of iron, and the vein releases enough current to feed several thousand transparent margins. Farther away, a basin animal draws its buried fans together. The pressure travels back through the passage. The shell rocks.

The small creature loses its footing.

It catches again.

Nothing about this is unusual. The attention does not mistake an ordinary event for a harmless one.

It remains beside the creature through the falling water.

***

The power could lift the animal out of the country.

There are places where nothing freezes against a living joint, where the ground stays still and food does not arrive through the labor of buried stone.

But the creature has no mouth for that food. Its body receives the plain through organs too small to see. Even its unsteady leg is growing toward a pressure that another country would not provide.

So the power learns the pressure.

It learns the intervals between feeding strokes. It learns which tremors mean a neighbor is rising and which mean that the surface beneath the neighbor is about to go. It learns the difference between a closed shell and an empty one.

Under the bank, a pale load-bearer holds open a passage with eleven legs.

One has been trapped for a long time.

The animals above it have grown heavier. Their sheltered shell layers have thickened; an old fracture in the bank has filled with bodies. Each flood draws the trapped limb a little farther from its socket.

The pale creature continues to hold the passage open.

The power attends to this also.

***

Its first alteration is smaller than a grain of sand.

A mineral lip beneath the trapped leg wears away.

Not all at once. The water carries it off over several strokes, with the same cloudy residue that any worn surface gives. The limb comes loose. The pale animal folds it against its underside and redistributes its weight.

Above it, three bodies settle.

The empty shell tilts toward the newly legged creature.

This time, when the pressure rises, the shell does not rock. Water slips beneath it through a passage kept open by a creature that has room to move.

The small animal feeds.

The power does not count this as finished.

***

Across the basin, a filter-creature has spent years dragging a ridge that should have worn through.

A metallic deposit has grown across the place where stone and living brace are joined. Each feeding stroke tightens the connection. Tissue has begun to thin along the animal’s forward channels.

The deposit is useful to a colony of grazers.

The power follows its current into the mud and finds another seam nearby, unoccupied because a crust prevents water from reaching it.

The crust opens by a hair.

Over many floods, the grazers spread. The old deposit ceases to grow. Water works beneath it; the ridge loosens.

When the filter-creature finally moves forward, its rear vents expel a long pale cloud.

It takes another stroke.

Behind it, on the abandoned ridge, small transparent bodies widen in the new current.

***

There are hurts here that cannot be untied by wearing one edge a little thinner.

The power finds them.

It stays with the swimmer whose fringe has frozen too heavily to lift. It stays with the prey folded beneath another swimmer in the arrested water. Where it can end a struggle without carrying suffering into another body, it does so. Where it cannot yet find such a way, it continues to look.

Its attention does not withdraw when a shell closes.

Nor does it require the plain to become quiet before it will remain.

There is work in the pressure, in the channels, in the old arrangements that have continued long after the conditions that formed them disappeared. Some can be changed. Others must be understood through more than one flood.

The power has time, but the creatures do not always have time.

It learns to be both patient and quick.

***

Near the white flats, a smooth body passes shallowly through the rock.

For an instant, the attention encounters something that does not fit inside the instant.

It makes no attempt to pull it out.

It follows as far as following is possible, then waits where the nearby seam will brighten.

When the first grazers gather, the current is steady. When their margins overlap, there is still enough wet surface for the lower ones to move.

***

The newly legged creature never sees what has come to the plain.

During the next rise, it climbs onto the empty shell. Its weak joint bends; another leg takes the load. Beneath it, a pale animal shifts comfortably into the space where a mineral lip used to be.

The flood passes.

Afterward, the creature lowers its mouth to the wet stone.

A neighbor presses against its back. It presses back, finding support and giving some.

Underneath them, the passage remains open.

The attention remains too.
