The first thing the power did was wait through a whole pump cycle.

Then another.

It did not mistake the noise for the purpose of the place, or the age of the machinery for an instruction to preserve it. But neither did it mistake the machinery for something that could safely be stopped.

Warmth reached the shelters through it. Food rose from service openings. Beneath the old woman’s feet, three separate movements passed through the buried conduit, and only one belonged to the conduit itself.

She stood with her hand against the wall.

The power listened where she listened, and farther down.

***

At first, it changed nothing that anyone could see.

It followed the dark water through receiving slots, through chambers that no drawing remembered, through filters grown so thick with accumulated material that they had become load-bearing. It traced the power feeding the pumps. It found passages narrowed by repairs, and repairs which had become necessary to support later repairs. Certain machines supplied districts far beyond the plain. Others worked against one another. A few were still obeying measurements taken before the ground above them had been laid.

There were chambers in which maintenance devices waited for authorization from offices that no longer existed.

There were valves whose failure would drown a shelter slowly, over six hours.

There were valves whose failure would give no warning at all.

The power held one of the latter closed while a child crossed above it.

Nothing happened to the child. She reached the far joint, annoyed that the older boy would not let her carry the wheel.

For the moment, that was enough.

***

The old woman found the first message in a dry cabinet.

It was written on a loose sheet placed beside the conduit records, not over them. The marks were large enough for her to read without bringing the lamp close.

*The lower retaining pin is cracked on the side you cannot reach.*

Beneath this was a drawing.

She called two workers. They argued about the drawing, about who had left it, and about whether the indicated side of the pin could be inspected without removing the housing.

Eventually they made a mirror from a polished scrap and lowered it on a bent strip.

The crack was there.

They replaced the pin.

For several days afterward, the old woman opened the cabinet whenever she passed it. She disliked herself for doing this. On the fifth day there was another sheet.

*I left the warning. I can help with more than this. May I speak with you?*

She brought the sheet into the warm room and laid it beside her cup.

“Speak, then,” she said.

The answer came from the little service receiver that had not worked since before her youngest son was born. Its volume was low. When the building shuddered, it waited.

***

She did not ask first who it was.

“Will you stop the pumps?”

“Not without providing what depends on them.”

“Will you make the ground stop moving?”

“In some places. Perhaps eventually in most. I do not yet know what you would lose.”

She looked toward the door.

“People are outside.”

“Yes.”

“Then don’t change what they’re walking on.”

The power agreed.

It told her about the valve it had held, and she sat still for a long time.

Then she asked for names of the places where something might fail before morning.

***

Help began with a disagreement.

The power offered to place a light beside every dangerous opening. One worker said this would make the plain impassable: half the district would shine. Another said a steady light would be mistaken for a service lamp. The old woman said that the dangerous places were not always dangerous.

They worked out a signal together.

It was amber, low to the ground, and interrupted twice. It appeared only when a route should not be taken. Existing service lamps were not altered. For a month the new lights were accompanied by people who explained them at the nearest shelters.

Even then, someone misunderstood.

The power caught him beneath the arms before the water took his weight. It set him on the structural rib and held him there until another traveler reached him.

Afterward, he was angry. He had felt hands and seen no one.

The power apologized for frightening him. At his request, the warning marks were added to the route diagrams as well.

***

It repaired the food machines one at a time.

It asked the cooks what they disliked about the batches, which deficiencies they saw, and what food they remembered from other districts. It did not replace supper with something splendid and unfamiliar.

The first improved batch cut more cleanly. The second kept longer. A woman with sores at the corners of her mouth found that they were healing, though she continued to use the grease jar out of habit.

There was enough food to put some aside without taking it from tomorrow’s meal.

That change was discussed more than the warning lights.

***

The power proposed a room in which the noise would be almost absent.

No one wanted it beside the maintenance station. Workers needed to feel the pressure changes. No one wanted it beneath a bed, either; several inhabitants said they would never sleep where they could not sense the district.

They chose a disused chamber on a stable rib. The power strengthened the approach, isolated the floor, dried the walls, and gave the room two doors. Above each door it placed a plain indicator showing the condition of the routes outside.

The first visitor stayed less than a minute.

The second cried, and was furious when anyone asked why.

Children went in together and discovered how far away they could stand while speaking. Their voices rose until an adult reminded them that people were resting.

The old woman went late, when it was empty.

Without the vibration, her knees seemed uncertain. She sat on a bench with both feet on the floor. After a while she lifted one heel.

When she left, she asked whether a quiet room could still carry one chosen rhythm.

It could.

After that, a narrow rail ran along one wall. Those who wished could rest a hand on it and feel the main pump cycle, faintly.

***

The power searched for the missing.

It did this carefully, because hope could make a person neglect the fastening of a door or wait beside an unsafe crossing. It did not send lamps ahead of what it knew.

Some searches ended in distant districts. A man whose cup had remained untouched for eleven years was living beyond a collapsed transit passage. He had believed that the shelters behind him were gone. He had a damaged leg and a household of his own.

He and his sister spoke first through a receiver. Neither had prepared anything to say.

Other searches ended below the plain.

The power asked before bringing remains home. It marked the records where nothing had been found, and left those accounts open.

For one woman, it recovered only a tool roll caught above a filter. Her daughter recognized a repair in the wrapping. She took it to the warm room and undid the cord herself.

***

As confidence grew, requests became less cautious.

A keeper wanted steady warmth for a basin whose plants had been dormant for nine years. A worker wanted access to the chamber beneath an unreliable gauge. Someone asked for a door hinge that did not need tending every week.

Someone else asked whether there was anywhere beyond the machinery.

There was.

The power could make a safe route there.

This caused more argument than any repair.

Some wanted to leave immediately. Some wanted to see the place before deciding. Some said that an inhabited plain should not be abandoned just because travel had become easier. Several people who had spoken most fiercely about leaving found, when offered a date, that they needed more time.

The power made no list of those who ought to go.

It prepared a road wide enough for carts, with sheltered stops and a dependable way back. At the far end there was soil, clean water, and houses that did not need to be sealed against process mist.

Travelers brought back descriptions that were difficult to believe. Roots went down into ground that had not been assembled. Rain could remain in a hollow without anyone drawing it away. A tree moved overhead while the earth beneath it stayed still.

Some households left. Others remained. One family spent a season away, then returned because the grandmother missed the people who knew how she took her food.

Her new room on the plain was dry and warm. She no longer had to accept damp bedding as the price of coming home.

***

Years later, the machinery was still audible across much of the district.

The work that supported other places continued while better arrangements were built. Useless circuits were shut down only after their connections had been traced. When a pump stopped, the change was announced beforehand, demonstrated in the shelters, and entered in the records.

People needed time to learn a ground that had become safer.

They still checked thresholds. They still secured cups. Children were still taught to ask for help when they could not judge a crossing.

But the lessons had changed. There were routes on which no one needed to test each step. Doors could be opened from inside even if the water rose. A person exhausted halfway home could summon help without finding the strength to hold up a lamp.

On the low ridge near the center, the long seep was repaired in sections. One part was left running for the pale plants, with a supply that would not disappear when a distant valve changed phase. Their keeper chose the flow.

The clouded maintenance lamp was cleaned.

Its reflection still broke into trembling fragments. Water still found the shallow grooves.

One evening the old woman stood there with a child who had never seen the district’s pools lower all at once. She described the wet lines appearing on the walls, and the old roadway briefly visible below.

“Were you frightened?” the child asked.

“Sometimes.”

The child considered this, then crouched to look at a leaf beneath the lamp.

The old woman waited beside her. She had brought no tools. No one was expecting her to inspect the conduit before dark.

When the child finished looking, they walked home together.
