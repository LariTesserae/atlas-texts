The power first paid attention to the woman carrying the bowl.

Not because she was more important than the animals taking her place, nor because her need was greater. It had to begin somewhere. She was moving carefully, keeping the water level despite a stiffness in one knee, and when something small ran across her foot she stopped rather than shaking it off.

The power followed her home.

Inside, she set the bowl beside a sleeping child. She poured a little into a covered vessel, a little into another, and left the rest for morning. Then she sat with her hands open on her thighs.

The cuts across her fingers had healed badly. One had caught a pale strand beneath the skin. She pressed beside it, experimentally, and winced.

The power could have removed the strand.

Instead it waited until she slept, and then removed it.

In the morning she examined the finger. She flexed it twice. There was work to do, and she went out.

***

For a long time, the power learned the high country.

It learned that the sound beneath an abandoned doorway was not one creature calling but three, their passages crossing at different depths.

It learned that a hollow could look full and already be failing. Water left behind in the lowest fold might be too bitter for the people, though the round grazers continued to feed there.

It learned that the great trees were not simply drawing from the ground. Some returned water to it through their buried parts, especially in the hours before sunrise. Other trees did not. The exchange changed with heat, injury, the state of the crown, and conditions farther below than any water keeper had reached.

Even the power, looking closely, found that there were things to learn.

It followed a broadmouth through twelve days of slow feeding. It felt the animal’s mouth find sound tissue, loosen it, work it free. It followed a stitchback until hunger narrowed the creature’s attention to a single trembling point beneath its jaw.

The power did not make the stitchback harmless.

It did not give the tissue-eaters knowledge of every place where a hunter waited.

It saw what those gifts would do.

But seeing this did not make the deaths insignificant. When a young climber lost its grip, the power knew the brief confusion of four hands closing on air. When a clutch fell, it knew each small body’s unsuccessful reach.

It did not become accustomed to them.

***

The first person who saw it awake was a water keeper from a settlement beyond the next broad valley.

He found it sitting beside an empty bowl in the ground.

Its shape was ordinary enough that he initially took it for a traveler. He glanced at the vessel near its knee and asked, quietly, how long it had been waiting.

“Since before dawn.”

“There is nothing here now.”

“I know.”

He crouched and pressed the heels of his hands against the rind.

The power watched him move one hand, then the other. His shoulders tightened.

“There is water below,” he said. “But not where we can reach it.”

“Would you want another way?”

He looked up.

“Another way to what?”

“To reach water without losing the chamber you have.”

He was silent for a while. Behind them, insects rattled into the heat.

“Show me,” he said at last. “Don’t open it yet.”

So the power showed him.

Not the whole underside of the plateau. That would have been useless and frightening. It gave him the shape of this one fold: the pocket they had used, the narrow reservoir beneath it, the thin wall dividing that reservoir from a deep, empty passage.

He put his palm over the dangerous place.

“Oh,” he said.

There had been a cut there years before. He had made it himself.

***

They brought two other keepers.

One thought the power was lying. One believed it too quickly. The first keeper asked them both to be quiet.

Together they examined the fold. The power let them feel what it had shown him, and waited through their questions.

They asked whether taking the deeper water would dry a pool farther down the slope.

It would.

They asked whether the reservoir filled again at night.

Sometimes.

They asked whether its wall could be made thicker.

“Yes,” said the power.

“Then why show us where to cut?”

“Because I do not yet know which change you would choose.”

They spoke well into the afternoon. They brought people who used the lower pool. A woman arrived carrying the rod with which she tested for detached feeders, and insisted that the bowl’s oldest inhabitant be considered too.

Eventually they settled on a small change.

The power thickened the reservoir’s weak lower wall. It made a shallow cup beside the upper pocket, joined to it through a passage too narrow to drain it quickly. When the upper water reached its usual level, a little would enter the cup. When the level fell, the cup would stop filling.

The people could inspect the whole arrangement with their tools. They could close it with a plug of seasoned wood.

No one had to trust an unseen promise every time they drank.

That evening, the cup held less than a bowlful.

The keepers were delighted.

***

Word traveled unevenly.

At one settlement, the story arrived as an account of a new kind of water pocket. At another, it became a warning about a stranger who could soften the ground beneath a house.

The power went where it was invited. Where an invitation was uncertain, it waited outside.

People asked for many things.

A reliable route between two failing settlements.

A roof that would not admit biting animals.

Help for a boy whose fever had outlasted every remedy they knew.

The boy was easier to help than the route. The power cooled him, cleared the infection, and stayed while his frightened parents asked whether the quiet sleep was a good sign.

It did not require gratitude. His mother was angry for some time that such help existed and had arrived so late.

The power listened to her.

For the route, it spent a season tracing water and shade. It walked with travelers. It marked resting places in ways the people chose, and made two new catchments where the ground could support them without depriving existing hollows.

It did not make a straight road. A straight road would have crossed a breeding chamber, a thin shelf, and a broadmouth’s oldest feeding ground.

The resulting journey was still long.

People stopped dying on it quite so often.

***

It attended to lives that could not invite it in words.

Under one tree, heat repeatedly killed animals brought down inside fallen branches. The power made a shallow shelter nearby, open on several sides, with a cool underside and a rough ceiling they could grip. Most still died. Some survived long enough for dusk, and the climbers and winged creatures among them found their way upward.

In a seam-runner’s chamber, it strengthened an egg-bearing membrane that had begun to tear. It did not seal the chamber against predators. It gave the eggs the chance the mother had tried to provide.

There were changes it reconsidered.

A new seep attracted so many drinkers that the soft ground around it broke. The power closed it, repaired the tissue, and began again with several smaller outlets farther apart.

A shelter became a hunting place.

It altered the entrances, then watched. The hunters moved elsewhere; some returned. There was no arrangement that ended hunger.

The power remained willing to repair what it had misunderstood.

***

Years passed.

The plateau did not become quiet.

Shells still fell through the crowns. Stitchbacks still waited along the grazing roads. Houses tilted, storms withheld their rain, and somewhere beyond any completed journey a great tree died standing, releasing creatures from its drying folds.

But some burdens had become smaller.

There were water cups a keeper could examine without making a dangerous cut. There were sleeping places whose seams stayed closed. There were people alive who would otherwise have been remembered only in family gestures and the names of paths.

The woman whom the power had first followed grew old.

One evening she went beneath the overhang with a child who was nearly ready to learn the blade. The power was there, holding a shallow bowl under the first droplets.

“You can leave that,” she told it. “We have enough tonight.”

It set the bowl down.

The child knelt and pressed both hands against the rind. The woman adjusted one of them slightly.

“Wait,” she said.

They waited.

Behind them, the first small feet began to scratch over the cooling ground. The power felt their approach and moved aside before the woman needed to ask.
