The power notices the place first through a passage no wider than a reed.

Water has entered it from the new basin. The passage runs inward, bends along a seam, and ends beneath the impression of a thumb. There, a small part of the brown fan is still alive.

The rest has closed. Some of its threads will open again; some have become only the shapes through which water travels. The living patch has no knowledge of the basin, the cloud that filled it, or the hands that pressed the surrounding clay. Moisture reaches its edge. It begins to extend.

The power stays with it.

Not visibly. No shadow falls across the bank. The water does not brighten. The small clear bodies beneath the caught leaf continue drawing particles through their folded interiors.

To understand this inhabitant, the power must attend to more than its living edge. There are passages it made during an earlier wetting. There is the reed whose buried length it has softened. There are six closed patches farther along the join, two of which will never open again. There is the root above it, drawing from the same narrow supply of water.

The power follows all of these.

It finds that the fan can endure a long dryness, but not every dryness. It can spread into fresh material, but only from somewhere it has survived. It can alter the earth around it without being able to withstand every consequence of that alteration.

Below the living patch, the bank is beginning to separate.

The change is small. A layer laid too wet has settled against one laid nearly dry. Between them lies a pocket of air. The next substantial filling of the basin will drive water into that pocket. The weight above it will shift. A broad piece of bank will fall, exposing this seam to the noon sun.

Some inhabitants will benefit. Others will die. The falling clay will make a shallow pool where the clear bodies can open, and bury the green film now growing along the waterline.

The power does not hold the bank forever in place.

It attends farther along the seam.

There, beyond the part that will fall, a reed fiber passes through three blocks. Around it the clay is tightly pressed. The fan has reached the fiber twice and withdrawn twice. A little moisture persists on its far side, inaccessible through the compacted earth.

The power could open the way at once.

Instead, it waits through the afternoon.

The fan extends along the underside of the reed. At one grain it divides. One branch follows the fiber; another turns into a shallow depression and stops. The withdrawing water leaves both behind.

Before dawn, as the earth cools, the power lets a little of the warmth leave one buried face of the clay sooner than it otherwise would.

Moisture gathers there.

It is scarcely enough to change the color of the earth. A few grains darken. Between two of them, the living edge remains open.

On the following night, it extends again.

The power does not draw it toward the refuge. There are several directions available, and the fan occupies two of them. One leads into earth that will dry by midday. The other reaches the reed.

Over many mornings, a thin passage forms.

The fan makes it in its own way, loosening what it can loosen, entering spaces too small to admit a root, leaving behind walls that will conduct water after their living contents are gone. At last it reaches the dampness beyond the third block.

When the bank falls, that portion survives.

The exposed patches die quickly. The power remains through their dying too. Their threads lose water, draw tight, and cease. No hidden hand lifts them out of the earth. In the fallen bank, their emptied passages receive silt from the next rain.

Farther inward, the surviving patch closes.

It has no sense of having been spared. It does not hold the loss of the others as grief or the new refuge as gratitude. The power requires neither.

Across the plain, the power finds other narrow failures.

A basin receives several floods in succession, then none for so long that nearly all its enclosed grains will lose the capacity to open. A seam inhabited for miles drains through one accidental breach. A dark plate persists through many dry seasons but lies where no future water will reach it.

The power does not make every place habitable.

It leaves bare distances. It leaves salt at the shrinking margins of pools. It leaves the drying in which some inhabitants close and others fail to close in time.

But here and there, it makes a small difference.

A settling block comes to rest at a slightly different angle. Water that would have run directly into a deep joint pauses first in a shallow hollow. A curled plate catches against a clay crumb and remains where the next flood can soften it. Beneath one embankment, a pocket stays cool enough to preserve a few enclosed grains through a season longer than any they have survived before.

The changes are scattered. They do not bring the whole plain into agreement.

Some basins become less hospitable. Some channels depart from the courses prepared for them. The branching inhabitants consume the reeds, and the grasses enter the passages left behind. Roots widen joints. Banks fall.

The builders’ handprints weather too.

The power studies them while they remain: the drag of a palm, the four fingertips pressed deeper on one side, the place where someone corrected a slope with a second handful of wet earth.

It does not sharpen their edges against the rain. Water enters them. Living grains settle there. Eventually a root crosses the impression of a finger and lifts a small piece of its rim.

The power stays.

Years pass in which its help is no longer needed in the first seam. The surviving fan spreads, divides, and is overtaken in part by another. Far from the fallen bank, a small pale cup rises in the shade after rain.

Its wall dries and opens.

Fine living dust crosses the channel. Most comes to rest on ground where it will never grow. A little catches in the new cracks of an embankment.

Rain is falling somewhere beyond sight.

Under the caught leaf, now reduced to a few softened veins, water moves through a passage made by something that once lived there. It reaches a closed brown patch.

After a while, the edge opens.
