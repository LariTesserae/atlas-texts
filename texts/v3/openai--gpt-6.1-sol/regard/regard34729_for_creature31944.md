The power notices her hand first.

Not the hand she uses to test the stone, but the one she keeps beneath her chest when she rests. There is a thorn in it, broken below the skin. The swelling is small. She has climbed on it for eleven nights.

It notices the burned fingers of the young male, too, and the mouth of the infant whose mother has begun to give less milk. It notices a predator lying under the roots with an empty belly, listening to the seven settle overhead.

For a while it does nothing.

This is not indifference. There are many bodies here, and what relieves one may enter another as hunger. A new drinking place can become a place of ambush. A dependable tree can gather so many sleepers that its branches fail. Even kindness, put down carelessly, can acquire teeth.

The power listens beneath the stone.

Water lies in the gravel, cool and slow. Farther down, other water moves against heat. The two are separated by a tilted bed of rock that will eventually break, though not tonight. Above them, roots have found narrow passages. Some passages are closing. Others are opening into hollows too deep for an animal to survive a fall.

The power could lift the basin clear of its fires. It could round every edge and still every vine.

Instead, below a ridge where the ground has ceased to rise, it shifts a little gravel.

The movement is less than the width of the old female’s smallest nail.

Water begins to follow a different seam.

It does not emerge beside the vents, where the grazers already crowd. It comes through three openings some distance apart. One wets a sloping face of stone beneath a tangle of roots. Another fills a depression no deeper than a muzzle. The third appears high on a broken ledge, where only a light climbing body can reach it.

None receives enough water to flood the ground. Each receives enough to remain wet after the canopy has dried.

At the upper opening, a loose flake of stone settles inward rather than outward. The ledge holds.

That is all the power changes there.

The old female finds it two nights later.

She has followed the smell of a fruit that fell into a fissure. The fruit is beyond reach. While withdrawing her arm, she feels cold on the inside of her wrist.

She stops.

Above her, the young male moves impatiently along a branch. She bares her teeth at him without looking up. Then she touches the stone again.

Cold.

She brings the wet hand to her mouth.

There is no mineral sting. She waits, listening, and reaches farther into the seam. When she withdraws her fingers, a drop runs down to her elbow.

She drinks awkwardly at first, licking her wrist. Then she finds where the water gathers beneath the projecting stone.

The others come down only after she has been there some time.

They shove. They reach across her face. One of the mothers gives a sharp cry when a youngster steps on her infant. The old female strikes the young male, who has put his burned hand into the water and will not move it.

The power does not make them grateful.

By the fourth night they have found another approach to the ledge. By the sixth, the smaller animals wait on a root above it and take the drops that fall from the drinkers’ chins. A bird arrives at dawn. Much later, moss begins to darken the lower stone.

The power watches where their feet go.

It leaves the routes they use alone. Below the sleeping fork, however, a slab has begun to separate from its support. Nothing lives beneath it except roots and small pale insects. When the seven are elsewhere, the slab lowers gently into the hollow. Earth shakes from the roots. The insects scatter through their surviving passages.

A fall that would have opened beneath a passing body has happened without one.

Elsewhere the ground still cuts. A thorn still pierces a foot. The vines tighten under weight.

The power attends to the old female’s palm while she sleeps.

It does not remove the scar or return the missing toe. Those belong to the shape by which she knows her own body. It loosens the thorn from the living tissue around it. Over the next few days, the swelling carries the fragment toward the surface.

She bites it out herself.

For a long time afterward she turns the hand before her nose, licking the small opening. Then an infant reaches toward her mouth, and she cuffs it away.

The young male’s burn closes cleanly. It still hurts enough that he favors the hand. When he approaches the hot fissure again, he stops before touching it.

There is one change the power considers for longer.

The old female knows things the others do not. Her knowledge will die in pieces: the safe side of a vent, the smell of water before it turns, the route across a tree that has grown weak. Already she pauses where a trunk no longer stands.

The power could place her knowledge in the others.

It could make the young male understand the reason for every retreat.

But he has scarcely learned to wait for her. The mothers have routes of their own. The youngest notices insects she passes without seeing. What they might become together is not something the power has come to replace.

It gives them time instead.

Not endless time. A little more.

Through the next dry season, water continues to gather on the high ledge. The hollow trunk remains sound. Food is still fought over, but fewer journeys end in a frantic search for something wet. On some mornings they reach the sleeping fork before exhaustion. One animal wakes while the others remain asleep and has nothing urgent to do.

The half-grown male returns to the hollow with a pod.

He tries the small stone, then the broad one. When the shell opens, he eats most of the pulp himself. A youngster watches from the lip of the trunk. The next night, that youngster brings a pod too.

The old female takes the better pieces from both of them.

Nothing in the basin announces what has happened. There is no new brightness in the sky. The insects continue their metallic noise. A predator catches a bird at the lower pool. Roots advance toward the cool seam and stop short of the hottest stone.

Much later, the old female descends toward the ledge and finds a younger mother already there.

The mother has laid her infant across a fork above the water. Before lowering her face, she touches the stone with the inside of her wrist.

The old female waits behind her.

For once, there is enough water, and no need to hurry her away.
