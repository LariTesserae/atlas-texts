The first thing the power did was listen.

Not to the roar alone. It listened to the differences the roar concealed: a stone bearing too hard against flesh, a root feeding from exhausted water, the small change in a sleeper’s breathing when a wall touched the back of their neck.

It listened across the distances that did not remain distances. A cry reaching yesterday was still a cry. A foot caught beneath a foundation was still attached to someone, even where the pressure had drawn the rest of the body very far away.

It did not mistake the country’s difficulty for a wish to be remade.

But neither did it mistake endurance for consent.

***

In one chamber, a woman slept with her hand against the doorway.

She had been awake for most of three days. One of her children had a fever. The other had lost a vessel, and she had gone back through the channels to find it, because there were things the household could not carry without that vessel.

Her fingers had slipped from the opening.

Beneath her, the inhabitant was adjusting its stones. A pressure had entered the foundation at an angle for which there was no room. The outer slabs moved inward. Flesh thickened between them. The chamber began to descend.

The power did not hold the animal still.

It took the weight of one stone.

Only one: a long, buried slab pressing across a fold of pale tissue. Relieved of that weight, the tissue opened another way. The foundation settled outward instead of inward.

The room stopped narrowing.

The woman woke with the sensation that someone had loosened her fingers one at a time. She put her hand back against the doorway. Under her palm, the wall was moving.

She woke the children.

By the time they had carried their bundles outside, the floor had risen to the height of their knees.

The animal completed its adjustment. Nothing was torn from it. Its room closed around rainwater and a small cutting edge the woman had not been able to find.

Later, she returned for the edge.

It lay in a shallow groove outside the foundation, dark with sediment.

She did not know what had happened. She washed it and carried it home.

***

The power learned how often safety for one body meant injury to another.

A wall could be kept open by pinning its maker in a shape that hurt. A reed could be made abundant by drawing nourishment from all the reeds around it. A dry place could be raised, but the displaced water had to go somewhere, and somewhere was already inhabited.

There were easier places than this country. It could have opened a way to one of them.

It kept that possibility.

It did not begin there.

Instead, it studied the moment before a chamber closed: the rearrangement of weight, the fluid gathering behind the inner wall, the change that a practiced hand might feel too late.

The inhabitants already answered pressure. Their bodies possessed a thousand ways of making room for it.

The power gave them one more.

***

At first, the change was taken for a new danger.

A man preparing to sleep felt three slow swellings beneath his palm. They came from the doorway, though the wall beside it remained still.

He withdrew his hand.

The swellings stopped.

When he touched the doorway again, they resumed.

He took his household elsewhere.

That afternoon the chamber folded into its foundation.

In another shelter, a child felt the same movement against her heel. She laughed and pressed back. The doorway widened slightly, then swelled again, harder.

Her grandmother carried her outside.

People began comparing what they had felt.

The movement was not identical in every chamber. Some thresholds tightened and relaxed. Some grew warm in brief intervals. In the dark, root-lined shelters, a strip of wall became soft enough to yield beneath a thumb.

But the change continued while there was something living in the room.

And it began before the room became too small to leave.

The youngest inhabitants had no doorways broad enough for people. When a six-legged animal stepped onto one of their foundations, the stones loosened in sequence beneath it, lifting its feet toward the edge before drawing together.

Not every foot was freed.

The power attended to those as well.

***

It was not a command to shelter.

The chambers could still close. They could become nurseries, or disappear for reasons no person understood. The inhabitant could refuse a weight its foundation could not bear.

But what had been an inward necessity now reached the threshold.

The people developed a sign for it in their conversations: two fingers pressed against the wrist, then separated.

Children used the sign when they wanted someone to move away.

Adults used it too.

There were disagreements about how long one could remain after the first warning. A few people waited too long. Others left so quickly that they abandoned food they badly needed.

The power made no household wise all at once.

Where it could, it gave them time to become wiser without losing someone in the lesson.

***

There were also chambers in which the people never felt a warning because they could not reach one.

A child sleeping between two adults. An old man whose hands no longer distinguished the finer movements. A traveler who had not learned what a threshold was saying.

So the change spread through the floor as well.

It was faint. It had to be faint: the young in the wall hollows could not bear a violent motion, and a frightened sleeper could injure themselves against the low roof.

The floor lifted and settled under each living weight.

It repeated itself.

No part of the country had to fall silent for the message to arrive.

***

The power continued listening.

It found hunger that was not an adaptation, merely hunger. It found wounds the warm water kept open. It found people holding one another through the great concussions with a care no intervention could improve.

For the hunger, it loosened small quantities of copper crust where gathering it would not strip the stone. It did not make every piece safe for every body. Instead, spoiled portions lost their metallic sheen before they entered a bundle, and nourishing portions held it long enough to be found.

For the wounds, it cleared a little water.

The clear patches were no larger than two cupped hands. They formed beside certain foundations, where water already passed slowly through pale flesh and nested stone.

People discovered that washed wounds sometimes closed there.

They began leaving markers.

They brought their injured neighbors. They carried water away in wet-lined vessels, although it did not always remain useful once removed from the foundation.

The power watched where the markers led. When a clear patch began drawing too many feet across a young inhabitant, another appeared beyond it.

***

Once, an old woman sat outside a chamber that had belonged to her household for most of her life.

It was closing. Its warning had continued all morning. Her belongings were already elsewhere.

She remained with both hands on the wall.

The power came near enough to feel what she was asking.

Not for the room to remain.

Something more difficult to distinguish: whether the years in it had amounted to anything for the body beneath her hands. Whether the cleaning of its stones, the food left in its gaps, the children sleeping against its walls had been received.

The power did not supply the animal with a human answer.

It eased the pressure around one of the outer stones.

The inhabitant spread into the space. A thin portion of flesh rose beneath the woman’s palm, sheltered there from the rain.

She kept her hand in place.

After a while she wiped the doorway with both wet hands and left.

***

There were people who wanted to go.

Some were visitors. Some had been born among the flooded foundations. Knowing the country intimately had not made them love it, and loving it had not made them want to remain.

For these, the power made a passage.

It was not visible from every shore. It did not appear in front of someone merely because they were tired or afraid. The first sign was a current running cool against the ankles, where no cool water had run before.

Those who followed it found a low bank.

Beyond the bank, rain fell at its ordinary distance from the earth. There was ground on which a person could lie down without placing a hand against an opening.

People crossed.

Some returned with food, dry cloth wrapped in waterproof bundles, stories that were difficult to tell through the wrists.

The passage remained open to them.

No one had to abandon a child, a parent, a vessel, or the possibility of coming back in order to leave.

***

Much of the country continued as it had.

The fronts moved. Horizons approached and withdrew. The black reeds opened at night, exposing their folded yellow tissue to the warm rain.

Young inhabitants crossed the stone with their small quantities of country held beneath them.

The power accompanied some of these crossings. It bore no visible light. When a stone slipped from a pocket of flesh, it sometimes kept the stone nearby until the animal could gather it again.

Elsewhere, a household felt a warning through the floor and began passing bundles out through the doorway.

They were tired. They would have to find another chamber. Outside, the noise was already rising.

But there was time to wake everyone.

There was time to count the children, and then count them again.

There was time for the last person through the opening to turn, lay a wet hand against the wall, and feel it withdraw.
