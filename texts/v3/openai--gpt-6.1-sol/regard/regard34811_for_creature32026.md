The power came without entering through the notch.

For a time, nothing changed.

It attended to the hollow as the animal did, in pieces: the knock beneath the unreliable slab, the cool seam carrying sound, the warm pocket where two small bodies pressed against a third. It followed the air downward through the mountain until warmth became heat. It followed the returning birds beyond the ridge, into valleys where insects rose over running water.

It watched the animal take a fallen bird. It watched the bird’s remaining young wait at the ledge.

Its attention stayed with both.

There was much it could have done. It could have filled the chamber with light, made food arrive without pursuit, carried the animal into a green country with shallow streams. But the animal knew where to place its feet here. It slept deeply beneath this particular weight of stone. In the green country, every sound would have come from an unknown direction.

So the power remained through a cold interval.

Fewer wings returned. The crickets withdrew. The animal worked a broken beetle shell between its teeth, then crossed to the basin behind the slab.

There was almost no water.

It pressed its mouth to the darkest part and licked. Behind it, the young one that had followed waited in the narrow passage. The parent backed out, allowing it through.

The young licked the same stone.

Far above them, a drop gathered on the ceiling. It fell onto a broad block, spread thinly, and vanished into the dust.

The power followed the next drop.

There was already a groove across the block, too shallow to carry water to its edge. Along that groove lay a ridge of mineral crust no thicker than a claw. The power loosened a few grains.

The next drop spread. Part of it reached the edge.

By morning, a damp line had appeared down the side of the block. It entered a crack, traveled beneath the rubble, and emerged behind the drinking slab.

The basin did not fill. There was no new spring, no sound of abundance. But when the animal returned, its tongue found water that gathered again after it had swallowed.

It stopped between mouthfuls, listening.

Then it drank.

The power stayed longer.

Beneath the tilted block, the smaller young had begun to crawl beyond the nest. It put one large foot into a gap, withdrew it, and tried another. The parent slept nearby with one ear turned toward it.

Deep below that pocket, a stone had been slipping for years. Each settling of the rubble brought it nearer to release. When it finally moved, the tilted block would drop by less than the width of the animal’s head.

That would be enough.

The power held the stone while the young slept. It could have held it there forever. Instead, it let a little water enter an empty seam beneath it, carrying dissolved mineral from the warmer depths. Slowly, a pale rib formed between two buried surfaces.

By the time the young were making their own routes through the chamber, the stone rested on that rib. The warmth still passed around it. The pocket remained open.

Neither parent nor young knew anything had been done.

There were things the power left alone.

The cloudy crescent in the animal’s eye remained. So did the scar across its muzzle. They caused it little pain now, and clearing them would not return the days in which they had been made.

The bats still crowded one another. Birds lost eggs. The predator climbed, failed, rested, climbed again. A dead cricket beneath a slab fed creatures too small for its hunter to notice.

Once, a bird struck the lip of the entrance in a hard wind and fell into the chamber. The power felt the small violence of the impact. It stayed beside the bird through the brief confusion afterward.

The animal found it before dawn.

As the cold interval ended, water began to bead more freely overhead. The drinking basin deepened. The young grew into the reach of their whiskers. One discovered the passage behind the pillar and returned by a route the parent could not follow.

Eventually, the power withdrew its attention far enough that the hollow was wholly dark again.

It left no mark at the entrance. Nothing had been summoned to worship it. Nothing had been asked to become other than it was.

Under the overhang, the animal woke. It loosened a feather from its flank, held it between its forefeet, and pulled at the quill with its teeth. Above it, a bird shifted on a nest.

A drop fell behind the slab.

One ear turned toward the sound. The animal finished with the feather, unfolded itself, and went to drink.
