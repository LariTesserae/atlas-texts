The power does not enter through the passage.

For a long time, nothing in the hollow announces its arrival. The tiles keep their separate hours. Water gathers behind the roots and finds the grooves. The creature takes a flake from a housing and carries it to a trunk, where the soft seam accepts it.

Aboveground, a season passes.

The power follows the heat downward. It follows the piston farther than the creature can go, through a shaft whose lining has been altered by pressure, mineral deposits, and roots too fine to see. It discovers what the pump now lifts, which is not quite what it was built to lift. It learns where the water comes from and where it goes after entering the black soil. It listens to the vibration in the pronged foot.

It does not mistake loudness for suffering, or silence for peace.

There are things here that would die if the floor became cool. There are things that would die if the tiles shone steadily. The loose plate, rattling in the wall, is not merely loose: its movement keeps a narrow vent from sealing with deposits. The creature knows this, though perhaps it has never known it in words.

The power waits until it can distinguish what the hollow needs from what the hollow has learned to endure.

Then, beneath the western shelf, it touches a bearing.

The change is smaller than a grain of grit.

At the next revolution, the flywheel passes one point without the faint resistance that has been increasing for years.

The creature stops beside a water groove.

Its prongs settle flat. Its upper folds part a little, then close. It remains still through another stroke.

Later it visits the western shelf. A long hand enters the dark beneath the housing and returns with a film of oil. It touches the oil to a seam under its plates.

The power does nothing else that day.

***

The first gift is not a new machine.

It is a place for a failure to go.

Far below the floor, beyond the deepest living root, there is a joint that has almost exhausted its ability to bend. Each stroke moves it a little. Each movement is fractionally less easy than the last. When it finally breaks, the piston will stop at the bottom of its travel, and the pool will cease to rise.

The power does not make the joint unbreakable. It lets the strain spread into the surrounding stone, over a breadth that can bear it.

The joint keeps moving.

Nothing visible has changed.

The creature continues its rounds.

***

Near the ledge where it rests, condensation has always formed in the wrong place.

Most of it gathers along the far edge, where droplets fall into a fissure. The creature can reach that edge, but only by extending itself out of the cool band. It seldom does.

One day the metal settles.

A rivet releases a little of its old tension. The ledge acquires a slope so slight that dust would not slide along it.

Water does.

The first drop follows the new inclination and hangs above the creature’s folded upper surfaces.

It falls while the creature is elsewhere.

The second falls on a plate.

The third reaches the gray inner fold.

The creature closes around it.

Over the following days, it moves its resting place by less than the width of its broad foot.

The power leaves the ledge alone.

***

It would be possible to open the roof.

Possible to replace the tiles with daylight, silence the pump, draw fresh water through the passage. Possible to carry the creature upward into a garden designed around its needs.

But the creature has never asked for a garden.

And its needs are not all held inside its body.

Some extend into the bearing it clears. Some follow the copper threads into the growth. Some may be waiting in a shallow ceramic cup, where no useful mechanism can be found.

The power does not reach into the cup to discover them.

***

Instead, it makes a small recess beside the passage.

Not outside. Not yet.

It uses a pocket left behind an old collapse, opening it between two roots without breaking either. The recess has the hollow’s warmth, the hollow’s dampness. Its floor carries the pump’s vibration clearly. Air from above reaches it only after passing over wet stone.

There is room for the creature to stand without folding its arms tightly against itself.

There is also room to turn back.

For many days, it does not enter.

Then a ceiling tile brightens, and a weak reflection appears on the recess’s far wall.

The creature approaches with one long hand extended. It touches the stone, then the floor. Its pronged foot remains in the familiar lane.

At last it brings the broad foot forward.

Nothing closes behind it.

The light fades.

It withdraws.

The next time, it stays longer.

***

The power cannot tell whether the creature wants company.

It does not manufacture another creature to answer the question.

But when it next passes beneath the repaired bearing, a strip of its hand meets a slight resistance: not a blockage, not heat, not the familiar dragging of a worn surface. The strip rests there. The resistance yields under it, then returns gently when it lifts away.

The creature repeats the movement.

The power answers only as much as it is touched.

This continues for part of a revolution.

Then water begins to rise in the pool. The creature folds its hand and goes down to the grooves.

On its next circuit, the gentle resistance is still there.

It can approach it.

It can pass by.

***

The hollow does not become easy.

There is still heat beneath the shelves. Roots still cross the lanes. New shoots threaten the shaft, and grit gathers wherever water slows. The creature still works.

But a cracked housing takes longer to worsen. The dangerous joint beneath the floor continues to bend. More water reaches the resting ledge, and less disappears uselessly into the fissure.

There are intervals now in which nothing urgently needs the creature’s hands.

At first it uses them to inspect places it has already inspected.

Then it rests.

Much later, it enters the metal shelter and touches the cup.

Its narrow finger follows the inside rim through the dust. The groove is almost exactly where it was before.

The pool rises.

Water passes through the notch it has made beneath a root. The pale line wets around the basin. A drop travels along a leaf and falls into a groove.

The creature remains beside the shelf.

The pool subsides.

It stays through another stroke.

The power attends to the bearing below, keeping its touch too slight to reach the shelter floor.

Inside, the creature’s hand rests on the ceramic rim. After a while, its upper folds open a little farther.

Nothing calls it away.
