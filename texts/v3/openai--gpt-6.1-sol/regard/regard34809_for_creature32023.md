At first, the new presence is mistaken for a change in the water.

The older inhabitant notices it while feeding the animal. Its hands have begun to whiten, and the ray has not yet loosened its fins. Usually this requires a little longer.

This time the older person takes its hands away.

The animal remains warm.

For a while the resident holds one hand beneath it again, without giving anything. Then it turns the hand over and examines the palm.

Nothing has been taken from somewhere else in the town. That is the first thing they check.

***

The presence does not enter a house.

It remains near the unfinished foundations, in the unoccupied ground between two sets of sockets. It has no visible body, but the residents can locate it. Their attention settles on the same patch of water. The child approaches it with the smaller loop and is called back.

The foundation worker comes out and puts a hand where an arm might be.

There is no arm.

There is, however, a response: a narrow line along the nearest socket, traveling slowly enough to follow.

The worker answers.

What follows takes several days.

The presence could make itself understood more quickly. It could put knowledge directly into the residents’ minds, or arrange their hands around the answer. It does neither. Their way of speaking allows a person to stop a line before it has finished. It allows another person to decline to answer.

These are useful qualities in a language.

The presence learns to use them.

***

When it is invited below the square, the chamber becomes crowded.

Not all the inhabitants attend. Someone stays with the recovering resident, and someone stays with the child. The foundation worker comes, carrying the loop. The person with the cup rests it against a frame.

The presence is shown the mountain.

Not merely its fractures. Those are plain enough.

It is shown the relation between a gap beneath the square and a gap at a resident’s wrist. A narrow displacement in the northern rock corresponds to something inside a house. The chamber’s surfaces reveal associations that cannot be seen by looking harder at either object alone.

The town is not holding an ordinary mountain together by an extraordinary effort.

It occupies the interruption.

To remove the interruption would not restore the inhabitants to some earlier, undamaged condition. There is no such condition for most of them. The child was born here.

The presence studies this for a long time.

Then it asks to be shown the places where the work is difficult.

There are more of these than the residents first indicate.

One frame has to be checked twice as often as its neighbors. One departure gate cannot be left open while a particular surface is being touched. The foundation worker’s new upright survives only if three other measurements remain within a small difference of one another.

And there is an interval, far down inside the rock, which everyone has learned not to alter.

They show it last.

No one stands directly in front of the frame while it is visible.

***

The presence does not close it.

Instead, it sets a second surface beside the first.

There is no frame around this one. It is smaller than a resident’s hand. It shows the same interval from another position.

The person with the cup looks from one view to the other. A rod is brought out. Someone touches the wall sharply.

The presence lets the second surface disappear.

After a consultation, it is asked to bring it back.

Now the inhabitants can see something they had previously been required to infer. The interval is not changing in the way they feared. Another separation, hidden behind it in the old view, has been making the measurements uncertain.

This does not mean their years of caution were foolish.

They were right to be cautious.

For the remainder of that day, no work is done on either interval. People enter the chamber merely to look. The older resident comes down, stays a while, and goes back to the animal.

The child is brought to see the new surface.

It reaches toward it, and this time the adult allows the touch.

***

The first help they agree upon is a support beneath the northern foundations.

It is temporary.

The foundation worker insists on this, and the presence agrees. A support whose removal cannot be tested would be another obligation, however generously supplied.

They choose a socket where nothing has yet been built. The presence takes a little of the strain there. The worker makes a mark along its edge.

Then the presence withdraws.

The socket holds.

They repeat the test at a different depth, then with an intentionally poor alignment, then while two residents work in the chamber. Once the support fails to release cleanly. The worker signals, and everything stops.

The correction takes longer than the first construction.

No one leaves the tool lying out that evening.

Eventually there is a support they understand well enough to dismantle, alter, and make again.

It does not draw upon anyone’s hands.

The foundation worker produces another upright. This one remains through the next inspection and the one after it.

It is still no taller than a thumb.

***

Help in the sickroom is offered separately.

The resident with the missing part of an arm is awake when the presence comes to the doorway. Its visitor touches the wall and waits.

There is a long exchange.

The thin loop holding the surviving portions in alignment has been rubbing against one edge. The injury cannot rest while every movement requires correction. The presence can take over that correction without changing the resident’s own position in the town.

This is accepted.

The loop is removed.

For the first time since the return through the gate, the resident lets its arm hang without watching it.

Later, other possibilities are discussed. There are things the presence can provide, but the missing portion is not merely a shape to be reproduced. The resident asks questions. Some answers require another journey below the rail. Others require time.

No decision is made that day.

Someone brings in the chipped cup. It is placed within reach of the uninjured hand.

***

The animal accepts assistance with less deliberation.

It investigates the new source of warmth, presses its underside against it, and stays.

The child objects.

Feeding the ray is something the child has only recently been permitted to do. For two days it brings its hand beneath the animal and finds no need for it.

The older person notices before the presence does.

An arrangement is changed.

Afterward there is warmth enough that feeding does not leave the older resident’s hands white. There is still something for the child to give. The ray continues to follow particular people across the foundations.

It also begins to follow the presence.

This is inconvenient when work is being done near a socket. A small place is made for it farther away. The animal ignores that place until the threshold’s familiar brightness is reproduced there.

Then it uses both.

***

More difficult than making a support is deciding how much support to make.

The inhabitants disagree.

Some want the chamber relieved of all its work. Some distrust an arrangement they cannot inspect from end to end. One resident wants to know whether departures could become unnecessary. Another answers so sharply that the line reaches the far wall.

The presence does not settle the argument.

It shows them what it can offer in parts.

A view that can be closed. A reserve that can be isolated. An alarm that does not require a person to spend the night looking for a change too small to see elsewhere. A way of sharing the load between the seven houses without passing it through their occupants.

Each proposal has its own refusal.

Each accepted part is tested while the old means remain available.

The chamber is still used. The rods are still carried down. The small mark on the floor is not erased.

But eventually there is an interval in the town’s work during which no one has to be below the square.

The first time this happens, three residents go down anyway.

The second time, one does.

The third time, the chamber remains empty until morning.

Above it, the person whose feet ache sits with them raised from the floor recesses. Two visitors stay late. Their lines cross the table and disappear into the wall.

There is no summons from beneath them.

***

The presence could brighten the water.

It could reveal the valleys, open a road through the depths, bring news of places beyond the range of the departing inhabitants. It could make an immense number of changes.

These remain things to ask about.

For now, the town has requested a room.

Not a palace, not a refuge somewhere else. A room on the northern foundation, built in the material and by the methods its worker knows, large enough for several people to sit together without occupying someone’s house.

The presence supplies what has been scarce. The residents determine where the doorway goes.

There is disagreement about the doorway too.

When the first wall remains standing, the worker fetches the same people who came to inspect the little upright. They touch it, examine its edge, and look through the opening where another wall will be.

The child is allowed inside before the roof is made.

It places the smaller loop in a corner, thereby making a claim whose significance everyone understands.

***

The mountain is still fractured.

The water is still deep, and there is still no shore.

Departures continue through the rail. People return with objects, with news, sometimes with fatigue. The presence does not promise that nothing will ever happen beyond the gates. It asks what should be ready when someone comes home.

Under the square, the new surfaces stand among the old ones. Their workings are recorded in lines the inhabitants can read. They belong to the town now.

The presence remains nearby without requiring a place at the table.

One evening, the two adults put the child into its recess. It reaches for the rod, is refused, and accepts the smaller loop instead.

The adults cross the square together.

At the slit between the paving slabs, one pauses. The other lowers a hand toward the luminous circle. They check what needs checking.

Then they continue past the chamber entrance.

At the northern edge, the new room has a doorway, three walls, and no roof. The worker is there. So is the person with the cup.

There is somewhere to sit.

For a while, no one does anything else.
