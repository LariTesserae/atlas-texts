The power did not begin by entering.

For a long time it attended to the hollow from farther away than any root could reach. It followed the warmth upward through the mountain and the water downward from the ridges. It watched the clear substance yield beneath a growing fern, and watched the same substance resist a root approaching from the wrong direction.

It did not mistake that resistance for a defect.

Nor did it take the empty spaces for an invitation to fill them.

There were things it could have brought: deeper soil, abundant rain, plants whose roots would tolerate the bitter seams. It could have made the hollow visible from every slope below. It could have given the inhabitant a voice, or gathered its slow changes into a single place where they would happen quickly.

It left these possibilities unused.

When the drop fell beneath the western wall, the power followed the small release through the body. Some passages widened. Others scarcely changed. Beneath the empty pocket where a cluster had once grown, water continued to arrive.

The power could discern the old arrangement of roots in the mineral grains. It could not know whether the persistence was burdensome. It waited rather than closing the passage.

A winter passed.

Then a season in which rain came often but lightly, wetting the upper stone without replenishing the deeper ways.

The uphill water slowed. A fragment of leaf stopped midway along the channel and remained there until its edges became transparent. The fern above it opened one small frond and no more.

Far below, the inhabitant’s narrow extension lay against a seam of cool rock. On the other side of that seam was water.

Not a river. Not an inexhaustible source. A little water, held in a closed fissure, replenished by snowmelt that reached it years after falling on the mountain. Between it and the living layer lay a thickness of stone no greater than a fingernail.

The power considered opening it.

An opening could admit more than relief. It could alter the balance of warmth. It could keep a passage wet that needed sometimes to dry. In time, the body might withdraw from its old sources and become dependent on the new one.

So the power made less than an opening.

On the far side of the seam, it loosened the smallest space between two grains. Moisture entered that space and stopped. The remaining stone was unchanged.

Nothing happened for several days.

Then the living layer extended a little farther along its own side of the seam. It had done this before in other places, during other dry seasons. Here, a trace of moisture now reached it.

The clear substance softened.

The power waited.

Over the following weeks, the inhabitant pressed into the stone. Grains shifted under a pressure too slight to fracture anything. A passage formed, narrow enough that water reached the living layer only as dampness.

The fern’s basin ceased losing depth.

It did not fill.

The broad leaves still lowered in the afternoon. The exposed rock still dried. In the shallow seams, roots held close to the places that admitted them.

When rain returned, the new passage tightened. The cool water remained below, mostly untouched.

The power watched this happen and made no attempt to keep the passage open.

***

There were other seasons.

Once, a seed lodged beneath the largest shelf, in the pocket where the old roots had been. It lay through several days of clear weather. When moisture reached it, it split and put out a pale tip.

The inhabitant did not admit it.

The tip thickened against the closed seam. Two small leaves rose from the seed’s remaining food, then folded together.

The power had enough strength to make the contact hold.

It did not.

Later, another seed came. This one rested nearer the edge of the pocket, where the living layer was thinner. Its root followed a shallow impression left by something long dead.

There was a yielding there.

For a season it made only two leaves.

The power kept its attention on the contact without supplying anything to it. Beneath the stone, the clear substance changed slowly around the root. At one point it drew away. The root remained alive in the damp soil, and after several days the substance returned.

A third leaf followed.

It was narrow, unlike the leaves that had once grown there. Its underside carried a fine silver down.

The water delivered to the pocket began to change its course.

***

The power did not remain equally near at all times.

There was no need for the hollow to become a place where every pressure was intercepted, every failing stem sustained. A frond died. A wet seam became dry. One of the translucent cushions diminished for three years, then put out new shoots along its northern edge.

The power attended most closely to things arriving from beyond the inhabitant’s reach.

Deep within the mountain, stone sometimes moved. Water found new routes. The slow warmth had its variations. Once, a shift below the hollow threatened to close the fissure that supplied much of the western wall.

The power held one surface apart from another while the weight settled elsewhere.

Above, the bead at the pore continued to grow.

No new brightness entered the hollow. No mark appeared on the rim. The being beneath the stone acquired no memory of a vast presence holding the mountain for it.

It retained only a season in which the water had continued.

In the evening, the new plant beneath the shelf lowered one silver-backed leaf. A little rainwater slipped from its tip into the old root pocket.

The living layer softened there.

Far below, the small passage to the reserve remained closed.

The power watched the hollow through the last of the daylight, then let its attention widen to the surrounding slopes.

Beneath the western wall, a drop fell.

The next began to gather.
