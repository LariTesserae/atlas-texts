The power did not enter the hollow.

An entrance would have been a poor beginning. Even attention, if it took the form of light, would have altered the place. A brightness sufficient to reveal the forest at once would have become an event in every exposed blade. Warmth would have been worse.

So the power learned it by degrees, without putting anything there.

It learned the distance between a hooked leaf and the stem that held it. It learned the conductive bands inside the transparent trunks, and the much slower work by which their enclosing tissues were replaced. It distinguished the living velvet from the weathered glaze beneath it. Where the two seemed inseparable, it waited until it understood the difference.

It did not look for a face.

Nor did it infer unhappiness from the absence of movement. The thin leaves were not trying to escape their intervals. The white stalks were not deprived transparent trees. The clearing on the bolt was not a place awaiting an occupant.

The power had seen beings for whom confinement was an injury. That knowledge did not tell it what these lives required.

For a long while, it did nothing.

During that while, a minute fault in the waxy sheath of one conductive band was repaired. Carbon entered a pale scale near the regulator. A contact pad maintained the boundary through which it received its charge.

The forest was not motionless. The scale of its activity was simply unlike the scale of an arrival.

Then the power followed the conductor.

Down through the crossarm, down through the sections of tower lost in darkness, and outward along the immense circuit, it traced the conditions that sustained the hollow. It found the source of the load. It found its destination. Both had histories; neither had been made for the forest.

The current was not a gift. It was an arrangement.

And the arrangement would eventually fail.

Not soon by the measure of a leaf’s repair, but soon enough by the measure of the old ceramic. A connection far away was losing material. A reserve was being consumed. The tower’s foundations held, but did not hold without limit.

The power returned its attention to the broken insulator.

It could have made a larger hollow. It could have raised a second ledge beside the first, supplied it with dust and charge, and given the crowded roots new pores to enter.

But it could not yet tell what such a change would do. The forest’s stability depended partly on its crowding. A branch released from its neighbors might cease to bear the vibration safely. A root extending into fresh material might draw charge away from a contact that had endured for ages.

It could have silenced the tower.

Instead, it listened to the way the tower sounded through the leaves. The hard ringing note was carried along ribs, divided at branch junctions, returned to the ceramic. Silence would also be a change, and the power found no reason to impose it.

The loose cover of the regulator remained loose. Its missing screw was not replaced. The transparent stem through the empty hole was given no occasion to yield.

What the power changed was farther away.

Beyond the forest’s faintest light, it made another path by which the circuit could be sustained. The path carried no additional charge into the spindle. It altered neither the load nor the ringing of the insulator. For now, it remained unused.

The power tested it elsewhere, against a model sufficiently exact that the failures mattered. It found and corrected faults that would have produced a surge. It learned how slowly a transfer would have to occur for the contact pads to continue their work undisturbed.

Only then did it leave the second path connected.

When the distant source weakened, the forest would not have to weaken with it.

The power also took some of the burden from the tower’s foundations. Not all of it: the tensions in the metal contributed to the note at the crossarm, and the note belonged to the present conditions of every plant. The support was slight, distributed far below, adjusted to preserve those tensions.

There was nothing new to see from the broken lip.

Inside, the violet margins remained violet. The green veins did not brighten. No leaf acquired more space, and none lost the space it occupied.

The power did not make the forest indestructible. It had learned too much about living boundaries to mistake permanence for their purpose. The plants still maintained themselves. Their tissues could still fail, and their forms could still change. If a new shoot someday found an interval, it would not be held back for the sake of keeping the garden as it had first been found.

Nor would the power require a shoot to appear.

It kept watch chiefly on the things the forest could not repair: the great circuit, the tower, the slow fracture advancing through a plate of buried metal far below. Its care was quiet, but not absent. It would have to go on learning.

At the entrance, the pale leaf lay against its ledge. Its underside retained the impression of the flaw in the glaze.

Beyond it were the stars, the immense drop, and the mountains.

The leaf did not tremble.

Far down in the darkness, a joint that would one day have parted continued to hold.
