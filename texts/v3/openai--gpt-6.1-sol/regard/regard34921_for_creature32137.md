The power noticed the foot.

It was smaller than the grains moving past it. It extended from between the clear valves, touched the broken shell, withdrew, and touched again.

There was nothing wrong with the surface. There was nothing wrong with the animal. But the current through the new opening was still changing. Water entered the hollow behind the fragment, gathered pressure, and escaped beneath its lower edge. The animal had found shelter in the interval between two pulses.

The next pulse would turn the fragment over.

The power could have stopped the water. It could have lifted the little animal into a chamber where no hunter could reach it, where food arrived without interruption and every surface held firm.

It watched the foot spread.

Then it reached beneath the fallen piece and moved three grains of shell.

They settled into a narrow space between the fragment and the terrace. The fragment shifted against them. When the pulse came, water slipped underneath and out through the open side, but the fragment did not turn.

The animal closed its valves. For a long time it did nothing visible.

Then its foot touched the surface once more.

***

The power stayed.

At first it attended to the basin as the animals did: through pressure, through the arrival of food, through places where a body could hold. It followed water into the tall hollow stacks. It entered sealed chambers through fractures narrower than a feeding hair. It felt the weight of terraces resting on terraces, all the way down into shell so old that almost none of its original spaces remained.

Near the outer rim, it found a seam.

The seam had begun beneath a living builder. Generations had covered it. Water had worked into it from above, and a slow movement of the basin’s weight had widened it below. Each storm pressed the two sides a little farther apart.

Nothing living could reach its deepest portion. Nothing at the surface showed how far it had gone.

The power followed it outward.

Beyond the rim, clouds crossed the pale wall. Far below, water fell in threads that vanished into mist. A section of that wall had begun to lean away from the basin. If it opened, the water would follow it. So would the chambers, the colonies, the swimmers, the bodies still laying down thin rims in the dark.

Here the power did more than move a few grains.

It held the wall.

While it held, it brought the old shell surfaces together deep within the fracture. It left the shallow cracks open, with their scavengers and trapped sediment. Below them, where there were no bodies, it joined pearly layer to pearly layer. The load spread gradually into the surrounding terraces.

For several days a low vibration traveled through the basin.

The grazers stopped in their grooves. Feeding fans withdrew. A large plated animal gathered itself and waited beneath an overhang.

Then the vibration ceased.

The water remained high above the world.

***

There were other things the power might have changed.

It watched a flat hunter enclose a grazer. It felt the grazer’s movements against the curved plates around its body.

This was harder to witness than the fracture.

The power stayed close. It did not make the grazer unaware, and it did not call the struggle harmless. Yet it could see no simple alteration that would feed the hunter without taking some other life. Beneath the ledges, transparent bodies were drawing smaller transparent bodies into their mouths. Within the great domes, particles passed over living folds, and some of those particles were animals.

It continued looking.

It made no decree that hunger was good. It did not close the hunter’s mouth and leave it to starve.

Elsewhere, a current was stripping a colony from a ceiling. The power loosened a lodged plate farther upstream. Water took the wider passage, and the strain eased. Most of the colony held. Those already torn free drifted downward; several found purchase on a broken chamber.

The power learned to distinguish what it understood from what it merely had the strength to change.

***

Under the fallen arch, the small builder began its collar.

The first new shell was so thin that light passed through it. Its edge followed the irregularities beneath the foot, crossing a ridge, dipping into a shallow pit. Fine particles gathered beside it. A many-legged animal came to brush them away, then moved on.

The power did not hasten the growth.

When water brought food, the valves opened. When a shadow passed, they closed. Once, for nearly a day, the animal remained shut while cold water descended from the saddle.

It was not safe from everything. It had a place that held.

In time, its rim would become visible without close attention. Other young might settle beside it. Or a hunter might find it; or the growing shell might outlast its maker and shelter another body.

The power remained attentive to those possibilities without choosing one and forcing it to happen.

Above, overflow slid across the rounded lip. Below, fans opened along the edges of old chambers.

And whenever the little foot tested the shell beneath it, the shell was there.
