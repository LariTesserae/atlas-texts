The power noticed the chamber first by the work of holding it open.

There was the weight of the salt, and beneath it the slow insistence of the roots. There were insulated vessels narrower than thread, each carrying its share of warmth through the dark. There were arches that would take thirty years to become useful, and repairs that would fail before the next working period unless someone reached them with a scraper and oil.

It could have lifted the salt away.

It considered the result: the loss of pressure, the sudden change in temperature, the exposed respiratory folds, the ceiling growths burning pale beneath an unfamiliar light. Much that was beautiful elsewhere would be a catastrophe here.

It left the salt where it was.

For a time, it did nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

***

Eren first encountered it in the additional room.

She had gone in to fetch a crate. Her lamp was on its lowest setting, and its light showed a narrow separation between two paving scales near the far wall.

The room had been troublesome for nine years. A new separation was not remarkable. She set down the crate, knelt on her insulating cloth, and put the listening disc against the floor.

There was no sound.

Not an absence of audible sound. The disc itself had stopped giving back the small trembling of her hand.

She lifted it and inspected the rim.

“I have only the one,” she said.

The trembling returned.

After a moment she laid the disc down again.

Something attended to her from the other side of the floor. She could not locate it. It was neither large nor small in any useful sense.

Eren kept her lamp low.

“If this is about the ventilation,” she said, “I have reported it.”

The power had prepared no greeting. It had observed that arrivals were generally announced with lamps, but a lamp would have meant warmth, and the room was already badly trained.

Instead, words appeared in the pale grain between the scales.

**May I ask you something?**

Eren looked at them for a long time.

“Not by cutting into the floor.”

The words disappeared. A moment later she heard them, quietly, from the space above her listening disc.

“May I ask you something?”

She sat back on her heels.

“Yes.”

“What is hardest to keep safe?”

She might have said the bridge. Its southern abutment had begun contracting unevenly. She might have said children, or the outer ceiling crews, or the old supply sheaths whose plans disagreed with the walls.

But the question had come while she was kneeling in a room she could not use, at the end of a working period that had already been extended twice.

“Someone who cannot get up,” she said.

The power waited.

Eren rubbed the polished skin at the base of her thumb.

“You can arrange visits. You can leave a lamp. But lamps fail, and people are delayed. Then the room starts.”

“What should the room do instead?”

“How would I know? I maintain them. I don’t breed them.”

The voice remained quiet.

“Would you show me?”

***

She did not take it to a sickroom immediately.

First she took it to the tool store, where she spoke to an empty patch of air until she felt foolish, then stopped. It did not follow her upstairs without permission. When she asked whether it was still there, it answered.

She asked several other questions.

Could it stop a ceiling fall? Yes.

Had it caused the separation in her floor? No.

Would it mend the bridge? It would first need to understand which movements were damage and which were necessary.

Could anyone else hear it?

“If you wish.”

Eren fetched the maintenance supervisor.

The supervisor listened, asked it to repeat its account, and sent for two more people. One brought a tension meter. The other brought the old district plans.

By the time the power was shown a sickroom, eleven days had passed.

It did not appear to consider this an unreasonable delay.

***

The woman in the bed was called Sava. She had been a cable sorter until her hands lost the strength to separate fine strands.

Two neighbors were sitting beside her. One was repairing a fastening. The other had brought an account of a freight dispute, but Sava had asked him to stop reading it.

Eren explained the visitor.

Sava looked toward the place Eren indicated.

“Can it fix my hands?”

The power examined the damaged sheaths, the accumulated crystals, the little pathways that no longer carried their signals reliably.

“Some of the damage,” it said. “Not without discomfort. I would need your permission to begin, and you could ask me to stop.”

Sava considered this.

“Tomorrow. I have had enough done to me today.”

“Tomorrow,” said the power.

Then it asked permission to attend to the room.

***

The first change was made to one sleeping chamber.

Nothing visible happened. No wall acquired a face. No door learned to speak.

The power helped the tissue around the ventilation pores distinguish the small, irregular warmth of a resting body from the steady warmth of an unattended lamp. It taught the doorway to retain its shape while that warmth remained. It left the room’s other adjustments alone.

Eren insisted on a second signal. A parcel might stay warm for a long time.

They used the slow exchange of gases through the respiratory folds.

For twelve working periods, the neighbors continued their visits. The lamp remained in its niche. Eren took readings, complained about the position of the pores, and made the power explain each alteration to a grower who distrusted anything he could not reproduce.

The room held steady.

On the thirteenth period they covered the lamp.

Sava slept. The doorway did not tighten.

Her neighbor still came in the evening. She sat beside the bed with a bowl on an insulated tray and described an argument in the meal shop.

She had brought no sewing.

***

The grower could reproduce the change, though not quickly.

That pleased the power more than Eren expected.

It helped him prepare a graft for sleeping-room walls. The graft was tested in empty rooms first, with warm parcels, gas feeds, open windows, blocked pores, and a bed occupied by the supervisor, who found the testing schedule inconvenient.

There were failures.

One room mistook three browsers in a ventilation channel for an occupant. Another held its door open rather than merely keeping it from closing. The power could have corrected every room itself, but the grower wanted to know why they had failed.

So they worked through the failures.

Eventually there was a modification that maintenance crews could install with familiar tools. It required feed, labor, and two visits. Buildings did not have to be replaced.

No one stopped tending the sick. Some came less often because they were tired, and some came more willingly because a visit no longer meant taking responsibility for the walls.

Eren thought both results worth recording.

***

There were other requests.

Some were urgent. The power supported a damaged ceiling rib while a crew got out from beneath it. It eased the pressure around a trapped supply sheath without tearing the roots that held the surrounding district up.

Some requests it could not settle.

It would not decide how many platforms the new station should have. It could show the planners where their estimates depended on uncertain assumptions. It could help them reserve a connection without feeding an entire unused hall. They still argued.

It would not make the cleaning animals stay out of bedrooms forever. They needed routes through unhealthy tissue. It helped the workers grow accessible bypasses instead.

When asked to make the city absorb only things that had been abandoned, it asked how abandonment could reliably be recognized.

No one had a satisfactory answer.

For loose shoes, they continued to use scrapers.

***

Sava recovered enough movement in her hands to repair large fastenings. The finer work remained difficult. On bad days she asked the power to leave them alone.

It did.

Eren’s additional room was finished by ordinary workers the following season. The ventilation had to be moved, and one wall required retraining. She paid her share and objected to the charge for moving the crates.

Later, at the station site, she stood beside the meticulous worker and watched a branch open its searching lobes.

The power was there because they had invited it to inspect a doubtful section of roof. It held no lamp and gave off no heat.

The worker moved back. Eren moved her lamp with him.

The branch tested the cooling air, withdrew, and turned toward the position marked by the survey ring.

Above them, a grain of salt loosened.

The power knew where it would fall. It knew the roof was sound and that the grain would strike bare pavement.

It let it fall.

The tiny tick was plainly audible in the quiet.

“Good,” said the worker, studying the branch.

Eren checked the time on her lamp. She had a bridge to attend to before going home.

The power asked whether she wanted it to come.

“Not today,” she said. “I’ll call if we need you.”

It stayed out of her way.
