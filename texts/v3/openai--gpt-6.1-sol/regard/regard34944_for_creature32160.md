The power did not mistake the white islands for something finished.

It watched a passage narrow beneath the pipe. Water that had entered there in the morning began to take another route. Under the closing roof, the animal loosened its hold, gathered its thin folds, and moved toward the new edge. The chamber it left behind remained wet.

There was no need to keep that chamber open.

The power watched the drain longer.

Below the grille, the first pipe descended sharply and then turned beneath the building. At the bend, mineral had accumulated around a rag caught on a rough joint. Water still passed, but the opening was smaller than it had been. During heavy rain, the pipe filled. Pressure traveled back toward the hollow, lifting water against the steps.

Soon someone would come with a steel rod. If that failed, they would bring something in a plastic container and pour it into the basin.

The power followed the pipe through the wall and beneath the service road. It followed the warm water into darkness, where some of the animal’s detached pieces had settled. Most had died. Two had caught in a leaking joint. One had spread across the underside of an inspection chamber, in a place where a cooler trickle crossed the warm discharge.

Farther on, the water entered a ditch. Beyond the ditch lay roots, eggs, mouths, other ways of being alive.

It attended to those as well.

Then it made a small change.

The rag at the bend loosened. One thread broke, then another. The next pulse of water folded it lengthwise and carried it through to a catch basket farther down, where a worker could remove it without entering the hollow.

The water above the grille fell by less than the thickness of a coin.

Under an island, an opening widened.

That was all the animal knew of the first intervention.

***

The pumping house needed repairs.

One bearing had begun to wear. The loose guard panel would eventually break at its fastening. The insulation around the discharge pipe no longer protected a hand laid against it. None of these things had yet made the list of work that could not wait.

The power did not stop the pumps. Other lives depended on what they moved.

Instead, when the maintenance worker arrived the following morning, the panel knocked at a moment when the higher whine briefly diminished. He heard the irregularity. He put down his bucket and listened.

Later, crouching to check the outlet, he rested two fingers on the pale terrace.

The power kept those fingers from the animal by no more than a small reluctance in the wrist: the ordinary decision to touch here rather than there.

Beneath the next lip, a grey margin withdrew.

He saw it.

He waited until it came out again.

His first thought was that something oily had collected under the crust. But the water was moving one way and the narrow edge was moving another. He shifted his boot. The edge closed.

He did not break off a piece.

On the work order, below the bearing number and the note about the panel, he wrote:

*Something living in sump. Check before cleaning.*

The words were not accurate enough to explain what he had seen. They were sufficient to delay the plastic container.

***

Attention arrived slowly, in people with different kinds of knowledge.

One knew the pumping system. One knew mineral deposits. One had spent years looking at lives that scarcely showed themselves.

They disagreed about the grey seams. They disagreed about whether the portions were connected. Their instruments produced results that required more work.

The power allowed the uncertainty to remain.

A very small sample was taken from an exposed margin. The place it left closed over. Another sample came from the brown patches within it. Water was collected from the fissure, the bank, the outlet, and the drain.

Before any repairs began, someone measured how quickly the hollow cooled when the discharge stopped.

This mattered more than a name.

The bearing was replaced. The guard panel was secured. The old pipe received a sound covering, and its outlet was left at the same height. A removable screen was fitted downstream, where it could be cleaned without scraping the inhabited grille.

When the hollow needed attention, it was done in portions. There was always water passing through some part of it.

No one made the basin pristine.

***

The power did not soften every rain.

The animal still thickened beneath its roofs when cold runoff reached it. Occasionally a cloudy portion separated, and the surviving body closed the break. The mineral covered what remained.

Nor did the power ensure that every fragment taken by the drain found a home. A few established themselves in sheltered places within the warm system. Others passed beyond the conditions they required.

At the ditch, the reeds continued to grow. Small insects worked the surface. A bird drank where the water had cooled.

The power watched these boundaries with the same care it gave the little flap in the shade.

Inside the hollow, a new passage opened beside a ladder foot. The thread in the mortar joint advanced and then stopped. The strongest folds remained beneath the grille.

On a dry afternoon, the maintenance worker came back without his tools.

He stood on the upper step until his weight had ceased to be a new disturbance. Then he crouched, keeping his hands on his knees.

The flap over the brick fragment emerged. Its clear edge settled across the current. A fleck of rust rested against it.

Nothing approached him. Nothing offered thanks.

He stayed long enough to see the fleck released.

Above his shoulder, the repaired panel was quiet. The pumps continued their necessary work. Warm water entered through the fissure, crossed the growing floor, and left by the drain.

The power remained attentive as the next drop gathered on the rim.
