The power first notices the forefoot.

Not the distances folded beneath the plain, nor the root tips with their impossible interiors. These are intricate, but they are not asking for anything.

The forefoot is still raised in the distant grove. Its last lobe has only just left your hand.

For a little while, the animal holds it above the ground.

Then it sets it down and passes another limb over the underside of its head.

The power watches until it has some understanding of what that gesture does. It does not mistake the interval for longing.

***

There are many things it could change.

It could bring water to the basin. The pale floor would darken; the stems would bend beneath the unfamiliar weight. There could be a reflection of the sky where now there is only stone.

But the basin is not thirsty.

It could send wind through the groves, round the mineral edges, persuade the half-open flowers to open fully. It could establish a sequence of mornings and evenings, provide the long preparation of a season.

Instead, it attends to the flower.

The narrow gap between its petals is not an interrupted motion. Nothing within it strains toward a wider opening. Small agreements pass through its tissue, maintaining a condition as complete as the spread of the mature leaves beside it.

The power leaves the flower alone.

It also leaves the flaws.

At first, these are the places that most readily attract its care: the hesitant gold line, the twice-sounded note, the little mismatch where a root occupies two deposits and agrees entirely with neither.

Then the broad animal lowers its mouth to a stem.

The power watches the fullness gather beneath its skin. It follows the violet lines without straightening them. Later, it watches the animal settle its belly against the plates and return something to the ground.

Afterward, the power no longer considers making the plain flawless.

***

You eventually stand.

The animal notices the change through an opening beside a trunk. One clear surface in its eye holds the basin; another holds the space beneath your departing heel.

It does not follow.

Your steps take you across the clearing. For a while, the basin remains nearer than it ought to. Then the leaves cease to frame it, and it is behind you.

The power stays.

It has no body that the animal could usefully examine. This seems worth correcting.

In a bare place beside the basin, it makes a small surface.

The surface is neither bright nor hidden. It has the cream color of the ground, but its edge is easy to distinguish. There are no corners that catch a foot, no warmth different from the warmth around it. It lies a little above the stone, leaving a shallow space underneath.

Nothing in the plain has to sustain it.

The power waits.

A six-legged creature comes near, turns toward it without turning, and is elsewhere.

Much later, the broad animal returns through the low arch between the stems.

It rests first. One limb lies along the rim. Its head is angled so that the new surface appears in two of its clear eye fields.

The power does not move the surface closer.

When the animal approaches, it does so by way of a broad leaf. A flattened foot touches the stem. The animal waits, withdraws, and comes around the other side.

It places one lobe against the unfamiliar edge.

There is a slight tension in the touch.

The surface yields.

Not much. Enough that the animal can feel its own pressure making a difference.

It withdraws the lobe, and the surface returns to its former shape.

The animal tests it again.

***

The power learns slowly on purpose.

It could infer the animal’s whole structure from one contact. It could arrange a thousand objects precisely suited to every possible motion of its limbs.

But the first surface is enough to begin with.

The animal presses along one edge, then another. It reaches underneath. Two limbs remain folded against its sides while a third explores the shallow space.

Once, the power yields too readily.

The animal withdraws all three lobes and lowers its head.

The surface becomes steady again.

There is a long interval before the next touch.

Nothing urges the animal to shorten it.

***

Over time, the surface acquires a few possibilities.

It can make a low arch, if the animal lifts it. It can hold that arch until touched again. Pressure along one side brings the opposite side nearer, but never so near that a limb is enclosed.

The animal sometimes uses it. Sometimes it passes without stopping. Once it places a copper filament underneath and leaves it there.

The power leaves the filament too.

It does not turn these visits into feeding. It does not make itself necessary to the roots, or easier to find than the animal’s accustomed resting places. When the animal goes elsewhere, there is no faint signal calling it back.

There are stretches in which the little surface rests undisturbed beside the basin.

During one of them, the power attends to a distant junction whose two clear notes have begun to interfere with an opening. It holds the passage steady while a small creature feeds there, then releases it.

The creature departs. The junction sounds once.

***

Eventually, the basin animal lies beside the surface without examining it.

Its back is full on one side. It has recently fed, and its eye fields turn downward.

One forelimb extends across the pale edge.

The power gives beneath it, very slightly.

The three lobes loosen.

Nothing else is required at that moment. The power remains attentive to their weight, to the leaf above them, and to the opening through which the animal will leave when it is ready.
