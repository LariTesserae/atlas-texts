The power did not lift the stone.

It could have done so without disturbing a grain of dust. But the weight of the stone belonged to the city. So did its shade, the stored warmth in its underside, and the narrow differences between one crack and another. Removing it would have been less like opening a lid than taking away a season.

Instead, the power waited at the broken corner.

It listened through a morning, a noon, an afternoon. At first it heard a multitude of sounds. Later it began to hear places: the workshop in which a shutter dragged, the wall behind which a stem was gathering tension, the cool interval beneath the porch.

It could hear the creature’s sixth foot against the step.

That contact was so slight that even the step scarcely received it. Yet the creature changed its position when the humming changed. Once, without waking fully, it lifted its body clear of a grain that had rolled beneath it.

The power waited longer.

It watched the smaller creature feed and return. It watched the yellow one descend around the post, finding holds with its damaged foot. It watched the unopened plate above the resting place. What happened within was not entirely hidden from it, but neither was it something to hurry.

The plate was thinning along one edge.

The young creature would have to open that edge itself.

For a time, the power considered the square. It would have been easy to quiet the drafts there, to make a broad safe crossing where the smaller creature had left its three scratches.

Then a seed blew out of a roof garden.

It crossed the square in three short flights, stopped beside a kiln, and disappeared into a fault.

The power left the square alone.

There were other crossings, though not all of them were usable yet. Beneath the covered passage, a flake of plaster had loosened. It hung by a narrow attachment above a gap in the masonry. The smaller creature could stretch across that gap; the yellow one could not. Both had approached it on different days and turned aside.

The power touched the flake.

It fell slowly, taking the path it might have taken in some later season, and lodged between the two edges. The hollow gave a brief, sharp tick. Under the porch, the pale creature lowered its raised foot.

The power waited until the usual humming returned.

Then it tested the fallen plaster with no more force than a foot would exert. It held. Air still passed beneath it. No root had been covered.

That was the first change inside the city.

The second was above it.

Late one morning, someone crossed the sun court carrying a basket. One sandal came down beside the worn stone. The stone shifted almost imperceptibly, pressing a roof below closer to the leaves.

The person continued walking.

The power followed the pressure through the pale mineral. There was an old settling beneath one edge, a space where the support had crumbled. It gathered the loose grains there and fitted them together. It did not seal the hairline faults. It did not straighten the stone or close its broken corner.

When the person returned, the stone stayed still.

In the court, beside that corner, the power made a small mark: a hollow with a raised rim, plainly deliberate. Someone cleaning the paving would find it. Someone curious might kneel.

What they would do afterward was not certain.

The power could have concealed the whole place. Instead it made the mark a little clearer, and left enough room beside it for a person to kneel without putting weight on the stone.

Below, the city passed into afternoon.

The closed plate quivered.

The creature under the porch turned and pressed its mouth to the edge. It remained there for a while, then climbed the root and withdrew into a fold of shadow.

A slit appeared in the plate.

The power stayed very still.

One fine limb came through, found nothing, and folded back. Two others followed. The plate opened farther. The body within turned against it, awkwardly, until a hooked foot caught the white root.

It emerged slowly.

For some time it did not move from the torn plate. Its limbs unfolded in an order that seemed troublesome. One caught beneath another. A tremor went through its back, too coarse yet to shake off the dust.

The older creature did not approach.

Above the porch, a shutter lifted. The humming deepened.

The young one put a foot against the root.

There was food within reach of the recess, on a joint behind the lowest step. The power had noticed it days before: a little amber, shaded and softer than the deposits on the roof. Between it and the torn plate lay a curl of dead leaf.

The young creature encountered the curl, climbed halfway onto it, and slipped.

The power almost moved the leaf.

But the creature had caught itself. Three feet held the root. Another searched along the leaf’s dark seam and found an edge.

It tried again.

By evening it had reached the amber.

The power remained until it had taken its first small, uneven bite.

Then, over several days, it withdrew from the hollow. It left the plaster bridge, the support beneath the paving stone, and the mark in the court. Nothing else had been added.

On its last afternoon beside the city, the pale creature stopped at the workbench.

Air was moving through the repaired mouthpiece. The creature placed a foot on its lip, and the pitch changed.

It lifted the foot.

After a pause, it set it down in a slightly different place.

The power listened without touching anything.

Under the porch, the young creature was finding a place to rest. Five feet settled against the root. The sixth remained searching for a little longer, then reached the underside of the step.
