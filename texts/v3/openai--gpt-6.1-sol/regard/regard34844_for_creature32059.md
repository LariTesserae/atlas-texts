The power first notices the woman because she is kneeling beside a pool she has already condemned.

No one is arguing with her now. The dark pebbles have been taken away. Her apprentices have gone uphill with the water vessels.

She stays because small animals are still coming to drink.

They approach from the dry side, where the crust gives no warning. Some retreat after touching the water. Others drink and become still.

She lays a broken roof plate across the easiest approach. It will not stop them all. She knows this. She goes to fetch another.

The power has seen enough worlds to recognize a kindness that has become an additional task.

It does not mistake her for the only being in the valley that matters.

There is the grazer lying beyond the pool, unable to rise under the weight of her own mineral ridges. There are the comb-limbed creatures beneath the roof plate. There is a hunter waiting for the woman to leave. Farther uphill, her son is carrying a vessel badly because he will not admit that his wrists hurt.

The power attends to these things together.

For a while, it does nothing.

It follows the water down through the stone.

Below the inhabited shelves are chambers under pressure, old faults, layers laid down in darkness. The luminous roof is not a sky that can simply be lifted away. The springs cannot all be quieted without starving the films that feed the grazers. Even the bitter water has creatures suited to it.

There are changes it could make without difficulty that would take a generation to reveal their cruelty.

So it returns to the woman.

She has put down the second plate. Sweat runs along the pale dust on her shins.

Beside her hand, on a patch of dry crust, a little water appears.

She draws back.

The water does not spread. It stands in a clear bead, and in the bead is something she cannot account for: her own face, seen from directly in front of her.

“What is that?” she says.

The answer comes without sound, but not without permission. She feels it waiting at the edge of her attention, and allows it nearer.

*Something that would like to help.*

She looks toward the houses.

“With what?”

*I have not decided.*

This reassures her more than a promise would have.

She sits on her heels. After a moment, she takes one of the cups from her waist and holds it beneath the bead.

“Then begin by telling me whether this is drinking water.”

It is.

She tests it anyway.

***

Their first conversation lasts until the bell rings.

The woman gets up at once. Whatever has come to speak to her can wait; the people in the low houses cannot.

The power goes with her, and sees how much knowledge is already present in the village. A child opens a pen without standing in front of the gate. Two neighbors who have not spoken for days lift the same roof panel. The bell keeper goes back for a man whose legs no longer straighten.

It also sees what knowledge cannot remedy.

There is only one sound crossing to the upper shelf. The oldest water vessel takes four hands to move safely, and those hands are needed elsewhere. A roof panel falls and breaks while its owners are carrying it. Someone must leave behind the fuel that was supposed to last through the next wet interval.

That night, the woman sits among the salvaged things.

“Earlier,” she says.

The power waits.

“If we had known earlier, we could have carried more. Not always. But today.”

*How much earlier?*

She does not answer quickly.

***

The changes begin beneath the valley, where nobody can see them.

They are small at first. The power works among faults and buried chambers, making paths by which pressure can announce itself before it breaks the inhabited surface.

It does not use a sign only the woman can perceive.

Along the margins of the rising ground, certain seams begin to click. Slowly, at first, with long pauses between them. Then more often. The sound travels through bare soles and through the legs of houses. Animals can hear it too.

The bell keeper spends weeks learning the difference between this new sound and the old ones. The woman’s son proves particularly good at it. He remembers sequences, intervals, which distant cracks answered which others.

His mother begins asking him what he thinks.

The warning is not infallible. The power says so, and makes the new seams distinctive enough that nobody has to guess whether an ordinary noise is a promise.

Usually, there is time to move the fuel.

***

The woman asks for water next.

Not a spring in the village center. She knows what gathers around a spring.

Together they choose several places above the busiest herd passages. The power opens modest seeps there, fed from water that has cooled and left its dangerous salts farther below. Each has an overflow into an existing channel. None is warm enough to tempt a pressure crust into growing rapidly around it.

The woman checks them every day.

Later, every few days.

There are pools for drinking and broad shallow basins where people can wash without fouling them. The dark pebbles still have to be renewed. Bladders still have to be carried and tended. But a sealed spring no longer means that a household must choose between thirst and water it fears.

When the settlements farther away hear of the seeps, the power helps them find places of their own.

It asks the people who know those places where the herds pass, where the young hunters shelter, where a new channel would cut off a neighbor.

Sometimes it is told no.

It goes elsewhere.

***

Not every help becomes part of the landscape.

The old grazer beside the bitter pool wakes one morning with a long mineral ridge lying broken next to her. Her back is tender where it has come away. She rests through the hottest hours, then rises.

The small creatures that lived in the ridge remain within it. Water gathers in its hollows. It becomes a low, crowded island.

Under a nesting ledge, a fallen young winged animal catches on a fold of hide that no one remembers spreading so far.

A hunter with a damaged mouth finds soft prey for several days. It heals and returns to hunting. The power does not require gratitude, or expect the prey to share it.

It cannot give every creature an untroubled life while leaving all the others theirs. Where it can lessen pain, it does. Where it does not yet know how, it stays attentive.

***

The woman does not become a priest.

She has more time, which she uses partly for teaching and partly for sitting with her feet in cool water. Her hair remains white at the temples. Her dark tooth still troubles her until she asks whether that, too, is something the power can help with.

It is.

Years later, an apprentice asks what she requested when it first came.

“Water,” she says.

“And after that?”

She thinks about the warning seams, the sound crossings, the households now able to keep stores through a bad season.

“Time enough to do what we knew how to do.”

Below them, a fresh crack opens in the plain. Mouths gather at the first beads of water.

The valley is still becoming.

But on a shelf above the flood lanes, roof plates lie stacked beside full vessels. Children are sorting the plates by size. No one is hurrying them.
