The power does not enter through the rim.

An arrival there would make a wake. The clear-jointed things would gather in it, the nearest chamber would inhale, and whatever the power intended would become, first, a meal or a collision.

Instead it attends to the basin from within an interval between blows.

It makes the interval no longer. The amber bulbs brighten in their accustomed order. The pale creature closes its underside. Beneath the floor, the pleated tissue draws tight.

The power waits.

Through many such intervals it learns which injuries belong to living here. A fin taken into a wall is not always a fin lost. A chamber growing smaller is not necessarily being crushed. The breaking floor makes places for the amber young; the floor’s consumption keeps those places from being buried. Even the clear knuckles need the violence. Where stone comes apart, they assemble.

Still, not everything that happens here is necessary.

Below the northern cleft, two deep fractures have begun to meet. When they join, a broad piece of the mountain will turn inward. The older chamber will go with it. So will the ledge, the bulbs, and a length of the low inhabitant whose tissue has grown thin around an inclusion of cold, unyielding stone.

The basin has survived such losses before.

This does not make the loss small.

The power attends more closely to the broad body beneath the floor. It finds no single place where the body looks outward. Its awareness, if that is what these responses are, lies in many contacts: the pull of chamber-strands, the weight of settled grit, the warmth of a crack opening above it. Across great distances, its contractions arrive late.

The power does not gather those responses into a voice.

It touches the hard inclusion with a warmth no greater than the warmth already passing through the tissue.

The tissue closes around it.

The power withdraws.

Several blows later, it offers the warmth again, this time along one edge. A narrow fold loosens there. The inclusion shifts by less than the thickness of a mineral shaving.

That is enough to begin.

Over the following cycles, the power keeps that small route open. It does not pull the stone through the body. It supports the adjacent floor while the body loosens, contracts, rests, and loosens again. Dust arrives. Pebbles are consumed. A pale creature feeds overhead, unaware that the rounded place beneath it is moving.

At last a dark stone emerges through a crack.

It lies on the floor with a fringe of clear knuckles flashing along its upper face. At the next concussion it breaks into three pieces. The smallest is taken into a chamber joint. The others slide eastward.

Below, the pleats close without the old sharp kink.

The power turns toward the northern cleft.

It could hold the mountain still. But the stone continually becoming stone would press against whatever held it. The pressure would find another route, perhaps through the chambers, perhaps through the broad body itself. A lasting stillness would require changes the power has not yet understood.

So it leaves the mountain its movement.

At the point where the two fractures are approaching, it makes a seam of stone slightly softer than the rest.

The next boom opens that seam.

A long, narrow shard rises from the floor, turns once, and falls across the cleft. Water drives through the gap beneath it. The shard cracks, settles, and cracks again. Each break takes a little of the strain that would have turned the great slab inward.

The northern roar changes pitch.

The older chamber spreads its upper stones. One of its blind passages opens, and a small pale body comes out farther than any of the others have come. It has two soft margins that are not yet fins.

A current takes it.

The power follows, but does not close a hand around it.

Ahead, water is folding against a sharp ridge. The power blunts one projecting grain. The youngster strikes the ridge, flattens, and catches in a shallow hollow. There it remains through the next blow.

Afterward, one margin has become a fin.

The power ceases attending to that particular hollow. The youngster will need to leave it. There are cracks nearby, and currents to test.

Beneath the ledge, a bubble sinks with a curved amber strip turning inside it. The bubble collapses. The strip lands beside new stone, uncurls, and begins to work its way under a flake.

The next concussion lifts the flake.

The power lets it rise, but slows its turning. The strip slips beneath the sheltered edge and adheres. A minute amber blister remains after the dust has passed.

Not all the strips receive such help. The power watches where they land, where their bodies fail, where the floor closes too soon. Its attention grows more exact. There are places where a grain held briefly against another grain gives a young body enough time. Elsewhere, the same shelter would leave it trapped.

Nothing in the basin becomes quiet.

The smallest chamber continues to contract. The green straps cast their beads into darkness. Fins enter walls, and creatures emerge altered, or do not emerge. Clear joints gather behind every falling fragment. The low body consumes the floor’s burdens and lays down fresh stone.

But the northern cleft no longer carries that deep, converging strain. Beneath the floor, one long contraction travels without snagging. Under a ledge, another bulb begins to swell.

The power remains.

Its care is not visible as a new thing in the hollow. It can be found, sometimes, in the way two stones come to rest: a little space left between them, still open after the blow.
