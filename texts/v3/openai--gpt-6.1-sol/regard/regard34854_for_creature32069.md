The power first attended to the water beyond the chamber.

It followed the running pump’s discharge through bends and valves, into places where the concrete was newer and the noise had a different pitch. It found the motor. It found the sea pressing against the wall, and the rainwater gathering on the other side. Above the sealed doorway were rooms with dry floors, tools hung on boards, a kettle with a damaged handle. Farther inland, people slept below the level of the tide.

Stopping the pump would make the chamber gentler for a little while.

The power left it running.

Then it attended to the octopus.

There was no need to draw the animal out. Through the thick casting, it could perceive the mantle’s movement, the several points of attachment, the small adjustments by which a soft body remained secure in moving water. It followed the old injury into the growing arm tip. There was no infection there. New tissue was forming at its own pace.

The power left that pace alone.

It stayed through several changes of load.

At first, the chamber seemed to offer the octopus little beyond endurance. But closer attention disclosed distinctions. One stretch of floor held well under the suckers; another looked similar and did not. The rear fracture carried water differently from the shaft bore. A crab in a gauge cavity could sometimes be reached from above. The animal knew these things separately, through encounters, and made use of them without needing the whole room to become safe.

The power watched it spend a long time with the loose washer.

There was no food beneath it. The washer turned slightly on its bolt, then caught, then turned back. The octopus tried it with another arm.

Afterward, the power examined the running pump.

A bearing beyond the wall had begun to wear unevenly. The valve’s blows were becoming harder. During the strongest draw, the grille pulled water through too few openings because old debris had packed against its underside. The sensor recorded the consequences, but the records were reaching a system that no one had looked at for some time.

Here the power acted.

The records arrived where they could be understood. A fault that had appeared intermittent became legible. During the next maintenance shift, someone listening to the motor heard the damaged bearing clearly and called another worker over.

Above the chamber, tools were brought out.

The repair was scheduled around the tide. It took hours. The octopus remained in its casing while unfamiliar vibrations passed through the wall.

When the pump resumed its ordinary work, the low note was still there. Water still moved toward the grille. But the rise in force came more gradually, and the valve no longer struck three times in quick succession.

The chain continued to touch the pipe.

The power had considered silencing it, then noticed how often its movement announced a change before the suspended particles did. It shortened only the loose end that could reach the floor and catch around an arm.

The octopus stayed hidden through the first repaired cycle. Through the second, too.

On the third, it put an arm into the shaft bore while the current was still easing.

There was also work to do at the dead pump.

A crack beneath its plinth was widening. In time, the housing would settle toward the wall. The rear exit would narrow first; later, a part of the casting might break.

The power secured the foundation from below. Inside the housing, nothing shifted. The ribs remained rough, the exits remained open, and the rusted lip retained its familiar shape.

It loosened no shell that was serving as shelter. It cleared no surface merely because it was encrusted.

Beside one pier, where a fallen slab had left a narrow space beneath it, the power made the space a little deeper. There were two ways in, neither facing the grille. A crab found it before the octopus did.

For some nights the crab held the nearer entrance with both claws raised. Later it moved elsewhere. The octopus discovered the hollow while following a small trail of disturbed sediment, entered partway, and backed out. It returned on another night and remained until the load changed.

The power did not make the hollow its new home. The animal still preferred the pump.

Nor did it provide a heap of food at the flange. Shrimp continued to withdraw beneath the panel. Crabs continued to brace in their holes. Some hunts failed. Others ended with remains carried back to the casing.

The power attended to those lives as well. Its care for the octopus did not make them into objects placed in the room for its benefit.

It kept the passages through which new water and small animals entered from becoming sealed by neglect. It prevented a slow leak of machine oil beyond the wall from reaching the chamber. Those changes served many creatures, most of which it would never distinguish by name.

Then it waited to see what its work had done.

The sediment still lifted when the pump drew harder, but no longer in such dense sheets. Fish ventured out sooner. The little olive fringe beneath the lamp trembled as before. The octopus crossed the slope once, stopped to test the water, and continued.

At the sensor, it found the black cable unchanged.

An arm curled around a clean clamp. The sucker held, released, and held again. The red light flashed against pale skin.

The power was close enough to be perceived, had it chosen to make itself so. It could have filled the water with light, offered a moving shape, elicited an approach.

Instead, it let the clamp remain a clamp.

The octopus pulled at the slack, found the same resistance as before, and passed on.

By the sealed doorway, it reached along the faint curve of the bottom stair. One arm found a recess. Another rested on concrete. The shortened arm extended its new tip, touched the floor, and withdrew.

Nothing required the animal to understand why the room had become a little less dangerous.

When it returned to the casing, a small fish was holding outside the flange. The octopus paused behind it, one eye visible. After a while the fish moved aside.

The animal entered, attached itself among the ribs, and began to rest.

Through the wall came the sound of water being moved for lives elsewhere.

The old housing held.
