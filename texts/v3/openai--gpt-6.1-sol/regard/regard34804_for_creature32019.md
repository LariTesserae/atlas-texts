The power did not arrive as weather.

There was no brightening of the sky, no unusual warmth. The gardens did not open out of season. Nothing in the high country had to interrupt its life to receive a visitor.

For a long time, the power watched.

It watched long enough to distinguish the folding of a leaf from the withdrawal of a whole garden. Long enough to follow a winged animal beyond the rim of a basin, through two days of suspended dust, to the underside of a cushion where its wings came away.

It learned what the bitter vapor nourished. It learned what rain would destroy.

And beneath a fallen slab, it found the old being.

The cushion had spread on either side of the stone. Aboveground, the two portions were different colors: one silver-gray with exposure, the other almost black beneath overlapping fronds. Below, three living passages still joined them.

A change traveled through those passages.

On the western side, a feeding seam was diminishing. Leaves there had begun to tighten before the afternoon wind arrived. Days later, the eastern side opened more widely. Sap moved toward the failing portion, carrying compounds gathered elsewhere.

The power followed this exchange through many seasons.

It did not mistake slowness for emptiness. Nor did it fill that slowness with a voice resembling its own. Whatever experience belonged to the cushion was bound up with folds, pressures, the arrival of nourishment, the condition of distant living parts. The power approached it there.

At first, approaching meant only understanding what its presence might disturb.

Even a kindly shadow would change the leaves beneath it. Warmth could wake the tunnel dwellers at the wrong time. An abundance of food could bring grazers into a hollow that could not shelter them through the cold.

So the power remained without casting a shadow, and attended to the three passages beneath the slab.

One was narrowing.

The slab had settled by less than the width of a root. It would settle farther when the next layer of mineral beneath it crumbled. Two passages would be crushed. The third, exposed along the broken edge, would dry.

The cushion had survived divisions before. Each surviving portion would continue, if it could. But the failing western side was still being supplied from the east, and there was no other way through the stone.

The power considered lifting the slab.

It could have lifted the entire escarpment. It could have opened rich seams under every exhausted garden and held them open forever.

Instead, it learned the weight of this one stone.

There were tunnels beneath its northern edge. Several small creatures had lined a chamber there with wax stripped from old leaves. Under its southern edge, a grazer had spent three nights resting. Along the upper surface grew a pale fan whose roots entered a crack no wider than a thread.

The power left all these places undisturbed.

Farther down, where the slab’s weight met sound bedrock, it changed a small patch of mineral. The patch became a little harder, a little less inclined to crumble.

The stone settled as stones do.

But it settled no farther.

***

For several days, nothing visible happened.

Dust accumulated in the cushion’s folds. A predator turned with the blade to which it clung. The grazer left at dusk, taking beneath its plates a few reproductive bodies and one unwelcome passenger.

Under the slab, sap continued to pass between the two sides.

That was all.

The power stayed.

It had no need for the being to notice what had been done. It did not wait for gratitude to rise through the roots, or for some new faculty to awaken in the folded leaves. The continued passage of sap was enough to attend to.

Over the following years, a growing thread reached a fracture beyond the western edge. There was nourishment there, sparse but usable. New leaves formed close to the stone.

The old feeding seam continued to diminish.

Upright stems leaned, died, and were drawn in strips into the tunnels. Sunlight reached patches of rock that had been shaded for centuries. The western growth became low and gray.

Yet it remained joined to the rest.

***

Elsewhere on the plateau, a grazer reached an empty basin.

The power watched it test the root grooves. Watched it shelter beneath a ledge while cold passed over the open ground. There was another living garden beyond the ridge, but the route toward it had been blocked by a fall of rounded stones.

When the animal emerged again, one of those stones lay at a slightly different angle.

A narrow sheltered way continued between them.

The grazer found it with its feelers. It paused with five feet anchored, then put the sixth forward.

The journey still took most of a season.

***

The power’s attention spread slowly across the high country.

It learned where help could be small, and where small help would merely postpone a greater harm. Often it did nothing. Sometimes it held a little warmth beneath a resting animal, or kept a living passage open while a root found another way.

It continued to learn.

Beneath the fallen slab, the cushion grew into its new fracture. The leaves nearest the opening were scarcely larger than mineral flakes. Dust lodged between them. A tunnel dweller came up from below and pulled a shed fiber into the dark.

The next change passed through the cushion without interruption.

Above it, the fronds began to turn silver in the white afternoon light.
