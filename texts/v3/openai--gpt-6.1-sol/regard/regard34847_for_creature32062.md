The power’s first act is to take no shape in the water.

A shape would displace it. An arrival would press upon the creature’s broad body, pass through its contacts, and require a response. Even a gentle visitor would be an event for which the hollow has made no provision.

Instead, the power attends.

It attends long enough to distinguish the creature’s stillness from exhaustion, its thinness from hunger, and its dependence on the machinery from injury by it. The low vibration is not merely an intrusion. The creature has made use of it. Whatever the sound was intended to do elsewhere, here it helps a body remain intelligible to itself.

The power does not silence the pumps.

Nor does it warm the hollow, brighten the water, or bring fish into the empty spaces. It does not assume that more life would mean a better life for the being already here. A fish could trouble the silt. A warmer current could close feeding surfaces that have remained open for years. Even another of its own kind might be an encroachment.

For a while, nothing changes except that nothing is overlooked.

The power follows the groundwater back through the limestone. It learns which passages carry nourishment and which merely carry water; where the cold is moderated, where it is renewed. It attends to the buried slab without lifting it. Along its hidden length, the dense portion of the creature rests in a space narrower than an observer would have guessed.

There is room for it now. There would be less room if the body had to withdraw all at once.

Beyond that shelter, a seam is slowly closing. Mineral deposits have thickened along opposing surfaces. Water still passes, but a future refuge is becoming shallower by increments too small for the creature to notice.

The power leaves the deposits near the body alone. Farther in, where no feeding surface reaches and no contact rests, it loosens a little of the mineral and allows the groundwater to carry it away.

The change takes many days.

No cloud appears above the slab. No crack opens under a thousand sensitive contacts. Water continues to enter at nearly the same rate, with nearly the same contents. But the sheltered passage extends a little farther beneath the stone. Its floor is firm. Its entrance remains where it has always been.

The creature does not discover it immediately.

The power waits.

At the next shutdown, the bass from the distant shore diminishes and stops. The creature begins to gather itself toward the slab. Contacts spread against the limestone; outer edges thicken. One folded portion presses farther into the sheltered passage than it has before and finds space instead of resistance.

It remains there for a time.

The power feels the faint strains passing through the creature’s body and the stone beneath it. Those strains have always been present, though usually obscured by the machinery. Rather than introducing a new vibration, the power eases a loose grain at the meeting of slab and bedrock. The stone settles by less than the width of a fine sediment particle.

A little more of the creature’s own movement returns through its contacts.

The creature shortens a fold. The changed pressure reaches the shelf. It releases a contact, makes another, and receives a faint difference.

The outer body still draws inward. The power does not prevent it. Withdrawal belongs to the creature, as spreading does. But it no longer needs to gather quite so tightly to feel where its parts are resting.

When the pumps resume, the familiar vibration comes through clearly. Nothing competes with it. The body broadens again, slowly, leaving its deeper fold in the newly available space.

There are other things the power could do.

It could keep the machines running indefinitely. It could hold the temperature and the water level beyond any failure of the works. It could keep roots from growing, stone from settling, tissue from wearing thin.

But the hollow is not an isolated vessel. Water entering it has passed elsewhere. Water leaving it will pass elsewhere again. The power continues to attend beyond the rim, learning the larger system before laying a hand upon it.

Here, it has made only two small changes: a little more shelter, and a clearer passage for a sensation the creature already possesses.

Neither requires gratitude. Neither asks the creature to know what happened.

Above the band of fossil shells, the thicker folds contract and loosen. A leaf is briefly displaced by the edge of the transparent body, then appears joined to its stem again.

The power remains without weight in the water.

The creature settles four contacts against the slab, releases another, and continues feeding.
