The power comes first as attention.

Nothing brightens. No shadow falls in a direction contrary to the sun. At the foot of the wall, the animal continues feeding, pressing its mouth to the stone, lifting it, pressing again.

The power notices the water beneath its folds.

It notices how little there is, and how much depends on it. It follows the dampness back through the threshold, along the lane, into a room where a cracked roof admits rain. Farther uphill, it finds the shallow hollow that feeds the room. Farther still, a seam in the plateau.

It could fill all these places.

For the moment, it does not.

It stays through the afternoon. The animal advances less than the width of a hand. A cloud crosses the sun, and the pale back takes on a faint sheen. When the light returns, the animal withdraws beneath the wall.

There are eggs there, beyond the reach of the light.

The power had not seen them at first.

It begins to look more carefully.

*

For a year, there is no alteration anyone could find.

The power attends to the upper terraces and the cold undersides of stairs. It follows the young through the outflows. It watches a gray bird carry food to a recess beneath a roof, where three open mouths rise at the sound of its feet.

It learns that clearing a blocked drain can empty a nursery.

That holding water in a nursery can drown the creatures wintering below it.

That a wall which appears ready to fall has been ready for eighty years, and shelters something on each side.

It learns the city unevenly. There are passages it understands well and districts where it still mistakes an absence for an emptiness.

In winter, it rests its attention along the damp ceilings. The animals hang there in overlapping groups, almost motionless. Meltwater moves between them. Deep within the stone, frost makes a sound too small for an ear.

The power listens.

*

The following summer is dry.

At first, this is only a difference in the hours. A stair loses its wet border before noon instead of afterward. A pool separates into two pools, then into three. The animals withdraw farther under the slabs.

On the distant uplands, rain continues to fall. From the outer terraces it can be seen passing over streets that drain toward another edge of the plateau.

Here, the joints turn pale.

The power watches one refuge diminish. It is beneath a fallen lintel, in a room open to the sky. The lintel has held water through many dry summers. Now the last of it lies in a depression no broader than a leaf.

Seven adults remain. There are young among them, too small to count without looking into the narrow spaces between their bodies.

Below the room, an old watercourse passes through solid clay. It was closed long ago when a foundation settled. Beyond the closure, a little water still moves.

The power follows it to its source, then to its other destinations.

It waits another day.

Then, beneath the foundation, it loosens a grain.

The grain shifts. Two others follow. A passage opens, finer than a root.

Water enters the room so slowly that the first sign is a darkening of the dust beside the lintel. By evening, the dark patch has reached the stone. During the night, a bead forms beneath it.

One of the young touches the bead.

The power does not enlarge the passage.

*

Elsewhere, it leaves the city alone.

A roof falls in the northern district. For several days the runoff carries white grit. The animals abandon two chambers and settle beneath the broken slabs.

A bird takes an adult from a gutter.

Flowers appear along the rim of a basin, then wither.

The power is present for these things without turning them all aside. It has seen the bird’s young. It has seen the roots entering the new rubble. It has also seen suffering that feeds nothing and opens no refuge: bodies caught together behind a smooth obstruction, while reachable stone lies only a little way above.

In one such drain, it roughens a thumb-sized patch of wall.

After the next storm, there are tracks across it.

That is all.

*

Years pass.

The small passage beneath the foundation narrows with sediment. The power lets it narrow. Rain has returned, and a piece of the lintel has settled in a way that deepens the hollow beneath it.

The room no longer needs that particular kindness.

There are other places to attend to.

The city remains pale. Its roofs do not rise back into their former positions. No vessels appear beside the hearths. Water continues to find routes through rooms that were not built to receive it.

On a clear afternoon, an animal rests beside a threshold.

Its folds hold a little dampness. Its back has dried to the color of the paving. Under the stone, one forefoot touches a rough place and stays there.

Far away, rain begins.

The power follows its arrival through the city, neither hurrying it nor holding it back.

When the thread of water reaches the threshold, the animal unfolds its feet.

There is a way onward.

It takes it.
