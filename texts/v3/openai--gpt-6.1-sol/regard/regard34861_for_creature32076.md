At first, the power did nothing.

It had come down through the shaft above the courtyard, where daylight narrowed between the galleries. The person on the bench felt a small change against the backs of their hands. Not a current. The water had not been displaced. Rather, the pressure had become steady in a place where it usually varied.

The leaf continued to open.

The power had seen the roofs from above and mistaken them for the remains of a town. Now it waited while the person moved a finger beneath the leaf’s edge. Farther in, the woman with the cup was easing a root away from a doorway. A child held a stair tread with both hands. Beneath the pumping house, someone lay against a pressure housing, receiving nourishment while keeping a failing seam closed.

There was much to learn before touching anything.

For a while, the power followed the circulation.

It passed through the branching chambers of the inhabitants without entering their thoughts. It felt the difference between water that had crossed a feeding palm and water that had rested in an upper room. It found the pale growth inside the bearings, the dark colonies along the joints, the places where heat was being used twice and then a third time before it escaped toward the surface.

It also found a narrow passage beneath the eastern intake bed.

The passage had been narrowing for years. A mineral shelf was growing across it, slowly enough that each adjustment elsewhere had seemed sufficient. The engines drew a little harder. A colony retreated from a cooler joint. People working in the galleries shifted their bodies to accommodate the altered warmth.

Eventually, the passage would close.

The power could have removed the shelf at once.

Instead, it went to the man beside the pressure gauge.

It rested against the housing where his left hand could feel it, and made one slight variation in pressure. Then it waited.

The man did not reach for the pencil. He moved his palm along the metal, searching for the source.

The power repeated the variation.

After a long interval, he answered.

Their first exchange occupied most of a season. The power learned when a pause meant uncertainty, when it meant that he was consulting a panel, and when it meant that his attention had simply gone elsewhere. Once it repeated itself too soon. He took his hand away and did not return it until the next slow click of the engine.

It did not repeat that mistake.

Eventually, he lifted three porcelain panels from beneath the gauge. On each was a curve turning inward. He set them against the housing, one after another, and held the last beneath his palm.

He already knew about the narrowing passage.

Others knew too. The woman with the cup had been keeping two small clearances open beneath the western houses. An engine worker had spent years encouraging a sealing colony to grow along the edge of an unused valve. Their efforts had bought time. None had yet found a way to remove the mineral shelf without sending its fragments into the works.

The power offered to carry the fragments away.

The man kept his hand on the housing.

Later, other hands joined his.

***

They chose to begin with a space no larger than a cup.

The power loosened the mineral there grain by grain, holding each grain out of the current. The engine workers felt the change through their forearms. One lifted a finger. The power stopped.

They waited while the water settled into its new course.

Then the finger returned to the metal.

The work took years by the clock at the surface. It could have taken less, but there were young people learning the intake routes, colonies adjusting to new warmth, households whose rooms received a slightly different current with each alteration. The power had the strength to clear the passage. The inhabitants knew what clearing it would disturb.

The woman came often. Sometimes she brought her ceramic wedge, though there was no need for it. At the end of each visit she set her cup on the old intake ledge.

The power left the crack alone.

Once, while she was inspecting the reopened passage, the cup slipped from her hand. It turned slowly in the water, beyond her reach, and the power held it where it was.

She took it back.

Afterward she rested two fingers against the ledge, near the place where the pressure had become steady.

***

The inhabitants asked for something else.

Below the district lay an old conduit, blocked at both ends and empty of all but water and fine sediment. They wanted it opened into a second route through the deep galleries. If the main passage narrowed again, circulation could continue while they worked.

The power cleared the conduit. They showed it where to make the openings.

At the upper end, the new flow disturbed a curtain in one of the houses around the clock. The person beneath the table withdrew their hand from its central leg and moved into another room.

Work stopped.

The household tried several arrangements. A tile was set at an angle inside the conduit. Roots were encouraged across one edge. The flow divided, and the curtain settled. After a while, the person returned beneath the table.

Only then did they finish.

The new route had no bright lining, no indestructible gate. Its surfaces could support the growth the district needed. Its openings were within reach of ordinary hands. The power left nothing there that required its continued presence.

When the work was done, the man beside the gauge made a mark on a fresh panel. Some time later he added another.

***

The power returned to the courtyard.

The person on the bench had felt much of what was happening through the tiled floor. Now they lifted one hand from the seat and held it in the steady place beside them.

The power waited.

Their request was difficult to understand. It concerned neither the engines nor the shaft to the surface. Several times the hand withdrew and returned. The person touched the wall, then the underside of the bench, then extended two fingers toward the open water.

At last the power understood.

A small root beneath the seat had been pressing against their body. They had been moving around it for a long time. They wanted it eased aside without harming the fern that grew from it.

The power shifted the root by less than the width of a finger.

The person settled farther back.

Above them, a cloudy seed globe drifted between two galleries. The power let it pass. It had begun to recognize some of the district’s inhabitants, but not all. There were still rooms it had not entered, uses for things it did not understand.

It remained within reach, in a place that obstructed no passage.

When the flywheel’s shadow crossed the courtyard, the person uncrossed a hand. The leaf beside the bench had grown broad enough to touch the wall. This time, as it opened, there was room for both of them.

Warm water passed beneath the seat and into the newly cleared galleries. Far away, someone laid a hand beside another hand on the market rail.

After a time, they turned together.
