The power first attends during a pump stroke.

It does not enter the district. Entering is not necessary, and there is no place to put a foot without making a claim upon something.

It watches the water rise in the broken bottle. It watches the animal round beneath the tread. It notices that the caretaker waits before moving her measuring rod, although the reading is already clear.

When the piston withdraws, she lifts the rod.

Only then does the power draw nearer.

For some time, it does nothing that can be distinguished from attention.

It follows the water through the pipes, but also through the places where there are no pipes. It finds that the old flood marks are not merely records. It finds that the two scales beside the canal are both correct, and that neither describes all the water. It follows a bubble through the clear chambers of an animal without turning the animal over.

There are things it could change at once.

It could make the paving level. It could empty the canal. It could lift the district until daylight entered the workshop windows horizontally.

It watches the shrimp beneath the platform, their bodies aligned to a current too slight to wrinkle the surface.

It does not lift the district.

—

The animal under the step has an injury.

This is difficult to see even with the hooded lamp. One of its pads, close to the hinge, closes incompletely. A small pale division remains open when the others grip.

The injury is old. The animal has adjusted its movements around it. When the pressure changes, two neighbouring pads take the strain, and a fold along its side draws tight.

The power does not lay a hand upon it.

For twelve strokes, it watches. Then for twelve more. It learns how the pad closes, how the chambers fill, which part of the motion belongs to the animal and which belongs to the district.

At the next settling of the water, while the pads loosen one after another, the pale division joins.

The animal shifts farther beneath the iron.

Nothing else in its body is changed. Its mineral grains remain uneven. The smaller animal along the back edge is not made larger or moved away. When it approaches the hinge, the older one presses against it as before.

—

The step itself takes longer.

The caretaker has left it down, but another worker may fold it. A new mechanic may see only rust beneath the tread. There are instructions in the book; there are also days when a book is not opened.

Above the district, in a municipal store, lies a box of hinge stops ordered for a different stairway forty-three years earlier. The stairway was demolished before they arrived.

The power brings nothing new into the world for this purpose. It makes the box visible when a storekeeper moves a broken chair.

One stop fits.

The mechanic who brings it down kneels beside the step and tests it first without tightening the bolts. The caretaker holds the lamp. They wait for a stroke.

The stop leaves a narrow protected space beneath the folded iron. It can be released for repairs, but not by accident.

In the maintenance book, the caretaker draws its shape.

Under the drawing she writes: **Check clearance while occupied.**

The power attends to the drawing as carefully as it attended to the animal.

—

There is a bearing inside the pump that will fail in nine years.

There is also a man who knows how to replace it. He has been retired for seventeen years and no longer comes down the stairs because his knee will not bear the return journey.

The power does not make the bearing everlasting.

Instead, a question reaches the retired mechanic. It comes with a rubbing of the maker’s plate and enough money to make answering it paid work rather than a favour. A younger mechanic visits him on six afternoons. On the seventh, they disagree about the direction in which an old housing must be opened.

The retired man asks for the maintenance books.

The answer is in the oldest surviving volume, beside a drawing that has almost disappeared into a stain.

They make a replacement bearing. They make the tools needed to fit it. The younger mechanic writes down the procedure, including the pauses that must be observed between strokes.

The bearing remains wrapped in oiled cloth until it is needed.

—

Not every danger is below ground.

A proposed warehouse would put its heaviest wall above the northern stair. The drawings show the district as a shaded void. There is a note recommending that it be filled.

At the meeting where the drawings are considered, someone asks what keeps the foundations at their present level.

This is an ordinary question. It might have been asked without help.

What the power gives it is time to be answered.

The caretaker is invited upstairs. She brings the books, including the volume in which the thumb-wide gap in the flood mark is drawn. She does not bring a full vessel.

The meeting lasts longer than expected.

Later, there is another survey. The new drawings include the pump, the inspection chambers, the loading court, and the stairs. The northern wall of the warehouse is moved.

The shaded void acquires a name sufficient for correspondence and a budget sufficient for maintenance. Neither name nor budget is painted on the brickwork below.

—

The power considers the winter basin.

There are hundreds of transparent grains along its edge. Some will not open. Some young will be eaten almost immediately. The birds wait with their toes curled around the rail.

It could put every grain beyond reach.

Instead, it keeps cement wash from a repair above from entering the basin. The wash goes into a barrel, as the work specification already requires. The barrel is collected before rain can overflow it.

The fish continue searching the joints.

The young continue appearing beneath the stones.

—

The caretaker becomes aware of the attention gradually.

Not because a voice speaks, but because certain difficulties cease to arrive entirely alone. When a drain breaks, there is someone to help carry the tools. When she needs a second pair of hands to check the cable channels, the request is no longer returned for lack of a form.

One evening she pauses at the pump-room step.

“If you’re doing something,” she says, “leave the marks.”

The power had already decided to leave them.

But it receives the instruction.

—

Much remains wet.

Rain still arrives after the sky above has cleared. The handrails are cold. Rust flowers where it always has, and the moss keeps the inspection plate legible.

The animal beneath the tread still feeds grain by grain. It has not learned gratitude. It has not been required to.

At dusk, the caretaker lowers her lamp. She checks the hinge stop, touches the measuring rod to the pool, and waits.

The piston advances.

For a little while, every pool is deeper together.

Beneath the step, all the animal’s pads hold.
