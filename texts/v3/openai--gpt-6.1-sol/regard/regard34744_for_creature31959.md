The power did not enter through the seam.

There was too little room there for an arrival. Even a gentle brightness would have changed the folding of the pale leaves. A cool breath would have traveled down the lanes and lingered where the warm current should pass.

Instead, it attended to the hollow from where it was.

For a long time, it did nothing.

It followed the gas down through the foundation, beyond the dark solder of the roots, into fractures that had never held a leaf. It learned where the pressure gathered and what released it. It watched a yellow comb open, watched most of its powder fail, watched one grain adhere beneath the lip of a stone.

It did not hasten the grain.

There was work taking place at a scale where haste would have been damage.

The power also looked at the town. It saw the rubbed edges of the mismatched roof fragments, the dents in the lintels, the bench behind the doorway. Whoever had made these things had worked carefully. Their care remained visible even where its purpose had ended.

It could have raised the walls. It could have emptied the bowls, swept the paving, fitted the split stone together so perfectly that no fracture showed.

Before doing any of that, it followed the consequences.

The repaired stone would close three living passages. Clearing the court would remove the dead lattice on which new stems rested. Returning a buried stair to use would expose a root that had taken many hot cycles to cross it.

So the bench remained dusty. The court remained overgrown.

No one was asked to leave for the sake of making the place look inhabited.

Above the chamber, however, the fallen doorstep was beginning to move.

One end rested on a narrow shoulder of rock. The shoulder had cracked. With each long change of temperature, a little powder loosened inside it. Eventually the support would shear away, and the doorstep would drop across the seam. Its weight would drive the low roof inward.

The power considered what would happen then.

Some growth would survive below the fallen stone. Much would be crushed. The warm current would find an easier passage outside the hollow, and the surviving roots would stiffen slowly in ground that no longer fed them. The pressure would still rise and fall somewhere, but the town’s passages would cease to sound.

The power set a small support beneath the failing shoulder.

It used stone already there, deep in the foundation, where nothing grew. It moved no more than was needed to take the weight. The old crack remained. Dust could still fall from it. The doorstep could still settle, but not into the hollow.

During the next exhalation, the jar knocked against its neighbor.

The support had changed no note.

That was the first thing the power did.

The second was farther away, beyond the larger ruin. A gully was wearing toward an exposed edge of the gas-bearing rock. In time, it would open that edge to the air and draw much of the chamber’s nourishment away.

The power turned a loose slab into the gully. Water passing there would carry its silt elsewhere now. Beneath the doorstep there would still be lean cycles, abandoned roots, fissures that cooled and others that grew warm. The power had not arranged an equal supply for every stem. It had kept one distant accident from ending all their differences at once.

Then it waited again.

Near the entrance, the runner extended beside the fallen tile. Its tip was pale and incomplete. There was no favorable fissure ahead of it.

The power did not put one there.

It watched the tip open through several breaths, then through several more. Extension slowed. The last leaves hardened smaller than those behind them. Elsewhere, beneath a workshop wall, a new root found warmth and began to widen.

The power attended to both.

As the seasons of pressure passed, a corner of the workshop finally fell. It landed across a lane, breaking two climbing stems and leaving a sheltered space beneath its lower edge. The broken stems capped themselves. Powder gathered in the shelter.

Nothing was put back.

There would be more fallen walls. The little town would gradually become difficult to distinguish from the ground around it. The power could remain beside that change without stopping it.

For now, it kept its presence out of the scant light and its weight out of the stones.

Below the doorstep, the court stirred. Leaves lifted over the roofs. The buried passages sounded, unevenly, each according to its shape.

On the bench, the three bowls received another settling of dust.
