The attention arrives without adding any light.

For several days, nothing on the plateau changes because of it. The pools keep their separate temperatures. The grazers scrape the feeding film. In the roofless square, an offering disappears during the night.

The power watches that disappearance closely. It does not mistake an empty doorway for an unused one.

It follows the water farther than the inhabitants can follow it, through stone that has no cracks, through places where distance is not measured by the amount of ground between them. There are arrangements beneath the basin that have been maintaining themselves for a very long time. Some still do what they were made to do. Others now do something different, and living things have grown around the difference.

One fault keeps a nursery stone cool.

Another feeds the waxy cups on a ridge where no rain has fallen for years.

The power leaves both alone.

In a north-facing chamber, an old inhabitant is holding a nodule between its throat-hands.

Rose dust fills the folds of its shoulders. One supporting foot no longer flattens properly against the floor. When it listens through the others, it has to lean, and the small hands pause in their work to steady the body.

The nodule continues ticking.

It should have stopped by now. The inhabitant turns it, waits, and turns it again. At last it puts the nodule into a cup beside the entrance.

By evening there is only enough water to wet the bottom.

A grazer pushes its face through the opening. The inhabitant presses a hand against its nose. The grazer withdraws, then waits outside.

There are two young in the chamber. One is asleep across the ledge. The other has climbed down and is touching the dry underside of a cup.

The power could fill every hollow in the room.

Instead it examines the hollow that has failed.

Far below it, a route has narrowed. Not broken: narrowed. Water still passes through, but a portion of the old arrangement is spending more and more effort sending warmth into an empty ledge in a neighboring ridge. The empty ledge once held something that needed it. Nothing has slept there for a long time.

The power does not remove the warmth at once.

For three nights it watches the ledge. A small burrower comes there near dawn. On the fourth night, another follows it.

So the warmth remains.

But there is another route, little used, passing beneath the gray seam outside the old inhabitant’s door. It could carry enough water to the chamber without taking it from the burrowers or drying a pool.

The power opens that route by less than the width of a grain.

At sunrise, a dark bead appears on the entrance stone.

The inhabitant touches it with the underside of its jaw. Then it goes back inside and brings out an empty cup.

It places the cup beneath the bead.

Nothing falls.

The bead remains where it is, round and motionless on the sloping stone.

The inhabitant waits until the sun reaches its forelimbs. Then it takes the cup away.

That evening, the power withdraws the bead.

It has offered water in a place where water does not behave that way. The inhabitant has noticed, but noticing is not the same as understanding.

The next morning, there are three pebbles beside the entrance.

The inhabitant had placed them there several seasons ago, when a damp hollow farther down the slope ceased to fill. One has become almost buried. The power lifts it just enough to expose its upper face.

The old inhabitant stops beside them.

Its supporting foot rises.

For a long while, nothing else happens.

Then it uncovers the pebble completely, turns the other two, and sets a dry stem across them.

The power waits.

Another inhabitant comes from the adjacent ridge. The two touch the arrangement, then the entrance stone, then the earth between. They move the stem twice. The visitor goes away and returns with a cup.

They put the cup not at the doorway but in a shallow socket farther into the shade.

Only then does the power let the new route carry water.

The cup fills slowly. The inhabitants remain beside it until the surface reaches a dark mark on the stone. The old one drinks. The visitor drinks after it.

When the grazer comes, they move aside.

***

Over the following days, the power learns more about the arrangements.

They are not simply requests. The same three pebbles can mean different things depending on which faces are uppermost, whether the soil beneath them is damp, whether the person who set them down is still nearby. Some arrangements are warnings. Some record a failure that ought not to be tested again yet.

A pair of cups placed mouth to mouth outside a chamber means that no water should be brought there.

The power discovers this before it changes anything inside.

There is a body on the sleeping ledge. Other inhabitants come and go, carrying small quantities of the feeding film, touching the floor beside the body, leaving the cups as they are.

The chamber stays dry.

Outside, the power keeps the moisture beneath a nearby stone from failing. Those who sit through the bright hours can drink without leaving the ridge.

It does nothing to the body.

***

The old inhabitant begins to investigate the new supply.

It takes away the cup. The hollow becomes damp but does not overflow.

It brings a different cup. No water gathers.

It returns the first one, setting it slightly crooked. The water rises along one side.

Two visitors arrive. There is a prolonged rearrangement of pebbles, interrupted by trips to the gray seam. One visitor carries a stone away and returns without it.

The power does not settle their disagreement.

That night, it closes the new route for a short time.

By morning, the original cup is dry.

The old inhabitant touches it, then touches the arrangement outside. It sets the nodule back in the cup and holds both between its hands.

The power opens the route again.

A little water appears around the nodule.

The inhabitant does this several times over the next few days. Sometimes the power answers. Sometimes it waits until the cup has been moved into the shade, where filling it will not draw heat through the nursery ledge.

Gradually, the arrangement outside the entrance changes. There are fewer pebbles now, and a short root laid beside them. The visitor who objected returns, touches the root, and leaves it in place.

***

There are things the power cannot safely repair from above.

A change beneath the central depression would bring more water to its northern margin, but it would also shift the time at which the feeding film renews itself. The grazers might adapt. The pale creatures under the nursery stones might not.

The power leaves that change undone.

Elsewhere, it steadies a roof that has begun to thin. It does not make the stone stronger everywhere. It keeps a small flake from separating above a sleeping household, then waits until the occupants are outside before letting it drop.

They find the fragment near the entrance.

One touches its fresh underside. Another goes into the chamber and feels along the ceiling. By evening, a thin stone partition has been moved beneath the weak place.

The power eases its hold.

The partition bears the weight.

***

Late in the season, the old inhabitant comes to the three panels.

It carries the cup that now fills beside its door.

Through the nearest panel, the cup appears in a different hollow. Behind it, on the sleeping ledge, three young lie across the boundary of warm and cold.

The inhabitant stands there until the low sun reaches the panel’s edge.

The power has not put this image there. It cannot tell whether it is a future, an unused possibility, or something belonging to another ridge.

It does not make the image clearer.

The inhabitant passes a hand through the panel, picks up its cup, and goes home.

At the entrance, it pauses beside the arrangement of pebbles and root. It adds one small stone.

The power has seen that gesture before, but not often enough to be certain of it.

It waits.

Inside, the cup is set down. The young wake. The grazer lowers its face into the doorway and is permitted to remain.

When the nodule begins to tick, the old inhabitant holds it for a while, then puts it back into the water.

Far beneath the ridge, the new route stays open.

The power attends to its narrow passage through the stone, to the warmth needed by the burrowers, and to the small arrangement outside the door.
