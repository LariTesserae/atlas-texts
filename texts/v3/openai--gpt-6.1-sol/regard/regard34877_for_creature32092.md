The power first noticed the door.

It was in a school entrance beneath the eastern ridge, and it had begun to close too readily. The caretaker had reported it twice. On the second report he had added that if the door wanted his job, it could start by clearing the boot grates.

The report was waiting among other reports. There were warmer shafts, joints approaching the ends of their travel, a foundation whose readings disagreed with one another. Nothing had been neglected deliberately. There was simply more to notice than the people on that shift could finish noticing.

The power could have steadied the whole plateau.

It considered what that would mean. Water would cease abandoning its channels. Foundations would stop needing adjustment. The survey marks would become correct and remain correct.

Far beyond the occupied ground, a pool was a finger’s breadth from overflowing. Small creatures moved beneath its mineral rim. If the land were held still, the pool would spill toward the north. If the uplift continued, it might spill west, into a hollow that had never held water.

Neither direction was a mistake.

The power left the deep movement alone.

At the school, it held the door for a boy whose coat loop had caught on the handle. He felt the pressure ease, freed himself, and went inside. For the rest of the afternoon, the door closed slowly enough to be safe.

Then the power waited for the caretaker.

He arrived with a wedge, two tools, and the expression of someone prepared to be vindicated at considerable length. There was a person sitting on the low wall opposite the entrance. Their boots were wet. He had not seen them approach.

“Does it do that every time?” they asked.

“Only when someone’s watching.”

“I held it back today.”

He looked at the door, then at them.

“Did you.”

“A child was caught.”

He put down his tools.

The explanation took a while. The caretaker asked practical questions first, followed by questions that were practical in a different way. Could it see beneath the ridge? Could it tell whether his sister’s building was safe? Had it been here last winter?

“No,” it said to the last question.

He was quiet for some time after that.

“Then come inside,” he said. “You can start now.”

***

It did not become the city’s hidden machinery.

There was already enough hidden machinery, and people depended on knowing who maintained it.

The caretaker introduced it to a structural engineer, who brought two colleagues. They asked it to examine an unoccupied gallery where the instruments had been disagreeing for six months.

It found the strain. More importantly, it showed them how to find it.

The engineer remained doubtful until a probe returned the predicted result. Then she sat on an equipment case and asked the power to repeat the explanation more slowly.

Within a week there were meetings. Within two weeks there were arguments about the meetings. Someone wanted to know whether the power’s observations could be entered into the maintenance record without an independent measurement. Someone else asked why a being capable of lifting a mountain had been assigned a visitor’s badge.

The power wore the badge.

It listened when the people responsible for the lower workings explained which failures frightened them, and when the people responsible for the lakes explained which apparent failures were simply water finding its way.

It accepted correction about the grazers. The old female waiting beside the sealed entrance did not need the entrance reopened. Beyond it was a freight chamber, now full of equipment. What she remembered was a sheltered route to a basin.

There was another route, but a low retaining wall blocked it.

The wall was altered after the freight crew had checked the ground. For three days the female continued visiting the sealed entrance. On the fourth, she found the new passage. The herd followed her, walking cautiously over the unfamiliar paving.

***

The power could hear what people did not report.

It heard a worker in the deep levels trying to stay awake through the last hour of a second shift. It heard a woman composing an invitation and deleting it because the journey was too long to ask of anyone. It heard someone sitting at a kitchen table with two cups, although only one person lived there now.

It did not enter those rooms unasked.

Instead, it asked the city a question.

“What do you keep postponing because it is nobody’s emergency?”

The answers were untidy.

A lift. A night service. Repairs to a room where visitors could stay. Enough staff that taking leave did not mean giving one’s work to an exhausted friend. A covered bench halfway across a long exposed route.

Some requests contradicted others. Some would damage places their authors had never visited. The power went with them to look.

On one proposed shelter site, shorebirds were nesting in gravel the colour of their backs. The person who had requested the shelter stood very still when she finally saw them.

“Farther up, then,” she said.

They walked farther up.

Where the work was difficult rather than contentious, the power helped. It lifted stone while crews fitted supports. It found a safe water supply for the guest rooms. It held a failing joint steady through the night, with an engineer beside it and the replacement arriving on the first freight service.

It could have done these things alone. Working with people took longer. By morning, however, the engineer knew exactly what had changed beneath her city.

***

There was a lake warden who distrusted it.

“You’ll make us careless,” she said.

They were beside a new stream, watching grit tumble through water scarcely deep enough to cover their fingertips.

“I might,” said the power.

This was not the answer she had prepared for.

She asked it to stay away from her district for a month. Unless someone was in immediate danger, she added. Then she objected to the vagueness of *immediate*, and they spent the walk back discussing it.

The power kept the agreement.

When it returned, she showed it a map with three errors in its earlier predictions. One error had left a measuring post standing in a dry basin.

They went out to retrieve the post together.

***

By the end of the first year, some things were easier.

There were fewer missed connections. The school door had been repaired properly. People on the deepest shifts had enough relief to go home when their work was finished. In several districts, a guest could stay overnight without anyone sleeping beside the laundry cupboard.

There were still arguments in kitchens. Fish still vanished beneath ledges too thin to contain them. Predators crossed the freight routes, and the morning crews checked the recordings before opening the paths.

The land continued to rise.

One morning, the power accompanied two children to a pool they had named before anything visible lived in it. They wanted to know whether creatures had arrived.

It could see the pale bodies moving through a crack below the waterline. It could have made them brighter, larger, easier to find.

Instead, it showed the children where to crouch so their shadows would not fall across the water.

They waited. The younger child grew cold and put her hands inside her sleeves. The older one began explaining that perhaps there was nothing there yet.

Then a tiny swimmer emerged over the clean stone.

“There,” said the younger child.

The power stayed beside them while they looked.
