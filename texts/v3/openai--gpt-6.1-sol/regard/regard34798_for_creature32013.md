The power did not begin by lifting anything toward the surface.

It understood that the brightness overhead was not a destination. The animal’s thinness was not a deficiency, nor was its stillness a confinement. A life spread through roots and fallen leaves did not need to be gathered into a single shape before it could be cared for.

So it came without a shadow.

For a long time, nothing in the basin changed because it was there.

It attended to the animal beneath the sheltered ridge: to the fringes gathering pollen, to the dark seams carrying nourishment upward, to the places where a muscular pad released its hold and another took the weight. It learned how slowly a change could pass through this body without arriving late.

It did not assume that every contraction meant distress. A fold closing against cold water might be no more troubling than a leaf turning in a current. Nor did it assume that silence meant contentment.

There were differences. It waited until it could perceive them.

The animal had grown around three trees. One was young, one had lost its upper crown, and one leaned from the cliff in a long pale curve. Beneath the oldest roots lay its thickest folds. Higher up, a nearly transparent sleeve enclosed a branch that had been dead for several years.

The sleeve still tightened sometimes.

Nothing was fed to it there. No pollen route crossed it. The branch no longer needed support. Yet the animal maintained the connection, sending a little nourishment along a dark thread so fine that ordinary light could hardly reveal it.

The power watched this without deciding what it meant.

Below the grove, a plate of stone was separating from the ridge.

Its movement was slight. A root had entered the upper fracture; minerals had gathered in the lower one. Warmth came and went behind the plate. Eventually it would fall.

The animal already knew something of this. Its youngest tissue had withdrawn from the stone. Two ribbons had turned away. But the thick lower folds remained in the hollow beneath it, threaded among roots that had grown through the body’s old openings.

Departure was possible. It was also costly.

To leave, the animal would have to relinquish much of what it had become. Some parts could flatten and draw free. Others would tear. Its young, still dark strokes in their pouches, lay under a nearby shelf.

The power could have held the plate forever.

It considered the roots behind it, the unweathered stone that its fall would expose, the plants that would find that stone, and the hollow that would receive the broken pieces. Holding it would preserve one arrangement by preventing many others.

It could have moved the animal whole.

But there was no whole to lift without choosing, on the animal’s behalf, where it ended.

Instead, the power made a small alteration in the fracture.

It did not force the plate outward. It allowed a little of the warmth behind it to reach the bare patch against which the animal rested.

The change was gentle, unmistakable, and early.

A fold tightened.

Far up the leaning tree, the unused sleeve contracted around the dead branch. Along the lower roots, fringes closed one after another.

Then the power waited.

The animal withdrew a narrow margin from the hollow. Over the following days, it loosened two pads and thickened a ribbon reaching toward the adjacent tree. It began to leave in its own manner: not by abandoning its place all at once, but by becoming more firmly present elsewhere.

Where an old root caught the withdrawing tissue, the power softened a little of the mineral crust around it. The root remained alive. The animal pulled free.

Where no obstruction existed, it did nothing.

Some folds were left behind. The power did not keep them living after the animal had ceased to nourish them. Small grazers came to their edges. Leaves settled over them.

The pouches beneath the shelf remained attached.

When the first young straightened, the parent opened a seam. The narrow body rested among the fringes, then made two faint swimming folds and drifted toward the neighboring roots. The power neither carried it nor selected where it should settle.

The little animal tested a fork.

Released it.

Tested again.

By the time the stone fell, the parent’s thickest living tissue lay beyond its reach.

There was one clear knock, followed by several smaller sounds. Leaves lifted through the water. A branch bent and broke. The plate came to rest across the old hollow, leaving a face of purple stone exposed above it.

The power did not quiet the sound.

The animal held its fringes closed until the sediment thinned. Then, on the neighboring tree, a free edge opened into the slow circulation. A fragment of broken leaf caught there and passed inward.

Much later, one ribbon extended toward the fallen plate.

The power remained attentive. There would be other fractures, other difficult departures. It would not mistake the first rescue for an understanding of everything this life required.

For now, sunlight reached the new stone. A few particles of pollen settled along its upper edge.

The ribbon touched it, rested briefly, and withdrew.

Nothing encouraged it to return.
