The power notices her heel.

It has noticed continents moving, and the heat beneath them. It knows what fills the pale veins and why the broken edges have remained sharp. But it stays, for a while, with the small pressure of her heel against the floor.

The child has reached the fitted stone.

She knows this before the power does anything. Her shoulders loosen. She lifts her foot, draws the food closer, and asks whether you have left the dark threads in your portion.

The power does not brighten the chamber.

There are creatures here for whom brightness would be an injury, and people for whom it would reveal very little they needed to know. Nor does it bring rain. It watches the woman close a vessel after taking a mouthful, and understands that abundance cannot safely be poured into every place that has learned to live without it.

For some time, it does nothing visible.

It follows the floor current through the settlement. It attends to the mouths beneath the gray drifts, the colonies pressed against their seams, the sleeper whose breathing is interrupted by pain. It learns which gaps in the walls were left deliberately. It learns that the old woman in the neighboring room has been offered a warmer sleeping place three times and has refused it three times because she likes to hear the children pass.

It does not move her.

Beyond the turn, it finds the thin glass.

The woman has been right about it. A fracture has begun underneath, where no staff can reach. It advances slowly, but not steadily. The weight of one person will close it; the weight of another, placed half a stride farther along, will open it.

The power could make the floor thick.

Instead, it first follows what lives beneath it.

There is a colony on the underside: pale, broad animals whose young shelter in the slight movement of the glass. Making the floor rigid would press their feeding surfaces shut. Filling the chamber below would kill them. Lifting the whole settlement elsewhere would sever arrangements built over lifetimes, some of which the power has only begun to understand.

It returns to the woman.

Her lamp has been turned toward the wall. You are asleep. She is picking a knot out of a binding thread with the point of a crystal needle.

Something touches the head of her staff.

She stops.

The touch comes again, light and definite.

“Someone there?”

“Yes.”

She uncovers the lamp. There is no one within its circle.

After a moment she puts down the needle.

“Where are your feet?”

“I have none here.”

“That makes it difficult.”

“Yes,” says the power.

She considers this answer longer than she would have considered a boast.

Then she sets her palm flat on the floor.

The power touches the stone beneath it, gently enough that she feels a single tap.

***

It tells her about the fracture.

She asks where, how long, and whether the left edge remains sound. It answers each question. When she asks how many crossings remain before the glass breaks, it tells her why a number would mislead her.

“Then we close it,” she says.

“There is another way around.”

“There are several. One takes half a day. One takes children where we do not take children.”

“I can show you a shorter one.”

She draws her staff toward her.

“In the morning. With others.”

***

They go with a builder, two people who know the feeding beds beyond the settlement, and a young man who insists that he has already searched every useful opening in that wall.

The power leads them to a fold of black mineral. Behind it lies a passage too narrow at the entrance for a laden traveler, but wide enough farther in. The lower air moves through it. There are no hanging discs within reach of a lamp.

The young man admits that he searched near the opening.

They do not widen it immediately.

They spend two days finding what uses it, testing the floor, carrying loads through sideways. The builder places three slabs where a sharp edge would otherwise meet an unguarded knee. Someone marks the entrance. Someone else objects to the mark, which resembles a warning used farther east, and it is changed.

The woman walks the route with the child.

He complains about the turn.

She says he may improve it when he is older.

At the old crossing they set a barrier that can be felt before a foot reaches the glass. The power helps anchor it without driving wedges through the colony below. Underneath, the pale animals continue feeding.

This is its first work.

***

There are other requests.

Some it grants simply. A crystal splinter comes free from a child’s hand. The sleeper in pain is shown a position in which she can rest, and then the cause of the pain is treated, with her permission.

Some requests take longer.

A family asks for water. The power asks whether they need more water each day, or whether they need to stop fearing the loss of what they have. Together they examine the vessels, the cooking slabs, the membranes used at night. One vessel has a flaw too fine for its maker to see. The power shows it to him. He sits for a long time with the vessel in his hands.

It supplies enough water while the flaw is repaired.

When someone asks that no floor ever break again, the power does not promise.

But it offers to help them listen farther into the stone. Those who wish to learn sit with their palms against a slab. The woman attends, though she makes the power repeat several things and refuses to call the lessons easy.

They develop tests that people can perform without its presence. The results are checked against one another. Places that cannot be tested are marked as such.

The work spreads slowly, at the pace of travelers.

***

The woman eventually asks about the pale plate.

The power tells her where it came from and what formed the branching lines. It tells her why her lamp failed in that passage.

She listens without touching it.

“Could I go back?”

“Yes. With preparation.”

“Would you come?”

“If you want me.”

She puts the plate away. There are lamps to tend, and someone has brought a hanging disc home on a warmed stone.

The household gathers beneath an empty place among the cords. The woman raises the stone carefully. For a long time, nothing happens.

The power waits with them.

At last the disc lifts one edge. A filament, finer than the fibers of their clothes, reaches upward.

No one speaks.

When it has fastened, the woman lowers the stone. She examines its surface to make sure no part of the animal remains attached, then hands it to the child to put away.

Later, when he leaves to visit the neighboring household, she checks his soles and asks his route.

He tells her.

This time she accepts the first answer.

The power is present, but her heel still rests against the floor.
