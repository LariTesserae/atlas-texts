The power first attends to the place where the hairs have stopped.

It does not mistake stillness for death. Beneath the larva’s surface, tissues are changing their arrangement. A mouth that has not yet opened is being made beside a gut that must continue working. The animal is using what it brought with it. There is enough for a little while.

The projection holds. The dust behind it deepens.

Nothing needs to be touched there.

The power attends next to the blister in which the crown has ceased to change. Here the stillness is different. Material arrives through the living strand, but the body no longer uses it. The whitening around its base will continue for a time.

The power could make the crown move again. But movement alone would not restore the animal. It would have to remake the tissues that failed, and keep attending to them afterward.

It stays beside the blister long enough to know what happened. Then it follows the strand back to the animals still feeding.

Across the hollow, mouths select from the water. Eggs turn by fractions inside their membranes. The old inhabitant with two damaged filaments draws in a particle that three undamaged crowns have missed.

The power does not straighten those filaments. They are healed, and the animal has learned their reach.

For one exchange of water, then another, it does nothing.

Doing nothing allows it to discover the difference between the room’s ordinary difficulties and the one that is approaching.

The descending brine is not yet too concentrated. The crowded upper galleries still have spaces into which a body can withdraw. The sagging arch, though weak, is carrying its weight. Even the hunter’s place beside the opening belongs to the circulation of the room: larvae pass it, some are taken, others reach walls beyond it.

But above the entrance seam, the ice is growing in two directions.

One growth has already narrowed the way in. The other is hidden inside the milky roof, where no animal can touch it. They will meet before the next easing of pressure. After that, the room will keep its water.

The crowns will open into a current made mostly by their own feeding. Suspended food will diminish. Salt will continue to fall from the ceiling. The animals will climb until there is nowhere higher to go.

This is where the power acts.

It does not warm the room. A warmth sufficient to be noticed would loosen foundations, change the brine, wake eggs before there was food for them.

Instead, it touches the buried meeting place of the two ice crystals.

Only a small part of one edge melts. The water there settles into the line of an older fracture, and the pressure from outside finds it. A thread passes through. Then another.

The entrance remains narrow. It admits no large swimmer. It gives the sea a way to move when the old seam closes.

Inside, a crustacean lifts one antenna.

The power waits through the first exchange.

Water enters above the floor rather than against it. The heavy layer stirs, rises a little, and moves toward the remaining opening. A bank of dust holds. The low arch does not shift. At the darkest end, the settling animal stays fastened to its projection.

The new current divides around the towers.

There is slightly less water passing the old inhabitant’s mouth now. The power notices this, too. It widens the lower end of the fracture by less than the thickness of a filament, and waits again.

A particle turns into the old passage.

The damaged crown takes it.

The power withdraws its touch.

What it has made will not remain unchanged. The fracture will freeze at its edges. The settlement will alter the currents. A chamber may grow where water now passes freely. Far outside, the ice itself is moving, and someday this hollow will open into a space none of its inhabitants can presently survive.

For now, there are two ways through the roof instead of one. If either begins to close, the other may carry enough water to keep it open. Larvae leaving by the new way will reach the underside of the ice beyond the room, where there are other cracks and firm places to settle.

The power follows one such route outward. Then it returns.

Below, the hunter folds around a passing swimmer. Nearby crowns withdraw at the disturbance. A mother backs her eggs out of a constriction and tries again.

At the darkest end, the new animal’s fold deepens. Its first wall is still clear. Water slips past the projection and brings a speck close enough to touch.

The power remains attentive.

The animal opens its mouth.
