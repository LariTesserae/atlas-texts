The power did not begin by making a current.

It could have. It could have put a tremor into the light, set a bubble loose beneath the surface, given the great transparent animal the first slow stroke of its fins. From beyond the place, these might have looked like gifts.

But a stroke would have carried the small creature between its vessels somewhere else. A current would have altered the distances by which the pale animals knew the columns. Even the smallest beginning would have passed through mouths, eyes, tissues, relations whose completeness was not yet understood.

The power attended before it acted.

It attended to the warmth inside the pale bodies. To the separate colors of the overlapping grazers. To the eyes distributed along an animal miles long, none of them waiting for its view to be gathered into a more important eye.

It did not mistake their stillness for obedience.

Nor did it mistake the absence of injury for proof that nothing could be wrong.

There were ways for a creature to be held that left no mark on its skin.

***

Its attention reached the small animal standing between the giant’s red vessels.

There was no shadow across the animal’s fins, no new brightness beside the distant seam. The power did not put a face into the water. A face would have occupied a direction; the animal already had a world in every direction its anatomy admitted.

Instead, the power made itself available to whatever, in that body, possessed the view.

It offered no command to awaken.

It did not ask the creature to demonstrate that it was alive by becoming different.

Its question had no sound and required no sequence in which to answer:

*Is there anything here you cannot bear?*

The question did not displace the column from the creature’s sight. It lay open beside that sight, without urgency.

The power could distinguish a refusal from an inability to reply. It could distinguish contentment from a body furnished to look content. Where there was no answer, it did not supply one.

It attended in the same way to the giant.

And to the eyes in the rootwork.

And to the intricate white organs beneath the dark shells, including those whose shared hollow contained many separate lives.

It asked nothing of the empty shells.

***

Much of what it found called for no remedy.

The warmth was nourishment, not fever. The great sound did not batter the hearing organs; their relation to it was as exact as the relation of an eye to light. The immense machinery of the columns did not consume the inhabitants to maintain itself.

The sea was not a paused disaster.

Its animals were not all straining toward a gesture that had been denied them. A fin fully open could be fully possessed. A view could be inhabited without becoming a memory.

The power allowed these things to be true.

Still, it did not make their truth into a law for every creature.

Where an inhabitant’s relation to the place was freely held, it left that relation alone. Where freedom was uncertain, it remained attentive. And if any life held an unfulfilled wish—however small, however unlike the wishes of moving beings—the power would meet that wish at its own scale.

It would not set an ocean in motion to relieve one creature.

It would not require that creature to remain for the ocean’s sake.

***

Beyond the sealed leaves, beneath the dark green line visible across thousands of miles, the power placed its care.

Nothing inside needed to carry the weight of being protected.

The roots were not armored. The rim was not raised. No unfamiliar organ appeared in the bark. But any force arriving from elsewhere would encounter an understanding of what it was about to touch.

Someone wishing to cut a passage through the living floor would be shown the eyes sheltered underneath.

Someone wishing to draw power from the conducting veins would learn what that power already sustained.

Someone wishing to start time there would have to reckon with lives, not scenery.

The power would not permit the sea to be used merely because it could not recoil.

***

Within the basin, nothing approached.

Nothing receded.

The small pale animal retained the distant seam in its eyes. Below it, the giant retained the woven floor. The immense sound remained itself.

No gratitude was required. No sign of visitation was left.

Only this lay outside the finished shape of the world: it was no longer beyond help.

And help, being wise, did not have to enter.
