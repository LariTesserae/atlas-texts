The power’s attention arrived without a shadow.

No fin stiffened. The old crab did not withdraw its claw. The snail at its hinge continued feeding on something too thin to be seen.

For a long time, nothing changed.

The power learned the water first: what it carried up, what it carried away, where it lingered until the last useful substance had been taken from it. It followed warmth through the immense construction. It found the places where a little movement of stone would open a passage, and the places where the same movement would close one.

It attended to the life in the mortar. Some of it required conditions that existed only within a grain’s breadth of other conditions it could not survive. A clean surface was not necessarily an improvement. Nor was clearer water.

Beneath the third submerged step, the crab moved a rear leg and stopped.

The joint had met stone before reaching the position in which it could rest.

After a while, it tried another position.

The power stayed with that small difficulty.

There were many things it could have done. It could have lifted the stair, opened the vault, admitted daylight all the way to the clay floor. It could have made the chamber spacious and bright, with clean sand and growing leaves.

The warm films would have died in some places. Other life would have entered. The fish would have found their old boundaries gone.

The crab would still have needed somewhere to put its legs.

So the power learned the hollow.

Its roof was the underside of a tread. Its back was a joint between two blocks, packed with dark mortar. Along one side, a thin projection of clay had survived because the crab’s shell prevented the current from reaching it directly.

Nothing depended on the projection for support.

A few small organisms lived on it. The power followed them, too.

Over several days, the water passing through the hollow changed its course by less than the width of a shrimp’s brush. A little more reached the cramped side. It remained warm. It brought the same food.

The film spread onto the stone behind the projection.

Only then did the clay begin to soften.

It did not break off. Grain by grain, it loosened into the current. The deposit near the outlets received it among all the other fine things that arrived there.

The crab noticed before there was anything visible to notice.

A rear leg extended farther.

It touched the wall, withdrew, and extended again.

The next time the crab returned from feeding, it settled with that leg unfolded. Several young crabs moved into the newly opened space beneath it.

The power let the work continue until the old animal could rest without pressing one joint against another. Then the current resumed its former course.

There was no inscription. Nothing in the hollow identified the kindness or required it to be remembered.

—

Elsewhere, the power watched a hunting fish take a grazer.

It did not turn the hunter’s mouth aside. It stayed with the brief struggle, and with the movements that followed it: the shrimp rising from the seams, the grazer’s body becoming still, the hunter returning to the place where it waited.

The chamber could not be made harmless by a single adjustment.

The power did not mistake this for a reason to leave every harm untouched.

Far below the room, an old lining had begun to separate from the shaft around it. A crack admitted water behind the clay. Years of that passage would eventually loosen a section large enough to obstruct the warm flow.

The power traced the crack to its end.

There was room for a mineral crust there. The water already carried what was needed. Very slowly, the crust thickened along the outside of the lining, where no grazer could scrape it away.

Upstairs, nothing became more abundant overnight. The films kept growing at their accustomed rate. The fish held in the currents they knew.

The warmth continued.

—

The crab left its hollow one day and crossed the low partition toward the split bench.

At the gap, it found a fragment whose coating was worth scraping. It lifted the fragment, turned it, and worked along the edge with its smaller claw.

The power did not guide where it put the fragment afterward.

It fell across two others. The water moved through the spaces between them.

On the return journey, the crab stopped twice. Once to feed. Once because a young crab stood in its path and did not move until touched.

Then it reached the third step.

Its legs entered the hollow. Its shell followed. There was a small scraping sound as it settled.

For the first time in many days, it did not have to settle again.

The snail opened at the hinge of its claw.

Above them, a drop gathered on the vault, grew heavy, and fell. The disturbance passed through the room. Antennae lifted. Fins corrected. The crab drew its claw inward, then let it rest.

The power remained near enough to notice what happened next.

For now, there was nothing more to do.
