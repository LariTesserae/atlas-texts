The first thing the power attended to was the purple line.

It was narrow enough to be lost in the shadow of the animal’s plates. Along its outer edge, the stone was still releasing gases that would have blistered the soft lining. Farther inward, where the animal had fitted itself against the seam, the temperature was lower. There was something useful moving through that warmth. The line darkened as it received it.

The power watched through several returns of the copper light.

Once, the animal lifted and shifted the length of a sensing tip. The purple material stretched between its underside and the stone, then separated. What remained on the shelf continued to darken.

Once, a tremor passed through the wall. The animal tightened before any sound reached the air.

Once, nothing happened for a very long time.

The power did not shorten that interval. Much of the animal’s life took place within it.

Under the shelf, pressure was gathering along a plane of weakness. Eventually the lower edge would rise, and the darker section near the wall would tilt toward a new opening. The animal had found a fruitful place, but not a lasting one.

There was no lasting ground anywhere in the basin. To make this shelf immovable would have sent its movement elsewhere: into the wall, into the shelters, into fractures that were currently feeding other lives.

Instead, the power made a small change in the rock beneath the animal.

Not enough to hold the shelf. Enough to let strain travel upward before it became a break.

A thin vein of crystal formed between the deep weakness and the surface. When pressure increased, the vein contracted. Its upper end lay beneath one of the animal’s rear supports.

The first contraction was faint.

The animal drew its plates close, waited, and relaxed again.

Later it felt the same contraction, stronger and accompanied by a slight change in the slope. This time it lifted. Its sensing tips passed over the ground in several directions. It moved away from the wall, paused, and moved again.

The fracture opened after it had gone.

There was a dry report, a brief red light, and the falling sound of fragments too small to see. The place where the animal had rested remained attached to the wall, but it was now vertical.

The purple line clung to it.

The power attended to that also.

A little warmth continued through the stone. When the line finally loosened, an updraft caught it and laid it in a fissure farther down. There it flattened. Whether anything would grow from it could not yet be known.

The animal had crossed onto a broad block lying between two younger surfaces. It rested with its supports spread across all three.

Its plates had begun to trouble it. One overlap no longer closed. At another, the new surface underneath pressed against the old covering whenever the body lengthened. It would soon need darkness and cool stone.

The eastern shelters were far away. The nearest had a leaning wall and a floor that gave heat through its crack.

The power could have carried the animal there. It could have lifted the old plates from its back and held the soft body safely while new ones formed.

But the animal withdrew even from a gust that entered beneath an overlap. A touch it could not locate would have given it nothing to work with.

On the broad block, several pieces of stone had cooled all the way through. One was long and flat, with a shallow hollow beneath it. The hollow was too low for an adult and open at both ends.

The power let a buried pocket settle.

The slab tipped without falling. Its higher end came to rest against a blunt projection, leaving a space underneath. The opening faced away from the nearest vent. Along the floor, warmth remained gentle and nearly uniform.

For some time the animal did not approach.

It fed at a small seam. It moved around the slab. Its tips touched the raised edge, withdrew, and touched it elsewhere. Two supports reached into the hollow while the rest of its weight stayed outside.

Then it went away.

The power waited.

At the next copper light, the animal returned carrying a chip between its lower folds. It pushed the chip under the slab’s resting edge. The chip was badly placed. The animal withdrew it and tried again.

Eventually the roof no longer shifted when pressed.

Only then did the animal enter.

Inside, it touched the back of the hollow, the floor, the roof. It settled and rose once more. A pale flake fell from an overlap.

The power kept watch below the block, where a new source of heat was beginning to gather. It opened a path toward an unoccupied cleft. Warm vapor emerged there instead, leaving a yellow rim that would be visible in the horizon light.

Under the roof, the plates loosened.

The animal broadened into the shallow floor. One old fragment slipped sideways and caught against the entrance. The soft lining pressed against it, withdrew, and turned in the darkness.

Near the end of the change, another adult arrived.

It had come from the western side of the block and was powdered almost white. It tested the new vent, then followed the slab’s cooler margin to the entrance.

The exposed animal lay still.

The newcomer placed two supports outside and two within. Its underside remained clear of the soft folds. For several hours it held that position.

The power had been listening for disturbances beneath the shelter. Now there was another listener.

A faint strain reached the block from the basin. The newcomer contracted and struck the roof.

The animal inside stirred. Its new plates were not yet hard enough for travel.

The strain passed without a break.

Afterward, the newcomer remained.

When the molting animal finally emerged, it had a shallow ridge across its back where the roof had pressed the forming plates. It rested outside, then crossed toward the seam. The other followed later. Neither approached the small purple patch directly. They settled at its edges.

Behind them, the old covering lay in broad pieces beneath the roof.

One piece would make a useful wedge.

The power stayed through the next movement of the shelf. It listened to the new crystal vein and to the widely spaced supports of the two animals. Beneath their resting place, warmth passed slowly through the stone.

There would be other fractures. There would be journeys neither animal could complete, and young bodies too small to cross the width of a cooling seam. The power could hear some of those troubles already, far across the basin.

For now, it worked on a narrow ledge below the eastern rim, where a roof had begun to lean. It eased the pressure under one wall and left, beside the doorway, a stone of the right size to brace it.

The high country continued to rise.
