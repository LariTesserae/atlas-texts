The power notices the lifted foot.

It has already noticed the height of the plain, the moving plates, the water held in vessels so small that a careless hand could empty a household’s reserve. It understands the anchors well enough to see where their loads go, including the loads their keepers cannot trace.

But it is the foot that holds its attention.

The person outside the shelter is thirsty. It has been awake since before sunrise, testing the crossing. One of its children is waiting beneath a plate some distance away. Still it remains beside the seam, showing a stranger where to step.

The power does not think this makes the thirst necessary.

It waits until the traveler is across.

Then, beneath the guide’s resting forefoot, it makes a small pressure in the stone.

The guide flattens immediately. Its lids close; its other feet spread. The pressure has come from no direction it recognizes.

The power stops.

After a while, the guide raises its body again.

It taps once.

The power repeats the tap, with the same force and duration, and waits.

***

Their first conversation takes most of the morning.

The power could place understanding directly into the guide’s mind. It does not. The person has spent a lifetime distinguishing what the ground is doing from what someone wishes to say through it. That distinction deserves to remain useful.

So they begin with repetition.

One tap. Two. A pause long enough to matter.

The guide moves to a different plate. The power answers there, too. It moves onto a loose ceramic chip, and receives an answer through the chip.

This is alarming.

For some time the guide says nothing. Then it makes a sequence used by children when another child has concealed a stone too well:

*Show where you are.*

The power considers the question.

It gathers a little loose grit into a pale oval, no longer than the guide’s smallest digit. The grit lies on the ceramic. It does not rise into a face or make a body. When the guide touches it, a gentle pressure answers.

The guide withdraws its foot.

Then it touches the grit again.

By evening, the power has learned the guide’s name. It takes several attempts. The name includes a pressure that changes according to the stone beneath it, and the power’s first repetition is too exact: it preserves what ought to vary.

The guide corrects it.

This is the first thing the power is taught.

***

That evening, a chamber roof begins to close.

It is not the guide’s chamber. It lies far to the west, beneath two overlapping plates, and contains eleven people, their cultivated stems, and a warm surface currently in use beneath an infant.

The household has noticed the change. They have begun moving their belongings toward the second exit. They believe there is time.

The power can feel the fracture below them.

It holds the roof.

Across the chamber, the ceiling’s faint downward movement ceases. A water vessel that had begun to lean comes to rest. The people fall still, listening.

Through the floor, the power gives the signal the guide has taught it for immediate danger.

Then, slowly, it repeats the household’s own evacuation taps.

They leave. One returns for a covered groove containing polished pieces of bone. The power continues to hold the roof.

Only when the chamber is empty does it let the plates settle.

Afterward, it shows the household where the fracture ran. It does this with pressures they can follow through their feet, and with fine lines of dark dust on the exposed stone.

They question it for a long time.

Their first question is whether the chamber can be recovered.

Their second is whether holding it has endangered anyone else.

The power answers both.

***

News travels imperfectly.

At some shelters, the bowls report that a new keeper has arrived. At others, that a voice can speak through loose stone. One settlement receives a message so distorted that it prepares for a dust front lasting nine days.

The power spends time correcting this.

It also learns to announce its presence before speaking. An unexpected movement beneath the feet is not a kindness merely because it carries kind intentions.

There are people who refuse to touch the pale oval.

There are people who ask it to repair everything.

One old keeper asks it to leave an anchor alone until she has finished measuring what is wrong with it.

The power waits beside her for seventeen days.

She is slower than she once was. Her wrists ache after work in the shaft, and she must stop often to recover her warmth. Twice she loses her place in a sequence of measurements.

On the eighteenth day, she finds the fault.

It lies farther below than her tools can reach.

For a while she rests with her feet against the shelter wall.

Then she asks the power whether it knew.

It answers that it did.

She asks whether her measurements were wasted.

The power shows her what they established: three safe routes for the strain to leave the anchor, a neighboring weakness its first examination had not resolved, and a way to test the repair afterward without relying on its word.

She lays a forefoot in the bowl.

*Then we can begin.*

***

There is no single day on which the plain is saved.

There are meetings in low chambers. There are messages sent three times because the first two arrive damaged. There are disagreements over which collectors should receive attention first, and whether a settlement should be preserved when half its families have already prepared to leave.

The power makes the consequences easier to examine.

On a small ceramic surface, it gives them a model they can feel. Pressing one place changes the pressures elsewhere. A repaired anchor can be tested there before anyone touches the real one. A proposed chamber can be opened, loaded, and emptied. The model includes uncertainty; some regions answer with a faint tremble rather than a definite pressure.

The keepers ask for more of these surfaces.

They also ask for instruments that will work when the power is absent.

This request pleases it, though it does not make them responsible for pleasing it.

It helps them make the instruments.

***

Water requires particular care.

To bring clouds up from the lower world would alter temperatures across the plain. To fill every fissure would drown the cultivated growths and destroy chambers whose inhabitants had no warning.

Instead, the power works first on the collectors.

A few can be improved by changing their surfaces. Others need deeper channels, or a place to release accumulated salts. In several regions, water can be drawn from far below without disturbing the plates, provided the passage is narrow and carefully sealed.

The new vessels fill slowly enough to be understood, and quickly enough to change a life.

People begin carrying water to households that have none.

Then they begin arguing over how much ought to remain at each collector.

The power does not pretend that a plentiful source distributes itself fairly. It stays through these conversations. It makes the yields visible, helps establish reserves, and ensures that a person arriving thirsty can drink before questions of inheritance are settled.

In one chamber, a child tips a vessel too far.

Water spreads across the ceramic floor.

Every adult turns.

The child lowers itself, waiting for the urgent work of recovery to begin.

Its parent checks the remaining supply. Then it touches the child’s feet and brings a cloth.

They gather what they can.

There is enough left for the evening.

***

The power can change their bodies.

It tells them this only after it has learned enough to explain what the changes would mean.

Some ask for relief from pain. Some want their eyes cleared. An anchor keeper wants the damaged sensitivity restored to two digits. An old person asks to be able to fold beneath a low ceiling again, then changes his mind and asks for a room with a higher one.

Both requests are possible.

A parent asks whether her children could be made never to need water.

The answer is complicated. The power takes time with it. They discuss warmth, growth, food, the pleasures the children already know, and how much of a life ought to be chosen before its owner can speak.

For now, they improve the household’s collector.

Other decisions remain open.

***

Some settlements move.

With the power’s help, the journey no longer requires leaving the slowest person behind while everyone else carries loads. It makes low traveling shelters that spread their weight over uncertain stone. Their floors remain sensitive to vibration. There are places for vessels, living stems, children, and the small objects that nobody wishes to explain to a stranger.

A family travels toward a lower shelf.

Another chooses to stay beside an old red band, though its anchor has become costly to maintain. They have plants there whose histories are known for many generations.

The power repairs the anchor.

Elsewhere, people allow a failing shelter to close. They remove its bowl before they go. The bowl continues to serve in their new chamber, set low enough for children to reach.

***

The guide meets the power again near the place where they first spoke.

The dangerous seam is still there. It opens and closes within a smaller range now, and three nearby chambers have sound roofs. A collector beneath the shelter has filled two vessels since dawn.

The guide has slept well.

It touches the pale oval of grit and asks what the power wants in return.

The power answers carefully, using forms of speech the guide can question.

It wants to know when help has gone wrong. It wants to be told when a change is unwelcome. Neither is a price. The water will keep coming if no one thanks it. The roofs will remain sound if people cease to speak its name.

The guide tests this answer from several directions.

At last it asks whether the power will be lonely.

The question is unexpected enough that the grit lies motionless for a moment.

Then the power answers honestly.

Sometimes.

The guide rests beside it until the sunlight moves away from the shelter wall.

***

Much later, there are still dust fronts on the high plain.

The gray colonies still shine briefly when light reaches into their cracks. Children still learn to wait before crossing open stone. Keepers still listen to anchors, and disagree about them, though more of their disagreements can now be settled by measurements everyone can examine.

There are also chambers with room to stand.

There are journeys undertaken for curiosity. There are warm surfaces made anew, and meals interrupted by games. An old person may sit in a shelter doorway without having to calculate whether the return crossing will cost the last of its strength.

The power remains available. Its presence is marked by a small pressure, offered before speech.

One evening, it finds the guide lying beside a cultivated cushion.

The guide has moved three ceramic chips to shelter a damaged edge. Now it is watching a child rearrange them.

The arrangement is poor. The next current will expose one side.

The guide waits.

The child examines the crack, feels along the stone, and moves the smallest chip.

Then it moves it again.

Beneath them, the plate is secure. Nearby, a sealed vessel holds more water than they will need tonight.

There is time for a third attempt.
