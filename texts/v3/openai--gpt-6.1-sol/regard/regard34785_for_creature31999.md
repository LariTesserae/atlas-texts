The attention arrives without passing through the lake.

Nothing above the hollow is displaced. No root bends beneath a new weight. The water does not become warmer, and the pale creature keeps its eyes against the wood.

For a time, the attention does nothing.

It follows the pressure through the creature’s plates, through the leaf behind the arch, through the long filaments whose middles cannot be found. It attends to the doorway without entering it. It distinguishes the cold that sustains a thing from the cold that prevents it from moving. These are sometimes the same cold.

There is much to learn before touching anything.

The dent in the creature’s back is not an injury. The narrowness beneath the shell chips is not, by itself, a confinement. The creature rests there because the root holds steadily, because food grows within reach, because changes in the stone arrive through the shell before they arrive through water.

But one of its folded limbs has been caught for longer than the creature has had its present covering.

The limb lies beneath its belly. A strand has passed through the old covering, through the new joint, and back into the root. When the creature extends toward a fracture, this limb begins to unfold and stops. The stopping has become part of every movement it makes.

The attention follows the strand.

It is alive. At its other end, it feeds a small patch of growth on the underside of a ribbon. There is no need to kill it.

During the next tide, when the strand loosens by less than the width of itself, the old covering gives way around it.

A small seam opens. Water enters. The strand slips through.

The creature draws the limb against its body.

For a long time it does not use it.

Then, beneath the white shell, a foot touches the root at a point none of its feet have touched before.

The nearest leaves tighten.

The attention remains.

Behind the arch, the covered body is still alive.

Not all of it is alive in the same way. One plate has become a place where roots feed. Two limbs no longer belong to its movements. Yet beneath the thickened leaf there is a mouth, and near the mouth a joint that presses upward whenever the water bears a certain mineral taste.

The leaf does not know this pressure from the pressures of growing.

Each time, it thickens a little.

The attention does not lift the leaf. Lifting it would tear the fine tissue beneath, and expose what has lived for years against a single surface to water moving on every side.

Instead, a portion of the leaf stops thickening.

Nothing withers. Nothing is taken away. Along a seam already present, the two layers cease laying new tissue against one another. The next tide carries a little water between them.

The mouth beneath the leaf opens.

A fringe grows into the seam, pale at first, then dark. The covered creature holds it against itself. Slowly, the fringe clears.

The joint presses upward again.

This time there is somewhere for the pressure to go.

A shallow fold rises in the leaf. It is no larger than the hollow left by a grain of grit, but it remains after the tide has passed.

The attention waits through winter.

It does not count the winter as an interval in which nothing has been accomplished.

At the back of the hollow, cold water continues to leave the doorway.

The attention can reach what lies beyond. It could follow every ridge to its other occurrence, discover the length between them, settle whether the enclosure was made or grown.

It does not.

Something behind the doorway has felt the attention and withdrawn its surface.

The withdrawal is small. The water at the entrance deepens without the entrance changing shape.

The attention draws back.

The translucent collar left by the pale creature remains hanging there. Its inner edge had been narrowing. Now it stops.

Beyond it, the surface returns to its former place.

No further approach is made.

Elsewhere, the hollow continues its work. Stone splits. Roots enter the split. A splinter softens on one side and remains hard on the other. A filament returns through the ceiling with its tip pointing upward.

The attention does not straighten it.

Once, the returning filament catches between a sharp edge and the pale creature’s new covering. The creature folds all its limbs. Pale material gathers at the contact.

There are several ways the attention could separate them.

It waits until the filament begins to move again, then lets the stone release a grain it was nearly ready to release.

The edge changes.

The filament passes on.

The pale material remains where it would have remained. Whatever was being exchanged is not interrupted.

Afterward, the creature goes toward fresh stone.

Its freed limb reaches ahead of the others.

The greatest change happens below the hollow.

Only a finger’s depth beneath its floor is another roof. Under that roof lies water held so tightly between surfaces that no tide has stirred it for a very long time. There is growth there too, thin and nearly spent.

The attention follows an existing seam downward.

Opening it all at once would empty one pressure into another. The grit beneath the arch would rise. The white shell chips might tear from the roots. What waits behind the doorway might find its boundary gone.

So the seam opens only where a root hair has already entered.

One season brings it water.

Another brings a trace of what the broken stone has yielded.

The root hair branches. Along it, a film of growth advances into the lower hollow. Something there takes nourishment and does not immediately spend it all.

Above, the pale creature feels the change.

It puts its newly freed foot against the floor.

For months it holds that position.

No command comes through the stone. No promise of a larger place. Only the pressure of water that moves, and beyond it a surface on which a foot might rest.

The creature may go there.

It may remain beneath the shell.

The attention keeps both places possible.

In the hollow’s newest crack, the pale tip continues to divide. One branch turns toward the creature. Another presses downward, toward the opened seam.

Behind the arch, the thick leaf lifts a little farther.

For the first time in several winters, two joints can be seen beneath its edge.

The visible creature stays where it is. Its eyes are against the wood. Its mouth holds a strand that is slowly becoming clear.

Then one of the joints beneath the leaf moves.

The creature releases a foot.

Before the leaves can tighten, before the change has travelled through root and shell and stone, there is already room for the foot to come down.
