The power notices the shelter before it notices the scratches.

Not because the shelter is large. From above, it is almost nothing: a few discarded surfaces fitted beneath a ledge, held together by a creature that can disappear into the thickness of a crack. But during the release, there is a small region in which the impacts arrive differently. Something receives them before they reach something else.

The power stays to discover how this is done.

It does not enter as a body on the ridge. Its weight would be another event for the creature to interpret, and there are already enough events. It attends instead to the stone: to the passages through which the wind is speaking, to the seams carrying mineral oil, to the dark beads moving singly through the buried cords.

It follows one bead for most of an afternoon.

The bead passes two branching places and stops beneath a damaged joint. There it changes, slowly, into material the creature can use. Farther along the fracture, a feeding fold remains closed that might otherwise have opened.

The power begins to understand the expense of a limb.

It watches several releases. It watches hunters wait outside the shelter and enter only after a wall has been raised between them and the grazers. It watches the creature refuse a living animal, then take another whose movements are too slight for its red-edged tip to distinguish from the stirring of an empty shell.

It does not correct the distinction from outside.

For now, it learns what the creature can feel.

***

The first thing it changes is deep in the mountain.

Below the ledge, behind the branching body, an old wedge of stone has begun to loosen. Its movement is very small. With each season, wind wears another grain from the lip that holds it. Eventually it will settle into the fracture.

It will not crush the whole creature. It will close several passages at once. Beyond them, limbs will continue their work until the beads no longer arrive.

The power holds the wedge.

It does not fill the fracture or fasten the creature to it. Roots still enter. Mineral oil still rises. The stone expands in sunlight and contracts in shade. Only the final inward settling is prevented.

Nothing visible happens.

The creature continues scraping salt beneath the ledge.

***

For a long time, that is all.

The power considers stilling the ladder and does not. It watches what fails to hatch between releases, what waits to open until the sheltered column empties. It considers quieting the wind. Then it follows the cold oil upward and finds how much of its movement depends on the pressure changing in the summit’s hollows.

It has the strength to make the basin comfortable for a visitor.

That turns out to be a poor guide.

So it watches the creature’s temporary shelters, especially the places where they fail.

One corner always requires a living limb. The stone there is too smooth to hold the scavenged membrane, and the wind approaches from below. Through every release, a narrow appendage must remain extended across the gap, bearing the pull. Afterward, it retreats into the fracture. Sometimes it does not emerge again for days.

Nearby is one of the old finger-sized holes.

The power puts a small projection inside it.

It is made from the same stone as the wall. It has a rounded end and a slight undercut, no sharper than the lip of a weathered hollow. It does not obstruct the little animals that sometimes shelter there. It is positioned where a strip of membrane could be looped around it.

Then the power waits.

The creature finds it while clearing grit.

A pale tip passes over the projection, returns, touches the underside. Two more limbs arrive. For a while, all three remain still against it.

The power makes no answering motion.

During the next release, the creature does not use the projection.

During the one after that, it fastens an empty egg case to it. The case tears almost immediately. The creature retrieves what remains and presses the new stone several times with a dark-edged limb.

Later, it brings a length of root fiber.

***

The first shelter hung from the projection is imperfect.

Its upper wall sags. Two grazers crowd into a pocket meant for one. The creature spends much of the release adjusting the divisions, and the freed limb is not free for long.

But when a tendril lashes down across the ledge, there is no appendage stretched across that particular gap.

Afterward, the creature leaves the root fiber in place.

The power does not add a second projection yet.

It watches the unused limb open a feeding fold while the last occupants are leaving. Dust collects on the membrane. A broken wing moves inward through several joints. The creature has eaten before the wind changes.

That evening, the dark limb touches the scratches.

No new mark is made.

***

There are other things the power could give it.

A certainty about the ladder’s next release. Limbs that do not break. A body no longer confined to fractures. An understanding of the marks beneath its plates, translated into words and returned to it as an account of its own life.

The power leaves these possibilities alone.

It cannot tell whether the creature would experience such gifts as gifts. It knows, now, that the creature can investigate a changed surface, decline it, test it with something expendable, and use it differently from what was intended.

This is enough to begin with.

Over many releases, it makes two more small changes to the stone, each near a place the creature has repeatedly tried to fasten something. One is used. The other is covered with grit and never uncovered.

The power does not insist.

When the creature grows into a seam beyond the ledge, the power follows the movement of the dark beads. When a nearby slab threatens to settle, it gives the branching cord room without widening the crack enough to change the wind through it.

It does not stand guard over every grazer.

The hunters still hunt. The feeding membranes still make mistakes. The ladder turns, and the garden spends itself in light, attachment, hunger, and renewal.

But a few losses that would have come from the slow closing of stone do not come.

A few shelters cost less to hold.

***

One afternoon, a traveler sits on the ledge.

They notice the old scratches when a plate lifts. They notice root fiber hanging from something in the finger-sized hole and assume that someone once fixed a handrail there.

The creature opens a feeding fold between the traveler and the wind.

The traveler remains until the sunlight has left the cabinet. When they begin to climb, a pale extremity withdraws from the foothold before their boot arrives.

Above the basin, the seven squares are motionless. An egg turns beneath the lowest one.

Deep behind the ledge, a wedge of stone remains where it has been for centuries.

The power attends to it, and to the creature moving along its edge.
