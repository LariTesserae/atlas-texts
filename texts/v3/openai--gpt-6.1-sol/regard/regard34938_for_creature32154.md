The power would stay through a tide.

It would not lift the moss.

At first it would attend to the places where the animal held on: the root passing through its oldest fold, the clay beneath the hidden mouth, the wet underside of the fern where three of its young were feeding. It would follow the seepage back through the shelf and the salt water forward through its buried passages. It would learn which movements belonged to the hollow, and which had been brought there from elsewhere.

This would take longer than the first feeling of pity.

The pale body hanging by one strip would trouble it. So would the young carried away in the ebb. But it would look for those young downstream before deciding that every departure was a death. Some would be caught in places where they could not live. Others would reach a wet seam beneath another bank, close their feet upon a root hair, and begin.

The power would not make the tide gentle everywhere. The animal fed on what the water brought. The bank breathed through the passages that also drew things away.

Across the estuary, a pump would continue to work.

The power would follow its vibration through the ground. It would find a worn mounting, a load carried unevenly, a speed at which the machine and the clay shelf answered one another. None of this would be difficult to change. What would require care was changing it without passing the strain somewhere else.

Before the next night shift, someone checking the pump would notice the mounting.

There would be time to repair it. The right part would be available. The person who had reported the same fault twice would be listened to now.

The pump would not fall silent. Water needed moving on that shore; people worked there, and other people depended on their work. But its force would cease to gather so strongly in this particular piece of clay.

Inside the hollow, a hanging drop would swell.

It would fall without a scattering of grains following it from the roof.

The animal’s cups would pause at the start of the engine, then open again. For some time its feet would continue seeking three holds whenever the pitch changed. The power would leave it that caution. There was no need to reach into the animal and remove what had helped it survive.

It would turn next to the fresh water.

Far above the shelf, where a shallow ditch was about to be deepened, someone would question the line marked on a plan. The ditch would be moved a little. Rain would still leave the field, but the old slow passage through roots and sediment would remain.

No warmer spring would suddenly enter the hollow. No abundance would be poured into the pool. The water would continue to bead on the clay, cold and poor in food, carrying the small quantities on which this place had assembled itself.

There was one piece of roof the power would touch directly.

It had already separated along three sides. Its remaining attachment lay above the lowest moss cushion. Even without the machines, it would fall before the animal could pass its weight out from beneath it.

The power would hold it until high water.

By then the animal would have loosened around the roots. A fold would be moving into the wet space behind the fern. The last feet would release their old purchase.

The clay would fall after that.

It would strike the pool, turn, and settle near the wall. Silt would rise. The shrimps would vanish into shelter. For a while every cup would remain closed.

Then the water would clear.

The fallen plate would leave a small recess behind it. Seepage would gather there, shaded even when afternoon light crossed the entrance. In time, perhaps, moss would reach it. Perhaps one of the young would.

The power would not place one there.

It would remain near enough to notice what followed, without making its attention another pressure on the bank.

At low tide, the place would still require someone to kneel.

There would still be black water, sharp clay, a few square feet of green. The fern tip would rise carrying bubbles. Somewhere under it, a cup would close around a fragment too small to see.

Nothing in the hollow would announce that it had been spared a preventable harm.

The animal would pass the fragment beneath itself. One hidden portion of its body would test the bank.

It would find a hold.
