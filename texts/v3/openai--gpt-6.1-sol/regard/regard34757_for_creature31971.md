The power’s first impulse is to bring rain.

It is an old kindness, learned in other countries.

Then it notices how carefully a cut seals, how the houses hold their scarce fluids away from the air, how much life is carried in the apparently barren ground. It lets the rain remain elsewhere.

It does not turn the plateau green.

For a long time, it attends.

It listens to the buried chord and the smaller sounds inside it: the running colonies beneath kitchen floors, the strain in a repaired freight stem, the separate breathing of people waiting for a discharge. It watches a browser withdraw its mouth just before the surface hums. It watches a maintenance worker hold a wind screen for someone whose rib membranes are open.

There is suffering here. There is also competence, affection, appetite, a child delighted by a stolen curl of stair.

The power takes care not to confuse them.

***

The inhabitant at the doorway has spent the morning waiting for their house to wake.

The house is not dying. Three neighbors have already said this, and all three are right. Its lower chambers respond promptly. Its foundations carry weight without complaint. But somewhere above the entrance, an old repair has begun conducting instructions poorly. The doorway must now be asked several times.

The inhabitant presses a hand to the wall.

Behind them, a freight transfer passes through the square. Their other hand rises to their jaw.

When they lower it, someone is standing a little way off.

The stranger wears temporary soles. Their coat is plain, and its seams do not shine. They have chosen a body that can stand on the living pavement without alarming it.

“Are you waiting to go in?” they ask.

“I am waiting for it to let me.”

“Would you like help?”

The inhabitant examines the stranger’s hands.

“With the door?”

“If that is what you want.”

After a pause, the inhabitant moves aside.

The power places its palm below the old repair. It could make the whole house answer at once. Instead it follows the instruction as far as it travels, finds where it becomes confused, and gives one small pressure.

The wall loosens.

“There,” says the inhabitant, with irritation so familiar it is almost tenderness.

They enter. After a moment, the doorway opens again.

“You can come in.”

***

Inside, the inhabitant unfolds from their outdoor posture. They are taller than the power’s chosen body.

There is a meal box on a shelf, unopened. A recording strip lies beside it, crowded with the marks of unfinished work.

The inhabitant is one of those who notice slow changes. For years they have helped the maintenance crews decide where to spend their effort. Lately, a hand against any connected surface brings them too much: a basin settling, an overloaded crop stem, the contradictory requests of children, a tower drawing wind across a connection that should have been rested.

They have become very useful.

People say this whenever they look tired.

The power sits where it is shown.

Outside, something heavy adjusts beneath the square. The inhabitant pauses with a food wafer halfway to their mouth.

“What would you ask for,” the power says, “if you did not have to decide whether it was reasonable?”

The inhabitant makes a low sound that might be amusement.

“A meal before anyone needs me.”

The power waits.

“And?”

The inhabitant looks toward the wall.

“To stop feeling that every loose threshold has happened inside my own bones.”

They press the wafer between their cheek plates.

“I don’t want a detached room yet.”

“Then we will not detach it.”

***

They begin with the chair.

The power asks before touching its roots. It asks the inhabitant where the ordinary sensations end and the tiring ones begin. This takes longer than repairing the door.

An interruption that seems trivial to the stranger is sometimes important: the arrival of a familiar neighbor, the pressure of a household member’s hand outside. A broad structural signal, useful in a public square, may be unbearable at supper.

The chair learns slowly.

For a while it muffles everything, and the inhabitant stands up at once.

“No.”

The power releases the change.

For another while it makes every signal equally faint. This is worse. The inhabitant finds themself straining to hear.

At last they find a form that works: a folded layer of living tissue between the seat and its roots, held open or closed by a small movement of the thumb. It can soften the broad traffic of the town without excluding a touch deliberately sent to this room. Urgent warnings still pass through.

The inhabitant sits.

A freight husk moves across the square. Their hand starts toward their jaw, then stops.

“That,” they say.

They finish the meal.

The power does nothing while they eat.

***

By the following evening, there are visitors.

Some want chairs. One wants an entire bedroom. A tower worker wants to know whether the same tissue might spare their fingertips. A shoemaker studies the fold and says its third layer will split within a season.

The power thanks the shoemaker.

Together they change it.

The town does not accept the new growth everywhere. In one square it retracts from a trial bench overnight. In another it sends roots around the softened connection, restoring almost all the noise.

The maintenance crews recognize this sort of difficulty. They move the benches rather than forcing the ground.

An elderly person tries one, disapproves, and asks for their old seat back.

They get it.

Another stays until dusk, then begins to cry with their mouth tightly closed. Their companion sits beside them, not touching, until a pale hand reaches across the space between the seats.

The power learns where to stand and where to leave.

***

There are other things it can do.

Beneath an out-of-service freight stem, it finds a fracture no instrument has yet reached. It brings the shape of that fracture to the crews in a form their hands can read. They argue over the repair. The power supplies material where they ask for it and holds the load while they cut.

Small animals are moved from the intake mouth first.

At a weather tower, it helps make a connection that releases before the operator’s fingers begin to burn. The workers test it against difficult winds. Twice it shuts too early; once it does not shut at all. No one is hurt, because the power remains there through the testing.

When it works, they record how it works.

The power could simply leave flawless objects behind. Instead, it stays until the shoemakers, growers, and tower crews can cultivate replacements, identify faults, and alter the designs without calling it back.

It does not erase the broad, lumpy repairs.

At the Split That Held, it lays a hand against the scar and feels what the old workers achieved with insufficient material and failing light. There is nothing shameful in the shape of it.

***

The inhabitant’s chair does not solve the town’s need for them.

People still knock during meals.

For some days they sit in their newly quiet room and feel guilty.

This, the power cannot mend in the chair.

It asks whether the inhabitant wants help speaking to the others. They do.

At the next gathering, the inhabitant explains which tasks must no longer come to their doorway every day. Their voice is quiet. Several neighbors are surprised; two are offended. One begins describing an urgent problem with a basin.

The inhabitant’s hand closes against their sleeve.

“Tomorrow,” they say.

The basin is looked at tomorrow. It survives the night.

Over the following weeks, three younger workers begin learning to read the slower signals. They are clumsy at first. One asks too hard and receives a confusing account of nearly every building in the depression.

The inhabitant laughs.

Then, with considerable patience, they begin again.

***

The power stays through a season of wind.

It leaves behind no palace, no demand for gratitude, no glowing mark by which the town must remember it. There are rested hands, some sounder connections, several disagreeable meetings, and new growth whose uses are still being discovered.

On its last evening, it returns to the house by the square.

The doorway opens on the first request.

Inside, the inhabitant is repairing a coat. A meal box sits empty beside them. Through the window membrane, children’s bright seams move between the rooted benches.

“Will you come back?” they ask.

“If you want me to.”

The inhabitant considers this.

“There will be things we haven’t thought of.”

“Yes.”

Outside, the black vanes rise. The country prepares to take the charge.

The inhabitant sets down the coat and turns the small fold beneath their chair, opening it. For tonight, they want to feel the approaching work.

The power sits with them.

When the flash comes, violet light runs through the wall. The thunder reaches their feet. Across the square someone steadies a bowl; farther away, a worker feels a root settle into its repaired support.

The inhabitant rests one hand against the house.

After the light has passed, they pick up the coat again.
