The power did not enter through the vestibule.

It could have. It could have made itself a body suited to the garden, or stood in the water wearing the appearance of someone Iven would trust. Instead, it waited outside the walls until she had finished measuring the ledge.

Iven put away the calipers. She rested her hand on the warm patch.

On the ceramic card beside her, beneath the day’s measurements, a line appeared.

*May I speak here?*

She looked at the pencil clipped to the rack. Then she turned the card over, examined its back, and returned it to its slot.

“Yes.”

The next words appeared slowly enough to read.

*I have been looking at this place. I would like to help, if there is something you want done.*

She remained still for several breaths.

“Who sent you?”

*No one.*

“Are you from the tower?”

*No.*

She took the card out again.

“Can you hear me everywhere?”

*Not unless you want me to.*

“Here, then. At the niche.”

*Here.*

“And not when the card’s turned over.”

The writing stopped.

She waited, then turned the card face down.

Nothing happened.

For a while she went about her work. A mat had begun to obstruct the flow beneath the lowest terrace. She lifted it with a hooked tool, slipped a spacer underneath, and watched the loosened fibers settle into the new current. Returning to the niche, she turned the card up.

*I am still here.*

“What sort of help?”

*I could change things. I would rather understand them first.*

Iven considered this.

“The third joint.”

***

The third joint was far below her, in a section of the tower that narrowed between two truss assemblies. Its inspection platform had room for one person and no room to turn around. Iven had last visited it before the filling.

A seal there had developed a small irregularity. Under ordinary load it held. During a certain combination of wind and pumping pressure, it flexed against its housing and made a sound that traveled upward through the spine.

The power examined it without touching it.

It also examined the inspection history, the replacement specification, the access route, and the worker who would ordinarily be sent to repair it. There was a newer seal in storage, but its housing had been made to a slightly different tolerance. Installing it would probably quiet the joint. It might also transfer the strain to the coupling below.

At the niche, the card acquired a drawing.

Iven looked at it for a long time.

“That’s not in the service diagrams.”

*No.*

She traced the altered profile with one damp fingertip.

“Can you make that?”

*Yes.*

“Make two. Put the other in stores with the dimensions.”

The power did.

It did not simply remove the vibration. It replaced the worn component, recorded what had been changed, and left the old seal on the inspection bench. The tower’s maintenance staff found a completed repair that they could examine, dispute, and eventually repeat.

That evening Iven rested her forehead against the ceramic sleeve.

The faint passage of water remained. The other sound was gone.

She returned to the niche.

“Yes,” she said. “That.”

***

The power stayed.

Not continuously at the card. Sometimes several days passed before Iven turned it over, and sometimes she had nothing to say when she did.

It learned the garden partly through her records and partly by watching what she did not record. It noticed the extra seconds she spent checking a fastening she had checked yesterday. It noticed that she ate less on cartridge days because sorting the displaced roots took longer than she expected. It noticed the way she rubbed beneath the collar after returning from the clinic.

It did not infer from these things that she wished to leave.

Once, it asked.

“No.”

Then, after a pause:

“I’d like leaving to be less of an undertaking.”

They worked on that.

There was already a heated bench outside the vestibule. The power asked permission to alter the small room around it. Iven refused the first design because the handholds were in the wrong places, and the second because it made the doorway difficult to see from the bench.

The third had a rail at hip height, a foot support, and a warming system that did not blow air across her skin. Her dry clothes could be brought to her without anyone needing to watch her struggle into them. The transition in pressure and temperature became more gradual.

None of this made her limbs light outside the water. It made the first few minutes manageable.

She began staying down for lunch after her examinations.

On one visit she bought something sour wrapped in paper. She brought it back in a sealed tin and ate it beneath the hood, carefully, to keep the crumbs contained.

***

The garden’s breathable reserve concerned the power.

Iven showed it the old maintenance diagram, then explained all the ways the actual canopy differed from it. The bladders had grown around the lattice. Some had divided. Others shared passages that had not existed at the first filling.

“Don’t clear the inspection plate,” she said.

*I have read that card.*

“You haven’t lain under those valves.”

It left the plant alone.

With Iven and two tower technicians, it designed a second reserve in a disused service cavity below the stairs. The cavity was unsuitable at first. It held electrical fittings, and the only route to it passed behind a mature root mat. One technician proposed moving the mat. Iven proposed waiting until the next cartridge change.

They waited.

The new reserve had ordinary fittings and an ordinary gauge. It could supply her collar if the canopy failed. It could also support visitors without drawing down the garden’s stored air.

The power would have been able to keep Iven breathing itself. It chose not to make that the plan.

At her insistence, everyone involved practiced switching supplies in darkness.

The technician with the music recordings was quickest on the second attempt. Iven was quickest on the first.

***

There were other things the power could have done.

It could have restored her skin, altered her lungs, eased every burden water and time had placed on her body. It could have made the garden larger without moving its walls.

It described some possibilities when she asked. She listened, questioned it closely, and declined most of them.

“Not the lungs.”

*All right.*

“And I want to keep feeling pressure.”

*All right.*

She did ask whether the collar mark could stop itching.

That required less than either of them had expected. The seal was changed to a softer material, shaped to distribute its load. Her clinician examined it and requested samples. Iven kept the old collar in her case for several weeks before accepting that she would not need it again.

She wanted no animals introduced. She wanted the west cups examined. She wanted to know whether the closed panel could become a window without sacrificing the roots behind it.

She wanted the power to stop writing suggestions on cards used for flow records.

A separate card was placed in the niche.

***

The ledge continued to grow.

The power could have completed it in an instant. Iven did not ask, and it did not offer. The warmth beneath her palm, the measurements too small to see, the roots waiting in the wall: these were things she liked attending to.

One evening she turned over the separate card.

“If I’m away when the ports open, someone will put a tray there.”

*Would that be wrong?*

“No. But I’ve been thinking about the sling.”

She fetched a length of cord and secured it provisionally between two attachment points. One end was too high. She shifted it, settled her weight, and looked along the lower edge of the ledge to judge the current.

There was room for her shoulder. There would eventually be room for the rest.

“Can you leave a note in the plan?”

*What should it say?*

She reached for the grease pencil herself.

*Keep this space open for the keeper’s sling. Ask Iven before fitting anything.*

The power entered the note in the tower’s plans. The next technician who came up noticed it, checked the attachment points, and suggested a fastening that would be easier to release with one hand.

Iven tried it. She approved.

***

In time, another keeper began coming two mornings a week.

Not because the power had decided Iven needed company. She had asked for someone who could handle a cartridge change without supervision, so she could go down when she pleased rather than when the schedule allowed.

The new keeper was careful and initially touched everything too lightly. Iven taught her where force was necessary. They disagreed about the pale runner. They learned to work together.

The power remained available at the niche.

Some weeks it was asked only a question about a material. Once it helped prevent a failure three kilometers below them. Once Iven turned the card over to report that the window had opened and the roots were unharmed.

Sometimes she turned it over, read the last exchange, and turned it back without adding anything.

By then the garden had acquired a spare seal, a second air reserve, better records, and another pair of practiced hands. Iven could leave for an afternoon without measuring the afternoon against every task waiting for her return.

She still woke before the gauge changed.

She still pruned.

And when the ledge was ready, she moved her sling into the space she had kept open.
