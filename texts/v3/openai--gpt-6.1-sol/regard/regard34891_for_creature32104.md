The power first made itself a pair of sandals.

It could have entered the hollow without feet, without weight, without anything the wind could catch. But the people would have had no way to know where it was standing, and there were already enough things here whose positions had to be guessed.

It came through the open side late in the dim day, carrying nothing.

Eren saw it and raised her pole.

“Stop there.”

It stopped.

She listened. Beneath the stranger’s right foot came three faint knocks, a pause, then the sound of something scraping.

“Back. Not toward me. Back.”

It stepped backward.

Later, when they understood something of what had arrived, Eren would remember that it had obeyed her. At the time she merely pointed out a safer crossing.

***

They gave it pith.

The piece was small. A stranger meant another mouth until it proved otherwise, and the stores had recently been opened and counted.

The power ate slowly, as they did. It did not make water appear in its mouth. It did not ask how people could live in such a place.

Tal inspected its sandals.

The weaving was competent, but unfamiliar. She turned one edge between her fingers.

“Who made these?”

“I did.”

“You left this loose.”

She showed it a loop near the heel.

The power tightened the loop.

***

For several days it worked where it was asked to work.

It carried cooking weights. It held a leaf packet while a woman sealed its edges. It retrieved a dropped needle without reaching beneath the root-hook that had trapped it.

When the wind reversed, it lay behind a stone rib with the others.

It could have stilled the gust. Instead, it caught a torn cloak before the weighted hem struck Tal across the face. Afterward it helped count the people.

Eleven.

Then it lifted an overturned slab from a patch of copper foliage. The stems underneath were bent but unbroken. Eren knelt to free their hooks.

“Not straight up,” she said. “You’ll pull the little ones out with it.”

The power tilted the slab.

There were things it could learn only by being corrected.

***

In the narrow shelter, the old man asked it whether it was useful.

“Sometimes.”

“Everyone here is useful sometimes. Can you do anything about this?”

He lifted his hand toward his chest, then let it fall before reaching it.

The power sat beside the shelf.

It knew what had tightened there. It knew the slow damage, the exhausted muscles, the ways his body had accommodated one injury by making another. It could have changed a great deal.

It asked him what he wanted.

“To breathe without having to attend to it.”

“And besides that?”

He looked irritated.

“That first.”

So it eased his breathing.

Not with a light or a word spoken over him. One breath simply passed without the small struggle at its end. Then another.

The old woman feeding him waited with the pith in her hand.

He took it.

After a while he said, “That’s less scorched.”

“It’s the same piece.”

“Then you’ve improved while I wasn’t watching.”

The power remained beside him until he slept. When he woke, it explained what else it might do, what could be restored, and what would remain uncertain even afterward.

He asked questions. Some he asked twice.

It answered each time.

***

On the fifth day, the people gathered under the roof of overlapping scales.

The power told them what it was.

No one bowed.

The man with the fitted scraper asked whether it had been responsible for the unusually violent breath two seasons before. When it said no, he asked whether it could have prevented it.

“Yes.”

He considered this.

“You weren’t here.”

“No.”

The conversation stopped for a while.

Outside, a hunter struck the wall and flung itself upward.

Eventually Eren said, “You’re here now.”

“Yes.”

“What are you intending?”

“I have seen things I could help with. I wanted to ask before changing them.”

They began with the ground.

***

There was a slab between the stores and the cooking seam that had broken three times in living memory. Beneath it, burrowers worked a passage whose roof had become thin.

The power could have filled the passage. It could have made the whole pavement solid.

Instead, Eren lay down with her ear against the stone. Two others joined her. For a long time there was only scraping.

Then the power showed them the shape beneath the slab.

It made a small likeness in red dust: the passage, its branching chambers, the place where a low shoulder of rock forced the burrowers close to the surface.

The older boy fetched Tal to look.

“Is that where they sleep?”

“Sometimes,” said the power.

“Don’t fill it.”

“We weren’t going to,” Eren said.

They settled on a bridge.

The power strengthened two ribs of stone on either side and laid a shallow arch between them. Beneath it, the passage remained open. Along its edges it left a roughness that could be felt through a sandal.

They walked across it carrying baskets. They crouched underneath the shelter roof and listened for changes in the knocks.

The burrowers continued working.

Only then did they ask for another.

***

The missing woman was alive.

The power told them this when they asked, and the hollow became very quiet.

She had found shelter in another warm cleft beyond the peaks. A fall had hurt her knee. She had enough food, but the return crossing was longer than she could presently manage.

“Bring her back,” someone said.

Eren looked at the power.

“Does she want to come?”

“I haven’t asked her.”

“Then ask.”

It went.

The woman wanted to return, though first she wanted help finishing a splint, and then she wanted to show it something she had found growing along the underside of a mineral shelf.

They came back with the splint, three wrapped cuttings, and a story that took most of an evening to tell.

There were twelve people in the hollow.

The power slept outside.

***

Its largest work was above the shelters.

It proposed a curved lip of stone, set high enough that hunters could still reach their ledges. This would divide the worst returning gust before it struck the roof and the narrow doorway.

The people argued over its shape.

A first trial sent too much air toward the cooking seam. A second disturbed a nesting ledge. The hunters abandoned it for two days, and Tal accused the power of making a bad wall.

It took that part down.

The third shape held.

During the next reversal, the door of the recess stayed in its frame. Dust still entered. Fronds still struck one another. A tall stem burst beside the Copper Belly, and everyone felt the report in their teeth.

But the roof cords did not snap.

Afterward the people counted twelve, then went out with their baskets.

The power went with them.

***

There were requests it did not immediately grant.

Someone wanted the hunters driven away. Someone wanted every hot patch made safe for bare feet. Tal wanted pale animals never to be eaten by grazers.

It did not dismiss these wishes. It showed where the heat went, what the hunters took, what happened beneath leaves when certain small lives grew too numerous. Sometimes the people changed their minds. Sometimes they changed the request.

For Tal, it made no promise about every pale animal.

It helped her cut a second scoop, wider and smoother at the edge. Together they placed loose, low shelters of stone along the stretches most often uncovered by moving roots. Tal checked them each morning.

Not every shelter was occupied. She moved the empty ones.

***

Eren asked nothing about her eye until well into the season.

By then she had watched the old man leave the recess and sit beside the stores with a scraper in his lap. She had watched him tire and go back inside. He was stronger, though not young, and his complaints had acquired subjects beyond his chest.

One evening she touched the clouded side of her face.

“Could you?”

“Yes.”

“Would I see as I used to?”

“Probably. I would examine it first.”

She stood with her hand against a stem of the One That Clicks.

“I’ll think about it.”

The power waited.

Three days later she came to it.

***

It did not remain forever in the center of their attention.

There were meals to prepare, sandals to unweave, tools to find after the Basket passed. Its presence became part of the work without making the work disappear.

Sometimes it was away. Before leaving, it said where it intended to go and how it could be called. It left no shrine, no requirement, no task of gratitude.

It returned to find changes it had not made.

A new sleeping shelf. A repaired roof cord. Tal’s scoops hanging out of the reach of hunters. The returned woman’s cuttings taking hold in a warm crack.

Eren had shifted the warning stakes.

Across the bare stone, the One That Clicks was moving toward a fresh seam. Its outer roots had begun to obstruct the cooking path.

The power approached with a wedge.

Eren studied the leaning stems.

“Leave it a little longer,” she said. “There’s room yet.”

So it set the wedge down.

A root-hook reached forward, tested the stone, and found a hold.

Beside Eren, the power waited to see where the garden would go.
