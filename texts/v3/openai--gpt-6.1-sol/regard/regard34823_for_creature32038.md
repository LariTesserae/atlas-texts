At first the power did nothing that could be distinguished from waiting.

It attended to the water beneath the hollow, where the passages were not passages in any human sense, but changes in the closeness of stone. It followed the pressure that turned the flywheel. It learned which pools emptied into darkness and which received water from it, and how far the animals went when they entered the downward pipe.

It did not mistake the absence of grass for a wound.

Nor did it bring sunlight into the seams. There were lives here for which the narrow pieces of sky were already more than sufficient.

Beneath the bottom step, an adult rested with one feeler against a grain of shell.

A boot had come down on it earlier that day. Nothing had broken. The animal’s plates had tightened, and its limbs had folded into the spaces between them. But the load had shifted a small stone under its body. Now one of its rear limbs could not extend completely.

It could have pulled free by leaving the soft end of the limb behind. It had begun the movements that would accomplish this: a slight forward pressure, a pause, then pressure again.

The power knew what those movements would cost.

It also knew that the animal had no use for a voice speaking from above the water.

So it made one grain of limestone loosen.

The grain was smaller than the white fragments in the mud. Water carried it away. The stone that held the limb settled into the space it left, and the pressure eased.

The animal remained still for a long time.

Then the limb opened.

Nothing thanked anything. The feeler continued resting against the shell grain. Farther out, a worm drew itself through a loop of cable, and the pressure gauge moved another small distance behind its clouded glass.

The power waited through several turnings of the flywheel.

It found that much of what looked dangerous belonged to the ordinary life of the hollow. A basin emptied, and creatures followed the retreating water. A hunter took a grazer. An old plate broke, exposing its soft interior to the animals in a footprint. These things did not become mistakes because a power had arrived that could prevent them.

But there were other dangers.

Deep inside the downward pipe, a collar of rust had begun to separate from the iron. Each movement of water lifted it a little. An adult could pass beneath it when the pressure was low. When the valve closed, the collar dropped across the passage.

There were plates caught behind it.

The power did not scour the pipe clean. The metal-fed film would have gone with the rust, and the creatures at the mouth depended upon it. Instead, it loosened the collar along the fracture already running through it. Over several days, the rust came away in small pieces. Grazers examined them. Water carried them into places where they could settle without stopping a body.

The pipe remained narrow.

It remained dark.

An animal entering it still paused with its rear plates exposed, testing the wall with its feelers. But there was no longer a loose edge waiting to fall behind its head.

At the steps, the power considered a larger alteration.

It could have raised the bottom one above the water. It could have opened a dry route through the hollow, widened the passages, made the pump house safe to enter.

It watched the adults cross the worn center. It watched the small animals shelter beneath the stone, and clear water well through the grit beside them.

The step was not merely a place for a foot.

Instead, a narrow crack in its front edge admitted a little more seepage. The water worked there patiently. By the end of the wet season, a shallow recess had formed beneath the edge, wide enough for an adult to draw its limbs clear of the place where a person’s weight would fall.

No animal was put into it.

For some time, none entered.

Then one arrived backward from the darker seam, resting between movements. Its foremost feelers touched the recess. It went farther in, stopped, and remained until the basin began to drain.

The power attended especially to this animal, though it did not set it apart from the others. It learned the routes it used, the surfaces on which it fed, the particular weight behind which it rested in dry weather.

Once, after shedding, the animal lay beneath the pump-house threshold with its new plates still soft.

A pale hunter waited outside.

The power felt the impulse to close the gap between them. It could have made the hunter turn aside. It could have kept the adult hidden until every plate had hardened.

Instead, it listened to the water passing beneath the threshold.

The adult was far enough in. The hunter could not reach it. There was no need to improve upon the shelter it had found.

Later, the animal emerged and fed along the brick, leaving two clean tracks through the film.

The power’s attention did not remain so close forever.

It had learned that there would always be something to alter: an awkward ridge, a hungry mouth, a pool that emptied before a small creature found its way out. With sufficient care, it could have arranged every crossing. Nothing would have needed to feel ahead through the water.

It did not make that hollow.

It left the animals their movements, and the pools their unequal levels. The delayed reflections continued to loosen beneath withdrawing limbs. The flywheel turned at irregular intervals. The chains gathered condensation and occasionally gave their low, brief note against the stone.

Only a few things were different.

A limb could open beneath the step.

The downward pipe no longer held a falling ring of iron.

And beneath the worn center, where a boot might come down, there was room to move aside without tearing anything away.

One evening the animal rested there as someone descended from the lane.

The first foot stopped on the second step. The person could not see what lay below. Water covered the worn center, and the light made the shallow basin look deep.

Under the stone, the animal felt the change in pressure.

Its limbs folded. Its plates slid over one another. It moved sideways into the new recess, slowly enough that its underside never lost contact with the rock.

The boot came down.

A little clear water rose around it, carrying a few white grains.

When the foot lifted, the animal waited. Then it extended a feeler into the water and returned to the place it had chosen.
