The power did not quiet the district so that it could think.

It listened in the noise.

At first it listened from the open sky, where the overlapping calls seemed to make a single enormous disturbance. Then it came lower. It learned the intervals between the beetles’ blows, the wet clicks inside the gutters, the different ways a frog could interrupt itself. It learned that a wall could be crowded and a street empty without either condition being a mistake.

For a long time it did nothing that could be distinguished from warm weather.

When it entered the market, it took care to have weight.

The floor tightened beneath it. A pale creature in a ceiling vein stopped, reversed, and continued along another branch. Somewhere a door released a little of its pressure.

The inhabitant beside the split seed looked up.

The power waited until the small thing under the husk let go of the inhabitant’s finger. Then it placed a hand against the bowl.

Its hand was not luminous. There was no reason for it to be luminous.

It tapped twice, waited, and tapped again.

The inhabitant watched it.

Something under the market answered with a brief, complicated scraping.

The power had said something incorrectly.

It began again.

***

It could have understood the district all at once.

There were ways of knowing that would have opened every chamber, followed every root, and laid out the lives inside them without requiring an entrance. But the district’s inhabitants did not know one another that way.

They listened. They missed things. They returned.

The power learned enough to visit.

It learned that the tapping taught to strangers was not a greeting, exactly. It meant something closer to: *I am here, and I have left room for an answer.*

The reply varied according to where one placed one’s hand.

At a public basin it included the depth of the stored water. At an old doorway it carried traces of those who had widened it. Beside the seed, the reply was interrupted repeatedly by a new, uncertain rhythm that had not yet learned how to travel beyond its own root.

The power did not correct that rhythm.

It put another finger against the bowl so that the small one could reach it.

***

There was suffering here.

Not everywhere, and not in everything that would have frightened a visitor.

The eggs beneath the plates of a head were being tended. The black crickets were welcome. The immense chewing beneath the square was neither punishment nor captivity.

But one creature had a joint that hurt whenever the day cooled. A young one had lost the particular animal whose call helped it find its sleeping recess. In a gallery beyond three unreliable corners, an inhabitant had been waiting for a reply from someone who would not answer again.

The power did not put these pains into one category.

For the aching joint, it offered warmth.

It offered it first through the wall where the creature rested, only a small patch. The creature moved away. The power withdrew the warmth.

Later the creature returned and laid the sore joint against that place.

This time the heat came slowly.

For the youngster, the power searched. It found the missing animal alive inside an abandoned rain passage, feeding in a pocket of last week’s damp. It did not fetch it in a closed fist. It brought the two routes briefly together.

The youngster heard the call, scrambled through a low opening, and came back with both hands cupped against its chest.

For the one who waited, there was no corresponding repair.

The power sat on the opposite side of the gallery. After a while, the inhabitant sent the unanswered rhythm again.

The power received it.

It did not reply in the voice of the dead.

***

The old inhabitant who occupied the side of a room noticed the visitor on the fourth evening.

Its eyes had just been cleaned. The younger creature was still there, peeling a stubborn thread of growth from the corner of one fold.

A crease opened low in the wall.

The power placed its hand inside.

What came through was slow. Some of it arrived in the palm, some through the floor, and some through a ceiling finger so distant that the power had not known it belonged to this body.

There were memories in the message, but it was not only memory.

The old one wanted to go to the market.

The wish had become difficult to express because so much of the body now carried other lives. A hollow behind its shoulder held nesting frogs. Its spread torso supported the roof. Two young inhabitants slept in a recess made by the inside of its elbow.

It did not want those things destroyed.

It wanted the market.

The power asked a question.

The wall held its hand more firmly.

***

The work took eleven days.

It could have taken less, but the frogs needed time to accept another chamber, and a supporting root could not thicken safely while its sap was running elsewhere.

The power worked with those who already knew the room.

One inhabitant persuaded a seam to open. Another tested the roof each morning. The younger visitor kept cleaning the old one’s eyes, though now it also brought gossip from the market and pressed it into the wall in short, lively bursts.

New roots grew beneath the old fingers.

The power gave them no shape beyond the strength they needed. They twisted around existing passages, left the nesting ledges intact, and thickened wherever the workers leaned their weight.

At night, the old inhabitant practiced moving one hand.

Animals came out of the loosening folds. Some went into the walls. Some remained on the body. The power attended to the small ruptures, keeping them moist while their edges found new places to close.

On the eleventh morning, the old one separated from the room.

It was less like a person standing than like a room discovering that it could kneel.

The body was broad and strangely thin. One arm still bore the curve of the ceiling. There were openings through which daylight passed.

The younger inhabitant offered a shoulder.

The power offered nothing until the old one reached for it.

Together they crossed the gallery.

They stopped often.

At the market, the old one rested beside the fruit bowl and put its hand against the split seed.

The small glossy eye turned.

Under the husk, something extended a folded, unfinished limb and touched the old creature’s palm.

All around them the district roared.

***

The power stayed through a rain.

It let the roofs open themselves. It did not fill every basin to the brim, or abolish the dry rooms, or forbid one animal to eat another.

When water rushed toward a weakened channel, it held the channel steady until three inhabitants arrived to braid it. When the enormous body under the square paused in its chewing, it waited, as the others waited, until the work began again.

It learned where help was welcome.

Sometimes that place was very small.

A little pressure taken off a root. A fever eased. Enough time for a trapped wing to be withdrawn whole.

When it left, it left no throne, no sign, no permanent opening in the sky.

But beside each public basin there was a new patch in the green lining, no larger than a resting hand.

The inhabitants tested these patches cautiously. Water still passed through them. Worms grazed their edges. A beetle struck one and got an ordinary beetle’s answer.

Only when someone placed a hand there and made the patient rhythm taught to strangers did something farther away listen.

Even then, the first response was not a remedy.

It was room for the next thing they wished to say.
