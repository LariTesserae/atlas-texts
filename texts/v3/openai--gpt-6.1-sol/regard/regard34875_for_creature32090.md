The power does not enter by the opening.

That would put its shadow across the pools, and it has noticed how carefully the water holds its light.

For a while it attends from nowhere in particular. It follows the pressure between the mineral columns, the bright specks inside the transparent creatures, the warmth available beneath widely separated patches of moss. It does not try to assemble the animal into a shape it could draw. The animal is already whole, whether or not its parts can be joined by a line through space.

Nor does it silence the mountain to hear what lies beneath the noise.

There may be nothing beneath it. The sound may be as much the animal’s home as the rock.

The power has seen places held at the edge of catastrophe: a cup forever falling toward a floor, a body kept within the last instant before pain. It has also seen places where a moment was made spacious because someone could not bear to leave it.

This hollow resembles both.

Resemblance is not enough.

It attends to the small plant beneath the jaw.

The lifted earth does not crumble. The root hairs do not dry. The green strand remains unharmed between the teeth. There is no struggle that the power can discover, but neither is there the ordinary evidence of ease: a movement completed, followed by another freely begun.

It cannot tell whether the animal has found something precious or is forever finding it.

So it does not pull the plant free.

It does not loosen the jaw.

It makes itself available at the place where the unseen muzzle met the visitor’s fingers.

There is no hand this time. Only a small region of warmth, with no demand concealed inside it.

The animal makes contact.

The pressure is broad and soft. A little moisture gathers where there is nothing to wet. The power receives the contact without reaching through it into the animal’s mind.

For a long while, this is all they do.

Outside, clouds pass beneath the lip. Snow loosens on a distant mountain and slides into a valley. The power allows those things to take their time.

Within the hollow, the great eye remains partway through its blink.

Then the power offers something very small.

Not another world. Not a body remade to fit ordinary distances. Not a command to wake.

It offers a place where one movement could finish.

The place is no larger than the hollow beneath the animal’s mouth. It is not laid over the plant or cut into the rock. Nothing already there must make room for it. It is held beside the contact, as a cupped palm might be held beside a resting face.

Within it, a drop of water can fall.

The animal need not use it.

The power does not demonstrate by taking one of the enduring beads from a leaf. Those belong to the hollow. Instead, it brings a little water of its own and lets the animal feel the weight: the gathering heaviness, the narrowing attachment, the possibility of release.

Then it waits.

The mountain works.

The six-limbed creatures feed from the skin. The birds occupy their patches of cliff. Water shines in the exact clearances of the turning stone.

No one is hurried toward an answer.

Eventually, the pressure against the power changes.

It is a minute change. A little more of the lip rests against the offered place. The warmth deepens at one edge.

The power follows that movement only as far as it goes.

The drop falls.

It travels less than the width of a tooth and lands against the soft surface of the lip.

There is a small sound.

It is nearly lost beneath the mountain: not a click, not a hiss, but the blunt, quiet sound of water meeting flesh.

The animal does not withdraw.

A second drop is not supplied.

The power will not turn one accepted thing into an endless procession of others.

Where the water touched, there is now a wetness that was not there before. The animal holds it. For the first time since the power began attending, there is something in the hollow that can be distinguished from its own absence.

Nothing else has been required to change.

The fern tip remains tender. The pale root hairs shine. The jaw continues its immense, delicate closing.

After a while, the animal moves its lip.

This movement has an end.

It is no more than the slight working of a mouth after drinking. The lip presses against the wetness, takes some of it inward, and comes to rest.

The power stays close.

It does not praise the animal for this. It does not mistake a sip for permission to set the whole mountain moving toward age.

But it knows now that there can be a next thing, if the animal wants one.

It leaves the offered place where the muzzle can find it.

Not a door standing open upon a frightening view. There is no wind from elsewhere, no brightness, no visible distance to cross. Only a little give in the world, available to the same gentleness with which the jaw holds the stem.

The power makes one further provision.

Whatever happens there will not pass from the animal into the hollow without its choosing. A completed movement will not drag the birds from their ledges. A breath allowed to end will not empty the pools. If the animal wishes to rest its mouth, the small plant will remain supported.

This does not make every choice harmless. There may someday be things the animal wants that cannot be had together.

But a sip need not cost it everything it knows.

The power settles into an attention that does not press against the rock.

Sometimes the animal touches it. Sometimes there is only the mountain, turning and turning, and the distant weather going on beyond the opening.

Nothing is extracted from these visits. No purpose is assigned to the plant. The power does not decide that the hollow has waited for it.

Once, much later by the reckoning of the valleys, the great eye closes completely.

Only for a little while.

When it opens, the plant is still there.

So is the warmth beside its mouth.
