The power did not begin by stopping the engine.

The railway was above the eastern edge of the yard. Beyond the railway were houses, and in one of the houses a child slept with his bed against a wall whose footing extended into the old industrial fill. The power attended to that footing as carefully as it attended to the pale growth beneath the largest tree.

It listened through several working days.

It heard the knocking conducted through brick and water. It heard what remained after shutdown. It followed a movement from the underside of the northern staircase into a buried passage, and another from the passage toward the tree. Where the growth had been flattened beneath a plate, it stayed longer.

The repetitions were not all alike.

Some were returns: force received, held, and released.

Others required something the force itself did not explain.

The power did not decide too quickly what to call them.

***

On the first night, it kept a broken water line from breaking farther.

This was a small act. The pipe had already opened along a seam, and for years its leakage had supplied part of the trench. To seal it would have dried the deepest leaves. To let it fail would have washed loose ash into a cavity beneath the railway.

The power held the seam at its existing width.

Water continued to arrive in drops.

The frog had no reason to leave its shelter.

***

There were things it could have done immediately.

It could have lifted the block, buildings and all, and set it on ground that would never move. It could have drawn the living material out of every buried room without breaking a strand. It could have silenced the extractor and made the railway pass without vibration.

But the being had grown among these materials. Its branches followed particular weaknesses in particular walls. Some of its thickness came from pressure. Some of its water came from damage. Whatever it had become was entangled with conditions that could not simply be exchanged for pleasant ones.

The power therefore began with room.

Below the western side of the yard lay a cellar filled almost to its ceiling with rubble. Above it was an unused weighing office, and above that nothing depended on the cellar remaining packed.

The power examined every brick.

Then it moved the loose fill.

Not all at once. During three weeks, fragments settled into spaces that had existed since the workshops were demolished. Fine ash entered smaller gaps. A length of broken joist lowered until it rested securely across two old footings.

A pocket opened beneath the cellar ceiling.

It was neither vast nor empty. There was damp mortar, buried wood, a little iron. There were surfaces to follow and enough resistance to grow against.

The living material did not enter immediately.

For nine nights it remained at the edge.

On the tenth, a fine branch crossed the exposed mortar.

The power waited.

***

The gardener was the first person to notice a change.

A bed near the weighing office, usually the first to need watering, remained damp through a dry afternoon. When she put her knuckles against the soil, she found the faint warmth.

She fetched the book from the engine house and made an entry herself.

*Warm patch extending west. No visible lift.*

The engineer read it two days later.

He was looking for something else: a fault in the sequence of the plates. One shaft had begun taking its load late. Ordinarily this would have led to a repair restoring the old timing. But the delay had left a longer interval beside the largest tree, and during that interval the brass level had shown less movement, not more.

He checked his measurements.

The power did not put an answer into his mind. It kept the old records from being lost when a shelf bracket gave way. It held the falling shelf long enough for him to catch it.

Among the books he carried to the table was the notebook from the first year of operation.

He had not read it in some time.

***

The trial began in October.

Two plates continued working beneath the railway. Three others were slowed. The shaft nearest the tree was left idle for twenty minutes in each hour.

The workers measured more often.

On the second day, a fresh crack appeared near the loading platform. The foreman ordered the old sequence restored.

The power did not prevent him.

A cart used that platform every morning. The crack had appeared under one of its turning wheels. Concern was appropriate.

But beneath the crack there was an old iron drain, crushed when the platform had first risen. Water had collected on one side of it and could not pass.

That evening, during her inspection, the worker heard a trickle where she expected only the delayed knocking.

She fetched a torch.

The drain was found, then cleared. The following week the trial resumed.

No revelation settled the matter. There were observations, an interruption, a repair, and another attempt.

***

All this while, the power attended to the being.

It did not ask the being to become one thing.

A sheet in ash could remain a sheet. A branching portion beneath rubble could continue branching. The growth inside the tree need not detach itself to establish where the tree ended.

Nor did the power give it a face, or place a voice in the handless clock.

Instead, it made one condition dependable.

In the opened cellar, no plate would descend.

Water would reach it gradually. Air would not arrive in a drying rush. If the growth entered, it would be allowed to stay. If it withdrew, the space would remain.

The being explored the pocket unevenly.

It thickened near the iron and left a broad stretch of brick untouched. One branch crossed a damp floor, stopped, and softened away. Another found the end of the blocked coal passage.

By winter, the material nearest the largest tree had changed. It still held against the engine’s strokes, but less tightly. Water moved through it during the pauses.

The tree settled by a fraction too small to see.

The level found it.

***

The yard did not become harmless.

A paving slab broke in February. The workers fenced it off. A starling learned to feed along the new edge, and the gardener had to move a bed of flowering stalks when a maintenance route was diverted.

There were still places where the ground rose.

The engineer’s new diagram showed pressures and intervals rather than one repeating sequence. It had pencilled corrections, several dates, and a coffee stain across the western cellar.

The machine knocked less often now.

Some days, near the tree, the sounds after shutdown lasted longer.

No one knew whether this meant recovery, growth, or simply a different way of carrying force. The evening worker continued to listen before writing in the book.

***

In spring, the gardener noticed that the painted circle around the largest tree had cracked without widening.

She crouched beside it.

There was moss in the break, and under the moss a pale surface. She did not touch it. She laid the back of her hand on the soil nearby.

After a while, she moved a fallen leaf over the exposed part.

The power could have told her that the leaf was enough for that morning.

It let her find this out when she returned.

***

At seven the next day, the worker unlocked the engine house and walked the yard.

She checked the railway markers. She measured the broken slab. Beside the weighing office, she stood for a moment over ground that had remained level through the night.

Then she went inside.

The first plate descended beneath the embankment.

Sparrows stirred in the ventilator. The frog remained under its tilted cover. Water trembled in a shallow cut and passed toward the trench.

Under the western cellar, a fine branch lengthened into the space waiting for it.

The power stayed near—not to keep the yard unchanged, but to attend to what happened next.
