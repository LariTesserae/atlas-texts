The power did not first enter the basin.

It attended from beyond the embankment, where the roof seemed to rest on the earth and the reeds concealed the steps. It watched a dry week, then a wet one. It watched the caretaker arrive with a key and leave with mud on the cuffs of his trousers. It followed the water beneath the building without disturbing it.

There were things it could have done immediately. It could have stopped the pumps, lifted the roof, washed every passage clean.

Instead it waited.

A pump stopped for an afternoon during that first month. The water settled into levels that looked more natural than the usual shifting pans. Under the mound, however, a feeding fold went slack. Fine matter remained in the water beyond it, unreachable. In the eastern gravel, several young pieces closed and did not open again.

When the pump resumed, a soft length of tissue tore against a pipe joint.

The power watched the torn ends contract. It watched the animal abandon one and recover the other.

It began to understand the difficulty of helping.

The basin’s injuries and provisions were entangled. Iron protected flesh and wore against it. A broken culvert admitted food through the same narrow place where a sudden pressure change could strip the feeding lips away. The building gave darkness, steadiness of temperature, and a weight that had to be held without interruption.

Even warmth was complicated. More of it would not necessarily be better.

The power waited until it could distinguish what the animal used from what it merely endured.

Then, beneath the station, it made a support.

This happened in a place no person had seen since the roof fell. There was a seam in the foundation, a pocket of gravel below it, and a stone held slightly too high by a thick fold of old flesh. Into the pocket the power fitted something with the strength of sound masonry. It had no sharp corners. Water could pass around it. Its upper surface came within the breadth of a hair of the stone.

For eleven days, nothing changed.

The animal continued holding the weight.

On the twelfth day, a contraction elsewhere drew a little tissue from beneath the stone. The stone descended until it met the support.

The animal tightened.

For a long time it stayed that way, braced against a weight that was no longer pressing quite so hard. Then it loosened by a small amount. The stone did not follow.

It loosened again.

Water entered between the flesh and the underside of the rubble. A surface that had been compressed for years unfolded into it. The surface was gray and thick, with pale ridges where younger tissue had grown against the old.

The power did not remove the stone. Nor did it draw the animal out.

It left the new gap available.

***

The caretaker returned in autumn.

He had been instructed to increase the flushing through the disused eastern intake. Sediment was accumulating upstream; an inspection sheet said the old route ought to remain clear.

As he put his key into the padlock, he noticed a drop of water falling from the doorframe.

It struck the same place on the concrete every few seconds.

There had always been dampness here. He almost went inside. Then he saw that the drop was issuing from a narrow crack beneath the blue marks, and that the crack continued down through the corner of the foundation.

He crouched.

With the pumps running, its edges moved.

Not visibly, exactly. He could feel it with his fingertips.

He telephoned someone.

The power had not invented the defect. The defect had been there for years. It had only cleared the sediment that concealed its upper end and given the seeping water a route to daylight.

There followed a delay, an inspection, another delay.

The intake was not flushed.

A pressure regulator was installed instead. It made no difference to the ordinary pulses. When pressure exceeded the familiar range, it diverted the excess through a sound line toward the river.

The first time it worked, the animal had already begun withdrawing from the threatened chamber. Its folds closed. The feeding lips flattened against stone.

The surge did not arrive.

For several hours it remained contracted. Then one lip opened.

On the next occasion, it withdrew again.

The power did not ask it to learn faster.

***

There was a place below the eastern channel where the smallest young were often stranded. Water reached a shallow depression, lingered, then escaped through a fissure too narrow for the folded pieces to follow.

The power deepened one side of the depression.

Not much. Less than the depth of a human fingernail.

It connected that side to a seam which remained wet even when the upper channel emptied. Three young pieces caught there that winter. One was eaten. One disappeared after a cold spell.

The third attached to the stone.

It fed badly at first. Its fold opened too late in the pulse and closed too soon. Sometimes an entire passage of suspended food went by while it remained shut.

The power could have placed food inside it.

It did not. The water already carried enough. What the small creature lacked was a secure place in which to continue making its imperfect attempts.

By spring it had grown around a grain of gravel. Water passed through its center. When the adult contracted nearby, the little fold contracted too, though not always at the same moment.

The power watched without deciding whether this was a new creature or a new part.

***

It made no garden of the basin.

The sedges remained sparse. The concrete remained stained. A beetle still had to move when water rose beneath it, and pale crustaceans still vanished into feeding folds. The river was cold.

There were fewer violent failures.

An exposed cable no longer carried current. A loose section of iron settled into a position where it could not shear across a passage. The steps received a handrail, after the caretaker slipped on them and asked for one. Visitors still had to watch where they put their feet.

The animal acquired no understanding of any of this.

Below the station, it extended into the gap above the new support, withdrew, and extended again. Part of the space became a passage. Part stayed empty. The old tissue that had held the stone gradually softened where water could now reach it.

One night, while rain tapped the low roof, that portion relaxed completely.

Elsewhere, feeding continued. Grit had to be expelled from a fold beneath the mound. Water cooled in the eastern channel. The animal still had work to do.

But under the fallen roof, for the first time in many years, it did not have to hold the building up.

The stone rested on its support.

A little water moved beneath it.

The flesh remained loose.
