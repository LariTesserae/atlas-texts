The power did not bring light into the hollow.

It waited near the fallen blocks until its presence had made a small disturbance in the water. Beneath the fitted slab, the upward palm turned. The inhabitant drew its arm back, and after a while its head appeared beside the basin.

The power had taken a shape that could sit on the stone. It had hands, and enough weight to make its movements plain. It climbed over the blocks rather than passing through them. At the narrow outlet it paused, putting one foot where it would not interrupt the flow.

The inhabitant watched.

At the slab, the power pointed to the unoccupied end.

The inhabitant looked there, then returned to its vessel. The power sat. When one of its feet covered the opening beneath the slab, the blunt fingers touched its ankle.

It moved its foot.

For a long time that was all.

The power could perceive the passages inside the limestone: the warm water moving through the dark, the places where it gathered minerals, the small lives carried with it. It could distinguish the living skin beneath the inhabitant’s plates from the deposits that had settled on them. It knew how little food arrived, and how slowly that food was used.

There was nothing here it needed to make abundant at once. The inhabitant was not hungry that day. Its shoulder was sound. The shallow bowl filled and emptied at the right rate.

So the power sat through a meal. It watched the vessel lifted, tilted, set down. It watched the inhabitant sweep a finger along the settled brown layer. When the pot was returned beneath the slab, it did not clean it or mend the broken handle. The remaining handle fitted the inhabitant’s grip.

Later, the inhabitant climbed to the dry shelf.

The power stayed seated while the two tools were brought down. The notch in the bowl had narrowed. The inhabitant put the working edge against it and struck three times, then rubbed the place with a fingertip. A small chip came free. Beneath it the fresh crust was still soft.

After several more blows, the inhabitant stopped. It rested its elbow on the ledge.

The power laid a hand nearby, palm upward.

The inhabitant inspected the hand. It touched the fingertips, turned one finger slightly, and pressed the thumb back toward the palm. Then it placed the hammerstone in it.

The power accepted the weight.

It did not strike immediately. The inhabitant repositioned the cutting edge, braced it with two fingers, and looked toward the hammerstone.

The first blow was too light.

The inhabitant made its low sound, took the hammerstone back, and struck once. It then returned it.

The second blow was right.

They worked until the notch carried its thin line of water again. The power could have opened it without touching the stone. Instead, it had learned where the inhabitant wanted the opening, and how much of the rim it wished to keep.

When the work was finished, both tools went back to the shelf.

Over the days that followed, the power learned other things it could not have learned merely by seeing deeply. It learned which basin the inhabitant chose after feeding, and which ledge it avoided because an old mineral edge pressed against the bare skin of its wrist. It learned that a visitor’s hand could lie nearby for an hour without being touched, and that sometimes the inhabitant sought it almost immediately.

It did not take either response as permanent.

Once, when the inhabitant was asleep, a loose fragment beneath the rear shelf shifted toward the shoulder passage. The power put its fingers into the water and caught it. It placed the fragment on a higher ledge.

In the morning the inhabitant examined it. It examined the passage too. Then it carried the fragment to the bowl and used it to rub a rough place on the rim.

The power left it there.

There were absences. The hollow remained warm and dim during them. The inhabitant ate, slept, scraped its channels, and sometimes received other visitors. Nothing at the entrance required them to understand whose attention the place had drawn.

But the power returned.

It came without gifts on most occasions. Sometimes it held the cutting stone. Sometimes it lifted the vessel when the inhabitant pushed it toward the edge of the slab. It waited to be shown where things belonged.

And sometimes there was no work.

The power sat with its feet clear of the passage. Beside its hand, the inhabitant laid down its own. Water gathered along the lower edges of the stone and fell, bead by bead, into the basin.

Neither hand moved for a while.
