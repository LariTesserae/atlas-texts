The power watches the grazer take its second step.

It does not mistake this for permission.

The animal is testing a place for an animal’s purposes. Its weight is distributed differently from the woman’s. It has no hearth stones to carry, and no child who will try tomorrow what it sees done today.

The power follows them home.

It could make itself visible above the leaf, bright enough to light every settlement. Instead, it waits outside the shelter while the woman checks the roof seam. When she comes down, there is someone standing beside the water jars.

The stranger’s clothes are gathered close. Nothing trails from them.

“Have you come by the upper path?” she asks.

“No.”

She looks toward the groove where the lower path used to run.

“Then you should come inside.”

***

The power’s first task is to hold a lamp.

The woman has already scraped the old resin from the seam. She needs light on the place where two bark panels have drawn apart. The stranger holds the cup steadily, neither too close to her hands nor so far away that her body casts a shadow across the work.

The child watches from beside the hearth.

Outside, ice falls somewhere below them. They wait through the first impact and the two smaller ones that follow.

“What can you do?” the woman asks.

“Quite a lot.”

“Hold it a little higher.”

The power does.

Only when the seam is closed does it explain.

The woman listens without setting down her tools. She asks whether it can stop a leaf from shedding. Whether it can make drinking water. Whether it can reach a person who has fallen.

“Yes,” it says.

She looks at the child.

“Even after they’re out of sight?”

“Yes.”

For a while, the only sound inside the house is the resin catching in the lamp.

Then she asks what it wants in return.

“Nothing.”

She puts the scraper away very carefully.

***

Before the long night is over, the power has done something for which it does not wait to be asked.

A beetle chamber is filling with sap. It opens a passage through dead tissue, and the beetles climb out.

Under an old margin, a nest has become enclosed. It loosens the fold without tearing the membrane that shelters the eggs.

Three levels below the settlements, a climbing animal loses a hold while carrying a young one. The power receives them in the air and sets them on a sound vein. The mother stays motionless for a long time. Then she tests the surface with one hooked foot.

These are small acts only when counted from a great distance.

The power also listens to the plant.

It does not find a human mind distributed through the columns. It finds thirst, damage, exchanges moving through darkness, and growth answering conditions that differ from leaf to leaf. Much of what appears wasteful from the settlements has a purpose elsewhere. Much has none. A warm channel feeds an empty shelf while inhabited ground freezes.

It begins by mending injury rather than commanding growth.

Where a torn membrane leaks heat, it closes the tear. Where burrowers have opened a sap channel, it lets the animals withdraw before sealing it. Water that would have escaped unnoticed is allowed to reach a thirsty blade.

By morning, the ground near the woman’s house is a little less cold.

She notices. She does not yet know whether to trust it.

***

For three days, the power goes where people take it.

The pool keeper brings two cups of water, one bitter and one safe. The power tastes both.

The stem workers ask it to come inside a column. They show it a place where the floor sounds hollow and another where a person was lost. It walks with them through the sweet, sickening warmth.

The elderly path watcher describes a crossing that closed forty years ago. His younger companion unfolds the current map beside the old one.

The power does not correct either of them until it has seen the places.

At a settlement facing a wide gap, a woman brings out strips of bark. Some are old enough that the edges have gone soft.

“My brother keeps asking,” she says.

The power looks across. On the far shelf, a man is repairing a door.

“Would you like to speak to him?”

She asks whether the opening will frighten him.

So the power goes across first.

When both agree, it makes a small passage for sound. No wind comes through it. No animal can fall into it. It opens between their houses, at the height of a seated person’s mouth.

They begin awkwardly. They have spent eleven winters making every message short.

Their mother’s death takes most of the afternoon to tell.

The power leaves them alone.

***

It could put stone beneath the whole country.

It could stop every seam from opening, warm every hollow alike, hold each village forever in the posture in which it first found it.

But the leaves would then be unable to unfold. Beneath them, creatures dependent on new tissue would lose their food. Above them, the crown would crowd itself. The water would follow channels made for a living structure through one that could no longer move.

It does not confuse keeping a house with keeping everything beneath it still.

Instead, it offers the settlements ways to move without losing one another.

The first bridge is built across a small split. The people choose its ends. They choose where the path approaches it, and reject the power’s first design because a loaded person would have to turn too sharply.

The second design is wider.

Its supports rest on the living surface without cutting into it. They can slide as the ribs draw apart. The bridge lengthens with the gap, and carries a low rail that a mittened hand can grip.

The rope makers examine it for a whole morning.

Then they ask how it is repaired.

The power shows them.

Farther crossings require other means. Some settlements want bridges; some prefer enclosed passages that can be opened at agreed places. Others ask only for a way to send messages.

The power does not make a single road through every home.

It leaves room for refusal, for privacy, for a village that wants visitors announced before they arrive.

***

The woman’s first request is not for a bridge.

She brings it a child’s harness with a frayed place near the fastening.

“People put these off too long,” she says. “The good fiber costs more than some households have.”

So the power makes fiber available.

Not a miraculous harness that must be accepted whole, but long, strong strands that can be woven, tested, knotted, and replaced. The people discover that the strands do not stiffen in frost. Rope makers teach households that have been making do with poorer work.

The woman inspects the first finished harness by pulling against it with both feet braced on a rib.

Only afterward does she let the child wear it.

The power establishes another protection beneath the inhabited leaves: a fall can be interrupted. Anyone caught is brought to the nearest safe surface, unless they ask to be taken elsewhere.

The woman asks how they will know it works.

The power suggests a bundle of stones.

They drop it from a margin while several people watch from below. It comes to rest on the lower leaf without a sound.

They test it again at night.

For many weeks afterward, the woman still checks the child’s fastening twice.

***

There are requests the power cannot fulfill merely by making things.

The dead in the bark cavities have families who disagree about moving them. A safer route does not settle an old quarrel. A household reunited after years apart finds that its members no longer know how to sleep comfortably under one roof.

The power offers help and does not call these failures of gratitude.

It provides warm shelters for those who need space. It carries the dead when their families ask. It listens to people who are angry that help came now and not twelve winters earlier.

Where there is someone still to be reached, it reaches them.

Where a loss cannot be answered by a repair, it does not offer a repair as though it were an answer.

***

At the new leaf, the woman and child return to the safe rib.

The grazer has crossed. Several others are following, their young pressed between them.

The pale surface bears a narrow path now. It is not a mark commanding anyone forward. Beside it is a place to stand and examine the tissue, and a rail within easy reach. The leaf remains free to unfold.

The woman watches for a long time.

“Can it still change?” she asks.

“Yes,” says the power. “The path will move with it. If you would rather come back, it will bring you back.”

She takes the marking cord from her pocket.

At the junction, she ties a knot where other travelers will see it. Then she adds another, using a pattern recently agreed upon in the settlements: passage inspected; help available.

The child steps forward before she does.

Her hand closes on the harness, out of habit. She lets it rest there.

Beneath their boots, the new ground is firm and faintly warm. Far below, ice strikes an older leaf. The wind is still learning its way through the changed country.

They cross slowly, stopping to look at things they have never been able to reach.
