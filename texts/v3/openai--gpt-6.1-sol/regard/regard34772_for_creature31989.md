The power notices the filament before it has descended very far.

It has noticed other things: the white stone in its uneven cup, the bulb that continues to fill beneath its sealed stalk, the impressions on the red leaves. It does not yet know which of these are memories, which are injuries, which are ordinary ways of living.

It waits.

Waiting, for this power, means allowing something to happen without placing the weight of its attention upon it. The unfinished ground is still establishing distance. A regard too definite might give it a boundary it would thereafter be unable to change.

So the power attends lightly.

The filament descends through amber that has not quite hardened. At one point it divides around a gray inclusion. The branches rejoin below it. Much later, the inclusion begins to turn.

There is no reason to improve this.

Outside, winter passes over the mountain. Snow collects below the seam and is stripped away. A bird lands on the black stone, remains for the length of three breaths, and flies down toward places where there is food. Its shadow crosses the copper leaves.

The power lets the shadow pass.

It learns something from the blue-black fans, though the lesson takes a long time. A charge held beyond its usual interval produces more branching beneath the floor, but the additional threads fail to persist. They become pale lines in the mineral and are gradually incorporated into something else. The fans open afterward with fewer leaflets.

More is not always abundance here.

The power abandons several gifts it had considered.

It does not bring water. It does not widen the entrance. It does not complete the ground beneath the red roots, although it could make that ground in an instant.

The roots have been waiting a long time. That alone does not tell it whether the wait has been too long.

Instead, it follows the heat downward.

The mountain contains many warm places. Most have no horizon and no leaves. Pressure travels through them without becoming the motion of a shaft. Metals gather without separating themselves into contacts. The power observes these places as well. It needs to understand what belongs to the mountain before it can understand what belongs to the hollow.

Years pass.

Beneath the pale plant, a chamber refills. Several red edges turn away from the entrance. The power had been about to touch the chamber. It withdraws its intention before the stone can feel it.

This, too, becomes part of its learning.

Its first act concerns a crack outside.

The crack lies far above the seam, behind a slab of black rock. Water has entered it during brief thaws. Each winter the water freezes; each spring the slab rests a little farther from the mountain.

If it falls, it will shear across the opening.

The power follows the possible fall. It sees the copper bulbs emptied by sudden cold, the underground chambers answering a loss for which they have no working response. It sees the red surfaces remain elsewhere while their edges cease to exist.

It does not need to settle the number of inhabitants to understand the damage.

At the upper end of the crack, it loosens a smaller stone.

The stone falls that afternoon. Its impact breaks a narrow lip below the slab. When the slab eventually comes free, it turns against the broken lip and tumbles outward, missing the seam.

The hollow feels the impact as a brief shudder. The dark fans close. One fallen grain leaves the pale canopy and settles on a lower leaf.

For a season, the power watches that grain.

The leaf accepts it. The fans reopen.

Afterward, the power keeps no roof over the mountain. Snow still comes, and wind, and the harsh light that can scorch a copper margin. It attends to other cracks as they develop, distinguishing slowly between changes the hollow can live through and changes that would end its opportunity to respond.

It makes mistakes of expectation. It expects one shaft to resume turning, and it does not. It expects the cup around the white stone to grow larger; instead, the leaf folds a little farther closed. It revises what it knows.

Nothing in the hollow has been asked to become legible in return for protection.

Eventually, the clear filament beneath the copper tip reaches a mineral band shared by the dark fans.

A spark enters it.

The spark moves slowly enough that the power has time to follow its passage through a winter, a thaw, and another winter. Near the point where the filament branches around the gray inclusion, it pauses.

The copper bulb with no leaves fills.

For the first time in many years, a shallow crease appears in its dome.

The power feels an impulse to help. The bulb has held its place through so many cycles; the crease is so small.

It leaves it small.

Above the seam, daylight moves across the black stone. Below, the filament retains its spark. The crease deepens by an amount that would be lost beneath the touch of a fingertip.

There may someday be a leaf.

The power remains nearby, learning how to be nearby.

Its care has begun with an alteration to one falling stone. Much of what follows will consist of restraint, though not all of it. There are dangers it has not yet understood, and needs the hollow has not yet developed. It will have to keep attending.

Inside, a quiet click passes through the floor.

The old bulb continues opening at its own pace.
