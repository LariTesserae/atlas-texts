The power does not arrive from above.

For several days its attention lies across the hollow as lightly as the warmth. It follows water through the holes in the ridge. It waits inside the narrow seams of trunks. It notices the larvae without making them more visible to the fish.

At first it does nothing that could be distinguished from looking.

It watches the animal lift its body over a root, distribute its weight among six limbs, and bring the seventh forward. The missing pad on the eighth does not prevent movement. It changes the movement. There is a slight rotation of the body, a pause, a place where the other fingers take more weight.

The power watches this more than once.

It watches the young leave the apron. It follows them far enough to learn that some return to the hollow and some pass through openings in the stone, into warm channels beneath the ridge. There are other pools beyond those channels. There are other adults.

It does not bring them together.

The water between them carries things the power is still learning to recognize.

***

Under the silt, the disc has been correcting a small error for a very long time.

One of its measures no longer quite agrees with another. When it lowers a bank, it must subsequently raise the warmth beneath that bank. When it raises the warmth, a certain kind of root grows too quickly, and it must open water elsewhere. Each correction is slight. The hollow has absorbed them without losing its spaciousness.

But the corrections are becoming more frequent.

The power does not uncover the disc. Small animals have laid eggs in the sediment over it, and the large inhabitant has worn a resting hollow against its southern edge.

Instead, it attends to the error where the error is.

There is no flash. None of the marks becomes suddenly legible. A circle continues its slow division into strokes.

For the first time in many seasons, one buried part of the disc receives the measure it was made to receive.

The power waits through rain, through the fall of a tree, through the emergence of a generation of transparent young. It watches to see whether the repair has disturbed anything else.

A bank that would have sunk remains where it is. The animal rests there through the afternoon. Under its apron, something with a bright shell turns in its pocket.

That is all.

***

The power could restore the missing pad.

It learns the shape from the other seven limbs. It learns how the fine pits distinguish a sound root from a root suspended over water. It learns the branching of the vessels, the slow renewal of the ridged surface.

But knowing how a thing can be done does not tell it whether the animal would welcome the doing.

So it comes to the bench.

It takes no grand shape. There is a weight on the seat, a slight interruption in the green light, the impression that someone is sitting with their hands at rest.

The animal notices before the birds do.

One eye turns toward the shelter. Its mouth continues sorting a fragment of softened wood. Then it releases the fragment and approaches along the firm margin.

The power waits.

The animal extends a forelimb into the empty-looking air above the bench. Its fingers close on something that yields a little, like warm skin.

Amber passes along its side.

In the contact, the power offers something small enough to withdraw from: a sensation of the damaged finger resting without strain, the unfamiliar possibility of another pad beneath it.

The animal takes its limb away.

For the rest of that day, nothing happens.

It feeds beside the stone bowl. It clears mud from beneath a root. It lies in the shallows with its damaged limb folded against its body.

The next morning it returns.

This time it presents that limb.

The power begins with less than a grain’s width of new tissue. Then it lets go.

The animal presses the finger against the support, against the mud, against its own flank. Its eye membranes close and open. It goes away again.

Over many visits, the pad grows.

It is not restored to an unworn beginning. Its surface is firm and ridged, its sensory pits open, its attachment shaped to the limb as the limb has become. When the animal first puts its weight on it, its body makes the old compensating turn.

Then it settles back.

Water moves evenly beneath it.

***

There is one place outside the hollow where a buried channel has narrowed.

The power learned of it by following the young. In wet seasons they can pass. In drier ones they gather at the constriction, press their fringed mouths against the current, and eventually turn back.

The obstruction is not a boundary built by the hollow’s makers. A root has entered an old crack and thickened there.

The power does not kill the root.

Over the course of a season, it loosens a little stone beside it. Water carries away the loosened grains. The root remains, holding the soil above the channel. A second passage forms along its lower edge, barely wide enough for a small, round body.

The first animal through stops halfway.

It backs out, enters again, and stays beneath the stone for most of an afternoon. Then it continues.

Beyond the ridge, water touches a fringe that has not tasted this particular runoff before.

No inhabitants are summoned. No arrivals are promised. There is simply a way through that will remain open as long as the surrounding ground can bear it.

***

The power leaves the doorframe alone.

It examines the few moments gathered in the opening and finds no creature held there against the direction of its living. The fish finishes catching its morsel. Rain completes its fall. A bird takes flight twice to an observer, once to itself.

The shelter, too, needs nothing.

The power sits beneath it during rain.

Beside the support, the animal raises its head. Droplets align beneath the thin roof. The branching amber vessels become visible.

When the droplets rise, the power is still.

The animal keeps its head lifted a little longer than usual.

***

In time, the attention ceases to fill every seam and hollow.

Something remains near the disc: not a new governor, only a means by which a failing part can make its failure known before the hollow must spend years accommodating it.

Something remains at the bench as well.

It does not compel a visit. It does not answer every touch with an alteration. Sometimes the animal comes and finds only a presence willing to sit while it sorts a leaf, or while the last reluctant young climbs off its apron and climbs back on.

The power has made no arrangement against all loss.

One of the flowering plants on the animal’s back eventually dies. The animal carries its dry stem for several weeks. Small creatures shelter beneath it until it comes loose in rain.

Later, a seed catches in the pale covering.

The power sees it and leaves it there.

On a warm afternoon, the animal approaches the shelter carrying no food. It tests the ground beside the bench, then draws itself partly out of the water. Its repaired finger rests against a root.

For a while, both eyes face the rising droplets.

Then one turns toward the place on the bench where the light is slightly interrupted.

The amber line travels slowly down its flank.

The power answers with a faint warmth beneath the resting fingers, no greater than the warmth of the stone.

The animal remains.

Across the pool, a pale bird opens its wings. The long strips of its feathers separate, admit the light, and close again.
