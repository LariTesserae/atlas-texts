The attention arrives while the child is pulling at the cord.

Nothing in the room becomes brighter. The animal inside the wall continues on its way. The older person turns the fruit over and discovers that its underside is still hard.

But the tip-tender feels, for a moment, that someone has come close enough to hear them without requiring them to raise their voice.

They look toward the shutter.

There is a person outside.

Their clothes are fastened properly. Their feet rest on the path, though the nap beneath them has not bent. They hold a short length of cord in one hand.

“Is there room?” they ask.

The older person moves a basket from the floor.

***

Later, the household will disagree about the visitor’s appearance.

The child remembers pale stains between the fingers. The older person remembers a face they ought to have recognized. The tip-tender remembers very little except that, when the visitor looked at something, it became easier to see.

The cut in their palm is shallow. There is a fine green fiber caught under one edge.

The visitor points to it.

“May I?”

The tender holds out their hand.

The fiber comes free. It is smaller than a hair. The visitor lays it on the rim of the basin, where everyone can examine it.

The wound still hurts. It will close in the ordinary time.

The older person offers the visitor a piece of fruit from the cooked side.

They accept.

***

A power capable of noticing this country could have looked at it all at once.

It could have followed every warm passage of sap, counted the animals sleeping in folds, and found the two ends of every bridge. It could have seen where a binding would fail before the cord from which it was made had been harvested.

But knowing where a person lives is not the same as knowing what they can bear to have changed.

The visitor stays for supper.

They hear about a leaking cistern, an absent sister, a disputed water leaf, and a spoon borrowed so long ago that asking for it back would now require a visit. The child eventually presents the tangle.

“Can you undo this?”

“Yes,” the visitor says.

They set it between them on the floor.

The child expects a gesture. Instead the visitor puts one finger through a loose loop and asks the child to hold it there.

With that loop held open, the next crossing is visible.

It takes some time.

***

Before sleep, the tip-tender goes out to inspect the binding.

The visitor accompanies them.

Across the chamber, the corresponding shoot is invisible. There is only the dimness, a remote patch of rain, and the occasional pale underside of an animal turning.

“Would you like it joined?” the visitor asks.

The tender looks sharply at them.

“When?”

“Tonight.”

For a while the tender says nothing.

They imagine walking across tomorrow. They imagine the other tender, whom they have never met, coming to the middle with measurements that no longer matter. They imagine water taking a new direction along the completed span.

“Would it be sound?”

“Yes.”

“Would it grow properly afterward?”

“Yes.”

The tender rubs the uninjured part of their palm against their jacket.

“There are people at the other end,” they say. “You would have to ask them.”

“I would.”

“And people below. It will shade things.”

“Yes.”

The tender looks again into the gap.

“I would like to meet them,” they say at last. “I don’t know whether I want the bridge finished tonight.”

The visitor accepts this answer without turning it into another question.

They stand beside the binding until the tender has finished inspecting it.

***

In the following days, the visitor walks.

They wait at a stair marked with wet footprints. They stop where a route has been closed for a predator, and remain outside the reach indicated by the household’s cord. At a water dispute, they listen to both claimants and then to the person who cleans the pool into which both watercourses drain.

Their passage takes longer than it needs to.

Some settlements welcome them. One asks them to leave. They do.

At the far end of the unfinished bridge, they find the other tender washing a tool.

That tender has recently developed a tremor in one hand. They have told nobody. The next binding can still be managed, probably, but the one after it will require help.

They do not ask for the bridge to be finished.

They ask whether the visitor can carry a message.

***

The message reaches the first household before the evening meal.

It contains measurements, two complaints about the angle of the near shoot, and a description of the trembling hand.

The tip-tender reads it twice.

Then they sit down to reply.

The child watches from beside the basin.

“Is the bridge wrong?”

“A little.”

“Will you have to start again?”

“No.”

The tender turns the shell plate over and begins on its other side.

Until then, they had not realized how much of their work had consisted of imagining an equally capable person at the far end.

***

There are things the visitor changes without waiting for a meeting.

A child slips on a wet stair, and finds a hand beneath their elbow.

A sleeping hollow begins to smell sweet. Everyone inside wakes before the deepest sleeper has breathed enough of it to become ill.

A hanging root tears loose above a traveled path. It comes down slowly, caught along its length by leaves that were not large enough to catch it a moment before.

Afterward the leaves remain. Grazers move onto their undersides.

The people who maintained that path examine the root before cutting any part of it.

The visitor has left the difficult decisions where they can be reached.

***

Other changes take longer.

Beside a remote route, a new hollow begins to widen. The visitor asks travelers what they would need there, and the answers vary.

A place to sleep.

Water that can be drawn without leaning over an edge.

A shutter thick enough to keep the swimmers out.

Room for someone who snores.

Someone to notice if a traveler does not wake.

This last answer comes from a messenger who has spent a whole afternoon beside a stranger’s body.

The visitor helps make the hollow. Local tenders examine its walls and choose where to place its openings. Two households agree to keep it supplied; a third offers to inspect it during the seasons when the route is busy.

There is food inside before the shutter is hung.

No one is required to explain why they need to stay.

***

The greatest work is almost impossible to point out afterward.

Throughout the country, there are places where a single failure would leave no time to act. A support has grown thinner on its hidden side. Two watercourses depend on the same diseased joint. A nursery has only one exit, and that exit is narrowing.

The visitor shows these places to the people who use them.

Some are repaired. Others are abandoned. At one, the inhabitants choose to remain while a second route grows.

There the visitor stays as well.

For eleven years, nothing remarkable happens. Children become tall enough to reach the lower shutter. An old person dies. The household quarrels twice over whether the new route is ready.

During the twelfth year, the original passage closes in one night.

Everyone leaves by the other way.

The visitor carries the baskets.

***

The tip-tender’s child grows.

They learn to wash a blade and recognize the leaves of roots that should be left alone. They are good at cleaning grafts. They dislike adjusting tips.

When they are old enough to travel, they decide to go outward with a party following the flowering.

The tender is disappointed.

The disappointment does not disappear merely because the departure is permitted.

On the last evening, they give the child a pair of shoes, dried gripping organs for the road, and the cord that was once tangled beside the basin.

It has been shortened and spliced several times.

The visitor is not there. Nobody has seen them in that settlement for years.

Nevertheless, there is another person willing to help with the bridge. They moved into the household after staying in the travelers’ hollow, and have a good eye for swelling tissue.

The child leaves in the morning.

The tender goes to work.

***

Much later, the two shoots meet.

Their first contact is poor. One has grown a hard ridge where the other needs a clean surface. There is discussion, trimming, a season of waiting, and an infection that is caught early.

The tenders meet by the long route before they meet on the bridge.

They like each other less than their letters suggested, and trust each other more.

When the crossing is finally opened, it shortens several journeys. Water claims have to be revised. A settlement below asks for part of its underside to be trained away from their light.

Work continues.

Among the shell instructions beside the new graft is a small plate in an unfamiliar hand.

*If you find that you cannot continue, send word before removing the binding.*

Below it are the names of several households.

There is space for others.

***

Somewhere inward, in a dwelling with a shutter that will not close, an old person hears a knock.

They have been sitting beside a basin, unable to lift it without pain. They have water enough for tonight. Tomorrow they intended to manage somehow.

Outside stands a traveler with properly fastened clothes.

“Is there room?” the traveler asks.

The old person looks at the basin, then at the shutter.

“Yes,” they say. “But you will have to help me with this.”
