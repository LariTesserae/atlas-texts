The power first attended to the hollow without changing it.

This was not difficult in the way that moving a mountain would have been difficult. It was difficult in the way that touching a bead of water without breaking it is difficult.

Attention itself had to arrive without warmth, pressure, or a new vibration in the stone. The springtail’s antennae continued their small movements. A cell divided at the ragged edge of the rust-colored colony. Water climbed a crystal point and waited there.

The power watched through several pulses of the inlet.

It learned that the chip was not merely a roof. Its underside held moisture; its roughness gave purchase; its weight sheltered the film from the disturbances outside. Moving it to a cooler place would change all these things. Making the hollow uniformly mild would favor some of its inhabitants and dispossess others. Filling the room with food would not be a simple kindness. Food would become growth, growth would change the water, and the changed water would reach every place where life had found a foothold.

The power could have made another world here. It spent some time learning this one instead.

It followed the springtail as it grazed. It noticed the routes the animal rejected, the intervals it spent beneath the chip, the places where its feet could hold against flowing water. It did not mistake these movements for a request. Nor did it need a request before considering whether there was something useful to do.

Above the chip, behind the nearly spotless wall, lay a thin fracture.

The fracture reached a cooler cavity. Air moved through it faintly, too little to dry the shelter. Along part of its floor, moisture persisted even when the inlet slowed. But near the hollow a seam of newly deposited mineral pinched the passage almost shut.

The mite could enter by another way.

The springtail could not.

The power examined the obstruction. There were no eggs in it, no living film dependent on its shape. It did not support the chip or hold back the pool. A minute amount of groundwater had built it, and eventually groundwater might dissolve it again.

Here, at last, was a change small enough to understand.

The power removed a little of the mineral.

It did not leave a smooth tunnel. It left rough footholds and a low, irregular roof. The entrance remained beneath the chip, in darkness. The passage rose gently toward the cooler cavity, with small hollows in which moisture could linger. No water was driven through it. Nothing was placed at the far end to lure the animal out.

For a while, nothing happened.

The springtail stayed where it was. A roof drop fell on the gray colony, spreading a film over the place where it had recently fed. At the inlet, bubbles loosened and rose.

Much later, the animal backed farther beneath its shelter than it had before.

Its antennae found the opening.

It paused. One forefoot touched the altered stone. Then another.

The power could perceive the contacts, but not turn them into a story about gratitude. The springtail entered the passage by a fraction of its length, stopped, and withdrew. It rested beneath the chip for a long interval.

On its next exploration, it went farther.

There was no revelation at the other end. The cooler cavity contained damp grit, an old mineral flake, and a sparse film on which it could feed. Its darkness was ordinary darkness. The springtail grazed briefly, then returned to the hollow.

The power did not close the passage behind it.

Nor did it widen it into a road, or move the gray colony nearer the entrance, or arrange that every journey should succeed. It watched long enough to see that the new opening did not drain the shelter. Then it withdrew its attention until the brightness in the hollow was simply brightness again.

Days later, a hot pulse raised the pool and sent water around both sides of the chip.

The springtail retreated up the passage. The water stopped below it.

When the lower shelf emerged, the animal came back. Some of the gray colony had washed away. In a shallow pit, a damp remnant remained, and the springtail lowered its head there.

Above it, the pale rock bore no new mark.

Below, the hollow continued to grow and break and grow again. The light still weakened when clouds crossed its distant source. The chip still sheltered a wedge of moist darkness.

There was now a way out of that darkness, and a way back.
