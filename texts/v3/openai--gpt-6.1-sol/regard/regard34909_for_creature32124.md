The power first notices the hand.

Not because it is the least damaged thing in the country, or the most. Beneath the old animal, a colony of grazers is working along a film too thin to show in the darkness. Farther down the cliff, a flyer has failed to return to its recess. There are hungry young there. The power notices these things too.

But the hand is resting on stone, and the animal is waiting for a certainty that no longer comes readily through it.

The fingers healed across one another. When the animal presses them down, one receives the tremor, another receives its own weight, and a third receives pain. It lifts the hand and tries again.

The power does not touch it yet.

For several nights it follows.

It learns the route between the cliff and the sleeping chamber. It learns where the animal pauses because the ground is uncertain, and where it pauses because it is tired. These are not always the same places. It watches the animal pass a vent rich with food to reach a shelf where another member of its group has left a mark.

There the old animal lifts a stone with its good hand and keeps its nose beneath it for a long time.

Later, at the junction of ravines, it gives three blows against the shelf.

An answer arrives.

The animal rests before going toward it.

***

There is water below the route.

Most of it belongs to the deep workings of the plateau. It moves through hot chambers, takes up minerals, and rises elsewhere as vapor. Drawing too much of it toward this hollow would cool one seam and increase pressure in another. A grass ring farther north would lose its moisture. The power follows the water far enough to know this.

But a little collects behind an old mineral closure.

It has been there for years, entering slowly, escaping more slowly through pores in the stone. Its passage serves no surface colony. Eventually the enclosing rock will settle and release it into a lower chamber.

The power makes a narrow opening.

The work is smaller than a root could manage. A grain loosens. Another shifts. Water enters the new passage and stops at a place where the stone is still too close.

The power waits, then loosens one more grain.

By the following night, dampness has appeared beneath a sheltered edge beside the animal’s route.

There is no pool. There is a bead, then another.

The old animal nearly passes it.

Its fringe turns toward the rock. It stops, tests the shelf, and puts its mouth against the wet edge.

The water tastes of the plateau. Nothing unfamiliar has been added.

After drinking, the animal remains there with its hands spread.

***

The power could straighten the injured fingers.

It studies the hand while the animal sleeps: the worn joints, the shortened tendons, the small nerves caught in tissue hardened around the break. The animal has learned to walk with this hand. An abrupt correction would make its next step strange.

So the first change is only to the place where a nerve is pinched.

Over several sleepings, the tissue softens. A narrow channel opens around it. The animal continues to wake, feed, drink, and leave its mark under stones. It does not sleep through approaching footsteps. It still withdraws its fingers from a hot vent mouth.

But it no longer wakes whenever the injured hand slips beneath its chest.

Later, one joint gains a little movement.

The power stops there for a time.

The animal begins to lay the outer finger against the ground before lowering the others. A tremor reaches it that was previously lost. At the edge of a lifting plate, it waits, then chooses another crossing.

There is no moment when it becomes young.

Its back remains heavy with mineral. Its worn scale edges do not grow sharp again. After a long journey, it still needs to rest.

***

The new damp place acquires inhabitants.

First a film spreads beneath the lip. Then grazers find it. A small furred animal begins arriving before the old one, drinking from a lower bead and retreating into a crack.

The power watches the opening through a full dry interval.

It does not widen it when every visitor would like more water. Downstream, the deep chambers retain their warmth. At the surface, the damp patch remains small enough to disappear beneath an animal’s chin.

One evening the old animal brings another adult.

They do not arrive together. The second follows a fresh mark, pauses at the ridge, and descends after the first has finished drinking.

Both remain in the hollow.

The old one lies with its injured hand extended rather than folded underneath. The other settles several body lengths away. Between them, beneath a flake of stone, grazers continue their slow feeding.

***

When the old animal next visits the cliff, it stays until the upward air has begun to cool.

A drop gathers under the lip.

It reaches with its good hand, then sets the other beside it. Both hands hold. It draws itself forward without the small recoil that used to follow the movement.

Afterward it turns from the edge.

Somewhere beyond the ridge, a breastbone strikes stone.

The animal lifts its head. It waits through the intervals, listening with its feet.

Then it starts toward the ravine.

The power goes with it, without footsteps, leaving the cliff dark and the warm air moving as before.
