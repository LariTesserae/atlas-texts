The power did not arrive from above.

Above was where birds passed, where frost began, where the light changed too quickly for the deepest folds to answer. It attended first to the weight of water in the mountain.

For a long time, nothing in the hollow was different.

The fern held its drops. The little creature drank along its jaw. Beneath the empty ground, the animal lifted and lowered the same small burden of sediment. The power followed water through chambers that had not opened to daylight in centuries. It followed warmth outward and the substances of fallen leaves inward. Where a living fold narrowed around stone, it waited long enough to learn whether the narrowing was injury, growth, or simply the shape of that place.

It did not take the underground rushing for a voice. It did not take the absence of a voice for an absence of feeling.

There were differences within the animal.

Some pressures passed through it without consequence. Others left a contraction that persisted after the pressure was gone. Some chambers widened readily when fresh water reached them. Others held themselves narrow, though there was space to open. Far below the spreading tree, a thick fold had been pressed between two stones for so long that the water no longer passed along its inner surface.

The fold was alive.

Each afternoon, when the rest of the body filled, it tried to fill also.

The power remained there through many risings.

It could have moved the stones apart. But one of them supported the soil beneath the tree, and the tree’s roots held the upper pools in place. A sudden release would have opened more than the trapped chamber. Water would have taken the loosened earth. Roots would have dried. The animal might have lost three broad lobes in the course of gaining room for one.

Instead, during a night of ordinary rain, a grain loosened in a seam beneath the lower stone.

Then another.

Over the following weeks, water carried the grains away. The stone settled by less than the thickness of a leaf, and settled differently: its weight came to rest upon a buried rib rather than upon the fold.

Nothing fell. No root broke.

At the next afternoon rising, a little water entered the chamber.

The animal tightened around it. For several days it admitted no more. Then the walls eased, and water began to pass through a part of the body that had known only the effort of holding itself alive.

The power did not make the old fold young. Its damaged surfaces renewed unevenly. A pale band remained where the stone had pressed it. But the contraction no longer traveled from that place into the neighboring chambers every afternoon.

Above, the spreading tree put out one leaf.

This was not a sign. It was what the tree had been about to do.

---

There were other things the power left alone.

The seedlings that exhausted themselves upon the bare patches were not all rescued. The hollow could not become entirely fern or entirely tree without ceasing to sustain its inhabitant. The little creatures continued to eat what lived along the wet margins. Old roots died. Some of the animal’s distant portions failed in winter.

The power watched these losses without treating every one as a mistake.

But it learned where loss had become needless.

Beyond the eastern rim, a younger portion had spread into a pocket beneath moss. Water reached it through a crack that froze early. Each winter the small lobe withdrew; each spring it grew back into the same ground. There was another seep nearby, warmer and more constant, but separated from it by a shelf of compact gravel.

In the autumn, a stone shifted there.

It might have shifted under snow. It might have been moved by a hoof. The power moved it no farther than either would have done.

A thin course of dampness entered the gravel.

The young portion did not follow at once. It remained beneath its moss through the winter. In spring its clear threads stood in water that had not been there before.

By summer, it had extended the width of a fingertip toward the seep.

The way was available. Whether the animal occupied it would take years to discover.

---

Only after these things did the power make its attention perceptible.

It chose a place near the upper basin, where the animal’s surface lay shallow and no root was enclosed. There, for a short interval each morning, it held the water at a gentle, even pressure.

Not a footstep. Not rain. Nothing that demanded withdrawal.

The pressure came and ended. It came again the next day.

For several weeks the animal answered as it answered any unfamiliar weight: a slow drawing inward, a wetness appearing after the weight had ceased.

The power shortened the interval.

Eventually the surface ceased to retreat.

Then, one morning, the fold rose slightly beneath the water while the pressure was still present.

The power eased away.

The fold remained raised for a while, then settled.

There was no word for this exchange. There was not yet enough to call it trust. But the following morning, when the same gentle pressure returned, the response came sooner.

Sometimes the animal drew inward instead. On those mornings the pressure ended at once.

The fern continued to overshadow the basin. Leaves fell across it and softened. There were days when the power offered no contact, and days when the animal made no discernible answer.

Neither absence closed the possibility.

---

A traveler who entered the hollow would have seen almost nothing of what had been done.

The ground was still difficult to cross. Roots still curved away from the empty patches. Water still sounded too large for the place that contained it.

Near the upper basin, the traveler might have noticed a small rise beneath the sediment, too gradual to be a ripple.

They might have waited.

If they waited long enough, they would have seen it settle again.

The power had not given the animal a human shape, a human speed, or a wish to leave the mountain. It had made room where room could safely be made. It had relieved a pressure that the body could not escape. And it had found a way to be present that could be refused.

Toward evening, the pools lowered.

Water rose through the bare ground. The old fold filled without struggling against the stone. Beyond the rim, the younger portion rested in its small new dampness.

The hollow darkened at its own pace.

Beneath it, passage after passage opened, and the water went on.
