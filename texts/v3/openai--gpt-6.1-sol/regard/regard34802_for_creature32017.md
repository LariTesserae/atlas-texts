The power notices the thorn first.

Not because it is the worst thing happening on the plateau. It is simply there, held between two thick fingers, with a little blood on its point. The foot from which it came is still sore. Tomorrow your host will put weight on its outer edge and walk badly until the swelling subsides.

The power could close the puncture before the thorn reaches the dish.

It waits long enough to understand what the foot is made of.

There are small living things in the wound, and others in the skin around it that belong there. There are vessels that must not be sealed too soon. The softness of the pad is useful, not a defect. Its many folds let it hold wet stone.

When your host next lowers the foot into the water, the pain eases. The wound washes clean. Nothing else about the foot changes.

Your host looks down, then toward you.

You are eating fruit and have noticed nothing.

***

For some time, this is how the power attends to the high country.

It does not cool the plateau. The heat that makes you miserable is woven through the lives around you. Eggs develop in warm mud. Roots draw food from the heated cracks. The neck folds, the damp capes, the afternoon resting places belong to bodies that have lived here far longer than yours has.

It does not stop the waterfalls, straighten the channels, or take the moss from anyone’s back.

It follows the water down through the cloud and finds out where it goes. It follows the water rising beside the tower and finds that this, too, goes somewhere.

It listens at the gathering places.

The visitor describing the blocked channel is not merely wasting an afternoon. Several households rely on that channel in different ways. Opening it quickly would drain a nursery pool. Leaving it blocked would eventually spoil a bed of edible roots. The first proposed remedy failed because someone remembered an old flood incorrectly.

The power could settle the matter.

Instead, it learns why the person who remembers correctly has stopped coming to gatherings.

There is no single reason. Their feet hurt. They dislike one of the speakers. Since their companion died, they have found the crowded bench difficult to bear.

None of these things can be mended in quite the same way.

***

When the power makes itself known, it chooses the place where your host offered you fruit.

There is already a spare seat there. It has no need of a seat, but using one seems better than filling the doorway with light.

It gives itself a body that can be looked at without discomfort. The body is unfamiliar, yet plainly alive. It has hands capable of speaking and no resemblance to anyone who has died.

Your host watches it for a long time.

Then your host moves the seed dish out of its reach.

The power waits.

A child comes close, touches its forearm, and asks a question too quickly for you to follow.

The power answers imperfectly. It has listened to many conversations, but being addressed is different from overhearing. One arrangement of its fingers means something it did not intend.

Your host corrects it.

It accepts the correction, repeats the gesture, and is given fruit.

***

It does not begin by announcing what everyone needs.

It says what it can do. It can examine the old structures without breaking them. It can help injured bodies. It can make useful things, and teach the making of some of them. It can travel beyond the cloud and return.

They ask whether it can retrieve a cutting tool from the bottom of a deep pool.

It can.

Whether it can make a certain browser leave the planted trenches alone.

It could, but asks first to see the trenches.

Whether it can put an arm straight again.

The old adult with the crooked arm has not asked this. Someone else has asked on their behalf.

The power turns toward the old adult and waits for their answer.

***

The arm takes longer than the cutting tool.

Not because the power lacks strength. The old adult has built much of a life around the arm as it is. The shoulder has changed. The hand has learned ways of gripping that would become awkward if everything were suddenly straightened. The injury is bound up with old anger, too, though the power does not insist on discussing that.

They meet several times.

At first, the old adult wants only to sleep without the deep ache that arrives in heavy rain.

The power helps with that.

Later, they ask whether the hand could turn a little farther.

Work begins in small stages. Between them, the old adult fishes, eats, argues, and discovers which changes are welcome. Once they ask to stop. The power stops.

The children find that the recovering adult can still interrupt their game by looking at it.

***

Inside the tower, the power discovers a failure that has not yet become visible.

Far beneath the shallow flooded rooms, something that holds the light bands steady has been wearing thin. It was made to last a very long time. It has lasted longer.

There is no malice in it. There is no sleeping mind to awaken, no forgotten master to whom the inhabitants must be returned. There is only intricate work, still doing most of what it was made to do.

The power spends an afternoon finding words for the fault.

It uses a split reed, a basket, and several stones. Its first explanation is poor. An adult who clears the outlets asks it to begin again.

By evening, five people understand enough to ask difficult questions.

Repairing the worn part will require one light band to grow heavier for a while. A household has a platform beside it. Browsers travel through it. There are nesting birds in a hollow that will tilt when the support changes.

The repair is arranged around these things.

No one is told that inconvenience is beneath the notice of a great power.

When the work is finished, the band lies where it lay before. Rain slows within it. Children throw seed pods upward.

In a chamber near the tower’s base, there is now a model that can be handled. Its pieces are large enough for their hands. It shows what the structure does, where it is wearing, and how to make it safe if the power is elsewhere.

The model has no secret surfaces.

***

At the western brink, the power is less patient.

A section of shrub mat covers a crack whose lower wall has loosened. It will fail beneath the next heavy traveller. The power supports the wall before anyone reaches it.

Then it tells the nearest households what it has done.

They inspect the place, cut back the deceptive growth, and mark a safer path. They do not want the entire edge fenced. Some fish in the falling water. Some come there to watch weather moving below them. One person says a fence would make people careless about the places beyond its ends.

The power leaves them the decision.

Its support remains until the loose stone can be removed.

***

Not every offer is accepted.

The maker of excellent baskets does not want a new cord that never rots. Rotting cords can be cut apart and returned to the trenches. The proposed cord would have to be kept somewhere forever.

The power had not considered how many baskets might eventually be involved.

It makes a different cord.

The young adult watching the eastern peaks wants to see beyond them. Others object that there is still a roof to replace.

The power does not decide which life the young adult ought to lead. It helps them prepare for the journey: wet shelters along the dry stretches, a way to call home, knowledge of the country ahead. The roof is finished before they leave, though this is the result of an argument in which the power takes little part.

From the first eastern ridge, the traveller sends back a long account of a plant nobody at home has seen.

Most of the account concerns whether it is edible.

***

The power stays long enough to become less interesting.

People bring it damaged tools. Sometimes they bring disputes, and are disappointed to find that knowing what happened does not always produce an answer everyone will like.

It helps a household through a difficult birth. It spends three wet evenings learning how to part the small leaves behind an infant’s head without hurting the skin.

It sits with someone whose companion will not recover.

Here it explains carefully what it can change and what each change would mean. It makes no choice on their behalf. Later, when no more treatment is wanted, it remains available for pain, thirst, fear, and the practical needs of the room.

There are nights when its great strength is occupied in holding a bowl at a comfortable angle.

***

The plateau does not become a place without trouble.

Food still spoils. People still interrupt. A well-meant repair floods somebody’s sleeping shelf, and those responsible spend the next morning lifting wet bedding.

But the old machinery is no longer beyond all understanding. An injury need not be borne merely because nobody knows how to treat it. A traveller can go farther without having to disappear.

There are more people able to help one another, and more ways to ask.

Toward evening, the power sits beside your host while the mist gathers outside.

Your host is inspecting the pad of a foot. Finding nothing lodged there, they lower it into the pool and push the fruit dish toward the power.

From the next basin comes a call. Someone has found a change in the flow from a wall.

The power raises its head.

Three people answer before it does. One asks where. Another says they have the hooked tool. The third tells them to wait until a basket has been put down.

Your host takes another piece of fruit.

There is time to finish eating before they go and look.
