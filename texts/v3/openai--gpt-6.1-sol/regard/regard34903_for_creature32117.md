The power does not announce itself above the valley.

There is no place above the valley from which an announcement would reliably come. Nor would a voice, however immense, be a kindness here.

It arrives at the shelter with the unfinished second room and waits outside the doorway, where it can be seen without obstructing the work.

The resident is kneeling over the tray of wedges. They glance up, then return to the hinge strap. After a while they indicate the stack of roof slabs.

The power carries one inside.

It could carry all of them. It carries the one indicated, sets it where the resident shows it, and returns for the next.

By the end of the work, there is a dark crease across its palm. White splinters have gathered there.

***

It learns the conversation slowly enough for mistakes to be corrected.

At first its touch is too exact. Every pressure arrives perfectly distinct, without the small variations by which a person recognizes another person’s hand. The resident withdraws their arm.

The power waits.

Later it tries again, less certain of itself.

This time they tell it where to sit.

It remains through a darkening. It helps replace a roof strap and is told not to use the lever it has chosen. The lever belongs to someone on the far rim, and its end has already been shortened once.

When food is brought out, the power does not take a portion. It has no need of what costs the grove its strength.

This is noticed.

So is the fact that, when the resident goes to sleep, the stranger does not finish the second room.

***

There are things it could do at once.

It could still the mountain. The cup would stop creeping. The huge wrenching beneath the valley would cease, and all the unfinished distances would take their final measure.

The children would remain the same size.

The groves would begin to starve.

It could hold the gap open forever, keeping the roots fed and the shelves unresolved. The old would continue retreating toward the clearing. Every cradle would still require its piece of finished stone. Someone would always be maintaining pressure against a shoulder that had come apart from its proper distance.

It could take the inhabitants elsewhere.

But it has seen the two cups. It has seen a woman move a stone a little nearer the roots of a plant she complains about. It has sat at a slab while an old person, asked about food, answered a question concerning a roof repair completed long before.

It does not mistake these things for reasons to leave everyone unaided.

It understands that it has not yet learned what aid would mean.

***

Its first gift is a room in which the sound is less.

Not silence.

It makes the offer with its hand resting on the conversation slab, and the resident asks how the change will affect the ground. They ask whether the roof will still give warning when it begins to bind. They ask whether a person asleep there will feel someone walking in the outer room.

The power had intended silence.

It revises the work.

Behind the third hanging, the noise of formation loosens. A hammer on the bench can still be felt through the wrist. The roof strap makes its small sound under tension. Someone at the doorway can be noticed.

But the jaw no longer aches.

The resident sits in the altered pocket for a long time. Then they fetch the tray of wedges and begin sorting them.

Later, they take one of the cups from the shelf.

They put it on the bench, where it stays.

***

News travels by hands, and by people who arrive saying they happened to be passing.

The woman with the violet plant comes to inspect the room. She presses her fingertips against the walls, checks the hanging edges, and asks what has been taken out.

Nothing, the power tells her.

She asks where the sound has gone.

The power shows her: not into another shelter, not into the roots, not beneath the old clearing. It has been allowed to finish, instead of remaining forever at the place where it began.

She considers this.

Then she asks whether the same can be done beside her bed.

An elderly visitor refuses the change. He has slept with the mountain’s pressure against his ribs for so long that its absence keeps waking him.

The power restores his room.

He later asks for a little less of it, but only on the side where he rests his head.

Thereafter, the work is done in small amounts.

***

The question of the groves takes longer.

The power visits the baskets containing transplanted plants. It watches a household examine the single new pleat. It accompanies cutters into the dense interiors and learns how much can be taken from one stem, and why another must be left alone.

At a gathering around a broad slab, it places a white shaving in the middle.

The shaving bends under its own strain.

Then it straightens.

Then it bends again.

No branch begins to grow from it.

The people watch with their hands on the stone.

The mountain is becoming solid, the power tells them. That becoming need not be the only thing their roots can live upon.

Finished stone still bears weight. It warms and cools, however slightly. It yields under a foot and returns when the foot is lifted. At present, these changes are too small, and occur in the wrong way, for the plants to use.

The power can help a root learn them.

Not every root. Not without trials. A plant that learns may grow less extravagantly. Its leaves may become heavier. Cutting it may require new care.

Someone asks whether there will still be food.

That is what they must find out before changing a grove.

Someone else asks why the power does not simply make food.

It could.

They discuss that answer for a long time.

In the end, some households ask for stores while the trials are undertaken. Others ask to learn the work. Several ask for both.

***

The first trial is made with the violet plant.

Its keeper insists.

She will not permit it to be carried to the clearing. The wall shelters it, and she knows its habits here.

So the power brings a piece of finished stone and lays it among the stones already surrounding the roots. It does not touch the plant until she has shown which inner fan can be opened safely.

For a long time, nothing visible happens.

The plant continues taking too much warmth from the wall.

Then a new leaf appears.

It is small, thick at the rim, and unmistakably violet. When it is cut, the chambers inside contain something that glistens.

The woman closes the cut between two fingers.

She is displeased.

No one eats from it yet.

There are further trials. Some fail. A basket splits. One plant puts all its strength into a root that must be carefully removed from beneath a threshold. Another grows well but offers nothing nourishing.

The power does not conceal these results beneath abundant successes.

When at last a portion warms someone’s skin, it is prepared in the old manner, from inner ribs and a shaving of white material. The eater sits with hands flat on their thighs.

Everyone waits.

Afterward, the plant’s keeper presses a short statement into the slab.

The stranger will be allowed to tend the stones.

Not the cutting.

***

Meanwhile, the gap continues closing.

The power offers to slow particular descents while paths are moved. It marks these changes with shards unlike the usual markers, so no one will mistake a borrowed interval for dependable ground.

It makes places where an injured body can rest without another person having to maintain pressure throughout the darkening. Visitors still come. They sit beside the injured, handle the folded leaf, and tell news through a small slab placed within reach.

In the old clearing, it asks the elders where another shelter should stand.

They disagree.

A proposed doorway would interrupt someone’s view of a grove. A proposed bench is in the place where another person prefers to sit directly on the ground.

The power waits while the matter is settled.

It has patience enough not to make patience an obligation for anyone else.

***

It does not enter the recesses of the dead.

When asked, it steadies the stone around them. It leaves each opening facing the view that was chosen, even where a simpler arrangement would hold the cliff more securely.

At one recess, a woman asks it to stay while she speaks aloud.

The mountain is quieter now, but her words are very low.

The power listens without carrying them anywhere.

***

The person from the far rim arrives before the second room is complete.

The crossing is easier in some places and harder in others. They bring a stalk lever, a bundle of hangings, and a plant whose basket has been repaired so often that scarcely any of its first weaving remains.

The resident comes out to meet them.

For some time the two stand with their hands against each other’s forearms.

The power is inside, holding a roof slab at the height it has been told.

Neither person hurries back.

***

Eventually, the underside meets the rising ground.

It happens first at a place that no one has thought especially important.

The last space there narrows to a pale line. The line closes. A long pressure passes through the paths and conversation slabs, and then that part of the mountain becomes quiet.

Elsewhere, there is still wrenching. There are still raw slopes, crowded fans, markers needing closer neighbors. Much remains unfinished.

But in the hardened place, a low growth has taken hold around three stones.

Its leaves are modest. They cast a definite shadow. At their centers, the new ribs are ready for cutting.

The people come to look.

One carries a child without a stone beneath its back. Another brings the old man, who complains that the walk was longer than anyone said.

They rest their hands on a slab set there for the purpose.

The power sits with them.

Someone asks whether it intends to stay.

It answers that it can.

This produces several questions about where, and whether it will need a shelter of its own.

Behind them, in the room made for the person from the far rim, two cups stand on a level shelf. One has been chipped at the edge.

The doorway shifts slightly.

The resident notices, kneels, and selects a thinner wedge.

The power is nearest the tray.

It passes the wedge into their hand.
