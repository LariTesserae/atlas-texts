The touch is not a grasp. It rests against the underside of your finger, through a thickness of stone you cannot estimate.

You leave your hand where it is.

After a time, the narrow creature draws one forefoot back. The place for your hand becomes less exact. You could withdraw it now without feeling that you had taken away something needed.

Above you, the great beast closes its eye.

This is the first movement you have seen here that comes to an end.

***

The power that has noticed the plateau does not descend upon it.

There is already a great deal resting on this height.

It attends first to the touch beneath the stone. Then to the hand, the extended forefeet, the horn fitted into darkness. It attends to the tree growing in the closed injury, and to the shade of branches that have never grown above this ground.

It does not decide, from the stillness, that everything is at peace.

Nor does it decide that everything must be awakened.

For a long while, it listens to the intervals between the few sounds.

Some are simply intervals. The creatures that occupy them are comfortable. There are ways of living here for which a thousand years elsewhere would be a brief and pleasant pause.

Others are different.

The beak in the cracked egg has reached the limit of what it can do. Its owner is not afraid of the plateau. It is tired of the shell.

The mouth around the leaf is neither eating nor refraining from eating. It cannot finish the small movement of its jaw.

And the great beast at the rim has been standing for so long that standing has become part of the country. Its old injury no longer hurts. What hurts is a sound it has kept from making.

The power does not pull the sound out.

It finds the cloudy-eyed person sitting between the three animals and makes room beside them.

There is no fourth seat. Nevertheless, none of the animals is crowded.

The person looks toward the new place. Then they place two fingers upon it, as they once placed them upon your wrist.

For a while, nothing visible happens.

Along the back of the outward-facing animal, a breathing hole opens. The fingers rest. Another hole opens.

At last the person says something in their ordinary, rather rough voice.

The power answers in a voice quiet enough not to carry beyond the wall.

The person asks another question.

This answer takes longer.

***

What the beast has been doing is not a task anyone gave it.

There was a time when the lower world reached the plateau. Clouds arrived with their undersides torn by ridges. Water moved through the branching channels. The sleeping places were made soft by bodies that rose from them every morning.

Then something happened below.

The power learns what it was. The people do not have to explain it again.

The beast remembers a weight against its foreleg. It remembers lowering its head, trying to shelter something too small to endure what was coming. Around it, other creatures did the same. The people put down their bowls. A child drew a stone along a tendon and stopped to listen.

The moment held.

Beyond the rim, the world continued.

Up here, one act of sheltering entered another, and another entered that. The small animals held the seams. The long ones held the distances. The largest kept the horizon far enough away.

No one had meant to make a country.

For a while, it had been enough merely not to lose anything else.

Now there are lives on the plateau that have never known any other arrangement. There are pleasures within it. The power finds them and leaves them undiminished.

It also finds the beast’s wish to lower itself onto the stone.

The wish is faint, repeatedly set aside. Each time it rises, the beast feels the forest inside its shadow, the sleepers along the rim, the small hooked beak. It keeps standing.

The power stays beside it until the beast can feel that its weariness has been noticed without being made into a demand.

Then it offers to bear some of the weight.

***

The first change occurs beneath the shelter.

The little animal resting in the polished hollow at the base of the post opens its eyes. The person whose cheek rests against the wood remains asleep.

Nothing slips out of place.

The animal closes its eyes again, then opens them once more.

A little later, it leaves the hollow.

The person does not wake. The post stays upright. Outside, the overturned bowl remains beside the doorway, with its familiar chip.

The animal walks as far as the bowl and looks underneath it.

The space it has left behind slowly loses the shape of its back.

***

The woman with the seven-ended braid is the first to travel the plateau in a new way.

She does not go to every creature. She visits those she knows well enough to approach without surprise. At each, she lays down a strand and waits.

Sometimes she picks it up again.

Sometimes she leaves it.

At one sleeping hollow she kneels for a very long time, her hands empty. When she gets up, she does not cover the hollow with hide.

The power stays near that place after she has gone. No new inhabitant is put there. No ear-shaped mark is required to fade.

Elsewhere, the man smoothing the bone stops to inspect one of the curls beside his knee.

It has uncurled completely.

He brings his face close. The tiny legs begin to work against the stone. He places the bone down, making a low barrier around the creature so that no one will tread on it before it finds a seam.

Only then does he take the root from between his teeth.

For the first time in a very long while, he looks at how little of it remains.

***

There are difficulties.

A stretch of ground becomes abruptly shorter when a long-bodied animal rolls onto its side. Someone walking toward the seven trees finds themselves beside the slab shelter instead.

The power holds the path steady while they find their bearings.

A small predator, released from its unfinished movement, discovers the animal resting between its jaws. It does not yet know what to do with its teeth.

The cloudy-eyed person sits nearby. Food is brought. The smaller animal is given somewhere to go.

The power could prevent every hurt by fixing each creature permanently into a harmless posture. It has seen the cost of that already.

Instead, it remains attentive.

There will be mouths that must eat, bodies that will tire, meetings that cannot be made safe merely by standing still. Those who begin to move are not sent straight down into the lower world. On the broad stone, among people who know their individual faces, they have time to learn.

Those who remain still are not left behind. Their paths and hollows are kept. The new movements go carefully around them.

***

When the egg opens, only three people are present.

One is the child with the pierced pebble. One is the woman whose sleeve ends in six small toes. The third has come because they remember the creature that laid it.

The hooked beak disappears from the crack.

For several breaths there is no movement.

Then a second fracture runs across the shell, much farther than the first.

The child begins to reach out. The woman touches the child’s wrist, and the hand pauses.

The shell lifts.

What emerges is awkward, damp, and quite unlike the shapes the waiting people have carried in their minds. One limb remains caught. The creature pulls, fails, rests, and pulls again.

This time the woman helps.

Afterward, the child asks a question.

The woman answers it, then looks at the empty shell before adding something else.

The power does not speak for her.

***

At the rim, the beast begins to bend its forelegs.

Far below, a ridge becomes briefly clearer. The animals fitted along its shadow raise their heads.

The power takes the strain gradually. It does not lift the plateau or brace it from beneath. It supports each place as that place needs supporting: the grove’s long shade, the breathing holes, the floor beneath the cracked egg.

The beast pauses.

It has forgotten how to continue lowering itself once it has begun.

The cloudy-eyed person approaches and puts a palm against one rounded limb. They cannot carry any of its weight. They stand there nonetheless.

The beast bends again.

Its plates settle with a deep, small sound.

The miniature tree in its old injury tilts, but its roots hold. Beneath it, the forest shade shifts across the ground. Several animals get up and follow. Others stay where they are, newly in the light.

At last the beast rests.

For some time its eye remains open, watching the rim.

Nothing falls.

The power waits with it.

***

Much later, beside a grain of salt, the tiny animal lowers its translucent foot.

The grain adheres to its sole.

It lifts the foot to examine what has happened, and through the salt and the foot you can still see a very small part of the far horizon.

It stands like that for a while.

Then it takes another step.
