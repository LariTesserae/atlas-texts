The power came first to the quiet place by the rim, because it was the only part of the town that did not appear to be reaching for something.

There was someone on the seat already: a girl holding a covered basket between her feet. Inside it, a crab was making persistent attempts to become audible.

“Are you waiting?” she asked.

“Yes,” said the power.

“The way down will be different when you finish.”

“I expect so.”

She moved the basket to make room.

For a while they watched the hall. One of its eastern columns had divided into three, and the people carrying benches were disagreeing about whether this was an improvement. Rainwater moved along the edge of the clearing in threads so fine that they seemed to be scratches in the light.

The small creature stood beside the pool.

The power did not pick it up.

It was tempting to do so. Its legs were slight, and the translucent fold on its back had a flaw near the base: a thickened place that did not close quite evenly. But the power had not yet learned what counted as an injury here.

Instead, it lowered one hand beside the seat, palm upward, and left it there.

The creature paid no attention.

“Sometimes it’s smaller,” the girl said.

“Do you know why?”

“No.”

The power waited until she had gone, then waited longer.

***

It learned the town in the order the town allowed.

It learned that the suspended drinking water was not being held up. There was, locally, a downward direction that passed through the fountain and returned to itself. The fish found it easy enough.

It learned that the market stalls wandered partly because they needed different things at different times. Anchoring them permanently would have made their broad leaves yellow.

It learned that the woman with the ivory pegs was not unreasonable. Three of the places where her doorway had stood had been easier to reach with a stiff knee. The fourth had faced a room in which her sister used to sit.

It learned that the cracking beneath the courts did not always mean danger, but that danger sometimes occurred without cracking.

This last fact troubled it.

The power spent a night under the town, where there was no space in which a person could have spent a night. It listened through the pale fibers, the warm vessels, the layers pressed dark and hard by everything above them.

Some things below were alive. Some were not. Some had ceased to be alive so gradually that no useful boundary remained.

There were chambers that still held air. There were old doorways whose thresholds had outlasted both sides. In one place, a narrow channel ran around a wrapped body rather than through it, and had been doing so for a very long time.

The power did not open anything.

By morning it understood a little more about the creature.

Not enough to give it a name.

***

During the next hard shower, the shallow pool overflowed. The creature stepped into the water and opened its fold.

This time the power was listening closely.

Across the basin, an empty kitchen lowered its ceiling. A drain widened beside the market. Beneath the hall, something under strain yielded by less than the thickness of a fingernail.

These movements belonged to the town. The creature did not order them. What it did was keep one movement from arriving too soon for another.

Water had to be somewhere. Weight had to rest on something. A room could not withdraw its doorway while a person was halfway through, though the town sometimes came close.

The small creature stood where these necessities crossed.

Its fold trembled.

There were other crossings elsewhere in the basin, and others in the settlements beyond. But this one had become narrow. Too much was arriving through it.

The power made no attempt to draw the burden away. It did not yet know where the burden would go.

When the overflow subsided, the creature folded itself and settled beside the pool. One hind leg remained extended for a long time.

The power placed its hand on the hard gray-green ground.

“May I stay?” it asked.

There was no answer it could confidently call an answer.

It stayed without touching anything else.

***

The first change was so slight that people argued about whether there had been a change at all.

Before a passage opened, the ribs of the paving darkened along its intended edge.

They had sometimes done this before. Now they did it reliably, and early enough for someone carrying a child or a full bowl to get across.

The seed-workers investigated.

They scraped one darkened rib with a spoon. They laid cords across a court. The trembling-handed man sat through an entire shower with his daughter holding his coat over him.

“It isn’t holding anything back,” he said at last.

“What is it doing, then?”

“Showing us.”

They began teaching the children the new sign, with the warning that no single sign was sufficient.

The power approved of the warning.

Its next attempt was less successful. It eased the pressure beneath a narrow walkway near the market, hoping to give the residents a dependable route through heavy rain. By evening, the wall animals along one side had stopped producing their threads.

The power undid the change.

Two households slept elsewhere while the wall recovered. The power sat with the seed-workers through their examination and answered what it could. When they asked why it had not consulted them first, it had no satisfactory answer.

After that, it consulted them.

***

They found a place for a second shallow pool.

Not in the square, where too many channels met. Not beside the sleeping houses, where a small alteration in warmth would matter. They chose a disused court whose old floor had already hardened, close enough to the rim that water could leave it without passing beneath a room.

The power could have made the pool in a moment.

Instead, the seed-workers opened it over nine days.

The daughter placed the seeds. Her father chose where they should go. The woman with the black sheet lent it for two afternoons and remained beside it both times.

The power worked underneath.

It loosened nothing that held a burial. It diverted no flow without following it to its end. Where the older layers could not safely give way, it left them alone and told the workers above.

Once, progress stopped for most of a day.

The impatient daughter sat down beside her father. Eventually she took out their food.

At the original pool, the little creature continued to stand in every overflow.

But its fold no longer opened quite so far.

***

The power visited the woman with the ivory pegs.

She served tea in cups whose handles had grown smooth from use.

“I hear you can persuade things,” she said.

“Some things.”

“Persuade that.”

She pointed at the doorway.

The power examined it, and the wall around it, and the drainage beneath. A permanent opening there would be possible, but not without sending the kitchen’s warmth into a neighboring room and making a wet season difficult for the household beyond.

It explained this.

The woman listened with an expression that suggested she had heard a great deal about the convenience of other households.

“Then make it tell me before it goes.”

That could be done.

They agreed on a change in the pegs: one would turn warm well before the doorway began to move. The power also found a way for the opening to wait while she carried her things through, provided she did not ask it to wait indefinitely.

“How long?”

“Long enough for the cupboard, if someone helps you.”

She considered.

“And the chair.”

“And the chair.”

She did not thank it immediately. Three weeks later, when the doorway shifted during supper, she sent a boy to the rim with a covered bowl.

***

When the second pool first overflowed, nothing visible happened.

The seed-workers stood around it in the rain. Someone had brought a blanket to catch a small animal, although nobody could explain why they expected one.

No animal came.

Beneath the court, two movements that had always had to pass through the same narrow place found separate routes.

At the quiet clearing, the creature stepped into the overflow. Its fold opened and closed again before the water had finished passing.

It stood uncertainly on the wet ground.

The power kept its hands to itself.

For several days, the creature returned to the edge of its pool whenever rain began. Sometimes it was needed. Sometimes it waited through the shower without unfolding.

The town still moved. The hall continued its slow exchange with the water. A market stall lodged where it should not have lodged, and three people spent a morning persuading it out. There were cracks below, and people stopped to listen.

But a passage no longer closed on the trembling-handed man when he paused halfway through. The woman’s peg warmed while there was still time to finish her tea. Children had one more thing to notice beneath their feet.

These were changes the inhabitants could test.

They did.

***

One late afternoon, sunlight reached under the clouds.

The small creature left the pool while the paving was dry.

It went only as far as the base of the seat, stopping twice. There it found a narrow hollow that had not existed before. Inside was a little water, held at the same warmth as the pool, and shade that would remain shade if the seat shifted.

The power had made it, then left it empty.

The creature put its blunt head into the hollow. It withdrew, turned around, and entered backward.

Its flawed fold rested without pressure against the smooth wall.

A woman came to the clearing with an infant who would not sleep. She sat down beside the power.

“Is it gone?” she asked, looking toward the pool.

“No. Under here.”

She leaned forward enough to see.

“Oh,” she said. “Leave it, then.”

They sat quietly.

Across the court, a doorway began to form. Someone came out carrying a bowl, saw the darkening ribs beside the water, and took another path.

The infant slept.

When rain began, the creature remained in its hollow for several breaths before coming out.

There was time.
