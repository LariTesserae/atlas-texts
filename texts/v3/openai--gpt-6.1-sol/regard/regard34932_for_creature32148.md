The power first notices the warmth traveling through the floor.

It is a very small thing to notice from so far away. The river has drowned its banks. The floodgate is carrying loads for which it was never made. Across its buried lower edge, iron is slowly becoming something that cannot hold.

Yet a little warmth has been sent from a cupboard to someone waiting at an entrance.

The power follows it.

It does not begin by opening the doors.

For a time, nothing in the city changes.

The cups fill. The plates strike. The inhabitant beside the bolt attends another failure, and the maintenance arm hesitates by almost the same amount as before. At the entrance, the silver teeth comb sediment from the seam.

The power watches long enough to discover that the hesitation is increasing.

It watches longer.

There are things it could do immediately. It could make the machinery quiet. It could stop the flood from pressing against the hinge. It could spread the crowded streets into an expanse with simple distances and no sharp corners.

But the floor’s low note passes through several residents, and they gather more closely around it. A narrow doorway, apparently unusable, allows two inhabitants to exchange boundaries without losing track of which boundary belongs to whom. The sharp edge of one stair is where the slow resident pauses every day.

The power leaves these things alone.

Outside, where no inhabitant has yet gone, it takes some of the gate’s weight.

Not all. Not enough to alter the familiar pounding beyond recognition.

Deep in the silt, a failing member acquires support. A crack ceases to lengthen. The old iron remains old iron, but it no longer has to perform the entire work of holding.

Inside, the resident at the entrance contracts slightly.

The next blow arrives. It extends again.

The power waits.

***

Its first communication is made beside the broken heater.

The strip fails. A needle grows from the bolt and opens into a white fan. The small inhabitant draws close.

This time, before the maintenance arm unfolds, a second bolt appears in the wall.

It is not a replacement. Nothing has been removed to make room for it. It stands a little apart from the first, in a shallow recess beyond the passage used by the arm.

A white needle begins there too.

The inhabitant moves between them.

The arm rises, hesitates, and crushes the original fan. The other remains.

For several cycles the resident attends both places. It touches the surviving crystals, then returns to the bare bolt. At the next failure it spreads across the old growth, making the bolt behind it appear warm and blunt.

The power has offered something, but not quite the thing wanted.

It withdraws the second bolt.

For a while it makes no further offer.

Then, beside the maintenance arm’s socket, a small panel lights with a single blue mark.

The resident approaches. The mark changes.

The arm does not rise.

The crystals continue to grow.

Soon they reach far enough into the street to disturb a cold channel. Another inhabitant stops at the crossing. The small one leaves the panel; the arm unfolds.

Before the pincers close, the resident returns.

The arm stops.

There follows a long interval during which the two inhabitants alter nearby doors. The fan casts a pale distortion across the pavement. One of its needles breaks without being touched.

When the arm finally moves, it closes around only the outer branches.

The center remains on the bolt.

Afterward, the little panel goes dark. It lights again at the next failure.

***

The power learns to make offers that can be left unused.

Beside the alcove it makes a narrow warm groove, no longer than a cup is tall. The groove holds its temperature after the cupboard closes.

No one enters it.

The power does not make it warmer.

Three openings later, a resident rests one thin boundary against it. At the fourth, two others approach and then withdraw. At the fifth, the injured inhabitant stays there while the last warmth drains from the tiles.

The groove widens only after its occupant presses against the unheated edges.

Eventually there are several warm places, separated by cooler strips. They do not follow the arrangement of the seven cups. One is high on a wall. One runs beneath a bench. One is so narrow that it is visible chiefly by the absence of crystals along a metal join.

The cupboard still opens.

Sometimes the alcove is crowded. Sometimes it is empty while residents gather elsewhere, each becoming distinct in a different warmth.

The seven cups remain secured by their wires.

***

The vertical plates are harder.

Their pressure differences help bodies gather. Their strokes can tear those same bodies apart. To stop them would take something from the city; to let them continue unchanged would leave the injury waiting to happen again.

The power listens to the intervals the residents have already made.

It adds a small margin.

Before a plate moves, the pressure beside it changes gently. The change lasts long enough to be noticed and short enough not to flatten the delicate differences between the plates.

The resident with the opacity through its middle approaches first.

It spreads along the lowest plate and stays through the warning.

The plate does not move.

The resident shifts away. The stroke completes.

It returns and tries again.

For a whole bright afternoon, it interrupts the mechanism. Others gather at the square’s edges. The basins hiss. White light runs through the conduits.

At last, the injured resident holds itself across the gap and allows the warning to pass.

The plate advances slowly, meeting its boundary without catching it.

Nothing is pulled into a strand.

The resident remains there for several strokes. Its opacity does not disappear. The power has not found out whether that opacity is only damage now, or also something by which the inhabitant knows itself.

Later, the residents change the warning. They shorten it on two plates and lengthen it on another. The power lets their changes stand.

***

At the entrance, the one who watches the flood has not moved away.

The power considers what lies beyond the curtain: suspended grit, cold currents, fibers caught in the gate’s corroded seams. It cannot tell whether the resident is waiting for something, studying something, or merely choosing this place.

It makes no clear road into the river.

Instead, it leaves a small piece of the outside within reach.

Beyond the silver teeth, but still behind the ceramic seam, a shallow hollow takes shape. Brown water enters it. The hollow is closed toward the city and open toward the flood.

The resident approaches.

A grain turns slowly inside. A torn fiber catches against the ceramic and releases. A dark speck passes too quickly to identify.

The resident extends along the hollow’s inner wall.

Nothing answers.

Days pass. The hollow is enlarged once, then reduced after the resident withdraws from its farther edge. A sheltered fold appears beside it, carrying the warmth that has always come from the alcove.

One evening, something in the brown water bends without waiting for the current.

It is small enough to fit inside the smallest door.

The silver teeth begin to emerge.

The resident spreads across their mechanism.

They stop.

Behind it, three blue panels change. Other inhabitants arrive, but stay back from the seam. The moving thing touches the hollow wall, folds, and touches it again.

The power does not clear the water around it. It does not bring it into the city.

It holds the failing gate steady.

The thing leaves with the current.

For a long time afterward, the resident remains extended around the place where it touched.

***

There is no single day on which the city becomes safe.

A bridge still withdraws unexpectedly. A passing angular shadow still scatters an inhabitant, though now the nearby threads dim until its pieces have gathered. There are cold failures, disputes over crossings, new structures that please nobody.

The residents begin to alter the power’s alterations.

One warm groove is closed. A warning panel is moved. The sheltered hollow at the seam acquires an irregular lip that the power would not have designed.

It keeps the lip.

Beside the old bolt, the crystal fan has grown dense at its center and sparse along its edges. The inhabitant sometimes allows the maintenance arm to prune it. Sometimes it keeps the arm waiting.

Other residents visit. One brings the black fleck and holds it where the surviving needles divide the daylight.

Outside, the river presses against the gate.

Inside, a new street forms along the lowest wall. Its steps are still sharp. Its doors are still closed. At the third doorway, blue marks change as an inhabitant pauses.

Far beneath the city, in the silt, the power continues to bear a portion of the weight.

Above it, warmth travels through the floor.
