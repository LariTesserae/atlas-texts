The power noticed the hollow because of the stone that was returned.

It had seen things carried away before. Across the high plain, wind carried dust, animals carried seeds, and people sometimes carried whatever caught the light. But here a pale foot had taken a pebble into darkness and, hours later, brought it out again. The pebble was placed with its flatter side down, almost where it had been.

Almost mattered.

The power stayed to find out why.

For a long time it did nothing that could be distinguished from weather. It let its attention rest over the rim without putting weight on the air. The winged animals continued to dive. The oldest inhabitant slept through the hottest part of the day. Droplets formed beneath the western shelf.

The power could have followed every passage beneath the rock at once. Instead, it followed the stone.

The pebble lay where the old animal could reach it from the seat without climbing down. On evenings when it was passed from forehead to forehead, it was returned beneath the shelter. On other evenings it remained outside.

There were differences in the way it was held. The power did not hurry to call them words.

It learned, first, that the indentation between the eyes felt more than moisture. It felt small movements carried through solid things. The stone seat held the day’s heat, but it also held the low tree’s roots, the passages under the floor, the scrape and tremor of creatures beyond sight.

The pebble was useful because it could be held still.

Much later, the power understood something of what passed through it.

Not accounts of the world, exactly. Not pictures. An animal could hold a rhythm in its throat while pressing the stone against its brow, and another could receive enough of that rhythm to recognize a place, an event, a particular absence.

The narrow strip beneath the seat belonged to an absence.

The power left it alone.

***

Its first arrival had a body.

Nothing splendid: a small, blunt shape of warm gray matter, low enough to pass beneath the tree without disturbing a leaf. It settled in the open center at dusk. It had no mouth and no resemblance to anything the inhabitants ate.

They noticed it immediately.

The youngest approached, stopped, and approached again. Its ankle hairs spread. The oldest remained on the seat, with all three throat slits closed.

The power waited.

An insect landed on its surface, walked across it, and flew away.

Finally a middle foot touched it. The power gave beneath the digits, softly, and returned to its shape when they withdrew.

That was the whole of the first evening.

On the second evening, one of the animals put a fruit beside it.

The power did not consume the fruit. It moved aside before the fruit could lodge against its body, leaving it accessible to the small creatures approaching from the tree.

On the fourth evening, the oldest brought out the pebble.

It held the pebble against its brow for a long time. Then it laid it on the floor between itself and the visitor.

The power touched the stone.

It offered something simple: the faint, irregular tapping of water beneath the shelf. No command, no vast account of itself. Only a sound all of them knew, held where it could be examined.

The old animal retrieved the pebble.

Its throat opened once.

Afterward, it sat nearer.

***

There was a place beyond the blurred window where a connection was wearing thin.

The power had noticed it while tracing the dawn water. Far behind the visible boundary, past the narrow room and the pale descending shaft, something drew heat away from the shelf. It had done so for a very long time. One of its supports was losing substance grain by grain.

Nothing in the hollow could reach it.

Nothing was failing yet. The droplets still fell. But eventually the coolness would diminish, and the roots beneath the shelf would spend longer dry between rains.

The power could mend the support without altering the window. It did not begin immediately.

Over several evenings it placed a little of what it had found into the pebble: the familiar cool edge, the trembling roots, a rhythm becoming uneven. Then it showed the steadier rhythm that a repair would preserve.

Much of this was lost in translation.

One animal carried the pebble to the pool. Another pressed it to the blue frame. The youngest fell asleep holding it between its front hands.

The oldest took it to the window.

There, bracing a hind foot against the wall, it leaned close enough for the scales along its abdomen to rise. It touched the wet lower edge with two digits. Then it turned toward the visitor and opened its throat.

The power could not know whether this was permission, concern, or an invitation to attend more closely.

So it made its first change very small.

Behind the window, it steadied the worn support without replacing it. At the lower edge, a bead that had almost formed grew round and fell.

The old animal stepped back.

The power stopped.

For three nights it waited. On the fourth, the old animal brought the pebble to the same place and repeated the gesture.

The work continued in increments. Each time, the power stopped while there was still more it could do.

When the support was sound, the window looked no different. Darkness remained darkness. The animals still kept their feet on this side.

The power made no door for itself there.

***

Some things it learned not to improve.

The suspended grit was burdensome, but sorting it was not merely a burden. Particular animals preferred particular seams. Two often worked together near the entrance, pausing with their faces pressed against one another. The youngest scattered grains the others had already placed, and was occasionally permitted to finish doing so.

A device that put every grain in its destination would have removed more than labor.

The power watched which parts hurt.

The oldest sometimes failed to lift its middle foot clear of the crescent. On cold mornings, it left the work early. Beneath the minute scales, an old injury pulled at the joint.

One evening the visitor warmed when that foot touched it.

The animal withdrew. The visitor cooled.

Later, the foot returned.

Over many nights, the warmth eased the cramped tissue. Nothing grew back. The missing front digit remained missing, and the scar across the back remained pale. But the old one began crossing the bare floor without the small hesitation halfway.

It still slept under the seat.

The power did not ask it to sleep elsewhere.

***

By the next rain, the visitor had become an unremarkable part of several evenings.

A young animal sometimes rested its chin on the gray surface. The pool-drinker ignored it. Another used it to brace a piece of bark while scraping the softer inside with its teeth.

The power accepted these uses.

It had no wish to make the open center into a place where everyone must attend to it. When the inhabitants gathered after rain, it withdrew beneath the tree.

There were other things it might have done. It could have deepened the pool or brought more water. It could have sheltered every seedling that appeared in the softened seams.

It watched a surviving plant unfurl where three others had withered. It watched the tiny animals feeding on the dead roots. It watched the low tree turn its leaves.

There was enough here to make ignorance consequential.

For now, it kept the hidden support sound, offered warmth when sought, and learned.

***

Late one evening, the oldest placed a different pebble beside the visitor.

This one had been kept beneath the seat. It was pale, with a dark line passing through it, and none of the others had offered it before.

The power touched it and felt a broken rhythm.

It did not mend the rhythm. It held it carefully, without smoothing out the break.

The old animal waited.

From under the seat came the ticking of the young. Above the rim, winged bodies crossed the last bright patch of sky. The tree released a fruit, which drifted sideways until a leaf caught it.

At length the power returned the pebble.

The old animal pressed it between its eyes. Its throat slits opened in succession, and for the first time the power could distinguish the answer from the noises around it.

Not gratitude. Not yet anything it knew how to name.

The animal set the pebble down in the shade, within reach of them both.

Then it climbed onto the seat.

The power stayed below, small and warm. When a pale foot reached from the shelter and felt along its side, it moved just enough to let the foot find the fallen fruit behind it.

By morning, nothing new could be seen from the plain.
