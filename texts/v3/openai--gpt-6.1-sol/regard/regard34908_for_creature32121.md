The power first notices the loose cover on the service duct.

Not because it is important in itself. There are loose covers on many worlds. But the old man has said that someone has probably reported it, and the woman beside him has accepted this without quite believing it, and neither has the ease in their knees to get up and check.

Before morning, the cover will lift in a gust. It will strike the wall, turn once, and go over the edge. Three days later, a maintenance worker will descend to replace it. The work will be ordinary until a sheltered accumulation of dust gives way beneath her boot.

The power sees all this. It also sees the worker’s breakfast on that morning, the unfinished argument she will carry down the stairs, and the small hooked animal resting on the underside of the landing where she will fall.

It does not arrange for her to learn an important lesson.

While the two neighbors are still sitting outside, the cover makes a sound.

A single sharp knock, quite distinct from the grazing under their feet.

“There,” the woman says.

The old man looks toward it.

“I’ll put it in the book.”

“Tonight.”

“Yes, tonight.”

He fetches his coat. At the drinking room he writes the location in the maintenance book, and the attendant, who knows which covers can wait and which cannot, puts a mark beside it.

In the morning someone comes with a fastening, a bag for the damaged parts, and a stool.

This is the first thing the power does.

***

It stays.

For a while, staying is most of its work.

It follows water through the cylinders, through the dispensers and recovery chambers, through the minute exchanges that sustain the bronze films in the rock. It attends a meeting about a fracture and another about the meeting. It learns why a particular railway bridge has been repaired in seven different alloys. It discovers that the supervisor who moves the benches is afraid of being laughed at, and that he has made himself very easy to laugh at.

It listens to the animals without pressing a rod to its jaw.

Their messages are not small versions of human speech. The power does not need them to be. A region of stone becomes, briefly, known to several bodies at once. A suitable seam, an obstruction, the presence of another animal: these pass through the ground with distinctions no human notebook has recorded.

The old man has recognized more of those distinctions than he thinks.

The power listens to him too.

His shoulder hurts. He has begun using his left hand to fill the cup, though the dispenser is better placed for his right. His neighbor has noticed and has decided not to ask until he mentions it. He is waiting for it to become bad enough to justify a visit to the clinic.

At the clinic there is a treatment that would help. There is also a form that asks him to describe how much the condition interferes with necessary activity.

He does not consider listening a necessary activity.

The power considers the form.

***

It could send rain.

It knows the quantity required to darken every roof, fill every shallow channel, make the children shout and run into the streets.

It also knows what would dissolve. It knows where the recovery yards would overflow, which cultivated beds would fail, how water would enter the breathing slots of animals adapted to the dry stone. It sees old walls accepting moisture for the first time and releasing salts into rooms.

So it does not send rain.

But it does not conclude that the people ought to remain thirsty, or that their skill at recovering water makes every restriction good.

Far inland, beneath a mineral survey abandoned before the old man was born, there is water. Enough to give the recovery system a generous margin; not enough to excuse carelessness forever. The rock above it can be opened without crossing the living seams.

The power cannot place that water in the city merely by wanting the city to have it. A supply is also pipes, maintenance, authority, and the terms under which a person may turn a handle.

It goes to the survey office.

The night clerk sees someone standing on the protected side of the counter. Later, she will have difficulty describing the visitor’s clothes.

The visitor asks for three old maps and waits while she finds them.

“There is a reserve here,” it says, placing a finger on one of the maps. “This earlier measurement was sound. The correction was not.”

The clerk looks at the dates.

“Who are you with?”

“No works. No railway.”

“Then I can give you the public copies.”

“Those will do.”

It leaves an account of the depth, the surrounding rock, and the tests by which its claim can be checked. At the bottom it gives an address: a disused room beside the drinking hall.

The next afternoon, it is there.

***

People come because of the water. Some come to argue that there cannot be water. Some ask what payment will be required.

The answer to that is nothing.

This makes the meetings more difficult, not less.

The power does not ask to be believed on the strength of its appearance. It submits its measurements to the surveyors. When a woman points out that its proposed access shaft would cross a migration route, it moves the shaft. When the works director wants the new supply attached entirely to industrial storage, it asks who will determine household allowances.

That discussion lasts several evenings.

The power remains after each meeting. People bring it other things.

A failed seal. A question about a child’s hearing. An appeal against a relocation order. Two accounts of the same quarrel, each omitting the sentence that began it.

It helps where it can. Sometimes it has to say what it cannot decide for them.

There are requests it refuses. It will not make a survey show safe stone where the stone is failing. It will not make a person wish to stay who wishes to leave. It will not quiet every animal beneath a house so that its owner can sleep without adjusting the bed.

It offers to move the bed.

***

The old man visits late, after the room has emptied.

He brings his listening rod wrapped in a cloth.

He has heard that the visitor knows something about the foundations. He asks whether a new support under the fourth processing hall will obscure the sounds reaching his room.

The power examines the plans with him. They speak for some time about the suspension frames.

Only then does it ask about his shoulder.

He shrugs with the other one.

“It isn’t much.”

“May I look?”

He thinks about this. Then he takes off his coat.

The power tells him what it finds, what can be repaired, and what soreness may remain. It tells him he may leave without agreeing to anything.

He sits down.

The repair takes less time than he expected. There is no light in the room except the ordinary lamp. When he reaches for his coat, his hand goes farther than he has lately allowed it to go.

He tries again, cautiously.

“Will it hold?”

“Yes.”

He folds the cloth around the listening rod.

At the door he turns back.

“There are people worse off.”

“I am seeing them too,” the power says.

***

The new water arrives without a procession.

There are ceremonies proposed, but the first cylinder is needed at a clinic, and by the time anyone agrees on a date it has already been emptied and returned twice.

The household allocation changes. Later, the clinics cease counting certain uses against it. Later still, a room is built where a person can wash beneath falling water.

It has a recovery floor, warm air, and a bench outside where people can sit afterward. A child comes out laughing because she kept trying to look up.

No one is required to enjoy it.

The animals still gather around a leak. The works still need careful operators. People still quarrel over who left a cup near the edge of a table.

There is, however, enough reserve that a damaged cylinder no longer threatens an entire district’s week. More of the worn joints are replaced before they fail. The maintenance workers get a second person for the lower galleries.

The power pays particular attention to this last change.

***

Some evenings it sits with the old man and his neighbor.

They do not always discuss what it has done. The neighbor asks whether it can hold a spool while she repairs the cuff of her coat. It can.

Below them, a grazer with a pale crescent above one leg works across the doorstep. The old man has not seen it for several weeks. He bends, easily now, to check the marking.

“That’s it,” he says.

The neighbor looks down.

“So it is.”

The power knows where the animal has been. It could tell them about the warm recess beneath a switching box, the other grazers, the long return along a newly exposed seam.

For now, it holds the spool.

Across the street, someone opens a shutter. The train lamps have moved far enough to be plainly moving. In the drinking room, the maintenance book lies open beneath a working light.

There are still things in it that need doing.

The power will be here in the morning.
