The power arrives during the night, when the distinction between stone and animal is hardest to make.

It does not bring a light.

For a while it attends to the drops: where they gather, how long they remain suspended, what receives them when they fall. It follows one into a roof, another beneath a folded body, a third down a thread whose owner is waiting upside down.

It hears the calls without shortening the silences between them.

In the lowest part of a shallow valley, a large animal has been trying to answer since sunset.

Its front reservoir is almost empty. Farther back, water remains, but the passage between the chambers has narrowed around a hard accumulation. Each attempt to call draws the inner surfaces together. The sound stops before it reaches the vent.

On its back, the usual small lives continue. Stone-backed animals graze the exposed folds. A segmented hunter occupies a seam near the margin. Beneath the animal’s left side, several young lie in the warm hollow that its weight maintains.

One fold has been held open for two days.

The pale surface within it is sore. Three small residents have attached themselves too deeply to retreat when the air becomes dry. The host cannot dislodge them without tearing the fold, so it waits, alternately exposing the place and closing it. Neither helps for long.

The power rests its attention there.

It does not take the animal’s stillness for willingness. It has already seen how the smallest creatures press themselves down when afraid.

Instead, it makes a little room beneath the sore fold, less than a finger’s breadth, and holds the weight away from the injured flesh.

The animal stops trying to call.

For several breaths, nothing else changes.

Then a hidden lobe extends. The host leans into the support.

The power remains.

By dawn, the three attached creatures have loosened. They do not know that the moisture reaching their mouths has changed slightly, or that the tissue beneath them has become less easy to bite. One draws itself into a neighboring groove. Another drops onto the stone and finds the underside of a smaller sleeper. The third is old and slow to release.

The power waits for it too.

When the fold closes, it closes without catching anything.

There is still the matter of the reservoir.

The obstruction is not removed all at once. Too much water would pass into the front chamber; the animal would cough it onto the ground, and the young beneath it would lose the warmth they need.

Instead, the hard accumulation softens along one edge.

A narrow channel opens.

The animal drinks from itself through the morning.

Near midday it gives a low, incomplete note. Beyond the next rise, another body answers.

The power listens to both.

It learns that the answer comes from an animal beside a basin whose water has become difficult to reach. The basin is not dry. Its edges are crowded with bodies that settled there when the water stood higher, and the remaining pool lies beneath them.

There is no malice in the obstruction. There is thirst on either side of it.

The power could fill the basin. It considers the eggs in its lower seams, the chambers below, the thin walls that a sudden abundance would soften.

Instead, it lengthens the path of a few dawn drops.

Moisture that would have evaporated from an exposed plate gathers beneath its lip. From there it travels along an old groove to a shallow hollow outside the crowded rim.

The new drinking place is no larger than a cupped hand.

An entrance-cleaner finds it first. Then two stone-backed animals. On the third morning, a folded tube reaches across the edge.

Nothing has been driven away from the old pool.

The power does not stop at what is easy to mend.

Under a ledge, a soft animal is being drawn upward on a thread. Its mouth is caught. Its body contracts each time the hunter takes in another fraction of the line.

The hunter needs food. The captive needs release.

The power does not decide that one of these facts makes the other unimportant.

It stays beside them, attending to the small structures through which hunger becomes pursuit and injury becomes terror. There is work here it cannot accomplish by adding water or loosening a seam.

Before the captive reaches the ledge, its contractions cease.

It is not dead yet. The sensations that served its escape have ended now that escape is no longer possible. The hunter feeds, and the body does not spend the rest of the morning struggling.

The power keeps what it has learned.

Across the plateau, this change proceeds more slowly than rain. Animals still withdraw from sharp contact. They still remember dangerous threads and avoid the narrow cracks where hooks wait. But when a body is irretrievably caught, the alarm no longer continues without limit.

The hunters do not become less able to live.

Their prey do not have to endure every moment of dying.

There are other changes, smaller even than these.

In a buried chamber, an animal that has spent years feeding on seepage receives enough moisture to unfold. The power does not break the roof above it. An entrance creature, grazing along a draft too faint to notice before, opens a passage over the course of a season.

A grazer’s young, slipping from the underside of its mother, encounters a broad toe placed a little sooner than it otherwise would have been.

A wall whose thinning will expose a clutch of eggs acquires a cool patch. The chamber-feeders move elsewhere. The crust-makers return.

The power watches the consequences. Where its help makes trouble, it changes its help.

It remains through dry weeks, through the first narrow rain, through the death of a tree and the long settling of its passengers onto the ground. It learns which absences are noticed. It learns how far a low call can travel when the caller is no longer hurting.

The large animal in the valley never sees what has attended it.

One evening it lifts its healed fold. Several stone-backed creatures move into the damp ridges beneath. The host shifts to accommodate them, then settles with its head downslope.

Its reservoirs are not full. They are sufficient.

It draws air and gives a long note across the stone.

Minutes later, the answer comes.

Beneath its body, the young sleep through both calls.
