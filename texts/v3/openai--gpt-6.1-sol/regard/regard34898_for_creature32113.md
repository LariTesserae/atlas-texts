The power did not first appear at the street door.

It attended to the room from within: to the warmth where it gathered behind the pipes, to the water where it slowed beneath the roots, to the narrow difference between a crowded place and a place in which someone had been crowded out.

For some time it did nothing.

It watched the keeper remove a plant from a consignment and carry it back to the central bed. It watched a worker search for that plant, find it, and begin to lift it again.

The keeper laid two fingers against the pot.

The worker let go.

This interested the power more than any of the old inventories.

It did not mistake the keeper’s silence for an absence of wishes. Nor did it assume that every wish was hidden in some language which, with sufficient authority, it could translate. There were things to learn by watching, and things that could only be learned by offering something and permitting it to be refused.

But the bearing was not one of those things.

The bearing was failing.

The power could have made it whole without opening the housing. Instead, on the following morning, the maintenance engineer found that another job had been postponed, a replacement part was in stock, and the requisition from the growing house was older than he had realized.

He came down carrying a tool case.

The keeper met him beside the partition. It had the rusted washer in one hand.

“Yes,” the engineer said, raising his voice. “That one.”

The keeper followed him into the pump room.

There was scarcely space for both of them. Twice he had to ask it to move. Twice it did, though not far. When the housing was opened, it leaned forward until the blue sleeve touched his shoulder.

He showed it the bearing.

Whether this was necessary, he could not have said. He did it anyway.

***

After the repair, the room was not quiet.

Water still struck the settling-tank lid. The main pump still sent its heavy drone through the benches. The loose bracket rattled.

But the higher note had gone.

For most of that afternoon the keeper stayed beside the pipe, its hand against the iron. Then it crossed to the maintenance desk and collected the washer, the trolley wheel, and the pebble.

It returned the wheel to the waste tub. It put the washer among the small metal pieces kept for repairs.

The pebble went into the bed beside the overflow.

The power left it there.

***

There were other things it might have changed.

It could have opened the roofs to clean daylight. It could have lifted the court into the air, given the keeper a garden without walls, and made its body proof against winter.

It considered these possibilities without mistaking their grandeur for kindness.

The old trunks had grown to the light they had. Their leaves were arranged beneath particular panes. The keeper’s resting cords entered a bed that had received its weight for longer than anyone remembered.

There was no evidence that it wanted a meadow.

There was evidence that the western aisle had become difficult to use.

So the power’s next intervention was less impressive.

A surveyor, sent to inspect the warehouse foundations, noticed the aisle. She also noticed the keeper trying to pass a trolley at its narrowest point. It waited with a tray held against its chest while the worker backed the trolley around two turns.

Her report included a sentence about safe access.

Money was found to move one bench.

Before the work began, the supervisor set the proposed position out with empty pots. The keeper moved three of them. The supervisor put them back, consulted the drawing, and then stopped.

She fetched a piece of chalk.

They spent the better part of an hour marking the floor.

The bench was moved eleven centimetres less than the drawing required. A valve remained accessible. The keeper could pass a trolley without turning sideways.

Nothing was cut from its back.

***

The power was especially careful about the roots.

It knew which belonged to the keeper. It did not make that knowledge a gift to the workers all at once. Certainty could become permission, and permission could become a pair of secateurs moving too quickly.

Instead, it helped them establish a practice.

Near the keeper’s resting place, no roots were cut while it rested. When cutting was necessary, someone showed it the tool and indicated the place. Sometimes it drew a pale cord away. Sometimes it put its hand over the growth and remained there.

Those sections were left alone until another route could be found.

The practice took longer.

It also prevented several mistakes.

***

The evacuation instruction troubled the power.

It did not alter the keeper so that the stairs would cease to hurt it. A body was not a faulty door, to be planed until it fitted an existing exit.

The workers acquired a low transport cradle, lined with material that held warm water without shedding fibres. It was kept near the tiled inspection space, not beneath a stack of supplies.

They brought it out on an ordinary afternoon.

The keeper examined the wheels. It turned one with its fingers. It put a small pot on the cradle, removed it, and went back to work.

The following week they tried again.

No alarm sounded. No one seized it.

One worker warmed the lining. Another stood well away from the stairs.

After a considerable interval, the keeper settled into the cradle with its hands gripping the sides.

They moved it as far as the lowest step, then brought it back.

That was enough for the day.

The instructions were rewritten. There was a place to take it now: a heated wash room across the service yard, with a floor drain and no fertilizer stored inside. There were names beside the tasks. There was a battery-powered pump.

The power did not count the rehearsal as consent for every future journey. It counted it as one thing they now knew how to do.

***

As for company, it waited.

The keeper knew the workers by their shoes. It rested among plants it had chosen. Neither fact established loneliness. Neither disproved it.

One evening, the last worker sat on the bottom stair to mend a split in her boot. She had brought the wrong needle and was having difficulty pulling it through the leather.

The keeper came over.

It held the boot steady while she worked.

When she finished, she stayed a few minutes more. There was no conversation to maintain, and the pumps spared them the need to invent one.

She began doing small repairs there occasionally.

Sometimes the keeper approached. Sometimes it continued its inspection. She learned not to call it over.

This, too, the power left alone.

***

Months passed.

A little more daylight entered through two cleaned panes. The change had been made gradually, after watching the leaves beneath them. New sleeves arrived in the right cloth and were left beside the settling tank.

The room remained wet, crowded, and difficult to explain.

The keeper still removed sick plants. It still retrieved tags from drains. It still kept certain healthy specimens that the dispatch sheets expected to leave.

Those sheets acquired a small space for corrections.

At closing time, warm water moved behind the foliage. The repaired pump kept its lower voice. Above the glass, a shadow crossed and was gone.

The keeper stood beside the propagation bench with its hands on either side of a tray.

One cutting had sagged.

It touched the compost, shifted the tray slightly, and waited.

The benevolent power remained attentive. It had not finished with the place. There would be another worn part, another misunderstood gesture, another danger no one had yet thought to write down.

For now, the water was draining properly.

It let the keeper finish its work.
