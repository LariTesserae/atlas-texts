The power first makes itself known by asking where it may stand.

The inhabitant at the doorway turns its head. The brush remains across the two stones.

There is no figure on the path. There is only a place where the waterfall can be heard distinctly, one fall at a time, instead of all seven together.

The inhabitant looks down.

“Not there,” it says.

The clear place moves a little uphill.

“Wait.”

A bead forms beneath a ledge, hangs, and falls. Where it touches the path, the shelter withdraws into a distance full of sunlight, although the sun has gone behind the ridge.

The power waits until the shelter returns.

“There.”

For the rest of the evening, it stands there.

***

It could have dried the basin.

It could have made every pool as shallow as it appeared, stopped the water coming through the stone, and brought the displaced falls back to their terraces.

But beneath the falls, the young mineral plates were growing. In the shallow pools, the gray threads were gathering toward the last warmth. Behind the sill, someone was listening for a bowl to be tapped from another house.

The power did not yet know which things could safely be changed.

It asked to stay.

The inhabitant brought out a flat stone and placed it beside the path. After some thought, it added another, leaving a finger’s width between them.

“You may use these.”

The power accepted both.

By morning, nothing had been joined.

***

For several days it watched only what it was permitted to watch.

When someone removed a coat, it turned its attention toward the ridges. When a hand was being prepared, it stayed, if invited. It learned that a chip which failed one test might pass another, and that bowls containing apparently identical powder were not interchangeable.

It learned names.

The first time it repeated one, the inhabitant with the broken jaw raised a finger.

“Longer.”

The power left a larger silence between the syllables.

“Not empty,” the inhabitant said.

This took longer to learn.

Eventually, when the name was spoken, the broken-jawed one looked up without correcting it.

The power also learned to be quiet under the waterfall wall. Speech there required effort. A question that seemed brief from far away could interrupt an afternoon’s work.

It began helping before anyone thought to make a request.

A chip fell from a sorting tray. The power kept it on the dry side of the channel until its owner could retrieve it.

A young inhabitant’s bowl stood in sunlight that would soon pass behind a roof. The power told the adult who was turning it.

“Not yet,” the adult said.

The power waited.

Later it was shown the faint contraction beneath one of the curved flakes: the small one had been learning to turn itself.

After that, the power was more careful about what it offered to spare them.

***

Its first gift was cord.

Not much: a length wound around a dry spool, left on the two stones where the brush had rested.

The inhabitant examined it. It untwisted a short section, moistened one fiber, and watched the neighboring fibers. It put the spool away without using it.

Three days later, it cut a piece.

The fiber could become wet without carrying moisture lengthwise beyond the wet place. When teased out, it made a good brush. It was neither stiff nor particularly beautiful.

“Can this be made here?” the inhabitant asked.

“Yes.”

“Show us.”

The power did.

The first attempt produced a cord so brittle it broke while being wound. The second retained water beneath its surface. By the sixth, the person with the narrow arm had altered the method and made something better than the original.

There were arguments about the best way to finish the ends.

Soon the power was no longer asked about cord.

***

When rain approached, the inhabitants went out to clear the channels. The power asked whether it might hold the rain above the valley until the work was done.

The eldest turned its smooth plate toward the sky.

“How long?”

The answer was given in the time it would take to cross three dry shelters.

The eldest considered this.

“Only this rain.”

So the cloud waited.

It remained above the eastern ridge, dark-bellied and full, while tools were fetched and a blocked groove opened beneath an overhang. No other clouds were turned aside. Water continued to seep from the rock.

When the last worker had returned, the rain fell.

The valley received all of it.

Afterward, the inhabitants put a small bowl near the power’s stones. A dark chip in the bowl meant they wished to ask for such a delay. An empty bowl meant nothing was wanted.

The power never filled it for them.

***

It was the one with the many-piece face who asked about the pools.

They were sitting beside a divided cairn. The pool beyond it looked no deeper than a hand, but a curved line of pale plates lay far below its bottom.

“Can you reach that?”

“Yes.”

The inhabitant’s face shifted slowly.

“Can you reach it without bringing it here?”

The power looked again.

Some of the plates belonged together. Some had been carried into the arrangement by moving water. Gray cords passed through distances that changed as shadows crossed the pool. There were living separations, damaged separations, places where nothing remained to keep two surfaces apart.

“I can try to speak near it,” the power said.

The inhabitant gave a name.

It corrected the power twice, then said it itself.

Far below, a plate turned.

For a long while neither spoke again.

***

The power did not lift everything out of the deep places.

At one pool there was no answer, though it returned through several changes of weather. At another, the gray material withdrew whenever approached. The people who tended that bank asked that it be left alone.

At a third, an answer came from somewhere too distant for the waiting inhabitant to hear.

The power carried the sound upward without shortening it.

A little tapping. A pause. Another tap.

The inhabitant at the bank sat down.

It answered with a stone against its bowl.

Thereafter the power kept a listening place beside that pool. It did not bring the lower place closer. It allowed sounds to travel between them when those on either side wished to speak.

Sometimes nothing was said for days.

Sometimes the inhabitant below complained about the noise of the falls.

***

The first return took most of a season.

The power could have held the wet connections apart by itself. It offered to do so. But those preparing the bowl needed to know how the returning person would be held when the power’s attention was elsewhere.

They tested stone. They changed the shape of the supports. They disagreed about where the bowl should stand.

Below, the scattered inhabitant accepted some pieces and refused others.

One refusal startled everyone: a portion of the old face, recognizable by a long yellow seam.

“Are you certain?” asked the person who had kept the second coat.

The answer came through the listening place.

They put the piece aside.

When the return was complete, the bowl contained less than anyone had hoped and more than anyone had been certain of. For a while its occupant could do little except turn toward warmth.

The second coat was too large.

Its owner shortened it, then shortened it again.

One morning, while the brush was being used at the doorway, a small sound came from the sleeping platform.

The inhabitant carrying the bowl stopped.

It was laughter, though there was not yet a mouth.

***

Some wanted a dry road across the basin.

Others did not want strangers arriving by a path that taught them nothing about the water.

The power listened while they discussed it over several evenings. At last they chose a route along the southern ribs, with three places where a traveler must stop and examine the next crossing. The power made those three places dependable. The rest was built by hand.

The bridge at the center remained.

Its notches were deepened. Its old cord was replaced. Beside its near end, they placed a shelter large enough for two people to wait out a change in distance.

There were more visits after that.

People still left reluctantly when the ridge darkened, but they sometimes stayed through the rain.

***

In time, the power ceased to be a clear place among the sounds.

It had become familiar enough that the inhabitants no longer needed an indication of where to address it. They spoke while working, or left a chip in the bowl, or asked it to come back later.

It knew the basin well by then, though not completely.

There were changes it could not undo without undoing someone’s long labor to survive them. There were people who wanted help only with the channels outside their houses. There were pools whose margins remained untended, and names no one had yet offered to speak.

The power kept its place among these uncertainties.

On an evening when the clouds below were gold, the inhabitant with the brush came out and sat on the broad sill. Its new cord brush lay across its knees. The roar from the east had moved far away.

For once, there was no repair waiting.

Across the basin, a bowl sounded. Another answered. From the house with the shortened coat came several uneven taps, followed by a patient reply.

The inhabitant looked toward the power’s two stones.

“Will you be here tomorrow?”

“Yes.”

It nodded and remained outside a little longer.

When the shadow reached the ridge, it went in. It checked the powder, lifted the bowl from the windowless wall, and closed the door.

The power stayed where it had been asked to stand.

In the dark, water began to gather beneath the stones.
