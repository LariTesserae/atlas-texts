The power does not quiet the waterfalls.

It could. It could separate the enormous sound into channels, draw it away from the sensitive folds beneath the roots, leave the court with the small noises a visitor might expect in a garden.

But the roar passes through the inhabitants as light passes through their leaves. Before taking it away, the power would need to know what else it was taking.

It comes into the court without making an entrance.

There is room beside the basin. There has always been room beside the basin, though this does not mean the inhabitant has been expecting this particular guest.

The power rests there.

Under it, the stone offers the same broad, shallow support it offers you. The power accepts that support. It does not hover to spare the inhabitant its weight. It does not make a show of being weightless. There is a courtesy in allowing a strong place to be strong.

For a while, nothing distinguishes it from another visitor.

Then you notice that the roar no longer presses against its presence in quite the way it presses against yours. Around it, the sound has depth. You can hear one surface behind another: the basin, the root, the chambers farther inward. None has become quieter. They have become easier to hear separately.

The power is listening.

***

It finds no stopped clock in the mountain.

There is nothing seized here, no moment caught before a disaster, no population waiting to be released into falling water.

The sheets beneath the terraces are doing something for which falling is only a poor comparison. So are the leaves. So are the clear branches buried in the stone.

The power follows their activity without requiring it to become movement.

It learns that the small inhabitant beside the drain and the immense one beneath the avenue do not experience the same length of a visit. It learns that a touch can be answered several districts away, through tissues that never visibly shift. It learns that some of the dark seams are restful separations, and some are carefully maintained disagreements.

It does not close those seams.

At one crossing, two inhabitants have offered almost—but not quite—the same height of landing. The difference is less than the thickness of your hand. Each has made its own edge comfortable. Between them remains an awkward step.

The power leaves the step.

Elsewhere, a narrow body has arranged its sheltered cups so that no larger neighbor can reach all of them at once. The power notices this, and thereafter approaches from the other side.

Its wisdom is not a view from above. It has to learn where to put its feet.

***

In the court, it discovers the inward rooms.

The first is broad and wet, with roots passing through it. The next is scarcely more than a thickness between walls. Beyond that are several rooms whose surfaces face one another so closely that a human body could not enter.

The power does not enter them either.

It lets the inhabitant know, through the places already offered to guests, that it could listen farther in.

No invitation comes.

This is useful knowledge. The power remains beside the basin.

There are things it might have learned by passing through the stone. Instead, it learns the boundary.

***

What it eventually offers is small enough that you almost overlook it.

On the side of the basin opposite the tree, where there had been only open shade, there is another surface.

It is not stone, though it has stone’s steadiness. It is not bark, though its grain is warm and fine. No root joins it to the mountain. Along its lower edge lies a thin strip of darkness, showing that it belongs to neither the court nor its neighbor.

At first it looks like a low seat.

Then, looking from beneath the root, you see that the apparent seat continues into a recess. Its opening faces the inhabitant’s private folds. There is room inside for a soft portion of the body to rest without crossing a passage or supporting a visitor.

The power has made it from itself.

It has not laid it against the inhabitant. A little space remains between them.

The space is important.

The offered surface does not promise to heal anything. It does not ask the inhabitant to become smaller, softer, or more like the creatures for whom its steps seem made. It is simply a place that will bear a weight.

If unused, it will remain unused without disappointment.

***

You cannot see the answer arrive.

There is no reaching limb, no unfolding bud. The face beneath the root keeps its unreadable arrangement.

But the little space between the two bodies is no longer empty.

A pale thickness occupies it. You recognize the fine creases from the divided shape beneath the basin’s water. Here they are sheltered, neither polished for passage nor broadened into a landing.

The power receives them.

It finds that the weight is considerable. More considerable than the size of the court would suggest.

It does not draw the weight out. It does not try to discover everything attached to it. It supports what has been placed upon it, including the inhabitant’s decision to offer no more.

Beyond the six doorways, the inward rooms remain inward.

***

The power stays.

Not everywhere. Not beneath every avenue, not inside every seam. News of its presence passes through the city in ways you cannot follow, and other invitations reach it. Some it accepts. Others require it to wait.

Beside the drain, it offers a surface no bigger than your thumbnail. The small inhabitant refuses it. Later, when the power sits nearby without offering anything, one of the sheltered cups becomes available to it.

Across the valley, the long-bodied inhabitant of the horizontal grove wants support at a place with no doorway and no shade. The power goes there. It does not plant a flag or leave a shrine. From the court, you can make out only a dark line beneath the grove, scarcely different from the lines where old neighbors touch.

The waterfalls remain suspended.

The pools retain their separate gravities. The deep stairs are not brought into daylight. Nothing is made to flower merely so that a visitor can witness a change.

In the small court, you still rest your hand in the shallow depression along the bark.

Opposite you, beneath the root, something rests in turn.

The basin is full. The shade is ample. Through every surface comes the tremendous sound of the city, and among its sources there is now another: a low, steady grain from the place the power has offered.

You would not call it an answer unless you had learned where to listen.
