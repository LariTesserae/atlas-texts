The power first notices the hollow by a delay.

A tremor passing through the frozen ground arrives at one side of it, then takes longer than it should to emerge from the other. Inside that interval, something has distributed the force among wet stems, sleeping bodies, and stone not yet hard enough to bear it.

The power attends to the interval.

For a long time, it does nothing.

It follows a bead of water from the ceiling to the floor. It waits beside a mineral sac until the lip opens. It listens to a white animal’s call through enough passages to distinguish the sound the animal makes from the changes the hollow makes to it.

The cold is not an injury. The dark is not an absence waiting to be filled.

When the narrow animal drinks behind a leaf scale, the power attends to the quantity it takes, and to what remains. When it presses the scale back, the power notices that the edges do not quite meet. A minute opening is left through which the blister can fill again.

It learns to recognize this opening.

***

There is trouble below the western ledge.

One of the older swimmers has died in a channel that supplies the youngest cushion. Its body has settled against a bend. Water still passes, but less of it, and the pressure has begun to rise elsewhere.

The vegetation is already responding.

A wall grows thin. A second passage slowly widens. In time, the water will find its way through.

But above the obstruction an ice splint is lengthening toward a mineral sac. The stone inside the sac has a small fault. The burrower that would have found it is elsewhere, sealed in a nodule whose lip will not open for several days.

The power follows all three changes.

If the splint reaches the sac, the stone will break. That is not itself a disaster. Most of its fragments will remain caught in tissue. One, however, will press through the wall of the narrow animal’s sleeping pocket.

The pocket can repair itself. The animal cannot repair what that fragment will do.

There are many ways to prevent it.

The power could remove the ice. It could complete the new water passage. It could take the swimmer’s body out of the channel.

Instead, it waits until the narrow animal next puts its face against a stem.

Then it allows one vibration from the western ledge to travel farther than it otherwise would.

Not a word. Not an instruction.

A short, uneven ringing reaches three of the animal’s fingers.

The animal remains still.

It touches the stem again, lower down. The ringing does not return, but the pressure beneath the skin is unfamiliar. It follows that pressure through a fold in the vegetation, tests another stem, and pauses beside a blister it would ordinarily drink from.

It does not drink.

By the time it reaches its pocket, it has become wakeful enough to notice the tightness along the upper wall. It puts its head inside, withdraws it, and moves on.

That night it sleeps in an older pocket nearer the pool.

The fragment falls two days later.

No one hears it clearly among the other sounds. The damaged pocket fills with fluid. Tiny bodies gather at the torn fibers. In the abandoned lining, a ceiling hatchling finds a vacant pore.

The power does not keep the animal awake after that.

***

It continues to watch.

There are losses it does not prevent. An old ceiling animal stops scraping. A clear grazer is taken between the teeth of a swimmer. Beneath a white fringe, a small body cools while the animal beside it sleeps.

The power attends to these too. It does not turn away merely because the hollow has uses for what remains.

Once, a young white animal sinks through a place where the floor looked firm. Its limbs catch in the fine strands beneath. The more it pulls, the tighter they become.

The power loosens one strand.

The animal turns. Its forefoot reaches a rib that can bear its weight. For several hours it climbs, resting between efforts. Afterward it sleeps with its fringe drawn close.

The loosened strand joins the floor again.

***

Years pass before the power makes a change that will remain.

Near the youngest cushion, a new channel develops beside an old one. Their fluids differ slightly. Their walls almost touch.

The power brings them no closer.

It gives one cell time to survive a pressure that would otherwise have broken it.

The cell divides.

Much later, there is a small exchange between the channels. A change learned in one folded leaf reaches its neighbor a little sooner. It still travels slowly. It can still be altered, or lost, along the way.

The hollow grows into the possibility by its own means.

***

The narrow animal grows old.

Its outer fingers become less sensitive. It takes longer to distinguish a closed blister from one ready to open. The pocket nearest the pool develops a shallow support beneath its chest.

The power watches it sleep there.

One finger remains against the wall, moving faintly as fluid passes underneath. At intervals the animal lifts its head, finds the same stem, and settles again.

Nothing is asked of it.

Above, snow settles into the shallow dip. Below, a burrower empties its pouch into a receptive pore. A swimmer folds through a new passage. On the youngest cushion, two leaves have drawn apart far enough to admit water.

The power keeps its attention on the place without occupying the room it is making.
