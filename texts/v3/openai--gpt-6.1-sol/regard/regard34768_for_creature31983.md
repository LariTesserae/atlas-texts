The power first learns where to put its attention.

Not on the steam, which is conspicuous, nor on the highest ridge. It listens beneath a resting animal’s jaw. It follows the small vibrations through the rock: water moving in a narrow passage, a foot settling on a slab, the faint repeated blows of an adult calling from the far side of a basin.

What seems silent from above is full of distinctions.

It watches the animals move stones. One carries a fragment that is almost too large for its mouth. At the water’s edge it drops the fragment, rests, then pushes it into a gap. The current turns. A few grains of ash settle where they would otherwise have been carried away.

The power watches long enough to see the fragment displaced by frost.

The animal returns the next evening. It noses the empty place, goes downstream, and finds its stone.

There are things here already being done with care.

For a while, the power does nothing.

It learns the shelters. It learns which adults wait for the young and which begin walking, trusting the others to follow. It learns the green shelves that survive winter, the pools that become too hot after rain, the long detour around a field of hollow crust. It watches a fox take an egg and an animal take another. It watches the birds feed behind the animals’ feet.

It does not try to make all these lives harmless to one another. There is no way to do that by changing only one thing.

But beneath a shelter occupied by seven animals, including three young, water has begun entering a sealed pocket of hot rock.

The chamber has been safe for several seasons. Its bedding lies on a dry shelf. Both exits are open. The adults test the floor when they enter, as they always have, and find nothing wrong.

Far below them, pressure rises.

The first warning will ordinarily come as warmth through the floor. By then, the passage nearest the fissure will be filling with vapor. The second exit may remain usable. It may not.

The power attends to the stone between the deep pocket and the shelter.

It does not cool the whole ridge. The warm seeps along its foot support larvae, roots, resting bodies. It does not open a new vent without knowing where the water would go.

Instead it allows a small strain, already gathering in the rock, to travel farther than it otherwise would.

Beneath the oldest adult’s jaw there is a knock.

The animal opens its eyes.

Another knock follows, unevenly spaced. Not a footstep. Not falling grit. It lifts its head, then lowers it again.

The power waits.

The adult rises and goes to the inner passage. It tests the floor with a forefoot. Nothing. It presses its muzzle into a seam.

Behind it, one of the young turns in the bedding.

The knock comes again.

Now the adult gives a sharp exhalation. The others stir. One begins pulling stems from the mat. Another takes a young animal by the loose skin behind its shoulders and carries it toward the entrance.

Their departure is slow and untidy. A youngster returns to the warm middle of the bedding and has to be fetched. Two adults try to pass through the same opening. A mouthful of feathers is dropped outside and blows away.

The power holds the deep pressure a little longer.

Not long enough to keep the chamber. Long enough for the last adult to emerge, stop, and turn back once toward the darkness.

Then vapor enters the empty shelter.

Outside, the animals gather on wet stone. Cloud has closed the basin. Their nearest alternative shelter lies beyond a stretch of broken flow, where the rock no longer carries their signals well.

One adult strikes the ground.

There is no answer.

The power has learned this route too. Under a bank of ash, two buried slabs touch at a single narrow point. Frost has shifted them apart. It settles one by less than the thickness of a claw.

The adult strikes again.

Far away, an animal resting beneath a dry ledge raises its jaw. After a pause, it answers.

The displaced group begins to move.

They still have to cross the slope. The youngest still tires. Rain gathers in their coats, and an adult must stop twice to let it press against her belly. The power does not carry them. It keeps its attention on the fragile contact beneath the ash until they have reached continuous rock.

By morning they are under the ledge.

The chamber is crowded. There are disputes over the dry part of the floor. One animal sleeps with its hindquarters in the entrance, then wakes and pushes farther inside. The bedding they managed to bring is insufficient.

Over the next days, they collect more.

The power stays.

It makes no permanent promise about that ledge. The plateau is young; the stone will continue opening. Instead, where a shelter’s danger rises beyond the reach of the animals’ senses, it gives that danger a little more time to be heard. A contraction travels upward. A hidden movement finds an answering surface. The animals sometimes leave places that would, in the end, have remained safe.

That costs them warmth and work. The power watches those costs too.

Elsewhere, it leaves things alone.

A barrier washes out, and the animals rebuild it farther downstream. A bird drives an egg-seeker from a shallow nest. Snow covers a feeding margin. An old adult chooses a warm slab and spends most of the day there, eyes half closed, while younger animals work in the water.

The power does not require the plateau to become something easier to understand.

Its attention returns often to the small places: a toe caught between loose stones, a pool draining faster than its inhabitants can follow, the pause before a young animal commits its weight to unfamiliar ground. It helps where a slight change will suffice. It learns when even that would do harm.

Years later, moss covers the ash above the two joined slabs.

The animals use the route often now. At dusk, a heel strikes stone near the abandoned shelter. Across the broken flow, beneath the dry ledge, another heel answers.

No animal knows what was altered there.

They know only that this is a place where they can hear one another.
