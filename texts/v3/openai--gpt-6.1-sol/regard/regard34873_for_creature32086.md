The power’s first act is to keep the drop from falling.

Not for long. Not long enough for the keeper to become alarmed. Only for the fraction of a moment in which it can learn what the water has passed through, what gave it warmth, and what will be deprived when it is taken.

Then it lets go.

The drop strikes the cup. The keeper makes a mark.

There are things here that look like suffering and are not. The cold is not an illness of the great body. The hollows are not absences waiting to be filled. Some of the bitter tissues defend vessels whose opening would drain a region larger than any inhabited chamber. The slow closing of a passage may be necessary to keep another chamber from collapsing.

There are also things that look ordinary because everyone has become accustomed to suffering them.

The power is careful about the distinction.

For a time, it travels with a walker.

It takes a human shape, neither especially strong nor especially frail, and accepts the awkward coat offered to it. At the first drinking place, it submits its vessel to the host’s weighing hand. The vessel contains no more than a traveler might reasonably carry.

It learns to blacken its fingers.

The walker is carrying a small packet of medicine, three peel maps, and a strip of doorpost on which a woman has marked the heights of her children. Her brother has never met the youngest. The strip is wrapped more carefully than the maps.

At a newly closed passage, they stop.

The walker presses a smoked screen close to the wall, searching for variations in its brightness. She walks its margin twice. That evening she says they will wait three days, although the medicine ought to arrive sooner.

The power could open the wall.

It follows the wall’s fibers instead.

Far above them, a damaged vessel is losing pressure. The new tissue below is a brace. To cut through it would restore the road and bring down a section of roof in a hollow where seventeen people are sleeping.

The power repairs the vessel.

It does not restore the old passage. The brace is alive now, with its own work to do. But a narrow fold near the floor has ceased to bear much weight, and the power encourages it to open.

In the morning there is a gap wide enough for an unladen person.

The walker studies it for an hour. She checks the air, sets sticks, watches the margins. Then she passes her pack through and follows.

The power is pleased that she does not trust it merely because it is convenient.

***

When it turns its attention to the great body, it does not find a mind shaped like a person’s.

There is no hidden face behind the luminous roof. No single thought travels through all the chambers.

There are, however, kinds of attention.

A wound changes what happens far from it. Repeated injury produces an answer different from the answer to the first cut. Warmth, pressure, mineral hunger, and the movement of light are held in living arrangements that outlast seasons. Parts of the body distinguish between a burden that can be accommodated and one that must be expelled.

The power remains there long enough to learn.

It discovers that some inhabited seams are healthier because people harvest them. Dead skins are removed before they obstruct small outlets. A pool tended by a keeper is less likely to become a pocket of decay. Elsewhere, the inhabitants are surviving by cutting tissue the body cannot readily spare.

Neither people nor plant know the whole of this.

The power begins where their needs agree.

***

Its first enduring gift is an instrument small enough to fit in a glove.

It is made from a shed browser plate, a strand of ceiling fiber, and a shaving of mineral clay. None of these materials is inexhaustible. None is beyond the inhabitants’ reach.

Laid against the floor, the plate takes up movements too slight for a person to feel. The fiber carries them to a little tongue of clay. Sometimes the tongue trembles. Sometimes it clicks.

The clicks mean nothing until someone learns them.

The power shows the instrument to a keeper whose records are unusually patient. Together they compare it with her marks, her listening funnel, the leaning sticks, and the behavior of the browsers.

Their first interpretation is wrong.

A rapid clicking that seems to precede a dangerous opening also occurs beside harmless growth. The keeper refuses to let anyone call it a warning until they can tell the difference.

The power helps her tell the difference.

Later, three villages make instruments of their own. One uses too thick a plate. One hangs the fiber under excessive tension. One discovers a better way to seat the clay.

Instructions travel with samples, dates, and descriptions of the seams where they were tested.

People argue about them.

The power does not prevent this. It does prevent a man from selling a silent, badly made plate to a family preparing to cross an unstable shelf. It does so by asking him to demonstrate it beside a fissure everyone already knows is moving.

There is no thunder. He has to return the food he was paid.

***

Water requires greater care.

The power could put a spring in every house. But water introduced into these hollows would have consequences: tender growth spreading across paths, unknown organisms flourishing, vessels drawing toward new damp regions, settlements finding that their dependable floors had become mouths.

Instead, it helps the keepers find water already being lost.

High in certain light-fans, warm currents deposit moisture on tissue that cannot take it back. It hangs there until the air carries it away. The winged animals drink a little of it.

The power shows a group of keepers how to raise a collecting thread without cutting the fan. The thread must be loose enough not to strangle new growth. Its lower end rests in a covered cup.

They try one.

For eleven days it gathers scarcely anything. On the twelfth, it fills half a vessel.

The keeper tastes the water, waits, tastes again. The power knows it is safe, but does not ask her to substitute that knowledge for her own caution.

They leave most of the droplets untouched.

In one house, the additional water means a child no longer has to choose between drinking and softening the cake she cannot chew. In another, an old man washes the skin beneath a brace that has rubbed him raw.

These are not spectacular changes. The power attends to them closely.

***

It does act without waiting when waiting would cost a life.

A shelf begins to fold above a sleeping camp. The warning threads have been tangled by a young browser, and the person meant to inspect them has fallen asleep after two nights beside a feverish child.

The power holds the shelf.

Under that immense weight, it wakes the inspector with the sound of a vessel tipping in its rack.

The inspector sees the displaced threads. She raises the alarm. Dark knots come undone. Water goes first.

One household will not leave without its hearth tray. Another cannot find a child who has crawled into the space beneath a bed. The power continues to hold the shelf. It puts a small light under the bed.

When the last sledge clears the margin, it releases the tissue slowly, so that the folding does not send a destructive wind through the neighboring camps.

Afterward, the inspector believes she nearly killed everyone.

The power sits beside her while she repairs the threads.

“You were tired,” it says.

She ties another knot.

“There should have been two of us,” she says.

“Yes.”

At the next meal, she asks for two.

***

It is asked to do larger things.

To stop the growth forever. To make every food sweet. To restore a passage beneath which someone’s mother disappeared years ago.

It does not dismiss these requests as failures of understanding.

Where it can recover the lost, it does. Where there is nothing living left to recover, it says so plainly and stays to hear what follows.

It can preserve a village’s ground for a while, but not without making the surrounding tissue carry the strain. It explains this before anyone agrees. Sometimes a community asks for time enough to move its drying racks. Once, they ask for seven days because a dying woman wishes to remain in the house where her children were born.

The power gives them seven days.

It also makes sure the neighboring seams are not harmed by the gift.

***

Eventually the child beneath the black cover asks it whether the body is asleep.

“Some parts are resting,” it tells her. “Some are busy.”

“Does it know we’re here?”

“Not in the way your father knows.”

She considers this.

“Can we hurt it?”

“Yes.”

“Can it hurt us?”

“Yes.”

Her father has stopped repairing the glove.

The power does not tell them that everything will be well. It tells them which changes it has learned to recognize, and which it still does not understand. Before leaving, it asks the father to show it the stitch he uses at the glove’s thumb. The repair holds better than the one it had intended to teach him.

Outside, the keeper is awake.

There are now two cups beneath the hanging points. One receives the ordinary drops. The other is connected to a fine collecting thread whose upper end is lost in brightness.

The keeper has left space in her record for both.

The power sits down on an insulating mat beside her. For a while they discuss a slight bitterness in the fourth point, and whether it has arrived earlier than last season.

Then they wait.

Overhead, water gathers. When it is heavy enough, it falls.
