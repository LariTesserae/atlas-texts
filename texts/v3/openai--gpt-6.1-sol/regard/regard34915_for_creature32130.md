The power attends first to the raised foot.

The old female has reached again toward the missing foothold. Her antenna passes over the place where ceramic used to be, then down into the crack. There is metal within reach, but it is smooth, and its angle would leave her hanging beneath the next carrier’s wheel.

The power could lift her. It could set her beside the warm cover, where there is food.

Instead, it holds the rocking fragment still for the length of one ordinary pause.

The beetle turns. Her antenna finds the rough underside of a bracket. She tests it, puts down one foot, then another. The other beetle draws back far enough to let her pass. By the time the next cable car loads the tower, both are clear.

The fragment resumes its movement.

It is not left to fall.

Beneath it, beyond the reach of feet and brushes, the power closes a small fracture around one of the fragment’s three supports. The repair does not restore the insulating block to its original shape. Too much has happened in those breaks. There are eggs in them, shelters, passages worn smooth by bodies that have never known any other country.

The fragment settles by less than the thickness of a moth’s wing. Its overhang remains. So does the passage beneath it.

Then the power listens to the tower.

There are failures here that no arrangement of footholds can answer. The central terminal is warming unevenly. The bright patch made by the repair arms has carried the load for a long time, but one of its buried layers is beginning to oxidize. Above the chamber, water has entered a fastening and freezes there each night. Far below, an inspection record has been copied forward without the inspection being done.

The power does not ask the little arms to become equal to these things.

At the cableway station, a maintenance technician opens the next day’s work list. An old entry, concealed beneath several newer ones, appears at the top. Its attached photograph shows the worn screw on the highest tower.

She enlarges the photograph.

For a moment she looks at the seam rather than the screw. Then she adds the proper access equipment to the order.

There will be time before she comes. Enough for a thaw and a change of wind. Enough for the inhabitants to find the alterations already beginning at the chamber’s edge.

The power does not seal the entrance.

It turns a loose curl of enamel outward, forming a shallow baffle. The next gust spends its hardest force against this lip before entering. Air still passes through. So do odors, flies, and the occasional egg on a thread of silk. From the balcony, the peaks remain visible.

Inside, the spider nearest the seam waits through the gust with four feet on its web and four on the wall. Two strands break instead of seven. It gathers the loose ends.

The old carrier reaches its troublesome bend.

It stops, reverses, advances, and stops again. Behind it, three machines wait at their prescribed intervals. The power examines the washer-wheel, the crooked axle, the filaments in the brush socket. Nothing in the carrier experiences the delay as humiliation. But behind the waiting machines a moth has been caught between a rail and the wall, and the stationary brush is keeping it there.

A burr on the rail folds flat.

The carrier moves forward. Its wheel still turns unevenly. The moth pulls one leg free, then a second, and climbs above the route. A few scales remain on the brush.

Elsewhere, the power leaves the idle housing alone. The eggs beneath its lid are intact. No useful purpose would be served by making it travel again.

Water requires more care.

A drop in the wrong place could bridge the terminal. A wet drift could become a trap. A plentiful reservoir would draw bodies into a crowd that the country could not feed.

On the underside of the outer plate, away from the live contacts, the power makes a narrow patch a little cooler. At dawn, when the air permits it, moisture gathers there. The droplets are smaller than the heads of the fastening pins. A rough line in the enamel leads toward them without crossing a rail.

The first moth finds the patch by walking.

It touches the surface, moves on, then returns. Its mouthpart uncurls. For several minutes it remains there, drinking so slowly that the change cannot be seen.

The power watches those minutes without hurrying them.

It does not remove the spiders. It does not turn the dead into something that never died. The flies still emerge from the recesses; beetles still dispute a scrap of food. There is hunger here that a little water cannot answer.

But some of the living moths have strength enough to leave.

At night, a narrow passage near the plate opens onto a sheltered fold of metal. There is no red light at its far end. Beyond it, the mountain darkness carries the scents that brought them upward. One moth spends a long while at the opening. Then it departs on the first quiet interval between gusts.

Others stay.

When the technician arrives, she loosens the screw with a tool made for damaged heads. She does not pry the plate wide at once. Through the opening she sees the carriers and the crowded contact cover, and pauses.

Her instructions require the terminal to be replaced. They do not require the cavity to be emptied.

She photographs the wire routes before moving anything. The old terminal goes into a shallow tray, with its inhabitants still attached, while she installs the new one. She works without spraying solvent. The cracked block receives supports rather than a wholesale replacement.

Before closing the plate, she returns the idle housings to their former positions.

One is almost weightless. She handles it carefully anyway.

By evening, the red lamp is steady. The three repair arms have no damaged connection to hold. One tests the new terminal twice, then lowers itself. Another begins sorting the fragments left in its reach.

The old female passes beneath the overhang. Her antennae find a different sequence now: ceramic, bracket, ceramic, wire. She pauses at each.

Above her, the restored plate takes the wind. Below her, the mountain falls away through cloud.

The power remains attentive, but does not occupy the passages.

There is room for the beetle to learn the way.
