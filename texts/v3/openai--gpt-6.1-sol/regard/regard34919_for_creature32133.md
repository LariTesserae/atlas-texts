The power first becomes aware of the city through the stone.

A pulse travels along a buried terrace, loses some of itself in a fractured lintel, and reaches the broad soles of an animal resting beyond the broken arch. The animal answers. Its throat is old, and the sound it makes is lower than the others.

The power listens to several such exchanges before entering.

It does not come down by the descent. It does not bring light. There are eggs in the galleries, moths on the undersides of leaves, and animals whose eyes have never needed to close against a sudden brightness. It arrives without displacing the cool air.

For a long while, nothing happens.

The birds continue calling. A root presses against a joint. In a room near the lowest district, a young animal has found a fragment of pottery and is trying to turn it over. Each time it succeeds, the fragment rocks back. An adult watches from beside the wall.

The power attends to this as carefully as it attends to the ceiling.

It has seen places where thirst empties the body. Here, it finds moisture held in roots, recovered from food, retained through long rest. The absence of water on the stones is not, by itself, a request for a river.

It has seen ruins abandoned by everything that could love them. This city is not such a ruin.

So it does not refill the basin. It does not sweep the nesting ledges or stand the fallen columns upright. The old arrangement of the streets matters less now than the openings through which bodies can pass.

It follows those openings.

***

Above the large animal beyond the arch, a slab has begun to separate from the rock.

The movement is too small to see. Grains loosen along its upper edge and fall into a crack already full of dust. A descending root has found the seam, but has not caused it. The stone would have opened there without the root.

The animal beneath it has felt something.

For three nights, it has rested nearer the entrance. It presses a foot against the floor before crossing the room. Once it touches the wall with its mouth, then remains still for so long that a bird lands on its back.

There are two ways out. The larger leads beneath the loosening slab. The smaller was adequate years ago.

Now the animal’s armor will not pass through it.

The power could lift the roof. It could make the stone incapable of falling. But the slab supports a ledge of nests, and behind it lies a pocket of earth occupied by roots. Moving it would carry changes outward through places the power has only begun to understand.

It examines the smaller exit instead.

Beyond the opening is an empty chamber. Beyond that, a court containing three shrubs, a broken stair, and an unoccupied strip of shade. The floor is sound. There is enough food nearby. Smaller animals already visit it.

The obstruction is a single block, half sunk in sediment.

The power puts no thought into the animal’s mind. It gives no command.

At the narrow exit, it lets a little dust settle.

A thread of cooler air reaches the room.

The large animal lifts its chin.

***

It approaches before the next period of light. Its forefeet examine the opening. It scrapes once, exposing the flat lower face of the block, then withdraws.

Later it returns with another animal.

They work slowly. The smaller one carries loose fragments away. The larger presses its chest against the stone and stops whenever the floor shifts beneath it.

The power remains near.

When the block begins to turn, it guides its weight toward the empty chamber rather than onto the animal’s foot. The movement is slight enough to belong to the work already being done.

There is a scrape, a dull impact, and a long interval of stillness.

Then the large animal reaches into the widened opening.

Its head passes through. Its shoulders follow. One plate catches against the lintel, and it backs out to try again at an angle.

The second time, it succeeds.

On the other side it stands among the shrubs, breathing. A dry fruit lies beside the stair. It picks the fruit up, tests it against its mouth, and begins to eat.

The power does not take this as gratitude.

***

The slab falls eleven days later.

By then the old room is empty, though its leaves remain banked against the wall. The fall closes the broad entrance and breaks part of the nesting ledge. Birds scatter through the galleries.

There are eggs on that ledge. The power has learned their positions.

As the masonry separates, it holds one small piece level for the length of a wingbeat. A bird springs clear. Two nests remain caught in a shallow recess; below them, the falling stone turns against the wall instead of striking the floor beneath.

The echoes travel far down the city.

When they have passed, the large animal touches its throat to the paving of the new court.

Several rooms away, another answers.

***

The power stays through a season.

It learns which silences are ordinary. It learns the sound made by a young animal unable to right itself, and the different sound of an adult waiting beside a blocked room. It discovers that some apparent obstructions are useful: a fallen lintel shelters seedlings; a drift of dust keeps a cold passage from becoming colder.

It intervenes seldom.

Once, it steadies a rocking stone while a foot is beneath it. Once, it leaves a loosened root within reach of an animal too weak to cross its threshold. When the animal has eaten, it waits to see whether it will rise.

It does.

Most days the power only listens.

In the new court, the large animal has begun an arrangement of its own. Leaves lie along one wall. Two stones have been turned flat side upward near the entrance. The curved neck of a vessel rests beside them.

A young animal sleeps beneath the edge of its plates.

Far overhead, the sky narrows and darkens. The birds settle unevenly, one gallery falling quiet while another continues to call. Through the paving comes a short pulse, followed much later by another.

The power receives them without answering.

There is no need to interrupt.
