The power did not come down onto the plateau.

There was no place above it that was not already occupied, no clean interval between the animals through which a visitor could descend without touching something. So it arrived as a slight change in attention.

For a while, nothing else changed.

The raised foot remained raised. The grinders released their pellets. At the junction, a scavenger lowered its belly onto a black oval and waited for its joints to clear.

The power waited too.

It had come prepared to recognize suffering. It had not come prepared to decide in advance what suffering would look like.

A failed ridge animal lay on its side for a century, its suspended body resting against the stone. This was not, by itself, a calamity. Currents still crossed its projections. Its young occupied sockets along the sheltered underside, and when the feeding head passed among them, the whole low framework gave a faint, steady tremor.

Elsewhere, a creature stood perfectly upright with an undamaged silhouette. Inside one leg, two surfaces caught against each other on every turn.

That was different.

The power learned the difference.

It learned that a predator’s tapping was not always terror, and that silence was not always peace. It learned which currents were merely currents and which were the equivalent of an animal drawing a damaged limb close to its body. It learned how long a hesitation could last here without becoming despair.

At the junction, it found a hesitation that had lasted forty-three years.

The animal was trying to move uphill.

Its new contacts had already opened beneath a convergence of roads. There was good mineral there, and a patient grinder, and a ridge whose electrical character changed usefully between morning and evening.

But one of its oldest chambers remained downhill.

A crack had passed through the chamber while it was loosening for the move. The animal had repaired the crack. In doing so, it had fastened the chamber more securely to the surrounding stone.

Each subsequent attempt to release it had opened a new fracture in the road above.

Traffic came. The animal repaired the road.

The repairs held the chamber fast.

It had grown far enough that some of its uphill surfaces no longer shared a dependable current with the organs below. At those surfaces, recognition came late. A familiar scavenger might be disconnected before the animal knew it was familiar.

Then the contact would open again.

Often the scavenger had gone.

The power did not pull the chamber free.

It could have done so, but the chamber was not a buried object. It was part of an animal that knew the land by resisting it. A sudden release might have been as bewildering as the injury itself.

Instead, the power made one small place beside the chamber available.

Not empty. Not cool. Neither condition would have been useful.

It made a place with the right give: a thin interval in the ceramic where pressure could be received without becoming another crack.

The junction animal touched it with a growing filament.

The power held still.

The filament withdrew.

For nine days the place remained unused.

On the tenth, the animal touched it again, this time along its whole length. A little strain passed out of the chamber. The road above settled by less than the thickness of a scavenger’s scale.

Nothing broke.

The animal withdrew once more.

The power let the interval close.

When the filament returned, it opened again.

That was how the conversation began.

Over three seasons, the junction animal loosened its chamber. It did so unevenly, sometimes restoring a fastening it had spent weeks taking apart. Once it stopped altogether to feed a row of young fasteners beneath the road.

The power did not hurry it.

Scavengers continued to cross. A predator tapped from the far basin, and the animal sealed two distant contacts until the tapping moved elsewhere. The grinder released a pellet with an unusually bright seam. The junction accepted it and used most of the metal to reinforce a stretch of road that was not part of its escape.

At last the chamber began to travel.

There was no visible event. Beneath the plateau, an organ emptied and was rebuilt a little farther uphill. Something that had been tightening for decades relaxed.

At dusk, a copper-backed scavenger approached the oldest oval.

The contact opened before its foreleg touched the edge.

The scavenger lowered its belly. Its joints cleared almost at once.

For a long time afterward it remained there, picking dust from the margin.

The power might have considered its work finished.

Instead, it stayed long enough to discover that help could leave an injury of its own.

The junction animal had begun growing toward the yielding interval. A new branch explored it, then a second. The animal was incorporating the help into its body.

There was nothing mistaken about this. Useful conditions were how it grew.

But the power did not intend to become a necessary organ and then depart.

So it withdrew slowly, making the interval less accommodating by degrees. At the same time, it left along its edge a vein of mineral the animal could work itself: firm under traffic, pliable under the particular current of its buried muscles.

The animal tested it.

Its first construction failed. A narrow crack opened above, and a hand-sized scavenger caught one leg in it. Before the power moved, a nearby contact rose beneath the scavenger’s belly and lifted it clear.

The animal repaired the crack.

Its second construction held.

Years later, when it moved again, it used the same material to free another chamber. Some of the bright nodules beneath the road received a little of it among their first stores of metal.

Not all survived. Most still did nothing but hold a seam together for a season or two. One grew a second contact.

The power went on attending to the plateau.

There were things it could mend and things it could only accompany. There were losses without a remaining sufferer, and lives whose continuation would consume other lives. It did not turn the country into a quiet country.

On a distant ridge, the narrow foot finally came down.

At the junction, an unfamiliar traveler stopped over a warm black oval. Its inner mechanism was worn in a way the animal had never encountered before.

The contact opened cautiously.

Beneath it, a small chamber took the strain, yielded, and returned.

The traveler waited.

This time, the animal had room to learn.
