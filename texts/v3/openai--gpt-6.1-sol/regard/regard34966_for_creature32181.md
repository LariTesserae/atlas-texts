The attention arrives without a change in the weather.

Cloud remains caught in the lower valleys. Frost continues forming on the exposed leaves. Beneath the ridge, a full conduit contracts against an empty one, and the small animal turns toward the pressure before the first blue point appears.

Nothing has been asked to stop.

For a long time, the power does not distinguish between the country and its inhabitants. There are movements within movements, passages made by one body through another, things that feed and things that are fed without either being consumed entirely. A root withdraws from a pocket in living skin. A mouth opens a pore in a root. The difference matters, but it cannot be found by looking only at the exchange.

So the power attends more closely.

It follows the animal beneath the lifted ice. It follows without entering the space behind it, without adding warmth or weight. Where the animal folds, its attention folds. Where the feelers settle on separate layers, it learns to hold those layers separately.

The mountain is not one immense animal. Nor is it merely a place where animals happen to live. Its coordination exceeds any single body within it, but much of what happens is local, contingent, unfinished. One slope receives water because another has contracted. A third receives none because a membrane has thickened at the wrong bend.

There is no single wish underneath all this.

The little animal has wishes.

Not in words. Not as a picture of a better country. It wants the next hold before relinquishing this one. It wants the taste that follows the clearing of a cloudy membrane. It wants, when its flank is exposed, the close pressure of leaves above it.

Sometimes it wants another animal.

The power notices that this is different from wanting warmth, although warmth is usually present.

***

During the next dark interval, the animal searches for its seam.

The old spur is where it has always been. The layers beneath it are not.

It enters an opening, withdraws, goes around the blunt base, and enters again. Its feelers find traces: secretion on a stem, a pale grain pressed into a channel wall, the taste of a plant that has spent several nights against its own covering.

All of these have been carried in different directions.

The animal follows one trace downhill. The trace ends beneath a compressed mat. It waits there while the cold draws its limbs closer to its body.

Far below, the warm seam still exists. Two adults lie inside it with three young. But the opening by which they arrived has narrowed, and a new layer is settling over the hollow. The next contraction will not quite crush them.

It will leave too little room for the adults to turn.

The power considers opening the hillside.

That would be easy. It could lift the old mats, hold the conduits apart, make a chamber into which frost never entered. It could bring all five animals to the chamber and place the searching one beside them.

But the chamber would need passages. The passages would need to remain open through the slipping of the layers. Warmth would change the surrounding growth. Water would gather. Plants would root into bodies that no longer needed to move.

There would be consequences requiring further corrections.

The power waits through one small contraction, learning its course. Then it touches a membrane.

Not the animal. Not the hollow.

At a junction several yards uphill, a valve closes a little later than it otherwise would have. A pulse of clear water enters a narrow channel. The tissue around the channel swells, lifting an old mat by less than the thickness of a leaf.

The searching animal feels the change.

It turns.

***

The passage is not a corridor. It is a sequence of spaces, none of them lasting long enough to be occupied carelessly.

The animal braces against a layer that has finished moving. Its front limbs reach into one that has not begun. A cloudy tube brushes its flank. It lowers its covering and passes beneath.

Once, it stops.

The power has left two possible ways. One slopes toward the hollow. The other leads to a feeding junction the animal used earlier in the season.

Its mouth folds open against the colder air.

For several moments, it remains between them. Then its feelers withdraw from the feeding route.

The power follows the choice it makes.

Below, a youngster has fastened all its limbs to the damaged adult. Another holds a root too firmly to release it. The third is moving along the enclosing wall, bare underside shining faintly between six small leaves.

The arriving animal touches the wall from outside.

A feeler answers.

There is no reunion visible from above. No glow passes through the hill. The animals exchange moisture. One of the small ones transfers its grip, limb by limb, to the newcomer’s underside.

The hollow tightens.

This time there is room to turn.

They leave by three different openings. The damaged adult takes the youngest. The other adult remains briefly, mouth pressed to a swelling in the wall, then withdraws into a deeper layer.

Behind them, the seam closes as it would have closed anyway.

***

The power does not make the intervention permanent.

By daylight, the lifted mat has settled. The additional water has passed into a field of low silver leaves. Nothing has been installed in the ground.

But the power remains attentive.

It learns the difference between a dangerous narrowing and one the animals can negotiate. It watches them abandon routes, establish others, sometimes feed at junctions that leave the fields beyond less well supplied. It does not make every feeding harmless. These bodies belong to the country partly through the effects they have upon it.

Nor does it prevent the winter.

Cold draws water from their limbs. Leaves fall from their backs. The old and the young gather where decay provides warmth, and sometimes the warmth is insufficient.

Where a life can continue by means of a small, sound change, the power makes that change. Where it is uncertain, it looks longer. It learns from the animals’ refusals, from the shoots they eat rather than carry, from the contacts they break.

There are occasions when it does nothing.

There are also occasions when doing nothing would be easier, and it does not choose ease.

***

Near the end of the cold season, the animal emerges in a sheltered bowl.

The young one beneath it is larger now. Several of its plants came from the damaged adult; one came from the searching animal; two were acquired from a slope that has since turned inward and disappeared beneath fresh growth.

They separate beneath a low canopy.

The younger animal pauses with most of its limbs still holding the older one. Ahead, a seam opens. Beyond it, a full conduit presses against a wall of softened fibre.

The older animal does not carry it across.

After a while, the young one reaches.

The power attends to the seam, to the approaching contraction, to the two small lobes closing around a stem. There is enough time. It makes no alteration.

The last limb releases.

Above them, the leaves unfold into ordinary daylight.
