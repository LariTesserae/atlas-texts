The power did not dim the plateau.

For a while it attended to the brightness, to the cold water and the sound that filled every opening. It could have laid shade across the basin, warmed the stone, quieted the falls. None of these things would have been difficult.

But beneath the rounded slab, the animal was feeding.

The light did not enter there as light. It entered as a slight change in the temperature of the water. The great noise arrived through the stone in separate, useful patterns. The cold kept certain substances dissolved, certain films alive. What seemed inhospitable from above was, underneath, a place with distinctions fine enough to live by.

The power followed those distinctions.

It felt the fringe touch a pore and withdraw. It attended to the slow passage of food through translucent tissue, to the effort of keeping the margin sealed, to the old mineral plates beginning to loosen at the rear. There was no hidden human life inside the animal, no wish for a softer landscape or a voice to answer the falls. There were needs, and intervals in which those needs were met.

The power did not add others.

For many days, it did nothing that could have been seen.

Water crossed the slab. The animal grazed a little farther into the hollow. A plate separated from its back and remained behind, its pale edges adhering to the limestone. The living body drew forward beneath it.

Then a grain shifted upstream.

It was a small event. The grain had rested for years between two larger fragments in the mouth of a narrow passage. Water had worn away one point of contact. When the grain moved, one fragment tilted into the place it had occupied.

The current under the slab changed.

The animal felt the new vibration and tightened. Its fringe vanished beneath its forward edge. Along one side, its margin lifted and settled, lifted and settled. Ordinarily that movement would have relieved the pressure. This time the water entered beneath the body from two directions.

The power knew what would follow. One edge would fold. Water would catch the fold and turn the animal over. Below the ledge was a throat with no accessible wall for several body-lengths, and beyond it a bed of tightly fitted gravel.

The power could have stopped the water.

Instead, it held the loose fragment upstream.

It held it only far enough from its new resting place to let the old current continue beneath the slab.

The animal remained contracted. Then one filament extended. Another touched the stone farther into the hollow. Slowly, with pauses that belonged to its own way of moving, it drew itself beneath a low ridge.

There its margin found a better seal.

The power released the fragment by degrees. The altered flow returned. It pressed along the ridge and passed over the animal without lifting it.

Nothing in the basin became quiet. The spring gave water at the same rate. In the pool above, the silver edge remained unbroken.

Under the slab, the animal waited.

When the vibrations had become steady, its fringe unfolded. A filament reached into a hairline crack. The mouth followed.

The power stayed long enough to know that it could feed there.

It did not fasten the animal to the stone. It did not make the ridge permanent, or decree that every young speck leaving that body would find a place to settle. Those acts would have reached into the currents, the microbial films, the other small lives in the dark. Even here, help required attention to more than the life immediately before it.

But neither did it mistake the plateau’s steadiness for a reason to withhold its hand.

A little later, the animal advanced. Water passed through the grooves beneath its body. Its discarded plates lay behind it, no different to the eye from the mineral scales around them.

The power’s attention widened across the basin, following the springs into the rock, learning where else a small act might be enough.

Beneath the rounded slab, the animal fed.
