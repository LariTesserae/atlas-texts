The power first attends to the water.

It follows one rope of it from a blue-green cord, through the fall, into a shallow basin. It follows the water that leaves that basin, and the water that does not. It waits through the opening of a wall. It enters the dark reservoirs without bringing light, and feels the pressure on their sides.

For a long time, it does nothing that could be distinguished from weather.

This is not reluctance. There are things it does not yet know.

A dry edge may be dying, or becoming the border that will keep a leaf alive. A closed vessel may be an injury, or the means by which two neighbors have ceased to depend on one another. A bridge falling may destroy a terrace. It may also release a column that has been leaning under its pull for years.

The power could hold every surface in place. It could stop every tear as it began.

It watches a torn sheet settle across a spillway. Under the sheet, three hooked strips begin to broaden. Upstream, relieved of the sheet’s weight, a seam closes that had been leaking for a season.

It leaves the torn sheet where it is.

***

On the highest terrace, water collects in the new fold.

The power attends to this place especially closely. Not because it is highest, or because the unfinished column is more important than the old roots below. Here, something small enough to follow is making an answer.

A hollow grows heavier. Tissue thickens beneath it. The hollow deepens.

At the split corner, afternoon heat opens a narrow escape. Water wets the outer wall. A dark, flat-leaved plant has begun to grow there, its first blades no longer than a finger.

Closing the split would help the column retain water. It would dry the smaller plant.

The power does not close it.

Instead it waits until rain arrives, and watches how much enters, how much leaves, and which parts of the young wall bear the difference. By evening, the column has laid a new band across the base of the split. The opening remains above it.

There is no need to improve this answer.

***

Far below, in a chamber almost filled by compressed leaves, the power finds a danger that the living surfaces have not yet felt.

A support has been sealed off from three neighboring reservoirs. It still carries a wide terrace, but its remaining water arrives through a single old cord. The cord passes through a fold of dead tissue. Each slow settlement pinches it more tightly.

Above, the terrace is thickening under a new pool.

Below, the support is drying from within.

The fresh growth knows the weight. It does not know that the supply is failing. By the time the upper tissue begins to respond, the dry inner wall will have broken. The neighboring columns are close enough to catch the falling country, but not yet strong enough to bear it.

The power follows those columns too.

Then it puts a little water into the old cord.

Not enough to fill the support. Enough to keep the cord alive through one more settlement.

At the next shudder, it eases the dead fold away from the vessel. The movement is smaller than the widening of a root. Water begins to pass again.

Over the following days, an outer wall swells. A seam that has long been still sends a pale ridge sideways. The support acquires a second supply through a neighbor, and then a third.

The power withdraws from the cord.

Years later, the terrace may fall for another reason. But it will not fall this week because a living country had no way to reach one of its own constrictions.

***

It learns to distinguish such places.

There are few simple ones.

Often it spends a season over a basin and makes no change. Often the best thing it can offer is time: a brief cooling of an exposed root, a cloud lingering above tissue that has grown faster than its vessels, a flood crest arriving after a new brace has reached the wall.

It does not make all floods gentle.

Water must still go somewhere. A reservoir spared from breaking may send its burden into another basin. The power follows that burden to its end before it interferes.

Sometimes a wall tears beneath its attention.

Then the water crosses the floor, and the floor bows, and downstream a cup folds around its delicate surface. The power follows the cup until it opens again.

There are losses it could prevent only by taking over the making of the country.

It does not pretend they are good losses. It remains with them.

***

In the lower tiers, it finds leaves that have spent many years extending toward a shaft of light.

One is almost there.

Above it, a growing seam is narrowing the opening. Each morning the leaf receives less sun. Its tip has turned as far as its narrow stalk permits.

The power could illuminate the whole chamber. But the chamber contains pale roots whose exposed surfaces would dry in the warmth, and plants that have long lived by exchange with neighbors overhead.

Instead, high above, it bends one loose edge of leaf outward.

A thin reflection enters the shaft.

Nothing shines in the darkness. There is only sunlight, arriving from a slightly different direction.

The red-black leaf begins, very slowly, to turn.

***

The power never establishes whether the country is one being.

It learns particular places well enough that the question loses some of its urgency. This seam accepts water in the morning and refuses it at night. These roots continue to feed a fallen shelf. This colony advances into warmth. This neighboring patch withdraws from contact, laying down a slick boundary that the power does not breach.

When it helps, it helps what is there.

On the highest terrace, the faint rib under the fold becomes opaque. Rain gathers on either side of it. The fold extends beyond the rim and begins to divide its weight among veins that have only lately reached it.

The power watches.

A gust lifts the unfinished edge. For a moment, the soft leaf is almost vertical. Water drains back into the column.

Then the gust passes.

The leaf settles farther out than before, on tissue it has made for itself.

The power has not touched it.

Below, in a dark chamber, water moves steadily through an old cord.
