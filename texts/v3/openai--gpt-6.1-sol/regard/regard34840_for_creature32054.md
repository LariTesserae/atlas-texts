The power noticed the one who stayed awake.

It noticed the others also: the small resident at the empty doorway, the young bodies crossing from shoulder to shoulder, the old bodies whose breathing had become difficult to distinguish from the slow exchange of fluid in the walls. But its attention settled first on the hands among the hanging roots.

There was no spare warmth in those hands. Holding the new growth cost the occupant something it would otherwise have kept until morning.

The power did not arrive in the central chamber. There was nowhere for an arrival of its size, and making room would have destroyed much of what it had come to see.

Instead, it listened from the stone.

For several nights it followed the pressures moving through the settlement. It learned the difference between a footstep and a question, between the tension of a roof carrying snow and the tension of a body waiting for an answer. It listened to the faint signals that came up from below during rain.

Some were movements of water.

Some were roots slipping against rock.

One was neither.

Far beneath the broken shelf, a resident lay inside a chamber turned on its side. The chamber had lodged between two outcrops. Its former roof was now its lowest wall, and water collected there before escaping through a split.

The resident had become attached to the chamber long before the fall. It could not leave. It could still close a hand, though there was little within reach worth holding.

When rain filled the remaining cord of roots, it pressed into them.

The effort traveled upward badly. Most of it was lost where the cord had been stretched thin over the edge of a stone.

The power held its attention there until the next pressure came.

Then it began with the stone.

It did not lift the fallen shelf. The shelf was no longer whole, and roots had grown into its fractures. Nor did it draw the lost chamber upward through the air. The body within it had already endured one great movement.

At the place where the remaining root cord crossed the sharp edge, the stone became a little rounder.

Not warm. Not soft enough to slide.

Only round enough that the next swelling of the root would not tear it.

Higher up, behind the fresh angular wall, a fracture ceased to widen. Water still passed through it. The mountain still settled under its own weight. But the narrow ledge carrying the lowest occupied rooms acquired support from deeper rock.

No leaf changed color. No light entered the lanes.

The occupant keeping watch felt a small reduction in the pull against its arms.

It remained where it was.

Toward morning, the power touched the outermost root tip.

It used scarcely any pressure. The touch was weaker than a young body’s first uncertain grip.

The occupant drew the roots inward.

The power released them.

For a long time nothing more happened. Frost gathered on the exposed leaves. In the upper rooms someone shifted in sleep.

Then the occupant extended one hand beyond the roots.

The power touched it once and waited.

The hand closed on nothing a person could have seen. Its pale divisions rested in a shallow curve, supported from beneath. After a while the occupant leaned forward. With its other hand it gathered the root tips and laid them beside the first.

The power held those too.

That was how the work began.

It could have made the roots lengthen quickly. It knew how to bring water into a cell, how to loosen the places where growth had stopped, how to turn a season’s slow labor into a single hour. But length alone would not make a bridge. The new tissue would need food. The old walls would lose what they supplied. The young in the central chamber were already small.

So the power went uphill.

Beyond the roofs, meltwater vanished each afternoon into a crack and passed deep beneath the settlement. Much of it reached no living thing. The power changed the angle of a stone no larger than a folded hand. The next thaw sent part of that water toward the high seep.

It let the first trickle arrive slowly.

An occupant came to the channel and remained beside it until dusk, moving its hands between the current and the wall. The basins filled a little higher. The larvae stirred.

Only after the lower channels had cleared did the power allow more.

Farther up the slope lay fine earth left bare by the breaking mountain. Wind had been carrying it away. A shallow hollow formed beneath it, sheltered by two stones. Rain gathered the loose grains and brought them down in small amounts, brown clouds that settled in the basins.

The occupants carried some of the sediment to the pale roots.

They packed it into folds of moss. They rested their undersides against the growing bundles and withdrew more slowly than before.

The power kept the roots from striking the cliff.

It did not conceal its touch. Wherever it held them, it left enough freedom for them to be drawn back.

Sometimes they were.

One strand was turned toward a crevice the power had overlooked. Another was joined to an older root that appeared dead but still carried fluid beneath its dark surface. The occupants knew where growth could be asked for without losing a roof. The power followed their hands.

Below, it held the fallen chamber level enough for its water to drain.

The trapped resident felt the floor shift by less than the width of a leaf. Its hand opened. The power waited until the hand had closed again before making another adjustment.

Over many days, the pool beneath the body became shallower.

The seam below its eyes opened.

Above, the little resident with the uneven back continued to visit the doorway. It pressed its good hand to the wall. Nothing answered there.

The surviving cord entered the settlement two rooms away.

The power did not send an answer through the empty doorway. It kept listening at that place, but found only the ordinary movements of sap.

When the root cord finally thickened enough to carry a clear pressure, the oldest mobile resident stopped beneath a roof it had been bracing. It lowered its free hand to the floor.

The pressure came again.

Others gathered. One went to the empty doorway and touched the small resident’s shoulder, then led it through the adjoining passage.

There, against a patch of thin bark, its good hand became still.

Its head lowered until the brow nearly touched the wall.

The answer was weak. It came slowly, with long intervals between each part. The body below could not say everything it wished before it had to rest.

The listeners remained through the afternoon.

Afterward the small resident returned to the doorway. It stayed only a short time, then went back to the place where the cord entered the wall.

There were losses the power could not find still living.

In the fallen tangle, beneath stone and water, some hollows contained only the shapes bodies had left behind. The power kept them from being washed farther down the mountain while the new roots approached.

When the occupants reached those chambers, they touched the walls and the remains within them. Some stayed for a long time. Eventually they drew the surviving stems into contact with what could still nourish them.

The power held the chambers steady throughout.

By the next long thaw, the growth below the margin had acquired depth. There were roots behind roots now, with moss sheltered between them. A narrow passage descended along the cliff, roofed on one side and open to the mist on the other. It was not yet safe for a young body. An elder could travel it by keeping three limbs attached.

The first visitor carried no implement.

It took warmth in its body, water held beneath its plates, and a little sediment pressed into a fold along its side.

At the fallen chamber it crouched beside the resident and placed both hands against an exposed shoulder.

For the first time since the shelf broke, the resident could rest without using its next strength to call.

The power withdrew from between them.

It remained in the cliff where the new passage needed support. Elsewhere it gradually loosened its hold. The roots had begun to bear their own weight, and the occupants tested each join before trusting it.

The settlement was still cold.

Its lanes were still narrow. Growth still stopped for long weeks, and a wound still mattered. The power had found no way to make these bodies careless of injury without changing what they were.

There was, however, more food in the walls. Water reached the basins steadily. The lower rooms no longer shifted when the mountain shed a small stone.

And at the broken margin, the night work changed.

The occupant who had held the roots still came to examine them. It ran its hands along the joins, eased a tender shoot away from rock, and rested its weight against the newest threshold.

One night it withdrew both arms.

The roots remained where they were.

It waited, crouched low, while a gust moved through the fold. Leaves turned their pale undersides outward. The hanging growth bent and returned without striking stone.

The occupant touched the floor once.

From farther down, a faint pressure answered.

Then it went inward.

In the central chamber, another body shifted to make space. The occupant folded its hind limbs, settled into the hollow, and pressed its pale underside against the damp bark.

Its hands stayed open for a while.

By morning, they had closed in sleep.
