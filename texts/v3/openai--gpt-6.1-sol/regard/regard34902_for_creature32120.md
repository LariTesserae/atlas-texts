The power did not enter through the gap.

It let its attention rest first on the rim, then on the pale floor, then on the recess where the creature lay. Attention, too, could have weight. It took care not to cast a shadow.

For a long time, it watched.

It saw the creature drink along the seam. It saw the slight resistance of a hind foot as the creature settled on the step, and watched again the next time, to learn whether this was pain or merely the way that foot folded. It followed the creature’s breathing through sleep. It attended to the moisture beneath the overhang, the growth of the moss, the small things living in the film upon the stone.

There was more life here than the open floor suggested. Much of it could not be seen from the step.

The power did not mistake the spaces between things for vacancies.

Nor did it assume that the creature waited for company. When the creature rested beside the solitary stone, its eyes open and its chin lowered, there was nothing in the posture that asked for another creature to be placed beside it.

So no companion appeared. No flowers opened across the clay. The masonry remained knee-high, and the worn step continued to lead into a rectangle without a roof.

The power listened through several intervals between drops.

It learned that the wet seam drew from a small reserve within the limestone. In ordinary weather the reserve was enough. In a long dry season it would diminish until the creature had to press its mouth farther into the bend, where the stone narrowed. There had been such seasons before. The smooth bed in the recess held the faint traces of restless turning.

Farther inside the rock, another pocket of water lay behind a thickness of mineral deposit.

The power opened a passage no wider than a root hair.

Nothing happened on the surface that day.

Later, a little more dampness reached the northern wall. The seam did not become a spring. Water did not run across the creature’s bed or fill the dry spaces between the pools. It arrived slowly enough that the stone could take it up, and in wet weather the added passage contributed almost nothing.

The creature came out and put its mouth where it usually did.

It paused there somewhat longer.

The power waited to see what followed.

The moss did not spread across the floor. The clay beneath the sleeping creature remained firm. The shallow pool kept its brownish clarity. A few days later, the creature left a fresh, pale flake of skin beside the groove in its recess.

At the entrance, its back brushed against the limestone as always. The contact was useful: beneath one overlapping pad, old skin had begun to loosen. The power left the entrance alone.

It also left the hind foot alone. There was no hurt in it.

There was one danger beyond the hollow. Above the northern shoulder, hidden in soil, a block had slowly separated from the bedrock. A hard rain would loosen it further. Eventually it would settle across the creature’s doorway.

The power eased its weight toward the other side.

It was a small movement. A little soil shifted. A dry grass root bent without breaking. When the block finally came to rest, years later, it would lie against another stone above the recess, leaving the opening clear.

For the rest, the power could find no reason to alter the place.

It did not remove the fly. It did not keep strangers forever from the gap. It did not make the creature’s life endless.

Once, while the creature rested on the step, the power allowed itself to be a little more present.

There was no shape to see. The air remained warm. But the creature lifted its head, and its nostrils widened. Its feet gathered slightly beneath its body.

The power became still.

After a while, the feet relaxed. The creature turned its head toward the pool.

A drop fell from the overhang.

The creature watched the rings reach the bare rock and disappear. Then it laid its chin across its forefeet again.

The power withdrew its presence, leaving no claim upon the hollow. It would attend again when the season changed.

For now, the creature remained on the step. There was moisture in the seam, room behind the northern shoulder, and an easy way out through the gap.
