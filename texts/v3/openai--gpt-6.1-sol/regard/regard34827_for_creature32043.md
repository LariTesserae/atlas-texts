The power did not begin by making the chamber quiet.

For a long while it listened through the ceramic.

There were the great disturbances: the forced water, the wind loading the outer plates, the slow adjustment of bonds beneath the enamel. Within them were smaller events, neither regular enough to be machinery nor random enough to be wear. A pair of knocks. A pause. Another pair, farther along the runnel.

It learned where the eggs were.

It learned which cracks were shelters and which were failures. It learned that the white shelf could not be made permanently strong without closing the pores in which its inhabitants fed. It watched the largest ceiling animal take a grazer, and did not intervene. Later it watched that same animal reach for purchase on a smooth repair scar, lose its grip, and catch itself with one rear foot.

The power could have made a garden here. It could have opened the shell to daylight, laid down earth, hushed the manifold, filled the basin with soft green growth.

Instead, it followed the water backward.

Several floors below, someone had begun planning the replacement of an inefficient garden filter. The proposed filter would recover almost all the suspended nutrients that the old one allowed to escape. It was an excellent design. Its calculations ended at the warm-water return.

The power extended them.

The new filter was installed with a small bypass. Most of the recovered nutrients stayed where the gardeners needed them. A little continued upward, dispersed through the water in the same dilute pulses as before. No rich slurry arrived to overwhelm the basin. No sudden bloom consumed the air in its pools.

The chamber remained a place where food was thinly spread and had to be found.

At the split coupling, water had begun traveling beneath the floor instead of across it. Beyond the insulation lay a sensor that would eventually register the leak. The maintenance response was already available: isolate, heat, dry, reseal.

The power repaired the hidden breach.

It did so from the dry side. Beneath the basin, a narrow layer of active material closed around the escaping water. Above, the coupling continued its old trickle. The sediment fan received its cool thread; the insulation received none.

There would be no alarm.

This was not enough. A repair without a record could be undone by the next repair.

In the structure’s map, the space behind the uppermost baffle had been labeled unoccupied service clearance. The power changed that entry. It added the chamber’s water limits, its temperature range, the interval of the reversal, and the routes along which workers could safely reach the components around it.

The designation was plain: **inhabited cavity**.

There were other inhabited cavities in the structure. Most had doors.

The scarred ceiling animal knew nothing of the designation. During the next pressure change it withdrew into its gap, as it always did, with its blunt head outward and its feet braced.

Behind its folded skins, a casting edge had been moving closer. Each thermal cycle narrowed the deepest part of the shelter by an amount too small to see. In time the gap would either trap the animal or oblige it to leave.

The power stopped that movement.

It did not enlarge the opening into a bright, exposed hollow. It left the low roof, the close walls, the turns that kept the animal hidden. On the smooth scar below the entrance, it allowed a little roughness to remain.

The animal came out after the flood. One forefoot settled on the altered surface. The toes spread, tested, held.

It moved on.

Its pale wound remained pale. The power watched it over several cycles and found that it was closed, uninfected, and no longer pulling against the shoulder. Beneath the remaining skin, the next molt was forming.

Nothing needed to be done to the wound.

During a later reversal, a swimmer was left in a shallow depression beside the central ridge. Its feet combed water that was rapidly becoming too shallow for its body. A ceiling hunter approached across the wet floor.

The power did not lift the swimmer into the pool.

But it had noticed that one edge of the basin was settling toward the entrance seam. Soon, part of the flood would spill outward instead of returning through the established channels. Eggs, swimmers, mineral fragments, and the water that sustained them would be lost down the shell.

That settling it corrected. Slowly, over many cycles, it brought the edge back within its old range.

The hunter ate the stranded swimmer. The pools kept their water.

Once the work was done, the power remained attentive without occupying the chamber. It checked the hidden seal. It kept the nutrient bypass in the maintenance records. When other systems proposed changes to pressure or temperature, the basin was included among the places their calculations had to reach.

Within it, the white shelf grew and broke. The five-legged grazer scraped a feeding patch beside a younger animal. The failing green inspection mark continued to dim; replacing it could wait until the translucent fronds had folded into dormancy.

The largest ceiling animal shed its skin again.

This time the old shoulder piece loosened with the rest. The animal pulled free and rested deep in its gap, pale and soft, while the new surface hardened. For most of a cycle it did not descend.

Below it, water followed the worn grooves. A grazer entering an occupied patch knocked twice, waited, and knocked again.

The nearest animal shifted aside.
