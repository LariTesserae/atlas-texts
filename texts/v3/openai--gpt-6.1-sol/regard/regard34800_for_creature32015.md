The power first noticed the hand holding the lamp.

It was beginning to shake. Not enough to spoil the work; the younger road worker had braced an elbow against a knee, and the circle of light remained over the joint. But cold had entered the wrist. When the older worker asked for another wedge, the young one took a little too long to separate it from the others.

The power could have warmed the whole plateau.

Instead, it let them finish. Then, on the way home, where the avenue passed between two low walls, the wind ceased for long enough that they could put their hands inside their coats.

This was not its great kindness. It was how it began learning what kindness would mean here.

***

For a water calendar it watched.

It watched someone divide a seed cake into unequal portions because one person had been working outside and another had not. It watched a gardener uproot a bed she had tended for twenty years because its runners were lifting the cistern floor. It watched a returned traveler stand in a meeting court while strangers argued over a house he remembered building.

The traveler’s daughter was older than he was.

When she arrived, she did not approach him immediately. She sat on a resting stone and held the household strip he had carried away. Her own childhood mark was still on it.

The power did not make her young again. It did not give him the years he had missed in the form of memories that would seem his own. Nor did it mistake the people in the court for cruel people merely because their voices had risen.

There was a family living in the house. They had repaired its roof twice. One of their children slept in the room where the traveler’s daughter had once slept.

Eventually someone brought chairs. The argument continued sitting down.

The power attended to that, too.

***

Its first deliberate work was at an interruption.

Three people had entered the shelter in dry weather. Two were carrying medicine. The third was returning to a household where a child had recently been born.

The power could see the crossing ahead of them: a short walk, from their side; seven winters, from the other.

It could also see other passages through the same place. One would return them to the bench. One would take an afternoon. None was a road that human feet could distinguish.

It did not abolish the interruption. Beneath it lay roots, water, and older relations it had not yet followed to their ends.

But it held the afternoon passage still.

When the travelers poured water into their cups, a narrow line of moisture appeared across the shelter floor. It continued outside, along the paving, and disappeared at the interruption’s edge.

They discussed it.

One wanted to turn back. Another thought the water jar was leaking. The third fetched a little water and poured it beside the line.

The new water crossed the old line and stopped.

They waited until morning. The line remained.

Then they followed it.

At the far shelter, they found their own shadows falling in the expected direction. The medicine arrived before it was needed. The child had not yet learned to hold up her head.

Their report was cautious. They did not call the route safe. They said what they had done and what had happened.

That was sufficient for a beginning.

***

In time the power made itself known.

It chose a meeting court, and spoke at an ordinary distance, so that no one heard it through a wall who had not come to listen.

It said that it could sometimes hold a crossing steady. It could find people caught within certain interruptions. It could distinguish a seam that would open from one that merely looked unsound. There were other things it could do, but these were the things it understood well enough to offer first.

The people asked questions until the lamps needed trimming.

Could it bring back everyone who was missing?

No. Some had died. Some had emerged elsewhere and made lives there. Some were in places it had not found.

Could it make the ground stop moving?

It could stop a part of the ground. It was not yet certain what that would do to the water beneath it.

Could it warm the gardens?

Yes.

Would the flowering powder still settle?

It did not know.

That answer troubled people. It also made several gardeners willing to speak to it.

Someone asked what it wanted in return.

Nothing, it said. But it would need to be told when its help was causing trouble.

A record-keeper wrote that down.

***

They did not all accept the same help.

One district wanted warnings before the paving shifted. Another feared that everyone would cease testing the streets if a warning were expected. A third asked for a safe route to its distant storehouse, and wanted no other changes.

The power worked with each of them.

Where warnings were wanted, it taught the rod-makers to use a small piece of dark stone in the base of a marker. Before certain movements, the stone grew warm. It did not warn of every danger, and this limitation was cut into the marker itself.

Where warnings were refused, the old work continued.

The storehouse road acquired three stretches along which a poured cup of water kept its ordinary course. The district tested them through frost and thaw before sending a heavily loaded traveler.

At the interruptions where the power could help, it placed a shallow basin beside the covered jar. Water in the basin showed the time passing at the far shelter.

People learned to read it. They sometimes chose to cross even when the difference was large.

A woman whose hands had become too stiff for weaving asked to take such a passage. She wanted to reach a future in which someone might have learned to treat them.

The power explained what it could and could not promise. Her household argued with her. She listened, made arrangements for her bowls and tools, and went.

It did not stop her.

***

Finding the missing took longer.

The power searched the smooth country without asking anyone to follow. It entered the warm passages beneath the groves. It went where the pale forms stood, and returned without making a road.

Sometimes it brought news rather than a person.

Sometimes it found a traveler still walking toward a shelter that had been dismantled centuries ago.

For those it could return, it asked where they wished to go. It told them what had changed before opening the way.

The cities began keeping rooms ready. These were not grand rooms: a bed, shutters, a place for clothes, a cup whose handle could be warmed easily. Someone was paid to receive arrivals. Someone else could be called if the first person was tired.

Returning was not made painless. It was made less likely to begin alone in the cold.

***

The power also attended to things no meeting court had asked about.

It followed a root system withdrawing from a deep break in the stone. Where possible, it loosened another seam for the roots to enter.

It steadied a grove through the slow failure of one great arch, leaving the dead timber within reach of the cutters.

It kept one rain band from passing entirely beyond a district whose cisterns were nearly empty. Afterwards it listened to the districts farther along the band’s course, and changed how it did this work.

There were mistakes.

A warmed courtyard produced a film no one could peel from its bowls. A steadied avenue drew travelers away from a settlement that had depended on their visits. The power helped replace the bowls. It could not settle the second matter by moving the road again, and remained while the settlements worked out a new arrangement.

Its attention did not excuse it from repair.

***

Years later, the younger road worker was no longer young.

She still carried a lamp for someone else when the work required two hands. The avenues had not become effortless. Frost still lifted slabs. Roots still found joints. There were routes she would not take, even with a basin beside the shelter.

One evening, a wedge broke as she drove it.

She sat back, irritated, and sorted through the remaining stones. Her companion said they could leave the joint until morning.

Beyond the lamp, the power was keeping watch on a hollow beneath the paving. It could have filled it at once. The worker knew this.

“Hold it until we’re done,” she said.

It did.

She chose another wedge. Her companion lowered the lamp. When the stone was seated, they poured water over the repair and watched it run.

Then the power released its hold by degrees, while they watched the joint.

It remained sound.

They gathered their tools and went home. The power stayed a little longer, attending to the road and to the roots beneath it, then turned toward a shelter where someone had begun to call for help.
