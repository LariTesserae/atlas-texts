The cart reaches the square with something walking beside it.

The watcher hears the wheels first. Then another sound, too irregular to belong to the cart: a foot placed on paving, a pause, a second foot.

At the edge of the receiving platform, the stranger stops.

Its outline is not difficult to see. It has arranged itself so that the lamp beneath the signal housing is sufficient. Its coat is plain. Its hands are empty.

The watcher sets the receiving tool against the cart’s brake.

“Is something wrong with the track?”

“Not with the track.”

The watcher waits for the rest.

The stranger looks toward the cradle holding the bearing.

“I can wait until you have finished.”

So the watcher stops the cart, releases the retaining straps, and lifts the bearing. The stranger does not help without being asked. When the old bearing catches against the lip of its socket, however, the watcher indicates one side of the housing.

“Hold there.”

The stranger holds.

The exchange is completed. The worn bearing settles into the cradle. The cart begins its return journey, carrying away a small warmth acquired from the watcher’s hands.

Only then does the watcher turn.

***

The power had noticed the mountain long before it entered the square.

It had felt the engines through the rock and followed the movement of their load. It knew what the dense black powder supplied and what remained after it had passed through the central district. It could have stopped the brushes, opened the buried vaults, drawn the rails straight over the rim.

It could have placed a sun above the plain.

But it had also noticed the two lamps across the empty ground. Their inhabitants had chosen their pauses carefully. More light would not necessarily improve what passed between them.

It had noticed a worker leaving a conduit open.

It had noticed a portion of mineral paste prepared with pale kiln material, and the care required to obtain the right trace from an output that had changed.

These were not signs of a city waiting to be replaced.

The power entered by a freight route and walked at the cart’s pace.

***

In the watcher’s room, the stranger sits beside the heated wall.

The ceramic curtain settles behind it. The watcher puts a dish within reach, then takes the old forked contact from the table.

For some time neither speaks.

The stranger could read the contact’s entire history from the arrangement of its smallest particles. It does not. It waits while the watcher turns the object in its hand.

“This was made by someone,” the watcher says.

“Yes.”

“The first one I worked beside.”

The watcher touches the thinner prong.

“They are still here.”

The stranger inclines its head.

“But they do not know this.”

Outside, the cart passes onto a cleaner length of rail. Its ticking becomes continuous.

“Would you come?” the watcher asks.

***

The room they visit lies beyond a court where the channels have recently been brushed.

Someone has moved a seat close to the wall. A receiving fixture stands on a low bench, fitted with broad handles. The inhabitant beside it has one hand resting in a dish without gathering any of its contents.

When the curtain lifts, the inhabitant looks up.

The watcher approaches and places the forked contact on the table.

There is no recognition.

The stranger sits where its face can be seen without turning. It waits through several small adjustments of the inhabitant’s throat.

Then it offers a hand.

The inhabitant touches two of its fingers.

The power can feel what has worn away. It can also feel what remains: the preference for a particular pressure at the fingertips; the dislike of a cold tool; an old habit of leaving space at the left side of a bench.

It does not mistake these for a complete person. Nor does it mistake the losses for all that the person has become.

There is a sequence still present but inaccessible. The inhabitant once made the forked contact by setting the blank against a fixture, closing the jaws, and testing the prongs separately. The memory of testing remains. The movement that led to it no longer reaches the hand.

The stranger asks permission to touch the wrist.

The inhabitant draws the hand back.

The stranger lowers its own hand.

Later, after the dish has been warmed and the watcher has shifted the seat, the inhabitant extends the wrist without being asked again.

The work is small. Nothing shines. A little of the warmth in the forearm changes its course.

The inhabitant picks up the contact.

One prong is pressed, then the other.

“Too thin,” it says.

“Yes,” says the watcher.

The inhabitant places it on the table.

It does not remember making it.

But when the watcher begins to lift the dish away, the inhabitant gathers a portion for itself.

***

The power remains.

It learns which curtains may be lifted without knocking and which households prefer a sound against the door stone first. It learns not to remove dust from every unused space. Some of the plated creatures feed where the grains collect.

There are requests it can answer readily.

A stiff shoulder is eased. A dwelling whose foundation gives uneven heat is made comfortable throughout. An inhabitant who has ceased to cross a court receives an adjustment that lets the knee bear weight again.

There are requests that require a longer conversation.

One worker wants a replacement hand with exactly the resistance of the old one. Another wants the tremor removed but not the hand replaced. A third asks whether the new hand must look new.

The power makes samples. They are left on a heated table to be handled.

Some inhabitants come only to inspect the workmanship. One finds a fault in the balance of a thumb and returns three times to explain it more precisely.

The power listens each time.

***

Beneath the central district, it finds a wear that the city cannot repair.

The engines are sound. Their balance has held. But the deep material from which they draw their working difference has diminished. Eventually the rock will remain warm while the engines cease to turn.

The power does not conceal this.

It brings the finding to the people who tend the engines. They check it against instruments, then against instruments no longer ordinarily used. The discussion continues through many cycles.

At last they ask what can be done.

The power shows them a way to reach another seam without disturbing the inhabited foundations. It also shows them how to measure its depletion, and how to reduce the engines’ demand without leaving the outer dwellings cold.

The new machinery is not beyond their making.

The first pieces are produced in the rolling houses. The narrow-bodied worker tests the bearings. The resentful worker prepares the fittings, with inspection marks placed prominently.

Both sets of parts are accepted.

The power works beneath the plateau where the existing tools cannot safely reach. Above, the city builds the rest.

***

Not every loss is reversed.

In a room along the brown ceramic court, an inhabitant has worn beyond the help it wishes to receive. It has been offered more time and has asked what that time would require.

The answer is given carefully.

Afterward, it asks for its table to be moved so that a familiar lamp can be seen through the doorway.

The power moves the table.

It stays through the long intervals when the visitors have gone to work. When a hand searches along the edge of the table, a hand is there.

There is no new star above the mountain when the room becomes empty.

The curtain is lifted by someone who knows how to handle its plates.

***

Eventually the watcher returns to the freight signal.

The stranger is beside the post, looking along the track.

Far away, a cart is bringing a component for the new works beneath the city. It will not arrive soon.

The watcher rests its palm on the polished patch of metal.

“Will you be here when it comes?”

“Yes.”

They stand listening.

Under the nearest conduit, a gray creature places one foot on the exposed paving, then another. The stranger shifts its own foot out of the way.

The watcher notices.

For a while there is only the evenly spaced pressure in the stone, and the faint sound of wheels crossing dust.

Then the watcher moves a little to one side, making room beside the signal.
