The power came first to the shelter with the casing above the sleeping shelf.

It did not arrive through the roof. Nothing opened, and no root withdrew. It stood in the entrance, where a visitor could be seen, and waited.

The inhabitant was resting. Along its wrists the fine strands lay slack, joined to the wall. Its eyes opened before its hands detached.

For a while neither moved.

Then the inhabitant reached into the recess and brought down the casing.

The power held out a hand, palm upward, but did not take it. The small translucent shape remained on the inhabitant’s fingertip between them.

It would have been easy to answer a question about escape. Or death. Or the difference between a body and the thing that had lived inside it.

But no question had yet been asked.

The inhabitant turned the casing. Its empty legs cast six faint lines across the offered palm. With its other hand, it touched the folded panels of its own back.

Then it touched the wall.

The power knelt beside it.

Together they looked at the opening in the casing.

***

For several days, nothing happened that could have been called a wonder.

The visitor walked the corridors. At a closed passage it waited while those who knew the place examined the stems. It did not pass through the obstruction, though it could have. Beside a well, it kept its feet beyond the waxy rim.

It learned the difference between a resting place and a place where someone could not wake easily.

It learned that the inhabitant with the shortened leg preferred the longer route beside the water, and disliked being steadied without warning.

It learned that some conversations continued through the country’s tissues, while others stopped when the speakers parted. There were things an inhabitant would say with a hand on the wall, and things it would say only after drawing that hand away.

At noon, when the heat settled into every surface, the visitor sat with a young one who could not keep its wrist strands joined.

The child detached, turned, joined, and detached again. Each time, an adult came back to straighten the leaf pulling at its shoulder.

On the fourth return, the adult stayed seated. Its back panels trembled slightly.

The visitor put a hand near the wall.

“May I?”

The adult watched the hand. Then it guided two fingers to a vein beneath the sleeping shelf.

The change was small. Warmth moved along the vein toward a broad, unoccupied rise farther down the corridor. The chamber cooled by less than the difference between sunlight and cloud in another country.

Here, it was enough to notice.

The young one stopped turning its head. Its fingers relaxed. The crooked leaf settled against its shoulder.

The adult remained awake a little longer, touching the place where the visitor’s fingers had been.

***

The power did not make rain.

It followed the water instead.

There was water held in the thick floor, water moving slowly through the ceiling, water confined in tissue far from any well. Some of it could not be spared. Some kept the roof firm; some sustained the ridges and the folded leaves. Drawing it out carelessly would have given the inhabitants a generous pool beneath a failing shelter.

Elsewhere, a swollen vein had been feeding a chamber that no one had occupied for a very long time.

The power brought the well-tenders there.

They stood with their hands against the growth and listened in their own fashion. One objected. Another went away and returned with someone older. The discussion continued after the visitor withdrew to the entrance.

Only when they called it back did it touch the vein.

A bead appeared over a dry well two corridors away.

It gathered slowly. By evening it had not fallen.

The next day it did.

The tenders marked the place with a particular bend of leaf. They watched it through many rests and wakings before asking for another change.

***

The inhabitant with the casing followed these visits without joining them.

Sometimes it stood at a distance. Sometimes the power found it waiting in its shelter, the little empty form already balanced on one finger.

One day it pressed the casing into the visitor’s palm.

Then it opened its back panels.

Their inner surfaces were greener than the walls. Fine ribs ran from the spine toward the edges. Near the base of one panel was a pale, thickened place that would not unfold.

The inhabitant touched it, then made a low sound.

The power listened.

It was not asking to be turned into something else.

Once, while waking, it had felt a movement there: a loosening, almost a reach. Since then it had waited for the movement to return. It had rested longer, attached more deeply, tried standing in different chambers. Nothing had followed.

The casing had given that waiting a shape.

“May I look?” the power asked.

The inhabitant lowered its head.

The thickened place contained no second creature, no wings packed inside a first body. It was living tissue folded around an old injury. A narrow channel had closed before it finished forming.

The power showed what it found, not in words alone, but through the hand the inhabitant placed against its wrist: the small obstruction, the tissue beyond it, the uncertainty.

The channel could be opened. It might restore the lost movement. It would not promise another life.

The inhabitant took back the casing.

For three visits, it asked for nothing.

On the fourth, it turned and spread its panels.

The power worked while two companions sat nearby. One held the inhabitant’s hand. The other kept contact with the shelter, attending to the slow exchange through its walls.

No skin was cut. The closed channel softened along the line it had once been forming. When the visitor withdrew its fingers, the inhabitant remained still.

Much later, the lower edge of the panel moved.

Only a little.

It drew inward and released.

The inhabitant looked over its shoulder as far as it could. Its companions leaned close to see. Then all three began making the quiet sounds the visitor had heard beside the wells when a drop finally fell.

***

There were requests after that.

A resting place from which an old inhabitant could detach without pain. A passage broad enough for two to walk beside someone who could not manage the slopes alone. Help finding a child whose faint contact with the country had become difficult to distinguish from the murmur of the stems.

There was also someone who wanted darkness.

The power asked how much, and for how long.

In a small chamber, with the owner’s agreement, it persuaded the overlapping leaves to admit less light. The walls became green, then gray-green. The sleeping shelf disappeared first; afterward the hand recesses.

At the entrance, the inhabitant held its fingers before its face.

It could no longer see their veins.

It stayed only a short while that first time. Then it came out quickly and stood beside the well.

The chamber remained available. Others visited it, mostly in company. Some laughed softly when their companions vanished beside them. One would not enter again. Another began resting there every few days.

Nothing elsewhere grew darker.

***

At last the inhabitant with the casing asked about the sky.

The power did not lift the roof.

It made a view in a shallow recess: a patch of blue, a white cloud moving through it, the distant turn of a bird. No heat passed through. No rain fell into the shelter. The living tissue behind it remained whole.

The inhabitant watched for a long time.

It touched the image and felt the firm leaf beneath.

Then it looked toward the entrance, where the familiar yellow-green light lay across the floor.

“Outside?” it asked.

“Yes.”

The mouth closed. The eyes returned to the bird, now almost gone.

The power waited.

There would be more to understand before a journey: air, cold, weight, hunger, the attachments a body needed and the attachments it could choose to leave. None of those things had to be settled that day.

Above the sleeping shelf, the casing still stood in its dry recess.

The inhabitant put it beside the little view of the sky, then went to call its companions.
