The attention arrives without a shadow.

Nothing closes beneath it. The floating meadows remain open. Along a ridge, an animal with a chipped central plate continues drawing food from the underside of a leaf.

For a long time, nothing else happens.

The power watches the animal finish. It watches the leaf rise, and the minute threads that remain along its edge begin to gather silt again. It follows the current through the grove, down into a trough, beneath the hanging stones. It attends to the roots growing into open air.

It does not lower the sea.

There is life beneath it as well as within it. Clouds pass over the distant country. Rain falls there, though no sound of it reaches the suspended forests. To set the water down would be to bring one inhabited world heavily upon another.

Nor does it make a sun.

The leaves have their ways of holding brightness. The animals have their ways of reading what brightness leaves behind.

At a gathering place, it waits through three changes of current.

One animal arrives and places a grain at the end of a groove. Much later, another removes it. The power follows neither gesture with an answer. It watches where the grain is put, what is touched before and afterward, which marks are renewed and which are permitted to vanish.

There are things here it could learn quickly by entering every living body.

It remains outside them.

When an old animal’s eye becomes clouded with silt, another comes to clean it. The work is imperfect. One bead remains half-covered, and the visitor departs to feed.

The power could clear it with no disturbance of the water.

Instead, it watches the old animal turn the uncovered portion toward a leaf. The eye opens. Something in the leaf’s retained light is still visible to it.

Later, a cold pocket travels across the ridge.

The adult feels it coming and opens its fan. Beneath its plates are four young bodies. Three fold inward. The fourth has transferred most of its limbs to a stalk and is too far along the slow business of leaving.

The power attends to the approaching boundary.

Within the cold, small growths are waking that have remained closed through years of warmth. A shell on the stone opens a narrow mouth. Farther down the slope, seeds buried beneath an old terrace begin to divide.

The cold is not empty of lives.

The power lets it continue.

But beneath the stalk holding the young animal, there is a shallow fold of rock. Water scarcely moves there. A loose flake lies across its entrance, too heavy for the young one to lift and too slight to have interested an adult.

The flake rises by the width of a pale joint.

The young animal has an eye turned toward it. Perhaps it sees the newly exposed stone. Perhaps it feels the small movement of water. Two limbs release their stalk and reach below.

The cold passes over the grove.

Under the flake, in water held warm by the mountain, the young body tightens but does not grow serrated. One limb remains outside. Its grip closes and will not open.

The power does not force the joint.

When warmth returns, the parent moves down the stalk. It touches the closed limb with the soft edge of its own. For a long time the two remain there.

Eventually the young one lets go.

The flake does not fall back.

This is the first alteration.

Others follow, but not everywhere.

A stone shifts beneath an overhang. A root, growing into a narrow passage, turns along its roof instead. In one basin there is already shelter enough, though the animals have not found all of it. There the power changes nothing.

It does not make the refuges smooth. Small irregularities are needed for a grip. It leaves several entrances, and openings through which an eye can inspect the water outside. Warmth moves slowly through them. Food gathers at their edges without becoming abundant enough to choke the stone.

For a season, most remain unused.

Then an adult, sheltering a young one beneath its plates, comes against a widened crevice. It tests the opening with its fan. A limb reaches inside.

Days later, it returns with another animal.

They carry a small stone between them.

The power withdraws from the crevice while they work.

They set the stone beneath its lip, not quite where the power would have set it. Their arrangement catches more sediment. It also gives a closed limb a place to rest.

Other stones follow. Leaves are turned aside. A groove is combed into the silt outside the entrance.

After that, the power no longer widens shelters on this ridge.

It watches the animals do so.

Sometimes their work fails. A roof settles. A refuge fills with roots. One clearing is abandoned after a current changes, and its carefully placed stones become the foundation of a small floating garden.

The power does not preserve every construction.

It remains attentive when the distant knock comes.

There is a place, far beyond the animals’ habitual country, where an old movement beneath the mountains has begun to press too hard against the sea’s lowest skin. The pressure has no intention. It will eventually release, and when it does, a whole hanging shoal will turn over.

The power goes there.

Its work is slow enough that the roots do not tear. It opens room along a buried seam and lets the pressure spend itself in many smaller movements. Above, forests sway. Below, a few wet stones descend a hand’s breadth into the air and stop.

The next knock is still heard.

Animals pause with their loads. Silt hangs motionless. When the water resumes, the youngest roots are holding.

No one knows what has been prevented.

In time, the animal with the chipped plate grows into its resting place. Visitors bring food. Young bodies shelter beneath it. The power stays nearby through the last small opening of its head’s shell, but does not make the shell open again.

Much later, one of those young bodies leaves the old plates and crosses the ridge.

It comes to a sheltered clearing where several grooves meet. It lowers its fan. It inspects a grain at the end of one line, then touches the shallow hollow beside it.

The power is there.

It has learned enough to recognize an account of a cold passage, though not every detail. It has learned that the hollow concerns someone who did not return.

The animal adds a mark.

The power leaves it undisturbed.

Beyond the clearing, a white root has reached the lip of a refuge. It divides there, one branch following the stone, the other extending into the current. The animal grips the stoneward branch and unfolds.

For the length of that movement, nothing needs to be changed.
