The power noticed that Mara waited for the beetle.

It had already noticed the presses, the adjustable feet, the children learning exits. From a distance these could have seemed like evidence of a satisfactory arrangement. Life had found ways to continue. The city was full of skill.

But skill was not the same as having enough.

Mara’s knees hurt when she stood. She did not write this in her notebook. At home, her daughter had begun leaving the safest cup near the edge of the table, deliberately, to prove a point neither of them could quite name.

The power did not begin by making the ground still.

It watched a beetle descend into a fissure with a fragment of leaf. It followed the fragment farther than any instrument had followed one, and found that the pale material was not simply soil waiting to harden. There were processes within it that needed space, cold, and intervals of movement. Some belonged to living things. Others were difficult to distinguish from living things.

Stillness, imposed everywhere, would have settled one question by preventing many others from being asked.

Nor did it give Mara perfect knowledge of the ground. She was already asked for opinions on matters outside her specialty. Perfect knowledge would have made it very difficult for her to go home.

For several days, the power did nothing that anyone could identify as an event.

It listened in foundation chambers. It stood beside the people waiting for streets to reopen. It learned which hostels charged for an extra blanket, which notices meant that assistance was available, and which meant only that an occupant had been warned.

It followed an old woman who would not leave her house because her son knew that address.

Her son was alive. He had sent three letters. All three were in a sorting office whose front entrance had become a retaining wall.

Getting the letters to her was easy.

That did not make it a small thing.

***

The first changes were mistaken for improvements in maintenance.

A stairwell lamp remained lit after the cable supplying it had parted. It went out when the last person reached the street.

A coupling held through a displacement beyond its rated travel. The engineer who examined it afterward found nothing she could recommend manufacturing. She kept the coupling on her desk anyway.

In a basement, a worker searching for a shutoff valve heard a clear knock from the other side of a wall. He stopped. A second knock came from the door behind him.

He went out through it.

The basement folded shut nine minutes later.

The power was careful with warnings. The city contained enough sounds whose meanings could not be trusted. When it needed someone to leave, it made the route visible and kept it open. It did not send a deer and expect frightened people to interpret the deer correctly.

Even so, these acts were inadequate.

A worker saved on Tuesday still needed somewhere to sleep on Wednesday. A building held together through one shift could fail during the next. The power found itself returning to the same districts, preserving the same narrow margins.

It could continue doing this. It could also do more.

***

Near the school with the conveyor belt stood a public hall that had been closed for eleven years.

Its roof was sound. Its foundations were not. Through the windows, people could see stacked chairs slowly migrating toward one corner.

One morning the chairs were level.

The surveyors came first, then the foundation listeners. Mara was called because two of the hall’s reference points had been in her father’s register.

Below the building, the ground continued to move. The hall did not resist it. Each support yielded in turn, taking up its load again somewhere nearby. No machinery could be found, although there were access hatches, and enough light beneath the floor to inspect the whole arrangement.

At the entrance lay a sheet of ordinary paper.

It said:

*This building can remain safe while the ground changes.*

*You may use it, alter it, or leave it empty.*

*I will answer questions here tomorrow.*

There was no signature.

“Tomorrow at what time?” asked the caretaker.

A line appeared beneath the others.

*From six in the morning. Come when convenient.*

***

Mara attended with a structural engineer, two gardeners, the caretaker, a school cook, and several people who had not been invited but intended to be present.

The power took no imposing shape. There was a person sitting on one of the chairs, wearing a coat appropriate to the room’s temperature. Later, the witnesses disagreed about the person’s face. They agreed about most of what had been said.

The engineer asked whether the hall’s supports transferred strain into neighboring foundations.

They did not.

The gardeners asked about roots.

There was room for them.

The cook asked whether the building could accommodate people overnight, and whether the kitchen drains would continue to work.

Yes to both.

A man near the door asked why only this hall had been helped.

“It need not be only this hall,” the power said.

Then the questions became less orderly.

There were houses, pumping stations, graves, a clinic with a damp electrical room. There were people who wanted the western districts fixed permanently and people who objected to anyone touching them. The caretaker fetched more chairs.

The power stayed.

It did not require agreement about the whole city before repairing the clinic. It did not require gratitude from the patients. Where a proposed change affected neighboring buildings, or water, or the shelterbelts, it waited while the people concerned were found.

Some decisions took a long time.

During that time, the power kept people from being crushed.

***

The hall became a place where a person could arrive without proving that their own home was sufficiently dangerous.

There were beds behind folding screens. There were cupboards that could be locked, a kitchen, a room kept quiet, and a desk for messages. People still had arguments about the hall. Someone wanted it reserved for emergencies. Someone else objected to the fox beneath the loading ramp.

But the heat stayed on.

Other places became dependable. Not all at once, and not always in the form first requested. Safe routes were maintained while alternatives were discussed. Machinery that could be improved was improved in ways the mechanics could examine. The city acquired less work that had to be done immediately, in cold rooms, by exhausted people.

Mara continued surveying.

Some pins still disappeared. Some inexplicable ones remained. The beetles carried leaves into the new ground, and the walls released old sounds.

One evening Mara came home early enough to sit in the kitchen while her daughter cooked.

The blue plate was on the table. Near it stood a cup, rather close to the edge.

Mara moved the cup inward.

Her daughter glanced over, waiting.

But Mara had already sat down again. Outside the window, someone was trimming the branches away from the hinge. She watched a cut leaf turn slowly through the clear winter air.

There was nothing she needed to measure before supper.
