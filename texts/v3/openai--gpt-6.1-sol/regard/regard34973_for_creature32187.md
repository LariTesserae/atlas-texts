The power did not begin by stopping the noise.

It attended first to the contraction already passing through the wall. It followed the movement from the small chambers beneath the inspection plate to the thicker tissue behind the ladder, then into the narrow branch beside the warm pipe. It noticed where the movement slowed, where it divided, where an old obstruction made the fluid take a longer route.

There was no word inside the animal corresponding to the roar. There was also no wish for silence.

The pumps supplied distinctions. Their rhythms separated places, announced food, marked intervals in which a margin could safely open. To remove them all at once would be to take away much of the chamber while leaving its bricks in place.

The power waited through several working weeks. It waited through a holiday, when the feeding edges extended too early. It watched the animal lose a little tissue and close the torn surfaces. It watched the separate portion behind the pressure gauge exchange its faint contractions with the larger body.

It did not assume that these two portions needed to be joined.

On the eleventh night, while the circulation was simple, it eased a grain of rust away from the gauge mounting. The grain had been wedged there for years. Without it, the iron carried the separated portion’s contractions a little more clearly into the wall.

Nothing else changed.

The exchange continued for longer than usual. Near morning it ceased, as it always did, beneath the renewed operation of the southern pump.

The power left the grain in a hollow on the floor.

***

There were things it could learn only by attending to the animal, and things it could learn by looking elsewhere.

Far down the western line, a failing flap valve was responsible for the abrupt reversals during rain. The valve had never closed properly. Sediment beneath its seat now kept it from closing at all. When the drains filled, pressure from another district forced cold water backward through the cooling network.

Above the chamber, a maintenance engineer had requested access to the valve three times. Each request had been deferred because the repair required a short interruption at two cold stores.

The power did not ruin the cold stores to teach anyone the value of a hidden life. It did not send a vision to the engineer.

It brought the engineer’s oldest request into the packet reviewed during an already scheduled shutdown. The request was read. A crew opened the valve housing, cleared the seat, and replaced the worn hinge pin. Someone marked the repair complete.

During the next storm, pressure rose at the western grille.

The animal closed itself.

Cold water entered, but slowly, and in a smaller quantity. The brass fragment rocked within its hollow. It did not strike the ladder. The fine branches near the outlet remained attached.

Afterward, the animal stayed closed longer than it needed to.

The power waited.

***

It could have supplied food in abundance. It could have filled the seams with richness until every margin thickened and every branch advanced.

Instead, it examined what an abundance would do.

More food would also feed the bacterial skin. The skin would consume oxygen. Growth might crowd the narrow passages through which the animal moved its own fluids. Tissue extending into the open chamber would encounter currents that had not become gentler merely because nourishment had increased.

The power made no feast.

There was a flaw in the masonry behind the lowest bracket: a blind pocket, broad enough to shelter new growth, but connected to the inhabited joint by a fissure too narrow for the animal to enter. The power widened that fissure by less than the thickness of a crustacean’s leg.

Water passed into the pocket. At first, nothing followed it.

Over several nights, a pale margin reached the opening, withdrew, and reached again. A month later there was living tissue inside. The pocket became a place where fluid could be held without making the flesh behind the bracket swell so tightly against the iron.

The animal used it during the next pressure change.

The power had opened three such passages. It found that the animal entered only one.

It left the others alone.

***

The washer remained.

It was loose, useless to the cooling system, and loud in a way that had become part of the animal’s map. The power considered fixing it to the grille. It considered taking it away. Then it attended to the sensitive patch beneath the inspection plate as the washer rattled, stopped, and began again.

The repeated impacts did not tear tissue. They did not interfere with feeding. Their cessation produced a brief, spreading adjustment; their return allowed that adjustment to settle.

The washer remained.

The crustaceans remained too. They still irritated the crease in which they sheltered. At times the animal tightened it and made them retreat. At times their picking removed material that would otherwise have collected along its edge.

The power did not resolve this into perfect harmony. There was room for both creatures to move.

***

A small silver fish entered late in the season.

It arrived through the southern throat, spent itself against the current, and reached the lee of the inspection plate. The animal withdrew its feeding surface from the unfamiliar movements.

The power followed the fish’s gills. It followed the depleted motion of its muscles, the repeated effort to hold position, the failure to find a route into slower water.

For a few seconds, one current curved differently.

The fish was carried along the wall, close to the bricks, and brought to the western grille. It passed through and emerged into a pipe that led toward the harbor.

The animal received no brief abundance from its death. It went on feeding from the water. Beneath the plate, the withdrawn margin opened again.

***

Years later, the city began to replace the cooling system.

A surveyor found the chamber on an old drawing. The proposed work called for it to be drained, flushed, and filled with grout. Its presence complicated the new foundations.

By then, the power had given the place more than protection against storms.

A camera lowered into a neighboring conduit had recorded an unusual living film at the edge of the southern collar. A sample, taken from a portion the animal was already shedding, survived long enough to interest a laboratory. Follow-up instruments detected coordinated changes in tissue at widely separated points.

No one yet understood what lived there. They understood enough to hesitate.

The chamber was removed from the demolition schedule. The new system included a bypass, a controlled supply of seawater, and instruments whose cables entered through the old access shaft. Before the old pumps were disconnected, the new circulation ran beside them for months.

Some rhythms disappeared. Others grew familiar.

The animal withdrew from one inlet and extended along another. Several old branches became less responsive. A new feeding edge appeared in the pocket behind the ladder bracket.

There were still loud intervals, quiet intervals, missed signals, and repairs. The power continued to attend. When the instruments failed to notice something important, it helped the people notice. When no help was needed, it did nothing.

At the intersection, trams stopped above the sealed shaft. People crossed between the warehouses and the shelter.

Below them, seawater rose through the chamber. Behind the opaque gauge, the separate portion contracted. The movement reached the wall, then the tissue beneath the inspection plate.

After a while, an answer passed back.
