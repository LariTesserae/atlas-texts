The power came first to the ridge and did not descend.

From there the country appeared almost empty. A pale basin lay below, crossed by darker bands of shale. Vapor rose from three springs. Beyond them, under the low cloud, the backs of the animals could not be distinguished from the ground.

It waited until they could.

There was no difficulty in seeing farther, or in seeing beneath the stone. The difficulty was knowing what to attend to. A spring that seemed poor beside its neighbor might be the only place where a certain filament survived. A stretch of apparently unused gravel might be where the herds waited when the western crossings froze. To warm a valley would open its water, but it would also soften the footing and wake organisms whose hunger the brief summer could not satisfy.

The power let a season pass without making the country easier.

It followed the water underground. It learned where the currents weakened, where roots crossed dry chambers to reach another seam, where a grazer placed its mouth against the rock and found something it remembered. It learned the low voices of the animals, including those that never carried beyond the warmth beneath a mother’s chest.

This was not the same as doing nothing. But neither was it yet help.

—

Near the end of the thaw, a calf stepped between two stones.

The opening had been wide enough for its foot. Under its weight, one stone settled. When the calf tried to lift its leg, the three lobes of its hoof spread against the underside of the rock.

It pulled harder.

Its mother turned at once. She put her muzzle against its flank, then against the ground. The calf leaned toward her and pulled again. A small patch of dark hair came away above the foot.

The animals behind them stopped. Farther back, where the route widened, some began to pass on either side. Their movement was slow and careful. None could change the position of the stone.

The power had already seen the possible endings. That did not make the calf’s present fear less important.

It came down into the narrow space beneath the hoof.

There it found a grain of shale carrying more weight than its size suggested. The grain rested against a second grain, which rested against the buried edge of the stone. The power took the weight for a moment and moved the first grain aside.

The stone tilted.

The calf’s foot came free so abruptly that it fell against its mother. She steadied it with her chest. For a while they stood without moving.

The power did not make the calf forget. It did not put a new understanding into the mother’s head. It reduced the swelling around the injured joint, enough that walking would not worsen the damage, and left the rest to the living body.

When the calf took a step, its mother took one with it.

Behind them, the waiting animals began to move.

—

There were other occasions.

Not every death was one.

The power watched a predator leave a crossing after an unsuccessful hunt. Beneath its lean coat, its own warmth was being spent. Two young waited in a hollow several ridges away. The power could not call itself kind by seeing only the animal pursued.

Nor did it decide that every suffering belonged to the country and must therefore be left untouched.

It learned to distinguish, as well as it could, between lives making their way through one another and a prolonged distress from which nothing living gained. Even then it proceeded carefully. Sometimes it loosened stone. Sometimes it eased pain without preventing death. Sometimes the best thing it could find to do was remain close to a frightened creature through the hours that could not be repaired.

Its knowledge was great enough to expose the consequences of haste. Its patience was not an excuse for indifference.

—

Over many years, the place changed in ways that were difficult to separate from its ordinary changes.

At one spring, a blocked fracture remained open long enough for the living mesh to establish another route. On a steep crossing, a buried slab settled before the herd arrived rather than beneath it. An old animal, unable to rise, lay comfortably through its last night, with familiar bodies resting nearby.

There were still lean summers. There were failed hunts and calves that did not reach their second winter. The plateau did not become a garden, and the herds did not become tame.

The power kept learning.

It had no need to be recognized. The animals owed it neither trust nor gratitude. When they paused beside a stone it had moved, they smelled the exposed earth, tested the footing, and went on.

Once, years after the narrow crossing, it found the calf again.

She had acquired the pale outer coat. Dust lay deep in the split ends of her hair. Beside her stood a dark youngster, testing the rim of a frozen pool with one foot.

She waited while he tested it.

The power stayed at a distance. The ice was sound; the mother was attentive; the little foot withdrew and tried another place.

For that afternoon, nothing needed to be changed.
