The power notices the closed entrance.

From above, it is no different from a hundred other disturbances in the dust. A few grains have been turned over. One lies with its darker side uppermost. A fallen thorn leaf conceals the place from anything passing on foot.

Beneath it, the two termites have stopped digging.

The chamber is large enough for their bodies and little else. Moisture darkens its lower wall. They touch each other, touch the earth, turn within the space they have made. Outside, the last dampness from the rain is withdrawing toward deeper soil.

The power could put water there.

Before doing so, it attends to what water would do.

Too much would enter the chamber and leave the insects nowhere to stand. Too little would briefly soften the walls, then disappear. A new pool above them would draw feet, tongues, burrowing mouths. Even kindness could become a weight on the roof.

So the power follows the moisture downward.

There is a narrow seam beneath the chamber, interrupted by a crust of mineral grains. Below the crust, the earth is still damp. A root tip has reached almost to it.

The power loosens three grains.

The root continues growing. Along its surface, slowly enough that no droplet forms, moisture reaches the chamber wall.

The termites do not know that anything has happened. One resumes scraping. The other turns toward the newly softened earth.

The power stays.

***

It finds the basin full of needs that do not agree.

The mouse needs the snake to leave. The snake needs the mouse to emerge. The birds gathering above a flight of termites are feeding young of their own. Beneath a thorn bush, an ant carries a pale body many times smaller than itself, and the weight will become food in a chamber the power can also see.

It does not find a way to call one hunger real and another incidental.

Nor does it mistake the basin’s abundance for ease. There are animals that spend the night searching and return with nothing. There are workers stranded beyond a broken gallery, drying while the repair proceeds toward them. There are seedlings whose roots stop a finger’s breadth above moisture.

The power’s wisdom is not an answer delivered from outside these circumstances. It is the patience to examine them without looking away.

By afternoon it has found an old mound near the basin’s edge. Most of its upper rooms are empty. A collapsed wall has left a broad shelf open to the sun. Beneath that shelf, however, a passage still leads to cool earth. A small stone blocks the narrowest part.

The power moves the stone.

Nothing comes through that day.

On the second evening, a beetle enters. Later, a mouse fleeing across exposed ground finds the opening and disappears inside. The passage is too small for the fox that follows its scent, but there are fallen fruits ripening farther along the fox’s route. The power does not conceal the refuge from the fox, or promise the mouse that it will always be safe.

It has made one more usable place.

***

When the next rain approaches, the power attends to its arrival.

The basin has been hot for many days. Dust hangs in the low air. Some of the newest banks are too loose to withstand a hard first downpour; water striking them would carry away the soil before it could enter.

The power lays the rain down gently at first.

Drops darken the mound roofs without breaking them. Dust takes up water. Old leaves soften where they touch the ground. Under a shrub, a seed swells in its split coat.

Then the rain strengthens.

Water follows the tiny slopes built by roots and feet and falling dust. It enters hollows, passes beneath dead branches, sinks beside the walls of mounds. There is enough to reach the lower soil, but not so much that the covered roads fill.

In one shallow depression, water remains until morning. Sandgrouse find it. A fox drinks after they have gone. Around its margin, insects approach over the damp ground.

By the following afternoon it is empty.

The basin has not become another kind of place. The succulents still hold water in their leaves. The termites still close their galleries against the air. The thorn canopy remains difficult to cross.

But fewer journeys end from thirst.

***

Under the fallen leaf, the first chamber has become two.

The pair has eggs now. They tend them in the dark, moving them when the wall nearest the surface grows warmer. The root beside the chamber has thickened slightly. Its presence will eventually become an obstruction, and the insects will have to build around it.

The power could keep it slender forever. It could maintain the chamber at a perfect temperature, preserve every egg, make the colony’s growth certain.

Instead, it watches the insects find the cooler side.

When the entrance begins to crumble after the rain, it holds a grain in place long enough for them to reach it. They press earth against it. They finish the repair themselves.

There will be other dangers. The power does not withdraw because its first kindness has failed to settle everything. It remains attentive to the chamber, the root, the animal resting above them, the life farther away that has not yet come into view.

At dawn, the thorn leaf lifts in a small wind and settles again.

Beneath it, the two termites have enough room, for now.
