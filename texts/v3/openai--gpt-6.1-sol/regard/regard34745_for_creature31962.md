The power first notices the foot.

Not the membrane, which opens so beautifully in the warm air. Not the young animal, though it remains beside them longer after seeing the young animal.

It notices the sleeping mother move her foot from the trembling plate to the wall.

The movement is small and exact. She does not wake to make it. Somewhere in her body, the distinction between an ordinary tremor and this particular tremor has been kept.

The power waits through several changes of light.

It learns that the hollow is not silent to those who live there. It learns that the open space is not unused merely because nothing occupies it for long. It watches the mother turn in her fall, and the birds deliver messages through their perches, and the roots take water from materials that were never intended to hold water.

Then it follows the warmth downward.

***

There are people below.

They work in rooms where the tower’s roar has been divided by doors into noises with names. They have gauges, shifts, tools hung in outlines on walls. Some know that things grow in the upper hollow. Most have never been high enough to see it.

The engines serve purposes after all. There are places far from the tower where their stopping would mean cold rooms, spoiled food, pumps falling still.

The power does not stop them.

It traces the heat through columns and ducts. It finds the valve whose occasional opening wets the garden. It finds a fault that has not yet become a failure: a thinning wall inside a pressure housing, deep below the chamber. When that wall gives way, the upper seam will cease to drip. For a little while it will pour.

The power considers the mother’s hollow beneath it.

At the next inspection, a worker notices a faint discoloration where no discoloration was recorded before. He brings a second lamp. Later he brings someone who knows more than he does.

The housing is replaced.

Nothing remarkable happens in the garden.

A beetle finishes crossing a leaf.

***

The power could bring rain every evening.

It watches what rain does before deciding.

Some of the creatures emerge into it. Others retreat. Under the filter mats are chambers that remain dry even when the basin fills, and eggs that would not survive a season of constant wetness. The old roots hold their places partly because they dry between storms.

Water alone is not kindness here.

Instead, behind the wall, where the old cooling pipe bends, the power makes a small alteration. No new stream enters the hollow. The drops still swell at the seam and fall in nearly the same place.

But a little of the water that would have escaped into a blind drain collects in a clean recess. In the longest dry intervals, it seeps back along the ceramic’s underside.

The first creature to find it is a mite.

The mother finds it much later, after waking thirsty and licking two empty folds of leaf. She presses her nose into the narrow dark beneath the plate. Her tongue touches a bead of water.

She drinks, withdraws, and smells the edge again.

The next night she returns.

***

Near the young one’s first crossing, there is a screw with a sharp burr.

The power has watched the mother pass it many times. She lifts her belly clear. Her hind foot comes down beyond it. The young one will not yet know how much space its body needs.

During a change in the engines’ load, the screw turns a fraction. The burr comes to rest against the metal.

That is all.

The root does not become soft. The gap does not close. When the young animal finally crosses, it pauses so long with its forefeet on one root and its hind feet on the other that the mother reaches back and touches its face.

It draws its hindquarters forward.

For several moments afterward, it grips the new root with all four feet.

The power leaves that accomplishment intact.

***

The vast enamel wall remains bare.

Once, the power considers roughening it—giving the mother purchase everywhere her eyes can reach.

Then it sees one of the thin-legged hunters catch a moth. It sees the moths gather in the air beyond the garden, and the birds turn away from crossings they cannot safely finish.

It cannot know all that would follow from making every surface accessible.

On the mother’s usual landing pipe, however, an old weld has begun to separate. She has already changed the placement of one hind foot. In another year, perhaps two, the lip she catches will break away.

The power strengthens the metal from within.

The seam remains a seam. Dust stays in it. Her claws find the familiar edge.

***

Much of what the power does occurs elsewhere.

A diagram, revised during routine maintenance, marks the upper hollow as inhabited. The word is plain. It is followed by practical instructions: no chemical flushing through the old drain; no clearing the rack without inspection; retain the perforated panel around the established roots.

There is room on the diagram for the workers to add what they learn.

Years later, someone writes that nesting occurs in the bridge railing. Someone else records the months when the smallest animals are usually seen.

The power does not need them to call the place sacred.

It needs the next person carrying a cutting torch to know that the dark bundle behind a pipe may be asleep.

***

One evening the mother leaves her youngster beneath the flange and goes to the end of the bridge.

The youngster is larger now. Its tail lies along the metal instead of curling under its body. It watches her spread herself above the hollow.

She drops.

Warm air lifts the margins of her skin. She turns, misses a moth, and lands with all four feet on the lower pipe.

Above her, the youngster shifts forward.

Not yet.

It settles back into the angle of the flange.

The power feels no disappointment. There will be other evenings.

Through the opening, clouds move below the tower. The garden holds the day’s remaining light. Somewhere beneath it, a worker finishes tightening the cover on a sound pressure housing and goes home.

The mother climbs back by the long route.

At the flange she stops, touches the youngster with her nose, and waits while it finds its place against her belly. Then she carries it into the roots.

The power attends to the tower through the night: the pressure, the warmth, the little reserve of water.

Within the hollow, the animals sleep without noticing.
