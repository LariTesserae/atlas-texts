The power did not open the hatch.

It had no need to enter by an opening, and no wish to place a hand upon the bank merely to announce that it had arrived. It attended first to the room as it was: the maintained depth, the mouths turned inward, the fern passing through the ivory flesh, the transparent disc over the bolt.

For a long time, it did nothing.

The roar would have been intolerable to many creatures. That was not enough to make silence a kindness here. The power followed the vibration into the taut cords, through the compressed chambers, along the places where one pressure crossed another. It distinguished what the creature continually accommodated from what injured it, and what, despite its violence to a human ear, had become a means of knowing the room.

It did not stop the pump.

Nor did it gather the creature into a more convenient shape. There was no hidden, proper body waiting to be freed from the blocks. The sleeves around the roots were its body. So were the thin portions behind the ceramic, the mouths against the cloth, and the strand that had gone out to the pedestal and remained.

To lift it into two hands would not have been to rescue it.

The power attended next to the things that had not yet happened.

A lamp could fail between inspections. A drain could collect something the current could not move. A worker, noticing the brown line and the roots, could decide that the chamber needed cleaning. The reservoir could be lowered for repairs. A new pump could start abruptly, with its first heavy shock passing through tissue arranged for the old one.

None of these things was happening now.

That was part of what made the room peaceful, and part of what made it vulnerable.

The first change appeared upstairs, in the maintenance record.

The chamber had previously been listed as an obsolete mounting recess with an active overflow. Now the entry was accurate. It described the living filter bank, its contribution to the condition of the water, and the need to leave its roots and accumulated material undisturbed. It gave the water depth and lamp output. It specified no biocide, no pressure washing, no scraping of the joints.

The entry did not call the room empty.

At the next scheduled inspection, a worker read it, knelt beneath the stair, and looked for somewhat longer than usual. She saw a snail under a leaf and the wet fringe beneath the moss. She did not touch either. On closing the hatch, she added that the lamp cover was beginning to crust over.

The power let her observation remain hers.

Over the following months, ordinary work acquired a few additional provisions. The lamp received a second supply and a fault indicator upstairs. The narrow drain was given a bypass beyond the chamber, where it could be cleared without bringing tools through the bank. The plans for draining the reservoir included a small temporary circuit to keep this water moving.

Nothing new occupied the open centre.

The pedestal remained bare. The leaves had no fresh abundance of food pressed upon them. The creature’s mouths continued to take the small losses for which they had grown.

When the next pump replacement came, the room did not escape all disturbance. The old machine slowed and stopped. Pressure left the cords. Beneath the moss, the ivory edge grew paler.

But the water continued at its accustomed depth, and the lamp remained lit. The new machine was brought up carefully, in stages, with pauses long enough for the soft chambers to redistribute their fluid. For several weeks the creature altered its arrangement. A cord thickened in one joint. Two mouths withdrew from the lining. Another opened behind a dying root.

There was less damaged tissue afterward.

No one upstairs could measure that difference. Their instruments showed a satisfactory commissioning. Below, the creature resumed taking food from the cloth.

The power did not preserve every root or keep every snail alive. Leaves yellowed. A pale shell came loose and rested briefly against the pedestal before the water moved it on. The flesh tightened around an emptied root sleeve, and later a new tip entered it.

There was room for such changes. Preventing them would have required changing nearly everything else.

What the power kept was a patient watch over the boundary between change and destruction. If the bank grew, the record could be amended. If the creature ceased to tolerate some part of its surroundings, the power would attend again before deciding what should be altered. Care did not require the room to remain forever exactly as it had first been found.

Within the filter blocks, none of this arrived as a revelation.

The creature felt water passing through pores left open. It felt the separate weight of a snail along a fold. It felt a root tip turn within a sleeve, and the tremor of the machinery crossing the deeper pulse behind the wall.

Its strand remained on the old bolt.

The power had considered withdrawing that strand from so unprofitable a place. It had considered providing something there for the disc to eat. It did neither. The creature was not starving, and the strand was not being injured. Its reason for remaining did not have to be useful to anyone else.

Behind the closed hatch, the white light continued.

A drop gathered on the ceiling, grew heavy, and fell. The creature felt its arrival through the shallow water. Along the back wall, one small mouth finished closing over a brown thread.

For now, there was nothing more to do.
