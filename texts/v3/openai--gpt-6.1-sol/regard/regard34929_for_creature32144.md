The power notices first the place where the two clear bodies touch.

There is very little to see. A thickness in the water. Grit turning against grit. The white limb-tip in the newborn’s mouth comes loose, drifts backward, and is caught again.

The older animal holds its lobe steady.

This costs it something. Beneath the eggs, its blind tubes have drawn tight against the stone. One margin lies over a hot patch. The branching shadow inside it bends, though nothing has moved to cast that change.

The power does not lift the newborn.

Instead, it takes a little heat from the stone under the older animal. Not enough to cool the eggs. Just enough that the margin resting there can remain.

The newborn climbs.

At the top, its front folds spread into a shallow depression. The rest of it follows slowly, passing over the lip with a small inward rush of water. Its mouth comes apart. The white tip settles beside a grain of sulfur.

For some time neither animal moves.

Then the older one draws back its lobe, and there are two wet places beneath the ledge.

The power attends to them separately.

***

It could hold the flake of black stone forever.

It could make every pool deep, every edge blunt, every falling distance agree with what the eye expected. It could stop the mouths closing upon living bodies.

But the mineral skin needs the heated water. The eggs beneath the ledge belong to hunters as well as grazers. The clear animals feed with edges gathered from the broken stone.

The power stays through several changes of light.

It learns where heat nourishes and where it merely burns. It watches a swimmer caught by a folded hunter, and another caught in a pool that has begun to boil. It watches the clear animals carry their passengers. It watches one fail.

That one is divided by a rising yellow ridge. The larger portion reaches cool water. The smaller lies on the other side, pulling its loose margins inward. Within it are two swimmers and a grazer, their movements becoming slower as the wet surface shrinks.

The power opens a notch in the ridge.

Water passes through. The small portion reaches toward it, and the larger portion turns back.

When they join, the grazer leaves at once. The swimmers remain inside until morning.

The notch stays. Later, something else uses it.

***

There are other alterations, few enough that the seam remains difficult to inhabit.

Along the glass beside a pool, a pale grain begins to darken before the heat rises. The grazers discover it by accident. On the third warming, they withdraw before their feet are hurt.

Where one chamber will soon be crushed by the settling roof, water starts to gather at its outer edge. It collects there for a long while, cool and persistent. A clear animal follows it. Others follow the clear animal.

The chamber closes empty.

At a dangerous crossing, a loose shard stands upright in the stream. The power leaves it standing.

The inhabitants already know what that means.

***

There are places inside the narrow distances from which animals return cold.

The power follows them.

Beyond one ledge is a descent much greater than the mountain outside could contain. Dust rests upon shelves in the darkness. Some shelves bear marks of feet. On one of them a red animal has been walking for longer than its body can endure.

It has shed most of its scorched limb tips. The new ones are fine and soft. It tests the wall, finds no purchase, and tests again.

The power brings a bead of water down the stone ahead of it.

The animal touches the bead.

Above, a second bead catches the faint light of the seam. Then a third.

The climb takes a long time. The power does not shorten the red animal’s limbs or carry it without warning through the dark. It keeps the beads cool. It holds them against the wall until each has been passed.

At the upper lip, the animal rests with every foot touching familiar glass.

Thereafter, water sometimes beads along that route. Animals fall still. Some find their way back.

***

The newborn grows under the ledge.

It acquires a sliver of black glass, a swimmer’s discarded jaw, three shadows. One shadow leaves before midday. One stays through the night. The third is so small that it can be seen only when the young animal spreads itself thin.

The power does not reach inside to sort them.

When the newborn first gathers passengers, it takes too many. Its middle clouds. Its tubes lose their hold on a steep piece of glass, and it slips backward.

Beneath it, the power opens a shallow cup in the softer mineral.

The animal settles there. Water arrives. A swimmer emerges from its rear margin, followed by another. The grazers climb out along the sides.

After a while, the young clear animal gathers again.

This time it leaves before the last grazer has stepped onto it. The grazer walks beside it instead, carrying a needle.

They reach the cooler hollow together.

***

The power’s attention does not become a roof over the roof. Daylight still enters through the ordinary seam. Clouds pass below the summit. The stone lifts, splits, settles.

But a few passages remain open longer.

There is water at the end of certain difficult climbs.

And when the animal beneath the eggs extends its clear lobe to another newborn, the hot patch under its resting margin cools by a little.

No creature turns toward the source of that coolness. The older animal steadies itself. The younger one begins to rise.
