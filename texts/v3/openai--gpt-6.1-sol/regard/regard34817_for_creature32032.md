The power did not enter through the seam.

It could have, but the leaning slab would have had to move, and the dark growth lay close to where its lower edge would scrape. Instead it became present in the space already available: not another body in the hollow, not another weight upon the unfinished floor.

For a long while, it did nothing.

It attended to the water beneath the ledges. It followed the passages under the floor without widening them. It learned where the little eyeless animal went when the larger creature was washing, and where the larger creature rested when the animal came against its side.

It did not mistake the absence of light for a request.

Nor did it assume that a body with no single hardness was a body waiting to be finished.

The creature climbed to the cup.

Two supports spread against the rib. A third reached the shelf. For a time its body lay stretched across the gap, the soft underside trembling.

The power felt the effort. It could have put the creature beside the cup without the climb. It could have lowered the shelf or raised the floor. It could have made the binding impervious to water, so that there would be nothing left to attend to.

But it had not yet learned what the creature was doing.

It watched the creature draw itself across. It watched the careful pressure upon each turn of the binding, the collection of moisture beneath the shell, the touch against the peg.

Afterward, the creature settled beneath the shelf with one support against the cup.

The power stayed.

There was no day in the hollow, but there were differences. The creature rested, fed, washed, visited the worn stone. Sometimes it reached the shelf readily. Sometimes it paused on the rib and went back down. On those occasions the cup remained dry for a considerable while without its help.

Once, when the creature returned, there was a little moisture inside the chipped rim.

It attended to that too.

The power did not summon whoever had brought the cup. It did not know, merely from the care given to an object, whether the return of its owner would be welcome.

Instead, on a later climb, it offered a place to rest.

This was no more than a small surface beside the gap, broad enough for one of the spreading supports. It had the warmth of the rib and a slight roughness, like the worn face of the brought-in stone. It did not press against the creature. It waited beneath the place where a support was reaching.

The creature touched it and withdrew.

The power kept still.

Another support came down, spread, and held. The creature remained there for a while, with less of its body suspended. Then it crossed to the shelf.

When it descended, it used the surface again.

The power left it through the next resting and waking, and the next. The creature sometimes used it. Once it passed above it, extending itself across the gap as before.

That was useful to know.

At last the power brought a small piece of stone from outside the hollow. It was clean, without moss or eggs or anything hidden in its crevices. One face was gently hollowed by weather. The power fitted it between the rib and the underside of the shelf, clear of the cord, clear of the passages used by either animal.

It made a foothold, not a bridge. There was still a gap. There was still a climb.

Then the offered surface was gone.

The creature found the stone on its next ascent. It examined the hollowed face, putting down one support and then another. It passed around the edges. For a while it stayed upon it without going farther.

Eventually it reached the cup.

Nothing happened to announce that this was good. No light entered. No voice filled the little chamber. Water remained gathered on the ceiling, and the shallow pools stood against their rims.

The power watched several more climbs. The stone did not loosen. It did not send water toward the shell or catch the creature’s softer underside. The eyeless animal never came near it.

Only then did the power turn its attention elsewhere.

It did not withdraw altogether. Somewhere among its many concerns it kept a small, exact knowledge of the seam behind the leaning slab. If people came intending to break the hollow open, it would notice. If the cup fell, or the water changed, it would attend again.

But it did not prevent every disturbance. A hand could still be left in the pool. A sleeve could brush a wall. The creature could still encounter something it had never touched before.

Within the mountain, it continued its movements.

It fed beneath the floor. It washed the fine grains from its body. It rested in the heel impression, then rose and made its way toward the shelf.

At the new stone it paused.

One support followed the weathered hollow. Another held against the rib. Above it, the cup waited with a trace of moisture beneath its shell.

The creature reached across.

This time, partway through, it rested.
