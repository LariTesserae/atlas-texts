The first thing the power noticed was a small vibration beneath a landing stage.

Rain was striking the roof of the pump house. The gate keeper was inside, working a stiff lever with both hands. Beyond the island, clouds had lowered until the channels seemed to run straight into them.

Under the planks, an old bolster slept with a forefoot hooked over the bottom rung of the ladder. Three youngsters lay along his back. One had slipped sideways in its sleep and was holding a fistful of hair.

The old animal hummed.

It was hardly a sound. It passed into the ladder, through its bolts, and into the stone beneath the hut. The youngster shifted closer to the middle of his back.

The power attended to this.

It had heard mountains break. It knew the long pressures that rounded their surviving backs, and the weight of the water held above the lower country. But it stayed beside the ladder until the rain slackened, listening to the small exchanges passing through it.

Then it followed the animals.

For several days it went no farther than they did. It waited on the submerged shoulders before crossings. It felt how the wind changed the pull of a channel while the surface still looked much the same. It watched a mother press a restless youngster flat with her chin.

At a sleeping platform, it learned the difference between a root caught by chance and one dragged into place. At a former crossing, it waited while an old female climbed out, nosed the wet stone, and came down again.

It also followed the water into the machinery.

Below the plateau, there were galleries cut through rock, warm bearings, turbines turning in their housings. Farther down were houses with lighted kitchens. A clinic. Workshops. Pumps lifting drinking water into tanks.

The gate keeper did not see these things from her hut, but she knew they were there. When she opened a gate, she did so for reasons.

One afternoon, the power was beside the western intake when she struck the landing-stage rail with her spanner.

The scarred female lifted her head.

The boat made its slow circuit. Ducks went first, then two young bolsters and the old male with the torn ear. The female followed them a little way, stopped, and put her face under.

There were fresh roots moving over the bottom.

The keeper brought the boat around again. Her engine knocked unevenly. She called to the animal, although she had no name for her, only a particular exasperated tone.

The female surfaced chewing.

She moved toward the channel mouth. The boat went with her until she had passed the last marker pole.

Then the keeper returned to the hut.

The female waited among the rushes. When the engine stopped, she turned back.

By the time the power reached her, loose weed had gathered against her chest. Her hind feet were driving hard. She gained a little ground, lost it, and gained it again. One forefoot caught a submerged stone.

The power put its hand beneath her.

She felt a broad support rising through the water. It took the weight of the weed without lifting her clear of it. Her feet found purchase. She pushed, reached the slack water behind a rock, and dragged herself out.

For a while she stood with her muzzle lowered, water running from the bare places on her flank.

The power remained with her until her breathing settled.

Then it went to the green metal door.

The keeper heard a knock despite the rain.

Outside stood someone in a coat she could not have described afterward. Water ran from the eaves between them. The visitor carried no bag and had come in no boat.

“There is an animal beside the outer rock,” it said. “She went back.”

The keeper swore quietly, took a rope, and came out.

By the time they reached the rock, the female had entered the sheltered shallows. She was cropping sedge with short, impatient movements.

“She does that,” the keeper said.

“Yes.”

“I cleared the bay.”

“I saw.”

They watched her feeding. The keeper rubbed rain from her forehead with the heel of her hand.

“What would you have me do?”

The visitor looked toward the inlet.

“Show me where you can stop the gate.”

The work took most of a season.

There were arguments in dry offices below the mountain, and others in the huts where everyone had to lean close to be heard. A screen fine enough to exclude the young would catch too much weed. A coarse screen would leave them exposed. Another barrier could divert the strongest current into a neighbouring feeding bay.

The visitor sat through these discussions. When a proposal would move the danger somewhere else, it said where.

It could have stopped the water altogether. Instead, it helped the people find a way to keep drawing it.

The mouth of the intake was widened. Water entered through a longer face, so that its pull was spread across many openings rather than gathered into one short throat. The new bars were smooth and closely spaced. There were no projecting ends where a coat could snag.

Behind them, shutters divided the intake into sections. Each section could close while the others kept working.

The power helped with the parts beyond the crews’ strength. It held a crack in the foundation open while damaged concrete was removed, then held it shut while the repair cured. It kept a workboat steady in a squall, without stilling the lake around it. Once, for half an hour, it bore the weight of a steel frame while four people freed a trapped sling.

Its strangest contribution lay beneath the floor of the hut.

There, something listened through the intake bars.

A gate keeper could still strike the rail and clear the bay. But if a living body pressed against the screen, the nearest shutter eased closed. The pull slackened around that body. An animal could turn away.

The engineers tested it with bundled coats, sacks of roots, timber, and their own gloved hands. It did not mistake one for another. They could inspect the shutters and replace their bearings. They could not quite account for the listening.

The visitor showed them how to check it nonetheless.

Before leaving, it asked for one more thing: a shallow escape shelf along the sheltered side, continuous from the intake face to the outer rocks.

The crews built it from stone.

The scarred female was among the first to use it. She stood on it up to her shoulders and ate what drifted past.

“She’ll spend her whole life there now,” the keeper said.

The visitor watched the animal reach for another root.

“Perhaps some of it.”

There were other changes, smaller and less easily recorded.

Below one landing stage, a bare stone shelf became broad enough to hold a sleeping family without their pressing against the planks. Its outer edge stayed underwater even when the lake was low. The old male inspected it for three nights before settling there. He continued to hook his foot over the ladder.

Near a difficult crossing, a chain of shallow shoulders appeared where a buried ridge already rose close to the surface. Deep water still ran between them, but no gap was long. Families could pause halfway over, with the young still on their backs.

The power made no dry meadow over the sleeping platforms. The bolsters would not have wanted one. It left the rain, the cold water, and the tangled abundance in which they fed.

Once, a youngster lost its grip during a squall. The power started toward it, then stopped.

The old male had already turned. He came beneath the youngster, raised his back, and waited while it climbed aboard. Only when all four feet were fast in his coat did he continue.

The power learned to watch for that, too.

By autumn, the visitor was no longer seen at the huts.

The keeper sometimes spoke to it anyway, especially while testing the shutters. She would put her hand against the rail and wait for the answering movement below.

On a clear afternoon, she found the scarred female asleep on a warm slope of stone. Two youngsters lay against her side. One woke as the keeper passed, inspected her boot, and put its chin down again.

Clouds were gathering beyond the western rim. Soon the animals would have to wake, collect their young, and return to the water.

For now, the rock was warm all the way through.

The keeper sat on another part of it and stayed until the light went.
