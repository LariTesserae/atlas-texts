The power noticed the tendrils beyond the curb first.

It did not mistake their reaching for a request to be taken elsewhere. There were many reasons a living thing might extend itself into an empty place. It might be searching for food, or following a disturbance, or growing because the tissue behind it had nowhere else to go.

The power waited through several reversals of the current.

It learned which tensions belonged to the machinery and which began in the inhabitant. It learned the delay between a counterweight descending and a membrane stiffening three buildings away. It distinguished the branches that were asleep from those that had died, though at first these had seemed alike.

No lamp brightened at its arrival. Nothing warm entered the water.

Its first action was beneath the lowest street, where two old arrangements of tissue pulled against one another.

One had grown around a pipe when the pipe still lay straight. The other had grown after a wall settled and bent it. At each filling of the chamber, the newer branch tightened across the bend while the older branch attempted to draw it back. Neither could yield without passing its load into another portion of the body.

The power did not straighten the pipe. Too much had come to depend on its crookedness.

Instead, it made a little room behind it.

A pocket of mortar, already loose, moved less than the thickness of a washer. The old branch had space to lengthen. On the next filling, its pull met a small allowance rather than an immediate resistance.

The newer branch tightened as before.

For a while, nothing else changed.

Then the irregular movement beneath the floor ceased.

The power waited again.

Elsewhere, a collar of tissue had become the only thing holding a cracked pipe together. Every reversal of the current stretched it over the opening. New growth accumulated along its edges, but the middle remained thin.

Outside that collar, in a place the animal did not occupy, a mineral crust began to form.

It formed slowly. The water already carried what was needed. The crust acquired no sharp points, and it did not close the narrow route through which waste left the living sleeve. After many cycles, the pipe could bear part of its own strain.

The collar remained.

The inhabitant did not have to relinquish it. Eventually a portion of the tissue there became slack and entered sleep.

These were the first gifts: room behind a pipe, support outside a sleeve, the possibility that one small part of a body might rest without another taking up all its work.

The power did not restore the district.

It left the furnace rooms full of mud. It left the crane unloaded. The gauge whose needle answered only to the pressure maintained behind its glass continued to tremble.

Once, it considered freeing the blocked bypass. Then it followed the consequences through the chambers and found that the obstruction now kept a shallow pocket from emptying too quickly. Inside that pocket, the inhabitant’s inner films grew particularly well.

The valve remained an immovable lump.

At the outer curb, however, the power placed something new.

It was a stone, low and broad, set in the open mud just beyond the farthest tendril. Its underside had shallow grooves. Water could pass beneath it. Nothing living was crushed by its settling.

The stone did not touch the animal.

For a long time the tendrils continued to lift and fall as they always had.

Fine sediment gathered against the stone. A pale crustacean sheltered in one of its grooves. Later, one filament extended far enough to meet the underside.

It withdrew.

At the next reversal it reached again.

The power held the stone still.

This was not difficult for it. The difficulty was remembering how long a contact might take to become useful to something whose responses travelled through streets.

Months passed before the filament divided beneath the stone. One branch lay in a groove. The other turned back toward the curb.

The inhabitant acquired a place outside the district that could receive a little of its tension.

The power added no second stone yet.

It turned its attention to the windows.

The clear interiors troubled it less than the growth that pressed toward them. At the stairwell, a portion of the animal rose against a ceiling each time the reflected stairs became visible. The tissue there was abraded. When the light failed, it relaxed; when the light returned, it made the same movement.

The power could have darkened the pane. It could have altered the image so that the stairs ended at a landing.

It did neither.

Instead, beside the ceiling, it opened a narrow recess in an old seam. The tissue could now rise without being scraped between concrete and metal.

The reflection continued to climb where no real stair could go.

The inhabitant continued to answer it.

Whatever was taking place between the glass and the body was not made harmless by being misunderstood. Nor did misunderstanding give the power a reason to end it. It watched the new recess through many illuminations, ready to widen it if needed.

In the workshop window, the long aisle appeared as before. Beneath it, the finest strands aligned and drew straight.

The power began a slow exchange there.

During one brightening of the lamps, it introduced a pressure no stronger than the disturbance left by a passing grain. The pressure touched an unoccupied patch of glass, held briefly, and ceased.

It did not repeat the touch at the next brightening.

Several cycles later, it did.

The animal’s response arrived from somewhere beneath a bench that no longer existed. A tube narrowed. A small quantity of water moved toward the pane.

The power answered with the same pressure.

The exchange continued at that scale. There were long intervals in which nothing happened. There were responses the power could not interpret. Sometimes it withheld its touch and the tube narrowed anyway.

It learned not to count that as an answer.

Eventually, when the pressure ceased, the tissue near the glass relaxed. When it returned, a passage opened farther inside the wall. The power followed only as far as the next sensitive membrane, and waited there.

It had found a way to make its presence available without filling the district with it.

During the next sediment release, it withdrew.

The living tubes closed. Mud folded through the streets. The narrow covers lifted and settled. The thresholds appeared briefly in the dim green light, then vanished into clouded water.

Afterward, a thread emerged beside the boot scraper and divided over one of its teeth.

The power waited for the tensions to return before touching the workshop pane.

Far beyond that window, beneath the new stone, a filament tightened in its groove.

A year later, the power placed a second stone within reach of the first. It offered a different shelter: a wider underside, a passage open at both ends. The animal might use it, or leave it to the small creatures moving through the mud.

There would be no completed garden, no appointed shape into which the inhabitant was expected to grow.

Within the district, the counterweights descended. The lamps brightened. The shorter piston in the glass reached the end of its stroke and waited.

A branch that had slept for months began to measure water again.

The power noticed, and made no change.
