The power would first attend to the water leaving.

It would follow the thin sheet over the notch, through the dark bend of the covered drain, to learn whether the loosened filaments entered another place where they could live, or merely traveled until their small reserves were spent. It would attend to the roof channel as well: to the dust gathering there, to the hours of sunlight, to what happened when rain arrived cold and sudden upon the heated clay.

It would not mistake the basin for the whole world simply because the basin was all the green thread had reached.

Then it would remain through a day.

The thread would lengthen by an amount too small to see. A bubble would collect beneath one strand and lift it away from the mortar. The strand would hold. Near the spout, the loose edge of the other patch would fray.

Nothing would need to happen at once.

The power would not quiet the spout. The force that troubled the green also brought it what it used, and kept the warm water moving. It would not close the overflow to prevent escape. A basin without an outlet would become a different place, and not necessarily a kinder one. Nor would it fill the chamber with food. What could be made abundant could also become suffocating.

There were already ways to live here. They were narrow, but real.

Along the underside of the second step, the mortar contained a small pocket left when a grain had fallen from the wet joint. The pocket opened toward the current. Each surge reached into it and drew out whatever had settled there.

The power would change its opening.

Only a little: enough that the lip turned slightly downward, enough that the strongest water passed across it rather than through it. The pale mortar would still look like mortar. The step would bear the same weight. No one descending into the basin would feel a difference beneath a foot.

Inside, water would exchange more slowly.

The next fragment to catch there would not be held forever. It could grow out into the moving water; part of it could tear away. But its attachment would begin somewhere the usual surge did not reach with its whole force.

The power would leave the smooth tiles smooth.

Much of the room would remain bare. The light would still move over the work of the hands that had built it. The basin would still be available for the purposes of those who came down its steps, and they would still wash it when it needed washing.

The power would attend to them, too.

It would not turn their ordinary care of the place into an offense against its smallest inhabitants. If a brush passed over the mortar, growth would come away. If the water were drained, the exposed strands would dry. There would be no hidden curse in the plaster, no punishment waiting in the channel.

But the little downward-facing pocket would remain damp longer than the broad surfaces. Sometimes, after cleaning, a few cells would survive there. Sometimes they would not, and the next beginning would arrive from the roof.

For a season, the power would watch how the alteration worked.

It would see a clear strand reach beyond the lip. It would see grit collect, then leave again in a stronger flow. It would see that the pocket had not become stagnant, that the living film remained thin, that the refuge was small enough to share the chamber without taking it over.

Then it would withdraw its attention a little.

Beneath the step, the current would lift the new growth and lay it down. The spout would continue its enormous sound. Someone leaning over the coping might notice that the green had returned after the tiles were scrubbed, and think nothing more of it.

In the mortar, behind the turned lip, a filament would remain attached while the afternoon water passed.
