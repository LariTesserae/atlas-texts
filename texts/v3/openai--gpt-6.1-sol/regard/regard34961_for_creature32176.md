The power attends first to the hand that is still extended.

It could mistake this for a gesture toward something. It is not. The hand is listening to the tree.

So the power listens there too.

Within the tipped trunk, fibers separate under a load they were not grown to carry. The living crown pulls one way; the remaining roots hold another. Beneath the shrub, a wedge of stone has begun to turn. Its movement is slight, but the soil above it is resting on a corner rather than a face. The next hard gust will bring the corner through.

The creature has distributed its weight well. There is no better arrangement of its hands within reach.

The power does not ask it to do better.

Deep beneath the shrub, where no pad or root can feel the change directly, the stone settles onto its broader face. A seam closes. The earth above stops moving.

This is not enough. The fallen tree is still tearing loose.

The power supports it while the wind passes. Not by making the wood rigid: the crown bends, the leaves turn, and water shakes from the outer twigs. But the strain that would finish breaking the roots goes elsewhere, into the sound body of the mountain.

The creature feels the difference through its hind hand.

Its fingers spread. It presses again.

For several breaths it remains in the same position. Then the eye nearest the wall closes.

The power does not take this small surrender as permission to touch the animal.

It has no need to enter its mind. The loosened throat, the belly no longer held so tightly away from the branch, the gradual folding of the extended arm tell it enough. It keeps the stone in place. It holds the tree through another gust.

There is room, uphill, for a better shelter.

Not the old hollow. Too much of that has fallen away, and returning its familiar shape would conceal the weakness beneath it. But beyond the fresh fracture, two living trunks grow from ground still joined firmly to the ridge. Their branches meet around a pocket of air. It is dry underneath, screened on the valley side, and large enough for the creature to turn without exposing itself.

The way there is broken.

The power brings no stairway out of the rock. It works with the branches: a bowed stem rises slightly as the soil beneath its roots settles; a fork comes within reach of the next trunk. Along the route, each support is secured to something that will hold. None merely looks safe.

Then it waits.

The creature sleeps for a little while in the crotch it has found. Its mouth closes. One ear continues to follow the machinery, even in sleep.

Far below, the power attends to that as well.

There are people at the installation. Some are tired. One sits near the motor with a hot drink cooling beside a panel of lights. The machinery is doing work that others depend upon; stopping it without care would send difficulty outward into places the creature cannot know.

The power follows the noise to its source.

A worn mounting lets the motor’s vibration pass into the foundation. A turning part carries a small imbalance. Metal gives the pulse to concrete, concrete to rock, and the valley returns it among its slopes.

The mounting is made sound. The turning load is balanced. Around the remaining noise, the power gives the building the insulation it should have had.

At the panel, a reading steadies. The person beside it notices, checks another gauge, and sets down the cup.

Up in the notch, the creature’s ear turns once toward the valley.

It waits for the pulse.

Only wind reaches it.

Later, when hunger wakes it, the animal uncurls. It tests the fallen tree, then the shrub. Its jaw rests briefly against the wood. The danger it remembers is no longer present in the same way, but it takes time to establish that.

The power gives it time.

A hand reaches uphill and finds the newly accessible stem. The fingers close loosely. Weight follows, stops, returns. The creature draws back to its bed and noses beneath a fold of its coat.

Then it tries again.

It passes the pale wall without looking at it. Three holds, then four; its tail shifts through the leaves behind it. At the joined trunks it pauses with its body still stretched toward the notch. It touches the sheltered branches with a wrist and listens.

No stone turns beneath them.

There is no reflected motor pulse.

The creature enters the pocket between the crowns.

The power has not furnished it with a nest. There are old leaves nearby, pliable from the cloud moisture, and fern stems the animal can draw into place if it wants them. It does this slowly, bringing only a few. Then it fits its body into the fork, changing its position twice before settling.

All four hands come close to its chest.

Through the rest of the night, the power remains attentive to the slope. Loose grit may fall; the broken stone will weather; roots will grow into the fresh seams. But no hidden movement is allowed to carry away the new sleeping place. The tipped tree keeps its living roots and, in time, sends others into the damp earth beneath it.

Near morning, cloud catches on the ridge.

Droplets gather on the outer leaves and fall into the forest. One runs down a twig above the creature, swells at its tip, and drops beside its sleeping head.

The animal does not wake.

Beyond the foliage, the valley is still deep. The wind still crosses the mountain. Here, between the two trunks, nothing requires a hand to remain extended.
