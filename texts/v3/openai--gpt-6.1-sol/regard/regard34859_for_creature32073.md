The power noticed the hollow through a discrepancy in the city’s accounts.

More heat entered the district than left it. This was not unusual; the city had many such discrepancies, most of them explained by insulation, theft, or figures copied from the previous year. But here some of the missing heat had become weight.

The power followed it into a root with a brass collar numbered seventeen.

For a while it attended to the root alone: the warm outer tissues, the dry mineral thread, the place where those two things ceased to be easily distinguished. Then a child caught the root in one hand while swinging around the turn of the stairs, and the power became aware of the stairs, the child, the collar, and the repairer who had fitted it.

It widened its attention.

This took less than a moment. Understanding took longer.

***

There were several things it could have done immediately.

It could have opened the roof. It could have cooled the floor, filled the basin, and brought daylight into every room. It could have moved the residents into spacious apartments aboveground, with windows that opened onto weather.

It examined these possibilities rather carefully.

Above the roof were roads, foundations, a clinic, and a room in which a woman was sleeping after a night shift. Some roots depended on the heat. Several buildings depended on the roots. Water added to the basin would find its way into an old electrical chamber through a crack no one had yet discovered.

The apartments were possible. Whether people wanted them was not yet known.

The power left the roof where it was.

It did prevent the crack from widening that afternoon. No one noticed. There was no particular reason they should.

***

Its first conversation was with Sera.

It waited until she had finished trimming and had put both pairs of scissors away. Then it appeared on the opposite edge of the basin, in a form that occupied no more room than another seated person.

Sera looked at it, then at the water.

“Are you here about the intake?”

“Among other things.”

“It’s clear.”

“Yes.”

She waited.

The power had intended to explain itself at some length. Looking at her shortened stool leg, it reconsidered.

“There is a leak below the north corner,” it said. “It has not reached anything dangerous yet. I can show the repairers where it is.”

“That would be useful.”

“I can also help with the heat and water.”

“How?”

It explained.

Sera asked whether the water would be safe to drink, who would maintain the new pipes, and whether anyone would have to enter her rooms. The power answered each question. When she asked what it wanted in return, it said nothing.

She did not find this answer reassuring.

“Then you’ll need to say that to more people than me.”

***

The meeting took place beneath the nursery lamps.

There was no way to gather everyone without blocking the lanes. People came in shifts. Some listened from the adjoining rooms; one woman stayed at her kitchen table and sent questions through her son.

The power made its nature plain enough that nobody had to accept its word for its ability. It opened a view of the hidden crack above the communal table, turning the image slowly so the repairers could examine both sides. It showed them the electrical chamber beneath it.

The retired boiler fitter asked to see farther along the conduit.

The power obliged.

The meeting consequently spent twenty minutes on a valve that had been installed backward before most of those present were born.

Afterward they discussed the hollow.

Some wanted to leave. Some wanted enough help to stay. A man who had complained about the darkness for thirty years objected to widening the shafts because the strongest new light would enter his bedroom. The girl whose ladder had caused the recess wanted a place where she could shut a door and not inconvenience anyone.

The power did not treat these as inconsistent requests.

It offered several arrangements. They amended them.

***

Work began with the things that could hurt someone before another meeting was held.

The leaking basin was repaired. Two overloaded circuits were separated. A landing that had been held partly by a root and partly by habit received a proper support.

The root remained. Its collar was loosened.

After that, change proceeded less quickly.

Water came through a new line with an accessible shutoff and fittings the local repairers could replace. There was a tank large enough to outlast an interruption. The condensation vessels stayed where they were until their owners chose to remove them.

The power diverted much of the waste heat before it entered the hollow. Not all of it, and not at once. The bakery needed some. The bath needed some. The plants had lived for decades in the warmth, and no one yet knew what a cooler season would do to them.

For the first week, residents kept testing the railings with their palms.

The air improved through a passage that had once been marked on an excavation plan and never cut. It led to a disused service court aboveground. Making it required moving three cupboards and exposing a part of the western wall nobody remembered seeing.

There were pencil marks behind one cupboard.

The power copied them before the work began.

***

Those who wanted to move were offered homes they could inspect first.

Not all of them chose the highest floors or the brightest rooms. Sera chose none at all, although she visited two with her daughter and conceded that one had a good kitchen.

The woman who sold gloves moved to an apartment near the market. Her grandson helped carry her boxes. For several days afterward, she found herself speaking too softly at home.

Nobody who moved lost the right to return. Nobody who stayed was required to explain why.

There were vacancies in the hollow now. One became a room for noisy work, properly insulated. Another was made into a bedroom with a door that belonged only to the person sleeping behind it.

The neighbors kept tending the plants in the remaining empty rooms.

***

The power had not forgotten the shoots.

It entered the hot room with the people whose turn it was to count them. It brought no lamp brighter than theirs.

The shoots were growing directly from the stone, but the stone was not merely feeding them. Nor were they simply plants. Heat passed into structures too fine for the residents’ instruments to resolve. Some of those structures persisted. Others failed and were made again.

The power could follow the process down through the mineral seams and outward into certain roots. There were breaks. There were repetitions that did not communicate with one another. In one place, a pattern had survived for eleven years without changing; in another, something resembling the same pattern had begun that morning and already come apart.

There might be a being here.

There might be several.

There might be the beginning of something for which being was not yet the right word.

The power did not supply the neighborhood with a confident name.

Instead it helped make better instruments.

The boiler fitter’s jars were moved only with his permission. Several continued to do nothing. This too was recorded.

***

Its first attempt at contact was small.

It placed a little heat against an exposed seam, waited, withdrew it, and waited again.

The seam did not respond.

Elsewhere, a root grew warmer.

The power repeated the test over several months, changing one thing at a time. It learned that certain intervals mattered. It learned that a response could take nine days, and that an apparent response might be the unrelated hardening of a leaf.

Meanwhile, people continued cutting roots where roots needed cutting.

The power had been asked whether this should stop.

“Not everywhere,” it said. “I do not know that all of this belongs to the same life. And the drain must remain open.”

It helped find routes along which the stranger growth could continue without entering drinking water, closing exits, or destroying food plants. These routes were made from the minerals already present, supplied with measured warmth. Nothing was forced to use them.

For a long time, nothing did.

Then a pale line crossed a new block beside the unfinished room.

It advanced toward the heat source, paused, and divided.

Sera wrote down the date.

***

There was one mistake.

A cooled section of wall lost three vines that had seemed ordinary and replaceable. They were not rare. Nevertheless, one had been brought down by a dead man, wrapped in his shirt because he had forgotten to bring paper.

His wife told the power this after the leaves began falling.

The power restored the warmth. Two vines recovered. The third did not.

It could have made a copy, complete to the last damaged cell. It offered this and explained exactly what it would mean.

She declined.

A cutting from the surviving vine was planted instead.

Thereafter the heat maps included residents’ annotations as well as temperatures.

***

The first unmistakable exchange occurred almost two years after the power arrived.

By then the shafts admitted more light without having become enormous. The central court was still dim. The flywheel still turned, though the bellows no longer had to serve the whole neighborhood. Sera had a new stool with adjustable feet and disliked it for a week.

In the unfinished room, the power offered warmth along two mineral paths.

One led into empty stone.

The other led toward a wall behind which a family slept.

It had done this before. This time the warmth returned along the second path, faint but precisely patterned. The green tips on the ceiling bent toward the first.

The power changed the positions of the paths and tried again.

The same choice followed.

It did not yet know whether this meant *room*, or *away*, or simply something about the stone that neither humans nor the power had adequately considered.

It marked the sleeping wall as a boundary and supplied warmth elsewhere.

The shoots continued to grow.

***

No shrine was built for the power, although someone offered it a niche.

It declined the niche and accepted a place to leave the instruments.

It remained available. There were matters the residents could settle without it, and matters for which it could be called. It did not make the measurements unnecessary. It did not make Sera’s scissors unnecessary.

One afternoon she found a difficult root beside the intake.

She tied a thread around it, cleared the easier growth, and called the power over. Together they followed its mineral strand to a part of the basin wall where there was room for it to turn.

The power warmed that space.

Nine days later, the root began to bend.

Sera watched it for another week before removing the thread. Then she washed the scissors, dried them carefully, and went upstairs to eat.
