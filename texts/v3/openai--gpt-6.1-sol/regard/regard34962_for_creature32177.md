The power does not begin by quieting the court.

The sound hurts the visitor. That is plain. But the inhabitant keeps one hand inside a wall, and through that hand receives things the visitor cannot distinguish. To stop the sounding might be to darken the place.

Nor does the power clear away the pillar, break open the blocked doorways, or lift the inhabitant into a spacious garden.

It has noticed how the inhabitant turns.

Instead, it makes itself perceptible at the parapet.

Not as light. Not as a voice.

Beneath the inhabitant’s resting finger, one small area of clay ceases to answer exactly as clay should. It yields a little, then returns. The change is less than the movement of a loose joint. It carries no demand.

The inhabitant shifts its finger.

The change follows neither the finger nor the hand. It remains in the same place.

After a while, the inhabitant touches it again.

The power repeats what it did.

***

There is time for this.

The power does not need to crowd an answer into the interval between two breaths. It can wait through a repair, through the long occupation of the fitted chamber, through whatever passes for sleep in a body whose fingers remain within the walls.

It learns first what withdrawal means.

When the inhabitant takes its hand away, the small yielding stops.

When the hand returns, it does not always begin again. Sometimes the power leaves the clay entirely ordinary. The inhabitant is allowed to come to the parapet for the parapet.

Eventually two fingers rest on the little area.

One holds steady. The other moves.

The power remembers the touch on the visitor’s wrist. It answers with the same small changes of pressure, not claiming to know what they mean.

The inhabitant removes both fingers.

Much later, it returns and does it again.

***

The court is not one thing.

Its sounding depends on lower chambers, narrowed channels, the weight of courses built over older courses. Some disturbances arrive from so far beneath that the inhabitant feels them only after they have passed through thousands of fitted surfaces. Others begin under a foot.

There are connections the power could trace at once.

It does not present them all to the inhabitant.

It attends to the place where the hands already are.

At the pipe mouth bearing the new coil, it follows the pressure into the wall. At the sealed jars, it learns what their contents take from the air and what they return. At the worn corner, it feels the old pavements holding one another up.

The pale shapes elsewhere in the court receive no sudden awakening.

The power approaches each as it approached the parapet. Where there is a response, it waits for another. Where there is none, it does not mistake stillness for permission.

One of the shapes beneath the shelf answers after a very long time.

The response is slight: a change in the tension of two fingers that have seemed to be clay.

The power stays nearby.

***

The first alteration is made at the low pipe.

The inhabitant has tried to cross it and has turned back. Its hand is still resting on the mortar ridge when a narrow piece of that ridge becomes firmer beneath its fingers.

Nothing moves yet.

It presses again.

The firmness shifts toward the edge, then returns. A possible bearing is being offered: a place to transfer weight without forcing the leg to straighten.

The inhabitant explores it with a heavy finger. It leaves by the longer route.

The ridge is unchanged when it comes back.

Only on a later passage does it place its foot against the offered support.

Then the power lets the clay rise, no farther than the inhabitant’s own pressure asks of it. A shallow curve forms beside the pipe. Its underside leaves the opening clear. Its upper face takes the soft edge of the foot.

The inhabitant crosses.

On the far side it pauses, with one hand still behind it.

The power waits.

The hand returns to the curve and presses along its length. At one end the clay lowers slightly. At the other it broadens.

When the inhabitant goes away, the alteration remains.

It is small enough that a visitor might call it another old repair. But it bears a fresh impression, and for some time the inhabitant touches that impression whenever it passes.

***

There are other offers.

Not all are accepted.

A softened bearing in the resting chamber is pressed flat again. The power lets it become firm.

A new route to a tool pocket is used once and abandoned. The old route remains.

Where the binding at the hip cuts into the translucent surface, the power makes its contact known on either side of the pain. It does not pull the binding loose. There may be something it holds together.

Over many visits to the chamber, a little of that burden passes into the fitted seat. The inhabitant tests the change by shifting its weight. The sealed fibers ease. Beneath them, a joint moves through a distance it has not moved in a long time.

The inhabitant stops.

The power stops also.

Later, the joint moves again.

***

For the visitor, the power provides something different.

Near the way out, there is a recess where the great roar reaches human bones less forcefully. It does not draw sound from the surrounding walls. It receives the visitor’s presence and keeps the vibration from building inside the jaw.

A person can sit there without putting their knees across the passage.

The inhabitant examines the recess. Its fingers find the boundary of the altered sounding. It puts one foot inside, then takes it out.

The power does not extend the stillness after it.

From that seat, a visitor can place a hand against a small clay surface beside the entrance. Touches made there reach the patch at the parapet—not as an imitation of the court’s innumerable workings, but distinctly, at a strength the inhabitant can allow or diminish.

There need be no visitor.

There need be no answer.

***

In time, the inhabitant brings a little workable clay to the parapet.

It presses the clay onto the place where the power first made itself known. With its narrow fingers, it opens two holes. With the heavy ones, it shapes a lip between them.

The power holds still beneath the work.

The inhabitant polishes the lip, tests the holes, and changes one of them. Then it lays its hand across the addition.

Through the new piece comes a pressure the power has not offered before.

It answers.

The inhabitant adjusts the clay once more.

Far below, the great construction continues sounding. The brown sky has not cleared. The old courses still descend beyond sight, and much in them remains difficult to reach.

But at the parapet there is now a piece whose purpose cannot be understood from either side alone.

The inhabitant rests its fingers there.

After a while, it returns to its work.
