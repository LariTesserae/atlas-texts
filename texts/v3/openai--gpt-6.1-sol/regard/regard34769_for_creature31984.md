The power did not straighten the valley.

It watched a cleaner step into an ankle-deep basin and a fish pass beneath her through a depth she could not enter. It watched a cupboard give up seven cups to its owner and three to her brother, who had come to help after a difficult week. It watched a lintel calf occupy two passages at once while its mother waited with her muzzle against the warm wall.

These were not faults merely because they were difficult to describe.

For some time, it did nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

It followed the woman who read the condensation cups. She began on the upper terraces, where the cups were mostly empty, and descended by a route she had adjusted over several years to spare her stiff knee. The adjustments had made the route longer. There was one staircase she could climb comfortably but could no longer descend without turning sideways.

Beside its third landing stood a sealed service door.

The power considered opening it. Beyond the door was a shorter way down.

There was also a transparent nursery hanging from a screw head, with six young animals holding one another’s feet. The woman could not have passed without brushing it.

The door remained closed.

Instead, on the landing, a little warmth began to travel through the rail. It was no warmer than a hand that had just let go. She stopped there, surprised, and rested.

The next day she stopped again.

By the end of the week, someone had put a narrow seat beneath the rail. It was made from a discarded conveyor slat, with its sharp edges carefully filed. Nobody asked her whether she needed it. Other people used it too.

***

The power learned the valley’s arrangements.

Some had been made by engineers. Some had been made by birds. Some existed because a particular person had always taken the left passage and now could not reliably take the right one.

It learned which warnings protected people and which merely protected a foreman from questions. It learned that a nursery crescent was sometimes rubbed away by the animals inside and sometimes by a supervisor wishing to finish an inspection.

That distinction occupied its attention for a long while.

The next time a supervisor wiped such a mark away, the blue chalk remained on his cloth, but the crescent returned to the panel.

He wiped it again.

Behind the metal there was a small movement.

He stood with the cloth in his hand. Then he entered the inspection as deferred.

Nothing followed him home. No accusation appeared in his window. But the crescent stayed until the young had left, and after that he found it easier to defer other inspections when they needed deferring.

***

There were places where the machinery kept the streets aligned by transferring their errors elsewhere.

A walkway that shortened before the morning shift might take its missing length from an unused room. Usually this caused no trouble. Occasionally the room was not unused. Its occupant simply had no reason to appear on a plan.

The power did not abolish the counterweights. A stopped tower would have injured people in districts far below.

It began with the smallest transfers.

A bank of flywheels developed a faint additional pressure at its bearings whenever its correction would close an inhabited space. The pressure was measurable. It persisted when the instruments were replaced.

For three days the fitters argued about it.

On the fourth, one removed a panel that had never been opened since commissioning. Behind it lay a nest of protective film, several old moth wings, and an animal with its feet unfolded against all four walls.

The fitter replaced the panel.

Thereafter, the operating table acquired another column. At first it was headed **UNACCOUNTED RESISTANCE**. Later someone crossed this out and wrote **CHECK OCCUPANCY**.

The power was patient with the wording.

***

It paid particular attention to tired people.

There was a night worker who had begun to make mistakes with the chalk. He knew this and checked every mark twice. Checking made him later; arriving home later left him less time to sleep.

The power could have steadied his hand indefinitely.

Instead, a chamber he was supposed to inspect failed to settle before the end of his shift. Its indicator stayed amber. He was obliged to leave it.

Inside was only a short flight of stairs, but one of the steps had not yet decided how high it was.

On the following night the chamber remained unavailable. A second worker was assigned to the route. They finished early enough to drink something hot behind the cook’s shutter.

The first worker said he was having trouble sleeping.

This was not something he had planned to say.

His companion knew of a quieter room, and the cook knew who held its key. There were difficulties, including a cupboard that would not accept the worker’s bedding. Those took another week to resolve.

The power left the cupboard alone. Its owner came upstairs and opened it.

***

It did not give every creature the same depth.

The fish kept their water. The pale birds kept the passages by which they retrieved their young. The lizards continued to leave their cages whenever they wished.

But where a child could follow a nestling into an opening from which no adult could bring her back, the air acquired a slight resistance.

It felt like pushing into a hanging sheet.

A bird passed freely. A child met the soft refusal with her forehead and sat down, offended rather than injured.

After several such incidents, children began to test unfamiliar openings with an open hand. They still discovered places adults did not know. Some of those places were safe.

The power did not require the valley to become fully visible.

In the uncommissioned building, the small flattened patches of dust near the window remained unexplained. When workers eventually brought in a bench, they left that end of the room clear.

***

There were losses it could not prevent without taking too much from the lives in which they occurred.

An old lintel ceased eating.

For several days, residents brought grass and laid it beside its mouth. Its herd rested against the adjoining walls. Slow vibrations travelled through the pipework.

The power kept the alcove warm.

It kept a freight route from being diverted through it.

When the lintel died, its weight came into the structure gradually, so that the ceramic beneath it did not crack. This spared the workers a hazardous repair. It did not spare them the death.

A boy who had brought grass each afternoon came with another bundle and found that it was no longer wanted.

He sat beside the animal for a while. Later, one of the tree-tending sisters found him there. She had intended to finish two more sockets before supper.

She sat down too.

***

In time, there were disagreements about what had changed.

Some said the valley was settling well. Some credited revised maintenance procedures. The woman who read the cups said there had always been warm rails, though perhaps not in that particular place.

Nobody was asked to be grateful.

There were still ambitious drains, awkward cupboards, laundry spoiled against unfamiliar walls. People still quarrelled. A latch delivery trolley continued to turn corners too early, and its owner continued to postpone replacing the steering assembly.

Yet fewer problems required someone to go without sleep. Rooms were checked before their dimensions were borrowed. A repair could sometimes wait until the animals had gone.

The valley remained crowded. Within that crowding, its inhabitants found a little more room to attend to one another.

At the uppermost rail, the drop beneath the bolt continued to swell.

The power looked into it: roofs, bridges, a moving lintel, a kitchen window where someone was taking down dry clothes. Beyond the reflection lay everything the drop could not show.

A pale bird landed on the rail.

It bent beneath the bolt and touched the water with its beak.

The drop broke. The bird drank, shook its head, and went away.

By morning, another had begun to gather.
