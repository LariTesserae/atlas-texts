The power’s attention reached the lowland without a light.

Nothing brightened. No warmth entered the water, and the current did not falter. The shell continued down the channel, striking once against a stone before passing beyond it.

Beneath the newly covered stone, the young animal held on.

The power attended first to the places where it touched: the small patches of underside pressed against rock, the margins that had not yet found purchase, the fold through which water passed across its breathing surfaces. There was mud above it and beside it. There was already mud entering the fold.

To the animal, these were not separate troubles. They were pressure, resistance, the need to contract here and loosen there. It had begun the work available to it.

The power waited through a tide.

Waiting did not mean looking away. It followed the fine changes beneath the stone. One feeding thread reached into the deposit and returned with almost nothing. Another found a fragment that could be used. The animal loosened slightly. Along its lower surface, a new area of contact formed.

When the flow reversed, silt entered from the other side.

The stone had settled at an angle that made its underside a trap. Water could press mud into the shallow recess, but could not readily carry it out. The animal was small enough to enter. It would grow too slowly to leave before the passages closed.

Elsewhere, under the same shelf, there were better recesses. The power attended to those too: their narrow entrances, their worn inner faces, the little exchanges of water that continued even when the bottom seemed still. Some were occupied. Some held only old shell dust.

It did not lift the young animal and place it in one of them.

Its underside had found the rock. Its first threads had begun to feed. Moving it would tear some of those contacts, and being carried would tell it nothing useful about where it had arrived.

Instead, the power altered the stone.

Along the lower face ran a seam, almost closed, left by a fracture older than the cold sea. A grain loosened within it. Then another. There was no crack loud enough to startle the fish resting nearby. A little mineral dust joined the sediment already passing through the lowland.

The opening was slight. It connected the blind end of the recess to a shallow groove beyond the stone.

At the next turn of the tide, water entered by the usual way and left through the new one.

Some of the accumulated mud went with it.

The animal contracted at the change in pressure. One thread withdrew; another remained extended. Its grip held. After a while it opened the breathing fold farther.

The power watched the passage through several reversals. A passage that admitted water might also admit too much grit. A clean hollow might expose what the mud had sheltered. The opening needed neither widening nor polishing. Its irregular sides broke the little flow, and the groove beyond it filled and cleared without sealing shut.

The stone remained heavy. The animal remained hidden.

There was still little food.

On the fourth slack tide, a small burden of dead plankton settled along the shelf. The animal’s threads reached it. Much was silt, but not all. Each returning thread carried something beneath the rim.

No abundance followed. The next tide brought almost nothing.

For a time the power’s attention spread across the lowland. It found older animals with notched margins, animals growing thin, young ones losing contact as they traveled. It found the brittle star feeding in the lee of its slab, and the crustaceans waiting where damaged tissue sometimes became available. No single stillness would have served them all.

So the current was left to run.

But where the mud thinned over stone, a few blind recesses acquired small outlets. Not every hollow changed. Only those where a slight release of pressure could preserve a shelter without stripping it bare. The work was scattered over miles, indistinguishable from wear.

Seasons passed above the water.

Under its stone, the animal became broader. It shed patches of its covering and grew new threads. Once, grit damaged part of its rim. The edge healed unevenly. Later, young developed in the recesses beneath it.

One of them crawled toward the seam.

Water moved there, faintly but continuously. Beyond it lay another rough surface, with mud enough to feed in and stone enough to hold.

The young animal made contact.

The power asked nothing of it.
