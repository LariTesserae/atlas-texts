The first thing the power did was wait.

It did not put a light through the entrance. It did not lift a leaf or clear the old leaves from the seam. For a time, its attention rested on the chamber without adding anything the chamber would have to accommodate.

A drop gathered along a ceiling root. The needle spray beneath it remained folded. When the drop fell, some of the water entered the hollow and some stayed on a clear cap at the end of a stem.

Days later, the needles had opened a little.

The power watched the moisture pass through the film beneath the soil. It followed the slow exchange at the ceramic ring, the field reaching the translucent patches under the broad leaves, the heat leaving through the three gray tabs. It watched long enough to distinguish an interruption from a pause.

The dry click behind the wall was not a fault. A part had moved as it was meant to move.

There was no reason to stop it.

The cold, too, belonged here. So did the dark. The tightly folded needles were not asking to be opened, and the closed buds were not failed flowers. Water poured generously onto the floor would have drowned some of the spaces through which the garden kept itself alive.

The power left these things alone.

Beyond the uppermost tab, however, a buried connection had begun to separate.

It lay outside the chamber, in a narrow cavity between the retaining wall and the packed ground. Each winter, a root pressed a little farther across it. The pressure had not yet changed the exchange enough for any leaf to respond. In time it would. First, growth would slow along one side of the garden. Then a broad leaf would remain curled after receiving water. The film would continue taking apart the dead tissue, but there would be less living tissue above it each year.

The power examined the connection and the root.

Then it made room.

A small amount of grit shifted into an empty pocket farther along the wall. The casing moved by less than the thickness of a needle. Around the connection, a brittle seal became sound again.

The root kept its course.

Inside, nothing brightened. No leaf lifted. The deposit on the uppermost tab continued its winter change from one gray to another.

The power also found the apparatus’s provision for failure: a second route for the exchange, unused and intact except at its farthest end. Whoever had installed it had made allowance for a chamber that would outlast regular visits.

That last connection was restored.

The second route remained idle. It would carry nothing unless the first could no longer carry enough, and even then it would supply only what the garden already received.

For several seasons, the power did nothing further.

It watched the pale shoot bend around the smooth stone. It watched the old injured leaf lose another portion of its living edge. The younger growth beside it received some of what the film released.

There was a moment when the power could have kept the old leaf alive.

It did not. The flattened joint was still sound, and the leaf’s remaining tissue was performing the small work available to it. Holding every part unchanged would eventually leave no room for the growth beneath.

When the leaf finally softened, its outline persisted for a long time.

Above the slab, the tree grew.

Its roots held the soil around the entrance, but one had begun to take purchase between the three tilted stones. The power followed the strain through the masonry. There was no immediate danger. There was, however, a likely future in which the stones would settle across the steps, closing the air passage and diverting the scant rainwater.

The power placed support beneath them, deep enough that it did not disturb the moss or the thread crossing onto the smooth stone.

The support did not hold the tree still. It allowed the root to thicken and the masonry to yield a little without falling.

From outside, the entrance remained a shallow recess full of old leaves.

There was no new marker. The garden had survived partly by being overlooked, and the power did not draw visitors to a place where a single careless step could take years to repair.

Nor did it close the entrance against everyone. Someone had placed the smooth stone beside the creeping stem. Someone had once come down the narrow stairs and noticed where a foot should not go.

That possibility was worth leaving open.

Under the stone slab, the exposed moss tip remained dry. Nothing restored it.

Behind it, the sheltered branch lengthened. It reached the edge of the old growth, where a slight irregularity in the stone held the last of a small condensation drop. The thread entered that hollow and stayed there.

The power watched until the next branch began.

Then its attention withdrew from the chamber, leaving the repaired connections, the unobtrusive support, and a watch on the few things beyond the garden’s reach.

The next drop gathered slowly.

Below it, the roots were already in place.
