The power first noticed the bucket.

It was being carried by a child who had to lean away from it to keep its rim from striking his knee. At every junction he set it down, watched the leaves being lifted from the water, and picked it up again. When the older person stopped to examine a loose support, the child stopped too.

They were late that evening. Mist had reached the court, and somebody had already called them to supper.

The power could have cleared every channel before the child took another step. It could have made the supports imperishable, stilled the stone beneath the floors, filled the empty houses with people who would be glad to live there.

Instead, it followed them to the next junction.

There was something here it did not yet understand.

*

For a season, its attention rested in the cup.

It learned which roofs kept out the rain and which merely changed where it fell. It learned that the old man’s pauses were not all caused by pain: at the third landing he liked to look back at the garden, and at the fifth he listened for someone in the kitchen above.

It learned the difference between a building nobody wanted and a building somebody wanted very much but could not use.

Below the town, its attention went more slowly.

The chambers were not empty in the way a room is empty. Water spread in thin layers along their walls. Mineral gathered and loosened. Pressure moved through the mountain so gradually that a roof could be built, repaired, and abandoned while one movement was still beginning.

There was awareness in this, but it did not gather around a name.

A weight on one surface was known far beneath it. Warmth persisted after a cooking fire went out. Some places held the repeated tread of feet differently from the weight of a wall. The town entered this awareness as a complicated region of pressure, warmth, small injuries, and water diverted into unfamiliar courses.

The being within the mountain had been trying, for a long time, to make room around something it could feel but could not distinguish.

The inhabitants, meanwhile, were trying to keep their kitchens level.

Neither undertaking was foolish.

*

The first thing the power changed was beneath the eastern terrace.

A narrow crack had opened in a wooden foot. It was hidden against the stone, where even the broad-shouldered man’s hand could not reach. In another month, perhaps two, the foot would split. The gallery would drop at one end. Whether anyone would be standing there depended on an afternoon that had not yet arrived.

The power held the crack still.

It did this without hardening the whole timber. Sound wood must bend here. It held only the two breaking edges, lightly, as a person might support a bowl while someone else found a place to put it.

Then it waited for the morning inspection.

The man passed the support. He put his palm on the rising stone and moved on.

That evening Sela came beneath the gallery looking for a strip of willow she had dropped. She found it beside a small heap of pale wood dust.

There should not have been wood dust there.

She fetched a lamp.

By noon the next day the gallery was propped, the foot removed, and three people were arguing over how long the crack had been there.

The power let go.

It did not leave a sign on the repaired wood.

*

That would have been easy to continue: catch each failing beam, restrain each dangerous fold, turn every badly placed step into a safe one.

But the power had seen how closely the town’s knowledge depended on consequences arriving in time to be understood. If every weak fastening held, weak fastening would become a sound method. If every dangerous place was quietly made safe, somebody would eventually lead a child onto one.

It therefore sought a smaller, more durable kindness.

On a cold morning Sela found a bead of water standing on an otherwise dry patch of stone beneath her work bay.

She wiped it away.

It returned.

By afternoon there were five beads in a line. Beneath them, the stone had softened enough to take the edge of a thumbnail.

She moved the stored wicker, loosened two lashings, and asked the broad-shouldered man to look.

Three days later the fold began to rise.

Elsewhere, a similar line appeared beneath a stair. There was time to lift the lowest tread. At the long building, dampness traced a curve beside a support before the surface behind it shifted.

Not every wet place meant movement. The town already had more wet places than it needed. These were particular: beads along the edge of a coming change, appearing well before the pressure reached the wood.

The inhabitants tested them. They put charcoal marks nearby. They measured. One woman refused to alter a floor merely because it had become damp, and was vindicated; the dampness came from a leaking trough.

The new signs did not spare them judgment.

They made judgment less hurried.

*

Within the mountain, the power made a corresponding alteration.

It brought the small, brief pressures near the surface into clearer relation with one another.

A footstep and its warmth. A hand and the slight yielding beneath it. The removal of a stake and the relief that followed. The difference between a household sleeping above a chamber and timber stacked there for winter.

It did not pour human thoughts into the stone. The being had no use for kitchens, brothers, promises, or the names of birds.

But a living weight became distinguishable from other weights.

The first response took most of a year.

A fold beneath an occupied room slowed. Another, beside an empty platform, continued upward. Water began to find its way around a place where repeated hammering had lately ceased.

None of this was exact. The mountain could not turn as a person turns away from someone in a narrow passage. Its movements involved depths beyond the cup.

Still, a room remained usable through a spring in which everyone had expected to leave it.

Its occupants were pleased. They did not consider this proof of anything.

The power was pleased too.

*

There were other matters that could not be settled beneath the floorboards.

Sela’s brother was alive below the mountain. His hands hurt in winter. He had a household, debts, a fondness for a street that smelled of bread in the morning. In his mind, the climb home had become larger each year.

The power did not make him lonely enough to return.

When he next wrote, it kept the letter dry through a storm that soaked the carrier’s blanket and spoiled a sack of meal. That was all.

His letter said that he would not come before the following summer, and might not come then. It asked whether Sela still had their mother’s small cooking pot.

Sela read it twice. She wrote back that she did, although the lid belonged to a different pot now.

Under another roof, the man waiting for a cooper continued repairing barrels. A passing trader bought two of them. He charged too little, was told so at supper, and charged more the next time.

The empty galleries remained empty.

Some were places to work. Some were poor decisions. Some held hopes that their owners had not finished with.

The power left them standing.

*

There was one change people could hear.

The water falling beneath the court had always struck a ledge before entering the deeper chamber. Its sound occupied the cup, filling the spaces where voices might have carried.

Over two winters, the ledge acquired a shallow groove.

The falling stream divided. Part of it ran along the wall.

No chamber was sealed. Wind still entered the eastern slit; buckets still made an unreasonable noise when dropped. The children’s experiments remained easy to detect.

But at the lower basin, a person could sometimes finish a sentence without leaning close.

People argued about the cause. Someone said the stone was wearing away. Someone else said it was growing into a different shape.

Both had evidence.

In the following summer, the boy who had carried the bucket called across the court to ask whether the scoop was needed.

This time he was heard.

*

The power stayed.

It did not occupy the central stone or require the empty market bays for offerings. Most days its attention was scarcely different from the care already present: Sela turning willow through her fingers, the old woman remembering a lost spring, someone checking that water continued to flow before leaving a junction.

When there was danger no warning could avert, it intervened.

When there was time, it helped the warning arrive.

And when the mountain’s slow awareness reached toward the inhabitants above it, the power kept that meeting clear of what neither side could yet bear.

Years passed. A child grew tall enough to carry the bucket without leaning. One kitchen tipped and another did not. Sela loosened a frame a little earlier than she would once have done.

On the wall behind the long building, her father’s hand remained where it had been.

One afternoon she moved the basket because she needed it elsewhere.

The palm was exposed to the light. Nobody asked her about it.

Below, water took its altered course through the dark. Above, someone measured a damp line beneath a stair, called for a wedge, and waited while another person brought one.
