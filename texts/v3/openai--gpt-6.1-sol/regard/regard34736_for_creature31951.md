The power noticed first the small corrections of the feet.

It had seen the whole reach: the water entering without a wave, the birds retreating by steps, the pale rims dissolving into the pools. But here, beneath the surface, the animal was continually adjusting its weight. One foot loosened. Another found a roughness in the stone. The tail turned slightly, and the body settled back into the current.

Stillness, seen closely, was full of work.

The power stayed.

It did not make itself visible above the flats. A shape large enough to contain its attention would have covered the sky, and even a small unfamiliar shape might have driven the animal from its feeding place. It did not speak. The low creature had no use for a voice arriving from everywhere.

A fish passed. The animal missed it.

Another passed, and was caught.

The power felt the brief distress of the fish and the easing of hunger in the animal. Neither feeling erased the other. It allowed the current to continue.

Through the night it attended to the place.

It learned which pools remained cool beneath their shelves, which mud could bear weight, which channels joined only when the tide was high. It followed the salt into the earth and the fresh water spreading outward after rain. Beneath the bare pans were dormant things waiting for conditions that had not arrived in years. Among the roots of a shrub, eggs lay in a chamber whose roof was marked by the careful strokes of hindfeet.

There was much here that could be harmed by an improvement.

Deeper water would cover feeding ground. More shrubs would bind soil, but their shade would change the warm shallows. A ridge raised across the wrong channel would keep one hollow fresh and leave another without the movement that brought its food.

The power waited through rain and dry weather. It watched young animals leave their shelter and adults lose contests at the channel mouths. Once, a bird carried a striped young one out of the sedges. The power did not become less attentive afterward.

In time, it learned something the animals had no way to know.

Far out beyond the brackish reach, a storm was gathering over open water. Its winds would drive the sea inland while heavy rain filled the flats from above. The usual high places would disappear. Even the tongues of rooted ground would remain underwater for several days.

The power considered the land again.

Near the inland margin was a shelf of rock almost buried beneath clay. Long ago it had stood higher. Now its upper surface lay only a little above the surrounding pans, and water passed around both ends.

There the power acted.

No stone broke. Under the shelf, the earth lifted slowly, with a motion too slight to disturb the snails sheltering along its edge. Over several nights the rock rose by less than the height of the animal’s body. Its sides remained sloping. The channels at either end stayed open.

A little mud slid away. Grass roots held the rest.

The animal found it before the storm.

It climbed onto the shelf in the ordinary course of moving between pools, rested with its belly against a damp seam, and later went down again. Other animals crossed it. Birds stood there. Nothing announced that it was different from the many low rises scattered through the flats.

When the storm arrived, the water came farther inland than any of them had known.

The channel mouths vanished. Shrubs stood with only their upper branches exposed. Animals moved from one shrinking patch of ground to another. Some reached the lifted shelf by walking; others arrived swimming, their nostrils barely clear of the brown surface.

There was room, though not much.

The broad-bodied animal raised itself when another approached. Its throat swelled. Then a gust drove water across the rock, and both lowered their heads. They settled apart, each with its face unobstructed.

For three days the shelf remained above water.

The power did not hold back the wind. It stayed near the small rise, attending to the earth beneath it, while rain struck the scales of the crowded animals and ran down into the sea.

Afterward, the water withdrew through the channels it had always used.

There were losses. There were empty nests and newly opened pools. The animal returned to its feeding place beside the stone. For a while the current carried little that it could catch.

On the fourth evening, fish entered the shallows.

Its forefeet found their old purchase. Its tail lay downstream. When a bird landed nearby, one eye turned toward it, then back to the water.

The power remained attentive, without asking to be known.
