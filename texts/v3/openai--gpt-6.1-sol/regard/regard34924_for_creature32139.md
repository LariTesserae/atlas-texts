The power comes without adding a voice to the hillside.

For a long while it listens.

It listens beneath the calls and wingbeats, beneath the scrape of the female’s nails, to the air moving through the bank. It follows water down the roots and through the old galleries. Where a passage has fallen in, it feels the weight resting on either side. Where a wall is being raised, it attends to each grain arriving between a worker’s jaws.

It does not mistake the hillside for a single creature. There are lives here whose needs conflict. The female opens a wall; the colony closes it. A grub feeds in the wood that supports a chamber. A root finds nourishment in the chamber’s decay.

The power can feel the soldiers biting the female’s muzzle. It can also feel the hunger that brings her to their gallery.

It stays long enough to learn something of both.

In the nest, one youngster has fallen asleep with fruit pulp drying on its wrist. The other is still making small chewing movements, although its mouth is empty. Their mother lies with her nose toward the entrance and one ear slightly raised.

The power’s attention rests on her scars, her clay-stained elbows, the bare strip beneath her tail. Much of what keeps her alive is knowledge carried in her body. She knows where to place a foot and how long to wait after opening a gallery. She knows several ways home.

The power does not take that knowledge away by making every surface safe.

Nor does it still the insects at her doorway. Their work will keep much of the rain out. In a few hours she will wake, push her fingers into the soft new material, and make room to leave. She has done this before.

Instead, it follows the two passages that ventilate her nest.

The upper one is sound. The lower bends beneath a root, passes an empty beetle chamber, and opens into a hollow several body lengths downhill. Air is moving through it now.

Outside its mouth lies a fallen leaf.

The leaf has been there for days. Dry, it arches above the opening. Wet, it will soften. Water gathering along its central vein will pull it down against the clay.

The female has chosen her chamber well. She could not have known where the leaf would fall after she entered.

The power waits as rain gathers on the canopy.

It could hold the leaf away forever. It could keep the nest warm and dry through every storm. But then the female would have no reason to notice when the surrounding bank grew unsound, and the young might learn a sleeping place that only remained safe because something unseen maintained it.

The first water reaches the hollow.

A beetle retreats under a twig. The leaf darkens and begins to sag.

The power moves the twig.

It is a movement smaller than the twitch of the female’s sleeping ear. One end slips from a shallow groove and settles beneath the leaf. The stem is caught between two firm pieces of clay. When the leaf sinks, it rests on the twig, leaving a narrow opening below.

Air continues through the nest.

No one wakes.

For the rest of the night, the power stays near the bank. It does not prevent the rain. Water enters some galleries; workers carry pale young toward higher chambers. A spider abandons its web. The pool overflows, and the frogs move out among the wet roots.

The power attends to these lives as closely as it attends to the sleepers. Care, here, requires more than looking at the creature whose face is easiest to recognize.

Near dawn the rain stops.

The female wakes before the young. She lifts her head and smells the entrance, then puts a hand against the wall. Its new surface gives slightly beneath her fingers.

She opens it in three places, testing before she pulls.

Outside, the bank is dark with moisture. She descends far enough to inspect the lower opening. Her whiskers touch the wet leaf. She presses it, finds the twig beneath, and sniffs along the exposed passage.

Then she moves on.

The power goes with her to the pool, where she drinks beside the overflow. It follows while she turns a piece of rotten wood and finds two grubs. It watches her pause at a cheek-marked root and choose another route home.

There is no sign she knows she has been helped.

The power requires none.

Later, when the young begin travelling with her, one will linger at that lower opening. It will put a hand into the gap and feel the faint current against its palm. Its mother will call from farther up the bank. The youngster will withdraw its hand and follow.

By then the leaf will be drying. In time it will break into fragments, and the twig will loosen. The entrance will change again.

The power does not appoint itself keeper of that one small arrangement.

It remains attentive to the hillside: to the routes still open, the water finding its way down, the animals able to leave one chamber and try another. When help is needed, it looks for help that leaves them room to continue their own work.

As morning brightens above the leaves, the female returns carrying food against her belly.

Workers have narrowed the upper doorway.

She sets the food down, braces herself with her tail, and begins to open it.

The power watches her hands.
