The power did not first appear as light.

There was already light enough for the lives that needed it. More would have drawn the small animals out of their hollows, changed the routes of the silver fish, exposed bodies whose safety depended on the dark.

Nor did it still the falling water.

The sea was speaking through that noise. To remove it would have been to remove something by which its inhabitants knew where they were.

For a long time, the power attended.

It followed the seep through the floor and the water over the banks. It listened beneath the slow exchanges of the terraces. It watched a body go over the crest, folded narrowly into the falling sheet, and followed it farther than any light in the basin could be seen.

Then it returned.

It did not close the way.

In the court where the old animal had subsided, the young one was trying to rest.

There was nothing urgently wrong. It was fed. Its sheath was whole. The fish that accompanied it were numerous. When it left the hollow, it could swim well, and several places nearby held its body more comfortably.

Yet it returned to this one.

The ridge that had supported the old animal’s head was too high. The depression beneath it was too broad. When the young one settled, one side of its belly touched the floor and the other hung above it. Its sensing organs reached into water.

It stayed like that for a while. Then it braced a fin against the ridge and shifted. Silt rose around its eyes.

The power could have remade the hollow in an instant.

It could have lifted the wood to meet every delicate fold, warmed the current, brought food to the mouth. It could have given the animal the exact comfort that another body had taken centuries to grow.

Instead, it waited through several returns.

On the fourth, the young one settled differently. It lay across the hollow rather than along it, with its mouth toward the open court. Its fins reached no support at all.

It remained.

Beneath it, the wood began its almost imperceptible work.

The power attended more closely.

What looked like a failure to fit was not simply a failure. The animal was finding a posture of its own. Part of the old hollow would remain beneath it. Part would disappear. The head ridge, if left alone, would become a shelter beside its flank.

There was no need to finish this on its behalf.

But there was something else.

When the young one called, most of its note went into the water.

It lifted a patch of belly and pressed it down. Because the floor was too far away, the movement ended softly, without the contact that carried a call through the basin. The nearby fish scattered and returned. A grazer lifted its head.

Far away, another long-lived animal continued its slow circuit, unaware.

The young one tried again.

The power listened to the note that had almost entered the wood.

It did not resemble the old animal’s call.

For a while, nothing changed.

Then a narrow tongue of living grain rose from the side of the hollow.

It rose no faster than a root might follow moisture. Transparent algae covered it as it grew. Its end remained soft, and beneath it there was room for the tiny creatures of the nap to pass. It did not reach up and take hold of the animal. It stopped short.

The young one left without touching it.

During its absence, a small grazer scraped the new growth. The scrape healed. A fish rested beneath it. Water moved around both sides.

When the young one returned, it put its mouth against the tongue and tasted it.

Then it settled elsewhere in the hollow.

The power waited.

Many shoals passed.

Eventually the animal shifted far enough that one of its belly ridges lay against the new wood. It lifted the ridge. Pressed it down.

The note entered the floor.

It travelled beneath the court, through a buttress, along the base of an arch worn smooth by resting jaws. The waterfall’s concussion thickened around it. For a moment it seemed lost.

Then, from beyond two terraces, a reply arrived.

The young one lifted its head.

Its small eyes brightened along the front edge of its body. It moved one fin, then another. Beneath it, a hundred sensing ridges opened against the wood.

The reply was not from the place toward which the old animal’s neighbours still turned.

It came from a living body, lying crookedly beside a channel, with a scar across its sheath and three small fish sheltering near its mouth.

The young one called again.

This time the note was different.

The distant animal answered differently too.

The power did not enter their exchange.

It remained beside the hollow while the calls passed through the floor. Sometimes there was a long interval. Sometimes the young one moved off the narrow tongue and lost contact. Once it swam away altogether, leaving the fish to wait above the empty depression.

When it returned, it brought a fringe of silt from another court.

It rested with its belly against the wood.

The tongue began to thicken at its base. Where it was no longer needed, it softened. The ordinary growth of the basin took possession of it, drawing it into the slow alteration of the hollow.

There would be no enduring mark to distinguish the power’s work from the work already happening there.

The power stayed longer than it needed to.

It learned which of the young animal’s motions meant discomfort, which meant feeding, and which meant nothing more than the pleasure of stretching a limb into cold water. It watched the animal leave the hollow to meet the distant caller. It watched them pass one another beneath an arch without touching, then turn, much later, and approach again.

It watched the young one return alone.

The hollow still did not fit.

Now one fin could reach the old head ridge. The animal rested it there, loosely. Its mouth faced the court. Beneath its lifted side, small translucent bodies occupied the space that remained.

The power withdrew its attention by degrees, so that nothing waiting beneath it would suddenly be left in silence.

Across the basin, the old exchanges continued. At the banks, short notes passed beneath the overflow. The water fell, carrying its travellers into darkness.

In the court, the young animal pressed its belly against the floor.

Somewhere far off, another body answered.

And under the animal’s slight weight, the hollow went on changing.
