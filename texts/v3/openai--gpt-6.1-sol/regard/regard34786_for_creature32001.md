The power first noticed the hand.

Not because the gesture was a prayer. It was not. The resident beneath the chilled pipe had no reason to suppose that anything watched them, and was not asking the drop to be more than it was.

The water spread across the contact pad. The hand steadied briefly.

The power followed the warmth back through the finger, through the wrist seals, into a body that had spent too much of the day accommodating temperatures it could survive but did not prefer. Then it followed the pipe. Beyond the elbow were a compressor, a supply schedule, and a drawing on which the district appeared as an area of recovered heat.

There were no stools on the drawing.

The power looked longer.

It could have cooled the excavation at once. It could have opened the concrete overhead, drawn a garden down through it, furnished every room with whatever a room ought to contain. None of these acts would have required much effort.

Instead, before morning, it removed a burr from the unfinished stair.

It left the tread its original shape. The polished place was scarcely wider than a finger.

Then it came down by the long route.

It used a body small enough for the passages, with hands that could remain visible. At the upper landing it waited while a resident finished crossing, then followed them into the court. It did not glow. The machinery did not fall silent.

The resident with the articulated neck looked at it over a tank.

“Are you looking for a connection?”

“No,” it said. “I would like to speak with the people who live here.”

“About what?”

“The heat. The routes. Anything else you want changed.”

The resident’s shutters narrowed.

“Which department?”

“None.”

This did not make the answer more reassuring.

The power stayed beside the grate, out of the way, until someone brought a stool. It sat. The stool was warm, and one leg was shorter than the others.

People came by in the course of doing other things. Some stopped. One asked whether the power could repair a hand. One asked who had sent it. Someone from the second row said that the lift was new and they were not prepared to have it replaced again.

“I can help,” the power said. “I have not been sent to inspect you.”

“Help with what?”

“I don’t know yet.”

That answer kept three people there who had been about to leave.

By evening, the power had heard about the shutters that discharged heat into the lowest passage; a door that would not close once its frame expanded; the bed contact that woke its occupant every time a neighboring pump changed load. It had also heard that the warm wall beside the cable box should remain warm, that the lift was useful despite its position, and that a particular loose tile must not be cemented down because someone kept spare contact pads underneath it.

The plant tender wanted cleaner rain.

The maker of small objects wanted nobody to reorganize the room behind the pump.

The resident who marked limits asked what would happen to the pencil lines.

“We can leave the surfaces they’re on,” said the power.

“They’re not all on surfaces we want left.”

They went together to look.

***

There was one intervention the power made without waiting for the next meeting.

A tank support had begun to fail inside its concrete footing. The damage was not yet visible; by the time it became visible, a room beneath it would already be collapsing.

The power held the tank where it was. Then it knocked on the door of the room.

It explained what was happening, including what it had already done.

The occupant took down their ceramic rack before allowing anyone inside.

For the repair, the power borrowed tools. This was slower than making the footing sound by other means, but it allowed the residents to see the new brace, inspect its attachments, and know how the weight would travel when the temporary support was withdrawn.

The occupant watched from the doorway.

“Is this safe now?”

“Yes.”

“Was it safe yesterday?”

“No.”

Their shutters closed for a long moment.

The power did not tell them how fortunate they had been.

***

Over the next several days, the district acquired something its revised plans had never quite provided: room to make a choice.

Not a finished room. Not yet.

The cutting machine was moved back, and the work lamps were left on a separate circuit. There would be no charge for postponing construction. The chalk doorway remained where it was. So did the bolts.

The evening discussions became longer, and occasionally less courteous.

One resident wanted a private cooling room because they were tired of everyone seeing their hands shake. Another pointed out that a private room could be occupied all evening by one person. The plant tender had already set three trays beneath a lamp and was unwilling to remove them merely because a decision had not been reached.

The power attended when invited. It answered questions about what was possible. When two proposals could not occupy the same space, it said so.

It did not make everyone agree.

Meanwhile, it went above.

The city had names for everything passing through the excavation: waste heat, service access, reserve volume. The power required those names to be corrected wherever they concealed a home.

That work took longer than cooling a wall would have taken. There were contracts, responsibilities distributed across offices, and drawings copied from drawings whose errors had acquired the authority of age.

The power stayed with it.

New arrangements made the district’s supply independent of how much labor its residents performed. Maintenance access no longer meant unannounced entry. A change in the city’s cooling demand would no longer be allowed to overheat occupied rooms.

The residents were given the plans in forms they could inspect, amend, and keep. Several insisted on ceramic copies.

No one had to accept repairs to their body in order to remain housed.

***

The hot exhaust was diverted first.

After that, a narrow circuit of temperate panels was installed in the passages and rooms. They could take in heat as well as provide it. Their controls were broad enough for trembling fingers.

The older warm surfaces stayed where people wanted them.

In the lowest passage, the chilled pipe received new insulation and a gutter beneath its troublesome elbow. Before the leak was stopped, the power asked the resident who caught the drops whether they wanted a small outlet fitted there.

“For cooling?”

“If you like.”

The resident touched the dry edge of the new gutter.

“Will it happen by itself?”

“It can.”

They considered this.

“Not regularly.”

So the outlet released an occasional bead, with intervals long enough to wait through. The resident could turn it off. The pipe behind it no longer depended on damaged insulation.

The green film was not scraped away. A shallow channel supplied its wall with water, and its upper and lower pencil marks remained visible. Later, a third line appeared. It was crooked and dated.

Clean water reached the vine. Its dry tendrils did not revive, but the new growth passed the last wet bolt.

***

The power offered to restore the unreadable designation on the worn chest plate.

Its owner declined.

The resident who remembered the uneven footsteps asked whether the missing person could be found. The power searched, with permission, and returned with an account of where it had looked and what it had failed to learn.

“I can keep looking,” it said.

“Not today.”

It stopped.

***

In the end, the new room became two rooms with a wide sliding partition. Neither had a duct passing through occupied space. One could be closed; the other remained open to the passage. The plant trays took up more of the shared floor than several residents thought reasonable.

There was still an argument about this.

By then the court had daylight for part of the morning. The louvers had been adjusted rather than removed, because the white sky above could itself become a source of heat. From one position beside the grate, it was possible to see a branch of a tree growing at street level.

The resident who described trees stood below it and pointed.

“That,” they said.

The others looked.

The branch moved, carrying its smaller branches with it. For once, the teller’s hands remained still.

The power did not remain in the court forever. It left a means of contact that worked from every room, and made sure the residents could use it without going through the city above. Then it departed by the stairs.

On the upper landing, it passed someone sitting with insulating cloth over their shoulders.

“Are you finished?” they asked.

“With what I came to do.”

The resident shifted slightly, making room on the landing.

Below them, a relay clicked. The floor began to tremble.

It was familiar enough that neither looked down.
