The power remained on the bench for a long time.

It did not become accustomed to the noise. It learned to hear more of it.

The prolonged scrape beneath the eaves was not the same thing as the rasp of a newly hatched animal. The heavy third foot in the wheel was not an error in the wheel’s rotation. Beneath the press, the movement of a lower jaw toward polished metal occurred more often near the end of a working interval.

The district answered shortages very quickly. Other conditions could persist for years without producing a signal that reached beyond the body in which they occurred.

The power paid particular attention to those.

It learned what the small transparent animals carried into the slack places beneath a broad back. It learned how long the canal creatures could release their mouths before the current became dangerous. It learned why the open ground was avoided.

Some of the answers were ordinary. The stone offered no purchase. A damp underside dried in the wind. A creature caught in the square could not make its warning audible over the pumping house.

Some answers were older than the district. Certain bodies tightened in exposure even when there was no danger left to justify it.

The power did not require them to overcome that.

For several days, nothing visible changed.

A bearing inside the pumping house ran a little cooler. A pipe ceased transmitting one particular vibration through the socket beneath the press. No fluid was lost. No mouth downstream went hungry.

The animal in the socket moved its head.

It moved it back again.

The power waited.

At the next rest interval, the animal placed its jaw against the side that had never been polished. It remained there until the ram began to rise.

That was the first alteration the power kept.

***

It would have been easy to stop the machinery.

The flywheel could have been stilled between one knock and the next. The doors could have opened. Every collar could have fallen apart without touching the flesh enclosed around it.

But the heat beneath the racks would have failed. The clear animals would have lost the moist pockets in which their young developed. Bodies in the canal would have loosened at the wrong time. In the sudden silence, warnings would have gone unanswered.

So the power began by making it possible for things to stop.

Under the paving, beside the existing return channels, it made a second set of channels. They carried the same water at the same temperature. Their masonry had joints of several widths, with sheltered hollows beyond them. At first nothing entered except mineral dust.

Then one of the canal animals released its grip at the customary vibration and arrived somewhere the current did not pull so hard.

It fastened itself to a joint.

Downstream, there was no shortage. The power had supplied what its absence would otherwise have withheld.

The animal stayed.

Others passed the opening. Some entered; some did not. One settled in the quiet channel for a considerable interval and then returned to the faster water. The power left both routes open.

In the workshops, it made a similar provision, although the work was more difficult.

A broad-backed animal could not simply be lifted out of its station. Its tissues depended on pressure. Some of the metal inside it had become necessary to the shape of its body. Removal would have been an injury.

The power took the load first.

For one stroke of the press, the ram descended and the ridge beneath it did not compress.

The cup still received its bead of dark fluid.

Small attendants gathered in the uncompressed folds. The animal held very still. At the following stroke the familiar pressure returned.

Over many intervals, the unburdened strokes became longer. The flesh around the collar softened. A passage opened where a pin had entered the flank. Nothing was pulled free. What had grown together was allowed to separate at the speed at which living tissue could bear separation.

There came a morning when the animal beneath the press could turn its head far enough to see the doorway.

It did not leave.

For a long time, being able to look was the use it made of the change.

***

The power made shelter across the square.

Not a roof over everything. The exposed stone had its inhabitants too, though they crossed it briefly, and some of the mineral exchanges required that it remain bare.

Instead, low lips appeared along certain paving joints. Beneath them ran narrow, damp passages. At the bollards these widened into chambers; between bollards they divided, offering more than one way through.

From the rim, the square still looked empty.

Close to the ground, it had become a place with roads.

The first crossing took most of a day. A seed-shaped animal advanced beneath one lip, withdrew, advanced again. Others gathered behind it without pushing. At dusk, though the sky had scarcely changed, it reached the shelter at the next bollard.

There was nothing useful there that it could not have found at home.

It stayed for a while and came back.

The power made no attempt to improve upon this journey.

***

Not every difficulty was a burden that could be taken away.

The grinding beneath the eaves belonged partly to the bodies making it. Their plates grew; their adhesive fields needed tending. Silencing them would have meant changing them more deeply than the power yet understood.

It gave them places where their calls could carry without being loud.

Narrow resonant chambers were formed behind the brickwork. The animals discovered them through the changed return of their own sounds. A short scrape began to travel as far as a long one. The gutter creatures answered with fewer stones.

The district grew quieter unevenly.

Some walls kept their old intervals. Some hatchlings learned the new ones. A wheel animal still paused at the heavy third footfall from the next station, even after its own wheel had ceased to require walking.

That footfall, it appeared, was something it wished to hear.

The power kept listening.

***

There were deaths it prevented, and deaths it could not prevent without making the dying creature into something else.

At a failing mouth in the canal, it lessened the current. It kept the body clean. It gave the animal a surface it could hold without effort.

When the mouth finally loosened, the body did not sweep away.

The small attendants arrived as they always had, but not while there was still anything there to feel them.

No monument was erected. The hollow in the masonry remained, and later a young animal occupied it.

Elsewhere, a shell at the bench’s foot was left empty longer than usual. Its neighbors continued to turn toward it. The power did not hurry them.

***

The largest change concerned the young beneath the workshop racks.

They had always been carried to places that needed bodies. A loose bracket, a warm bearing, a vacant socket: these had determined how they unfolded.

Now the power made several places that needed nothing.

They were warm enough. There were folds of shelter, shallow water, surfaces of different hardness. The long-legged carriers brought young there, took some away, and returned with others.

One creature developed a broad back without a press above it.

Another grew into a shape for which the district had no station.

For a time it seemed to do very little. It lay with its limbs spread beneath a ledge. Transparent animals moved through the folds along its sides.

Then it began making a sound.

The nearest adults answered with their usual interval. The creature repeated its sound. It did not fit.

The adults answered again.

Eventually, one of them changed its reply.

***

The power did not leave when these things had happened.

It remained in the temperature of the spare channels, in the support beneath the idle ram, in the careful provision that kept one animal’s rest from becoming another animal’s hunger. Much of what it did would never be visible from the rim.

The district still wore its old masonry. Water crossed it. Bodies gathered beneath doorsteps. There were preferences the power had not yet learned to recognize, and it continued learning.

At the bench, the flat-backed creature still rose to meet a visitor’s weight.

It pressed once, finding the load.

Then a support beneath the paving took over, gently enough that the animal could keep contact if it wished.

Sometimes it did.

Sometimes it lowered itself and moved away beneath the seat.

If you stood, the creatures around the iron legs noticed. A few mouths changed their hold. The wood lifted slightly.

Nothing had to hurry to replace you.
