The power did not begin by making the mountain safe.

There were warm fissures whose closing would kill the roots. There were loose stones holding small quantities of water that had nowhere else to go. The noise, which might have seemed the first thing to remove, carried much of what the inhabitant knew about its surroundings.

For a while the power attended to these things without changing them.

It followed the water down through the mountain and back. It learned what was taken from the pressure vessel, and where that useful thing went. It watched the visitor climb the last exposed stretch of path with a bag held against one hip. It noticed that the visitor stopped twice, not to admire the clouds, but to let a pain behind one knee diminish.

Under the grove, it waited through several of the inhabitant’s long periods of stillness.

The cut in the right hand was not healing. Each morning the edges had drawn a little closer; each morning the work at the drain pulled them apart again.

That was where the power began.

***

The next time the inhabitant came onto the apron, someone was already sitting beside the drain.

The newcomer was low to the ground, with no bag, no tools, and no dry place beneath them. Water from the roof struck one shoulder at irregular intervals.

The inhabitant stopped among the leaves.

The newcomer kept their hands in view. After a time they lifted a torn leaf from the slot and put it beside the usual heap. Then another. When they encountered a living root, they stopped, leaving it untouched.

The inhabitant came forward.

It rested its uninjured hand on the concrete. The newcomer moved back far enough to leave room.

The root was caught under a lip of metal. The inhabitant worked it free, eased it toward the soil, and remained there while the newcomer cleared the dead stems around it. Neither hurried. Once, a small snail emerged on the underside of a leaf. The newcomer set that leaf apart from the heap.

When the drain was clear, the inhabitant laid its left palm over the buried elbow.

The newcomer waited.

Only when the inhabitant lifted its hand did they move again.

***

There was no sudden closing of the wound.

The power could have done that. It could also have mistaken the mineral pressed into the cut for dirt, or the softness of the wet skin for disease. Its capacity to act was not a reason to act before it understood.

On the second morning it brought a small piece of pale material.

This was not a dry cloth. Water passed through it slowly, while its inner surface remained smooth. It would yield when the fingers spread and would not tighten around them. Nothing in it would poison the channel after it was shed.

The newcomer placed it on the apron.

The inhabitant turned it over with its left hand. It pressed the material against a wrist, then carried it beneath the grove.

The power did not follow.

By evening the material lay in the hollow, caught among the roots. The injured hand remained bare.

The following morning, beside the drain, the newcomer wrapped a second piece around two fingers of their own hand. They spread the fingers, closed them, and put the hand into the flowing water. Then they removed the wrapping and laid it down.

The inhabitant watched from beneath its dripping eye-folds.

Later it pushed the first piece toward the newcomer.

The hand followed, held out with the cut uppermost.

The power supported it from beneath. It did not hold the fingers open longer than necessary. When the inhabitant drew back once, the power let go at once.

Eventually the wrapping was in place.

A little of the wound’s pain ceased. The inhabitant looked down at its hand and then pressed it against the concrete, cautiously, as though testing a repaired fitting.

***

The visitor found them together three days later.

By then the newcomer was clearing the dead material from the drain, while the inhabitant dealt with the roots.

The visitor stood at the edge of the apron for some time. Then they set down the bag.

“Is that staying?” they asked, pointing at the wrapping.

“For as long as it helps.”

The motor made further conversation difficult. Inside, the newcomer listened while the visitor explained the two fittings, the temper of the pressure valve, and the part that could no longer be ordered.

They did not interrupt with a better design.

The visitor had kept this installation running through years of weather, shortage, and difficult journeys. Some of the repairs were inelegant. Several were ingenious. One prevented a failure the power had not yet noticed, because it happened only when cold water entered after a long shutdown.

They examined that arrangement together.

Outside, the inhabitant waited with one side of its head against a pipe.

***

The first alteration to the machinery was very small.

A mounting beneath the motor had begun to fail. Its vibration had been travelling into the foundation, where a crack was growing toward the front bore.

The power repaired the mounting without changing the motor’s speed.

For two days the inhabitant approached the pump house, listened, and withdrew. The visitor sat on the bench during part of that time. The power stayed outside.

On the third day the inhabitant opened the door and fetched the bent rod.

After that, other work proceeded slowly.

A second route for circulation was made deep inside the mountain. It remained closed while the existing route worked freely. If a bearing seized or a pipe broke, warm water would still reach the hollow and the pool while repairs were made.

Before opening any part of it, the power showed the inhabitant where it ran.

The creature traced the route across the mineral ground. At one place it stopped, pressed its throat against a fissure, and stayed until evening.

That section was moved.

No one demanded an explanation.

The visitor received replacement parts that fitted, and a way to call for help without descending through the lower weather. The dangerous stretch of the approach acquired sound footholds. They were placed where feet already sought purchase.

At the outer rim, where obstructions sometimes gathered, a handhold was fixed within reach of the inward-leaning trees. It did not require the inhabitant to stand closer to the drop.

The power left the grove dark beneath its leaves. It left the snails, the fermenting flowers, the milky sediment. It did not scour the iron steps.

***

When the hand had healed, the inhabitant removed the wrapping itself.

It examined the pale material for a long time. Then it took it to the dead pool where the broken plates of its skin were kept and placed it along the rim.

The power left it there.

The fern behind the left shoulder also remained. Once, while the inhabitant permitted a loose mineral plate to be lifted away, the power touched the fern’s lowest frond.

The creature turned sharply.

The hand withdrew.

Afterward, the plate was returned as usual.

***

There remained the matter of the six days beside the rear wall.

The power listened in the deep warm passages. It found moving water, chambers under pressure, stone shifting too slowly for the visitor to hear. Farther down were ways it could have entered only by changing them.

It did not break them open.

Instead, it made sure that neither the circulation nor the new safeguards closed any passage that had been open before. Whatever might travel through the mountain would still be able to travel.

It could not promise the inhabitant an answer.

Sometimes it sat beside the rear wall anyway.

***

There were dry cloths on the bench now, kept in a box the inhabitant could open. They were ordinary cloths. Each would become wet, then worn, then unrecognizable. The power did not replace them with something everlasting.

One evening the inhabitant brought a stone to the iron steps.

It descended into the pool, held the stone underwater, and turned it between both hands. The healed fingers spread without opening.

The power sat on the upper tread.

After a while the inhabitant lifted the stone and placed it beside them.

They held it in the water as they had seen the creature do. Its corners were almost smooth. There was a shallow place on one face where a thumb settled comfortably.

When they offered it back, the inhabitant did not take it at once.

They remained on the steps until the light left the pale stones at the bottom.

Behind them the motor continued to run. The familiar knock came, and drops loosened from the leaves. Beneath the grove, warm water passed through the hollow and went on into the dark.
