The power did not first touch the animal.

It attended to the water.

There were things in the runoff that the animal could detect long before the folds closed: substances arriving from a cleaned market stall, a leaking container, a drain into which someone had poured what they did not want to carry upstairs. Some passed through the court without visible consequence. Others left a narrow band of injured tissue beneath the plinth.

The power followed each of these back to its source.

This took it into kitchens, storerooms, a delivery yard, the cramped office of a market supervisor. It did not accuse anyone. A container acquired a lid that fitted. A waste collection began arriving on the day it was supposed to arrive. Someone who had been told to rinse a floor as quickly as possible was given enough time to use a bucket.

The changes were ordinary enough to be overlooked.

Below the street, the animal opened its folds a little sooner after rain.

The power watched this, but did not take it as permission to proceed with everything else.

It was easy to imagine a better court. Cool air, clear water, clean walls, daylight spread across the floor. Easy, too, to forget what lived in the warmth and dirt. The animal had grown around the court’s deficiencies until some of them were part of its shelter. A repaired joint might deprive it of water. A lowered temperature might alter what its body could do with food. The removal of an apparently useless concrete plinth would expose most of it at once.

So the power learned the valves.

It learned which pipe was hot because it needed to be hot, which was hot because insulation had failed, and which leak could be stopped only after another supply had been found. It listened through the pump strokes. It followed the dark channels inside the animal without forcing them open, and found that even this did not tell it everything.

There were connections whose use changed with the water level. There were folds that closed before the surrounding tissue registered danger. There were places where an injury was answered at once across the whole body, and others where the answer travelled slowly, or not at all.

Behind the slab, a pale length lay close to another without joining it.

The power left them as it found them.

On the next maintenance visit, the worker who usually scraped the inspection cover brought a second lamp. He had brought it because the fixed one had begun to flicker, but with its light held low he saw the cleared band differently.

The thick outer edge was not hardened sealant. A soft fringe lay beneath it, closed tightly against the chamber.

He kept the scraper in his hand for a while.

Then he called the other worker over.

They crouched beside the cover. One touched the pale material with the wooden end of a brush. The fringe withdrew.

After that, they marked its position in chalk.

The power did not put words into either person’s mouth. It had made the faulty lamp fail on a morning when there was time to fetch a replacement, rather than halfway through an urgent repair. The looking, and the decision to look again, belonged to them.

There were photographs. Most were poor. A ruler laid alongside an exposed lobe gave the images a resemblance to pictures of damaged masonry, except that in the second photograph the lobe had moved.

A request went upstairs.

It was returned once, asking whether the material was hazardous.

No one knew.

For a time, this uncertainty nearly resulted in the court being dosed with something intended for fungus. The power attended to that, too: the unanswered call, the form with no suitable category, the person trying to make a decision from a blurred image while three other matters waited.

The dose was postponed.

A woman came down with sample jars and stayed through two pump cycles. She did not take the animal from the wall. She collected a piece already loose beneath a mineral crust, and some water, and the crust itself.

When she left, she asked that any living scraps be kept damp in a separate covered tray, not discarded and not tipped into another drain. She could not yet say whether each scrap could become an animal. She thought it best not to find out by accident.

The workers were pleased to have an instruction that agreed with something they already knew.

The brick beside the walkway was harder.

The power could have put it back. Nothing in the court would have stopped it.

Instead it attended to the route from the cabinet: the same narrow crossing, the same pause on exposed concrete, the return the following spring. It could not tell whether the animal was seeking shelter it remembered, following a persistent trace, or responding to something still beneath the floor.

It set the brick near the old place, with one edge raised on two small pieces of ceramic.

Underneath was darkness, wet grit, and room for a thin body to enter without carrying the brick’s weight.

For eleven days, nothing happened.

A rat stepped onto it and used it to reach a pipe.

On the twelfth night a growing end emerged from the cabinet crack. It passed the nearer corner, followed a bead of water along the ceramic, and disappeared beneath the brick.

The power did not count this as gratitude.

It watched whether the animal stayed.

By the next maintenance visit, a pale margin showed along the sheltered underside. The workers saw it and moved their boots around the brick.

The spring bolt hole remained as it was.

Here the power had less certainty than it wished. The repeated loss of the projection was plain. What drew it into the hole was not.

It let down a thread of water beside the opening, enough to keep the surrounding clay damp but not enough to fill the hole. Nearby, beneath a broken fixing plate, it opened a shallow wet passage.

The animal entered both.

As the summer dried the clay, the projection in the bolt hole darkened at its tip. The power kept the water available. It did not hold the tissue alive by force or draw it out.

That year the animal abandoned less of the projection.

The next year it abandoned more.

The power stayed with the difficulty.

Not all its work was below ground.

One of the maintenance workers had a cough that worsened after an hour in the court. The extractor fan could be repaired without cooling the water beneath the plinth, but only if the work was paid for, and only if someone was allowed to spend more than the customary hour doing it.

There was money set aside for the repair. There was time on the rota. The replacement fan moved air above the walkway rather than driving it across the wet floor.

The stair rail became bearable.

The animal still tightened when the pumps began.

A screen was fitted upstream to catch the larger market waste before it reached the sump. It had an accessible handle and a place where it could be emptied without a worker leaning over hot water. The animal’s folds received less food for a while. Then the settled silt, larvae, and small drifting matter became enough again.

The power watched its margins through that lean interval. Where growth stopped, it waited. Where tissue began to thin, it made the change more slowly.

No one filled the court with grass. No one opened the vault to the sky.

At the shaft, the few long blades continued to lean toward daylight. The weed flowered once, poorly. Cockroaches occupied the cabinet hinges. Rats kept their ledge, though with less market waste coming down there were fewer of them.

The court remained warm and wet, with broad bare surfaces between its crowded edges.

Over several years the animal changed shape.

A passage beneath the stair grew thicker. One lobe withdrew from a place where a bearing had to be reached, and was not allowed to spread back across it. Along the wall, a sheltered channel gave it another route. Sometimes it used that route. Sometimes it stopped short for months.

The inspection cover was still lifted. The pumps were still serviced. Where the animal could not be spared a cut, the cut was made narrowly, with a clean tool, and the loose tissue was caught before it fell.

The power could have ended all of this negotiation.

It could have carried the animal into a chamber made precisely for it, supplied every fold, guarded every growing end. But it did not know what else it would be carrying away from: the pressure of the pump through concrete, the traces in the water, the routes it had laid down over years, whatever persisted in the threaded hole.

Nor did it know that staying was always better.

So it made a refuge beside the unfinished wall, connected by a damp passage to the existing drains. There were shallow hollows, rough undersides, warm water without detergent. The refuge was neither a trap nor the only safe place left.

A narrow end entered it in autumn.

By winter, two folds had opened there.

Most of the animal remained beneath the pump plinth.

The power continued its attention: to the refuge, to the plinth, to the workers climbing down with tools. It kept no demand waiting at the end of its care.

One morning, as the pump fell quiet, a worker rested his lamp on the floor to oil a bearing. Its beam passed beneath the returned brick. Through the thin edge of the lobe there, he could see dark channels branching and joining, with water moving between the transparent folds.

He shifted the lamp slightly so it would not shine directly on them.

Then he went on with the bearing.
