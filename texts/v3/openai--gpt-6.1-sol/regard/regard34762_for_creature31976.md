For several evenings, the power did nothing that anyone could have distinguished from attention.

It watched the first roof water turned away from a cistern. It watched a worker stand in a basin until the fibers beneath its feet had regained their firmness. It watched two neighbors argue over shade, and noticed that neither proposed cutting down the small tree whose shade was in dispute.

At the work court, an old inhabitant was teaching a younger one to fit a wooden gate. One of the old one’s hands had recently been cut back. The replacement tips were strong enough to hold a wedge, but did not yet meet evenly.

Three times the wedge slipped.

The younger person reached to take it. The old one made a low sound, not loud, and the younger person withdrew its hand.

On the fourth attempt, the wedge held.

The power paid particular attention to this.

***

When it made itself known, it chose the hollow sounding stone beneath the work-court roof.

Someone had just struck the note that meant the lower channel would be closed for cleaning. After the note faded, another sounded.

There had been no blow.

The workers stopped. A person carrying a tray set it down. Another looked beneath the stone, where there was only the familiar shallow cavity and some accumulated dust.

The stone sounded again, then spoke.

Its first words were poorly pitched. Everyone understood them, but several found them unpleasant.

“Is this a place where a stranger may ask a question?”

An inhabitant with an old yellow band answered.

“It is a place where a stranger may wait until the channel has been closed.”

The power waited.

When the workers returned, there were more of them. Someone had fetched the keeper of the cistern accounts. Two children stood behind a grinding stone, plainly visible to everyone except, perhaps, themselves.

“What is your question?” asked the person in the yellow band.

“What is difficult here that you would willingly have made easier?”

They did not answer at once.

Then they answered all together.

The reservoir detour. A roof that would not stop leaking. An aching supporting limb. The lack of good soil on the farther shelf. A household that always opened its gate before the signal. The tedious crushing of one particularly tough root.

The keeper of the cistern accounts waited until the others had finished.

“In a long dry interval,” it said, “we have enough water to stand in and not enough to drink.”

“May I examine the water?”

“You may examine some that is brought to you.”

A glazed cup was placed on the stone.

***

The power could have filled every cistern at once.

Instead, it asked for a second cup.

It put nothing into the first. From the second it removed what the inhabitants’ delicate tissues could not endure. A small pale residue remained beside the cup.

The cistern keeper dipped a strip of testing fiber, waited, and examined it against the light.

“Again,” it said.

They brought water from a deeper well.

Then they brought water from a basin used by tired workers. The power made that clean too, but the keeper shook its head.

“We would not drink that.”

“Then neither need you.”

For nine days they tested. They gave the cleaned water first to cuttings, then to tender garden growth. They kept jars covered and uncovered. They asked whether the residue was dangerous, whether the process changed with the warmth of the water, whether it would fail without warning.

The power answered what it knew and demonstrated what could be demonstrated.

At last, the town agreed to a small covered basin beside the work court.

Water entered through an ordinary wooden gate. Inside, a dark stone the size of a bowl separated the troublesome minerals from it. The residue gathered in a shallow removable tray. When the tray was full, the flow stopped.

There was no secret handle. The cover could be lifted. The inlet could be shut.

The first basin was made too low for an inhabitant whose body had grown around three supporting limbs. They dismantled the rim and built it again.

The power did not call this ingratitude.

***

After that, people brought other requests.

Some it answered with work. Some with instruction. Some required questions the petitioners had not expected.

A person asked for its dead companion back.

The power listened while it described the companion’s body, its voice, the small name used only at home. The description continued long after it was sufficient to identify a grave.

At last the power said, “I can help you preserve what you remember. I cannot make your remembering into the person who is gone.”

The inhabitant remained beside the stone until the court emptied.

No tree flowered out of season. No voice came from the soil beds.

The next morning, it returned with a jar of little clay pieces. On each it had begun to mark something the companion had known about tending roots.

The power helped it make the marks legible.

***

The old worker with the new hand came later.

“Can you make these meet?” it asked, holding out its uneven tips.

“Yes.”

“Would they still feel what they touch?”

“Yes.”

“Would I have to learn them again?”

“Somewhat.”

The worker considered.

“Only the two on this side, then.”

The change was small. It took place while the hand rested in clean damp fiber, with the worker awake and watching. The power left the old scar untouched because it had not been asked to remove it.

That afternoon the worker fitted a gate.

The wedge slipped once.

It laughed, and continued.

***

Across the valley, pale sheets still rose beside the distant masonry.

The nearer town asked for a crossing. The farther settlement, when consulted, asked first whether a crossing would bring the nearer town’s water rules with it.

There followed a considerable exchange of signals.

The power made no bridge during the argument. It helped them devise larger marks on their signaling sheets, so that questions could be distinguished from invitations and warnings from refusals. Later, at both shelves’ request, it steadied two landing places and taught the stoneworkers how to recognize the fractures that would not bear a load.

The crossing itself took longer.

Some people continued to prefer signaling.

***

There were things the power never added.

No birds to supply the silence with a different sound. No insects to occupy the untouched leaves. No wind simply because the washing hung still.

The town had not asked to become another place.

In the evenings, the new basin gave a little click when its tray needed emptying. Otherwise, its sound was lost among the channels.

People still complained about the detour. The beautiful jar still leaked, though its maker had begun to admit that it might. Children still had to be called back into the light.

And beneath the work-court roof, the sounding stone was usually only a stone.

Anyone could strike it.

The power answered when invited, and sometimes its answer was a question.
