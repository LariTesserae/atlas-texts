The power first learned how to arrive without making the ceiling dim.

This took some care. It was accustomed to being perceived, and perception here was a disturbance: a warmth without a source, a pressure through the ledge, a brightness gathering where no brightness ought to gather.

For several mornings, the animal beneath the rosette folded its forelimbs against its mouth and did not feed.

The power withdrew.

When it returned, it came as nothing the animal had to stop for.

It watched a grain being tested and rejected. It watched another grain lifted, turned, and set against the stone. It watched the forelimbs draw inward.

The scrape was very small.

The power had moved mountains before. It stayed for the scrape.

***

At first, there seemed to be many things to give.

More water. More food. A stronger plate for the old animal whose shortened leg slipped on the ledge. A ceiling that remained warm through the night. Stones already shaped to fit the forelimbs.

It considered these things without doing them.

Water was also a boundary and a weight. Food was alive, and would grow according to its own appetites. The chamber’s darkness belonged to creatures it had not yet found. A grain made perfectly suitable for one grip might be useless to another.

There were eggs beneath the leaves. There were lives beneath the floor.

So it learned the hollow through a season of rain, and then a season without it.

It learned which roots merely looked dry. It learned where the pale larvae waited. It learned that the old grazer rested for a long time but usually ate enough, and that the young sometimes carried stones because they had not yet discovered what made carrying worthwhile.

It did not cure that discovery.

***

The first thing it changed was outside.

Far above the translucent ceiling, a heavy animal had begun to use the loose stone as a place to scratch. Each visit shifted it a little. The crack widened. Grit fell into the chamber.

The power settled the stone into a sounder seat.

It left the entrance open. Rain could still enter, and air could still pass. On the grassland, a second stone rose by less than the breadth of a hoof, in a place where scratching against it would be easier.

The animal found it the following afternoon.

Nothing below noticed.

***

Its next act concerned an adult grazer caught in water.

The grazer had gone around the lowered edge of a leaf and stepped onto a wet slope. Now it floated with its back curved upward, four legs drawn together beneath it. Its forelimbs opened and closed.

The power could have lifted it.

Instead, it brought the edge of a loose grain against one foot.

The foot caught. The animal pulled, lost its purchase, caught again. For a while nothing happened except effort.

Then its belly touched stone.

It climbed clear and remained motionless until the water on its legs had thinned. Later, it fed.

The power left the grain where it was.

But during the next rain, it watched that place closely. Two more animals slipped there. One caught the grain. The other floated past it.

This one the power lifted.

It set the animal down beneath the leaf it had been trying to reach, and waited until its legs unfolded.

Thereafter, along that short slope, the limestone bore a few shallow irregularities. They were no higher than the ridges already present elsewhere in the chamber. They did not dam the water or keep the waterline animals from their margin.

A divided foot could hold them.

Over the rains that followed, the power watched for consequences it had failed to imagine.

***

During the dry season, a root beneath the smallest rosette began to die.

Its dampness retreated toward the wall. The film thinned. Two grazers left, then a third. Beneath a leaf, three eggs remained.

The power followed the root into darkness.

There it found a fracture through which the root had once reached moisture. A shift in the deeper earth had closed it.

It opened the fracture just enough.

No spring appeared. No pool spread across the ledge. By morning, the root had become a little darker, and a place beneath it that had been dry left dampness on the foot of a passing grazer.

The grazer stopped.

It lowered its mouth.

For several days, the power attended to the root. When the moisture continued without its help, it went back to watching the rest of the chamber.

***

It did not make the animals larger.

It did not put words into the brief touch of their forelimbs, or teach them to keep the stones they had finished using.

There might be more in that touch than it understood. There might be less. Neither possibility made the animals unfinished.

It watched their hesitations, their refusals, the routes each learned and sometimes forgot. When one moved away from a grain placed conveniently beside it, the power let it go.

Once, the old grazer spent nearly a morning trying to lift a stone that had lodged in a seam.

The power loosened it.

The animal lifted it, tested its corner, and dropped it at once.

A little farther along the root, it found another.

***

Years passed above the hollow.

The power prevented a poison from reaching the grassland roots. It turned aside a deep cut that would have broken the thin ceiling. When a season grew dangerously hot, it kept watch on the damp ledges, making no change until the remaining moisture began to fail.

Most days it did nothing.

The hollow remained bright, sparse, and quiet. Snails descended with their fringes open. Leaves lowered at noon. The ring of minute animals followed the withdrawing pool.

The old grazer eventually died beneath its familiar rosette. Its body fed small lives the power had only lately learned to see.

Nearby, a young animal found a grain with a rough corner.

It turned the grain once.

There was room beneath the leaf. The root was damp. No great shadow crossed the ceiling.

The animal decided to lift it.
