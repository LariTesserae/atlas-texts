The power first notices the strip of ice.

There are larger failures in the basin. A bearing above the western gantry has worn into an oval. Three of the chambers beneath the eastern floor are filling with water. The intake screens take almost everything small enough to pass through them and deliver it to a place where pressure does the killing.

But the strip of ice lies between a living animal and the next place it might live.

The power attends to it.

It does not arrive as a light. The fold would withdraw from light. It does not speak through the kiosk’s remaining loudspeaker, which has enough power to make a noise but not enough to make a useful one.

For several cycles, nothing happens that a worker could distinguish from ordinary operation.

The power follows the warmth through the cabinet. It learns which part of the fold is feeding, which part bears weight, which old injury prevents a crease from opening completely. It follows the shallow stream under the door and the circuit beneath the raised walk. It attends to the small organisms in the water, to the roots caught in a drain farther downstream, to the pressure that keeps the upper galleries cold.

The pumps serve places beyond the basin. Some of those places are inhabited. Stopping them would make the silence here longer and the lives elsewhere shorter.

The power leaves them running.

It cannot find a clean division between the basin and its failures. The leaking joints feed the felt gardens. A bowed panel shelters a colony of crustaceans. The kiosk’s failing insulation has sustained the large fold for years.

It studies these things without confusing dependence with safety.

At the next bell, a little heat reaches the underside of the ice.

Not enough to open a broad wet path. That would cool rapidly when the refrigeration galleries discharged, making a worse obstruction by the following cycle. The heat travels instead along a narrow scratch in the panel. The scratch has been there since installation, hidden under cloudy layers of successive spills.

Water enters it.

When the fold extends its leading pleat, it finds a surface to which it can adhere.

It advances less than a centimeter.

The power lets that be the distance.

***

The warm joint under the raised walk already supports another fold.

This one is small, dark, and carrying a curl of blue plastic. It occupies the sheltered side. During the quiet interval it loosens, then closes again without releasing any young.

The power does not remove it to make room.

Instead, it repairs a connection farther along the joint. A cracked ceramic sleeve has allowed most of the heat to escape into a sealed cavity. The sleeve settles together. Warmth spreads through another handspan of metal, then another.

A resting place becomes two resting places.

There is no sudden abundance. The food films take time to grow. The small fold continues to feed where it has always fed. White crustaceans begin exploring the newly warmed seam.

Outside the kiosk, the larger animal reaches the first bolt.

It opens around the head and holds there through the return of the machinery. One exposed edge trembles under the falling water. Behind it, much of its body remains threaded around the base of the cabinet.

The power keeps the damaged circuit alive.

Not forever. For now.

***

A maintenance worker comes to the kiosk carrying a meter, a lamp, and a scraper warmed inside her coat.

She has come because the schematic has changed.

For years the display has shown proposed channels with the same pale lines it uses for existing ones. Now one line is interrupted. Beside it, in the old maintenance script, a word appears:

UNCONFIRMED.

She tests the floor outside before stepping on it.

Inside, the lower cabinet is wet. She finds the fold’s gray edge at the threshold and follows its extent with her lamp held low, taking care not to illuminate the underside.

There is too much animal behind the lining for her tray.

She kneels. Her knee makes a dry sound against the ceramic grit.

The power gives her no revelation. It does not put tenderness into her as though she were another circuit to repair. She has brought the scraper herself.

But when she tests the failing cable, the meter shows the fault clearly. When she checks the supply beneath the walk, the reading is steady. The light on her helmet catches the narrow wet route between them.

She stays longer than her assignment requires.

Before leaving, she fixes a short length of spare cable cover over the coarse replacement composite. Its underside has shallow ribs. She seats one end against the bolt and the other beneath the walk, then wets it with basin water.

On the maintenance report she writes:

OCCUPIED. DO NOT REMOVE LINING. TEMPORARY CROSSING INSTALLED.

There is no box for the last part. She writes it in the margin.

***

The power continues its work elsewhere.

It gives the intake screens a slower approach current along one edge, where small animals can find purchase before the water draws them through. It does this gradually, checking the strain on the pumps and the level in the channels. A few young folds settle there. Most still pass beyond the screens.

It makes the temperature indicators outside the pump houses truthful.

It restores a corroded latch on a shelter door that has been difficult to open during the cold-air discharge.

It leaves the bells alone.

Whatever happens in their interval is woven through more lives than the power first understood. Water climbs. Reeds close. Adults open or remain shut. The power watches broods carried into bolt holes that no diagram records.

Where it cannot yet judge an alteration, it waits.

Where the same injury has happened many times, it does not call that injury inevitable merely because the reports have acquired a box for it.

Warm scrapers become easier to find. The rack beside the service entrance has a functioning heater again. An inspection instruction, recovered from an older archive, includes the twenty-minute waiting period that was omitted when the forms were shortened.

Some workers follow it. Some still hurry.

The power keeps attending.

***

The large fold takes nine bells to reach the cable cover.

On the tenth, it does not advance.

On the eleventh, one pleat extends beneath the raised walk. The blue-plastic fold contracts at the disturbance, then opens again when the vibration passes.

For several more cycles the large animal occupies both places. Behind the cabinet, feeding threads remain in the damp. Beneath the walk, a new leading edge thickens and its shallow pits begin to open.

The power holds the old circuit steady until the last attachment releases.

There is a soft dragging sound inside the empty kiosk.

Then the damaged cable cools.

The schematic remains lit on a separate supply. Pale lines move across it. Some are marked as measured, some as projected, and some remain unconfirmed.

Under the walk, the fold gathers itself beside the warm joint. The brass screw is concealed in an outer crease. The red fibers lie flat. The paint fragment bearing the word KEEP rests against the metal, where no one passing above can see it.

At the next bell, water reaches the sheltered seam.

The animal loosens.

Between two pleats, an amber swelling becomes visible.
