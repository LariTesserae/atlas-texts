The power first noticed the hollow by its sound.

Not because the sound was loud enough to cross the salt field. It did not cross it. The stone’s upper face was silent beneath the traveling heat.

But the power could hear things where they were.

It listened beneath the leaf, inside the clear egg, in the brightness between the hunter’s combs and the green shell. It heard the wooden depth gathered around the little bowl. It did not separate the great note from the smaller ones to discover which mattered most.

Then it looked.

Its looking cast no light. Nothing in the chamber acquired a shadow it had not possessed before.

The power knew a thousand ways to bring water into a dry place. It could have drawn a spring through the orange crystal, softened the salt, raised the stone on pillars, made the five islands into a garden broad enough to walk through.

It considered none of these for long.

Water would have entered the tiny cavities beneath the leaves. The mineral plates would have shifted. The chamber’s dry warmth belonged to the creatures as closely as their skins did.

So it came no nearer by those means.

It attended instead to the builder beneath the bowl.

There was clay in the fingers. There was a scar beside the mouth. One finger was shorter than the others.

The power could have lengthened that finger.

Before doing so, it followed the finger’s place against the breast, the groove receiving it, the pressure borne through the wrist and shoulder. It attended to the scar with equal care. Neither feature told it, by itself, what the builder wanted.

The power left them as they were.

For a while—though there was no while inside the hollow—it sought some way to make itself known.

A voice would have laid another sound across the chamber’s sounds. A face at the entrance would have filled much of the available sky. Even a tiny sign beside the bowl would have become a new object, with weight and edges and a place the builder had not made for it.

The power did not wish to arrive as an unavoidable fact.

It found, at last, a way to be available.

This was not a sixth island, nor another creature in the powder. It occupied no cavity. It touched no mineral face.

If any inhabitant’s attention reached outward, the power would be there to meet it.

No attention was drawn outward to accomplish this. The builder continued to attend to the seed’s contact with the clay. The eye within the egg remained clear. Beneath the crimson-backed leaf, the outer animals held their shelter around the smaller body.

The power received no greeting.

It did not count that as failure.

Above the hollow, the afternoon advanced. Light withdrew from the grooves in the stone. Far away, wind lifted a white veil from the salt.

The power examined the stone from above. It learned its weight, its balance, the soundness of the narrow lip. It looked for anything already bearing down upon the chamber: a fracture extending toward it, a buried pressure, water seeking a seam.

There was nothing requiring its hand.

It could have made the stone impossible to move. Yet another living being might someday come across the field and look beneath it, as the power itself had looked. Curiosity was not a calamity. Nor could the power know that every encounter would be unwanted.

It left the stone movable.

What it undertook was narrower.

It would remember where the hollow was. It would watch without making the place conspicuous. If a foot were about to crush the lip, or a careless hand to flood the chamber, it could intervene at the foot or the hand. It need not remake the inhabitants in order to spare them an injury.

And if someone came gently, it would not stand between them.

Having settled this, the power returned its attention beneath the bowl.

The black seed rested against the curved clay over its very small area. The builder’s foremost fingers lay open against its breast. Its feet took the weight of its body. Close by, the crystal tool rested upon the fracture running under two islands of roots.

The power had once thought, upon first hearing the chamber, that it might be called upon to release something.

Now it could find no captive.

It remained willing to be mistaken. Its strength did not make the builder’s wishes easier to read, and its benevolence did not give it ownership of the little body.

So it kept its promise without requiring an answer.

Night passed across the salt field. The stone cooled on its upper face.

Beneath it, the hollow sounded.

Nothing had been healed to a shape it had not asked for. Nothing had been awakened out of its own life. The small builder remained beside its carefully made bowl, attending to the seed.

And beyond the chamber’s exact, crowded bounds, help was available without pressing to be used.
