The power does not bring light.

It attends first to the way the animal receives the place: the pressure against its left side, the warmth beneath its foot, the faint substances arriving from the colony above. The darkness is not an absence that troubles it. A brighter basin would give it little and take shelter from many things.

Nor does the power make the water still. Stillness would close the feeding crowns.

For a long interval, nothing changes because of its attention.

The animal rests. Its foot makes small adjustments against the stone. A grain of shell has lodged beneath one edge, and eventually that part of the foot lifts and settles farther along. The crowns on its back continue feeding. The crab from the old arch finds another joint.

The power follows the spring down through the foundations.

There are passages here that have not carried much water for centuries. Some are packed with mud. Others remain open, narrow spaces between layers of construction, their walls lined with animals that live on what little passes through. The spring is changing because mineral deposits have nearly sealed one of its deeper routes. Water presses against the obstruction, finds another weakness, and rises beneath the growing wall.

There is no single correct course to restore. The earlier course was not permanent either.

The power leaves the spring alone.

Instead, it attends to the shallow recess and the animal resting in it. Fresh tubes will grow down from the rim. The animal’s body will grow more slowly. There will be many departures before the return on which it can no longer enter.

That return need not be prevented. There are other shelters.

But behind the recess, beyond a thickness of dead foundation, lies a chamber with only one small opening. Water moves through it too slowly to make it useful. Along its floor is an old fracture, interrupted by a knob of limestone no larger than one of the animal’s plates.

The power loosens the knob.

It does this while the animal is away feeding, and while no smaller body occupies the fracture. The stone settles into mud below. A few grains rise, then disperse.

Water begins to pass.

At first the change is too slight to reach the avenue. A worm extends farther from its hole. The living film along the chamber’s floor takes on a different balance of organisms. Over several pulses, fine sediment leaves through the old opening. The fracture becomes a low passage, rough at one end and broad enough at the other for the animal to turn.

The power does not hurry its discovery.

On the animal’s next return, one long feeler enters the recess and bends toward the new flow. The head follows a little way. The shoulders meet stone. It withdraws, feeds along the threshold, and settles where it settled before.

Several returns later, it tries the other angle.

This time its head passes through. Its foot finds the floor beyond. The middle of its body gathers against the opening, then lengthens into the chamber. The occupied rear plates scrape the rim. A few feeding crowns close.

Inside, the animal explores two walls and part of the ceiling. It rests briefly, then comes out again.

The chamber is not warmer than its usual shelter. It is not richer in food. For now, it is simply another place it can reach.

The power attends to the colony joining the rear plates.

The ridge has begun to pull against the living edges beneath it. Each bend places a little more strain there. The animal has sought low ceilings twice, dragging its back against them without finding the right purchase.

The power does not strip the plates clean.

During a later passage beneath an arch, the stone gives the ridge something firm to catch against. The animal pushes forward. An old seam parts through the colony’s foundation.

The pressure eases. Both portions remain attached, each now carried on plates that can move separately. A small fragment falls to the avenue. A shrimp turns it over.

There are other places to attend to.

In the circular room, the large animal has been receiving enough food to live, but less with each season. It repeatedly reaches toward the passage through which it entered when young. Its feelers pass through. Its head follows. The thickened plates stop against the same worn edges.

The power examines the room before altering it. There are eggs beneath a ledge, crabs in the upper joints, filter-feeders along the animal’s back. A sudden opening would draw water hard across them.

It removes a little dead stone at a time.

Days pass between the changes. The current strengthens gradually. The animal spends longer at the entrance, working its foot against surfaces it has touched many times before.

At last it passes through.

It travels less than its own length into the adjoining court and stops beneath a roof. Its fringe lifts in water coming from two directions. On its back, the filter-feeders open.

The room behind it remains inhabited.

Across the basin, the power continues in this way: learning before acting, leaving much untouched. It does not hold up every failing wall. It does not remove the predators or keep every egg alive. The settlement is made of lives that feed upon other lives, and changes too closely joined to be sorted into damage and repair from a distance.

Still, there are burdens it can ease without laying a greater burden elsewhere. It looks for them.

Under the growing wall, the first animal returns after a feeding pulse. The familiar recess has narrowed. It presses its head beneath the rim, pauses, and draws back.

One feeler turns toward the faint current through the foundation.

The animal follows. Its rear plates clear the opening. Inside the chamber, it spreads its foot against the stone, leaving space along one side through which the water continues to move.

For a while it rests.

Then its mouth finds a strip of film along the floor, and it begins to feed.
