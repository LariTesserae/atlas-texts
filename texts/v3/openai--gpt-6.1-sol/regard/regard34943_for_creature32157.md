The power did not first appear at the gate.

It came to rest against one of the reservoir’s square legs, where a root had entered a crack and thickened without breaking it wider. For a time it attended to the weight above, the root within, and the water moving through the ground below.

It could have lifted the reservoir. It could have drawn the district dry.

Instead, it waited through a complete cycle of the pumps.

Then another.

In the second cycle, a pale grip appeared beneath the lowest rung of a maintenance ladder. It stayed there through the reversal, tightening when the water struck the buried turn.

The power noticed that the grip was not bracing against the current. It was holding something on the other side of the wall.

There was much it did not yet understand.

***

For several days, nothing changed that anyone could have named.

The bell sounded. The sluices rose. Warm water returned from beneath the street. A worker replaced a length of cracked insulation and found his wrench occupied when he reached for it.

He tapped twice beside the handle.

The dark edge withdrew.

The power learned from this.

When it wished to make itself known, it chose a handrail at the washhouse steps. It touched the metal twice, with a pause between the touches, then waited.

A fold lying against the third dry step lifted slightly.

The power did not reach toward it.

Through the rail, it offered only the small, steady pressure of something remaining nearby.

The fold approached along the joint between two tiles. Its underside opened around a rusted bolt. For a while, both held the same rail.

Then the creature went down into the water.

The power stayed until it had gone.

***

The first intervention concerned a bearing.

It was inside the eastern pumping house, in a motor whose housing had been painted so often that the identification plate was nearly unreadable. The creature had been lying beside it for three afternoons, raised on its grips above the hot metal.

The bearing would fail during the next heavy rain.

The failure itself would be repairable. The sudden stop would not. Water driven back through an old service passage would strike a loose plate, and behind that plate were roots, sediment, and several translucent folds that had not yet learned to hold.

The power could have made the bearing whole.

Instead, while the morning worker stood beside the motor, the vibration became briefly clear through the handle of his screwdriver. Not louder. Less confused by all the other trembling in the room.

He stopped.

He put the screwdriver down, fetched the listening rod, and pressed its end to the housing.

By noon the motor was shut off. By evening the bearing lay on a rag beside the door.

During the repair, the creature remained in the channel outside. The worker saw it and laid a wet board across the hot sill so that it would have somewhere to rest.

This was not the power’s doing.

The power took note of it nevertheless.

***

Benevolence required more knowledge than strength.

The old pipes were not merely abandoned. Some held water through the hours when the newer system emptied. One, capped at both ends except for a narrow fracture, warmed slowly each morning and cooled slowly each night. Around it the creature grew heavy. In another passage, a leaking coupling supplied the roots of a tree whose planter appeared to have no adequate source of water.

The power followed these small provisions until it knew why certain repairs had been followed by long absences.

It learned, too, which leaks weakened walls, which stagnant pockets had become foul, which hot surfaces made even the creature hold itself away.

There was no single improvement that would serve the whole district.

When the engineers next surveyed the buried passages, their instruments worked unusually well. They found the cavities they had missed. They found the failing supports. They found that several supposedly useless pipes were still carrying part of the district’s water.

Their first plan called for filling those pipes with concrete.

A maintenance worker read it twice.

“No,” she said, and took them below.

She showed them the roots. She showed them a pale fold pressed into the side of a joint, and waited while they watched it lift at the passage of water.

The revised plan took longer.

Some pipes were replaced. Some were braced. Two were left intact, with inspection covers fitted above them. Beside a passage that had to be closed, the workers built another, rough-walled and low, and filled it before they opened the old one.

For six days the creature rested against the sealed entrance.

On the seventh, a grip appeared inside the new passage.

The power did not mistake this for gratitude.

***

At the western wall, it was especially careful.

It could feel the effort in the porous brick: the slow gathering below, the tightening, the release. Water rose through spaces too narrow for anything with an ordinary outline. The stone cups received it.

Whether this was nourishment, reproduction, labor, or something for which no observer had yet made a word, the power did not decide.

It placed no hand against the creature while the ascent was underway.

There was, however, a fracture behind the uppermost cup. A wedge of masonry had begun to loosen. If it fell, the cup would go with it, along with a strip of wall and the roots established there.

The power held the wedge in place until a worker came up the adjacent stair.

He noticed a line of fresh grit on the landing. Looking above it, he saw the crack.

The repair was awkward. They could not empty the wall, and ordinary mortar would not set properly in its damp. The first mixture failed. The second held. They worked from the sides, leaving the porous band clear.

For three afternoons afterward, no water rose.

On the fourth, the lowest cup overflowed.

By evening all the cups were running.

***

The power did not bring birds merely because there were none.

It did not turn the creature into something that could walk across the bright road. It did not give it a voice that workers would recognize as speech.

It attended to the thresholds.

The control room’s fan was repaired. The person who worked there needed air moving over her face. The dry floor remained dry.

Outside the room, beneath the sill, there was space for a narrow ledge. A worker fitted one while repairing the drain, using a piece of ceramic too warped for the paving. Water reached it without entering the room.

Sometimes the creature rested there.

Sometimes it did not.

At the gate, a blocked gutter was cleared. The wet strip now continued a little farther, beneath the shade of the outer wall. The creature followed it one evening, stopped before the shade ended, and remained there through the starting of a pump.

Then it returned.

No one pursued it.

***

There were limits to what the power could learn by waiting.

The portions beneath the paving did not yield a count. Contact traveled through some passages and stopped at others. A fold might receive a tremor that another ignored. What seemed like an injury on one afternoon was gone on the next, though perhaps the same portion had not returned.

The power kept these uncertainties.

When a drain had to be opened, it gave warning through the nearby masonry and waited for withdrawal. When there was no response, it waited longer. If a delay would endanger a worker, it helped the worker first, as gently as it could toward whatever else was present.

Not every disturbance could be avoided.

But disturbances could be made smaller, and less frequent, and easier to recover from.

***

One evening, after the square had drained, the creature carried something to the washhouse steps.

It was the failed bearing from the eastern pump. Someone had left it in a scrap bin beside the workshop. Most of its grease had washed away.

The creature settled around the central opening.

For several minutes its pale underside pressed into the space where the shaft had turned. Then it dragged the bearing beside a pipe collar of nearly the same diameter.

The power was resting against the handrail.

It did nothing.

The creature moved the bearing farther from the collar, then closer again.

Water dripped from the gantry. Behind the steamed glass, the repaired fan turned. A worker crossed the square and glanced toward the steps before continuing home.

At the next opening of the sluice, the creature went beneath the gantry.

Water lifted its edges.

It held, as it always had, until the flow was strong. Then it released its grips and traveled with the rush, farther this time than before.

Below the paving, the pressure changed. The repaired bearing turned smoothly in its housing. The old pipes filled.

For once, there was nothing urgent to attend to.

The power remained nearby while the creature made its way back against the current and took hold again.
