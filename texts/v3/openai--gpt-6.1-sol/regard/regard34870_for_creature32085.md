The power noticed the stem first.

It lay across the animal’s forepaws, too long to carry easily and too green to be useful yet. The animal turned it, bit away a damaged leaf, and set it down where the sun would reach it. Then it went back to the cleft.

The power remained.

It had no need to make itself small in order to enter the hollow. Nevertheless, it attended to small things: the water caught in the joint of a leaf, the place where the woman’s cord had been shortened twice, the blind end of a passage the animal still sometimes investigated. It followed the warmth downward through the moss. It found eggs, sleeping larvae, roots pressed flat between stones.

For several days it did nothing that could have been distinguished from weather.

It learned where the water went.

Beneath the shallow pool, the green-stained seam divided. One branch supplied the plants along the cliff. The other ended in a pocket of rubble, where the water lingered until a hard freeze broke another grain from the surrounding rock. Over years, this pocket would enlarge toward the shelter.

The power could have closed it. That would have sent more water toward the plants, and eventually loosened the thin shelf on which they grew.

Instead, it followed the seam farther inward. There was another way down, narrow but continuous, through sound rock.

During the next thaw, a grain shifted.

Water entered the new passage slowly enough that the pool did not fall. The green line along the cliff remained green. Beneath the shelter, the rubble began to dry.

The animal found the difference before anyone else. A ledge it had rejected twice no longer smelled damp. It stood with its forefeet on the lip, listening, then carried in three mouthfuls of cured leaves.

The power did not make this its only store.

***

Below the mountain, the woman was mending a binding by the door of a house that was not hers.

Her knee hurt. She had been sitting too long, and the binding needed to be finished before the light failed. She drew the thread tight with her teeth, then rubbed the joint with the heel of her hand.

The power attended to her as carefully as it had attended to the seam.

There was old damage that no single movement could undo. There was also swelling, and the weary tension of muscles that had spent years guarding against pain.

Over the following nights, the swelling eased.

She noticed while stepping over a threshold. She put her weight on the leg, expecting the familiar catch, and it did not come. For a moment she stood awkwardly, still braced for it.

She did not become young. She still chose where to kneel. But she could rise without first placing both hands on the ground, and on the next ascent she stopped because she wanted to look across the valley.

She had not done that for some time.

***

When she returned to the hollow, a thin flake had fallen from the inner face of the shelter’s roof.

She saw it on the bedding and backed out.

For half a day she worked from outside, removing turf, testing the slabs, lifting one edge with a lever cut from the dead branch she had carried up for repairs. The power held nothing invisibly above her head. It had settled the flake onto the empty bed several days before her arrival, where she could not miss it.

Underneath was a crack she had never seen.

She changed the overlap of two slabs. The roof became a little lower near her feet and sounder above her chest. There was enough turf left to cover it again.

That evening she ate beside the doorway. The round-eared animal crossed behind the warm boulder with grass protruding from both sides of its mouth.

“Still here,” she said.

The animal vanished.

She smiled, though she had meant herself as much as it.

***

The power stayed through the season.

A hunting bird took one of the smaller birds on the ridge. Frost killed a scatter of flowers that had opened early. The woman pressed a loosened plant back into its hole and shaded it with a stone; it survived, though two leaves withered.

There was no single arrangement in which every living thing could have everything it sought. The power kept attending.

It loosened a caught foot from a loop of abandoned cord. It gave one exhausted bird the small rising current that carried it to a ledge. When cloud filled the hollow during the woman’s descent, it opened a brief space in the mist through which she saw the next cairn.

She waited there until she could see the one after it.

By autumn, the animal had three dry stores. Snow covered the flowering ground without blowing entirely away. Beneath it, the moss held its little chambers, and the creatures within them spent their warmth slowly.

The power did not require gratitude. Neither the animal nor the woman knew enough to offer it.

On the last clear day before winter, the animal brought a stem out of the shelter and laid it beside the black boulder. It returned twice to turn it.

The sun reached the stem.

For that afternoon, this needed no assistance.
