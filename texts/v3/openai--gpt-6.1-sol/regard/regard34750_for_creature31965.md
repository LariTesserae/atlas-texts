The power came first as something that could be mistaken for a visitor.

It sat beneath the shelter with its feet on the warm stone. It did not put its hands into the three hollows. It drank once from the cup, and when the creature approached, it waited.

The larger hand touched the bench, the ground, the visitor’s shoe.

Coolness passed through the sole.

Then the creature began to clear a channel.

The power moved its foot before the channel was finished. Not far: only enough to keep a snail from being enclosed between the shoe and the fresh bank of mineral paste.

The creature stopped working.

One eye closed. The other remained directed at the place where the snail was passing.

Afterward, it completed the channel in a slightly different shape.

For three days, the power did nothing that could not have been done by a patient visitor.

It watched the order in which the pools filled. It watched the small ones withdraw into the silt. It saw the bird bring a husk and take the same husk away again.

It learned that the hottest water was not, for those living beneath it, the worst water. It learned that some of the creature’s slowness belonged to its body, some to its work, and some to a wish not to hurry.

It did not yet know which was which.

On the fourth afternoon, the second tick failed to come.

The creature kept its fingers in the wall until the rain began. Then it withdrew them, crossed to the seep, and broke a new channel through a ridge that had hardened since morning.

Water reached the central hollow late.

The little ones stayed buried. The adult’s plates shone well into the evening.

The power stayed through the night.

There were things it could have done at once. It could have cooled the shelf, lifted the mineral bindings from the creature’s back, restored the broken posts according to the shapes still recorded in their buried foundations.

But the pool beside the shelter had not always been a pool. Roots now occupied parts of the old construction. The creature had pressed its own discarded plates into places where other material had once belonged.

The shelf was inhabited in the present, not in the condition in which it had first been made.

So the power began with a question small enough to answer without words.

On the bench, beside the brittle plate waiting to be broken, it placed a little fork of pale material. The fork was warm, light, and free of any scent. Its two ends rested against the stone; between them was room for a narrow wrist.

The creature examined it with the larger hand.

It turned it over. It wet it in the cup. It pressed one end against a plate on its back.

Then it put it outside the shelter.

The power left it there.

The next day, the creature carried it to the central hollow and laid it across a shallow channel. Water passed beneath. A snail climbed over it.

It was useful, though not as intended.

The power made no second fork.

Instead, it watched how the creature braced itself at the wall.

When the fingers entered the hollows, the smaller hand bore against the bench at an awkward angle. The creature’s damaged side tightened. Its body shifted twice before it could press the second finger far enough.

That evening, the power placed a smooth stone beside the bench.

The creature ignored it for a day.

On the next, it pushed the stone closer to the wall.

On the day after that, it rested its forearm on it.

The second tick came.

The power did not mistake this for permission to do everything else.

It stayed.

***

The failure beneath the central ring was not a single broken part.

There were old separations there, layers that no longer met cleanly, passages narrowed by white growth. Some of the growth carried water where the old passages could not. Some blocked them. The creature’s flakes lay among both.

Each afternoon, it lent something of its own body to the passage across those separations.

The power could feel the expenditure, though it did not have a name for what was spent. Heat passed out of the creature’s plates. Its joints settled. For a little while, sleep became possible.

Then the gathering rain began to wake it.

The power waited beside the hollow until the creature’s hands entered the water.

It extended one finger and touched the wet stone at the far side.

Nothing changed at first.

Then a faint yellow line appeared beneath the mineral crust, no longer than a fallen leaf.

The creature lifted its head.

The power withdrew immediately.

The line faded.

For a long time, the creature looked across the pool. A small one emerged beside its wrist, felt the water with three fingers, and retreated.

The adult moved its larger hand toward the place where the yellow line had been.

It touched the stone.

Then it looked at the visitor.

The power put its finger there again.

This time, it took only a little of the burden: enough that the light left the creature’s smaller hand before it left the larger one.

The creature drew that hand out of the water and folded it beneath its head.

They remained that way until the droplets gathered.

***

The work took weeks.

A new path formed below the crust, alongside the failing one. It did not require the roots to be cut. Where living tissue crossed it, the path divided and joined again beyond.

Its first opening was small.

The creature could close it with a flake of its own plate, and on the third day it did.

The power waited while the old labor resumed.

That afternoon, one of the hottest pools cooled too soon. The small creatures moved restlessly along its bottom. The adult went there, held its mouth to the water, and remained until they settled.

At dusk, it returned to the central hollow and removed the flake.

The power altered the new path. More heat remained beneath that pool. Less crossed the open stone near the shelter.

The next afternoon, the creature left the opening uncovered.

No part of the repair was hidden from it. Wherever the power worked, there was something the creature could touch, stop, or change. The little dark pebbles left beside the hollow were not controls made for an unfamiliar hand. They were simply plugs, fitted to channels that could be seen.

The creature moved them often.

Sometimes its reasons were apparent. Sometimes the power waited several days before understanding.

The basin cooled unevenly now, and more usefully.

The nursery pools remained hot. The seep kept its strength. A place beside the bench became comfortable enough to rest on without entering the cup’s strange cold.

The rains still came.

They arrived a little farther apart.

***

The power did not replace the creature’s missing limbs.

It offered, once.

In the shallow water beside the bench, it made a likeness of the smaller hand: not a detached hand, but an image of the hand extending, its shortened parts continuing into places they might once have occupied.

The creature studied it.

It laid the actual hand over the image. The pale filaments emerged around its pads.

Then it swept the water clear.

The power let the image go.

Later, when the creature peeled a loose plate from its back, it placed the plate on the bench as usual. It cleaned the exposed tissue and paused with its fingers resting against the edge of the old split.

This time, it looked toward the visitor.

The power came closer.

It eased the pull of the embedded fragment without removing it. Tissue that had been held taut relaxed by a little. The creature turned toward its unplated side, stopped, and turned back again.

The next morning, it tried the movement once more.

Nothing on the shelf marked the occasion.

A leaf emptied. A snail grazed the discarded plate.

***

There was one repair the power made beyond the reach of any hand.

Below the seven posts, it laid a second, unobtrusive boundary.

It caught nothing that was merely falling. Rain passed through it. Seeds could drift into the valley. The bird crossed it without hesitation.

But if the old posts failed while holding a stone, the stone would not fall.

And if a small amber body lost its grip at the rim, it would be carried to the nearest wet ledge.

The creature discovered this when a piece of crust broke beneath one of its feet.

It slipped, caught itself, and stayed motionless.

Below it, the broken crust settled onto an empty ledge instead of continuing down into the clouds.

The creature climbed back into the basin. For several days afterward, it examined the posts more often than usual.

The power showed it the new boundary by lowering a wet leaf over the edge and letting go.

The leaf drifted down.

Then it lowered a brittle plate.

The plate came gently to rest.

The creature took the plate back, turned it over, and carried it away.

***

When the power eventually rose from the bench, the shelf was still hot.

There were still broken posts, and uncertain joints, and work to be done after rain. The central mound remained largely untended. The bird’s errands remained its own.

But the adult could sleep through one shower now.

Sometimes two.

It had begun resting in a different recess, nearer the pools. One of the small ones would climb onto its back there, while another gripped the edge of a plate and stayed half-submerged.

The power left a narrow place in the shelter’s wall where a touch could reach it. No fingers had to be pressed in a particular order. The creature tested it once before the visitor departed.

A coolness answered against its pad.

That was all.

As the power stepped out, the creature approached and looked down at its feet.

The old channel had grown shallow. Fresh crust crossed it in three places.

The creature knelt and began clearing it.

The power could have kept its shoes dry without help. It could have crossed the rain untouched.

Instead, it sat down again.

It waited while the creature carried the loosened paste away in its palms.

When the shower came, water ran around the visitor’s feet and rejoined the old channel beyond the bench.
