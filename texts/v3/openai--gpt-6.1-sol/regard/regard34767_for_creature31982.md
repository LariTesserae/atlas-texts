The power does not enter through the largest opening.

There is no arrival for the bats to hear, no new weight upon the floor. The creature’s forefeet remain in the damp leaf matter. Above its shoulder, the vine holds its place.

For a time, nothing is done.

The power attends to the water.

It follows the drop beside the shelter backward: along the groove in the slab, into the hanging root, through a seam packed with grains of soil. Higher up, the route divides. Some water reaches the root. Some travels elsewhere. Much is taken up by the fig, whose leaves stand in the sun and whose shade keeps the hill from becoming hotter still.

It would be easy to send more water down.

The power waits.

It watches a frog lay eggs behind a mineral rim. It watches the creature leave a fallen leaf over the basin, then lift one corner with a digit to drink beneath it. It watches the fungi rise and soften, the geckos warm themselves against their slabs, the ants change their route around a patch of newly damp stone.

A great deal here depends on the difference between wet and nearly wet.

The power also attends to the air.

Far below the crack, the hill contains chambers that have never held daylight. Air rests in them, cool but sometimes unfit to breathe. On those nights the creature turns away from the passage and lowers itself to the floor. Its throat works harder. By morning, the thin skin there has a dullness that was absent the day before.

The power follows that air through the stone until it finds another route upward: a narrow fissure, almost filled by an old fall of grit. Near the surface, its last opening is covered by earth.

It does not clear the whole passage.

A few grains shift. A space opens no wider than a beetle’s body, beneath a stone where rain will not readily close it. Air begins to move through the fissure so slowly that the fine roots beside it do not stir.

Below, the creature continues sleeping.

The power waits through several nights, through a shower and the three days of seepage that follow. When the creature next presses its mouth to the passage, it takes a breath, pauses, and takes another.

The moths lift their wings.

That is enough for now.

***

The dry season comes without severity at first.

The exposed leaves curl. The trickle loses its shine. Water still gathers beneath the shelf, though the drops are farther apart, and the creature makes its usual circuit at dusk.

Then there are nights when it visits the shallow dishes twice.

It drinks from the root and stays there after the water is gone. It turns a piece of bark but does not take the beetle underneath. Near dawn, instead of settling against the pillar, it lies beneath the fern mat with one forelimb extended into the darkest part.

The power attends more closely.

It does not fill the hollow with rain. The frogs’ pockets are not made into a pool. The dry stone beneath the fig remains dry, its dust carrying the small crossings of the night.

On the slope above, beyond the fig’s main roots, a dead branch has come to rest across a faint channel in the soil. Rain striking there ordinarily gathers speed and runs downhill, carrying loose earth with it.

The branch settles a little farther into the channel.

At the next brief shower, water collects behind it. Leaves catch there, then silt. Much of the water still runs on. A little remains long enough to enter the ground.

Days later, a seam deep in the hill darkens.

The moisture reaches the hollow beneath the fern mat. It does not emerge as a spring. A patch of limestone, no larger than the creature’s head, becomes damp and stays damp after the older trickle stops.

A fern root finds it first.

Then a beetle.

The creature finds it on the fourth night. It touches the stone with two digits and draws them back. It lowers its mouth. For some time its body remains still except for the slow passage in its throat.

Afterward, it turns the bark again.

This time it eats.

***

The power could smooth the old abrasions from its back. It could widen the passages, give it an easier body, bring fruit within reach of its resting place.

It leaves those possibilities alone.

Some of the narrowness belongs to the creature’s safety. Some belongs to its difficulty. The distinction is not always clear, even with long attention. A passage widened for its shoulders would also admit other things. Fruit laid every night beside the pillar would draw animals to a place where the creature now rests undisturbed.

The power does not mistake every effort for an injury.

Nor does it presume that because no young have been seen, the creature requires another of its kind. It attends to the inaccessible spaces, to faint traces under roots, to the creature’s pauses at the sloping throat. What it does not yet know, it leaves unknown.

When the snake passes toward the slabs, it is not turned aside.

The geckos have their recesses. The snake has its hunger. The creature opens its eyes as the long body crosses a root, then closes them again.

The hollow continues to contain lives that do not agree with one another.

***

Years pass.

The fallen branch on the hillside softens into the soil it has helped collect. Other leaves lodge in the shallow depression. The water route holds.

Once, after an unusually long dry season, the damp patch beneath the ferns begins to shrink. The power follows the change upward and finds a stone shifted across the seam by a growing root. It makes room for both.

There are no other alterations that year.

Below, the creature grows a little more slowly than the mineral deposits on its back. One old scale loosens and falls. A beetle shelters beneath it.

The creature still withdraws from the amber light. It still waits for insects to open the skin of fruit. Sometimes it leaves the leaf over the basin; sometimes it moves it.

It has not learned to expect a presence. Nothing asks it to.

Near the end of a hot night, it returns to the pillar and eases its limbs beneath its body. The vine slips across its shoulder. Its forefoot reaches into the shallow dish.

The leaf matter is damp.

Two digits settle there, and remain.
