The power arrived without making the fern move.

For a long while, it did nothing.

Its attention entered by the four holes at the base of the root. It followed a worker past the crossing fiber, through the passage roofed with bark, into a chamber where the young lay against one another. It remained beside the queen without bringing light into her darkness.

Then it followed the dampness beneath the fallen leaf.

There were lives there for which the courtyard was already an enormous place. There were mouths working at surfaces, threads entering wood, a body sheltered inside the thing it was consuming. The power attended to these as carefully as it attended to the ants. It did not need to enlarge them in order to see them.

When the spider advanced toward the insect caught in its web, the power stayed.

The insect struggled. The spider drew nearer. There was no arrangement the power could make in which every hunger was fed and nothing was eaten. It could have placed a meal beside the spider, but a meal would still have come from somewhere. It could have abolished hunger, but then the hollow would no longer be this hollow, with its slow making of earth and its lives dependent upon one another.

It did not look away. Nor did it take the spider’s food.

By afternoon, it had learned the three routes of water. It knew which chambers would darken first after rain and which remained dry longest. It knew the underside of each seed plate, the reach of the web, the looseness of the grain the worker had pressed beside the pale chip.

It saw many things it could change.

The bark roof could be strengthened. The nursery could be given firmer walls. Water could be kept in the basin through the hottest hours.

But a lasting pool would change the clearing. A firmer wall might close the seam through which air entered. Even a gift of food would draw new traffic across the moss, perhaps toward the web, perhaps away from some other necessary task.

The power waited through the warmth. It watched the ants make another adjustment of their own.

Toward evening, something approached that none of the inhabitants had much means of answering.

A person was coming between the trees, looking upward. They were following a bird through the canopy and had left the narrow path without noticing. Their next step would come down beside the buttress root. The heel would enter the hollow, crush the bark roof, and press the loose foundation into the chambers below.

The power could have lifted the foot. It could have made the root immense, or enclosed the courtyard in stone.

Instead, it let the person notice where they were.

A fern brushed their wrist. They glanced down and saw the uneven ground, the roots, the small dark opening beneath the frond. They set their foot elsewhere. Then they looked back for the path and returned to it.

The bird went on calling.

Nothing in the hollow knew what had happened. No worker paused to receive a blessing. The queen remained surrounded by touch. Under the seed plate, the feeding continued.

The power was content with that.

Night gathered in the courtyard before it gathered in the canopy. The air cooled. A bead of moisture formed on the strand of web that already held one during the day. Beneath the leaf, the dampness began to spread toward a margin that had dried.

The power stayed, but did not station itself over every hazard.

There would be rain enough to require moving the young. There would be a fallen twig, an abandoned entrance, a passage opened by decay and later closed by it. Workers would lose their way. Some would not return. The spider, too, would sometimes wait hungry against the root.

These were not small matters to the creatures living through them. The power knew that. Its restraint was not indifference; it kept attending, ready to learn what help might be possible without taking the place apart.

At one entrance, a worker emerged carrying a pale fragment.

She climbed the collar of earth and stopped where the route narrowed. The fragment caught against another piece. She pulled it free, turned, and carried it around the far side of the mound.

The power did not move it for her.

She found a place, set it down, and tested it with her antennae.

It held.

She went below.
