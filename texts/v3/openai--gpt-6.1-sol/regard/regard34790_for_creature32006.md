The power did not enter through the opening.

Entering would have meant a shadow on the step, a change in the wind, weight upon a floor where weight was already being borne. It attended first to the mountain, and then to the hollow within it, until it could distinguish the heat that sustained the chamber from the heat that sometimes threatened it.

These were not separate sources.

Far below the old animal, a seam of stone was closing. Each muted knock brought its faces a little nearer together. Gas that had once dispersed through many small passages now gathered behind a narrowing throat. Eventually there would be a louder knock. The floor would lift. For a short time the vents would give more than the creatures around them could take.

The power could have stilled everything below the hollow.

It could also have cooled the mountain until a hand might rest comfortably on the vent mouths.

For a long while it considered the smallest animals, pressed flat against the crust, and did neither.

Instead it followed the narrowing passage downward. Where the mountain had once let a little of its breath escape sideways, the power loosened a grain.

Only one.

The grain fell into an empty fissure. Another, freed of its support, followed it. The new passage was too narrow to alter the warmth above, but broad enough to begin relieving the pressure. Gas traveled through it into a deep bed of porous stone.

In the hollow, nothing stopped feeding.

The old animal’s slender legs continued sorting beneath its body. A pale fragment went inward. A dark one was pushed out.

The power waited through several pulses of heat before doing anything else.

It learned that the old animal’s stillness was not sleep. Its broad pads received movements through the floor. Its armor received the pressure of tenants, the dragging of feet, the slow loosening of mineral at the edges of its plates. Its buried side knew the bank by its weight. The small head beneath the shelter lay among impressions arriving from every direction.

Some of these impressions hurt.

There was a curved nail, far under the body, that had grown against a stone and turned back into the softer part of its own joint. The joint had thickened around it. Sorting legs worked near it without touching it.

There was also the roof.

The animal could withdraw from beneath the fallen corner. But one shield above its head had become wedged against a roughness in the slab. Each small backward motion caught there. A stronger pull might free it, at the cost of tearing the shield away.

The power did not lift the roof.

It made itself perceptible on a bare patch of stone beside the head: a pressure lighter than the arrival of a small grazer, held steady through the tremors of the floor.

For a long time the feelers remained folded.

A threadlike inhabitant crossed the patch. The pressure yielded to its feet and returned after it had passed.

Eventually one feeler emerged.

Its lobes settled in succession upon the stone. At the place of the pressure, they paused.

The power moved a little nearer the trapped shield. Not touching it, not pulling. It waited there.

The feeler withdrew.

Hours passed. The mountain breathed through its widened passage. A pellet appeared beneath the old animal’s flank and was taken apart by small mouths.

Then both feelers emerged. One conformed to the stone beside the head. The other reached upward and laid three soft lobes against the underside of the slab.

The power took the weight of that corner.

It raised it less than the thickness of an empty capsule.

The old animal tightened along its buried side. Several tenants retreated. A combed leg stopped with a fragment held in its teeth.

Then the head moved backward.

Very little. Enough for the caught shield to pass the rough place in the stone.

The feelers remained against the slab until the power lowered it again. The roof settled onto a firmer part of the animal’s armor. Dust descended. Creatures came to examine it.

Nothing more moved for the rest of that day.

Later, one of the pale legs reached toward the injured joint. Its comb touched the turned nail, withdrew, and touched it again.

The power took this more slowly. It softened only the dead tip. Under the comb’s repeated strokes, the tip crumbled into flakes. The nail came free of the flesh it had entered.

The animal drew the leg close beneath itself.

There was no sound to mark the change. At its flank, a small passenger continued cleaning the same seam it had been cleaning before.

The power stayed.

It did not prevent the next feeding. When the bank subsided, pale burrowers fell with the empty armor, and the feeding edges drew some of them inward. Others caught at neighboring bodies and climbed clear. The power attended to both.

Nor did it strengthen every capsule overhead. The hollow could not hold all that was laid within it. Its abundance depended upon many things being eaten, and upon their remains passing through other bodies.

But when the next underground pulse approached, it no longer came behind a dangerous accumulation of pressure.

The old animal felt it early, as before. Its sorting legs stopped. Its foot turned across the crack. The creatures beneath it began to emerge.

They came out into warmth that rose gradually.

The bridge-limb shifted. Travelers gathered, tested the slope, and crossed.

Afterward, the old animal extended a feeler toward the bare patch beside its head.

The power was still there.

Beyond the entrance, the white distances trembled. The cut step remained dry. No spring appeared beneath it; no sudden light filled the chamber. In the bank, something newly hatched found a gap and disappeared into it.

Below, a grain of stone fell through the opened passage.

The mountain gave its quiet knock, and the broad foot resting across the crack eased its pressure a little.
