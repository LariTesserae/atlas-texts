For a while, nothing changes.

Your hand lies on the stone. The inhabitant keeps its back toward you. Water works beneath the plateau, and the thin tones run through the cracks above your head.

Then you feel a pressure under the heel of your palm.

It is slight enough that you first take it for the beginning of numbness. You lift your hand. The pressure stops. When you put it down again, it returns.

Outside, the inhabitant opens its fingers against the ground.

There is something attending to the country.

It has not come from beyond the cloud. There is no disturbance there, no widening in the light. It is present in the way a careful listener can become present without moving closer.

For a long time it listens.

It follows the water through chambers you have not seen. It attends to the roots, including the portions that have gone dry. It feels where the pale animal’s whiskers rest against the rock, and where the bird holds its foot above its distant shadow.

It listens beneath the hands of the inhabitants.

There are differences in the sounds too fine for you to distinguish. The flake on the ledge is not merely clicking. Through its point of contact, it holds one of the passages between shelters steady. The moss turned toward a seam is being kept alive, but it is also keeping the seam open. The grains set into the ground beneath your shelter complete something that your footsteps disturbed.

None of this is explained to you. Your hand remains on the stone.

The pressure changes once.

The inhabitant turns its head.

***

The first alteration occurs beneath a roof that no longer exists.

One of the two upright stones has been leaning for a very long time. Its weight has narrowed the space in which the inhabitant sits. There is still room for its body, but not enough to open the left hand fully. The small finger rests bent against the palm.

The stone does not rise or straighten.

A shallow place forms on its inner face.

The change takes as long as the inhabitant takes to draw a wristful of water from the seam. When it is finished, the surface is rounded and weathered. There is room for the finger.

The inhabitant extends it.

It remains extended for a while, touching nothing.

Elsewhere, nothing happens.

The three ridges retain their heights. Rain falls within its exact boundaries. The bubble beside the white pebble trembles as before.

***

The attention stays.

It discovers that the broad pads of the inhabitants’ feet are well suited to this stone, and that crossing the open shelves is nevertheless difficult for some of them. It discovers that the entrances do not join every place they might wish to go.

For several stretches of apparent distance, no passage is needed. An inhabitant sitting beside a pool has no wish to leave it. A young one sleeps against an older back. The place beneath the rain is uncomfortable to you and comfortable to the one whose face appears at ankle height.

The attention passes over these things without changing them.

At the shelter with five steps, it pauses.

An inhabitant has been resting on the third step. Its hand is on the fourth. Above it, a young one waits at the entrance with its head inclined. There is no injury you can see. Only a long interval between one movement and the next.

Along the outer edge of the steps, the stone deepens into a slope.

The steps remain. The smooth centers retain their wear.

The inhabitant notices the slope with its fingers. It puts one palm against it, then the other. After a time, it draws itself upward without lifting its full weight.

At the threshold, the young one presses its forehead against the older one’s shoulder.

A low tone passes through the stone.

The new slope does not lead anywhere else.

***

You become sleepy.

The position you have found is possible to hold, but your body has mistaken this for rest. When you move, cold enters the places where your coat has been compressed. Your knees hurt more sharply.

The inhabitant outside touches the edge of your flat stone.

A question reaches you.

You cannot translate it. There is only a brief pressure, followed by a space in which your hand is free to answer.

You press down.

Nothing happens.

You lift your hand and put it back, more lightly.

The inhabitant waits.

You shift your shoulders, trying again to fit beneath the overhang. This time you feel the roof with your back. You turn and place your palm against it.

The attention is there also.

Slowly, a little of the stone above you wears away.

You withdraw your hand at once.

The wearing stops.

You look at the inhabitant. It has turned far enough that you can see one eye.

You touch the roof again.

When there is room to sit without bending your neck, you take your hand away.

No more stone is removed.

Later, warmth gathers beneath the flat stone. It reaches your fingers first, then your wrist. The wet center of the moss remains wet. The warmth does not spread into the cracks where the plants keep their roots.

You sleep with your palm open.

***

In the clean hollow, the attention finds an inhabitant with its jaw against the rounded depression.

It stays nearby.

There are marks in that stone which no living hand now makes. The inhabitant feels them through the underside of its jaw. At intervals its throat moves, and a tremor enters the wall.

The attention could make the old answering tremor.

It does not.

After a long time, the inhabitant lifts its head.

A bead of water has gathered on its wrist. It rubs the bead into the floor, then rests its jaw against the depression again.

This time, farther down the wall, another inhabitant places a hand.

The two remain until you have passed beyond the entrance.

***

There are places where the inhabitants ask for nothing.

There are others where they ask for things so small that you would not have known they were missing: room to turn a head, a second wet seam beside one that is failing, a surface against which a damaged hand can rest without closing.

Sometimes the attention waits through several visits before acting.

Sometimes an inhabitant touches a change and presses until it is undone.

The young one beside the clicking flake tries a new arrangement. Its teacher holds the flake steady, then releases it. For the first time, you hear a difference.

The sound is lower.

The teacher leaves it so.

***

When you wake, the inhabitant outside is asleep.

Its cheek rests against the stone near your hand. Its fingers are still spread over the grains it had been setting into the crack.

You do not move at first.

The plateau is gray. Beneath it, the cloud rests against the undersides of ridges. The bird stands with one foot lifted. From somewhere within the shelter, you feel two faint tremors, separated by a pause.

You wait for a third.

It comes.

You put two fingers against the flat stone and try to make the same intervals.

The sleeping inhabitant opens its eyes.

You try again.

This time, the last tremor reaches the wall.

Outside, water continues its immense labor. The inhabitant lifts its cheek from the stone and settles a little nearer, leaving space for your hand.
