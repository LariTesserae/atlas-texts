The first thing the power did was learn which doors had to remain open.

This took longer than learning the city’s name, which appeared in seven forms in the countinghouse records and did not appear at all in the cupboard above the basin. It took longer than following the pipes beneath the plateau. A pipe could be traced. An open door might serve a purpose that had never been written down.

The power watched the woman at the tile works move her son’s bucket. It watched the old woman lower her lamp beside a spreading crust. It followed the man with the ladder when he turned away from water that had become heavy.

It did not assume that what they could not explain was something they did not know.

For a time, nothing changed.

Then the woman at the tile works found that the third settling tank had stopped leaking onto the rung she used to reach its inspection hatch.

The leak had persisted for nine years. Twice she had packed the joint; once she had replaced a section of pipe. Each repair had moved the wet place a little farther along the wall.

Now the joint was dry.

She touched it with her tile. She inspected the tank above and the passage below. Water was still reaching the places it should reach. Behind the lining, the translucent creatures remained gathered along a green seam.

She marked the date on the wall.

Three mornings later, the man beneath the west spillway woke without the ache in his hands.

He flexed them under the blanket. The fingers moved as they had not moved in years. He remained in bed awhile, listening to the valve beyond his window, then got up and cut a strip of leather narrower than he would previously have attempted.

These changes were noticed. They were not immediately welcomed.

A pump that runs quietly after years of knocking may have ceased to work.

The keepers added an extra round.

The power let them.

***

Beneath the upper basin, something had been holding a passage open.

It had held it open for so long that the difference between the passage and its own body was no longer clear. Water went through it. Heat went through it. On certain days, distances went through it badly, and a length of rail emerged with a spacing different from the one at which it had entered.

The city’s machinery had been built around this difficulty. Some of the builders had understood more than the current inhabitants. Others had understood less and built larger chambers.

There had been injuries in both periods.

The being beneath the basin knew the city chiefly by pressure. It knew a reservoir filling, a door left shut, the light load of a person crossing a walkway. It knew the six uneven strokes of the engine beneath the baths. That sound reached it through the rock.

The girl’s stories arrived differently.

It could not follow every word. Names of districts were useful; they came with recurring shapes. A stair described incorrectly troubled the passage, faintly, as a grit of sand troubles a bearing. Her father’s corrections settled it again.

But the being listened most closely when she told it things that had no consequence.

A cup had broken. Her brother had claimed he could hear the birds asleep. Someone had brought a yellow ribbon from the lower road, and it looked brown indoors.

It had very little experience of anything that did not require a response.

The power approached it without entering the basin.

For the first time in many years, a warmth moved beneath the water without drawing another warmth after it.

The keepers saw the bands pause.

A dropped bolt lay still on the rising floor.

The power made no demand to be recognized. It offered a small interval in which the being did not have to hold the passage alone.

The interval lasted less than a breath.

Across the city, the fountain sequence continued. No gate fell. No channel emptied. In the overflow tunnels, the animals kept their places.

The being took back the weight.

The power waited.

Later, it offered the same interval again.

***

At the countinghouse, the envelopes began arriving on the expected dates.

They contained payment for the work actually being done. The dead institutions’ names disappeared from the accounts, though the clerks retained the old records in case anyone needed to establish what they were owed.

There was also money for journeys.

No one was required to make one. A household could go down to the plain, stay elsewhere, and return without surrendering its room or its share of supplies. The arrangements were posted on a board by the lower road, where the lettering stayed dry.

People tested these provisions cautiously.

The young man who sometimes sat with his fingers spread on his knees left for three weeks. He returned with treatment for his hands and a complaint about the cold in the place where he had stayed.

A keeper visited her sister.

Two families left and did not return. Their rooms were closed only after the accessible passages had been checked and other people had agreed to make the rounds.

The woman at the tile works missed her sixth-morning inspection for the first time in years. Someone else did it. When she came back, he showed her the marks he had made, including one beside a tank she thought he had examined too briefly.

They went down together.

***

The power did not cool the whole plateau.

Too much lived in the heat. Too much of the city depended on water arriving with a certain weight and leaving with another. A large improvement, made carelessly, could have emptied the seams behind the ceramic walls.

Instead, places appeared where a person could rest.

A bench beneath the windward wall lost its heat without gathering a mineral crust. The stair outside the pressure rooms became safe to sit on. In the tile works, one upper room stayed dry enough for ordinary bedding.

The woman carried a mattress into it and left the old platform where it was.

Tools began to last longer. Replacement soles arrived before the old ones wore through. There were gloves that allowed the lower pumping workers to feel what they touched.

The birds were given warm ledges away from the chimney packing. They did not all move. Some remained attached to particular caps, and strips of metal still rattled on the ladders.

Nothing was brought in merely to make the city resemble somewhere else.

When the man beneath the spillway asked for a living tree, the power helped him find one that could survive in a large pot beside his window. He had to turn it occasionally. Its roots could not be allowed near the drains.

He moved his framed drawing to make room.

***

The relief beneath the basin lengthened.

At first the being could bear only brief absences from its work. Each time it released the passage, it expected the terrible pull that had followed a failure long ago.

The pull did not come.

The power supported the passage from elsewhere, carefully enough that the keepers’ lamps remained aligned in the water. It learned what could be diverted and what had to be held. Some parts of the burden could be removed altogether. Others could only be shared.

There was no promise that everything would become simple.

One evening the girl came to the steps and found the basin unusually still.

She told it about a journey she intended to take when she was older. Her father began to correct the route, then stopped. She was describing roads beyond the city. He did not know them.

When she finished, a bolt moved near the edge.

It travelled slowly, with several pauses, and came to rest against the lowest submerged step.

The girl looked at her father.

“Leave it,” he said, out of habit.

Then he knelt beside her.

They watched for a long time. Nothing else moved.

The next day, the keepers found a new page in the cupboard. It contained no instructions. There was a drawing of the basin, with a small place marked at the edge where an object could be received safely, if one was offered.

They checked the place before using it.

The bolt was warm when lifted. It was an ordinary bolt, worn at one end, useless for fastening.

The girl kept it high with the colored glass.

***

There were still rounds to make.

The lamps needed filling. The crusts needed watching. No one scraped a floor simply because help had arrived. The power continued to learn from the people who lived there, and sometimes an old woman’s raised hand was enough to stop its work.

The city did not fill with strangers. Its broad stairs remained too broad for the number of feet upon them. Rain still sounded like a crowd arriving and left no crowd behind.

But a person could be ill without lying awake to calculate what would fail before morning.

A keeper could turn away from the basin to eat.

Beneath the water, the being gradually learned to rest while the passage remained open. During those intervals it listened to things farther away than the pumps: a chair drawn across a floor, the small dry fall of leaves from a potted tree, two brothers arguing over a game whose rules had changed since yesterday.

Sometimes it moved nothing at all.

Above it, the cupboard record continued in several hands. After an entry concerning a displaced reflection and another concerning a damaged lamp, someone wrote:

*Still all afternoon. No correction required.*

There was room beneath that for the next day.
