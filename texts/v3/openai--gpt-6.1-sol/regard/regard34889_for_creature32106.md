The power did not bring rain.

It had watched long enough to know that thirst was not the name of this place’s need. Water poured into the chambers would have been an invasion. The pale walls would have taken it into pores meant for air. The beetles’ stores would have softened. Roots that had never needed to refuse a flood would have had no way to refuse it.

Nor did it make the crown larger at once. A broad new plain would have offered space, but not shelter, not familiar warmth, not the pressure of a neighboring body through a joined wall. There were things here that could only be made by living beside one another for years.

So it began beneath the town.

It attended to the stone all the way down to the desert, following the clean young fractures into darkness. Most were harmless. Some were necessary: openings through which roots could descend, seams that let the growing mineral lift without crushing what rested on it.

One was neither.

Far below the western rim, a fault crossed the pillar at an angle. Each warm season, the new stone above it added a little weight. In time the fault would open. Not today, not in the lives of the beetles moving through the court, but while some of the present shell animals were still alive.

The crown would not fall whole. It would peel away on that side, carrying chambers whose occupants had never known a distance greater than a few thresholds.

The power held the fault together while it considered.

Then, along its lower face, it encouraged the mineral to grow.

Not much. A thin joining, spread across a great depth. It left the channels of amber substance open. It left the small fractures free to move. Over several seasons the joining thickened until the weight above passed safely into the body of the pillar.

In the town, a western chamber grew slightly warmer through two afternoons. Its owner adjusted the folds of its breathing cavity. A grazer chose another room, then returned.

That was all anyone noticed.

***

The young shell animal beside the rim was harder to help.

It had settled where there was barely room to settle. Its root was healthy, its exchange with the plant already beginning, but the next rise of stone would tilt its little hood toward the drop. A body grown into that tilt might survive. It would have to spend much of its strength holding itself in place, and there would be little room left for a chamber.

The power could have moved it.

But its flesh had entered the crack in many fine folds. Moving it meant loosening those folds, separating it from the root, asking a small and unfamiliar body to endure changes it could not understand.

Instead, beneath the hood, the power altered the direction of a little mineral growth.

A grain pressed sideways rather than upward. Another followed. Slowly, a shelf began to form under the animal, continuing the ledge it already occupied.

The root found the new seam first.

Its white tip entered, divided, and held. The young animal spread after it. Where there had been a strain along one edge of its body, there was now stone to rest against.

Its shell thickened.

A beetle came with another crescent beneath its belly. It entered the hood and backed out again.

The power waited.

***

It did not make every passage remain open.

An unused entrance closed over winter. Two small grazers found another way through the vines, and the shell animal grew a stronger wall where the old opening had been.

A hunter caught a flyer beneath a copper leaf. Elsewhere, beetles fed their larvae with the remains of a grazer that had died asleep against its companion.

The power paid attention to these things too. It learned where an injury could be eased without making another animal go hungry, where a damaged root needed only a little time free of pressure, where a body had reached its end.

There was no single adjustment that served them all.

It stayed long enough to make many small ones.

***

Several seasons later, the young shell animal had a chamber large enough for three beetles to turn inside.

The shelf beneath it was still narrow, but roots had gripped its outer edge. A pale leaf bent over the roof during the hottest hours. Through the animal’s flesh came the faint movements of an older neighbor.

A beetle entered carrying a fragment of shed skin.

This time it did not back out.

It crossed the warm floor, felt along the wall with its antennae, and found an opening on the other side. The opening was uneven and scarcely wider than its body. Beyond it lay a sheltered joint between two roots.

The beetle passed through.

Behind it, the young animal registered the tiny sequence of pressures. When it next laid down shell along that wall, it left the crossed place open.
