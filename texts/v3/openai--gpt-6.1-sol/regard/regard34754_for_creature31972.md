The power’s attention enters without light.

Nothing in the seam needs to see it. It rests first upon the lifted basalt skin, then upon the shelf beneath it, then upon the separate points where the mite’s feet touch stone. It attends to the heat at each point. To the grain caught in a joint and already shed. To the moisture held in spaces where there is no room for a drop.

For a while, it does nothing.

The mite’s stillness is not an appeal. Nor is the mountain’s heat a fault. A place can be difficult and still be a home. The power waits long enough to distinguish the difficulties through which life is making its way from those that are about to leave it no way at all.

Beneath the shelf, a fracture continues downward.

Far below, the drill has reached a band of rock under strain. Each change of load sends a pulse through it. Most disperse. One returns along the fracture, weakened but precisely timed. At the summit, a small plate moves a little farther from its support.

When it falls, it will close the cooler passage.

The hot route will remain open.

The power follows this consequence without hurrying past its scale. For a human, the plate is a fragment that could be brushed from a sleeve. For the mite, it is a ceiling, a turn in the road, and the difference between two kinds of air.

The power could hold it up.

It could hold every loose thing in the mountain forever.

Instead, far below, it makes the fault legible.

An instrument that has been recording noise records a pattern. The pattern persists when the technician checks the connection. It appears on a second instrument. There is enough evidence to interrupt the ordinary pressure to continue.

People argue over it. Someone wants another hour of drilling. Someone else points to the returning pulse.

The motor stops.

This time the absence lasts.

In the seam, the plate settles against a neighboring splinter. Dust slips from its edge. The cooler passage narrows, but remains wide enough.

The power stays beside it until the settling is done.

There is another difficulty, less dramatic. Warm gas from the fresh crack is drying the shelf where the mites rest. The wind draws too strongly through one small opening. The power attends to the loose fragments around that opening, to the pressure of the leaning block, to the slow contraction of cooling basalt.

It gives a single grain the movement it was nearly ready to make.

The grain falls into a notch.

Air still passes. The mineral gases still escape. But the current beneath the lifted skin weakens, and a little more vapor remains among the ash.

No water appears. The chamber does not become a garden.

Over the next hours, its smallest hollows lose moisture more slowly.

The dark mite unfolds a foreleg.

It touches the floor, then the edge of the crystal. It lifts its body and takes the familiar route toward the broken wing. At the narrowed passage it pauses. Its front legs reach into the gap.

One finds stone.

The other finds space.

It goes through.

The power does not make the moth wing inexhaustible. It does not remove the spider from its hollow or decree that every mite must live. It watches the dark body pass beyond the dusty web, and watches another, smaller body caught there. Its care includes a world whose inhabitants need different things.

Below the summit, the people begin examining the fracture. Their work becomes slower. They move an instrument, then a planned bore. The mountain has other routes into it, and they have learned something about the cost of this one.

The power does not stay to demand gratitude.

Before withdrawing, it attends once more to the seam: the black walls, the retained scraps, the pale mineral rim growing around the vent. The place will change. A later fall may close a chamber. Rain may enter. The stone will cool, and animals suited to other conditions may arrive.

There is no promise that this home will last forever.

For now, the passage is open.

Beneath the wing, the mite lowers its mouthparts into the fine debris. A springtail moves overhead, upside down along a rib. The grain lodged in the notch holds. Warmth rises through the floor, unevenly as before.

And for the rest of that hour, the ground is quiet enough that the beetle’s scraping can be heard.
