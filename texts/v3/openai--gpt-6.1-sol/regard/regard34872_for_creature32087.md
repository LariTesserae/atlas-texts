The power’s attention arrived without a change in the light.

The animal kept its face in the basin. A shrimp worked along the edge of one open fringe, pulling free something too small to see. Behind the wall, a chamber tightened, another filled, and water passed beneath the animal’s chin.

At first the power did nothing.

It followed the water out through the shaded openings. It followed it into the marsh, where warmth rose through mud and submerged stems, and farther still, to the shallow channels that fed the bank. It found the places where rain lingered, the places where roots held silt, and the old bed of a stream that carried water only in the wettest months.

Then it returned to the hollow and stayed through the night.

The youngest animal came down after the light had gone. At the difficult turn above the entrance, one hind foot slipped from the polished crease and caught in the fibers. The little body stopped. Below it, the older animal lifted its head.

The power could have set the foot free.

Before it did, the youngest shifted its weight. Its toes opened. One fiber slid out from between the pads, then another. It went on down, and the animal waiting below moved aside.

The power watched it reach a fallen pellet.

It had no need to be thanked, and so had no need to become visible.

For several days it attended to the pumps. Their rhythm was less regular than it had first seemed. One chamber took longer to empty in the cool hours before dawn. Another shortened its stroke when the marsh water thickened after rain. The broad releases of pressure were not all alike; some relieved strain, while others sent a little more water toward the leaves.

The great body made these adjustments slowly, with tissue and pressure and the closing of lips. The animals lived among them. A change in the floor’s warmth brought one out of its recess. A bitter taste at a seam kept another licking there until the deposit loosened.

The power learned which interruptions mattered.

A fallen vein lay across a small outlet for most of an afternoon. Nothing suffered. Water found the neighboring openings, and a worm eventually drew the vein beneath the floor.

A stalk stopped producing pellets for a night. The animals searched the older paths and ate other things.

There were losses, too. One of the flies died beside the syrup bead. By morning, mites had gathered beneath its folded wings. The power watched the other flies feed. It did not make the dead one rise.

Beyond the bank, however, it found something the hollow could not adjust to.

An old channel was closing.

Silt had gathered at a bend well upstream. Reeds were rooting in it, catching more silt each wet season. For now, plenty of water passed around them. In a dry year, the remaining passage would become a chain of warm pools. In two dry years together, the water reaching the hollow might cease.

The organism could endure a short drought. Its buried chambers held reserves; its leaves could fold. But the power followed the length of those reserves and the work they would have to do. It stayed with that knowledge for a while.

Then it went to the bend.

It did not clear the reeds. Birds had nested there, and small fish sheltered among their stems. Instead, it loosened a little earth along the channel’s outer edge, where no root had yet taken hold.

The next rain did most of the work.

Water entered the loosened place and carried away a narrow tongue of mud. The new passage was shallow and crooked. Leaves lodged in it. A frog crossed it in one step. But water kept moving through, and the bend no longer had only one way to feed the marsh.

The power waited through another rain before touching it again.

Over the following season it made a few such changes, all beyond the hollow: a little room for water here, a firmer hold for a bank there. In one place it left a fallen branch untouched because the pool behind it supplied the marsh long after the rain had stopped. In another it eased the branch’s weight off a crushed stem.

It did nothing to make the hollow quieter.

The great hum continued through the animals’ sleep. The knocking still ran beneath it. Beetles waited behind beetles, and the basin trembled when pressure passed through the roots.

Once, the animal in the shadowed recess failed to come out for longer than usual. The power attended closely. There was food in the pocket beneath its jaw. Its blood moved slowly but evenly; its fringes opened when it drank. Fresh tissue had thickened around a worn place on one foot.

It was resting.

The power left it to rest.

Late in the dry season, the marsh lowered. The entrance showed a little more dark bank beneath its leaves. Outside, insects gathered at the remaining pools.

Inside, the food stalk produced fewer pellets. The gray-green animal spent longer nosing along a seam. The great body reduced the flow to its upper leaves and kept the basin filled.

Through the new passage upstream, water continued to arrive.

It was not much. It was enough.

The youngest animal, now less transparent, came down the narrow path. It paused at the old difficult place, spread its toes, and crossed without catching.

Below, the older animal was asleep. A drop had formed between the soft projections on its back. With each pulse of the wall, the drop trembled but did not fall.

The power remained nearby until rain returned.

Then it withdrew its close attention. There were other banks, other closing channels. Here, the water moved, the valves held, and the living floor rose slowly behind each passing foot.

At the basin, a shrimp climbed onto the animal’s open fringe.

The animal waited for it to finish.
