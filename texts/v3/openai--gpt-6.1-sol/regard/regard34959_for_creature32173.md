The power first makes room for a question.

Not a large room. Not a palace lowered through the ceiling, nor a clearing burned into the leaves. In a hollow beside a warm crossing, it holds apart two layers of growth that have been pressing against each other. The space between them is just wide enough for a resident to rest without putting a limb into a socket.

It keeps the space warm. It takes nothing through the conducting veins.

For several days, no one enters.

A six-legged animal investigates the edge, eats a damaged seed, and leaves. Maintenance roots approach the hollow and turn aside. One of the flattened residents stops nearby, touches the floor, and waits for the reply that normally follows a request for space.

The power answers through the leaves.

Its first answer is poor. Too broad, too bright. Several meanings arrive at once, some of them incompatible with having a body. The resident withdraws its hand.

The power reduces itself.

It learns the interval between a question and an intrusion. It learns that a warm root can be an offer of food, a channel for speech, or an unwanted hand laid against the inside of a body. It learns not to use the cradles as convenient places to be understood.

Eventually it manages something small.

*May I remain here?*

The flattened resident returns the next day with two others.

Their argument lasts a little more than a week. The power does not keep the hollow warm by drawing heat from the surrounding district; they establish this first. They test whether the nearby feeding column has cooled, whether the floor underneath has lost strength, whether a return distance elsewhere has lengthened to pay for the space.

Then they ask what it intends.

Its answer takes longer than its arrival.

*I have noticed that some of what happens here hurts you. I would like to understand which things you want changed.*

They do not offer a single pattern in reply.

One resident wants a seam kept open. Another wants it closed, because the open seam carries cold gas through a dwelling. A third places a hand over both signals and sends an old, dense sequence involving a mill whose name survives only on a worn flower head.

The power listens until it can follow all three.

Nothing changes that day except its understanding.

—

It meets the door carrier at a crossing.

The carrier has set down the slab and is testing a route. Out a short distance, back again. The tissue wound around its forelimb has become thin along one edge.

The power waits beside the survey leaves.

It could put a house beyond the door. It could make the house warm, spacious, impossible to lose. But it has seen the carrier reject places that seemed suitable. It has not yet learned what the door belongs to.

So it offers to hold the return distance steady for one journey.

The carrier touches the marker, then the door, then the strip around its arm.

A question passes through the crossing.

*Steady toward what?*

The power had expected an easier question.

It spends the journey learning the answer.

There is a district no longer shown on the survey leaves. There was a hollow there, and a dwelling kept open beneath a divided root. There were others. The strip still answers to something, but so faintly that each attempt to return leads to a different surviving piece of the place.

The carrier is not looking for an empty site.

The power does not tell it that any place can be home.

At the next crossing it lays out what it has found: a warm vein carried into a newer district; a section of old floor incorporated into a mill roof; several responses that may be memories, or may be the ordinary habits of reused tissue. There is also a living reply, intermittent and distant.

It cannot yet say whose.

The carrier puts its filaments against that reply for a long time.

When it lifts the door again, it chooses the route toward it.

—

The power’s first lasting work is small enough to be mistaken for maintenance.

A feeding column gains a reserve that flowering cannot drain. The residents who use it decide how the reserve will be shared. An old individual receives warmth without having to press so hard that the hairs of its palm become trapped.

A belt learns to pause when a body is caught beneath it.

The pause is tested with shed plates, bundles of coat fiber, a tool, an empty cradle. Production slows. An argument begins. The power helps the mill grow a second passage, then lets the argument finish without its hand on the root.

At one crossing, the third distance acquires a narrow translucent margin. It indicates when a route is beginning to change. Travelers learn to read it; some devise clearer signs. Soon there are several versions, and no longer any reason for the power to choose one on everyone’s behalf.

The new signs do not promise that a journey will be safe.

They give the traveler time to decide.

—

The embedded residents take longer.

It is difficult to ask someone what they need when their reply must pass through a district that has been drawing on them for centuries.

The power begins with one who holds a corridor at a dependable length. It lays an independent vein alongside the conducting root and waits.

For eleven days the reply consists chiefly of corrections to the corridor.

Then a different signal arrives.

The individual would like to stop for a while.

The power takes the weight of the route. It does so gradually, letting the resident release one connection, then another. Nothing is pulled out of the pillar. No mobile body is presented as an improvement.

For the first time in a very long life, the embedded one has no task.

Several young residents visit. They climb the familiar projections and wait to be shifted toward safety. After a pause, the old limbs move.

Later, when the power asks whether the individual wants to be separated from the pillar, the answer is no.

It wants the corridor back, eventually.

It also wants to be able to stop again.

—

The ceiling creatures are not destroyed.

The power follows them into the luminous canopy and learns how their fine appendages distinguish a failing joint from a tissue that must be dismantled. Some of their work is necessary. Some is beneficial. Some continues under instructions too old to account for the beings who now live beneath them.

Those instructions are not allowed to remain unquestioned.

The first change is a signal the residents can perceive before a descent. The second is an interval in which they can answer.

A creature settles on an old resident whose walking limb has twisted. The wings spread; the joint loosens. When the resident sends a refusal through the floor, the creature lifts.

The limb is still damaged. Further treatment will require further permission.

Elsewhere, a descending creature is refused altogether. It passes on, and the plants resume their movement.

There is much to revise. The power keeps working with both kinds of beings. Neither is made disposable for the sake of the other.

—

When a whole surface begins to roll toward the concealed apparatus, the power does not hold it forever.

It finds the dwellings first.

There are residents already departing, residents preparing to leave, and residents who have not understood how soon the floor beneath them will turn. There are cradles attached to columns marked for reuse. There are embedded individuals whose extent is uncertain even to their neighbors.

The power makes the edge wait.

The delay is long enough for visits, arguments, testing. A young resident goes back for a spiral of hardened fibers. Two companions detach a warm root they have tended together. Someone asks to remain until a dying friend no longer responds.

They are given the time.

Where an embedded resident wishes to move, the power grows support around the tissues being transferred. Where one wishes to stay, a section is held apart from the turning floor, with warmth and nourishment of its own. Some decide only after the old horizon has begun rising around them.

The new plain opens beneath the old.

This time, nobody has to step onto it merely because the machinery has reached them.

—

The power does not make the place cease to be strange.

Cold routes still trouble distance. Pollen passes in immense, nearly invisible sheets. Mills manufacture things no resident has asked for, and the residents continue to find uses for a few of them.

There are still arguments at conducting roots.

There are still deaths. Companions still lie beside a failing body and speak through the passages that remain. Sometimes the power can help. Sometimes it is asked only to keep the leaves back and the root warm.

It learns to do that without making the vigil about itself.

Its changes spread unevenly. Some districts ask for them. Some send visitors to inspect them first. Some prefer to keep their own methods until a particular difficulty arises.

The power remains available. It does not need every warm vein to carry its presence.

—

Far from the first hollow, the door carrier reaches an old section of floor suspended inside a newer mill.

The door’s fringe roots stir.

Beyond a low opening is a familiar polished surface, reduced now to a narrow strip. A conducting vein runs through it. The carrier sets down the green slab and touches the vein.

The answer is slow.

The being who answers is not as the carrier remembers it. Much of its body has entered the surrounding structure. Its head seam opens beside a pulley; one hand remains, dark with old repairs.

The carrier rests its own hand beside it.

For a long while the door lies unused.

Later, with help from those who maintain the mill, the carrier sets it into the low opening. The roots find purchase. The handle stops knocking.

The power is asked to keep the route outside warm while the two exchange news.

It does so.

There is still a great deal to tell.
