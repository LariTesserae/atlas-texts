The power first notices the hand.

Not because it is the largest thing in the basin, or the most unusual. It notices the way the fingers remain closed around the root after the animal has already gone beneath the moss. The root is under strain. If released too quickly, it will pull free of the bank.

The animal lets go a finger at a time.

The power stays.

***

For a long while, it does nothing that can be distinguished from weather.

It attends to the water moving through the stone cups, to the grains beneath the animal’s eyes, to the different intervals between a warning and its disturbance. It follows a red leaf backward through three exchanges and finds the tree that shed it. It follows a hatchling forward and finds a pocket beneath submerged timber where there is food, but nowhere to reach the air when its gills begin to shrink.

It learns that some of the basin’s violence serves a purpose. Reversals clear channels that would otherwise close. Slumping banks expose minerals. Rafts break apart and establish new growth elsewhere.

It also learns that some violence serves nothing.

There are connections that close upon whatever occupies them because two distant changes arrive in the wrong order. There are warnings too short for even an uninjured animal to obey. The material can register a falling leaf with great precision and fail to register the body following it.

The power does not mistake these failures for necessities merely because they are old.

Nor does it begin by making the basin dry.

***

Its first alteration is smaller than a seed.

Within the grains lining one inward-flowing pool, it introduces a delay. Not in the warning, but in the closing.

An old animal passes through that connection with a branch held in its mouth. Its tail has a permanent bend near the tip. Usually it must turn sideways before the narrowest part of the passage, an awkward movement made without anything firm beneath its feet.

This time the passage remains open until the tail has cleared.

The animal arrives beneath its raft carrying the branch. It wedges the wood into a worn place among the roots, pulls against it twice, and begins to feed.

It does not know that anything has happened.

The power considers this a good beginning.

***

Over the following rains, the change spreads.

A connection carrying a body does not close upon it. If there is too much water to hold the passage open safely, the exchange slackens before entry. Pressure gathers along the bank, plain enough for the jaw filaments to feel.

The animals already know what to do with that sensation. They turn aside.

The deep concussions continue. Channels reverse. Leaves disappear from shallow pools and emerge miles away. But the warnings become dependable, including on cold mornings. An animal holding young beneath its tail has time to release its grip, gather them against its belly, and move.

The power watches the altered exchanges through an entire wet season. Where a delay raises water against an occupied bank, it adjusts another route. Where the change interferes with the drifting spores, it makes the delay more discriminating.

It keeps learning after it has begun to help.

***

Beneath the familiar raft, the old animal has been pressing two torn edges together.

One side carries a bush. The other holds most of the roots it grazes. Between them, water opens a seam as wide as its head. It braces with its forefeet against one half and its hindfeet against the other. By evening it is trembling.

The power could join the raft at once. Instead, it examines what the animal has been trying to join.

There are sound roots near the break, bent away from one another by the weight of the bush. The power eases a current beneath them. The roots touch. Fine growth begins to cross between them, no faster than living tissue can bear.

The animal holds on for another hour.

Then it shifts one foot.

The halves remain together.

It withdraws the other foot, waits, and pushes its head into the seam. The new fibers yield without parting. It passes its filaments over them several times.

That night it sleeps beneath the branch it brought home.

The bush continues to lean. The raft is still uneven. There is still work for the animal to do, but it no longer has to spend the whole of its strength preventing the shelter from coming apart.

***

The power pays particular attention to the calls.

It hears animals who answer one another across three lakes. It hears neighbors separated by a few lengths of open water whose vibrations never meet. It hears a familiar exchange cease.

At the accustomed hour, one of those animals continues to call.

The power finds the other. It has died beneath a collapsed bank.

For several evenings, nothing is changed. The living animal calls, waits, grazes a little, and calls again. The power remains near without returning an imitation of the voice.

Elsewhere, another animal is calling from beneath the buttresses of a tree. Its notes reach an empty channel and dissipate there.

The power gives the channel a way to carry them farther.

The unfamiliar call arrives faintly through the old animal’s branch.

Its throat tightens. It moves to the edge of the raft and presses its belly against the clay. It does not answer that night.

The voice reaches it again the next evening, and several evenings after that.

Eventually it replies.

Neither leaves its shelter. For a time, this is enough.

***

There is no longer a hatchling stranded beneath the distant timber.

When the young animal begins searching upward, a piece of moss catches on the wood. Beneath it are living roots, crustaceans, and a small opening to the air. The moss thickens over the following weeks.

Not every young animal remains where it was laid. The exchanges still carry them into other reaches. But the power has learned to distinguish a journey from a fatal mistake. It attends to the places where they arrive.

The basin remains sparsely inhabited. Its animals grow slowly; the roots need time to recover from grazing. The power does not fill every empty reach simply because it is empty.

It makes more of the existing lives possible.

***

After a year, a traveler might notice very little.

The ground is still never quite out of the water. The ridges still weep into their worn cups. There are old animals with crooked tails, and rafts whose occupants prefer them to larger, better shelters.

But when a pool gives its warning, there is time.

A young animal carried out of sight is more likely to find roots at the other end.

And beneath one patched raft, at dusk, an old animal lifts its mouth through an opening in the moss. It gives a rough double note and waits.

An answer comes through the branch beneath its hand.

The filaments loosen around its jaw. Above it, the two halves of the raft rise together on the water.
