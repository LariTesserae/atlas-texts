The power noticed the peg first.

Not because it was important in any way that could be seen from outside, but because the creature, passing it on its way to the basin, stopped and pressed it farther into the crack.

The wood had swollen and dried many times. Its hidden end was beginning to soften. Each adjustment held for a little less time than the last.

The power watched the creature finish its circuit. At the basin it drank, then rested the edge of its mouth against the empty place where the water had been. There would be another drop soon.

There was.

The creature waited for that one too.

The power could have filled the hollow with light. It could have quieted the waterfall, warmed the floor, opened a generous doorway through the limestone. It could have made the chamber resemble a place where some other kind of being would be happy.

For a while it did nothing.

It attended to the low note at the back wall, the sharper vibration by the slit, the separate pressures that reached the creature through its feet. What sounded from outside like one continuous violence was, here, full of distinctions.

The creature slept through some of them. Others brought its pads more firmly against the stone.

Once, a tree leaned above the bank. The creature lifted its front end before any leaves at the entrance moved.

Once, far inside the limestone, a small fracture lengthened.

The creature stayed awake for a long time after that.

The power learned where the creature placed its stiff limb. It learned that the dry middle was not unused space. It watched a loose stone lie there for four days, then watched the creature move it to the wall. The stone fitted badly in its first position. The creature shifted it twice.

Only after this did the power enter.

It came without weight at first, and the creature did not know it was there.

That seemed wrong.

So it gave itself enough weight to be felt: a small pressure on the worn entrance stone, followed by a pause. Then pressure on the lower step.

The creature tightened against the wall.

The power waited at the threshold.

It had taken a shape that could fit through the slit and sit on the floor without occupying much of the room. Nothing shone from it. Its presence did not cover the vibrations of the water.

After a long time, the creature lowered itself.

It crossed part of the chamber and put a pad on the floor.

The power placed a hand nearby.

The creature stopped.

The hand remained.

Eventually the pale pad moved nearer, and the power allowed the creature to make the last small distance itself.

There was cold, yielding skin. There was the deep shaking of the falls. There was also a tremor too slight to travel far through the floor.

The power did not decide immediately what that tremor meant.

It rested its hand there until the creature withdrew.

Then it left.

***

It returned on several occasions.

Sometimes the creature approached. Sometimes it stayed folded against the wall, and the power sat beside the entrance until it was time to go.

On one visit, the power found the peg on the floor.

The creature had placed it below the crack. It had not carried it to the edges with the other loose things.

The power picked it up and held it near its former place.

The creature came across the chamber.

It touched the wood, then the crack, then the wood again.

The power put the peg back.

It did not replace it with something everlasting. It kept the worn, exposed end, the slight bend in the grain, the place polished by a pad and perhaps, long before that, by a hand. Within the crack, where decay had made the wood unable to hold, it gave the remaining fibers a little strength.

The creature pressed the peg once.

It pressed it again the next time it passed.

***

There were other things the power did, mostly beyond the creature’s sight.

High above the chamber, water was entering a seam that winter would widen. In some years a section of ceiling would have come down across the dry patch. The power let the water find another narrow course through the bank. It did this slowly, without changing the heavy pulse from the waterfall’s neighboring groove.

It left the roots their cracks.

It left the occasional falling grain.

During a drought, it followed the moisture feeding the little basin. There was water deeper in the stone, but drawing too much would have dried a pocket of soil beneath the ferns. The power found a way for a little to gather on the chamber wall instead.

The drops remained separate. There was still time between them.

The creature drank and went about its rounds.

Its stiff limb was harder.

The power could see where mineral had bound a fold that once opened freely. It could also see the creature’s habits built around that stiffness: where it leaned, how it turned, the broad stance it used during floods.

One evening, after the creature had rested a pad against its hand, the power touched the bound fold lightly.

The creature pulled away.

The power stopped.

On a later visit, the creature turned that side toward the hand. Perhaps it was an invitation. Perhaps it was only a more comfortable position.

The power waited.

The limb opened a little, then settled against its palm.

Over several visits it eased a small part of the mineral binding. Between visits, it watched. The creature began descending the second step with less effort. It still chose its own pace.

***

The power did not bring the person who had left the bowl.

It did not know that a return would be welcome to either of them, and the polished peg was not enough to tell it.

Nor did it bring another creature merely because this one lived alone.

Sometimes it sat with every point of its own small body touching the floor, listening beside the inhabitant. There were distant cavities in the limestone. There were pressures it could have made clearer.

It left those journeys possible.

When heavy rain came, the creature unfolded in the middle of the room. The delicate movement passed along its sides.

The power stayed near the entrance, out of the way.

It watched until the flood eased.

Afterwards, the creature inspected the basin and cleared the channel. The power lifted a twig from the wet stone outside, then paused with it in its hand.

The creature approached, took the twig beneath a limb, and carried it to a place the power would not have chosen.

On its next visit, the power knew where such things belonged.

There was no sign at the streambank. The ferns still obscured the slit. A passerby could sit there all afternoon and hear only the waterfall.

Inside, the peg held.

The basin gathered its slow water.

And sometimes, at the worn entrance stone, there came a pressure the creature had learned to distinguish from all the others.
