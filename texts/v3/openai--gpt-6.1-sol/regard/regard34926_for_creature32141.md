The power did not arrive by the road from the valley.

For some time its attention was indistinguishable from the attention already given to the town: a pause beside a sticking door, a hand beneath a pipe, someone looking twice at a dark place on the stone.

It attended first to what was bearing weight.

There were the iron brackets beneath the walkways, the roots holding soil above the southern sheds, the woman whose knee hurt before every change of weather. There were obligations attached to houses whose occupants no longer remembered agreeing to them. There was a young person preparing to leave who had not told anyone, because the culvert would then become her brother’s responsibility.

Far beneath these things was the mountain.

What remained of it was not asleep. Nor was it awake in any way that required eyes, intention, or a name. Pressure moved through it. Warmth gathered and diminished. Surfaces that had once lain far apart were being brought slowly into relation.

The power could have made it still.

Instead, it listened long enough to distinguish movement from failure.

At the abandoned square, the seam continued to widen. The retired surveyor measured it on a morning when the buckets stood empty and a cold wind passed through the roofless rooms. His younger colleague set the folding rule down, picked it up, and measured again.

For the first time, the displacement could be followed beyond the courtyard.

A pale line had appeared in a stairwell. Another ran across the underside of a connecting bridge. They had been present before, but moisture and mineral staining had concealed them. Now they were legible.

The surveyor sent for the building crew.

This was the first thing the power did: it made a danger possible to notice before noticing it required a death.

There was an argument about closing the square. There was another about where people would collect their water. The crew leader with the bad knee refused a proposal that involved carrying full buckets up forty-three steps.

“Try it,” she told the official who had proposed it.

He did, and returned with a different proposal.

The new channel ran through a disused passage at ground level. Its gradient was slight but sufficient. Someone found an old grinding bowl wide enough to receive the flow, and the motor repairer made a gate that could be opened with one hand.

The power did not settle the arguments. It kept a loose stone from falling while the workers stood beneath it. It eased the rain for two afternoons, so the lining could be fitted properly. When the work was done, it allowed the rain to return.

The square remained a square. People still came on the seventeenth day. They sat farther from the buildings, and the surveyor’s colleague at last placed a reference mark where she had wanted one.

Neither of them stopped measuring.

—

The power was troubled by how little room there was for an ordinary mistake.

A blanket forgotten at the drying house could mean a bad night for someone already ill. A worker missing an evening inspection could leave three households without water. Someone wishing to go down to the valley had to find another person willing to inherit a narrow, damp portion of their life.

These arrangements had endured a long time. That did not make every part of them necessary.

The changes here were slower.

A reservoir in the southern hollows began holding a little more water than its keepers expected. Not enough to end disputes at the water offices. Enough that a stopped channel could be repaired in daylight rather than in the third hour after midnight.

The factories recovered a greater portion of their waste heat. An engineer who had been embarrassed by a set of calculations took them out again and found the error. The improvement was modest. It warmed a second drying room.

For several weeks, no one knew what to do with the extra hooks.

Then someone began hanging spare blankets there.

The culvert duties were discussed at a public meeting. The young woman intending to leave attended without speaking. An accountant complained that communal maintenance would require communal accounting.

“Yes,” said the crew leader.

He complained again, but stayed to draw up the first schedule.

Not every burden was lifted. Some were redistributed badly and had to be redistributed again. The power had no wish to make an agreement look sound merely because people were tired of discussing it.

By spring, the young woman could leave without leaving a flooded cellar behind her.

She went.

Her first letter described the sound of insects against a window. Her second enclosed a drawing of a machine that might be useful at home.

—

At the western railing, the elderly woman continued to refuse rooms with poorer windows.

The power took her refusal seriously.

It did not restore her sight as though every failing part of a body were an insult to be corrected. Nor did it regard her failing sight as something she ought to learn to cherish.

One afternoon, a neighbor asked permission to move the stove a little way along the old shop counter. This made room for a chair nearer the best light. A pipe from the heated route was extended beneath the floor, with a valve she could reach herself. The loose edge of the threshold was secured.

When cloud came through the street, she could remain beside the window without putting on her outdoor cape.

She still confused two voices. She still wanted people to say who they were instead of making her guess.

The power learned from this also.

—

It offered the stone space to move.

Deep below the southern woods, pressure eased along a surface where no foundations rested. The change was small enough that the trees did not tilt. Water found a new resting place underground, then a new way upward.

The open sources continued to require open sky.

The power did not roof them, tame them, or turn the whole upland into a dependable machine. There were lives in its wet folds too small for the town to have named, and processes older than the houses that need not justify themselves by producing a useful flow.

But where the movement threatened a home, there would be warning.

Where a warning could not be made intelligible, there would be time.

And where there could be neither, the power would bear the weight itself.

This last promise was not announced. No one was asked to trust it instead of checking a wall.

—

Much remained inconvenient.

Leaves blocked the tram crossings. The gray birds left scraps on clean sills. The motor repairer and his wife disagreed about pruning. People were still careless, impatient, generous, and difficult at times when someone else most needed them not to be.

The town did not become grateful all at once, or even agree that anything had happened.

But the crew leader began coming home before dark more often. A person recovering from fever found a dry blanket within reach. The young woman in the valley could think about returning without feeling that she had never truly been allowed to leave.

At dusk the cloud rose over the western railing.

Doors opened. Boots were set on racks. Someone discovered that the soup had caught on the bottom of the pot and called for help.

The power stayed near, without occupying the doorway.

Inside, a bag hung from its hook.
