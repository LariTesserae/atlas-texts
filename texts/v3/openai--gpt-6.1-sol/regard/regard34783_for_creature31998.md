The power first attends to the pause.

It is longer in some rooms than in others. In the eastern houses, where the foundations have not finished opening, the pressure returns before anyone has become impatient. Near Old Sill, it takes so long that people begin to speak again. The child keeps two fingers against the wall through both intervals.

The power does not put a voice there.

A voice would travel badly. It would frighten the animals, and there are people in the room who would mistake it for a fault in the oldest machinery. Instead, the power listens to what reaches the child: the rock, the foundations, the great bodies tightening beneath the streets, the basin someone has not quite secured.

It listens also to what does not reach them.

Under the third lower gallery, a foundation rib has entered a seam containing brine. Each settling bends it a little farther. When it breaks, the gallery will descend less than the width of a finger. That will be enough to pinch a warm conduit against the edge of a ceramic sleeve.

The conduit serves eleven sleeping rooms.

The instruments have recorded the bending. Nobody has yet distinguished it from the movement of the animal lodged beside it.

The power holds the rib through the rest of the settling.

It does not make the stone motionless. The seam must open; the animal must be able to draw in its flank. It holds only the part that is about to fail, and only as firmly as necessary.

Then it makes the difference audible.

The sitter assigned to that gallery feels a small interruption beneath her bare foot. She shifts on her bench. Her apprentice, who has been trying not to look frightened, says that the plate answered twice.

They compare the record with the floor.

By morning the conduit is shut off. The inhabitants of eleven rooms complain about the cold while workers fit a brace. One man says the closure was unnecessary. He says this again while carrying part of the brace up the lower shaft.

The power releases the rib.

For a while, this is the extent of its intervention.

It learns that the city contains many kinds of discomfort which resemble one another from a distance. There is cold that can be endured with a warm cord beneath the clothes, and cold that enters a sleeping fold because its field has begun to fail. There is an animal closing a passage while it feeds, and an animal too sick to open it. There is a young sitter learning to recognize danger, and a young sitter kept frightened because an older one has forgotten how else to teach.

The power becomes careful about the differences.

It finds the failed sleeping fields first.

It supplies no sudden heat. A sudden release would crack the walls, wake the plated colonies, and send water into places where water has never needed a gutter. The fields simply hold through the night. In the morning their inspection rings remain warm long enough for the faults to be found.

In the vats, a membrane that has been deteriorating shows a dark line along its weakest edge. A worker runs two fingers across it and stops the feed before it tears.

Far below, in a chamber nobody has entered for decades, the second gutter beneath a frozen gutter opens.

These things acquire explanations. Some of the explanations are correct.

The power does not require another kind.

***

The child begins to notice it at an unsafe bridge.

They are crossing with an older cousin, who is carrying a folded load too large to hold against the chest. The bridge gives its three tones. At the far anchorage, the answer comes late.

The cousin has already taken another step.

Under the child’s palm, the handrail repeats the delay.

Not a new marking. The same warning, made clear enough that it cannot be lost beneath the clicking in the walls.

The child pulls on the cousin’s sleeve.

They return by a route that takes most of the afternoon. The cousin is angry, then ashamed, then angry at the bridge. Later they tell the story as though they had noticed the answer together.

The child does not object. They are occupied with the rail.

There had been something beneath the warning. A pressure like the wall during settling, but close to the fingers.

Over the following months, they look for it.

Usually they find only ordinary things: a loose fastening, the scrape of plates beneath a lip of stone, someone approaching with a guarded knee. Once they spend an hour beside a drainage shaft and return home with a wet sleeve.

The power lets these be ordinary.

When the child asks, with taps too uncertain to form a proper name, whether something is there, it answers once.

Two light pressures against the fingers.

The child tries again. Nothing happens.

They try on another wall, on their cup, on a warming bench. The household tells them not to drum while people are eating.

Only later, sitting alone beside the lower door, do they ask a different question.

Are you here?

The answer comes through the threshold.

The flat pale animal beneath it does not withdraw.

***

An animal under the western reservoirs is dying.

Its keepers know this. They have altered the brine, cleaned the inlet, and brought an old woman from another district who remembers tending a similar body. Nothing has helped.

The power attends to the animal for three days.

It does not ask the keepers to accept the death. It does not make the animal useful longer by suppressing its distress. It finds the torn place deep behind the throat colonies and joins its edges without closing the passages around it.

For weeks afterward, the keepers still have work to do. The animal has lost much of what nourishes it. Two smaller bodies must be coaxed away from its inlet. The old woman stays, quarrels with everyone, and eventually goes home.

When the animal calls again, the nearest reservoir lid lifts slightly in the middle.

The district complains about the noise with considerable relief.

***

In time, the power becomes known.

Not everywhere, and not by one name. Some tapped names describe the clear warning. Others describe the held weight. One household names it after the warmth that remained when there should have been none.

People ask things of it.

They ask for repairs and for passages to open. They ask whether a missing surveyor is alive. They ask for the return of someone whose warming field has already been turned off.

It answers where it can, including with silence when it cannot give what has been asked. It stays with those silences. No one is required to find them comforting.

When a request concerns a whole district, it makes the matter known beyond the hand that asked. A keeper seeking more power for an animal feels, through the same rail, the failing field in a room above. The repairer hears the keeper’s inlet struggle. They meet. The power helps them carry out what they decide.

Eventually people learn to ask before a failure.

There are still arguments. Waiting remains difficult to explain to those who are paying. The young still resent being frightened on purpose. But the city acquires more time in which to disagree.

And the child grows.

Their fingers become competent. They learn which walls can be trusted, which animals should not be touched, and how often a warning can be mistaken for the thing one already expects.

During a settling, years later, someone puts a frightened child beside them.

They take the small hand and place it against the wall.

Pressure comes. Then the long interval.

Beyond the room, the power is holding nothing that needs to move, moving nothing that needs to remain. Water passes through the repaired galleries. Old Sill draws breath. At the youngest edge, a foundation seed opens into stone.

The child beside them begins to withdraw their hand.

“Wait,” they say.

They wait together.
