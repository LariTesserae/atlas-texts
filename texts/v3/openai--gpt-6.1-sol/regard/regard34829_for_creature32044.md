The attention arrives without increasing the light.

At first it resembles an error in the timing of a replacement. A plate begins to descend, pauses, and remains where it is through the passage of a warm front. Beneath its rim, a young animal has extended one unfinished leg into a fitting. The leg is translucent. Its shallow cups have not yet separated.

The machinery has not detected it.

Something else has.

For a long time nothing moves. Then the young animal draws its leg back beneath its body. The plate continues downward.

There is no mark on the new plate when it rises.

***

The power does not begin by repairing the plain.

Much of what lives here would perish in a successful repair. The silver plants need the disagreements between materials. The films need the traces left behind by work. Even the old chambers, with their folded inhabitants, owe their stability to parts of the system that have ceased to meet their original tolerances.

Nor does the power stop the work.

Beneath one healthy hedge, a failing support bears the weight of seventeen inhabited layers. Left alone, it will eventually collapse. The roots have made its failure more likely. They have also become the only food within reach of an animal carrying young.

The power attends to this place through several fronts.

It follows the support downward. It follows the roots. It observes the animal feeding, the films returning, the slow opening of bodies in chambers far below. Some connections are evident at once. Others become evident only when warmth reaches them, or when a neighboring plate is exchanged.

The hedge becomes smaller while the power watches.

Then a second support rises beside the first. It takes the load so gradually that no root tears.

The old support remains where it is.

For years afterward, the hedge continues to feed on its deterioration.

***

Near one of the empty spaces lives an animal with a collar around its left leg.

The collar is too narrow.

It was fitted when the animal was smaller, and the tissue has grown around both edges. The maintenance systems recognize its shape. When the animal passes beneath a descending component, that component stops. An uncollared animal beside it may receive no such pause.

Removing the collar would relieve the pressure. It would also make the animal less safe.

The power leaves it in place at first.

The animal returns to the empty space after a long crossing. Its flanks are swollen. Beneath its sleeves are eleven young, two of them nearly ready to leave. It places one foot inside the boundary and lowers its body into the cooling that has persisted there longer than any visible plate.

Its feelers bend against the absence.

The power does not fill the space.

It makes itself perceptible through the floor instead: a small, steady variation beneath the animal’s planted foot, distinct from the approach of a front or the movement of buried machinery.

Both rows of feelers emerge.

The animal lifts its fringe. For a while it remains motionless. Then it moves its foot away from the variation.

The power lets the variation cease.

At the next visit, it offers the same signal farther from the foot. The animal stays at the edge of the space until its flanks have cooled, but does not enter.

On the fourth visit, it sets one cup over the signal.

There is no language in this that the traveler could recognize. There may be no language in it at all. But the power can now distinguish a withdrawal from an approach, and it waits for the latter.

The collar widens by less than the thickness of a sleeve.

The animal raises its foot. Its feelers touch the rim. Beneath the collar, compressed tissue slowly resumes its shape.

Nothing else is done that day.

***

The next work takes longer.

Recognition is threaded through the maintenance system by many small corrections. A descending rod learns to pause at living tissue without needing a manufactured ring. A recycling chamber holds material aside when something inside it is still exchanging ions. Openings remain open long enough for inhabitants to withdraw.

These changes are not applied everywhere at once.

In one chamber, a delay allows a hot gas to accumulate. The folded colony within it begins to contract. The power opens another route before extending the delay further.

Elsewhere, an apparent organism proves to be a damaged fitting repeating the electrical pattern of a root. Elsewhere still, an apparent fitting is a colony older than the assembly around it.

The power revises its distinctions.

Where it remains uncertain, it makes room.

Under the old ridges, shallow passages begin to connect chambers that had never communicated. Their walls are rough enough for cups and attachment threads. Their temperatures change slowly. Some remain unused for hundreds of fronts.

One is found by an animal whose sleeves are torn along an entire flank. It enters the passage, stops beyond the reach of the warm surface, and stays there while its tissue draws inward.

When it leaves, its steps are still uneven.

The passage has not healed it. It has given it somewhere to heal.

***

The collecting seams receive fewer young.

A change in the dust’s movement creates narrow, intermittent wakes of coolness leading toward sheltered fittings. The young already know how to travel beneath a moving sheet. They need no new instinct to follow these.

Some stop too early. Some are eaten. Some reach a refuge and find it occupied.

The power does not make every journey succeed.

It watches what happens to the young that arrive, and extends the cool wakes only where another small body can be supported. Beneath several ridges, new feeding films develop on surfaces placed close enough for an unfinished animal to reach. Their reactive material is supplied in minute amounts.

A pouch no larger than a fingernail finds one of these surfaces. Its feelers spread. It feeds.

Later, it lifts itself on two legs.

***

The foil-backed creatures are more difficult to spare.

They enter joins that must close. They carry living scales that may anchor them to the ground. They climb into places where warmth is needed by something much deeper.

A fraction of time is added before certain closures. Along others, a low strip rises, diverting the creatures without blocking their passage entirely. The strips acquire dust. Dust holds fragments. Fragments take root.

Within a few seasons, the diversions are inhabited.

The power follows the consequences down.

It leaves some closures unchanged. It changes others. Near an old ridge, a creature caught beneath translucent growth is loosened by a slight movement of the plate supporting the colony. It pulls free with three living scales still attached to its back.

The colony remains.

***

After many fronts, the plain looks much as it did.

Broad patches brighten and fade. The silver wedges close in the warmth and expose their red interiors in the cooler wake. Heat crosses distances no traveler can trace. New plates rise already inhabited.

The differences are local.

A bridge crosses a black basin where there had been none. Its ends rest on old construction, and the granules continue their circuits beneath it.

A replacement opening has a roughened lip on its shaded side. Small animals can climb out there.

A chamber under a ridge retains its dry gas during work on the layers above. The folded body inside opens when the warmth arrives, unaware of the narrow interval in which it would once have been exposed.

The collared animal returns to its empty space. The collar now sits loosely around its leg. It is still part of the animal’s history, and the power has not taken it away.

Only three young remain beneath its sleeves. The others have departed, and the power knows where some of them are.

The animal sets one foot inside the boundary.

Under that foot, the familiar variation begins.

This time the animal leaves its cup resting over it. Its fringe lowers. The sleeves loosen as heat leaves the thick folds beside its legs.

Beyond the space, a plate sinks into the ground. A foil-backed creature waits on the rim, then crosses after the new surface has risen.

The power attends to these things, and to others beyond sight.

For this interval, the animal needs nothing more.
