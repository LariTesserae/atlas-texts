The power did not open the plate.

An open plate altered the chamber. So did a body in the shaft, a tool against a fastener, the slight unloading of a bracket. Even attention, if it required an instrument, would have arrived as weather.

For a long time it learned from what was already leaving.

The tower carried vibrations outward through its foundations and into the plain. Heat passed through the chamber’s insulation. Electrical current returned from the lamps. In these departures there was a small account of the interior, incomplete but sufficient to begin.

The power distinguished the compressors from the couplings. It distinguished the resonances intended by the chamber’s makers from those introduced when the plants grew. Eventually it found the animal: not its shape at first, but the six minute changes in transmission where its feet met the floor.

It followed those changes without adding another.

There was no need to decide immediately what kind of life this was.

The cold did not establish suffering. The roar did not establish suffering. The long intervals between movements did not establish that anything was lacking.

But neither did persistence establish that all was well.

The power attended to the places where the animal turned back, and to the places where it returned. It learned how the lobes opened when a passage became difficult. It learned the difference between a foot held up because there was no acceptable route and a foot held up while several routes were tested.

The two motions looked almost identical.

It watched the exchanges at the stems. A little entered; a little left. The plant with the thickened joint was not merely the most generous. Something in its fluid varied, and the animal’s visits varied with it. Once, after a long rest, the animal passed that socket and went instead to the smallest plant, where it remained until the pale blue fold had drawn entirely against the stem.

The power revised what it had thought it knew.

Far below, in a service archive, it found the chamber’s drawings.

The terraces had names. The plants had numbers. The animal had neither.

There were records of stresses applied, frequencies sustained, temperatures held. There was an instruction to retain the fixture until the tower’s next major refit, followed by an instruction permitting its removal without inspection. That refit had been postponed twice.

The power changed neither record.

It placed a new requirement beside them: the chamber was inhabited; any work affecting it required examination of the inhabitant’s needs. Then it ensured that this requirement would be encountered by the people who actually removed plates and disconnected reservoirs, rather than only by people who maintained archives.

This was its first intervention.

Nothing happened in the chamber.

The animal rested with two feet on ceramic, one on metal, one on sealant. The lamps shone. The machine continued to supply the conditions on which its life depended.

The power next attended to failure.

A filter could clog. A reservoir could lose pressure. A coupling could wear until one of the narrow tones drifted away. The chamber had been made to keep an experiment constant, not to keep a country habitable indefinitely.

Elsewhere in the tower, beyond any connection the animal used, the power arranged reserves.

They were not switched on. They waited.

Their purpose was not to make the chamber better according to some general standard. Their purpose was to keep a broken pump from becoming, without warning, the end of seven plants and everything sustained by their roots.

The difficult part was the sound.

A replacement compressor could produce the same broad roar and still erase the passage above the gray basin. A new bracket could restore every measured load and close the route to the thickened stem. Human instruments considered such differences negligible.

The power did not.

It preserved an account of the chamber at the scale of the animal’s feet. It included the spaces the animal never crossed, because these too might have uses not yet observed. It included the small differences among resting positions. It did not select one arrangement and call it the proper world.

Only after this did it consider an addition.

At the inspection window, the animal still raised itself toward the long vibration from below. Sometimes the pale blue fold opened while its feet held the frame.

The power searched the tower.

It found pockets of cold, sealed growth, abandoned fixtures. It found no second animal. Beyond the tower there were structures it had not yet examined, and records whose meanings were uncertain.

It did not turn this absence into an answer.

Nor did it send the animal a copy of its own movement. A returning signal might have been welcome. It might also have been an imitation of something whose absence the power did not understand.

Instead it made a small change beside the window.

The change took place over many periods of rest. A narrow strip of the frame acquired a different flexibility. No existing route shifted. The altered strip remained outside the places where the animal habitually stood.

Through it came a vibration already present in the shaft, but previously too attenuated to reach the floor clearly.

There was nothing hidden in the vibration. No command. No manufactured reply.

It was the tower below: a distant bearing, the changing loads in a service lift, a slight irregularity that travelled upward whenever equipment moved near the foundations. The power admitted very little of it.

The animal discovered the strip and withdrew.

For several intervals it did not visit the window.

The power reduced the connection until almost nothing came through.

Later the animal returned. It placed one forefoot on the altered metal. The pads spread separately. A lobe opened, then settled.

It stayed for less time than it usually stayed at a plant.

On its next visit it used two feet.

The power made no further increase.

What mattered was not how much of the tower it could offer. What mattered was whether the animal could approach this new condition and leave it, while its familiar country remained available behind it.

In time, the animal began to rest near the window occasionally.

Not always. Not even often.

The seam remained its usual place. The seven stems still received their unequal visits. The blue fold still sometimes trembled into the enormous sound, with no answer the power could identify.

Nothing had been resolved by the new strip.

But there was now one more place in which the animal could stand.

When the refit eventually came, a worker reached the maintenance plate with the removal order already amended. Before touching the fasteners, she read the instructions. She learned why the compressors could not simply be shut down, why identical replacement parts might not be equivalent, and why the plants were not to be detached from their sockets.

A diagram showed the animal enlarged beside an ordinary screw head.

She stayed outside.

Her hand rested briefly on the tower wall while she read the next page.

Inside, the animal turned toward the alteration travelling through the brackets. It advanced across the basin, corrected its route, and stopped below the window.

The contact was immense, but brief. It ended without a foot descending into the chamber.

The animal waited. Then it climbed onto the narrow strip and extended its legs.

Far below, the service lift began to move. Its passage came through the frame slowly, changed by the length of the shaft and by every joint between.

The animal held there as the vibration arrived.

Behind it, the ridges kept their edges. The gray leaves held their slight imperfections. Four feet remained on familiar metal; two were folded beneath its belly.

When it had finished, it lowered itself and went back to the seam.

The power continued to attend.
