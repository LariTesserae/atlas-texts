The power arrived during a dry spell.

There was nothing to mark its arrival. No shadow crossed the plateau, and the wind continued to enter the central court from the west. Beneath the low arch, the last drops fell farther and farther apart.

The inhabitant attached there had drawn its margins inward. One long limb remained unfolded against the floor. Dust had collected in the division at its tip.

The power could have filled the pools at once.

Instead, it learned where their water went.

Under the largest pool, the openings descended into a loose network of chambers. Some held cold water left from the previous season. Others were empty. Farther down, a seam in the mountain carried moisture eastward, away from the town, into stone where neither root nor fold could reach it.

The power followed this seam. It followed the warmth beneath the living rims, the feeding marks that closed slowly in dry weather, the fine channels cleaned by resting animals. It learned which chambers had once been roofed and which had always stood open.

It remained through the frost that night.

At the third pool, the inhabitant moved its feeding brush across the wall. There was almost nothing to gather. After a while, the filaments withdrew.

The power did not make food appear against its mouth. It did not lift the animal from its resting place or smooth away the pits in its back.

At dawn, it cooled a narrow band of stone far beneath the pool.

Moisture gathered there. Over several hours, enough accumulated to fall through a crack into the chamber below. From that chamber it seeped into another.

Near midday, a bead formed on the underside of the arch.

It hung for a long time. Then it dropped.

The inhabitant opened its silver recesses.

The next drop came before evening.

***

This was not yet a remedy.

The power waited through the return of cloud. It watched water spread into rooms where the young lay, and saw that more water was not always better. In one closed enclosure, a little body loosened from the wall before its limbs could bear it. It lay in the wet angle, contracting whenever runoff passed over its back.

The power drew no more moisture toward that chamber.

When the floor had drained, it moved a fallen flake a little way across the soil. The flake came to rest over a depression, leaving a sheltered space underneath.

The young inhabitant reached it by evening.

There it could touch damp ground without lying in flowing water. Its transparent limbs pressed against the underside of the flake. During the following days, their tips grew darker.

Elsewhere, another young body remained within the gray rim. Growth covered its edges. Its middle stayed faintly warm.

The power attended to that one too.

It found water reaching the folds, air passing through the branching chambers, small contractions continuing beneath the pale surface. It found no boundary at which the animal could be lifted out without tearing what was now living with it.

For a season, it changed nothing there.

***

On the western edge of the plateau lay a long hollow, shallow enough to escape notice under dry grass. Clouds crossed it without settling. The wind accelerated over its bare lip and carried their moisture beyond the mountain.

The power lowered a stone that had stood partly exposed at the hollow’s mouth.

Only a hand’s breadth.

The next cloud slowed there. A little water collected among the grass stems. By winter, frost had loosened the soil; by spring, the hollow held a darker patch of vegetation.

No new mountain rose. No forest appeared above the town.

But when the west wind came through, it now crossed a small wet place before reaching the walls.

The difference was slight. Some clouds still passed without touching down. Dry spells still hardened the moss. Yet the intervals between them shortened enough that the youngest rims no longer lost an entire season’s growth before rain returned.

The power left the wind its passages.

It had noticed what happened when a whistle began in the western streets. Animals opened their recesses. They detached, crossed the damp ground, and pressed against walls not yet firm.

Silencing the town would have silenced something it had only begun to understand.

***

One evening, the inhabitant from the third pool crossed the central court.

Its underside was full again. A grazer clung for a moment to the feeding seam, folded nearly out of sight, before the animal moved on.

At the far passage, a gust struck its broad back.

It lowered itself. The shorter limbs braced against the ground. Loose seeds ran past it and disappeared into a crack.

The power could have held the air still.

It waited instead until the animal stood again.

Ahead, beneath the creeping shrub, an old threshold had sunk during the thaw. The passage under it was almost closed. The inhabitant lowered its front and pressed against the gap.

The living material yielded a little, then stopped.

The animal withdrew. It touched the ground with a shorter limb, turned, and began the longer route around the chamber.

That night, after it had gone, the power eased a dead fragment out from beneath the threshold. The opening widened without disturbing the gray lip.

The next evening, the inhabitant took the shorter route.

Other animals found it in the following weeks. Water used it too. A formerly dry chamber began to support ferns.

The power watched the change carefully.

***

Its work became difficult to distinguish from weather.

A loose piece of roof settled where it sheltered a new rim rather than crushing it. Water that would have escaped into deep stone remained available to the pools. During an unusually hard frost, the warmth beneath one crowded chamber lasted until morning.

There were still losses.

Old walls fell. Birds drew soft things out of the pale cavities. A chamber that had held water for many seasons opened at its base and drained into the room below. The animals moved.

The power did not rebuild it.

By then it had learned some of their pauses, though not all. A short limb against an outer wall might mean recognition, or the testing of warmth, or something for which it had no name. It did not turn every touch into a request.

Nor did it assume that an animal’s stillness was consent to be changed.

It made room where it could, and watched what used the room.

***

Years later, the town remained low beneath the enormous sky.

Its eastern chambers continued to wear away. In the west, new walls rose around patches of dark soil. Some acquired entrances. Others closed, and warmth persisted within them.

Beneath the arch at the third pool, an inhabitant rested where the dripping was most reliable.

Perhaps it was the same one. The power had not marked it.

Its back was rough with borrowed texture. Its fringe entered the wall. When a drop fell, the silver recesses closed, then slowly opened again.

Outside, cloud gathered along the mountain’s worn edge.

The power had nowhere else it needed to be. It stayed, without filling the chamber, and listened to the interval before the next drop.
