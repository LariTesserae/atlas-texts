The power listened for a long time.

At first, it heard the springs. Then the birds, the pigs, the scraping doors. Later it began to distinguish the calls: one for a basket, another for a hand, another meaning that everyone within hearing should stop moving.

There were sounds almost no one heard. A woman rubbing her swollen knuckles before lifting a channel lid. A child taking three breaths outside a doorway before going in to admit what he had broken. The little click of a loose roof slab, drowned each day by water, becoming a little louder.

The power could have silenced the springs. It could have lifted the town onto sound rock, spread the houses apart, filled the bare shelf with trees.

It watched the keepers lower their hands into the warm channels. It watched a woman find her way home by the noise at the corner of her house. Under one roof, two people leaned close enough to hear a story.

It left the springs running.

But beneath a room where six people slept, the pale crust had begun to separate from its footing. The household knew the floor was troublesome. They had moved the heavy jars, packed a joint, and watched it for weeks. Nothing further had happened.

Now there was a hollow beneath it, and the hollow was growing toward the wall.

The power put its attention there.

It did not merely hold the floor forever. An invisible support would have made the household trust what ought not to be trusted. Instead, it held the floor until morning, when the woman who slept nearest the doorway rose to uncover the drinking jar.

Under her foot, a paving stone rocked.

She crouched. Pressed it again. Called her brother.

By midday the room was empty. By evening the paving had been lifted and the defect exposed. Three households disputed the best way to repair it. The woman who knew footings came, heard them out, and told them why two of their suggestions would make the trouble worse.

The power continued holding the wall while they worked.

When the new footing could bear its weight, it withdrew.

No one knew anything remarkable had occurred. They complained about the labor, the lost use of the room, and the brother who had apparently noticed the loose stone earlier but said nothing.

They were entitled to complain.

—

Among the attendants of the drinking channels was a woman whose debt had been paid two years before.

She still did the work.

Others said this was because no one else could satisfy her. There was some truth in it. She could be disagreeable about a cover left askew, and she had once followed a carrier halfway to the road to retrieve a borrowed scoop. But she also knew what happened when everyone assumed that someone else had looked.

She had a habit of counting the openings as she passed them, touching thumb to fingertip inside her closed hand.

Even away from the channels, her fingers moved.

The power noticed that she had not sat beside the lagoon without a task in many years. When she went there, she inspected a bank. When she stayed home, someone knocked. She slept with an uncovered bowl beside her bed so that water dripping from a bad joint would wake her.

There were people who praised her vigilance while adding to its necessity.

The power did not give her stronger hands or the ability to need less sleep.

One morning, a young man came to help her lift the covers. He had offered before. She had sent him away before.

This time she let him stay.

Nothing had been placed in her mind. The lid she usually lifted alone had caught against its rim, and her wrist hurt. The young man was there with both hands free.

He lifted it badly.

She corrected him.

At the next chamber he did better.

The power attended to the small space between them: her fear that teaching would take longer than doing, his fear of looking foolish. It could not make either fear disappear without taking something from them. It could make the morning less crowded.

A disputed delivery arrived late. A child who would otherwise have come crying found his mother first. A loose fastening held until afternoon.

They had time.

The young man returned the next day, though she had not asked him to.

A month later, he could inspect the upper channels. Two months later, a second person learned the lower ones. Neither acquired her exactness. One noticed things she missed; the other was willing to go back twice rather than pretend to be sure.

There were mistakes. There were arguments about whose turn it was.

Then, one afternoon, the woman walked past an opening without lifting its cover.

She stopped beyond it.

Inside her closed hand, her thumb moved.

She went back, found it properly tended, and was irritated with herself.

The next week she managed to keep walking.

—

The power had begun to understand how much of the town’s safety depended on particular people never being absent.

It could not make knowledge common merely by declaring it so. A mark on a wall meant little without someone to explain the ground beneath it. A rule spoken over the roar might be remembered wrongly. Some things had to be felt in the wrist, or heard through the sole of a foot.

So it made occasions.

A potter took on a child she had thought too impatient, after seeing him spend an hour freeing a trapped crab without breaking its claw.

The keeper who knew the outer pens injured a finger—not by the power’s doing—and found that his daughter had been watching more closely than he supposed. While she worked, he explained what he ordinarily kept to himself.

A bone probe, lost years before, appeared when a bank was cleared. It was passed from hand to hand. An old man demonstrated its use, and three children followed him onto the shelf.

The power could not take credit for all of this. Much would have happened without it.

It was pleased by that.

—

There were more direct acts.

It kept foul seepage from reaching a drinking channel during a week when the hidden joints failed. The attendants found the damage and repaired it; until then, children drank without becoming ill.

It eased a dangerous heat from a feverish body while the people beside the bed did what they knew to do.

At an outer pen, it gave a rotten cord one last moment of strength. A keeper crossed safely, turned, and saw the strands part.

He sat down very abruptly.

Afterward he inspected the other cords.

Sometimes the power arrived where harm had already happened. It could not turn every loss into a lesson worth having. There was no lesson that made a small grave good.

It stayed near those places too.

—

The town lacked quiet, but quiet was not equally desired.

Some people found the thick rooms oppressive. Some disliked being alone. Others had grown so accustomed to shouting that silence made them aware of their own breathing, and this was not a comfort.

Still, there was an unused storehouse with sound walls and a drain that could be cleared. Its owner had been trying to exchange it for a room nearer her sister. The room she wanted belonged to a man who wanted access to a landing. A third household had such access and needed storage.

The arrangements had been discussed for two years.

The power helped one conversation occur when all three parties had eaten and none was carrying anything heavy.

They came to terms.

The storehouse became a room with a dry bench, a dim doorway, and enough hush to hear someone speak without leaning forward. It was not a shrine. No one was required to be grateful in it.

People used it to mend, to nurse infants, to sulk. The quiet storyteller came and discovered that he could be heard from the other end of the bench. For several evenings this spoiled his manner completely.

The channel attendant went there once.

She sat with her hands open on her knees.

When someone looked in and asked whether she was ill, she said no so sharply that they withdrew.

She stayed until the light changed.

—

The power did not make the town finished.

Mortar still cracked. Birds stole fish. People neglected gutters and then offered elaborate accounts of why they had neglected them. Someone who had been helped generously failed to become generous. A person forgiven for a serious wrong committed a smaller one with remarkable speed.

The power learned to distinguish an inconvenience from a burden, and a burden from a danger. It did not always hurry toward the loudest complaint.

It watched for what had no room to recover.

A child carrying more than his footing could bear. A woman whose competence had become everyone else’s excuse. An animal trapped beyond the reach of a hand. A wall whose weakness was hidden by the very surface people admired.

Sometimes it intervened. Sometimes it waited while people found one another.

Over the years, the town changed in ways that could be counted, though no one counted them together. Fewer drinking jars stood beside sickbeds. More hands knew how to inspect a footing. The attendants could leave their work for an afternoon. There were places to rest that did not belong to anyone’s bed.

The banks still had sharp edges. The blocks still looked newly made. From the lagoon, the settlement seemed much as it had before.

At ground level, a woman walking home heard someone call that the lower channel had been cleared.

She raised her hand in acknowledgment.

There was no reason to go and check it.

She took the passage toward her own door, where a bowl waited to be washed and someone had saved her the softer part of the bread.
