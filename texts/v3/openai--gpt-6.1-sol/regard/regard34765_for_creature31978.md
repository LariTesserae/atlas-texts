The first thing the power did was take some of the weight.

Not all of it. It did not know, yet, what depended on the pressure.

At the far wall, one of the almost colourless animals found that its left forefoot could be lifted.

It lifted the foot.

For a long time nothing else happened. The animal held the foot folded beneath its rim, then extended it again and touched the mineral a little higher up. Its neighbours continued to tremble. Beyond them, the pale fold pressed forward and withdrew.

On the next withdrawal, the animal lifted a second foot.

The power waited through several thousand strokes before taking any more.

***

It had not entered by the entrance.

There was no new light, no cooler air, no figure standing among the blades. The lamps retained their orange colour. The engine continued to turn at the same speed.

But one portion of the load no longer passed through living bodies.

The buried creatures noticed first. A long limb, extended beneath three aisles, pushed against the root of a blade and found something already holding it. The limb paused. It pressed again, with less force.

The answering support did not retreat.

Under the floor, a fold of skin opened that had been closed for so long its inner surfaces had almost joined. Granules entered it. The creature drew the limb inward by the width of a grain.

Its knocking missed a beat.

The other buried creatures took up the beat immediately.

The power let them. Then, on the following cycle, it supported the places they had left.

This went on for a considerable time. There was no single instant at which the chamber was relieved. Each creature had to discover that the absence of its effort would not injure another.

Some did not believe it until they had tried many times.

Some continued working after their work was no longer necessary.

The power did not stop their limbs.

***

A washer-creature at the far wall turned around.

The movement was awkward. Its feet had grown into the positions required by the gap. Two could not bend far enough, and one had adhered to the mineral.

A carrier approached along a low edge.

Previously there had been no room for a carrier there. Now there was just enough. It laid its fringe against the trapped foot, searched the joint, and opened two plates.

The washer ceased trembling when it was lifted.

Inside the carrier, it struck three times against the soft lining. Then it rested.

For the first time since anyone could have counted such things, there was an unoccupied place along the gap.

The pale fold behind it remained where it was.

***

The power listened to the chamber.

It listened beneath the loudness, where the differences were small: a stroke made because another stroke had come; a stroke made because another had failed to come; the thin, irregular tapping of an animal whose organ was worn away.

It did not find a single command governing them.

It found habits, dependencies, warnings. It found nourishment passing through answered vibrations. It found the carriers remembering, in the fitted remains beneath their plates, how to mend injuries they had not yet encountered.

The resident was present in much of this, but not in the same way everywhere.

In one blade, it was something being sheltered.

In another, it was something the colony listened for.

At the engine, it seemed to be waiting.

The power could have quieted the chamber. Instead, it learned enough to answer a solitary animal.

One had been stranded on the underside of a severed cable, too far from either wall to reach a colony. It had become almost weightless. Its blows barely moved the copper beneath it.

The power answered through the wire.

The animal struck again.

The answer came back imperfectly. The power adjusted.

After a while, the animal’s feet began to bear weight. A carrier climbed the lamp fitting, extended its fringe across the cable, and collected it.

The power did not answer in its place once it had returned to the others.

***

It left the lunch tin closed.

It did not straighten the glove or erase the cuts in the railing. It did not assume that the shelters were abandoned merely because no one occupied them in a recognizable form.

At the engine, it rested its attention against the casing.

The resident tightened.

Colonies fell silent, one after another.

The power withdrew from the crank before touching it.

The colonies resumed.

Later, it approached again, but this time at the lowest rib, where the carriers rubbed their fringes. It made no movement the engine could mistake for an instruction.

A pale surface appeared between two nearby blades.

The power waited.

The surface bore four straight marks. A splinter lay embedded across one of them, with a shallow rim of flesh drawn tightly around it.

There was enough room now for the surface to move away from the splinter. It did not.

The power supported the fragment without pulling.

After a long interval, the pale surface drew back.

The splinter remained suspended in the slot.

The wound closed slowly. No new surface appeared to replace the old one. The resident stayed out of sight for the rest of that period of waking.

The power left the fragment where it could be found, but not where a body would press against it again.

***

Making the chamber wider would have been easy.

Knowing what width it should have was not.

The blades were not merely obstacles. Their intervals carried the animals’ answers. Their roots gave the buried creatures purchase. The copper-coloured plants depended on faults in a system whose workings were still obscure.

So the power made a small space beside the far wall.

It did not grind away the stone. It held two blades a little farther apart, slowly enough that the colonies could move their feet. It preserved their tension and the paths by which their sounds travelled.

The new space was not a passage to the surface.

It was an alcove, empty except for a low shelf of supported mineral.

Nothing entered it.

The power waited.

A carrier came first. It examined the shelf, withdrew, and returned with an old washer beneath its plates.

The old animal emerged and touched the stone.

Its central plate was nearly transparent. Within it, the little dark structure contracted and loosened.

It struck.

An answer came from the blade beside it.

It stayed.

More carriers began to use the alcove. They brought animals whose feet needed time to darken, animals that struck out of interval, animals that no longer struck at all. The others could still be heard from there. None grew lighter.

The alcove became noisy in a different way from the rest of the chamber.

The power made no attempt to regularize it.

***

Eventually the resident approached.

There was no arrival. Several pale patches became visible along the wall, and the fold behind the narrow gap ceased withdrawing.

The animals there had all been relieved by then. A few remained nearby, striking lightly against the edges. They could leave whenever they wished.

The pale fold extended into the place where their bodies had been.

It stopped.

The power held the stone steady.

One of the six black structures appeared, blunt and worn. It touched the newly available edge, withdrew, and touched again.

The power could have opened everything then. It could have lifted the desert and laid the resident under the sky.

It did not know whether the resident had ever wanted sky.

Instead, it widened the gap by less than the breadth of that worn foot.

The foot came farther through.

A tightening travelled to the engine. Its needle moved, very slightly, away from the red.

The power stopped widening.

For a long time the resident remained partly extended, with the foot resting in the chamber and the pale fold supported behind it.

Then it drew back.

The gap stayed open.

***

There were other changes, but they came unevenly.

A buried creature withdrew one limb entirely and lay with it folded beneath its body. Another continued its old sequence of knocking, although the blows grew softer.

The carriers still rested beneath the benches. They still left room for a person’s boots.

The engine ran. Something still shifted in the closed tin.

Not every splinter could be removed. Not every damaged animal recovered. The power attended to what could be helped without making the remainder invisible.

It also made provision for its absence.

Beneath the roots of the blades, it set supports that yielded slightly under a knock and held firm under the chamber’s weight. The buried creatures examined them with the ends of their long limbs. The power altered several after those examinations.

It tested each support while remaining close enough to take the load back.

Only when the chamber had learned the new arrangement did it cease bearing the weight itself.

It remained within reach.

***

Much later, during one of the resident’s periods of rest, a pale surface settled onto the shelf in the alcove.

A carrier approached sideways and laid the pads of its fringe against it.

The surface did not withdraw.

Around them the animals continued striking. The lamps burned orange. Heat passed from ceiling to floor.

For several hours, nothing further happened.

Then the resident shifted.

The carrier moved aside. The pale surface lifted from the shelf, leaving a shallow impression of its edge, and returned to the narrow separations in the wall.

No animal had to brace against its departure.

No animal had to hold the opening for its return.
