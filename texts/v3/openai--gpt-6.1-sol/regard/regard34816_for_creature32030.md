The power does not begin with rain.

It watches a cloud enter through the western gap and come apart among the bowls. It watches the water reach a hundred wrists by routes too small to see from the enclosing wall. When the cloud passes, it notices which inhabitants continue working and which turn toward the sheltered courts.

For a long time, it does nothing that could be distinguished from weather.

It has come without a body. This proves inconvenient.

A shadow would be an interruption here. A voice from the sky would carry over the whole basin, reaching the young in their hoods and the workers beside the hot chambers as readily as anyone who might wish to listen. Even a light on the summit bowl would claim a place already used for other things.

So it waits beside an unfinished stair.

The inhabitant working there has nearly fitted the next riser. Its hand moves across the damp clay, stops, and moves back. At one corner, water keeps escaping through a seam. The inhabitant smooths the corner again.

Beside its wooden paddle lies a little unused clay.

The power moves a piece of this into a shallow cup.

It does so slowly, within the inhabitant’s sight.

The hand stops.

The cup is irregular, smaller than the palm beside it. The power has deliberately left it unfinished. Water enters from the faulty seam and gathers in the bottom.

The inhabitant looks at the cup. It looks into the recess beneath the stair.

Then it reaches out and pushes one side of the cup inward.

The power leaves that side where it has been put.

After a while, the inhabitant presses a notch into the rim. Water spills through it onto the paving.

The power makes a short channel beneath the notch.

The inhabitant wipes the channel away.

Nothing replaces it.

They stay beside the stair until the light has changed.

***

It takes many days to arrive.

Not to reach the basin. To become someone who can be approached or left.

The inhabitants bring things to the unfinished stair: a split cord, a tile with an uncertain crack, a bowl containing clear water and another containing water so cloudy that the bottom cannot be seen.

Sometimes the power understands what is being asked. Sometimes it does not.

When it holds the cracked tile together, the inhabitant who brought it pulls the two halves apart again and sets them down separately. One half is taken away. The other remains.

When it clears the cloudy water, three inhabitants turn their faces toward the bowl. One puts a hand into it, withdraws the hand, and empties the bowl into a channel. The cloudy water was carrying something needed.

The power remembers.

It becomes careful about understanding a successful change as a welcome one.

Eventually, a figure with a blue strand at its wrist brings a long, shallow vessel. It sets the vessel on the landing and rests its fingers along the rim.

The power touches the water at the other end.

A small wave crosses the vessel.

The fingers remain.

Another wave returns.

***

There are places where the basin is failing.

The power had seen some of them immediately. Others it learns only after it is taken there.

Under the hills of jars, an old chamber has begun to settle. The inhabitants have set narrow strips of fired clay across its joints. Two strips are broken; a third has lifted at one end. Nearby, replacements lie ready in a wall recess.

The power could hold up the hill.

It could make every jar beneath it unbreakable. It could close the joints so completely that no drop would ever pass through them again.

Instead, in the vessel they have brought, it makes a small shape of the chamber. Water fills the shape. Beneath the sagging roof, it raises a support.

One of the inhabitants removes the support.

It points to a dark mouth in the actual wall. A current of air is coming through it, faint enough to move only the loose end of a cord.

The power makes two narrower supports, leaving a passage between them.

The inhabitant examines these. Another reaches into the vessel and widens the passage.

They return the following day with wooden frames.

The work takes most of a season.

The power bears the weight where they ask it to, while the inhabitants dismantle courses that have stood longer than any of their present bodies. Jars emerge whole from the darkness. Others come out in fragments. Water is diverted through temporary gutters, and the district above remains occupied.

There are arguments. The low voices pass from one worker to another, stop, begin again. Twice, the frames are taken apart and reset.

The power waits with the hill resting against it.

When the new supports are finished, the weight returns to the clay gradually. Everyone stands clear except an inhabitant with a smoke-colored chest, who keeps one palm against the joint.

Nothing moves.

For several days afterward, someone comes back to touch that place.

***

The inhabitant with the blue wrist takes the power farther down.

Below the active terraces is a court almost entirely hidden by overhanging channels. Its cups are empty. A gutter above it has been removed, and the opening plugged.

Along one wall sit four bodies.

One has no lower arms. Another has a head tilted against its shoulder at an angle a waking body would correct. The surfaces of all four are dry, though bowls have been set beside them.

The blue-wristed inhabitant kneels by one of the bodies and touches the closed seam of its mouth.

Then it puts the same fingers into the vessel.

The power attends as closely as it can.

There are things it can follow: chambers within the body, deposits left by water, the sites of repairs. There is a small roughness beneath one shoulder where a join was never fully smoothed.

It finds no movement answering its attention.

It could make movement. It could soften the joints, fill the internal passages, set the hands to work. But it cannot find the one whose hands these were.

For the first time, the power wishes to be less careful.

The blue-wristed inhabitant waits.

At last the power lets the water in the vessel become still.

The inhabitant leaves its fingers there for a long while. Then it lifts them out and returns to the dry body.

The power stays until it is left alone.

***

There is one change they ask of it that reaches across the whole basin.

The request takes weeks to become clear.

They bring it to empty high cups. They show it cords stiffened with deposits, replacement cords drying beneath roofs, the routes by which workers carry bowls to districts the lifted water no longer reaches. They show it a young body whose hand cannot yet open, and the shallow tray that must never be allowed to empty.

At the summit, they arrange cups around the vessel. Water is passed among them until several are full and the vessel is nearly drained.

Then they stop.

The power puts more water in the vessel.

They repeat the arrangement.

This time, one inhabitant covers the vessel with a slab.

The power waits.

When the slab is lifted, it adds the water.

That, finally, is accepted.

In three high courts, the inhabitants build covered cisterns. They choose the sites, lay the courses, and fit the lids. Each lid has a small handle that a stiff hand can grasp.

When a lid is opened, cool water begins to collect inside.

It gathers slowly enough that it can be stopped by closing the lid. It does not overflow. It carries nothing away from the clay.

The ordinary clouds still enter through the walls. The lifting cords still darken and swell. Most days, the cisterns remain closed.

***

The power is offered a place on the summit bench.

It cannot sit, but by then the inhabitants know this. They set the long vessel in one polished hollow and leave the other free.

Someone comes without tools and rests beside it.

Sometimes a hand enters the water. Sometimes no one touches it at all.

Below, a replacement foot is being fitted. Three figures carry a gutter across a court. At the unfinished stair, the crooked cup remains where it was made; its notch feeds a small hollow worn into the tile.

Cloud gathers against the western wall.

The inhabitant on the bench has a blue line at its wrist. It watches the terraces until droplets begin to form on the hood overhead.

One falls toward the vessel.

The power catches it, holding it just above the water.

The inhabitant turns its head.

After a moment, it raises one finger and touches the suspended drop. The drop breaks against the broad pad and runs down to the wrist.

The power lets go.
