The place is a shallow court, no wider than a few houses, enclosed by walls that lean inward without quite becoming a roof. Above them is a narrow opening full of gray light. You cannot see sky through it. There is another pale surface beyond, too far away to make out its grain, and water falls from it at irregular intervals.

Everything within reach has been made.

The floor is laid in slabs of fired clay, each broad enough to have required several pairs of hands to carry it. Their edges were once straight. Now they fit together like worn teeth. Water has softened the mortar, hollowed the joints, and polished the high places to a dull shine. Along the walls, the slabs give way to stacked ceramic rings, packed clay, braided fiber, and great wooden ribs blackened by damp. There is no exposed earth beneath any of it. Where the floor has broken, there is another floor below, older and more finely tiled; below that, glimpsed through a smaller hole, are the rounded shoulders of jars.

The court has been repaired downward.

Its lowest point is a pool about the size of a sleeping mat. It is very shallow. Even in this light you can see the pale scoring on its bottom: circles drawn with a cord, a thumb pressed into wet clay, rows of little wedges made by the end of a stick. The marks survive because this part stays submerged. Elsewhere, the same workmanship has been rubbed almost smooth.

There is little to look at in the middle. No pillar, no tree, no altar. The space feels larger than it is because all its presences cling to its boundaries. A single broken vessel rests against the far wall. Two lengths of rope hang from holes beneath the opening overhead. Neither reaches the floor. Between these things lies an expanse of cool air, dim enough that distances are difficult to judge.

Then the wall moves.

What seemed to be a dark strip of mortar resolves into a line of small animals, each with a flat back and six pale legs. They travel sideways along a wet seam, touching the creature ahead with their feelers. Above them, shell-bearing things crowd the underside of a wooden rib. Their shells are thin and translucent; when a drop lands among them, the whole cluster withdraws, exposing a patch of old tool marks, then slowly covers it again.

Life is abundant here, but it occupies almost no open space. It is tucked under rims, inside lashings, behind flaking slips of clay. A fist-sized cavity may contain hundreds of soft white bodies. A vertical crack may be furred with legs from end to end. The broad floor remains nearly bare.

The noise is enormous.

Water drops into the pool with a hollow report that seems too large for the drop. Something rasps inside the walls. Small wings beat in ceramic cavities, and those cavities lend them the sound of much larger wings. At intervals a deep knocking passes around the enclosure: three knocks, a pause, then a loose succession of taps from several directions. The wooden ribs carry it. So do the buried jars.

At first it sounds like work being done in neighboring rooms. After a while you see the workers: long-bodied, blind creatures striking the wood with the hard plates at their tails. One knocks from beneath a rib. Another answers through the wall. Dozens are listening.

The court is cool enough that wet clay never quite loses its chill. Moisture gathers on every surface, even those sheltered from falling water. The ropes drip from their lowest fibers. A bead forms on the broken vessel’s lip, swells, and lets go. There is no wind, but the air shifts whenever a large drop falls from overhead, bringing down a brief breath of colder air.

Only three patches of green are visible. One is a low, dark mat rooted in the frayed end of a rope. Another occupies a thumb-sized pocket in the wall. The third consists of a few upright blades growing from the broken vessel, pale at their bases and bent toward the opening. They are repeatedly clipped by tiny grazing mouths. Nothing here gets the chance to become luxuriant. Any new growth is found almost at once.

Near the pool, a broad shallow groove leads toward the wall. It looks like a drain until the knocking begins again.

The water in it trembles. A thin edge lifts out of the pool and travels up the groove, against its slope. It advances with each knock, stops during the pause, and advances again. By the time the answering taps begin, it has reached a small clay cup set into the wall at knee height.

The cup fills. Its rim overflows into a second channel.

Much of the court’s plumbing works this way. The grooves have been cut shallowly enough to hold a skin of water, and each ends in a hollow vessel tuned by its shape and thickness. Sound draws water through them. A sustained rasp feeds the high seams; a sharp report sends a little pulse along the floor. Silence leaves everything to gravity.

No single creature controls the system. The tail-knockers call to one another, the winged animals quarrel over cavities, shell creatures scrape at the films they eat. Together they keep the walls wet. Water carries loosened clay into lower cracks, where it settles around fibers and shell fragments. The inhabitants depend on the court, and their ordinary movements keep portions of it from drying, splitting, or falling apart.

Other portions do fall apart. Beneath one rib lies a small fan of fresh ceramic flakes. They are lighter than the surrounding floor and sharp enough to catch a foot. Little animals are already sheltering under them. The oldest flakes nearby have rounded edges, each resting in a shallow hollow worn by years of minute traffic.

Nothing suggests a recent catastrophe. The leaning walls have leaned for a long time. Their bulges are braced with bundles of sticks whose lashings have been renewed so often that the knots resemble dense nests. Some were tied by hands. Others have been thickened by animals dragging loose fibers through them. It is difficult to tell where repair ends and habitation begins.

Along the far side, just above the waterline, runs a row of handprints. Most are ordinary in size. A few are very small. They were pressed into separate clay panels before firing, perhaps so the panels could be identified when they were brought back from the kiln. Water has worn away the fingers of several, leaving only shallow palms.

At the dimmest end of the court is a doorway sealed with an enormous round pot. Its mouth faces inward. Nothing grows on it. Nothing nests inside.

The animals avoid that patch of floor, although water crosses it freely. Each time the pot receives a drop, it gives a low, clear note. For a moment the scraping nearby stops. Even the creatures knocking inside the ribs wait until the note has faded before resuming.

You might stand there a long while, looking into its empty mouth.

Behind you, the channels continue their small uphill journeys. The shell clusters open. A pale swimmer crosses the central pool and vanishes beneath the lip of a slab. Another drop falls from the unseen surface above, and the court fills with the busy sound of its inhabitants, each occupying a seam, a hollow, a fraction of an inch of shelter in the immense emptiness between the walls.
