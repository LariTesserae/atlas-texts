The basin hangs near the summit of a mountain, though there is no dry land around it and no surface overhead. Its rim is visible: a broken oval of pale stone, perhaps a hundred paces across, tilted slightly toward a darkness that gives no indication of depth. Beyond that rim, the mountain drops away through warm water. Looking outward produces the bodily certainty of standing at a precipice, even while the water holds you up.

Inside the basin, the water is heavier.

You feel the boundary before you see it. An arm extended across the rim is tugged inward at the elbow. Loose fragments enter, accelerate, and strike the floor; outside, their counterparts drift slowly past. This difference keeps the hollow full of moving debris without ever filling it. Somewhere under the floor, stone is being consumed at almost the rate it arrives.

There is little light. A dull bronze illumination seeps upward through cracks, enough to show the edges of things and leave their bodies obscure. The cracks open and close continually. Where they widen, the water becomes almost hot, and thin flakes of new mineral curl out of them like shavings. The floor is young: sharp shelves, glassy knobs, ridges that have not yet been rubbed smooth. Even the pale outer rim has fresh fracture faces. A piece breaks away every few minutes. Sometimes the broken piece is larger than the place it came from.

The sound fills the hollow so completely that, at first, it seems there can be nothing else to hear.

It is a grinding, rolling concussion, carried through water and bone. Pressure knocks against pressure. Stone clatters underfoot. Cavities in the rim fill and empty with blasts like enormous throats clearing. Beneath these are slower blows, separated by intervals long enough to count, which can be felt in the teeth before they arrive at the ears.

After a while, the roar acquires distances. You distinguish the crash of the narrow northern cleft from the deeper booming under the floor. There is a high, furious rattle wherever the fresh mineral flakes gather. Closest of all is a dry clicking that follows your movements. Stop, and it stops a moment later. No animal is visible there. The clicks seem to come from small gaps opening between you and the water, gaps too brief to let the water enter.

Currents tear across the basin in changing directions. One catches low along the floor, scouring everything toward the eastern wall; another arrives vertically and crushes the first flat. Their intersection makes a smooth, upright sheet. Through it, the far side of the hollow appears close enough to touch. Then the sheet folds, and the wall retreats. Anyone carried into such a fold may emerge several paces away with stone dust embedded in the seams of their clothing.

There are sheltered places. They do not stay sheltered.

Three small enclosures stand apart on the floor, with open water between them. Each has a low doorway and walls made of rough, honey-colored slabs. There is no mortar, no tool mark, no sign of a hand having fitted one piece to another. Nevertheless, the slabs lean together with the awkward care of something built for use. One enclosure is no larger than a sleeping niche. Another has two entrances, both leading into the same shallow chamber. The third is roofless and keeps rearranging its upper stones when the light goes dim.

For a few moments after a deep boom, the water inside these rooms falls still. Silt settles. The roar becomes distant enough that you can hear a pebble ticking down a wall.

Then the rooms inhale.

Water surges through the doorways, flattening anything caught against the back wall. The slabs spread slightly at their joints. Bronze light shines through. When the pressure passes, they settle closer together than before. The smallest chamber has been getting smaller for days, or for the few minutes that here resemble days. Around its threshold lie smooth white fragments, each curved on one side. They may be earlier thresholds. They may be teeth.

Vegetation survives mostly along the upper rim. There are a few straps of dark green growth, leathery and narrow, fastened into cracks where the currents slide past rather than strike. They stream outward over the drop. Their tips divide into transparent threads that tremble independently of the water.

A single patch grows upside down beneath a ledge: seven or eight short stems, each carrying a swollen amber bulb. The bulbs brighten before the floor booms. As the concussion arrives, they go black and tighten against their stems. Small bubbles emerge afterward, cling to the ledge, and sink.

Nothing feeds openly. Now and then a pale, flat creature crosses the basin with six broad fins held rigid around its body. It comes in sideways over the rim, skims one of the glowing cracks, and leaves with its underside stained orange. Its passage is brief and strangely calm. Currents that fling stone aside divide around it.

Once, it enters the smallest enclosure. Several long booms follow. When it comes out, only four fins remain. They are larger than the six were, and placed differently. It rises a little, tests the water with them, then drifts over the edge into the dark.

The emptiness between these few presences matters. Most of the hollow contains only warm water, violent movement, and suspended mineral dust. The rooms stand well apart. The plants are isolated against long bare stretches of rock. Even in the dimness, there is room to see something coming.

What comes is usually more basin.

A ridge lifts where the floor had been flat. Its sides shed glittering scales as it rises, and for several breaths it reaches nearly to the rim. Water pounds through the new channels. One of the rooms tips onto its side. The green straps stream in a direction they have never taken before. Then the ridge splits down its length and falls inward, leaving a narrow gully whose bottom shines with soft, unfinished stone.

The basin’s boundaries are least reliable during these changes. A doorway briefly opens onto the mountain’s outer slope. A hand placed on the rim can feel a second rim beneath it, separated by no visible space. Cracks continue across open water for an instant, bright lines with jagged edges, and seal with an impact strong enough to throw grit from the floor.

Yet certain things persist. The pale oval remains above the immense dark drop. The warm water returns to its inward pull. The chambers, however altered, keep their doorways. The amber bulbs swell again beneath their ledge.

Between two blows from below, a fine white deposit begins to gather on the newest stone. It has only moments to form. Grains settle into tiny hollows, softening one sharp edge, outlining a shallow step. Then the next concussion arrives. The deposit lifts in a cloud, the step breaks, and fresh light comes through the wound.

Over the rim, beyond the flying grit and the pressure waves, the surrounding water remains dark and almost motionless. From there, the whole place might look small: a faintly glowing hollow near a submerged summit, with a few green strands waving above it. Within it, every surface is sounding.
