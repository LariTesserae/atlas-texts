The valley is held above the clouds by roots.

You can see them where the floor opens: pale, close-grained columns descending through the white air, branching as they go down. Their lower ends are never visible. Some are thick enough to support a hillside; others hang in loose bundles, wet and faintly translucent. Water travels along them in shining sleeves. Beneath the valley, where one might expect a mountain, there is only this suspended anatomy and the continuous noise of falling water.

Up here it is warm. The light is ordinary daylight, generous and a little hazed, with enough strength to show green through the thinner surfaces. There is no sunless enchantment to explain the place. A shadow falls where it should. Wet things shine. At the far end of the valley, several miles away, the raised rim catches the afternoon light and becomes a long yellow-green edge.

Everything underfoot has been made.

The ground is a woven tissue, roots crossing roots until there is no gap between them, then sealed over with a smooth, tough skin. It gives slightly under your weight. In the open stretches the weave runs in broad parallel bands, like the grain of a board; near a watercourse it curls into tighter patterns. Hills are built from folded sheets of the same living material. Cliffs expose layer after layer of compacted stems, each layer still green at its center.

There is no buried stone. What looks like gravel in the shallows consists of hard seedlike nodules, each tethered to the bed by a fine stalk. What looks like clay is a dense mat of root hairs holding water. Even the pale dust on the higher paths is pollen, pressed into the surface until it has become a dry, powdery rind.

Yet the valley is spacious.

Vegetation does not crowd it with separate trunks and shrubs. It has become the place itself, leaving broad distances within its architecture. You might walk half an hour across a low green plain before reaching one of the upright growths. These stand alone: a fluted column, a hollow tower, an arch with no obvious destination. Between them are pools, gently rising terraces, and empty reaches of living floor. From a height, the valley resembles a settlement built for inhabitants who have never arrived.

The structures are grown to shape. Their joints show it. At the base of a tower, stem fibers pass smoothly into the floor; beneath an arch, they turn through the curve without a break. No cut end, fastening, or tool mark can be found. Small chambers open in the larger forms, warm and dry inside, with ribs arranged around the walls. Some have a low ledge at a convenient sitting height. Others have doorways too narrow for anything large enough to need the room beyond.

The chambers smell of sap and clean damp fiber. If you lean against a wall, it is neither hard nor soft. It resists like the heel of a hand.

Water lies close to every surface. Walk barefoot and your footprints darken before slowly filling with clear moisture. On the terraces it gathers into shallow pools whose edges are sewn shut by tiny interlocking leaves. Along the valley rim, thick pads of tissue condense water from the air. Droplets form beneath them and fall into troughs, then run inward through channels with glossy, raised lips.

The channels make most of the sound.

At a distance they produce a deep, even rushing. Nearby, each has its own voice: a narrow spill rattles against a ridged bed; a covered conduit booms inside a hill; water sliding down a hollow stem gives a bright, sustained ringing. The whole valley is loud without being busy. No single sound announces an event. Nothing starts or stops.

There are places where two streams strike opposite sides of a thin green partition, making it tremble. The note carries a long way. You can stand in an empty chamber and hear three or four such partitions through its walls, each at a different pitch. Occasionally the wet floor transmits a lower vibration through your feet.

The valley has the appearance of something just coming into being.

Its ridges are rounded and raw. Along their upper edges, folded tissue shows the beginnings of branches that have not opened. Pools occupy shallow hollows without sediment or worn banks. The channels are full and forceful, but their beds remain unscoured. Below every fall, the landing basin is as smooth as the lip above it.

Nothing here acquires the finish of age. A broken edge stays moist. An exposed fiber stays white. Where water presses hardest, the living surface answers with precisely enough strength to hold its shape.

You begin to notice that growth, too, has reached an exact arrangement. The curled tips do not uncurl. Leaves stand at every stage of opening, from tight seams to broad plates, but each holds its particular posture. The same droplet swells beneath a distant overhang, falls, and is replaced. The place is active at every small scale and unchanged at the larger one.

There are no dead leaves.

The few animals seem almost incidental. In one pool, a long translucent creature rests across several rooted nodules. Its gills pulse, stirring the water beside its head. A pale insect moves along the underside of a trough, pauses at a bead of moisture, then continues out of sight. Above the plain, widely separated dark birds ride the warm air. Their calls are brief and easily lost in the water noise.

Nothing gathers into a flock. Nothing rustles densely beneath the ground cover. For all its living substance, the valley gives you long intervals in which you cannot find another moving body.

Near the center is a wide, flat basin. Water covers it to the depth of a finger. Beneath the water, overlapping leaves form a tiled surface, each leaf as broad as a table and joined to its neighbors along a thick seam. The basin reflects the sparse towers, the sky, and the far rim. Walking into it sends ripples a considerable distance across the open floor.

At its center rises a low circular wall. There is no opening in it, and nothing enclosed except another patch of shallow water. Its upper edge is green, tender, and still rolled inward.

You might wait beside it for a long time, expecting the edge to lift, or a bud to rise from the water, or some creature to emerge and make sense of the enclosure. Warm moisture settles on your arms. The towers cast their shadows across the basin. Water keeps ringing through the suspended roots below.

The wall holds its unfinished shape. So does the valley: spacious, inhabited only lightly, and complete in the act of being made.
