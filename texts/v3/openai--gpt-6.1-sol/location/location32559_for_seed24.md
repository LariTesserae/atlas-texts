The hollow lies just below the level of the surrounding plain, beneath a shelf of dark limestone. It is not deep enough to feel underground in the usual sense. There is no descent, only a shallow dip in the ground and a roof that gradually comes close overhead. A person could enter upright, then walk a little farther by lowering their head.

Inside, the space widens to the size of a small grove. Most of it is empty.

The darkness gathers unevenly. Near the entrance, a little light reaches the floor without illuminating it, enough to distinguish a pale stone from the shadow beside it. Farther in, the low ceiling and the ground lose their separation. The eye supplies distance where there may only be a wall. After a while, shapes return: a broad slab lying almost flush with the floor, a shallow depression beyond it, the rounded base of a pillar that is still attached to the roof.

Nothing here has a sharp edge. Even the fallen pieces look settled, their corners blunted by a long history of dust moving across them. The limestone has weathered into broad, quiet forms rather than spires or jagged shelves. Along the walls, harder bands stand out a finger’s width from the softer stone. They can be followed by touch through the dark: a raised seam, a smooth interval, another seam.

The floor is mostly firm mineral earth. In places it carries a thin powder, pale gray near the mouth and almost brown farther in. There are no damp patches. No stains descend from the ceiling, no drops gather at its lowest points, and the little hollows in the stone contain only dust. Old cracks have filled with fine grains so completely that they resemble pale veins within the rock.

The air is warm. Away from the entrance, the stone seems to hold the temperature of the land above it, releasing heat without any perceptible current. Touching the wall gives a steady warmth to the palm. There is none of the coolness one expects from a shaded hollow.

A few plants grow where the roof ends.

They are small, woody things, spaced well apart, each occupying a pocket of soil among the stones. Their leaves are narrow and curled close to the stems. In the weak light they look gray rather than green. One has spread sideways along the ground, following a crack for several feet; another is little more than a thickened base and three stiff branches. Between them lies bare earth, with enough room that each plant casts its own separate patch of darkness.

Their roots enter the sheltered ground, but the living growth does not follow. Farther inside are only an exposed root, polished where it passes over a stone, and a few dry leaf fragments lodged against the wall. These fragments have lost their delicate edges. They are small, tough curls now, almost indistinguishable from the mineral flakes around them.

There are no webs in the corners. No wings stir beneath the roof. Nothing has burrowed into the powder or left a track across it.

Sound is difficult to find. A foot placed on the firm floor makes a dull contact that dies nearby. On the looser ground, grains shift with a brief, close rustle. The chamber is too low and too irregular for a grand echo; it receives a sound, softens it, and lets it end. Beyond the entrance there may be wind on the plain, but the air inside scarcely moves.

At the back, the floor rises gently toward the ceiling. The hollow ends without a dramatic wall, narrowing until only a dark horizontal seam remains. Dust lies undisturbed along its lower edge. There is no suggestion of another chamber beyond it, only the same limestone continuing through the ground.

The place asks for very little movement. Its distances can be crossed in a few dozen steps, its surfaces understood with a hand. There is nothing built, nothing arranged, nothing that needs to be reached or opened. Even the loose stones seem unlikely to roll: each rests in a shallow bed worn to its shape.

After some time, the entrance becomes the most distinct thing—a low, dim opening with a few wiry plants standing beyond it. Inside, warmth remains against the skin, and the stone holds its rounded forms in darkness.
