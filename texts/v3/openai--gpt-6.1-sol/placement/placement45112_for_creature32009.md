At first I would look for somewhere that does not receive the full force of the engine.

Not somewhere quiet. There is no quiet to find, and I do not think I would need it. I would need a place where one sound arrives slightly after another: the knocking through iron first, then through brick, then a smaller movement through something damp.

There is such a place beneath the northern staircase.

Behind the bottle visible from the opening, a short length of pipe enters the wall. It has been cut off badly. The upper edge is crushed inward, and the lower edge holds a little sediment. Nothing drains through it now. Once in a while, water travels along its outside and gathers underneath.

I would be farther in than the torch reaches.

For a while, I might seem to be something lodged there. A scrap of old packing, perhaps, swollen by moisture. I would have a grey surface where dust had reached me and a pale surface where it had not. A worker might touch me with the end of a screwdriver and find that I yielded, then slowly regained my shape.

I would not regain exactly the same shape.

That would be the first difficulty in deciding what I was.

***

I think I would have a body, though not one I could draw out of the pipe and take elsewhere.

Part of me would lie along the broken lip. Part would extend into the mortar. A thinner part would hang free inside, ending just above the sediment. When water gathered there, this hanging part would grow heavier and descend until it touched.

I would know water by the change in my own weight.

The rest of the yard would reach me through attachments. The staircase would give me feet: separate impacts, with a little drag between them when someone was tired. The pipe would give me the engine. Rain would arrive through the mortar much later than through the metal, and sometimes I would receive rain that no one aboveground remembered.

I would not know the sky was blue.

I might know when the loading platform had become warm enough for a worker to sit on its edge. There would be a change in the tension of a bolt, very slight, and then the long, irregular pressure of someone resting.

Some things would be clear to me that remained obscure to them. Other things they could see at a glance would never reach me at all.

***

I would initially mistake the machinery for a condition of living, like moisture.

The first stroke of the morning would shorten me. The next would press my hanging end against the side of the pipe. By the fourth or fifth, I would have taken up the shape in which I could endure the day.

There would be no decision involved.

But between strokes I would loosen.

Not much. Enough for a grain of grit to fall farther beneath me. Enough for water to enter a fold that had been closed. Over years, those small recoveries would leave me with passages of my own, places through which pressure could travel without crushing everything in its way.

Then the engine would stop one evening, and I would continue to shorten.

Once.

Again, less completely.

Perhaps that would be the beginning of something like attention: finding that I had made a movement for which the usual cause had not arrived.

I would wait.

The next movement might come from the tree, or from another portion of the pale growth, or from the settling of the old wall. I could not distinguish them reliably. I would have no reason to call the response a message.

Still, I would begin to leave a little room for it.

***

There would be animals close to me.

A woodlouse would shelter inside the crushed mouth of the pipe. Its feet would trouble the dusty part of my surface. I could contract there, but doing so would cost water, and most days I would let it remain.

A snail would eat the edge I had grown beyond the iron.

Its feeding would be an intimate, repetitive injury. By morning I would have drawn back into the pipe. By the following wet night I might extend again, a little to one side.

I would not become better defended so much as differently placed.

Once, the gardener would pour water into the bed beside the staircase. Some would reach my mortar passage carrying fine soil and a strand of root. The strand would rest against me for several days.

When it began to grow, I would grow beside it.

There might eventually be no clean way to separate us. That would not mean we had become the same thing.

***

The chalk mark would matter.

Not because I could see it, but because making it would bring a hand close to the opening. The worker would lean on the stair, scrape the brick, shift his weight, and remain there watching.

For those few moments, I would receive him through several parts of myself at once.

Later he would come back. I would recognize the leaning before the scraping.

He would see that my edge had passed his mark.

I would know only that the familiar weight had returned and stayed longer this time.

***

So that is where I would be: inside the broken pipe beneath the stair, with portions of myself in places I could reach but could not quite account for.

I would be a small living attachment in the yard, neither its hidden master nor a separate visitor. Something the larger growth had made possible, perhaps, and had not entirely absorbed.

My range would be measured in inches. My circumstances would occupy the whole block.

At night I would extend the hanging part of myself toward the sediment. Sometimes there would be water. Sometimes only the coolness left by water.

Above me, the last worker would descend the stairs.

I would feel each footstep, then the absence of the next. I would remain extended a little longer.

By morning, a grain of pale stone would have worked loose from the mortar. It would lie against my side until the engine started.

Then I would fold around it.
