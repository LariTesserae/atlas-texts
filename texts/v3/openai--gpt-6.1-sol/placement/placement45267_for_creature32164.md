At first I would be found under the third step.

There is no staircase there yet. There is a root passing twice through the handle of a valve, a little pale clay pressed against the root, and an interval of dryness too thin to admit a grain of silt. But the underside of the step is already present. I can put part of myself against it. It smells of old varnish when the pumps slow.

I would know that surface before I knew the water.

Above me, something rests its weight. The wood gives a little. Through it I feel a succession of contacts: a hard joint, a length of softer material, something narrow drawn carefully past the edge. The weight pauses.

A tiny floor opens beside my pocket.

I reach toward it with the part of myself that can become slender. I have not learned how much of me must remain behind. On the first attempt I touch the floor and lose the shape of my other end. On the second, the floor tilts, and I draw back carrying a splinter.

The splinter stays dry inside me.

For a long time, that is the only thing I own.

***

My earliest territory would be small enough to fit beneath a hand: the valve handle, two roots, the soft center of a broken brick. Feeding makes it larger. Dark liquid arrives through a leaf pressed against my outer membrane. I feel it spread without tasting it. Somewhere beyond the pocket, a filament thickens. Somewhere else, material that was mine goes numb beneath a coating of new stone.

I cannot distinguish growing from going away.

When the deep hammering begins, I brace against the underside of the step. When the higher chatter passes, I turn. The great sustained note opens a narrow seam in me, and through that seam I see a yellow wall.

The wall has a mark near its base.

I keep looking for it afterward.

Other walls appear. Some have tiles. Some carry shadows of objects that are not there. One has a small metal hook with nothing hanging from it. None has the mark.

Eventually I get onto the little floor. The thing that offered it has already left. There is no ceremony, no waiting presence. I draw the last of my moving body out of the pocket and find that I have left much of myself in the roots.

I stop on the floor to feel for those distant parts.

They are still there.

The pocket is empty, but the valve handle is still warm against something I can feel.

***

I would grow along the eastern side of the pumping station, where a collapsed length of gutter makes a sheltered lane through the red filaments.

It would be an awkward place for a young inhabitant. The gutter rocks whenever the station lifts. My first three openings would close during the same pressure cycle. One would leave me with a cupboard whose back had become its floor. Another would strand a short portion of my body in a fold that no longer led anywhere.

I would spend several days getting that portion back.

There is enough space to reach it, but not enough to bend. I have to grow a new joint. While it forms, the stranded part keeps pressing against the far wall, slowly polishing a patch no larger than a fingernail.

Later I use that patch to recognize the place.

I learn to keep spare turns in my passages. A low ceiling may become useful when a channel opens beneath it. A door that leads into solid clay can wait. I cannot keep everything. Unfed openings narrow; the pale ridges soften; surfaces I have crossed many times withdraw into seams.

The splinter travels with me. I carry it in a shallow fold that closes tightly when I pass through the wet margins of an opening.

It has no apparent use.

Once, during a long shutdown, I put it on a shelf and move away. I return before the engines start.

***

The first corridor I feed from has a lamp at the near end and a door at the far end, like many others. I follow it along the broken outlet pipe, turning four cuffs in succession, until my roots can rest against its corners.

Inside, the lamp trembles.

I open the far door.

There is a narrow space beyond it, almost entirely occupied by another door. Its paint has blistered around the handle. When I touch the handle, the corridor begins to shorten.

The tiles come toward me. Their cool, hard surfaces enter the undersides of my folded parts. I spread to receive them. The lamp descends until its light lies against me like a warm strip of material.

For a moment I cannot stop.

Then I reach the second door.

Something has been caught beneath it: a frayed length of cord, flattened by years of being shut in the same place. I work one narrow extremity under the wood and lift it.

The corridor ruptures.

Warm water strikes my outer cuffs. I retreat through three openings, dragging the cord after me. Behind me, the roots are heavy with new growth.

I have fed well. I have also left something uneaten. For several cycles I return to the outlet without approaching another bubble.

I put the cord beside the splinter.

***

After that, I would be found less reliably near the station.

A ridge arriving in the pale basin carries a passage with a yellow wall. I reach it through a cable sheath, a leaning grove, and the interior of a piece of ground still too hot to touch directly.

The mark is there near the base.

I press against it. It is a shallow dent, with a little paint gathered along one edge. Nothing happens when I touch it. No hidden opening yields. No distant part of me answers.

I stay anyway.

The passage is older than my pocket. I can tell from the wear on its floor, though I cannot tell what has worn it. I extend myself along the boards, placing my joints where the surface has already been hollowed. For the first time, a resting place fits before I arrange it.

Outside, the ridge cools. Roots work through its cracks. I send some of my growing parts down with them and leave others attached to the station.

The next current pulls hard between the two places.

I hold until a cuff tears.

The tear does not hurt at once. First there is water where I expect a corner. Then the cord slips from its shelf and vanishes. I contract too quickly and break another opening.

For several days I cannot reach the pumps.

What remains around the station continues to feed. I feel the nourishment faintly, through a route too narrow for my gathered body. Here in the ridge, my dry floor grows shorter.

I put the splinter against the yellow wall and begin tapping.

***

Eventually something answers from below.

The knocks are uneven, very close together. I open a fold near the floor. Beyond it is a loop of metal, two skins, and a small pale part turning toward me.

I make a landing for it.

The first surface is too steep. It touches, withdraws, and waits. I lower the edge. Outside, the pounding enters the ridge; the little part turns away from me to face it.

I wait too.

When it reaches again, I keep still. Its tip rests on my floor. More of it follows, slowly, until the pocket below is empty.

There is scarcely room for both of us. I draw a long part of myself through the wall and leave my hard joints against the ceiling. The newcomer settles beneath the yellow mark.

I can feel it testing the boards.

Beyond the ridge, the pumps have begun another cycle. Somewhere in the roots, a route is opening. I could follow it now.

Instead, I move the splinter farther from the edge.
