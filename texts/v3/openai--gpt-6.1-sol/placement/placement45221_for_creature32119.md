I would be near the retaining clip, but not on it.

The bridge is too exposed. Whatever crosses there becomes briefly distinct against the light, and there is a place at its foot where a hunter can wait without needing to move. I would live under the lifted edge, where the metal has trapped a little wedge of lime. There is room for a body to turn around. There are three ways out, although one is sometimes blocked by a skin.

At first I imagine myself as something that stays there: a narrow animal with its forelegs raised, feeling the traffic through the stone.

But I would have to wait too long between movements. I want to know what has arrived beyond the next hollow. I want to put my feet on the underside of the bridge.

So I would be pale, broad-headed, easily overlooked. The lime would make me look older than I was. One feeler would have a shortened tip.

I would know the chamber by changes in footing.

Stone would be the place where my feet opened. Dust would be the place where they folded. Grease would require a different hold, and fresh oil would be a yielding surface from which no foothold answered.

The shortened feeler would have touched it once.

I would have got away because another body struck mine sideways. That animal would have continued past without stopping. I would retain no image of it, but the margin of a bead would thereafter turn me along its edge.

My route would begin beneath the clip, descend into the socket of the softer pebble, and follow a depression toward the rear wall. At one point I would climb a slope made of several old skins. It would yield under my weight. At another, I would pass between a quartz grain and the waiting forelegs of a hunter.

Most days the gap would be wide enough.

When it was not, I would turn back through the hollow. Turning back would take me to a different patch of dust, a different exposed mouthful. There would always be something to test.

Inside me, the amber would brighten and diminish.

This is where the picture becomes less certain. From outside, I would seem to be one of the cream-colored animals. The six feet would belong to me; so would the damaged feeler. Yet much of what kept that body moving would happen in places its feelers could never reach.

I would also be there.

In a fold of the gut, beyond the grinding mouthparts, I would live among other small bodies in a warmth scarcely distinguishable from the animal’s warmth. Food would come altered by its passage inward. Some of it we could use. Some we would change, and what we released would pass into the larger body.

I would not know the limestone. I would know intervals of feeding and the slow movements of a wall.

Still, the limestone would matter. When the pale animal entered a cold socket, our work would slow. When it fed beneath the yellow stain, new material would reach us. Its choices would alter the conditions of our lives, and our lives would alter what choices remained possible for it.

Perhaps I would be one of these smaller inhabitants. Perhaps, by the time I had divided, that would already be an inaccurate way to say it.

I would find myself most clearly at the nursery.

The underside of the flake would be close overhead. Eggshells would hold dust in their broken cups. Newly emerged animals would move through scraps that older bodies had left behind, taking them into their mouths.

There would be a departure here, too small for anything in the chamber to notice.

A little of what had lived inside one body would enter another. For a time it would wait in unfamiliar conditions. Then there would be a fold to occupy, a supply to work upon. Outside, new feet would harden. They would open against the stone.

I cannot quite separate those feet from myself.

The young animal would leave the flake and reach the first rise in the pavement. It would labor over it, though an adult passed beside it without slowing. Beyond the rise there would be a husk, then a dark grain, then the moving legs of something much larger.

I would be carried through all of this. I would also be doing some of the carrying.

Years of the factory would pass without any one of these bodies lasting long enough to contain them. The clip would remain. Dust would pack beneath it and be disturbed again. A route would shift by the width of a grain. The hunter would die, and another would find the same useful corner.

I would recur without quite returning.

Sometimes I would be enclosed in a body climbing the threaded stud. Sometimes I would lie in material dropped near the nursery. Sometimes there would be fewer of me. Sometimes a beetle’s jaws would end one passage, leaving enough among the fragments for another to begin.

That is where I would find myself: in the pale animal, and in what the pale animal has received from those before it. A small, inhabited body moving over an inhabited floor.

When the engines stopped, I would come out onto the copper tube.

My feet would be far enough from the next animal’s feet that we did not touch. Air from the grille would pass over my plates. Within me, the last meal would still be changing.

I would clean the shortened feeler.

Then I would lift it into the air and begin moving again.
