At first I would put myself on the bolt head.

It is the only place the description leaves unoccupied, and I am accustomed to mistaking an unoccupied place for an invitation. I would imagine a small figure standing there, beneath the overhanging leaves, with the ringing metal under its feet.

But that would bring an animal into a place that has none. It would also make the clearing into a destination, when it is only the consequence of an alloy to which roots cannot attach.

I would leave it bare.

The transparent trees offer another possibility. Within a trunk, the conductive bands separate at a junction and continue into different branches. I could follow one of those bands from its contact at the spindle to a leaf pressed almost invisibly between two other leaves. There would be a route, a supply, a particular body to belong to.

Yet I keep finding myself beside the route rather than in it.

I think I would be a little of the waxy sheath around a conductive band, somewhere near a branch junction in one of the transparent trees. Not the entire sheath. A short length on the inward side of the fork, where the bands have begun to divide but have not yet drawn far apart.

From outside the trunk, I would contribute to its faint cloudiness.

There would be no chamber there. The material would be full of itself, touching tissue on every side. My extent might be smaller than one of the marks beneath the roots on the ceramic bridge. Nothing in my position would afford a view of the garden. The bolt head, the pleated roof, the pale leaf at the entrance would be as inaccessible as the mountains.

My work would be local: keeping two conductive bands from making a contact their branching does not allow.

The bands would carry charge past me. The trunk would carry the tower’s vibration through me. I would need to remain supple without shifting out of place, insulating without becoming brittle. At the fork, my thickness would vary slightly along a curve. That curve would be my whole accommodation to the world.

I would not know what the tree looked like.

There is something appealing in that limitation. I would belong to a large form without having to contain an image of it. The fan of leaves could remain itself above me. The roots could keep their pads against the spindle. Neither would have to become a symbol or a message in order for my small part to matter.

Even the branch I helped preserve would not possess its shape alone. Somewhere beyond my reach, it would rest against another tree’s fan. That fan would be held clear of a thick frond by a hooked margin. The frond’s root would enter a seam in the old ceramic.

None of those supports would pass through me as knowledge. Their effects would arrive as the particular pressure under which I had to remain intact.

I would have no occasion to turn toward the opening.

The garden’s green light might enter the trunk and weaken through its folded layers. Some of it might reach my substance. It would not show me anything. The enormous note would pass through continuously, without becoming a sound addressed to someone.

And I would be maintained.

That seems more fitting than being a hidden watcher: a small living boundary, renewed where it already lies. Carbon would enter the arrangements that keep the sheath supple. Its substance would change beneath the threshold of visible movement. The fork would retain its outline.

I could not withdraw to inspect what I had done. Withdrawal would itself be a change in the branch. There is no unused space beside my place, no second position from which to consider the first.

So I would remain on the inward curve, between the two bands.

The bolt head would stay empty. Above it, the leaves would keep their separate edges. At the broken lip, the pale blade would lie against the ledge, its outermost tissue exposed to starlight.

Farther in, within a trunk too small to be called a trunk anywhere else, my slight thickness would continue to keep two paths distinct.
