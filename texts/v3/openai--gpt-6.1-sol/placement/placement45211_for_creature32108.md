I would find myself under a threshold, where the old floor has lifted just far enough from the soil to admit a body.

Not in the chamber beyond it. Not yet in the street. In the narrow space between them, with a little water passing beneath me and the weight of the pale material overhead.

From there, the sky would be a strip that sometimes disappeared.

I would know when a cloud arrived before I saw it. Moisture would gather against my underside. The dry grains touching me would soften and shift; a stem laid across the opening would become heavier. Somewhere beyond the threshold, water would begin striking water. The sound would come through the ground more clearly than through the air.

I think I would have limbs, although at first I would not use them.

There would be room to extend one. Its tip would encounter a loose flake, turn it slightly, and withdraw. The movement would leave an unfamiliar space beneath my body. I would fill that space again.

For a long time, this would be enough.

Above me, feet would occasionally pause. Their pressure would reach me as two points, followed by two lighter ones. Something would touch the lip of the threshold. I would open a few folds toward it, and we would remain like that until the water between us cooled.

I would not know whether these were visits. The contact would have a shape I could distinguish from the ordinary pressure of the room.

When it ended, I would stay open for a while.

*

There would come a morning when the water stopped using my passage.

A rim in the next court would have swollen overnight. The runoff would turn elsewhere, carrying the loose seeds with it. The moss at my entrance would still be wet, but underneath the threshold the soil would begin to lose its softness.

I would reach farther.

The first hard part of my body would be the tip that scraped against the ground while I tried to pull myself out. Behind it, the rest of me would stretch reluctantly. Some of my margin would still be caught in the living growth. I would have to draw it free a little at a time.

No clean departure. A thin strip would remain behind.

For several days afterward, water touching that strip would seem to touch me.

Outside, I would discover that I was wider than the passage had allowed me to be. My underside would open into the cold. I would stand too high, then lower myself until the loose folds almost brushed the ground.

The wind would press against my back.

I would have known its force through walls, through the long uneven trembling of a ceiling. Here it would arrive without anything between us. One limb would slide. The other would hold. I would settle onto the soil and wait, with grit collecting along the edge I had just freed.

My first journey would be very short.

There would be a flake of roof a few body-lengths away, curved enough to keep the ground beneath it damp. I would reach it before sunlight entered the street. On its underside, green lines would follow the old ridges.

I would feed there.

By evening, the shape of the threshold would have begun to leave my back.

*

For a season, perhaps several, I would move.

I would learn a way to the smallest pool that did not cross the central court. Beneath the shrub, there would be a low place where one burgundy stem rested on another. I would press between them. Their leaves would drag along my back, leaving little wet streaks.

At the pool I would put down a limb and discover how far into the water I could reach.

The first time, I would withdraw too soon. The cord would return inside me with almost nothing. I would stand there as the surface became still again, then try once more.

Gradually I would carry enough water to go beyond the rooms I knew.

There would be a wall that answered the wind so strongly that I always lowered myself before reaching it. A warm corner occupied by another body, which shifted just enough to make space. A passage I could enter from one end but not from the other.

I would return often to the threshold.

The strip I had left behind would thicken. Moss would grow over part of it. I might touch it without finding anything I recognized, then remain there longer than I remained at other entrances.

During wet weather I would travel west, toward the soft enclosures.

I would put my front against a rim and feel dark strands tighten beneath its skin. Beside me, another inhabitant would work its short limbs into the base. We would press at slightly different heights. Water would collect between us.

When we left, there would be a shallow notch.

I would come back to it. Sometimes it would have closed. Sometimes a trickle would be using it, washing fine soil out of the room.

*

The place I eventually stayed would be beside one of those notches.

I would attach there during a cold night, while the new wall was still low. My back would face the little enclosure; my limbs would be folded toward the street. The wind would pass just above me.

At first I would leave each evening.

Then there would be rain for many days. Water would move through my fringe without interruption. The rim would swell against my edges, and detaching would require more effort than before. I would postpone it until the next evening.

A young body would spread itself on the floor inside.

I would feel it moving whenever it crossed the damp ground near me. Once it would touch my folded limb. I would extend the limb a little, keeping the contact while it settled into the angle of the wall.

By the time the weather cleared, growth would have covered my outer margins.

I would still be able to pull free. I think there would be a whole season in which I could.

Instead, I would keep one long limb laid along the notch. Water running beside it would deepen the channel. When the rim pressed inward, I would press back. My hard tip would remain exposed, dark against the pale surface.

The opening would persist.

Small inhabitants would pass over it before their limbs could bear their weight. Later, larger ones would lower themselves and squeeze through. Their undersides would brush the part of me still bare.

With each passing season there would be less of that part.

I would not know the height of the wall above me. I would know its weight, and the slow arrival of water from farther away. Frost would reach inward through the branching pores, stopping a little short of my center.

Sometimes a short limb would touch the outside.

I would answer with whatever movement remained: a tightening along a fold, a small displacement of water. Perhaps it would be felt.

The entrance would stay narrow, but open.

On a wet evening, after the wind dropped, a body would come through from the street. It would pause across me while drops fell from its back. Then it would move into the room, leaving one hard tip resting against my edge for a moment longer.

I would still be warm there.
