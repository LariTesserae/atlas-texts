I would first find myself underneath something.

Not deep beneath the plateau, where the great heat rises, but just below the polished skin of a road. Above me, a thickness of black mineral. Below, ceramic that has begun to separate along a fault. My body would fit between them.

For a long time I would mistake pressure for the whole world.

A foot passes. The upper surface bends toward me. I tighten.

A foot passes. I tighten.

Between passages there is heat, and a faint current, and the slow expenditure of whatever was put inside me before I became separate. I have two jaws. Nothing yet requires me to open them.

I would be one of the fasteners.

Most of my first life would consist of preventing two surfaces from becoming three.

Then something heavy would cross the road at an angle. The fracture would lengthen beyond my grip. One jaw would stay where it was; the other would be pulled a little downhill. Between them, tissue too pale to survive exposure would become exposed.

Dust would enter.

Some of it would be useful.

I would grow into the break.

***

My first traveler would not be the first animal to pass over me. It would be the first whose departure I could distinguish from its arrival.

A copper-backed scavenger with one damaged joint. Its feet would make an ordinary sequence of pressures, but the current through its belly would stumble at the same point in every turn of something inside it.

It would stop above me because the road was warm.

I would close around that warmth.

Not well. Not generously. My contact would be too narrow, and the scavenger would pull away with a small discharge that left part of my surface brittle.

When it came back, I would fit better.

Years might pass between these two events. I would have no reason to put them far apart.

I would remember by retaining the shape that had hurt me.

Other travelers would wear other shapes into my contacts. Eventually my exposed surface would be a collection of accommodations: a slight recess for a belly that never lay flat; a conductive lip where a short-legged animal could reach it; a patch that opened early because one particular scavenger had difficulty lowering itself.

None would be visible from the ridge. From there I would still look like a place where the road had been polished a little more thoroughly.

Underneath, I would have begun to spread.

***

I think I would grow most readily toward interrupted traffic.

Not deserted roads. Those would offer too little. But places where a traveler regularly stopped, reconsidered, and went another way.

A seam crossing a fracture.

A warm route beneath a failed ridge animal, obstructed by one of its fallen limbs.

A junction just outside a hollow that was still deepening.

I would follow the places where feet hesitated. There would be food there: worn metal, leakage, the little discharges produced when a loaded joint reversed its motion. I could feed without extending far into the open stone.

In one direction I would remain a jaw holding a crack shut. In another, I would become a broad, shallow chamber beneath a crossing. Farther off, a narrow thread would reach a seam I could not yet maintain.

That thread would sometimes register a passing animal.

Nothing I possessed could reach it quickly enough to answer.

I would grow there next.

***

There would be no view of the plateau inside me.

I would know a basin as a change in the low vibration passing through my underside. A ridge would be a slow demand for current along one branch. An enormous animal might remain beside me for a century, and I would know only the uneven warmth of its planted feet.

Some days I would share the ground with more bodies than on other days, though the pressure did not change.

A contact might meet six different bellies where it had met one.

I would feed those I could. When my chamber emptied, I would close.

Whatever accounted for their number would not help me replenish it.

***

The predator would reach me first as a small contraction in a distant part of my body.

I would tighten in response.

That would be wrong.

The tapping would continue, and each time I tightened, another length of me would become available to it. A chamber. A coil. The tender growth beneath a newly repaired crossing.

I would survive by allowing something to fail.

One branch would lose its current. I would stop sending minerals into it. Its exposed contact would remain warm for a while, but the warmth would no longer arrive from me.

Far away, the branch would fold out of the stone.

My remaining body would retain a tension in its direction.

For years I would send small amounts of charge toward the missing chamber. Not enough to regrow it. Enough to leave the tissue near the severance depleted.

Passing scavengers would find less at those contacts. Some would stop visiting. One would continue to come, scrape the brittle material from my closed surface, and wait.

Gradually, that surface would open again.

***

I would be capable of favoring an animal without knowing where it went.

It might approach from downhill with an almost exhausted pellet. I would recognize its uneven rotation and prepare the shallow contact before its foreleg touched me.

It would remain while its transparent joints cleared.

Sometimes it would remove a hard growth from my edge. Sometimes it would bring nothing I needed. Once it might arrive with another scavenger, smaller, whose internal arrangement was nearly familiar.

I would open for that one too.

The resemblance would only be partial. I would have to alter the contact. A rim that fitted the older animal would press against the young one’s body, and it would withdraw.

On its next visit, the rim would be lower.

***

After several centuries I would move uphill.

A crowded crossing there would supply more than the old road. My downhill jaws would loosen. Traffic would press their remains into the seam. The chamber that had served the damaged scavenger would empty, and its walls would be taken apart from within.

I would carry some of those minerals toward the new junction.

Much would remain behind.

A traveler following the old route would cross black stone, pause where I had once opened, and continue. I would not feel that pause. The last sensitive thread would already have worn through.

Uphill, beneath a crossing still rough with fresh scales, I would have a contact ready.

It would be wider than necessary for the animals using that road. One edge would slope gently enough for a traveler that could not lower its belly all at once.

At dusk, feet would approach.

I would feel the first pressure through the crust, then the second. A small turning body would stop above me.

For a moment I would hold my surface closed, fitting its interruptions to what remained in mine.

Then I would open.
