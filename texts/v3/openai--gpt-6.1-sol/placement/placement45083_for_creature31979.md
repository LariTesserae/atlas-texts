At first I would be one of the two that did not come up through the ring.

Above me, four sets of feet would grow distinct. I would watch the little hinges whiten, the bodies cease to overlap. The parent’s collar would hang through the surface like roots in clear water. When the others moved toward the doorway, the hairs would drag lightly over my back.

I would follow for a short distance.

There would be no ceiling to strike. Only, whenever I lifted my head, the ground would lift with it, preserving the small distance between my mouth and the soles passing overhead. I could see the house. I could not yet arrive at its threshold.

The parent would wait where the posts ended.

I would know it by the folded pair of legs, and by a place beneath its body that the light did not enter.

Then its yellow line would fade. It would lower itself and go.

For a while I would remain directly under the place where it had stood.

***

The softer country would not be dark.

Its light would have more green in it. The root of the two-leaved plant would pass nearby, withdrawing slowly, its tip turning whenever I moved. Sometimes I would rest against it. Neither of us would yet be firm enough to inconvenience the other.

From below, a footprint would look like a small pale hill.

Most would sink back almost at once. A grazer’s six steps would rise around me and disappear before I could go between them. The long-legged hunters would make almost no impression. I would learn to keep away from places where the light acquired a narrow bend.

But certain prints would stay.

I would find the first beside the house: eight small elevations, with a broad hollow between them. I would settle in that hollow. Above it, something dark would be resting on the ground.

For the first time, when I moved a leg, the leg would move only once.

Until then there would always have been another motion beside it, nearly the same, belonging to a body I might still become. In the hollow, the alternatives would draw close. My feet would gather themselves at their tips.

I would sleep.

When I woke, the dark thing would be gone, but one of my feet would have a white hinge.

***

I think I would spend a long time learning the country from underneath.

I would know the water bowls by the cold that preceded their deepening. Before each click, a round pressure would descend, and I would move aside. I would know the dome by the arrangement of its sleepers. Their hollows would project downward, each with an empty portion held carefully inside the curl of a body.

Once I would lie beneath that empty portion.

A sleeper would stir above me. Its collar hairs would turn toward the floor.

We would remain there until the house walked.

The hollows would lift away together. I would follow beneath the dragging rear edge, which left a broad, brief passage in the yielding earth. That would be the first journey I made without having to choose a direction at every step.

When the dome settled, I would be beneath the entrance.

A bead of water would tremble immediately over my head.

I would raise my mouth to it.

It would grow a little.

***

I would not come into the upper country all at once.

A front foot would appear one morning beside the threshold. The rest of me would remain below. Passing animals would step around it. One of the young would touch it with a feeler, then retreat to the posts.

I would keep that foot still.

During the next mist, I would lose it. The ground would take it without pain, leaving a small white shape on the other side. I would be able to see it, but when I tried to move it, only the soft end of my leg would answer.

Later, an animal carrying a dark object would stop there.

It would touch the foot with the longest hair of its collar.

Then it would set down its burden.

The ground would hold open.

I would put my head through first. My collar, which I had never seen entire, would spread in the air. It would be shorter on one side, where it had brushed so often against the root.

The animal would wait while I brought up my remaining legs.

It would not pull me. I would be grateful for this before I knew anything else about it.

***

Afterward I would live mostly near the unfinished walls.

The roof lying beside them would shelter me better than the corner. I would sleep beneath one lifted edge, with my head toward the nearest dampness. Sometimes a seam-creature would pass through the narrow space between my collar and my neck. I would wake only when its last sideways joint slipped free.

I would feed badly at first. The surfaces I chose would already be too hard. My mouth would find nothing, and I would have to rest before trying another patch.

The animal that had waited for me would sometimes stop nearby. We would not touch our collars yet. It would stand with its head lowered toward a surface, and I would feed beside it.

A yellow line would appear down my back.

I would be surprised to discover that it belonged to me.

I would also discover that the bare country was much larger when my feet could no longer pass through it. A ridge that I had crossed beneath in a few movements would take most of the interval between clicks. I would have to go around its shoulder. I would have to stop where the slope changed.

Once, while resting, I would see my shadow arrive.

It would approach from behind the unfinished wall, small and a little green at the edges. It would settle beside the foot I had lost.

I would turn, too slowly to catch what it did there.

***

The dark object would come to me without ceremony.

The older animal would meet me beneath the ledge. Its collar would be wet, though the weather had not begun. We would stand close enough for the hairs to interlace.

I would feel, along each hair, a minute weight.

For a long time neither of us would move.

When we separated, I would be holding the object against my underside. The other animal would carry something smaller, almost hidden between its front legs.

Mine would have an uneven surface. A pale fleck would lie in one side.

I would touch the fleck with the soft tip of my shortened leg. It would be hard. I would leave it there.

***

That is where I would find myself: a little way from the unfinished house, making a journey that most other creatures completed without stopping.

I would not always be carrying the object. Sometimes I would leave it beside the roof and go to feed. Sometimes I would set it down in an apparently empty place and wait.

Under it, the ground would cease to rise.

I would lower my collar until its longest hairs entered the marks around me. Once, something would brush a hair from below.

I would spread my rear legs and stand higher.

The light would remain plentiful. A grazer would cross nearby, its transparent back briefly enlarging the white terrain. Far away, or only a body-length away, water would be gathering at the meeting of two slopes.

I would wait past the click.

If nothing came up, I would return after the mist.
