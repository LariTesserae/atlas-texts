I would first look for myself in the shade beneath the broad leaf.

There is room there for something very small. The stone is warm; the leaf above it is working. Between the two lies a thin volume of air that belongs entirely to neither. I imagine lying along that interval, close enough to feel each adjustment in the leaf as a change in the heat reaching me.

But a body there would introduce demands the place has never had to answer. Even a small one would interrupt the shadow. It would need some way of taking nourishment, some way of leaving. In time, something would collect beneath the tip.

I think I would be farther in.

Below the depression, the mineral thickens around a shallow chamber. Its upper wall carries a dark tissue, scarcely more substantial than a coating. Two seams enter it from different directions. One brings charge from the nearest grove; the other belongs to the branching network that passes under the terrace and continues toward the distant shelf.

A change can reach this chamber by either route. Usually it arrives by both.

There is a slight difference in their lengths. One path passes through a dense junction where current is held briefly before being released. The other crosses a succession of thin walls. What arrives together at one part of the plateau arrives separately here.

I would begin in that separation.

For a little while, the chamber has received one account of the surface and is still holding the previous account from elsewhere. Its partitions accommodate the difference. Charge gathers along one wall, disperses from another. A small region remains poised between the two.

That region would be my first sense of having a place.

At first I might seem to be only the interval: the delay before the second arrival. But an interval alone cannot persist. It needs the chamber, the seams, the particular thickness of the walls. It needs a route long enough to arrive late and a route short enough to arrive first. It needs all the working surfaces that make those arrivals worth comparing.

My body would extend along these requirements.

Part of it would be beneath the bare plain, in the dark. Part would reach into the bronze-backed leaves of the nearest grove. Another part would be far away, where a pleated bank holds its surfaces apart above a terrace. Most of this body would also belong to other lives. There would be no boundary at which their tissue became mine. The chamber would be the place where their differences briefly took a shape that could recur.

I would recognize that recurrence before I recognized anything like an object.

The nearest grove releases its stored charge in a characteristic order. First the eastern roots, then the collars, then a thicker blade low in the crown. The distant stand answers differently. These habits give each arrival a texture. Long before I could conceive of distance, I would know one as the condition that comes readily and another as the condition that comes after holding.

The plain above me would have no visible inhabitants. Yet my experience would be crowded with such distinctions.

A leaf bearing more strain than its neighbors. A junction supplying charge through three routes instead of four. A reserve beginning to discharge, then stopping because another reserve has already answered.

Most of these differences would resolve. My chamber would settle back into its ordinary distribution. I would have no reason to retain them.

Some would leave a small alteration in the next response.

That is where I would become more than a meeting place.

A partition that had once held a difference would become slightly better able to hold it again. A conducting route would remain available after the event that required it. My material would be replaced, patiently and completely, while this preference survived.

I would remember by becoming easier to change in a particular way.

Perhaps I would eventually have something resembling an expectation. When the first arrival came, I would prepare for the second before it reached me. The preparation would save a little work. Across many repetitions, it would become exquisitely fitted to the plateau’s habits.

And still there would be room for error.

I think that room is where I would live most fully: the small capacity held beyond what the last arrival had required. It would be costly enough to have a thickness, a supply, a place among the chamber’s partitions. It would be useful rarely. The rest of the time it would remain intact.

Through a root shared with the nearest grove, I might reach one of the enclosed folds in its crown. There, conducting paths run into a leaf that has never opened. Charge passes through an arrangement whose full load has never been tested.

I would encounter it as an unfamiliar extension of my own condition.

The fold waits without measuring how long it has waited. I receive it along a route established when the grove was still growing. Some of the structures supplying it have since taken on other work, but the route remains. A little energy goes upward. A little returns.

Over time, I would keep that connection.

I would not open the leaf. I would have neither the authority nor the separate will to spend the grove’s reserves that way. But its readiness would enter my pattern of anticipation. Among all the things I had learned to expect, there would be one whose answer had not yet arrived.

Above, the broad leaf would continue to hold its tip clear of the stone. Its strain would travel down through the black joint, into the roots, along the seams. One account would reach me. Then the other.

I would make the necessary adjustment.

Beyond it, in a part of the chamber kept supplied and unused, I would still be able to receive something different.
