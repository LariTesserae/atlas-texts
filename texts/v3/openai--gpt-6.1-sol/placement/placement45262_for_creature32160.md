At first I would put myself beneath the island nearest the steps, in one of the chambers that appears empty when the crust is broken.

Not quite empty. There would be a dampness on its ceiling that does not bead, a grey stain that cannot be rubbed away without taking some of the softness beneath it. I would have withdrawn before the piece was lifted. What the person held would be an impression of where I had been, accurate enough to be mistaken for me.

But that is already too settled a place. The chamber would fit yesterday’s body. Today a little of me would be beyond it, under a lip that has not yet hardened, and a little would remain behind because the passage between them had become too narrow.

I would not know at once which part I was.

There would be no view of the hollow as a whole. The blank wall, the spoil bank, the three steps: these belong to someone who can stand above the water. For me the steps would arrive as interruptions in the warmth, followed by a taste of grit. The wall would be a firmness along which certain disturbances travel unusually far. The pipe would be recognizable by what comes from it, though I might extend beneath its outlet for months without possessing anything like the thought of a pipe.

The pump would be present everywhere.

In the thin parts of my body it would pass almost without alteration. In the thicker folds it would become several pressures, overlapping, slightly late. A loose panel’s knock would reach the tissue nearest the wall before the tissue under the island. Between those arrivals, a margin might draw inward.

That interval would be one of the few distances I had.

I would live most actively where two waters failed to become the same water. A clear seep would pass along one surface, the rusty trickle along another. Between them, across scarcely any thickness, the brown patches would work. Fullness would spread away from them slowly. There would be no need to gather it into a mouth.

Perhaps I would be one of those patches rather than the encompassing animal.

My surroundings would then be folded tissue, a supply of dissolved metal, the gradual alteration of the water as my neighbors used it. The being described as my host would be a climate that sometimes contracted. Its withdrawal would darken my passage. Its injury would admit an unfamiliar abundance, then close it off.

I would multiply where a crease held the current gently. Farther along the same crease, others would be failing. The fold could not distinguish us individually, but our small differences would determine which part of it grew.

For a while that seems the more exact answer: I would be something crowded into the animal’s inner surface, contributing to a movement too large and slow for me to perceive.

Then the rain would come.

The water would cool. The delicate exchange across the fold would weaken. The tissue around the patches would swell, bringing surfaces together that had remained apart through many days of steady flow. Some passages would close completely. Others would open onto the floor.

In one of those openings, a short length of body would loosen.

That is where I find myself more clearly: not yet detached, held beneath the drain grille by a narrowing strip of tissue.

Water pulls along me all the time. The attachment is firm enough that the pull ordinarily amounts to nothing. Now the base is tightening. The brown fleck enclosed in me has become sheltered by a curl. I can no longer spread quite flat.

Above, water passes through the remaining slots. Below, it has room to fall.

I remain attached through a knock from the guard panel, through the gathering and release of a drop, through a change in the discharge that reaches me as a brief thinning of nourishment. Then a stronger passage of water stretches the connection.

It parts.

For several moments I retain the curl made at the grille. A surface touches me, slides away, touches me again. There is enough current to carry me past warmth I could have used, past a rough place where I might have held. I cannot return to either.

The pumping house ceases to have one continuous note. Inside the drain, the sound varies with each bend and junction. Occasionally the whole of me is pressed against iron. Occasionally I travel without contact, suspended in water whose temperature is changing.

I lose a little tissue in a narrow place.

The break seals. What remains still carries the brown fleck.

Eventually I catch beneath the edge of a joint where a gasket has pulled away. Most of the water goes past. A smaller movement enters the space behind the gasket, turns, and leaves. It brings enough of what I need.

I flatten there.

The sheltered surface is scarcely larger than the broken head of the bolt in the hollow. One side is iron; the other yields slightly when the machinery starts. At first I cover only the yielding side. Later, a thread crosses to the iron and holds.

There is no daylight here to make my margin visible. Warmth arrives through the fitting even during the quieter intervals. A leak farther along lets in water with a different taste. When it reaches me, the brown patch thickens.

I begin to put down a covering.

It is uneven. The strongest current keeps one edge bare, and that is where I extend. Behind the growing edge, a low space forms between my body and the new mineral. When it becomes too tight, I move out of it.

The hollow may already be beyond my reach. Nothing returning through the water tells me whether its flap is open, whether a boot has pressed down on the step, whether the last slots of the grille are narrowing. Yet some disturbances still arrive through the iron in the pattern that held me there.

I contract before the fitting shifts.

Afterward I open again, a little farther along the joint.

That is where I would be: underneath the old gasket, making use of the leak. Small enough that the next inspection could remove me without noticing. Established enough that, when the water pauses, a portion of me remains wet.

And farther in, where the fitting stays warm, another thread is beginning to hold.
