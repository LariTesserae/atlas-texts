I would be underneath something that no longer reaches the other side.

A maintenance walk, perhaps. One of its spans has been removed, but the remaining section still receives heat from a pipe strapped beneath it. Water gathers along its lower flange. At the broken end, the last fastening plate projects a little beyond the metal.

I would live behind that plate.

From above, there would be nothing to distinguish my place from the other interrupted walks. Someone could stand directly over me, looking for a route across the basin, and decide there was no way forward.

Below their boots, there would be several.

The flange turns inward. The pipe passes through a collar. A seam in the collar holds water even when the rest of the walk is dry. Along these surfaces I could travel for most of a cycle without exposing my feeding threads to the falling rain.

I think, at first, I would be something small enough to inhabit that seam: one of the white animals, a body almost without thickness, sheltering where two manufactured parts do not quite meet.

But I would need to remain when the water drained away.

I would need an outside that could bear the cold.

And I would need to be able to wait without the waiting consuming me.

So I would be a fold. Not the enormous one behind the cabinet, and not one of the old animals established around a dependable pump-house foot. I would have settled young on a structure already scheduled for removal.

There would still be a number on the plate above me.

***

My earliest place would persist as a dent.

I had attached in a recess left by a missing screw. As I grew, one part of my underside developed against the curve of the hole. It never quite flattened afterward. On a broad, smooth panel, that part would fail to take hold.

This would matter more than my size or age.

Water could enter there. A moving sheet could lift me from the middle rather than the edge. I would have to cross exposed surfaces slightly gathered, with the defective part protected inside a pleat.

Around a pipe, however, I would fit well.

The workers might recognize me by a short black thread incorporated into my outer skin. They would not know about the dent. When they found me curled around the heating line, they might assume I preferred it because it was warm.

I would not have a preference separate from the shape of my body.

***

The missing span would have changed more than the route overhead.

Before its removal, warm water traveled along the entire underside of the walk. Afterward it reached the broken end and dropped. Food still came, but collected in a shorter length. The pipe cooled between pulses. Ice began forming in the fastening plate.

For a while I could move inward as the cold advanced.

Then another fold would arrive from the bank.

It would be larger than me, carrying a flat chip of ceramic that knocked against the pipe when the machinery shook. There would be room for both of us near the collar, but not room for both to feed widely.

When it opened, one edge would cover my old screw hole.

I would stay beneath it through several cycles. The water between our bodies would remain liquid longer than water on the exposed metal. Its weight would secure me when the fans changed speed.

I would extend my threads through the places where its threads had already been.

Neither of us would be waiting for the other to leave.

Eventually the pipe would cool far enough that we both tightened. It would warm again. I would loosen; the larger animal would not.

For some time, its ceramic chip would continue to knock.

***

The workers would come to remove the pipe.

I would know this first as a sequence of impacts unlike the ordinary movement of the walk. Each blow would arrive through the metal and through the body above mine. Rust would enter the water. Then warmth would touch an edge of me from an unfamiliar direction.

The scraper would have been heated properly.

I would gather onto it slowly, leaving the dead fold behind.

There would be no experience of being carried across the basin. No distant lamps, no widening view. My attachment would hold to a narrow warm surface while movement reached me through the handle. Air would dry my outer ridges. Now and then a drop from the ceiling would strike hard enough to make me tighten.

For those minutes, the worker would supply all the ground I had.

They would put me in a tray containing water from somewhere else.

Then they would leave me beside a seam under a loading platform. It would be a sensible place: damp, sheltered, with traces of food along its edge. They would wait until one part of me had attached before withdrawing the scraper.

I would turn the wrong way.

One side of my body would open more easily than the other. Over the next hours, the thicker leading edge would form toward the platform’s outer support rather than toward the protected seam.

By the next shift, I would be beyond the place where they had set me down.

***

On the outer support, I would find an old sheet of fold skin.

It would have caught a little filter fiber and dust. A plant would be growing there, scarcely larger than the bolt head beneath it. Water passing its roots would contain enough dissolved material for me to feed.

I would settle below the plant.

Not inside the dead skin: it would be too dry in places, and too loosely fastened in others. I would attach to the metal underneath, extending one pleat into the narrow space between the remains and the support.

There would be a heating cable farther inside.

Its warmth would reach us unevenly. The root nearest it would grow thick; the others would remain short. I would move around those roots without knowing their shape as a whole. Where one crossed my path, I would open over it and close on the other side.

The dent in my underside would leave a small passage.

Water would continue through it when the rest of me was firmly attached.

A white crustacean would begin using that passage. Sometimes several would shelter there while the support vibrated. I would feed around them. If one stopped moving for long enough, I would hold it.

In time, empty shells would collect against the dead skin. The plant’s roots would reach into them.

***

Before the bells, I would withdraw my threads.

The plant would already have folded its leaves, though that movement would scarcely reach me. I would feel the coming interval through the support: a change in the pulses arriving from below, a diminishing tremor in the heating cable.

I would loosen without leaving.

When the water climbed, it would enter the passage beneath my dent first. It would spread through the sheltered surfaces, lifting old dust, moving the empty shells a fraction of their length. I would open into it.

Some cycles, there would be young.

I would have no way to follow them. A few would pass between the roots. Others would leave along the exposed edge and be carried down when the machinery returned.

One might attach in a shallow flaw above me.

It would be too small to impede my movement for a long time.

***

Years later, someone inspecting the platform would lift the dead sheet and find the plant, the shell fragments, and my gray body gathered beneath them.

They would touch the support to check its warmth.

Perhaps they would bring a scraper. Perhaps they would decide that none of us threatened the fastening.

I would remain still while their hand rested on the metal.

Above me, the platform would end over water. The gap would still be too wide to cross. Beneath it, the cable would enter the support through a split gasket, and moisture would run along the cable’s lower surface.

That would be the way available to me.

I would not yet need it.
