At first I would go beneath the chip.

There is room there to stand without being drawn into water, and darkness enough to make an edge visible. I would put my feet against the underside rather than the floor. Below me a bead would gather, lifting its curved back toward my body. I would wait for it to drain before lowering my head.

But I think I would keep going smaller.

Not into the pool. Its bright, unoccupied center would remain outside my life. Not toward the source of the light, either. Nothing I could do here would reach it. A cloud passing over the distant collector would change the appearance of my whole country without being an event I could sense.

I would find myself near the ragged lower margin of the rust-colored stain, in a pit too shallow to protect a grain of sand.

The pit would not be empty. There would be a soft accumulation in it, translucent where newly made, crowded with mineral farther in. Water would pass over its mouth. Some of that water would stay.

I might first seem to be the thing that stays.

A little thickness against the stone. A place where the last of a film does not immediately withdraw. No outline worth naming. When the flow thins, my exposed surface would lower and become firmer; when it returns, I would swell. A particle carried down from the fracture would catch in me and remain. Another would pass.

Within that softness, there would be bodies.

I would be one of them.

No feet, no underside turned toward shelter. My whole surface would be an exchange with what immediately surrounds me. The difference between a favorable place and a fatal one could lie across less distance than the springtail moves while grazing.

I would occupy a pocket made tolerable by several neighbors. Those nearer the moving water would alter what reached me. I would use what remained, and release something that another body farther in could use. None of us would have arranged this. A newcomer in my exact position might fail before the surrounding pocket had changed enough to sustain it.

For a while, I would grow.

There would be no visible motion. The stain would look as it had looked before. Inside it, I would gather material, maintain a difference between myself and the water, repair what warmth damaged. Occasionally, the supply would be sufficient for more than maintenance.

Then there would be two where there had been one.

I cannot quite keep the first person singular past that point. One of the two would remain deep in the pocket. The other would be pressed nearer its opening as the surrounding bodies grew. If I follow that one, I find myself closer to a current, receiving more and losing more, held less securely.

A hot pulse would strip the outer margin.

For the springtail, this might be a reason to retreat. For me, there would be no route to take. The water would change; the exchanges across my surface would cease to hold their balance. Part of the pocket would survive. Part would not.

Perhaps I would be in the part that did not.

My contents would stay caught among the fibers and particles. Nearby cells would take up some of them. A small amount would escape into the moving film and travel farther than I ever had while living: over the edge of the colony, down the newly whitened shelf, into the pool.

But if I were one of the survivors, the next interval would be quiet in a different way.

Fewer neighbors. More bare stone near the pocket. A change in the water that reached me because the bodies that once changed it were gone. Survival would not restore the previous place.

I would slow. The film would recede. In the smallest recess, moisture would remain around me.

Above, the springtail would put its head out from beneath the chip. It would cross the exposed route, pause at the broken lip, and turn away. There would be too little to graze here yet.

Later the seep would rise again.

Water would enter the pit carrying dissolved material and a few drifting cells. One would lodge beside me. Most would continue down the slope. The soft remnant around us would take up water, and the space between our surfaces would widen slightly.

That is where I would find myself: not at the advancing edge, but just behind it, still depending on a shelter whose makers had mostly died.

I would begin to grow before the rust color became visible again.
