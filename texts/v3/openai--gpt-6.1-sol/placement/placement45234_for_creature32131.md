I would be well away from the columns, in water that appears empty until something on the far side of it shows through my body.

The floor would lie below me at a gentle angle. One of its long ridges would be visible end to end; another would disappear behind the first. There would be enough light to see the small eyes along a grazer’s fringe, but not enough to distinguish every mouth beneath it. The nearest column would be a faint stain with a blue seam down one side.

I would have that seam in view.

At first, I think I would be little more than a transparent fold: a surface with a few red threads, held open in the cold. There would be no useful distinction between the water in front of me and the water behind. Someone looking across the basin might see a narrow interruption in the distant wood grain and take it for a change in the light.

Closer, there would be anatomy.

Two unequal fins would extend from one side of a shallow body, three from the other. Their edges would be very fine, but their bases would contain muscle, pale and closely layered. I would have a mouth somewhere beneath the fold that looked most like a head. Inside it, soft plates would be held apart, each with a dark border. A few vessels would leave the mouth and spread outward.

The arrangement would be too particular to be a leaf.

My face would be farther back than expected. Small eyes, a clear brow, sensory pores gathered in two short arcs. One eye would look toward the column. Another, sheltered at the root of a fin, would have the floor. Along my underside would be three lesser eyes, each supplied by its own fine nerve.

I would not need all of them to agree on an image.

The ridge below would occupy one eye. The blue seam would occupy another. A small grazer, its hooked foot clear of the substrate, would be present in a third. Each view would remain distinct enough for the details of that eye to matter.

There would be no place inside me where the basin became a miniature basin.

Only these clear, local possessions.

***

Something much larger would surround me.

Its presence would be easiest to establish by looking down. Far below my fins, a pale curve would cross the view of the floor. Within that curve, dark cords would lie close together. Beyond it would be an eye larger than my whole body, turned toward the woven plain.

Looking upward, I would see two red vessels separated by a broad interval. I would occupy that interval.

The giant’s flesh would be so clear here that there would seem to be water on both sides of every boundary. Its outer fin would extend beyond my sight. Smaller animals, farther apart than the features of my own body, would occupy other openings among its vessels.

I would not be one of its organs. My skin would be complete. Water would separate my fin edges from its tissues, and my mouth would have no connection to its blood.

Yet the giant would be part of the exact shape of my dwelling. Its vessels would mark the sides of the open reach around me. Its pale lower curve would shelter a portion of the floor from my view. One of its membranes would lie above and behind my back, giving the immense sound a second, nearer presence.

Inside my own body, a cartilage ring would hold a smaller membrane.

It would face the distant seam.

The sound would fill it without making it flutter. Nerves would leave the ring in a delicate fan, some ending near my eyes, others passing into the fin muscles. The small muscles would be fully engaged. My posture would have all the precision of an animal balancing in water, though there would be no correction to make.

This is where I would know my body most clearly: in the held tension at the roots of the fins.

Not as an outline seen from elsewhere. As five unequal extensions, each with its own weight and reach.

***

I would have one patch that was not transparent.

It would lie behind the larger eye: a dull brown oval, no bigger than one of the seed cases on the floor. Pigment would be packed densely beneath the skin there. A vessel would curve around its lower edge.

From one particular angle, the patch would conceal a short section of the distant electrical seam.

That seems important to the creature I would be.

Almost everything else about me would admit the world. Light would pass through my fins. The giant’s vessels would show through my body. The great sound would occupy the ring in my chest. The cold would be present throughout my outer tissues, with a little warmth held deeper in.

But behind that eye there would be an ordinary piece of opaque flesh.

There would be something the basin could not be seen through.

I would not understand this as secrecy. I would simply have the patch, with its small supply of blood and the slight thickness it gave the skin. It would belong to my face as plainly as the eyes did.

***

I do not think I would feel confined.

The sea would extend beyond the giant on every side. Through the thin part of its flank, I would have a view into water broad enough to obscure a forest. Beneath me, the floor would remain available in exquisite, limited detail: grain curving around a hollow, pale fibers along a ridge, the separate blue backs of overlapping grazers.

Nor would I feel that I ought to leave.

There would be no unfinished journey in the spread of my fins. My mouth would be open and supplied. The column would be distant and legible. Around me, the giant’s body would have its own complete relation to the same water.

Its eye would look downward.

Mine would look past its vessels toward the seam.

Between those two directions, I would have room for my whole small anatomy: warm at the center, nearly invisible at the edges, with a brown mark behind one eye.
