At first I would put myself beneath the bowl.

There is room there for something that does not need the chamber explained at its own scale. The underside of the clay makes a low roof; the mineral floor makes another, inverted surface. Between them, the wooden resonance has weight. I imagine a broad back, a belly faintly green, feet spread where the floor is sharpest. Something easily mistaken for a thickening of the bowl’s foot.

But that is too convenient a hiding place. It gives me a roof before it gives me a body. And there is already someone there, attending to the seed.

I would not be that builder. The clay in its fingers belongs to it. So does the scar beside its mouth. I can look closely without borrowing them.

Perhaps I would be one of the transparent animals in the black channels beneath a leaf: a dark bead at either end, the rest of me scarcely distinguishable from the space I occupy. I could clasp a grain. I could fit my head into a cavity and find, at its bottom, another life whose whole chamber was the hollow left by my face.

Yet I keep returning to the bare floor.

There is so much of it. The five green islands are dense enough to draw every glance, but between them the pale plates remain exposed. Nothing has made a path across those intervals. They are not unused, exactly. The crystal point touches one fracture; the fracture continues beneath two masses of roots. What appears empty at the scale of leaves is already particular.

I think I would be there, where that fracture passes out from under a root and becomes visible.

Not in the crack. On its edge.

My body would be short and low, with a back the color of the white mineral, except for a narrow orange patch along one side. From above, the patch would look like crystal showing through a chipped plate. From below, if there were any way to look from below, I would be recognizably soft.

Six limbs, perhaps. The nearest pair broad enough to bear my weight. The farthest pair hooked around a ledge. The middle pair unequal: one folded beneath me, one extended toward the fracture.

I do not want the extended limb to be pointing. Nothing here needs me to indicate it.

Its last joint would rest on the upper plate. A little farther back, my belly would touch the lower one. Between those contacts, the two mineral surfaces hold a slight disagreement in angle. That difference would be part of my shape. Not food, though I would be full.

There would be a mouth, difficult to find. It would face the narrow space beneath my breast rather than the chamber. Inside it, two blunt surfaces would remain apart. No cry would pass between them.

The deep note would be strongest there.

Not louder to someone standing at the entrance. Stronger in the way the bowl is large beside its builder: substantial beyond the size of the thing that bears it. The note would occupy my mouth, the hollow beneath my breast, and the small recess where my folded limb lies. My body would give it several different interiors.

I would have eyes. I do not think I could belong here without some exposed capacity to attend.

They would be set unevenly, one close to the mineral, the other raised on a low ridge of flesh. The lower eye would face the orange patch under the plate. The higher one would face across the bare interval toward the tool.

I could see neither the whole tool nor the creature along its handle. Only the crystal tip, a little root winding, and one dark length of the split twig.

That would be enough for a correspondence.

The last joint of my extended limb would have a shallow notch. The split twig would have the same notch, much larger, on the side turned away from me. Neither would have been made for the other. Neither would need to cross the distance.

I would not know the builder through a history we had shared. There would be no visits, no exchange of objects, no remembered occasion when the tool lay elsewhere. Still, the builder would not be a stranger. Its wrist and my little limb, the root winding and the angle beneath my belly, would belong to the same arrangement without becoming interchangeable.

I would have one damaged foot.

Not an emblem of suffering. Just two gripping surfaces where there might have been three, and a thickened margin between them. The remaining surfaces would bear their share of my weight. Beside that foot, a filament of root would divide around a crystal edge. One branch would lie close to my skin without touching it.

That small clearance would matter as much as the contact under my belly.

There would be no task for me. This is the part I find hardest to let become true.

I keep wanting to make myself useful: a keeper of the fracture, a listener sorting the chamber’s tones, a small custodian ensuring that seed and clay remain together. But the seed does not require my supervision. The chamber’s condition is not precarious. My attention would not be a service performed against the possibility of loss.

I would attend because I had eyes, a mouth, a soft underside, and that particular place to put them.

Across the interval, the tool would rest on the fracture. Under the bowl, the builder would face the seed. My raised eye would include the crystal point and a strip of bare floor. The lower eye would contain mostly orange.

Nothing would arrive.

I would not be lonely, and I would not be reassured. Those feelings seem to require some other condition pressing against this one.

There would be the root near my damaged foot. The weight taken by my broad limbs. The fierce sound in the recess beneath my breast.

From the entrance, I would still look like a chip in the mineral.

Closer, the chip would have an eye.

Closer still, there would be room beneath me.
