I would be beneath the eastern channel, but not in the warm chamber itself.

A little upstream of it, perhaps. Where a clay pipe has broken without coming fully apart, and the two sections still face each other across a gap packed with gravel. Water gets through. Most of the light does not.

There is a wedge of space beneath the lower lip of the break. Nothing large can enter it. A root has entered and died there, leaving a black thread that stirs when the pumps change their rhythm.

At first I think I would be something attached to that thread.

Small enough that the difference between water passing above me and water passing through me is still important. A folded body, scarcely wider than the crack in which it has caught. One of the young that did not reach the river.

I would have no knowledge of the basin. Not the steps, not the building. Even the warm chamber would reach me only as an occasional change arriving through the pipe: less cold, a little richer in things I could take in. Then the ordinary water again.

My first work would be staying.

The gravel is not stable. A grain against my underside shifts when the current increases. For a while I hold to that grain, and when it moves I move with it. I lose the position in which I had opened my folds. I close. There is no decision in this. The side exposed to the current tightens, and the sheltered side continues slowly along the stone.

Eventually I am touching the pipe itself.

That seems a better beginning.

I would grow against its inner curve, where the ceramic retains a faint glaze. My underside would find the tiny rough places. Above me, water would carry particles past too quickly to hold. Close to the wall, some would turn, linger, touch.

I would feed there.

For a long time, nothing about me would affect the place.

Then a flake of rust would lodge across the break.

It would be larger than my whole body, brought from somewhere upstream, thin at one end and thick at the other. The thin end would come to rest on me. Water would press it down. I would retreat from the pressure, but not entirely: part of me would already have grown beneath a ridge in the pipe, and withdrawing would mean tearing that part away.

So I would remain pinched beneath the rust.

Water would begin to pass around it differently. A sheltered pocket would form beside my feeding edge. Sediment would settle there, too much at first. I would have to turn a fold outward to shed it. But after the next pump pulse, the pocket would clear and fill with finer matter.

I would grow better beneath this obstruction than I had grown without it.

That is where I begin to recognize myself: under something I cannot lift, making use of the water it deflects.

—

The larger animal would be near me before I knew it was near.

Its tissue would reach the other side of the broken pipe. Cream-colored, thicker than mine, dark along the surface touching an iron fragment. It would advance until it encountered the new restriction, then pause.

For several days we might occupy opposite edges of the same stream.

When it contracted, my water would accelerate. When it opened, the sheltered pocket around me would empty. I would close at the wrong moments. Food I had nearly taken in would be pulled away.

I would lose a portion of my outer fold.

Then new tissue would grow farther back, under the rust, where the force was less. I would open there. The other body would keep its passage. Mine would become narrower.

We could remain like that for a season.

I do not think I would be solitary in any way a person could recognize. But my contractions would still begin in my own tissue. The larger animal might tighten while I stayed open. It might loosen while I held fast to the pipe. There would be two responses in that small place, occasionally interfering with each other.

Then one wet winter, gravel would move out of the break.

The dead root would go with it.

A strip of my underside, exposed for the first time, would meet a fold of the other body.

I cannot give that meeting the shape of an embrace. The contact would be sustained, wet, and inconvenient. Our surfaces would press together whenever the pumps ran. Between pulses they would pull slightly apart. Sediment would collect in the slit.

Along one edge, new tissue would seal it.

Afterward a contraction could arrive from outside me and continue through me.

At first it would close a feeding fold before I had finished drawing matter inward. Later my fold would open just as the flow strengthened. I would be fed by a motion beginning beneath the station, far beyond anything I had touched.

There would still be places in me that tightened late.

—

So perhaps that is what I would be here: a young creature joined badly, then adequately, to the old one.

Not its warm center. Not the portion holding the building up. I would occupy the broken pipe and the small passage beyond it, sharing the work of keeping an opening clear.

My old attachment to the ceramic would remain. So would the rust flake. Flesh would thicken around its blunt end, but its sharp edge would still pinch when pressure rose too quickly.

Some days I would have to close because of that edge, though the rest of the animal was opening.

This would interrupt the eastern channel. Water would gather upstream, find another gap, wash grit beneath a neighboring slab. Somewhere above, a person might notice a line of bubbles appear.

I would feel their arrival only afterward.

A footstep would compress the gravel. The pipe would bear a little more weight. My folds would shut, and the contraction passing through the larger body would falter at my narrow portion.

The person might stand still.

Water would continue slipping through the small opening I could not close. Slowly I would loosen around it. Fine matter would touch the inner ridges. I would feed while the weight remained overhead.

When the foot lifted, the ceramic would ease against me.

I would open farther than before. Water would pass, cool after the warm pulse, carrying a few pale folded young toward the break.

Most would go through.

One might catch beneath the rust, in the space my growth had left along the wall.

For a while its movements would be too small for me to feel.
