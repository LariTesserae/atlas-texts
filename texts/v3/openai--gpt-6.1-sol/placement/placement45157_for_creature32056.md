At first I would look for a place beside the pool.

There is a dark patch between the worn flake and the animal’s wrist that seems unoccupied. From above, it has the shape of somewhere a small body could lie. Then I would lean closer and see that the darkness has a wet edge, and that the edge continues beneath the stone.

I think I would be there already.

Very little of me would be visible. A fold of skin, perhaps, pale enough to be mistaken for the exposed underside of a root. The rest would lie beneath the flake, following its shallow irregularities. My back would be broad and low. Where the stone thinned toward its edge, I would thin with it.

I would have to learn my shape by the things touching it.

Stone above. Beneath me, a saturated cushion of growth so fine that no individual leaf could be felt. Along one side, the animal’s almost hairless wrist. Along the other, a channel of water narrow enough to hold its surface curved between my skin and the moss.

There would be feet somewhere under me. Small ones, with the toes turned inward. I would know them less by their shape than by the few grains of grit held between them.

For a while I would suppose I was a soft-bodied creature sheltering beneath a useful object. The animal could lift the flake, and there I would be: exposed, flattened, my limbs gathered under me.

But that imagined lifting belongs to a place where one condition follows another. Here the thumb rests in its groove. The flake presses against my back. Beneath the thumb, beneath the stone, my body completes the fit.

I would not be waiting for either weight to leave.

My head would lie near the pool. It would be difficult to distinguish from my shoulders until the eye found the mouth: a short seam facing the stone, with two minute feelers laid along its edge. One feeler would reach the water. The other would touch a strand of fur on the animal’s wrist.

That would be enough distance to occupy my attention.

The fur would have a thickened place near its end. I would know the thickening by touch, and the small interval between it and the skin, and the bead of water held there. Farther away, beyond anything I could reach, a snail would adhere to the animal’s finger. Its weight would alter the pressure of the hand so slightly that my whole body would receive it.

I would have eyes, though they might take a long time to find. Two dark points under the lip of the flake. Nothing in front of them would be empty. A piece of submerged root would fill most of one eye’s field; the other would hold the amber underside of a salamander’s jaw.

I could live with those two views.

Perhaps the stone would be food. My mouth would rest against a softened mineral seam, its fine teeth seated in roughness too slight for the animal’s thumb to notice. There would be appetite in that contact. A slow, exact pressure. The stone would retain its seam, and I would retain the taste of it.

Under my belly there might be eggs.

I would discover them last, because they would feel so much like the rest of what supported me: several smooth, yielding points among the moss. Each would touch a different part of my skin. I would have no way to look down at them. Their number would be known through those separate touches, as surely as the flake above me was known through its weight.

The animal beside the pool would know me in that way too. Its wrist would lie along my side without quite resting on it. There would be a place in its fur shaped by my small shoulder. If it has a thought of me, the thought would need no inspection.

I would not speak into the hollow. A word could reach almost everything, but my mouth would be occupied, and I would have nothing to bring to the attention of the other bodies.

I would lie beneath the stone, with my feeler at the water and my feet holding their few grains.

From above, there would still appear to be a dark patch beside the animal’s wrist. Leaning closer would reveal the wet edge, then an eye.

Closer still, the eggs.
