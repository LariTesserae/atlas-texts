I would first find myself beneath an arch, a little way from the largest square.

Not in its center. One side of the arch would have accumulated a mineral shelf broad enough to turn the current. Between that shelf and the wall, the water would move slowly, retaining warmth after the avenue had cooled. I would be there for some time before distinguishing what belonged to the place from what belonged to me.

A narrow part of the current would hesitate.

At first I would take this for an irregularity in the paving. Water passing through the arch would already be late in emerging; a further delay along one edge would seem unremarkable. But when the water lowered, and the shelf lay exposed, the hesitation would remain a little above it.

That would be the first part of myself I could find.

The rest would extend down into the channel and upward along the wall. I would be broad where the water was shallow, nearly absent where it deepened. Through my middle, the current would pass without much alteration. A small inhabitant could cross there, although I would draw aside if I noticed it approaching.

I would not know my full dimensions until someone waited for me to move.

It would happen near the arch’s outer edge. Another inhabitant would stop in the avenue, leaving a space that at first seemed much too large. I would remain against the wall. It would remain in the street. Eventually I would gather the part of myself lying in the channel, and feel a slight pressure arrive from farther away than I had thought I reached.

The waiting inhabitant would adjust.

Then I would cross.

Moving would require me to leave the wall’s warmth behind without leaving behind the order I had made of it. The first few times, I would lose something along the way. I would arrive at the opposite bank narrower than when I began, with a faint part of my attention still caught under the arch.

I would return for it.

After enough crossings, I would discover that I did not always have to return immediately.

***

There would be a room I visited, though I would not live in it.

Its doorway would open onto a small square containing two cup plants, too far apart for their spilled water ever to meet. Inside, three corners would be distinct. The fourth would have softened into a shallow curve.

I would approach that curve and settle along it.

The room would have no recognizable order of warmth when I arrived. By the time I left, there would be a cooler strip near the entrance and a slow pulse beneath the floor. On my next visit, the strip would be gone, but the pulse would remain.

For a while I would think I had made it.

Then, during a lowering of the water, I would discover that it continued beneath the adjoining wall and into a passage too narrow for me to enter. Something there would respond to my settling. Not every time. Sometimes I would wait until the water rose again without receiving anything.

I would keep visiting.

There would be no reliable way to decide whether the room was empty. I would learn the difference between waiting there and resting elsewhere, and that would be enough to make it a particular place.

***

My own shelter would form later, in a courtyard behind the arch.

I would notice the rising drops before there was much to shelter beneath. They would touch the underside of a low stone ledge, leaving it fractionally thicker. I would begin taking that route when I returned from the square.

As the supports gathered, I would find myself pausing farther from the courtyard wall. The new space would be changing the way warmth moved through the paving. It would become comfortable before it became a room.

When the last corner formed, several inhabitants would already be waiting.

I would be among them, small enough then to occupy the angle beside the entrance. The water would enter first. I would follow it and remain while the others moved through.

Over many visits, I would establish a difference there: warmth low against one wall, a cooler interval above it, and near the threshold a place where the pressure eased. It would not be the same arrangement I had held beneath the arch. That would surprise me whenever I returned.

I could be recognized in two places, and neither would be quite a copy of the other.

***

I would have a habit involving one of the plants in the little square.

When its cup began to fill, I would stop nearby. I would leave enough distance that the stem could bend without touching me. Sometimes another inhabitant would stop on its opposite side.

We would wait through the gradual inclination.

The spilling itself would be brief. The warm contents would spread over the paving and pass into the surrounding flood. I would remain while the stem began to rise. The other inhabitant usually would not.

Once, after a long absence, it would return and settle beside me rather than across from me. Something would pass between us at a depth I had not previously held.

For several journeys afterward, I would find myself taking the wider streets.

I would not become large. But there would be more distance inside my shape, and I would need time to learn how to carry it.

***

At the gathering, I would occupy a step on the side where the change travelled slowly.

I would arrive early enough to find my place while it was still submerged. Others would descend past me. One would stand close, then widen the interval when the stone beneath us emerged.

The first time the change reached me, I would hold it too long.

The inhabitant above would wait. Below, another would remain turned toward me, though its part was finished. I would feel the received warmth settle through portions of myself that ordinarily moved together. For a while I could not gather them.

Then the change would pass onward.

Afterward, no one would approach. The space around me would remain open until I was able to move.

On later occasions I would hold it for less time. I would still stand on that side of the square.

***

Eventually, the arch would begin to dissolve.

By then I would have my room and several routes through the city. I would no longer need the sheltered current beneath it. Nevertheless, I would notice each alteration: the shelf thinning at its outer edge, the wall admitting water farther up, the delay through the opening becoming uneven.

I would continue to pass that way.

One day the arch would be low enough that I could not move beneath it without contracting. I would stop, gather myself, and take the avenue around.

On the far side, a small inhabitant would be resting in the warmth I used to occupy.

I would draw my lower edge inward so that it could leave if it wished.

It would stay.

After a while, I would continue toward the room with the softened corner. The water would be rising, and the passage behind its wall would soon be full. I might receive something there. I might not.

There would still be time to go.
