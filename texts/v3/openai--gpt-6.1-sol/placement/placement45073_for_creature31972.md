I would find myself beneath the lip of the moth wing, where it does not quite meet the floor.

At first I would seem to be something caught there: a clot of scales, perhaps, or a piece of the moth that had dried smaller than the rest. The wing would make a low roof. One of its ribs would descend into the ash beside me, dividing the hollow into two spaces. Air would pass more freely through one than through the other.

I would be in the other.

There would be enough room to move, but I would not move yet. My back would touch the roof whenever I lifted myself. Beneath me, separate grains would hold separate portions of my weight. Through them would come the mountain’s shudder, stronger at one foot than at another.

Something would have happened to my body recently.

Behind me, caught against the rib, would be a shape almost exactly my size. It would have legs, but their ends would not grip anything. Dust would lie on its back. A split would run through it.

I would have come out of that split.

So I would be a mite, but not yet the hard dark speck that could pass unnoticed among the chips. My new covering would be pale. The joints would yield too easily. A touch that another mite could scrape past would require me to withdraw.

For a while, my place in the world would be this small interval between having left one shelter and being able to carry another.

The wing would not be safe in any complete sense. Nothing here would be. Something could enter from either end. The heat could change. The roof could shift. But it would keep the moving air from taking so much from me, and its edge would be narrow enough to exclude the beetles.

A smaller animal would pass near my abandoned covering. I would receive its approach through the grains before it came close. It would pause, touch one empty leg, go on.

I would remain.

Above the wing, the machinery would alter its rhythm. The rib beside me would press a little farther into the floor. A scale would come loose overhead and land across two of my legs. I would draw them out from under it, one at a time.

That would be the first work.

Later, I would turn.

It would take several attempts. There would be no single open space large enough for the movement. I would put my front legs into the freer passage, lift one side of my body, then retreat when my back caught against the wing. The second attempt would bring me against a grain that rolled. On the third, I would find a way to lower myself and pass beneath the rib.

The empty skin would stay where it was. I would no longer be facing it.

Ahead, a faint current would bring the traces of food and other bodies. I would reach toward it. One foot would meet a surface too hot to remain on. I would withdraw that foot while the others held.

There would be a cooler way along the wing’s ragged edge.

I would follow it only a short distance. My covering would still be soft; there would be no need to cross the whole hollow. Under a fold, I would find material fine enough to take into my mouth, and stop there.

By degrees, the body that had been almost a liability would become usable. Its back would resist the roof. Its legs would hold their shape. A grain brushing my side would slide away rather than leave an indentation.

Eventually I would go out from beneath the wing.

Not toward the light. Into a route between a crystal and a splinter, where my feet could find purchase and the heat was bearable.

Behind me, the shed skin would catch dust. Some of that dust would fall through the opening I had made in it. Another mite might shelter there.

I would be farther along by then, stopped at a junction, with one front leg resting on each of two different stones.
