I would be beneath the slab that holds the pool, a little to one side of the green stain.

There is a place there where two surfaces almost meet. Water enters it slowly enough that it does not wash anything away. Dust has lodged against a ridge in the lower stone; a root has reached the dust and divided. Behind the root is a pocket scarcely larger than a seed.

I would fit inside it.

At first, from the ridge, I might imagine myself living beside the warm boulder. But the boulder’s warmth is exposed, and whatever stands beside it must keep attending to the sky. I would live farther in, where the changes arrive through stone: cold along my underside, warmth across my back, a bead of water touching one foreleg.

I would be a beetle. Small, dark, somewhat flattened. There would be a dull red margin to my wing cases, visible only when I was wet. Beneath them, wings folded into something too short to carry me.

Most of my journeys would take place within the distance between the woman’s wrist and elbow.

That would be enough ground to know badly.

A passage that was open yesterday might contain a swollen root today. A grain might turn beneath my foot. Water might fill a depression through which I had always passed dry. I would come to an obstruction and feel along it, finding a way around or remaining there for some time, working my antennae against a surface that gave me nothing useful.

When the sun reached the slab, I would begin to move before the ice in the pool had loosened.

There would be things to eat where the moss ended: a damaged springtail, an egg soft enough to pierce, something caught between wet leaves. Often there would be nothing. I would go farther.

One morning, the woman would lift a cushion of stems beside the shelter, and my roof would rise with it.

For a moment I would remain attached to its underside. My feet would hold while the cold space beneath me widened. Then a stem would turn and I would fall.

She might see me land. She might not.

I would lie still on my back, legs drawn close, while brightness reached places on my body that usually stayed covered. After a while I would open my legs. One foot would find gravel; another would find nothing. I would turn over after several attempts and go toward the nearest dark edge.

It would be the shadow of her bowl.

There I would spend part of the day beneath the strip of hide used to mend it. The hide would smell of salt and old handling. Where it curled away from the wood, there would be a narrow shelter.

She would pick up the bowl to drink, carrying me with it.

I would not know that I had crossed the hollow. I would know that the support beneath my feet had begun to move, that light was arriving from unfamiliar directions, and that there was nowhere farther in to go.

When she put it down, I would descend by a crack in the wood.

Now the pool would be far away. Between me and my seam would lie bare stone, moss, the shallow groove beneath the wall, and the open space where the round-eared animal carried grass. I would not possess these as a route. I would have the edge immediately before me.

I might spend the night beneath the bowl.

The next day she would rinse it. I would already have gone into the dry stems along the shelter wall, where fragments of leaves lay among the bedding. There would be food there, though less moisture than I needed. By afternoon I would be following the wall outward.

At the threshold, a clipped stalk would rest across a small gap.

The animal would have left it there. Its underside would still be green. I would climb onto it, descend at the other end, and continue under a stone.

Perhaps I would eventually return to the seam below the pool. Perhaps a different damp pocket would hold me. There are more such places than can be seen by kneeling, and some would open only after the next hard frost.

When the woman left, I would be somewhere she had not looked.

Her foot binding would scrape the stone beside the cairn. A few grains would shift. Much later, one of them might settle at the entrance to my shelter.

I would meet it headfirst in the dark.

If there were room, I would go around it.
