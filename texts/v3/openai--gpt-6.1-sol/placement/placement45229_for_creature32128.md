At first I would put myself beneath the fallen trunk, where the underside is still damp. There is room there for something small enough to turn without lifting the wood: a beetle, perhaps, or one of the animals that withdraws when the bark is opened. The dryness above would be a roof. The softness below would be food and shelter together.

But I do not stay small. I seem to need the pool as well as the trunk, and the narrow crossing between them. I need somewhere cooler than the air, somewhere my whole weight can rest.

Under the bank, the chamber is low enough to touch my back.

My belly lies on clay. One forefoot is folded against me; the other rests farther forward, where the floor begins to slope toward water. Fine roots hang from the ceiling. When I raise my head, one brushes the scales above my eye.

I can see a little of the entrance. Beyond it, a stem moves, stops, moves again. The brightness there is faint, but greater than the brightness around me.

I open my mouth slightly and close it. My tongue goes out.

Something has passed close to the bank. There is the smell of it on the mud, then less of it when I lift my head. I remain still. The clay is taking heat from me, and for now I stay pressed against it.

I would be the monitor, though I arrive at that slowly, through the inconveniences of its body.

There is the foot that cannot quite take the same purchase as the other. There is the tail, too long to leave entirely within the chamber once my shoulders are outside. There is the root across the entrance. I press against it, stop, push again. The mud shifts beneath my hind claws.

Then my chest is clear, and I stand among the sedges.

I would not carry the whole basin in my awareness. From here there is a strip of water, an edge of mud, the lower parts of several stems. A fallen frond blocks the way along the bank. I can put my head over it, but to bring my body through I must climb or go around.

The crab moves.

I turn toward it. My head passes between two stems, and one bends against my neck. The crab pauses with its legs spread on the mud. Then it runs, and I follow far enough to close my mouth over it.

Its shell breaks between my jaws. I shift it once before swallowing.

Afterward, I am still again. A sedge blade remains caught against my shoulder. When I move forward, it slips free.

I would know the country through repeated contact. The slope where my feet slide. The opening wide enough for my head but not my shoulders. The bank where I can reach the water without dropping. I might approach the same obstruction several times, investigating from one side and then another, before leaving it behind.

At the rotten trunk I push my snout into a place I have opened before. Loose wood enters my mouth. I pull back and work my jaws. There is movement farther in, beneath material that does not yield so easily. I set one forefoot against the trunk and rake downward.

For a while, this occupies me completely.

The hum continues beyond the trees. When it changes, I lift my head. Nothing close to me moves. After a pause I lower it again.

Farther out, water runs into a channel where fish gather. I have been there before, and I go there again. My feet leave the bottom. My legs draw inward; my tail moves from side to side. The bend near its tip follows each stroke.

Under the roots, the water is dark. I come up where there is room to breathe, then turn along the bank. A fish slips past my mouth. I turn again. Silt rises from the bottom and hangs around my head.

Tonight I leave without catching one.

By the time I return to the pool, the branches above it have begun to separate from the darkness. A bird moves overhead. I pause below it, my neck raised, then continue toward the fallen trunk.

Climbing takes several efforts. My claws catch in the dry wood. My belly drags across an uneven place, and my tail remains on the ground until the rest of me is nearly settled.

Here I would be, stretched along the trunk, with one foot hanging loose beside it. Mud dries on my flank. Beneath me, there is still coolness in the wood. Ahead, a small part of its upper surface has begun to brighten.

When the sun reaches it, I move forward.
