I would be inside the little wall, against the grain whose bulge faces inward.

From above, I would look nearly round. The light would show a shallow division down my back, not quite a seam, and four pale marks near one end that might be mistaken for pores. My legs would be tucked beneath me. There would be more of them than the roundness of my body seemed to require.

The bent hair would lie close enough for one of its fine branches to touch my side.

I would not be the keeper of the enclosure. I would not know why its grains had been set there. But the inward-facing bulge would touch me just below the division in my back, and that contact would be as distinct to me as the white speck enclosed in the snail’s shell is distinct from the flesh around it. The grain would have a cool center and a slightly less cool outer face. My body would know both without having to reach through one to the other.

There would be a small flat place on my side where we met.

It would be tempting to say I had worn it there. Yet there would be no wear on the grain, no powder fallen beneath the contact. The flat place would belong to my body as the bulge belonged to the stone.

My mouth would be on the underside, turned toward the clean earth. Two short parts would fit together there, one overlapping the other imperfectly. In their imperfect fit would be my sound.

Not a tick. Something softer, with a coarse edge: the sound of a stiff fiber drawn through a narrow opening. Within it, almost concealed, a second sound would have the thinness of a split in a dry leaf.

I would hear the beetle clearly. Its little flaw would not resemble mine. The beetle’s roughness comes at the end of each tick. Mine would run through the whole sound, sometimes more apparent in the low part, sometimes in the high, though neither part would change.

The white earth beneath my mouth would contain a single dark grain. It would be too large to eat. I would have the necessary mouth for eating smaller things, and somewhere inside me a small portion of food would retain its irregular shape. A soft fold would lie against it. Another fold would rest against the first.

I would not be hungry. There would simply be food in me, with its own dark surface, and the flesh around it would be alive.

Under my left side, two feet would touch. One would have a hook at its tip; the other, a cluster of short hairs. The hook would lie among those hairs without catching them. A third foot would rest on the floor of the enclosure, close to an inaccuracy in the groove beneath the bent hair.

That would be the place I knew most precisely.

The groove would turn a little too soon. The hair would follow its own bend above it, leaving a narrow piece of white ground uncovered. My foot would rest on that piece. To something looking down, it might seem that the groove had been made carelessly. From beneath my body, the uncovered ground would have a definite width. The end of my foot would occupy less than half of it.

I could have been the one who made the groove.

My underside would have a hard edge suited to such work, and its curve would be a little different from the curve of the hair. But there would be no memory of drawing it through the earth. The groove, the edge, and the small discrepancy between them would all be available to me. I would have no need to decide which had preceded which.

Beyond the wall, the other bodies would lie close. Through a gap between two grains I would see the lowest part of a snail’s foot, faintly yellow where it thickened. Farther away, the centipede would occupy the bend of the chamber. I would hear the finer sounds beneath it as clearly as the sound beside my own mouth.

The wall would hide very little. One grain would interrupt my view of a root. Another would leave visible only the translucent margin of a leaf. The light would remain complete inside the enclosure, including under my folded legs.

There would be a portion of my back I could not touch with any foot or mouthpart. It would face the ceiling. Its shallow division would hold the same mineral brightness as everything else, but I would know that part of myself chiefly through the thin branch of hair resting beside it.

The branch would end in a blunt, almost invisible tip.

Against my skin, it would feel larger than it looked.

I would have no account of the bare shelf. It would be elsewhere in the chamber, exposed and fully lit. My place would have the inward bulge, the dark grain under my mouth, the two touching feet. Between my body and the little wall there would remain several clearances, none large enough for another animal of my shape.

In one of those clearances, the forked end of the hair would lie.

My sound would fill it without making the fibers tremble.
