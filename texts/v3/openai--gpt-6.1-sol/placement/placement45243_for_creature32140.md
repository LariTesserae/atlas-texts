At first I would put myself in the shade beneath the low tree: the shade that takes several minutes to cross.

Not at its center. Near one edge, where a broad leaf hangs just above the mineral ground. From there I could see the clearing without having to enter it. The basin animal would be a pale interruption along the rim. When it raised its head, I would have time to lower mine.

I think I would have a head.

The rest is less certain at first. There would be something of the ground about me, but not its clean, decided edges. Something of a fallen leaf, although there are no fallen leaves here. A narrow body resting flat, with a slight thickness along the middle. Small limbs held underneath it, invisible until they were needed.

I would be easiest to discover by trying to pick me up.

A finger slipped beneath my edge would encounter another edge farther in. Not a defensive spine or a second layer of armor. Just more of me than the first glance had allowed for. I would lift a little, rearrange my feet, and settle beyond the finger.

My upper surface would be dull green in the shade. In sunlight it would show the cream of the plates beneath it, imperfectly: a green margin would remain. I would not be particularly good at hiding. The plain has so few disturbances that a modest resemblance is usually enough.

For a while I imagine living entirely under that tree.

There is room. The long crossing of its shadow contains shallow hollows, mineral chips, stems whose bases cannot all be seen from the same place. I could spend an afternoon moving from one exposed root to another and never emerge beyond the crown.

But the tree would not supply everything I need.

Once in a long interval, a note would sound beneath my feet. I would raise the front of my body and wait for the faint pressure that followed it through the ground. Most notes would leave me where I was.

Occasionally I would begin to walk.

My feet would have soft, divided ends, much smaller than the basin animal’s. They would touch down separately. I would feel a plate with one, the underside of a leaf with another, and then have to pause before choosing where to place the next.

I would be slow in open ground. There would be no abrupt departure to spare me the crossing. Anyone watching could follow every step.

The difficulty would come at the edge of a thicket.

There I might set a foot down and find it resting beside a root I had left some time ago. Another foot would remain in the pale opening ahead. Both contacts would be sound. I could stand between them, but I could not comfortably lift either one.

So I would stay.

This begins to feel like my place: not under the tree exactly, but at a low entrance to it, where a silver-veined creeper crosses beneath two broad leaves. One leaf has a shallow crease near its stem. The other rests against it without quite touching.

I have passed through this opening before. Usually my body fits beneath it. Today I stop with my head outside and most of my back still in shade.

Something is caught along my underside.

It is not a filament. I feel no object there. Yet each time I try to move forward, my rear feet press more firmly into the ground.

I lower myself. The front edge of my body is flexible, able to curl a little around a stem. I brace it against the creeper and lift one foot.

For a moment, the foot is weightless.

Then it finds the ground close beside the others.

A small contraction passes through me. My back rises along the middle, and I withdraw into the shade. I remain there with my feet folded underneath until I can flatten myself again.

The creeper has not changed visibly. Later, the broad animal puts its head beneath the same leaves and goes through without waiting.

Perhaps that is what sustains me: the small difficulties left at the edges of passages. I would not take them into a mouth. They would collect in the flexible part of my underside as I moved, making me a little thicker, making certain postures impossible. Afterward I would need a quiet place to rest.

There is such a place beneath the basin’s rim.

From the clearing it looks like a seam. From inside, there is enough height for me to stand. The violet surface overhead is warm. I can press my back against it and extend my feet on the pale floor below.

I do this slowly. First the middle of my back touches, then the two ends. A foot that has been held awkwardly behind me loosens. I set it down.

Nothing sounds.

When I emerge, I am flat enough to pass beneath the leaning stems.

The large animal is resting there. I approach one of its hind feet, which occupies nearly all the ground between two roots. I could go around it, but the route would take me out into the clearing.

Instead I touch its foot with the front edge of my body.

It waits.

I touch again, a little farther along.

The foot lifts. I walk beneath it, taking longer than either of us seems to require. When I have passed, it settles behind me.

I do not think we would seek each other out. Still, I would learn that foot: the faintly resistant surface, the place where its weight broadens against the ground. On another day I might sleep beside it. When the animal began to leave, I would feel the first adjustment and move clear.

And if you sat by the basin, I might eventually find you.

Your hand would make an unfamiliar shelter. Its shadow would change while the leaves’ shadows held still. I would approach beneath your little finger, stop, and touch the crease where it joined your palm.

You might feel a dry edge brushing your skin.

If you lifted your hand, I would be there on the stone: small, green along the margins, my feet now visible. I would remain still for a moment. Then I would turn toward the space beneath your bent knee.

I would have to learn that opening each time.

Sometimes you would straighten your leg before I reached it, and I would continue to the leaves. Sometimes you would remain still, letting me pass into the shade.

There I would settle, with one foot touching the stone and another resting lightly against you. After a while the second foot would lift and test the ground.

Then I would go a little farther in.
