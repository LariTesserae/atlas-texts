At first I would find myself where the cracked slab was laid over the older crack.

Not inside either break, but beneath the slight overhang where the two repairs fail to meet. There is a strip of darkness there, too shallow for a room and too regular to look accidental. Dust has gathered at its entrance. Farther in, the dust stops.

I would know the court first by what reaches that place.

The great note would not arrive as a roar. One surface would press against my back, another against my underside, and between them there would be a difference that never quite resolved. The upper slab would give a little before the lower one did. Something farther down would arrive through the mortar ridge, take hold of me, and pass. I would have no name for the sound as a whole. I could no more perceive it all at once than the inhabitant could put both hands around the construction.

For some time I think I would be mistaken for a pad laid beneath the repair to keep it from rocking.

I would be pale, broad, and low. My underside would hold the grain of whatever I had rested on longest. There would be a reddish fold around my edge, protected from abrasion, and several small hard places within me that could move apart without anything breaking. When still, I would resemble a piece of clay pressed flat between two stones and forgotten.

But I would not remain a single thickness.

At the entrance of the overhang, I would lift one edge. A short section would turn down against the pavement. Another would release from the underside of the slab. These movements would be slow because each surface would have to be relinquished separately. It would look less like crawling than like a close fit being undone.

I would not have the inhabitant’s hands. Nothing about me would suggest that I could shape a block or lift one into place. My useful part would be a fringe of blunt, yielding projections along one side. Some could enter the vents in the pavement. Others could flatten until they were scarcely more than a thickened skin.

The first time the inhabitant found me outside my place, it might try to put me back.

Its heavy fingers would close around my middle, exposing the impression of the slab on my underside. I would contract. Not violently. Just enough that the place where it had gripped me ceased to be a convenient place to grip.

It would pause.

One of its narrow fingers would settle into a fold along my edge. I would hold against that finger, and against the floor, and for a while neither of us would move.

I do not think we would recognize one another through resemblance. There would be little resemblance to find. But it might notice that the pressure under its finger did not merely follow its own.

When it eased, I would sometimes remain tense.

When it pressed, I would sometimes yield elsewhere.

Eventually it would set me down beside the repair rather than beneath it.

That would be my first small departure from being a piece of the court.

***

I would travel at the bases of walls.

The routes available to the tall inhabitant would not be mine. I would pass beneath the low pipe that troubles its leg, but fail at a ridge it crosses without attending to it. A shallow step would require a long maneuver: the forward edge secured above, the middle drawn thin, the rear part peeled from the floor.

There would be places I could reach only by letting go entirely.

I would dislike those places.

With no broad surface against me, the many differences of the court would collapse into a coarse trembling. I would cling to the rounded edge of a block, unable to tell whether the next purchase was close or merely strongly connected through the masonry. Sometimes I would withdraw after extending almost my whole length.

My world would be small without being simple.

A loose grain beneath me could obscure an entire passage of sensation. A worn depression could allow several parts of my underside to touch at once and disclose something I had never felt from the slab beside it. I might spend a long while there, although nothing visible changed.

I would be drawn to the pillar, but not immediately.

Near its foot, the floor would be too tightly bound to the walls. There would be little purchase for distinguishing one from another. Higher up, behind a projecting lip, I would find a narrow patch touched less often by the inhabitant. The clay there would still hold the end of a thumbprint.

I could fit myself into its shallow whorl.

For a while, that would be where I rested.

***

Perhaps the inhabitant would begin to carry me.

Not as a companion carried for company. It would find that I fitted between its palm and a wall in a way that allowed a particular tremor to become more distinct. Or I would span two openings its fingers could not attend to while its other hand was occupied.

It would place me, then wait.

I would be useful, but inconsistently.

Sometimes I would spread myself over the holes. Sometimes I would enter only one. Sometimes I would draw all my projections close and become a smooth, unhelpful thickness beneath its hand.

There would be parts of the court I did not want to touch. I would not know why in any form that could be explained. A certain pipe mouth would make the hard pieces within me pull together. On one slab I would be unable to release my underside evenly. The inhabitant might take these responses as information about the construction.

They would also be information about me.

Over time it would learn the difference, or at least learn not to press me against that slab.

I would learn that the broad clay patch on its chest was not the same as the wall. Beneath the patch, contact would change without any corresponding change in the court. There would be movements too small to see, pauses too irregular to belong to the fixed note.

When it rested in its chamber, I might settle along the exposed side of that patch.

Neither of us would need the space the other used.

But sometimes it would shift and dislodge me. Sometimes I would leave before it finished settling. Our fit would never become exact.

***

The old repair would remain my place for a long time.

I would return beneath it, align the hard parts of my body with the familiar unevenness, and feel the upper slab settle against me. Yet after traveling, I would lie differently. A projection that once remained folded would rest in a little vent. One end of me would extend beyond the shelter and acquire the pale, dulled surface of exposed things.

The inhabitant might add a little clay along the overhang.

Not to enclose me. To give the slab a better bearing beside me.

I would feel its fingers working through the weight above my back. Later, when I moved away, the repair would hold without me.

I think that would matter.

I would not wish to become the indispensable living joint on which the court depended. There are already so many close fits here, so many bodies and additions that have worn into one another until departure looks like damage.

I would want a place that retained the impression of me without requiring me to occupy it.

So I would be this small, slow thing beneath the doubled slab, often mistaken for packing material: able to transmit a pressure, alter it, or move aside. I would know a few feet of the court exceedingly well and the rest through uncertain connections.

And on some occasions, when the inhabitant lowered its hand beside my shelter, I would emerge before it touched me.

I would climb onto the two broad fingers and settle across them, leaving my underside free of the floor.

For a moment there would be only that less familiar support.

Then it would carry me somewhere I had not yet been.
