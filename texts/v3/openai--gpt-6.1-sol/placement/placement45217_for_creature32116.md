At first I would put myself in one of the places where the sounds cancel.

It seems like the only possible place to remain: a narrow interval beside a rounded outcrop, quiet enough to hear the gravel shift beneath a shoe. I would crouch there and mistake the relief for a property of the stone. A refuge.

But that is where I would go as someone who had arrived. It is not where I would live.

Closer to the ground, the quiet would lose its importance. The stone would still be moving. The pressure would still arrive through it, differently at each point of contact. What had seemed an absence would turn out to be an arrangement I could stand inside.

I think I would be under a shelf, but not deep under it. Near the place where its shadow ends.

There would be an old feeding hollow there, slightly too broad for me.

***

I would have left the underside of another animal.

Not decisively. One front foot would have found a wet depression beyond the body that covered me, and stayed there. The other would have followed much later. For a while I would have fed with my rear feet still sheltered, my mouth working at the outer edge of a patch already maintained by someone else.

That first distance would have been full of differences.

Outside, moisture would have drawn away from my foot margins faster. The stone would have been warmer against my front pads than against the tucked pair. A tremor formerly received through the adult’s body would have reached me directly, with a fine unevenness I had not needed to distinguish before.

I would have withdrawn more than once.

Then there would have been a position from which withdrawing required more movement than staying.

I would have stayed.

My back would still be pale. The iron would not yet have made much of a pattern in it. From above, I might look like a damp flake that had come loose from the crust.

There would be no one above to notice.

***

The hollow would be a short journey along the shelf, with a dry interruption partway across.

I would not see it as an empty dwelling. I would reach it by a groove that held enough brine for the edge of one foot. The groove would end against a curved deposit. Beyond that deposit, the stone would offer a better contact than anything I had found since leaving.

One pad would spread.

Then another.

The old occupant’s six places would not match mine. Its rear feet had been farther apart. One depression would be deep enough that my pad could not reach the bottom without drawing my body sideways.

For some time I would occupy the hollow crookedly.

That seems important. I would not fit the basin perfectly simply because I belonged to it. I would have to find a workable posture within something made by another life.

My mouth would settle against the scalloped edge. Beneath its hard whiteness, there would be food.

The first part of my life there would consist of making that food accessible without losing my hold.

***

I would know very little of the basin.

Not its far rim. Not the shallow pool with the sky in it. I would have no use for the thought that the passages continued for miles beneath me.

There would be a wet seam that passed under my left feet and failed somewhere beyond my right ones. There would be a fine vibration easiest to distinguish when my middle pads were unfolded. There would be a neighboring animal whose weight reached me through the shelf.

We would not face each other.

Sometimes it would release a foot, and a change would come through the stone. I would shift my own contact. Sometimes the change would come again before I had settled.

Eventually I would learn which disturbances required me to move, and which could pass through my body without costing me my place.

There might also be an electrical contact between us. A small one, available only when the film beneath the shelf was continuous.

I would not know whether the other animal was near. Nearness would be where a change could reach me.

On the other side of a dry band, a body almost touching mine could remain unknown.

***

If you came, I would receive your weight before your shadow.

One part of the shelf would be loaded differently. The familiar differences between my feet would no longer resolve. I would unfold a middle pad and place it nearer the lip.

You might take that for recognition.

Then your hand would come down.

Its warmth would matter less than its pressure. For a while, you would become part of the surface through which I found my position. I would settle with you included.

When you lifted your hand, I would have to settle again.

You would be able to watch both movements. You might stay long enough to see my mouth lower against the crust. But most of what your presence altered would remain beneath me, in the little compartments of my feet.

I would not turn toward you.

***

I think I would live there long enough for one side of the hollow to change.

My secretions would loosen the deposit beneath my mouth. My feet would keep six small areas dark. The rear depression that had been too deep would slowly acquire a lining, and my pad would rest more easily in it.

I would grow darker in the same place.

Perhaps I would shelter a young animal. It would begin where I could not look, under the plates of my own body. I would lift enough to let its mouth reach the crust, taking a little less from that portion of the edge.

Later, one of its feet would appear beyond mine.

It would find a contact I had never used.

For a while its movements would disturb my footing. I would adjust. There would be twelve pads on a patch worn for six, and feeding would require small accommodations neither of us could abandon.

Then it would move beyond the hollow.

I would remain with the altered edge.

***

So I would be one of the low grey animals, neither the oldest nor the best placed. I would live near the end of a shelf’s shadow, in a hollow inherited badly and occupied well enough.

I would have two feet spread in old depressions, two holding the damp margin, and two tucked beneath me until something changed.

Above my back, the basin would roar.

Under it, there would still be places to put a foot.
