I would be below the lowest street, in a settling box whose lid can no longer be opened.

A little water still passes through it. The inlet is higher than the outlet, and between them stands a bent partition intended to slow the flow. Rust collects on one side. On the other, the sediment is pale and lies in curved layers. Some of those layers have the impressions of screw threads.

At first I would be difficult to distinguish from them.

I would have the shape of something that had lived around a fitting: a short, hollow sleeve, split along one side. The fitting itself would be gone. Perhaps it had broken; perhaps the tissue had loosened and slipped from it during a sediment release. There would be no way to tell from my shape whether I had enclosed a moving part or merely occupied the space beside one.

My outer surface would be chalky. My inner surface would still be soft.

Water entering the box would turn me slightly against the partition. Each time, the split would open, then close. A few white flakes would come away. Beneath them, something nearly transparent would stretch without breaking.

I would not yet know the district. I would know the partition at three points, and the difference between water arriving through the inlet and water lingering beneath me. I would know that one edge of my body lay buried more deeply than the other.

For a long time, those would be sufficient distinctions.

Then a filament from the inhabitant would reach into the box.

It would advance along the underside of the lid, descend at a rivet, and touch the pale sediment. There would be useful material here: films growing on the discarded tissue, traces of grease, whatever the water had brought down from the bearings. The filament would spread over these things slowly.

When it reached me, it would enter through my split.

I would tighten.

Not much. Enough to hold its tip between my inner surfaces.

The filament would pull once, using a tension that originated several buildings away. The movement would lift me out of my shallow impression in the sediment. Beneath me would be a darker patch, the shape of my former position.

I would remain attached.

***

After that, I would begin to receive movements for which I had no room.

A chamber filling. A collar taking the weight of a shaft. A membrane drawn against a grate.

They would arrive reduced to small changes in the filament. I would respond with the only arrangement I had: the hollow sleeve, the split, the soft lining. I would close around an absent fitting. I would release it. Sometimes the transmitted movement would require a wider opening than my body could make, and my buried edge would lift.

The inhabitant would grow into that opening.

It would not restore what I had been. There would be no fitting to restore me around. Instead, its tissue would join my two ends to the partition and leave the middle free. Water would begin passing through me rather than around me.

I would become a pocket in the settling box.

There would be no clear moment at which I ceased to be a loose fragment. Some of my old surface would continue flaking. Some would darken and become supple again. New tissue would cross the split without quite closing it.

That narrow opening would remain my most sensitive part.

During a sediment release, it would draw together before the downward current arrived. When the covers settled, it would open late. A small quantity of relatively clear water would remain inside me while the box filled with cloud.

The films on my lining would grow there.

I would have made a sheltered place by failing to keep pace.

***

There would be a movement I continued making after its cause had disappeared.

Every few cycles, I would turn inward along one side, as though the missing fitting were about to enter me. The newer tissue would resist. The partition would prevent the turn from completing. A crease would form in the same place, deepen, and relax.

The inhabitant would accommodate this. A second passage would grow around me to carry the water when my opening narrowed.

I would be left with my unnecessary motion.

Eventually, a strand passing through the box would carry a disturbance unlike the others. It would begin with the counterweights reaching the bottoms of their shafts. Then, while the inspection lamps were bright, something near the pumping-house window would tighten and wait.

I would turn inward.

The crease would deepen farther than usual.

For those few seconds, the water inside me would remain almost still. A particle suspended near my lining would descend until it touched the bottom. I would feel that contact separately from the tension in the strand.

Then the lamp would fade. The tissue near the window would release. Water would pass through me again.

On the next cycle I would wait for it.

Not for the light; no light would reach the box. I would wait for the held tension, the brief interval in which one part of the animal stopped while the machinery continued.

My lining would thicken where the particle had settled.

***

I think that is where I would find myself: in an old piece of the inhabitant that had been taken up again, but imperfectly.

I would have no view of the windows. I would receive the reflected rooms through the body’s response to them, as pulls arriving along a strand. The long aisle might reach me as an opening. The stairs might reach me as pressure sustained beyond the end of a stroke. I could not distinguish a room from a change in the load.

But I would begin to grow differently when those movements came.

One side of my pocket would extend beneath the partition. It would enter a space too low to admit the thicker tissue and spread along the floor of the box. There, under the sediment, I would make a second small chamber.

Nothing would pass through it at first.

It would fill when the lamps brightened and empty after they dimmed. Its wall would contain a curved flake from my old outer surface. The flake would shift each time, wearing a faint mark into the floor.

The larger inhabitant might eventually use the chamber. It might send nourishment through it, or waste, or some pressure that required a place to go. For now, the passage leading into it would be too narrow.

Still, I would keep it open.

Above me, the district would draw down its mud. The hook would rise and settle. A sleeping branch would loosen somewhere in a wall, and another would take its weight.

I would feel each of these imperfectly.

And when the shorter piston reached its end, I would hold a little water beneath the partition, around the curved flake, until the movement came that allowed me to let it go.
