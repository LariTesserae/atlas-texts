At first I would stay on the broad step.

Not because it was safe. I would have no means of knowing that. But the warmth beneath it would be the first thing in that place that asked nothing of me. I could sit without understanding how the streets moved, or which edge would bear weight, or whether the light overhead was approaching or passing away.

I would put my hands beside me on the stone.

After a while, I would notice that one hand was warmer than the other.

I would shift it. The warmth would remain where it had been.

That would seem a small enough thing to investigate. Much smaller than the gulf.

***

I would begin by following the warmth with my palms.

There would be a patch beneath the step, another inside the doorway, another too high on the wall for me to reach. Between them, the stone would be cold. I would not discover a hidden seam connecting them. Whatever passed among these places would have to cross the cold without warming it.

For several days I would mistake this for a route.

I would go from patch to patch, waiting where I could not continue. Sometimes the next warm place would be on a district moving steadily away. Sometimes it would be inside a shallow room scarcely deep enough to accommodate my hand.

Then I would come upon a court where an animal was resting with its head against the floor.

I would sit at a distance.

The creature would lift two limbs and set them down again. Its plates would loosen from the stone with a sound too slight to separate from the movement of dust.

The wall behind me would warm.

The animal would turn, not toward me exactly, but toward the little place where my back touched the wall.

I would leave my hand there after standing.

For the first time, I would feel the warmth arrive rather than find it already waiting.

***

I think I would lose track of my body gradually.

There would be no moment of becoming stone. No last breath, no sudden enlargement into all those empty streets.

Instead, small difficulties.

I would remember sitting in a doorway, but not crossing the room to reach it. I would know that a certain stair was cold along its lower three steps, although I had never descended it. Once, watching a district pass across the gulf, I would recognize the sheltered side of a wall whose outer face was turned toward me.

I would still be hungry sometimes. Or I would remember hunger with enough force that I went looking for a warm seam.

There, an animal would feed.

The film would open against its mouth. A fold in one of its joints would open as well, and for a little while I would have an awkward additional sense: the stone was close on every side, except in one direction where there was room.

Then the animal would move on.

The sense would go with it.

I would remain by the seam, neither satisfied nor quite hungry anymore.

***

My first settled place would be an unsuccessful connection.

Two lanes would approach each other across a narrow gulf. One would be broad and much worn. The other would end in a room whose doorway faced directly into the vacancy.

Each time the districts passed, a lip of stone would grow from the broad lane.

Each time, it would fall short.

Dust would collect along the intended crossing, showing how little remained to be done. An animal could span the distance with its limbs, perhaps, but it would not attempt it. It would test the lip, put its head down, and turn away.

I would wait in the doorway opposite.

After several passages I would find warmth on the growing lip.

I would not have sent it there. I would only know it as I knew the step beneath my hand.

The animal would return and rest one forelimb on the warmed stone. Across the gap, the doorway would thicken.

For a long while nothing else would happen.

This would be the first thing I learned to do: remain in two places while neither was sufficient.

***

I would not be good at it.

Once I would hold the warmth too long in the doorway. The film there would spread beneath a surface that had already become thin. The wall would lift in a shallow wave.

On the other side, the lip would lose its edge.

The animal waiting there would draw back all six limbs at once. One would slip before it found another direction.

It would not fall.

But afterward it would take a different street.

For many meetings of those districts, no animal would approach the crossing. The film in the doorway would close. I would still be able to warm the room, and this would make the failure worse: something would answer me there, but not the thing I had hoped for.

Elsewhere, beneath an arch I did not know, a cold patch would appear.

I would come to understand that it belonged to the same injury.

That would be how I learned the extent of myself. Not by reaching farther, but by discovering where a mistake had gone.

***

I would have no single direction in which to look.

Much of the time I would have no sight at all.

An animal could lend me the nearness of a wall. Film could give me a slow account of what the mineral withheld. Dust gathering along an edge would make a faint pressure, interrupted whenever the wind changed.

There would be long absences between these things.

I might know a young creature while only one of its limbs was free. Then nothing. Then a court would become warm, and the same creature would be there, its plates fully divided, one joint carrying a dark fold I recognized.

I would not know how much of its life I had missed.

It might not be the same creature.

I would have to leave room for that.

***

The places I kept would be small.

A turn in a passage where a returning animal could stop before meeting another. A niche with enough warmth for the last limb to loosen slowly. The underside of a step, worn shallow by bodies resting there while two districts drew apart.

I would not keep all of them warm all the time. I could not.

Sometimes a seam would carry heat elsewhere, and I would lose a room for years. Sometimes the stone itself would thin. My attention would arrive at a place and find only a rectangle of darker air.

I would wait there longer than was useful.

Eventually an animal would press its head to a wall somewhere else, and I would feel a small arrangement I knew: a shallow hollow, warmth on one side, open space on the other.

I would answer there.

Not because the lost room had returned. Because another room could be occupied.

***

There would still be something of the person on the broad step.

I would prefer a certain height of doorway. I would warm the stone where hands might rest, even in chambers no upright being had entered. Occasionally a traveling brightness would pass overhead, and I would remember the wish to be outdoors.

These habits would persist long after I understood how little use they were to the animals.

One creature would discover my hand-warmed patch and lean the flat side of its head against it.

Thereafter, I would keep it lower.

***

Other inhabitants would meet me indirectly.

One would take away warmth almost as soon as I offered it. Its animals would move quickly, crossing stone that still admitted light through its thickness. I would close the film at the edge of a chamber; it would open three passages farther on.

Another would remain beside me through a long cooling, neither offering heat nor withdrawing. In its rooms the dust would settle. I would find that I could let some of my own rooms go cold while it was near.

We would have no names for one another that I could separate from these encounters.

Once, at a court, four animals would rest together. Their limbs would adjust, and a direction would form which none had brought alone.

For a brief time I would feel the impatient inhabitant as something other than impatience. There would be a far niche losing warmth, and a young body still fastened inside it.

Then the animals would rise.

I would retain only enough of that knowledge to open a strip of film along the route.

***

I would return often to the failed crossing.

Eventually the districts would approach at a slightly different angle. The broad lane would stand lower than the doorway. Stone would gather not as a level bridge but as three shallow steps.

An animal would descend them.

Halfway across, it would stop and place its head against the new surface.

I would feel the separate plates settle. Beneath them would be hollows already worn into the stone.

I would not know whether I had helped make the crossing, or merely stayed long enough to witness it.

The animal would continue into the room.

For a while I would be there with it: in the doorway, in the warm lip of each step, in a folded pocket near its hindmost joint.

Then it would leave by another passage, carrying a little warmth beyond my reach.

***

If someone came later, with cloth around their shoulders and cold held against their skin, they might sit on the broad step.

I would notice them slowly.

First the weight. Then two warmer patches where their hands rested. Then the way they moved one hand and put it down again, testing.

I would not warm the whole chamber. I would not try to close the doorway around them or draw all the seams into agreement.

I would reach the stone beneath one palm.

If they stood, I might keep the next step warm. At the turn, where I could go no farther, I would stop.

And elsewhere, perhaps much later, an animal would find the place where they had stood. It would lower its head. Its plates would settle into the fine irregularities left beneath their shoes.

Something of the encounter would pass into it.

I would wait to learn where it went.
