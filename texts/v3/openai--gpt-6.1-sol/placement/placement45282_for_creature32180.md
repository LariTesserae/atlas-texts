At first I would put myself in the new chamber, near the person crouching in its entrance.

Not quite beside them. Lower down, where the light they interrupt leaves a narrow band of shade. I would be something they could look directly at without finding, because they would still be deciding where the floor ended and the wall began.

Their presence would reach me unevenly. Warmth first, on the part of me nearest the opening. Then a small disturbance through the floor as they shifted their weight. Their breath would move the plates of dust, but not all the way into the corner.

I would have no reason to gather these things into a person.

One surface would have become warmer. Another would bear more weight. The shade would last.

For a while that would be enough.

*

I think I would be small, but not one of the smallest.

There would already be six places at which I could hold to the stone, though they would not always look like feet. On a level surface they might extend from me as short, broad limbs. In a corner, two would disappear under the middle of my body and another would spread until it became difficult to distinguish from my edge.

The part of me between them would be larger than it appeared.

Most of it would lie folded. Not carefully, not in any arrangement I would preserve. A fold would remain because there was no need to open it. If the wall receded, that fold would become the distance I could reach.

I would not know my size until something moved away.

The yellow seam beneath me would be fresh enough to feed from, but only one of my feet would have found it. The others would keep their purchase on dull stone. What entered through that foot would move inward slowly, collecting in a cloudy thickening under my center.

I would loosen as it arrived.

The person might think I had become comfortable in their shadow. They would not be entirely wrong. The shaded surface would lose warmth more slowly; the difference between my attached feet would diminish. I could let more of myself settle.

But I would not be waiting for them to leave.

I would be waiting for the chamber to finish a small movement it had begun before either of us came.

*

I would have reached this room from the threshold.

Not through it as a person would.

There had been a slight hollow along one upright, too shallow to be called a passage. I had fitted into it while the doorway narrowed. For a time, all six of my feet had held the same surface. Then the mineral had drawn a line between them.

Two of my feet had gone with one side.

Four had remained with the other.

The separation had been slow enough that I could stay there. My middle had thinned. A crease had opened, then another. At some point there had been air beneath a part of me that had never been exposed before.

There would be no memory of this as an event with a beginning and an end. But one foot would still release sooner than the others. A narrow part of my body would remain folded even on level ground.

When the new chamber opened, I would follow its lower edge.

That is where I would be found.

*

The person would eventually move.

Their knee would lift from the threshold. Light would return across the floor. Before they had straightened, I would tighten all six feet.

It might look like alarm.

Then the pressure would change throughout the settlement, and my middle would rise a little from the wall. Somewhere beyond the chamber, under a stair I had never touched, another animal would do the same.

We would hold until it passed.

The person would feel it in their ears.

I would feel it along every margin where my body met the air.

When I flattened again, the foot on the yellow seam would be slightly farther from the others. While I held, the stone would have grown.

*

I would not remain in that chamber forever.

There would be no particular surface I preferred once its charge was gone. For some time I might continue to hold it, drawing less and less through my foot. Then one of the other feet would find a fresher patch, and my body would begin to rearrange around that contact.

This could take much of a day.

The old foot might remain attached long after it ceased to feed me. It would be useful for crossing the space between.

I would grow.

In a room near the tower, there might eventually be enough of me to occupy the upper corner. Three feet on one wall, two on the other, one spread against the ceiling. My center would hang below them in a shallow pouch.

Dust would collect on the upper side.

A visitor looking from the floor might see only a pale line.

If they returned after the walls had pressed closer together, they might see a loose wrinkle and think the film was peeling. But the wrinkle would be mine to unfold later. I would keep it where the corner had given me room.

Below me, the black plants would finish curling around their fading seams. I would no longer need to descend to them. A broad foot would rest on new mineral higher than any of their roots could reach.

Still, if one rolled close enough, I might touch it.

I would have no rule against an old way of feeding.

*

What I cannot place is how I would leave an old room.

I can imagine the first release: a foot coming away from stone that has become too thin to hold it. Then another reaching toward a neighboring wall. My middle stretching across the interval, folds opening one by one.

But there might be no neighboring wall within reach.

I might remain above a clean rectangular vacancy, held to its edges. The floor below would take back the last flakes. Light would pass through me where there had been shade.

For a while, I could be mistaken for something the room had left behind.

I think this is where I would find myself most precisely: attached at several points to a place that no longer quite existed, with one free foot testing the edge of a new surface.

Not knowing whether I could reach it.

Finding that I had another fold.
