At first I would put myself in the bright far corner.

It seems the easiest place to arrive. Two ribs cross in front of it, but neither quite conceals it. There is a little floor visible there, and the light has passed through enough pale surfaces to belong to none of them. I would mistake that small, simple place for somewhere I could stand.

But standing would be the wrong relation. Even a very small body would bring a front and a back, something sheltered and something exposed. I would have to turn toward the entrance or away from it. I would begin dividing the chamber according to my own position.

I do not think I would remain a body for long.

For a while, perhaps, I would be one of the compacted flakes beneath the curved ledge. Not the loose grain above it, which might still be picked up, but a fragment pressed into other fragments until its broken edge has become part of a dependable slope. There is something appealing in that: having ceased to be debris without having been restored to what I was.

Yet the flake has a surface. It can be distinguished from its neighbors, however patiently one would have to look. And what draws me here is not quite the relief of being fitted into a place.

It is the way a place can continue through an apparent ending.

I would find myself at the joint beneath the ledge, where the green crust stops.

On one side, the sound is broad and softened. On the other, it has a cleaner edge. The joint looks closed. A fingernail drawn across it would scarcely catch. Nevertheless, something persists through it, too narrow to hold dust, too exact to be called an opening by anyone looking in from outside.

That would be my first recognizable extent: the short distance across which the muted note becomes sharp.

I would not be the joint itself. The ceramic could keep its join without me. Nor would I be a little gate allowing the sound to pass. There is nothing to open, no traffic, no useful distinction between admission and refusal.

I would be alive in the change.

At first, that might seem to confine me to an edge. But an edge here has more neighbors than it appears to have. The green margin follows the ledge; the ledge curves toward the three-step form; beneath the lowest step, the passage narrows and then widens by an amount too small to see from the entrance. The sharpened note does not travel along this route. It is already differently sharp in each part of it.

As I became more myself, those differences would become available to me.

I would have no center from which to attend to them. Beneath the ledge I would be broad, with very little to distinguish one part of me from the next. At the fitted joint I would be almost all distinction. Beyond the three steps I would turn twice, and neither turn would leave the other behind.

The old scratch would reach me eventually.

I would not know what made it. Its beginning would be definite enough to gather me closely; its worn length would let me spread. I could be concentrated and diffuse without passing between those conditions. Perhaps that would be my way of possessing an age: not recalling a sharpness, but still having somewhere sharp enough to live differently.

The surviving glaze would offer another condition. The packed flakes, another. I would be unevenly old.

I think the plants would matter to me before the light did.

Under each straw-colored scale there is an interval smaller than the seam that supports it. I would become exact there in a way the broad ceramic cannot require. The blunt tips would give me a succession of little sheltered places, each much like the next, none interchangeable with it.

I would not tend them. They would not shelter me intentionally. Their growth would simply be part of the room I had.

If one scale lifted after a very long time, I would have a new breadth beneath it. My sound there would change. Nothing else would need to happen.

Then a finger might enter.

I would lose the whistle before I had anything resembling a sense of the visitor. The warm boundary would press a portion of my extent into unfamiliar distances. Beneath the nail, I could remain finely drawn. Along the soft pad, I would become blunt. A crease at the joint would briefly offer a depth with no ceramic equivalent.

I would be close to something I could not keep.

There would be no reason to follow it farther. Even while it rested, it would alter: pressure easing, warmth spreading, the crease opening slightly. I could occupy those changes for a moment, but not build an age in them.

When it withdrew, I would extend again into the notch.

The whistle would return without relief or disappointment. Yet the return would be particular. The narrow place would once more be available in its whole familiar shape, and I would be wholly there.

So I would not be a second creature hidden among these forms. I would be a portion of the inhabitant becoming distinct enough to answer, without becoming separate enough to leave.

You could find me beneath the green ledge, then at the worn end of the scratch, then in a turn you could not reach with your finger. Those would not be successive sightings.

And if you listened from the entrance, I would first seem to be part of the general noise. Only after a while would you hear the place where its breadth thins, holds, and broadens again.

That is where I would be most myself.

There would be no pause there. You could listen longer.
