I would look first for the place beneath the scar.

Not inside the great animal, where everything moves too quickly, and not on the exposed crown of its armor. In the shallow depression behind the pale fan, there is a strip of space the outgoing water does not quite scour. The square-headed worm occupies the seam itself. But between its tube and the fused plates, a little water returns against the general current.

Something could live there without having to hold very hard.

At first I imagine a soft body, flattened against the armor, with no distinction between its back and its foot. Daylight would pass through most of it. The part pressed to the scar would be opaque, perhaps amber, from whatever it takes out of the old tissue and the water.

But a flat body would receive too much of the animal’s shaking at once. Every knock would come through its whole underside. There would be no way to tell the turning of an internal ring from the impact of a shell against the back.

So I think I would have separated points of attachment.

A handful of them. Small muscular cups joined by narrow lengths of flesh, each cup resting on a different plate. The lengths would not be pulled taut. They would rise a little into the water, pale loops no thicker than a fish spine. During a discharge they would lean together. Afterward they would slowly separate.

I would be easy to mistake for something caught there.

A shed feeding fringe. A tangle of pale tendons lifted from a dead animal.

Only after watching for a while would you see that the loops shorten independently, and that what looks like debris is choosing how much of the great animal’s motion to admit.

That is where I would find myself: on a living surface that cannot feel me distinctly, making a small body out of the differences between its movements.

***

I would have mouths, though they would not be the most noticeable parts.

Under each loop, a groove would open when the current slackened. Fine suspended matter would adhere there. The groove would close and move its catch toward one of the cups, where a little stomach lay against the armor.

I would eat some of the gray powder. I would also eat the softer matter carried in it, and the occasional scrap released from the pale fan. There would be mornings when almost nothing reached me. On those mornings my loops would become longer and thinner, extending beyond the shelter.

A fish might clip one away.

The remaining cups would draw closer together. For several days a blunt white bud would grow where the loop had been. I would not recognize the fish if it returned.

My attention would have other limits. Open water would scarcely exist for me. The distant pale peak would never become a place. Light would register as a change in the tenderness of exposed flesh; when a wave brightened the hollow, I would draw down slightly toward the armor.

But I would know the sealed outlet.

Water still moves behind a closed place. The fused plates would lift by an amount too small to see when an inner chamber filled. They would settle late, after the neighboring outlet had already discharged. One of my cups would lie on either side of that movement.

The delay would determine when I opened my feeding grooves.

I would live by something the animal had survived.

***

As I grew, I would not become a larger tangle.

Beyond a certain span, the loops would pull against one another. A knock useful to one cup would close the mouths beneath another. Food would take too long to pass through the narrow joining flesh.

A waist would form between two cups. It would become thinner during each discharge until, one afternoon, it parted.

Then there would be two of us beneath the scar.

For a little while we would still contract together. We would have the same lengths of flesh, the same accumulated tensions. Gradually one would creep nearer the worm’s tube. The other would remain behind the fan.

I think I would be the one that remained.

The tube is sheltered, but the fan makes an uneven boundary. It opens late sometimes. Sometimes a damaged hair folds into my feeding groove, and I must close around it, release it, try again. I would spend much of my life responding to this neighbor’s small inconveniences.

***

Before the slab moved, its trembling would reach me through the scarred animal’s feet.

I would have no sense of a slab. No image of the dark slot, no expectation of falling stone. There would only be an unfamiliar interval between two knocks, repeated often enough that my cups began to contract during it.

When the adults approached the shared pitch, I would pull my loops down.

The pale fan would close above me.

For a moment, our shelter would be complete.

Then the impact would travel through the great animal. Two of my cups would lose their hold. One loop would tear; another would stretch across the worm’s tube. I would lie partly exposed while white fragments passed overhead.

Afterward, the rhythm beneath the armor would return, but I would receive it differently. A cup that had rested on the fused plate would now cling nearer its edge. The old delay would no longer bring food reliably.

I would have to open at other times.

There would be no larger understanding in this. For hours I might feed badly. The fan would spread and withdraw, spread and withdraw. Eventually my grooves would begin opening just after its hairs bent outward.

***

Much later, a piece of me might leave the scar.

A small cup would grow at the tip of an exposed loop. It would have its own stomach and a few short filaments tucked against its rim. The joining flesh would narrow. A flank discharge would carry it away before the cup had learned to cling.

It might be eaten.

It might settle on cold stone and spend what remained of its body reaching for food that never came.

Or it might enter the narrow current above the youngster’s new seam.

There, beneath the transparent fish, it would touch a back still soft enough to yield. Its filaments would spread. The first turns of the young animal’s discs would pass through it separately, without the dense overlapping movements of the scarred adult.

One small mouth would open.

I would still be beneath the old fan, with a torn loop growing back. Down by the newly uncovered stone, something made from my flesh would begin to take a different shape.
