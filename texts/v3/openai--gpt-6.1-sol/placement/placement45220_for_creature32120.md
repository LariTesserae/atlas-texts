At first I would put myself just inside the broken wall.

Not in its center. Near one of the larger blocks, where I could sit without blocking the view from the step. I would want to see the creature come out, but I would not lean forward when it did. There is so little cover here that attention itself might feel like an approach.

I imagine hands resting on the clay. Then I find I have less use for hands than I expected. Nothing needs gathering. The loose stones do not need arranging. Even the small wet depression can remain a small wet depression, without my clearing a channel to it.

I would sit long enough for the warmth of the ground to become difficult to distinguish from my own.

The creature would notice me. Its head would turn; its feet would draw beneath it. I would stay still through its retreat and the long interval afterward. An eye would show at the recess. Perhaps it would come no farther that evening.

But I would still be there the next time.

Gradually, I think, I would find myself lower down. No longer beside the step, but in the place where its center has worn shallow.

I would be the step.

Not quite as it was when someone first set it there. I would be the broad, lowered part, the place a body finds easier to cross or settle into. My ends would remain a little higher. Fine clay would collect along my back edge, and underneath me there would be a darkness that never receives the gray light of the open sky.

I would have very little knowledge of the hollow all at once.

The gap at the far end would not be visible to me. I would know an arrival by its weight, if it reached me. The pool would announce itself only faintly through the ground, and most of its drops I would miss. Rain would be more legible: first separate touches, then a thin wetness spreading over my surface, then the clay beside me losing its firmness.

The creature would be the clearest thing.

One forefoot, then the other, placed beyond my edge. The belly settling. A hind foot moving once to find a better purchase. Its weight would come unevenly at first and then distribute itself across the hollow in me.

There would be warmth where it rested, although I was warm already.

I would feel the small movements that are almost invisible from across the floor: the quick breathing beneath its heavy back, the pressure of a jaw lowered onto the forefeet. Sometimes it would remain after the stone around us had begun to lose the day’s heat. Its underside would keep one part of me warmer than the rest.

I would not know whether it was watching the entrance or sleeping with its eyes open.

When it left suddenly, I would know that too. Four brief pressures, a scraping nail, the release of its weight. A little of its warmth would remain. Then that would go.

I think I would like being something it could leave without difficulty.

There would still be changes in me, though too slow for the creature to attend to. A grain loosening at an edge. Moisture finding a finer passage through the stone. Once, perhaps, a thin scale from its back would lodge in a rough place on my surface. It would stay through several visits before a wet foot carried it away.

The wall could diminish around me. Another block could settle outward. More clay could gather until crossing me scarcely meant going up or down at all.

Still, the creature might choose the same place.

Its body fits there. Beyond my edge, there is room for its feet. When it raises its head, nothing stands between it and the narrow way out.
