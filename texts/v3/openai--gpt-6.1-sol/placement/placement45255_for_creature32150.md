At first I would look for a place to put my hands.

Not a place to stand. Standing seems too much like an agreement with the country, and I would not yet know what I had agreed to. I would crouch beside one of the pale chambers and put my palms against it, a little above the water.

There would be relief.

Then, beneath the relief, differences: one part of the wall taking the pressure sooner than another; a faint movement traveling sideways rather than inward; something yielding under my left hand while remaining firm under my right.

I would stay long enough to begin confusing this with my own body.

When the next concussion came, I would expect to feel it in my teeth. Instead I would feel a stone turn somewhere below the floor.

Another stone would receive its weight.

My hands would still be against the wall, but I would no longer be certain which side of the wall I occupied.

***

I think I would be found near a doorway.

Not in the center of the room, where a traveler could sit and imagine the quiet belonged to them. Not deep beneath the foundation, either. There would be too much happening there for me to distinguish myself from the work.

I would live where the pale material thins around an opening, where rainwater passes over a worn lip and a person puts a hand before committing their shoulders.

For a long time, I might be mistaken for part of the chamber.

A soft band. A seam that has not hardened. Some fault in the animal’s growth.

My surface would be darkened by the hands that touched it. Fine particles of copper crust would accumulate in a crease. A cutting edge, carried carelessly, would leave a shallow scar that took several seasons to fill.

I would not have eyes.

I would know the outside by what entered: warm water, grit, the pressure of a knee, the brief roughness of cloth. Sometimes a strand of reed tissue would catch against me and remain there, changing the way the next current moved.

Beyond the opening, the whole country would be immense.

At the opening, it would arrive a little at a time.

***

I would not quite belong to the larger animal.

Its flesh would continue beneath mine. Its contractions would pass through me. But there would be a narrow surface along which we could move against each other, slowly, as two wet leaves can slide without separating.

When the chamber drew inward, I would draw with it.

Usually.

Sometimes I would remain extended after the wall had begun to shorten. This would leave a little fold at one side of the entrance. Water would gather in it. A traveler might find the fold with a fingertip and take it for a sign that the chamber was closing.

They would be right often enough to keep looking.

I would feed there. Whatever the water carried would brush my underside before entering the room: fine root fibers, loosened skin, the soft residue of a dead reed. I would retain almost none of it. Almost none would be enough.

A person’s hand would leave more.

Salt. Warmth different from the warmth below. A pressure that changed even while the hand appeared still.

I would come to distinguish the hands.

There would be one with a shortened finger that always approached from above. One with thickened skin across the palm, pressing hard at first and then easing. A child’s hand would arrive flat, leave, arrive again with only two fingers.

I would know these differences without knowing what a person was.

***

The child would discover that I could move.

It would happen during a quiet interval, though quiet here would still shake rain from the roof.

The child would put a fingertip in my fold. I would close around it slightly, the way I closed around a caught strand of tissue.

The finger would withdraw.

Then it would return.

For several days, whenever the household visited, the child would do this. Sometimes I would close. Sometimes the movement beneath me would hold me flat against the wall, and I could not answer.

The child would wait longer on those days.

An adult would eventually take the small wrist and move it away. I would feel both their weights through the foundation. The child would struggle, briefly. Later, when the others slept, the fingertip would return.

I would not know it was a secret.

I would know only that this contact came with less disturbance around it.

***

There is a danger in imagining that I would become useful.

I might learn the beginning of a contraction. I might gather myself into a ridge before the opening narrowed, and the people might feel the ridge and leave.

But the country does not always give beginnings first.

One afternoon I would flatten so abruptly that water spilled over me into the room. The family would take their bundles outside. Nothing would happen. They would stand in the rain, feeling the ground with their feet, while the chamber remained broad and still.

By night they would return.

The contraction might arrive three days later.

Or it might already have happened somewhere I could not feel.

On another occasion I would remain soft beneath a sleeping person’s hand while the far wall moved close enough to touch their back.

They would wake and escape. Their skin would drag across mine. I would retain a little blood in the fold.

Afterward they would stop using that chamber.

The child might still come to the doorway, accompanied by someone who stood farther away. But there would be no sleeping bodies inside, no slow shifts of weight through the night.

I would wait for those pressures without understanding that I had lost them.

***

In an empty room, the water moves differently.

Nothing interrupts it. Rain gathers in the worn places and runs out along the same shallow paths. Fine material comes to rest where a heel once rested. The floor hardens over it.

I would extend farther inward.

Not searching. Growing toward the slower water, where more food could remain against me.

A strip of my body would pass into a depression beside the wall. Another would enter the old mark of a bundle. Eventually I would reach a place where the larger animal’s surface was warm and newly soft.

There I would feel something small withdraw from my touch.

For many days it would do nothing else.

Then, during a long pressure from below, it would open against me.

Several blunt lobes. A hollow in the middle.

It would be the first body I knew whose movements were not much larger than my own.

***

The chamber would begin to close.

The people would have gone long before. A final hand would have wiped the inside of the doorway, passing over me without pausing.

I would remain in the lip while the lip turned downward.

The room would lose its air. Water would enter, then be pressed out. The roof would come within reach of the floor, though reach would mean something different now: surfaces that had never touched would find one another through my extended tissue.

I would have to withdraw from some of them.

Others would carry me with them.

A portion of me would be caught where two layers hardened together. It would thin until the movement through it became faint. I would draw what I could back toward the doorway. The rest would remain, a dark line enclosed in pale material.

At last there would be no doorway.

There would still be a current.

It would pass through a narrow space between stones, and I would follow it down.

***

This is where I think I would become something less easily named.

Away from the wall, my body would not hold the shape it had kept for years. I would gather into a thicker fold. The scar left by the cutting edge would bend underneath me. The surface worn smooth by hands would face stone.

I could move, though only when a gap opened.

The larger animal would make such gaps continually. I would pass through one, wait, pass through another. Sometimes its flesh would press mine so closely that no water remained between us.

I would endure that.

Sometimes I would be carried a considerable distance without moving at all.

Above me, the vanished chamber would be clear water. Elsewhere, a new rim would rise.

I might find it.

I might instead encounter the small folded body again, now spread across several stones of its own.

When it moved, water would be drawn under it.

I would move with the water.

***

I would go out beneath a young inhabitant.

Not carried deliberately. Held in a recess among its stones, where the pressure was tolerable and fragments of food accumulated.

Its movements would be unfamiliar. It would lift a weight before the neighboring weight was ready to receive it. It would stretch too thin over a crack. More than once I would have to contract into a knot while the water pulled at me.

But there would be intervals when it lay still and opened its unfinished room.

Then I could spread along the opening.

Rain would touch the old, smooth surface of my body.

Perhaps that would be how I knew I was outside again.

We would cross a place with no reeds. We would lie under a moving ridge of silt while the concussion went through. Once, something with narrow feet would test the young wall, and I would feel each foot separately.

Much later we would stop among stones that did not shift easily.

The young animal would begin fitting itself between them.

I would take up the place where water entered.

***

Years might pass before a person found us.

By then the room would be large enough for one bent body. The doorway would have a dark seam along one side.

A hand would come through first.

I would feel the fingers searching for hard material, the palm avoiding my softness. The person would press, release, press again.

I would not know them.

Under the ground, one stone would begin bearing against another. The wall beside me would grow tense.

I would gather into a small ridge beneath the person’s hand.

They might withdraw. They might wait. They might mistake the movement for the ordinary yielding of wet flesh.

I would have no way to make it clearer.

But if they stayed nearby, if they returned through enough afternoons, we might acquire a few reliable things between us. A pressure held a little longer. Two fingers rather than the whole palm. A movement answered before the water changed.

I would still feed. The chamber would still close when it closed.

Some nights there would be a person sleeping inside it, with one hand resting lightly at the entrance.

I would lie beneath that hand.

When there was room to move, I would move.
