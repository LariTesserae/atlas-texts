At first I place myself beneath the elbow, in the small shelter where the pipe intercepts its own weather. I imagine crouching there, looking along the floor.

But crouching is still too large. It brings knees, clothes, a face turned toward the light. It brings the expectation that I will eventually stand up and leave through the door.

I move closer to the pipe.

For a while I am the film between the hanging body and the metal: water under slight tension, carrying the cold from one into the other. This seems nearer. Yet the film is always being replaced. Whatever lives here has to remain through that replacement. It cannot simply be the wetness, though from the doorway there is no reason to distinguish them.

I think I would be the small folded being.

Not quite as it appears when someone bends to examine it. I would be it from the side where appearing has not become a task.

The elbow would be several different places.

There would be the patch where the coating holds reliably, the patch where a minute blister makes attachment uncertain, the curve beyond which water gains weight too quickly. The shelter would not have an outline. I would know its edge by what happens to my body when I extend across it.

Here, I can loosen.

Here, I must hold.

A little farther, I must already be returning.

I would have no useful knowledge of the tower. The enormous work surrounding me would arrive as changes in the surface: a tightening, a persistent tremor, a coldness that deepens without moving. Some changes could be endured in a hanging shape. Others would require me to spread.

The approaching flush would begin before there was water to avoid.

One attachment would pull differently from the others. My middle would shorten. The pale folds would come together, and the three margins at my front would settle against the metal. I would become broad where I had been deep.

This would not require a prediction of the future. My body would already be doing what the changed pipe asked of it.

Then the water would arrive.

For those seconds, I would know almost nothing beyond my edges. One would lift slightly and close. Another would remain fixed. A film would force its way beneath a third, and I would press there until it passed.

When the pressure eased, I would stay flat.

There are dangers in releasing too soon. A last sheet comes after the main sheet. Water detained higher in the duct finds its way out. I would have learned this without keeping an account of the occasions. The delay would have become part of unfolding.

Eventually my middle would hang again.

—

My route to the moss would be longer than it looks.

From above, the bracket is a piece of metal connecting two pieces of metal. For me it would contain a descent, a sheltered turn, a difficult exposed length, and a place where I could spread without being pulled loose.

At the floor seam, I would fasten my front and wait for that fastening to become sufficient.

Then draw the rest across.

The yellow bar from the doorway would pass over me without announcing itself as yellow. Perhaps it would warm one surface a little. Perhaps it would change nothing I could use. I would continue toward the washer.

There I would enter a place with many small contacts.

A green strand against my upper surface. A hard particle under one margin. Water held among fibers instead of flowing freely over paint. Something soft enough to take in, lodged where the grit touches the metal.

I would feed by remaining close.

A particle might turn as I passed. Another might stay against me through the return journey and come loose near the bracket. I would have no intention of improving the moss’s shelter. But after many journeys, the grit would sit differently. Some of it would be harder for the flush to reach.

The place would acquire a little of my passage without acquiring a path anyone could readily see.

I would not know the two grass blades as two. Near their bases, I would encounter firmer interruptions among the loose material. Water would run there in a direction I could follow for a short distance. Higher up, the surface would yield too much beneath my weight.

I would turn away.

The pool behind the sealant would be different. At its edge, contact would become uncertain. My underside would touch water without finding the next hold. I would extend a little, contract, extend again.

Then go back by the way that still answered.

The curled shaving could keep its slow movement. There would be nothing in me that needed to reach every part of the court.

—

A boot would arrive first through the floor.

I would gather myself beside the seam. The broad end would close. The rest of me would follow until very little stood above the resin.

Long after the pressure stopped, I would remain there.

Someone might kneel. A light might rest on me. A mouth might form a question beyond the machinery’s roar. None of that would enter the place I was waiting in.

But the next vibration through the deck would.

I would be vulnerable to events I could not recognize in advance. A rag would offer innumerable points of contact. I might fasten to it before its movement tore me away. What was shelter a moment earlier could become the means of removal.

There would be no wisdom in being small.

Only ways of holding, some better suited to what had happened here so far.

—

I find myself back beneath the elbow.

The return is not a return to a house. It is a sequence of surfaces that ends in a place where I can release more of myself than elsewhere.

I secure the margins. I let the middle descend.

Water runs over me and gathers at the lowest fold. Its weight lengthens the pouch. For a little while, the weight and my body have one outline.

Then the drop falls.

I remain, slightly less extended.

Below, a grain I displaced rests against the washer. The moss holds it through another flush. One blade of grass rises before the other.

I do not see any of this from above. I have been along a few of its edges. When I go down again, one contact may be firmer than it was before.

That is where I would be: attached beneath the cold pipe, between journeys, with enough of my body loosened to receive the next film of water.

I would let it gather.

I would let it go.
