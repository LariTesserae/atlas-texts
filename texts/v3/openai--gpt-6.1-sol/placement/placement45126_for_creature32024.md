At first I would be where the water hesitates.

Not in the seam, where each opening brings enough force to carry a body through, but behind the small projection at the darkest end. Dust arrives there sideways. It touches the floor, lifts, touches again. Some of it stays.

I would arrive with it, though I would still be able to leave.

For a while I would have a fringe of moving hairs and enough stored food to turn away from a surface that did not hold. The floor would offer no clear distinction between a place and something passing through. A grain turns under my weight. A membrane gives way. I settle on the edge of a chalk cup and the animal inside withdraws, changing the water around me.

I lift again.

What finally holds me is less than a ledge: a roughness on the side of the projection, beneath the two new chambers. My front presses against it. Water pulls at my rear. The contact persists through several strokes of the hairs, and then through several more.

I would not know that this is the darkest end. There would be a difference between one side of my body and the other, a weak direction in the blue, but no room gathered into a view. The crown opening above me would first be a change in the supply of particles. Its withdrawal would be another change. The wall beside me would be contact, then chemistry, then something my skin began to answer.

For a time I keep swimming without going anywhere.

The hairs draw a little food past my mouth. They also pull against the fastening. Their strokes become uneven, and the place where I touch the projection broadens.

There is no last stroke I could distinguish from the others. Some hairs stop before the rest. Water continues to move along the body after the body has ceased moving itself.

Inside, things shift.

The food I brought is used where there is not yet a mouth. A fold deepens. A small tract closes at one end and opens at another. Parts that served me in open water are drawn inward or broken down. Beneath my outer surface, the filaments of a crown lie close together.

I would be soft enough then for a grain to leave an impression.

One does. It lodges against my side during a heavier descent of brine, and the pressure remains after the current weakens. My new wall forms around it. Later, when the grain comes loose, there will be a shallow hollow there, an irregularity smaller than the opening through which I feed.

The two chambers above continue their work. Material passes through the strand joining them. Close to my fastening, a swelling extends toward me.

I cannot reach for it. There is no limb available for that, no movement that would shorten the distance. But the surfaces grow. A narrow contact forms, thickens, and becomes a connection through which nourishment can pass.

Then there are changes in me that do not begin here.

A touch at the upper chamber reaches my body as tension. My unfinished crown draws closer against itself. The tension eases. Nothing has touched my own wall.

Later, material arrives through the connection while my opening is closed.

I would remain this small body, with its own gut and its own crown. Food taken by a neighbor would not become an experience of feeding in me. Yet some of it could become my wall.

My first extension into the water would be incomplete. The crown opens crookedly because one filament remains caught against another. A particle travels toward the mouth, pauses, and slips away. Another sticks.

I withdraw with it.

The next time, the caught filament separates.

My opening faces the passage between the two chambers. The passage is not generous. A glassy animal can enter only with its legs gathered close, and its back sometimes brushes my rim. Each touch closes me. At first I remain withdrawn long after it has gone. Eventually I extend while its compact pellets are still descending nearby.

Feeding changes the water immediately outside my house. Not much: a curve in the path of a speck, a small clearance beside the rim. Dust settles a little farther away. Dust that had settled lifts.

Below me, the sheltered bank grows.

I would have no use for the word *street*. The passage would be known through what reaches the crown: food from one direction, waste from another, a repeated disturbance at nearly the same interval as the swimmer returns. Its width would matter when the currents changed.

During the next freezing, heavy water enters along the floor.

My lower filaments stop sorting well. I fold them in. The neighboring mouths close too, but not together. One continues working long enough to send nourishment through the strand after I have withdrawn.

The brine rises around the chamber.

I cannot climb. The worm beside me draws its exposed length upward, leaving the pore empty. My opening remains where it was made.

For a while, little passes through the crown or the connection. The soft door holds against the rim. Behind it, my body contracts once, then again.

When the seam eases, the first incoming water does not reach me directly. It passes the upper mouths, divides around the projection, and enters the narrow gap along my side. The salt lessens there before it lessens beneath me.

I open into that difference.

By then my wall has whitened. The hollow left by the grain is still visible. Under it, another small fold has begun, supplied through the strand.

I would be found low on the projection at the back of the room, neither quite on the floor nor safely above it: one builder in a young chamber, opening toward the passage.

Water still goes through. I take what I can from it.
