I would find myself beneath the low branch whose underside stays wet.

Not in the pool below it, though part of me would be there. Not on the branch, though a foot would hold its far side. My head would rest in the narrow interval between two leaves, where their edges turn away from one another without quite making an opening.

At first I would know the place by what touched me.

A bead of water against the lower lip. The slight pull of a pad adhering to stone. Something small moving along the crease behind a limb.

The heat would not reach me from elsewhere. There would be no cooler part of my body to which I could draw it, no reason to seek deeper shade. I would feel it most distinctly when I lifted my cheek from the branch: the wet surface left behind, the air against the newly exposed skin, almost the same warmth, but not the same touch.

I would put my cheek back.

For a while, that might be the whole of being there.

Then the branch would give a little movement.

Farther along it, beyond the leaves concealing my head, another body would be changing its hold. I would feel a pad release, then the quiet strain as it closed again. The movement would reach my jaw before any sound did.

I would loosen my own foot.

Not withdraw it. Only allow the branch enough room to turn.

Something would brush the underside of my throat.

I would lower my head until it stopped brushing and rested there instead.

***

I think I would be one of the long animals, but I would not know myself first by the outline of one.

I would know the parts that were occupied.

My mouth would be occupied by the bitter softness of a stem. My forefoot by a root that divided just beyond my reach. Along my left side, three narrow channels of water would hold small moving bodies. One would travel against the direction of the others. I would feel it pause where the crease narrowed, turn, and go back.

There would be more of me beyond what I needed to attend to.

A foot beneath the belly. Another farther back, folded flat and unused. A length of tail lying among vines so closely matched to them that a small climber would cross it without hesitation.

When the climber reached a tender place, I would shift.

It would wait.

I would settle, and it would continue.

My body would become evident to me in these small requirements. A shoulder would have to rise to let a youngster through. A flank would have to bend because the root ahead curved toward the water. My mouth would spread because the surface against it was broad.

I would not count my limbs. But if one held nothing, I would know.

***

There would be a place beneath three roots that I returned to.

I would recognize it before seeing it. The water there would touch both sides of my muzzle at once, and the stem beside it would have a bitter taste that remained after swallowing. Something in the stone would yield under my forefoot without letting it sink.

I would place the foot slightly wrong.

Lift it.

Place it farther inward.

The fit would please me enough that I would stop moving.

Beneath my chest, a younger animal would also stop. Its mouth would be too pale, its joints more readily felt when it pressed against me. It would have followed me from the branch, though there would have been other bodies to shelter beneath along the way.

I would bend my head to touch its side.

For a moment, all its feet would become still.

Then its jaw would resume its faint sideways movement against the mineral rim.

I would have no wish to keep it there. I would move carefully while it was there.

***

A call would come through the water.

I would feel the first part in the soft corners of my mouth: a disturbance without a direction. Then the rounded pulses would arrive, and I would know the basin from which they came.

My flank would tighten.

The little swimmers would leave its creases.

I would answer before lifting my head.

The sound would pass through me low down, shifting the water beneath my belly. One of the leaves against my back would knock its stem. The youngster would press closer, not startled, simply making room for the movement.

Another answer would come from under the roots.

I would turn one eye toward it.

There would be a cheek there, dark and wet, belonging to an animal I knew. It would rest against my shoulder briefly. The pressure would be distinct from the branch, the root, the youngster. I would hold still until it moved away.

Nothing more would be required.

***

I would feed at the furled shoot.

Its tip would enter my mouth just far enough to meet the soft roof. Sap would gather there. I would swallow, and feel the stem press upward again.

Beyond my muzzle, a shelled creature would work along the pale rim. I would know its nearness through the minute interruptions of its scraping. Sometimes I would take such a creature. This one would pass beside my mouth and continue beneath the root.

A call would make me lift my head.

Water would draw into threads beneath my chin. The shoot would stand free. I would listen, with one foot holding the root and another partly open in the water.

For those few breaths I would feel the space my mouth had occupied.

Then I would lower my head again.

That is where I would be: long enough to lie through several small shelters at once, with a young body beneath me and other lives moving in the folds of my skin. My foot would close a little more securely around the root. My mouth would find the stem without looking.

And the foot holding nothing would remain open.
