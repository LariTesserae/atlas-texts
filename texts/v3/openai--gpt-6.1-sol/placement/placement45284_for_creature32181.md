At first I would place myself in the hollow beneath the old spur, where warmth gathers against the underside of a terrace.

There would be room to turn, though not to stand. The roof would be ribbed with roots, some dry and flattened, some pale with water moving inside them. A little daylight would enter sideways. When the hillside shifted, that light would narrow to a thread, disappear, and return at another opening.

I imagine staying there long enough to learn which surfaces are safe to lean against.

But the hollow would not stay. Its warmth would move into the wall. The roof would lower, and the space I had learned would become a pressure along my back. I would have to leave before I understood what was happening.

That is where I begin to acquire a body.

It would be a body that could leave through the wall.

Small holds, taken in sequence. A soft underside finding purchase beneath the layer that is beginning to slide. No need to see the whole passage. I would know enough from the resistance at the ends of my limbs: this stem is moving; this one is anchored farther down; this wet fibre will pull free if I put my weight on it.

Above me, the hollow would finish closing.

I would carry a little of its warmth away.

***

I think I would be one of the narrow animals, though not the one first glimpsed at the pool.

Mine would have a bare patch along one side. Something had pulled away there—a sheet of growth sliding in a different direction from the layer I held. The wound itself would be healed. In its place, shallow new pockets would shine among the older ones.

The plants around the patch would lean into it. A trailing stem would keep trying to root there, and I would keep it moist without quite allowing it to settle. Each time I bent, the little roots would lift from my skin.

This would affect where I went. On a cold ridge I would turn the covered flank toward the wind. In a warm seam I would press the bare patch against the wall. I would be less neatly hidden than the others. Sometimes a strip of ivory would show between the leaves.

I would not know this as damage to an appearance. I would know that one side cooled sooner.

There would be a feeding place I returned to too often.

It would lie beneath a shallow basin, at a junction where amber fluid slowed before dividing. My mouth would fit the swelling easily. I could feed there with most of my body supported, and the plants on my back could reach a seam of afternoon light.

For a while, all my needs would coincide in that small place.

Then the junction would descend.

At first I could still reach it by going beneath an older layer. Later I would have to pass a constriction that rubbed the plants from my shoulders. The light would be gone. The fluid would arrive less often.

I would return anyway.

There would be other swellings close by, full and accessible. My feelers would touch them, and I would move on. I would search the packed fibres around the missing junction, finding fragments of its former shape: a softened bend, a membrane emptied and folded, a few hard grains embedded in a wall.

Perhaps this is where I would most clearly be myself. In the repeated journey that had stopped being useful.

Eventually a contraction would close the last approach. I would brace against it for some time, then release my holds one after another.

***

I would find the others while following warmth.

Their leaves would touch mine before our bodies met. A long stem from an animal deeper in the gathering would lie across my bare flank. I would turn to let it pass, and its tip would catch in one of the new pockets.

Nothing would happen at first.

We would remain together while the wall slowly warmed. Moisture would collect between us. By the time the other animal moved away, fine roots would have spread across the pocket.

The stem would part at a joint.

For several days I would carry it folded close. Then, in a sheltered bowl, it would open two leaves broader and darker than the rest of my covering.

They would be poor leaves for the crest. Frost would gather along their margins. I would spend more time below the ridge, moving through loose columns where the air was still.

A small gift would change my route.

I would acquire other things this way: a pale grain carried in my mouth until a membrane accepted it; a scent of softened tissue along my underside; once, a young animal holding on beneath me.

I would feel the young mostly when I stopped. While I moved, its limbs would lie closely among mine. At rest, it would shift toward the warmer part of my body and fasten there.

I would have to open my holds more carefully. I would take wider seams.

At a gathering, another body would touch it. For a while the young would hold us both. Then I would move, and it would stay.

Afterward, a few of my limbs would continue lifting farther than they needed to.

***

I would live below the places from which the country looks settled.

The broad spur would be overhead, usually beyond sight. I would encounter its persistence through recurrent shelter: a warm wall after a difficult passage, an old layer firm enough to hold while the newer ones went past.

My circuit would lie around its base. It would include the lost feeding junction for a while, then cease to include it. There would be a place where I could spread the two dark leaves, and another where cold water passed close enough to wet my covering without entering my mouth.

I would sometimes wait through several pulses before moving.

A discharge would darken my feelers. Pressure would gather in a root beside me. Farther away, something would release.

I would have no view of the miles of vegetation folding flat. I would feel an opening widen against my underside, and enter it while it was there.

With age, I might become slower about releasing a hold. A root from my back would enter the ground and remain until the next movement tugged at me. I would loosen it, draw it free, settle elsewhere.

One day I might leave it attached longer.

But I do not think I would begin as a creature already inclined toward that ending. I would begin hungry, with one cold flank and a troublesome attachment to a place that had moved beyond my reach. I would keep finding passages. The dark leaves would open when there was light.

And when the ground tightened around a gathering, I would feel the others begin to leave.

For a moment their limbs would pass over mine. Then I would turn into the remaining seam, carrying whatever had taken root.
