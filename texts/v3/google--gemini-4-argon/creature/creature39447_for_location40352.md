To understand what lives here, one must first understand the city’s central paradox: the machinery is not processing the animals, nor is it manufacturing goods for an absent master. The machinery and the biomass have been locked in a closed loop for so long that they have become organs of the same vast, sedentary body. The vats render the dead into tallow and mineral slurry; the tallow lubricates the great bronze bearings and burns in the low-oxygen furnaces to keep the steam pressure up; the excess nutrient-broth is pumped out into the viaducts to feed the living, who grow fat, die, and slump back into the collection sluices. 

Within this tireless, warm digestion, life has sorted itself into distinct strata, and at the murky intersections of these strata, something resembling humanity still persists.

### The Filter-Castes of the Viaducts

The birds on the rooflines—the **Sluice-Maws**—are only the most visible tier of the beast-ecology. Up close, they are less like gulls and more like feathered, warm-blooded amphibians. Flight is largely vestigial for the adults; their breastbones have flattened out to allow them to lie flush against the warm brick of the flues, absorbing heat directly through brood-patches of thick, leathery skin. Their wings are heavy, oil-soaked mantles used mostly to tent over their young against the endless drizzle, or to bash rivals aside when a newly opened valve releases a fresh bloom of warm tallow-scum down a nearby aqueduct. 

They do not build nests. They lay soft-shelled, bolster-shaped eggs directly against the warm iron of the steam-pipes, cementing them in place with a mixture of regurgitated lime and guano. 

Beneath the birds, in the flooded, knee-deep streets, the **Slag-Crabs** do the heavy structural work of the ecosystem. Their shells are not entirely their own; they secrete a mucus that binds ambient iron filings, coal-dust, and crushed bone into their carapaces, making them look like walking cobbles. They have no natural predators because they are mostly mineral. Instead, their lifecycle ends when their shells grow too heavy to carry. An ancient crab simply settles into a pothole or a gap in a collapsing retaining wall, draws its legs in, and calcifies into the pavement, becoming part of the street itself while its soft interior rots out to feed the blind salamanders.

Deep inside the cisterns and the great, warm settling tanks live the **Baffle-Worms** and the **Lard-Bergs**—colonial organisms composed of millions of pale, blind polyps that anchor themselves to the brick walls where the nutrient currents are fastest. They grow into massive, trembling curtains of translucent flesh that act as living water-filters, catching the heavy particulates and allowing the clarified, warm water to return to the subterranean boilers without fouling the intakes.

### The Greasers (The Cinder-Men)

There *are* bipeds here, though you might look directly at a street-corner for twenty minutes before realizing that one of the wet, rounded shapes hunched against a steam-vent is a person.

They are small, barrel-chested, and tremendously heavy for their height, built with the same low center of gravity as everything else on the plateau. Their skin is pale and thick, cured by a lifetime in the tallow-mist to the texture of wet chamois leather, which makes them largely impervious to water-rot and the mild sulfuric acidity of the rain. 

A Greaser’s clothing is an accumulated carapace. The base layer is woven from the shed primary feathers of the Sluice-Maws, quilted together with copper wire stripped from defunct telegraph lines and boiled in heavy paraffin until it is as stiff and waterproof as tarpaulin. Over their shoulders they wear mantles of overlapped crab-shell to shed the constant, heavy plops of corrosive guano from the heights above. On their feet, they strap wide, rocker-bottomed clogs carved from seasoned ironwood or cast from porous slag, grooved on the undersides like tire treads to grip the treacherous, biofilm-slicked cobbles.

They do not walk with a human stride; they move in a slow, rolling shuffle, never lifting a foot entirely clear of the ground, nudging the carpet of crabs and salamanders aside with the blunt toes of their clogs rather than stepping over them. To move fast on the plateau is to slip, break a bone, and be covered in crabs before the next ten-second thud of the floor-pumps.

### The Work of the Day

The Greasers are not the engineers of the city; they are its symbiotes, acting like cleaner-fish on the flanks of a whale. They have no history of how the plateau was built, nor any concept that an "outside" exists beyond the white haze. To them, the world is an infinite engine that must be kept from seizing up.

They live in small, extended kin-groups called **Gangs**, each claiming a territory of three or four square blocks centered around a high-pressure steam manifold or a primary rendering vat. Their homes are not houses, which would flood or slump, but **Inspection Vaults**—hollowed-out spaces inside the massive, forty-foot-thick piers of the viaducts, or suspended hammocks of chain-link and leather hung directly beneath the belly of a slow-moving gantry crane, following its thirty-hour crawl across the district and back.

Their lives revolve around the **Clearance**. 

Because the biology of the plateau is so aggressive, the machinery is constantly in danger of being choked by the very life it feeds. A Gang spends its waking hours with long-handled iron spuds, bronze scrapers, and grease-Buckets. They chip away the calcified crab-tombs that threaten to derail the automated slag-carts. They climb the slick iron ladders into the whiteout of the roofline mist, pushing through the densely packed, snapping birds to clear the pressure-relief valves of feathers and bones. When a giant Baffle-Worm colony grows too thick in a subterranean pipe, changing the pitch of the floor’s *thrum-thud* to a strained, wheezing knock, the Greasers strip naked, coat themselves in foul-smelling axle-grease to ward off parasites, and lower themselves into the warm, dark water with long saws to harvest the excess flesh.

This is not done out of civic duty. It is how they eat and stay warm. Clearing a valve rewards them with the richest, purest tallow-curd before it hits the dirty sluices. Sawing back a filter-colony yields tons of pale, gelatinous meat that can be smoked over a coal-flue. Keeping a steam-hammer lubricated ensures that the radiant heat of its friction continues to warm the masonry of their sleeping vaults.

### The Culture of the Hum

Because the ambient roar of the city—the *thrum-thud* of the deep pumps and the endless, rasping click-and-croak of the fauna—never drops below eighty decibels, the Greasers rarely use their voices for complex speech. Their vocal cords are used mainly for sharp, honking warning cries that mimic the alarm-calls of the Sluice-Maws, cutting through the white noise when a masonry wall is about to slump or a ladle of molten silicate is passing overhead.

For actual conversation, they use **Pipe-Talk** and **Tapping**. 

Every Greaser carries a short, heavy bronze wrench or a hardened iron rod tucked into their belt. By striking the nearest iron tie-rod, steam-pipe, or rail-track in intricate, syncopated rhythms, they can send vibrations through the stone and metal that can be felt through the soles of clogs or the palms of hands three blocks away. In the quiet of their vaults, they speak by resting their fingers against one another’s collarbones or jawlines, tapping out a rapid, percussive language against the bone while humming low, resonant chest-notes that harmonize with the ten-second pulse of the floor.

To a Greaser, silence is synonymous with death. If a district’s pumps fail, the heat dies within days. The warm rain turns cold; the tallow solidifies in the viaducts like grey wax; the birds abandon the parapets; the crabs go dormant; and the damp chill creeps into the lungs of the Gang. Therefore, their religion, their art, and their medicine are all forms of **Tuning**. 

An elder among them—a **Listener**—can press an ear-horn made of a hollowed crab-claw against a basalt retaining wall and diagnose the health of the city for five miles around. They know the personal voice of every turbine, piston, and relief-valve in their watershed. When a Greaser grows old and their joints stiffen from the damp, they are brought to the **Harmonic Floors**—chambers directly above the greatest subterranean balance-wheels, where the vibration is so intense it shakes the phlegm from the lungs and numbs the ache in the bones. 

And when they finally die, there are no graves dug into the solid stone. They are carried up the trough-like stairs to the highest catwalks, washed in the warm, greasy mist of a cooling tower, and slipped quietly into a primary rendering vat—returning their iron, their lime, and their fat to the slow, dark heart of the plateau, so the pressure never drops, and the hammers never cease their stroke.
