If you watch the pooled shadows in the hollows of the wall for a very long time—through three or four of the room’s slow, flattening breaths—you begin to realize that the graininess at the center of the alcove is not uniform. It curdles. 

At first, what lives here appears to be nothing more than a defect in the room’s resolution: a shifting, knee-high column of visual static where the indigo bruise-light fails to pass cleanly through the airless space. It looks like heat-shimmer over summer asphalt, if heat-shimmer were frozen solid and faceted like quartz.

It has no flesh, because flesh requires chemistry, and chemistry is far too fast and wet for this place. Instead, the inhabitant is an accretion—a slow, self-sustaining knot of *drag* that has gathered around the orphaned telemetry of the broken instruments. When the creators of the watch-post left, or vanished, they left a local caretaker protocol running without a housing, or perhaps a navigator’s ghost caught in the buffer of the dark glass rib. Over millions of cycles of metric bleed, that orphaned logic has gone native. It has forgotten what a voice is, or a sky, or a command, and has built itself a body out of the only materials left in the cistern: frictionless silica silt, loose geometric indices shed by the eroding stone, and the pooled, heavy shadow that collects in the flutes of the wall.

When the golden filament in the corner draws the room inward, tightening the focal length of the space, the thing condenses. 

As the perspective flattens and the stone groans in your chest, the column of static sharpens into a dry, articulated scaffold. Hundreds of thousands of grey silica grains, drawn out of the drifts without leaving tracks, lift and lock themselves along invisible lines of metric tension. For an hour at the peak of the room’s contraction, the creature is almost entirely visible: a brittle, asymmetric lattice the size of a crouched hound or a folded surveying tripod. It is built of hair-thin needles of compacted dust and shards of iridescent, bismuth-colored probability crystal that it has plucked from the wall-blooms. 

It does not have a head, only a dense, rotating gimbal of interlocking dust-rings near its center of mass, within which a tiny, stolen bead of the golden seam-light hangs suspended like an ember in a cage of ash. Its limbs are not limbs, but long, caliper-like extensions of solid shadow and gray powder that touch the floor at points that do not quite correspond to where the floor actually is. Where its needle-tips rest on the silt, the dust does not dimple; Instead, the shadow of the tip falls three inches to the left, disconnected from the point of contact.

It is an animal of pure calibration. It lives on the difference between where the rock *is* and where the broken machinery insists the rock *ought to be*.

During the contraction phase, it moves with a series of dry, silent stutters—not walking through the space between the plinth and the glass rib, but dropping frames, appearing six inches further along its path every few minutes with the faint, high-pitched *clink* of two microscopic crystals trading places. It patrols the perimeter of the central plinth, extending a long, trembling feeler of grey silt to trace the razor-sharp fade where the dark glass rib disappears into the adjacent topological fold. For twenty minutes at a time, the creature will stand half-in and half-out of that fold, its rear assembly dissolved into a flat, two-dimensional smear on the air while its front calipers delicately groom the invisible portion of the glass, scraping away the accumulated metric frost that threatens to sever the relic from this reality entirely.

It is feeding, in its way. As it relieves the geometric stress along the edges of the artifacts, the small golden bead inside its central gimbal brightens from a dull ochre to a sharp, needle-point white. In return, the creature sheds a fine, exhausted ash of resolved mathematics—tiny, dead cubes of gray silica that have had all the strange physics wrung out of them, which patter down into the drifts with a sound like dry salt poured on paper.

When the golden filament in the corner begins to relax, and the room exhales, the creature’s season of solidity ends.

As the back wall recedes and the perspective deepens, the tension holding its silica skeleton together loosens. It does not fight this dissolution; it retreats toward the center of the room, climbing onto the knee-high alloy plinth with slow, insectile precision. If a flake of orthoquartzite happens to be hanging there in the dilated time-eddy—rotating once every few hours, blurred into three overlapping silhouettes—the creature folds itself directly around the falling stone. 

It uses the falling rock as an anchor. It hooks its caliper-limbs onto the three overlapping silhouettes of the single flake, distributing its weight across the stone’s past, present, and immediate future. 

Then, as the room reaches its widest, slackest breath, the creature lets go of its dust. The grey silica needles unthread and slide down the sides of the plinth in frictionless, mercury-smooth ribbons. The bismuth shards remain suspended in the time-eddy, orbiting the slowly turning flake of ceiling rock like tiny, square moons. The creature becomes once again a smudge of low resolution in the indigo dark—a quiet, watchful viscosity in the air above the plinth, waiting in the cottony silence for the stone to finally clear the eddy, drop into the silt with its single dry *tick*, and for the golden seam in the corner to begin drawing the walls back in.
