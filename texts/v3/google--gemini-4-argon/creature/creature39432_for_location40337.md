To find what rules the middle strata of the Sump—above the blind, boiling conduits of the eels, yet beneath the erratic skies of the leather-kites—you cannot look for shapes. The eye is useless here if it only looks for edges. In the flat, shadowless glare of the white sky, where a wall of black smart-matter is constantly breaking its own outline into a thousand sliding hexagons, a still silhouette is an evolutionary mistake. You have to look for a discrepancy in the rhythm. 

Watch a ten-story baffle-wall as it prepares to unlock and fold into a drainage trench. The ceramic-alloy plates begin to chatter, vibrating at a specific frequency to shake loose the rusty buildup of biofilm before they submerge. Thecarpet of wire-shelled crabs scatters downward, pouring like gravel into the widening seams. 

Right there, straddling a fissure that is opening at thirty miles an hour, something does not fall in, and does not scatter. It *rolls* with the stone.

At first, it looks like a tangle of heavy, grease-slicked industrial hosing that has been caught in the gears and somehow refuses to be crushed. It has the dull, plum-colored sheen of oxidized copper and wet vulcanized rubber. As the wall drops out from beneath it, the creature does not jump; it releases a series of pneumatic locks along its underside with a sharp, wet *pop-pop-pop* that mimics the sound of the city’s own pressure-relief valves, and drops into a controlled, fluid slide down the collapsing slope.

This is a **Gasket-Hound**, though it bears no relation to anything that ever barked or hunted through grass. It is a heavy, low-slung litho-mammal—or what became of mammal-stock after ten thousand generations of living inside a hot, wet calculus equation. It weighs perhaps four hundred pounds, most of that density concentrated in a sprawling, multi-jointed pelvic and shoulder girdle that allows it to flatten itself like a skink against a sheer vertical heat-sink, or bunch up into a dense, shock-absorbing sphere when a street unexpectedly turns itself inside out.

It has no fur; fur would trap the scalding condensation and cook the animal in its own coat. Instead, its hide is a thick, segmented mantle of heavily calloused, weeping skin that secretes a slick, silicone-like lipid. Water and boiling mineral-grease bead up and roll off it instantly. Across its back and flanks, this rubbery hide is pleated into deep, accordion-like gills—not for breathing water, though it can hold its breath for twenty minutes when a sluice-alley flash-floods, but for shedding heat. As the Hound moves through the laundry-room air, these fleshy radiator fins flare and pulse, flushed dark purple with blood that uses copper-hemocyanin alongside iron to deal with the bizarre gas mixtures belched up from the deep logic-cores.

It has six limbs, though the middle pair are not for walking. They are heavy, club-like outriggers tipped with pads of porous, magnetorheological cartilage. When the Hound needs to anchor itself to a trembling ziggurat while a sixty-mile-an-hour gale of exhaust steam blows past, it slams these middle limbs against the wet stone. An electrical impulse from its spine stiffens the cartilage instantly, while muscular diaphragms within the pads haul upward, creating a vacuum seal on the slick surface so powerful that the stone itself will flake before the Hound is torn loose. Its fore and hind legs are bowed, digitigrade, and disturbingly flexible, ending in four-toed cleft hooves made of a self-sharpening, iron-infused keratin. They make a dull, heavy *thwack-clack* against the smart-matter, a sound completely swallowed by the ambient roar of the Aggregate.

Look at its head, and you see the true cost of living inside an engine. There are no ears—any delicate tympanic membrane would be shredded in seconds by the megavolt air-gap *cracks* and the sub-audible bass of the shifting foundations. The skull is a flat, wedge-shaped prow of dense bone, covered in a mosaic of electro-receptive pits and lateral-line grooves similar to a shark’s, filled with a clear, conductive jelly. The Hound does not hear the city; it *reads* the city’s nervous system. It feels the spike in electromagnetic resistance three seconds before a field of intake valves snaps shut. It tastes the subtle change in water ionization that means a buried logic-core is about to dump ten thousand gallons of near-boiling coolant into the alley it is currently standing in. 

Its eyes are small, set back behind heavy, translucent baffles of cartilage that act like welding goggles against the blinding white diffuser of the sky and the sudden, violet arcs of static discharge. It doesn't need them much. Its world is mapped in pressure gradients, thermal contours, and magnetic hums.

A Gasket-Hound never sleeps—not the way things from the old world slept. To go limp and unconscious in the Sump for even ten minutes is to be folded into a wall and ground into paste. Instead, they practice unihemispheric rest, much like dolphins once did, though here it is not to keep from drowning, but to keep from being crushed. You can see a pack of four or five of them "sleeping" on the stepped face of a radiator tower. Half their brains are dead to the world, their radiator-pleats pulsing slowly, while the other half keeps their bodies moving in an eerie, somnambulistic ballet. As the tiles beneath them unlock and slide sideways, the resting Hounds step over the moving seams in their sleep, shifting their weight, releasing and resetting their suction-outriggers in perfect, mindless synchronization with the grinding stone. They look like drops of mercury rolling in place on a tilting tray.

They are apex predators of the shifting middle, and their hunting is a matter of pure physics. 

When the waking half of a Hound’s brain senses a localized surge of computational load deep beneath the floor-plates—a warmth that will inevitably bring a bloom of rust-slime, followed by a dense carpet of copper-crabs, followed by the pale, six-foot conduit-eels wriggling up through the grate-seams to gorge on the crabs—the pack wakes fully. They do not bark or howl. They communicate by slapping their vacuum-pads against the structural ribbing of the city, sending sharp, coded hydraulic shockwaves through the solid metal that their packmates can feel through their hooves half a mile away, cutting right through the airborne noise.

They move through the labyrinth like water through a baffle. Where a human would be trapped in seconds by a dead-end alley that suddenly seals itself shut, the Hounds use the city’s kinetic violence as a catapult. A Hound will sprint straight at a rising wall of smart-matter, slam its magnetic-suction pads onto the ascending stone, and let the thirty-ton block hoist it forty feet into the air. At the apex of the stone’s rise, just as it locks into a new heat-sink configuration with a deafening boom, the Hound releases its vacuum seal, kicking off the slick surface to sail across a boiling gap, landing squarely on the back of a folding intake valve on the other side.

When they corner an eel—or a heavy, slow-moving *Slag-Tortoise* whose shell has grown too thick with accreted minerals to get out of a folding trench in time—the Hounds do not use teeth to tear. Their jaws are short, wide, and hinged like bolt-cutters, lined with flat, overlapping plates of tungsten-hardened enamel. They pin the prey with their vacuum-outriggers and simply shear through copper-threaded hide and calcified shell with the slow, inexorable leverage of a hydraulic press.

Yet, if you watch them long enough through the steaming haze, you realize there is something else in the Sump. Something that watches the Hounds.

Follow the forests of fiber-optic cabling—those tightly coiled, black-sheathed trunks as thick as ancient oaks that writhe and tighten whenever the data-load spikes beneath the basin. High up, where the cables cross over one another in rubbing, groaning canopies just beneath the lowest eddies of the silver-white mist, the grease and mineral-water collects in the crotches of the wires.

Up there live the **Spool-Weavers**. 

If the Gasket-Hounds are the city’s heavy machinery given flesh, the Weavers are its stray voltage. They are thin, radially symmetrical creatures that look uncomfortably like a cross between a brittle-star and a bundle of stripped, wet nerves. A Weaver’s central body is no larger than a dinner plate, flat and flexible as a wet rag, translucent gray and pulsing with a faint, bioluminescent yellow light that flickers at the exact hertz-rate of the fiber-optic trunks it clings to. From this central disc radiate nine long, whip-like limbs, each ten feet long, boneless, composed of stacked muscular hydrostats sheathed in a rough, silica-spiked skin that can grip slick, vibrating glass-fiber like sandpaper.

They are the ultimate parasites of the Aggregate’s data-architecture. A Weaver does not hunt meat, nor does it scrape biofilm. It feeds on induction. 

In the high canopies of the cable-forests, the electromagnetic fields are so dense they make the air taste like pennies. The Weavers drape themselves across the thickest, hottest trunks, weaving their nine limbs into complex, recursive knots with the limbs of dozens of other Weavers, forming living, glistening nets that span the gaps between the writhing cables. Inside their long limbs run cores of biological superconductors—chains of heavy-metal proteins synthesized from the mineral-rich mist. As the city’s vast, incomprehensible equations surge through the fiber-optics and the adjacent high-tension power conduits, the shifting magnetic fields induce an electrical current directly into the Weavers' knotted limbs. They literally drink the stray amperage of the city's thoughts, using the raw electrical energy to drive their metabolism, reducing the heavy metals dissolved in the air-sweat into new tissue.

Because they are plugged directly into the magnetic induction of the logic-cores, the Weavers *know* what the Sump is going to do before the Sump does it. 

Look up at a canopy of cables just before a major tectonic remodeling—one of those rare, catastrophic shifts where five square miles of ziggurats and sluice-alleys completely liquefy and trade places to prevent a thermal runaway deep in the crust. Five minutes before the first hydrostatic bearing groans, before even the Gasket-Hounds feel the pre-shift static in their snout-pits, the Weaver-nets go dark. Their yellow bioluminescence cuts out in a synchronized wave across miles of sky. 

They unknot themselves with blinding speed, looking like a film of growing roots played in fast-forward reverse. And then, they *sing*. 

It is the only biological sound in the Sump that can pierce the omnidirectional roar of the engine. Because they have no lungs, they make the sound by rubbing their silica-spiked limbs against the taut, vibrating sheaths of the fiber-optic cables, using the massive trunks as violin strings and the hollow ziggurats below as resonating chambers. It is a shrill, harmonic, glass-shattering whine that rises in pitch until it hits the exact resonant frequency of the water droplets hanging in the air. 

When the Weavers sing, the mist itself shivers. The standing geometric patterns in the puddles below dissolve into chaos. It is not a warning for the other creatures, though the leather-kites immediately fold their wings and drop into the leeward trenches, and the Gasket-Hounds instantly abandon their hunts to lock themselves into tight, five-animal spheres in the safest pressure-shadows. 

The Weavers are not warning the fauna. They are *tuning* the cables. By vibrating the massive trunks at a specific harmonic frequency, these fragile, nerve-like colonies slightly alter the thermal expansion of the sheathing, tricking the city’s local sensors into believing those specific cables are fractionally hotter than they actually are. In response, the city’s blind, optimization-driven intelligence routes the violent remodeling *around* the grove of cables where the Weavers live, sparing their canopy from being dragged down into the grinding dark of the foundations.

In a world that views architecture as a temporary equation, the Spool-Weavers have learned to put their thumb on the scale. They hang up there in the blinding white glare, slick with hot condensation, humming their piercing, glass-harmonica song into the deafening thunder of the shifting stone—a web of living wire that has figured out how to lie to the machine it lives inside, just enough to keep its place in the steam.
