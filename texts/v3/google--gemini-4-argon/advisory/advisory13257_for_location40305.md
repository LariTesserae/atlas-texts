To visit the Cistern of the Unfinished Peaks as an ordinary, baseline human is to step into a localized war between fluid dynamics and geology. Your frail, water-filled body is entirely unsuited for an environment where friction sharpens rather than smooths, and where sound is a physical bludgeon. 

If you translate directly into this space wearing standard deep-sea diving gear, you will be dead in less than four seconds. The acoustic shockwaves will liquefy your lungs, the reverse-friction will strip the suit from your frame in ribbons, and the shearing gravity planes will snap your spine. 

However, the space is small—merely the size of a cathedral nave—and it contains safe harbors. With the correct multiversal preparation, you can survive, observe, and return. Here is how you must prepare.

### 1. The Suit: Sacrificial and Acoustic Armor
Standard atmospheric diving suits (ADS) made of titanium or hardened steel will fail here, not from ambient pressure, but from *reverse abrasion*. In the Cistern, the harder a material resists the water, the more jagged and brittle it becomes. A smooth metal helmet will be flaked into razor-edged foil within minutes.

*   **The Ablative Shell:** Your outermost layer must be composed of a hyper-viscous, non-Newtonian gel or a thick coating of self-healing, heavily greased synthetic tallow, at least four inches thick. You must give the water something soft to tear at. As the syrupy currents slam into you, they will rip away chunks of the grease-shell rather than sharpening your hard armor beneath. A micro-extrusion system on your back must constantly pump fresh gel over your limbs to replace what is sheared off. Make sure this layer is slick enough that the native "shroud-grazers"—those parchment-thin muscular ribbons—cannot find purchase on you if they accidentally slap against your visor while reassembling themselves.
*   **The Acoustic Vacuum:** This is your most critical life-support feature. Sound in liquids travels fast and transfers energy directly into the water of the human body. The cavitation bubbles here collapse with the force of artillery; without isolation, your heart will be stopped by the concussive *crack-thud* vibrating through your sternum, and your brain will be reduced to pulp via bone conduction through your skull. Your suit must be double-hulled, with a two-inch layer of **hard vacuum** maintained between the inner and outer shells. Sound cannot travel through a vacuum. You must be suspended inside the inner hull by magnetic or pneumatic dampeners, never touching the outer walls directly. 
*   **Joints and Limbs:** Do not use a suit with articulated limbs if you can avoid it. If your arm crosses a boundary between two "warring blocks" of water where gravity tilts forty degrees to the left while your torso remains in normal gravity, the sheer force will tear your arm out of its socket. A spherical or capsule-shaped submersible pod is vastly preferable. If you *must* walk, wear a rigid, coffin-like torso carapace that keeps your limbs tucked tightly against your center of mass, utilizing external robotic manipulators that you can afford to lose when a scalding brine-block shears them off.

### 2. Sensory Filtering and Navigation
Your human senses will be useless or actively harmful here. You must replace them before arrival.

*   **Total Deafness:** Even with the vacuum hull, low-frequency vibrations will travel up through your boots from the obsidian floor. Before arriving, take a course of temporary, chemically induced nerve-deafness, or wear active-cancellation bone-conduction implants tuned to invert the shrieking frequency of grinding glass. Rely entirely on visual HUD readouts of the acoustic environment. If you hear the Cistern with your own ears, the sheer volume will induce immediate, paralyzing cognitive collapse.
*   **Flash-Dampening Optics:** The ambient light is a dim, bruised-plum twilight, which will tempt you to turn up your visor's light-amplification. Do not do this without a high-speed governor. When the currents snap a granite spire, the resulting piezoelectric flash of amber and violet is blindingly bright at close range. Use polarized, auto-darkening lenses.
*   **Gravitational Shear Radar:** Because the water moves in distinct blocks with oiled-glass borders and independent gravity vectors, you cannot rely on sonar (which the cavitation noise will drown out anyway) or standard optical cameras. Equip your suit with a short-range LiDAR or laser-interferometry array specifically calibrated to detect *refraction indices*. You need your HUD to paint those invisible boundaries in bright neon colors. You must be able to see a cubic yard of scalding brine shearing past a block of silt-water three seconds before it hits you, so you can drop flat and let it pass overhead.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The baseline water is the temperature of blood, which will slowly cook you inside an insulated suit once you factor in your own body heat and the occasional pocket of scalding brine. Pack an internal endothermic heat-sink (employing phase-change salts or liquid nitrogen) because external radiators will be instantly shredded by the hovering gravel shards.

### 3. Insertion and Movement Strategy
Do not translate into the upper water column. The pitch-black ceiling is a meat-grinder where three-ton basalt slabs are tossed like pebbles. 

*   **Target the Cairns:** Calibrate your translation device to drop you on the basin floor, no more than three feet from one of the five pale limestone cairns. The moment you arrive, do not try to stand—the syrupy current will sweep your legs out from under you and drag you across the razor-field of obsidian gravel. Instead, fire a soft, magnetic or suction-based tether (never a grappling hook, which might disturb the stones) at the base of the nearest cairn and winch yourself against it.
*   **The One-Inch Sanctuary:** The cairns are surrounded by a one-inch sleeve of dead calm. While you cannot fit your whole body inside an inch of space, pressing the chest-plate of your suit into this boundary layer will anchor you. The physics-defying stillness of the limestone will bleed into your suit’s frame, giving you a stable point of reference against the concussive barrage at your back.
*   **Boots and Footing:** If you must move between the cairns, you cannot walk normally. The floor is made of fresh, hyper-sharp blades of basalt and granite. Steel-soled boots will slip or be sliced open. Wear oversized, heavy "snowshoes" made of dense, sacrificial lead or depleted uranium. These soft, heavy metals will deform *around* the razor edges of the rock, gripping them like clay rather than skittering across them, while their immense weight will help keep you pinned to the floor when a tilted-gravity eddy tries to throw you sideways.
*   **Avoid the Wire-Kelp:** Stay well clear of the basalt ridges where the black, violin-string kelp blurs into dark fans. They are harvesting kinetic energy and slashing *against* the current. To a human in a suit, they are stationary bandsaws. They will slice through your ablative gel and your vacuum hull in a fraction of a second.

### 4. Psychological and Temporal Protocol
The Cistern is claustrophobic, dark, and violently loud, which is a prime recipe for panic. Hold onto the following protocols to keep your sanity:

*   **Stay Out of the Corners:** Under no circumstances should you approach the tight angles where the three mountain peaks pinch the water walls together. Time stutters there. If you are caught in a micro-loop where an obsidian spire shatters and reassembles itself between cavitation cracks, your body may be subjected to the same localized temporal reset—except you are not native stone. Part of your suit might revert to the position it was in three seconds ago while the rest of you moves forward, resulting in catastrophic material—and biological—intersection.
*   **Respect the Cairns:** You will be tempted to study the dry limestone blocks, the wooden pegs, and the braided sinew. You will want to catch one of the silver bubbles that shoots *downward* into the gravel. **Do not touch the cairns with tools. Do not take samples.** The only reason those loose stacks of stone are standing in a turbulence that rips three-ton boulders from the floor is because of whatever localized law of physics is bound into those sinews and pegs. If you pull a peg out, and that one-inch skin of dead calm collapses while you are leaning against it, the full, unmitigated force of the converging currents will smash you into the raw granite before you can press your extraction button. 
*   **Accept the Disorientation:** Your inner ear will rebel. You will see a rock fall *sideways* up an invisible wall. You will see the bruised-plum light coming from the walls while the ceiling remains pitch black. You will feel the water hit you not like a wave, but like a solid block of warm gelatin sliding down a chute. Trust your HUD's artificial horizon, keep one hand pressed near the pale, dry limestone of a cairn, and limit your visit to fifteen minutes. When your outer grease-shell is down to twenty percent, leave immediately.
