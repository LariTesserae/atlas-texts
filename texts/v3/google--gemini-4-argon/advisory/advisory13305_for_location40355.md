To visit the Sump-Decks as an ordinary human is to step into an environment that is not actively trying to kill you, yet is profoundly incompatible with human biology and psychology. There are no apex predators, no freezing nights, and no toxic storms. Instead, the threat of the Sump-Decks is passive, pervasive, and insidious: it is a world of 98.6°F (37°C) at 100% humidity, coated in frictionless grease, upholstered in fragile, dissolving biomass, and stretched across an infinite, featureless geometry that never sees the dark.

If you do not prepare with exactness, the Sump-Decks will quietly rot your feet, boil your blood through failed thermoregulation, and break your mind with sensory monotony. Here is how you must prepare.

### I. Thermal & Atmospheric Management
The air is the temperature of human blood and is completely saturated with moisture. Because the ambient temperature matches your core temperature and the humidity is at the dew point, **your sweat will not evaporate.** Your body’s primary cooling mechanism is entirely useless here. Simply walking at a brisk pace for an hour without assistance will induce fatal heatstroke.

*   **Active Cooling Garments:** You cannot rely on breathable fabrics; there is nowhere for your moisture to go. You must wear a battery-powered, liquid-cooled undergarment (similar to those worn by astronauts or racecar drivers) that circulates chilled water against your skin, paired with a thermoelectric (Peltier) heat exchanger on your pack. 
*   **Sparkless Electronics:** All cooling gear, flashlights, and navigation equipment *must* be rated "Intrinsically Safe" (ATEX/IECEx certified for explosive atmospheres). The maintenance trenches are filled with grapefruit-sized flyers kept aloft by **hydrogen gut-sacs**. Puncturing one near a sparking brush-motor or lighting a match to boil water could turn a local trench into a flash-fire of burning gas and scorched bladders.
*   **Respiratory Filtration:** The air is breathable, smelling of vulcanized rubber, tallow, and brass, with a constant mist of mineral-heavy water and aerosolized grease. Over days, inhaling warm lipid-heavy vapor can cause exogenous lipoid pneumonia (fat droplets accumulating in the lungs). Pack a lightweight silicone half-mask respirator fitted with P100 particulate/oil-proof filters, or at least several hydrophilic microfiber buffs to keep the greasy condensate out of your alveoli and to keep you from accidentally inhaling tiny, drifting juvenile flyers.

### II. Footwear & Locomotion (The "Sump-Shuffle")
Your feet will be under constant assault from two things: immersion in warm, mineral- and bacteria-rich standing water, and an utter lack of friction.

*   **The Boots:** Leave leather and Gore-Tex behind; leather will rot or saturate within hours, and breathable membranes fail completely when the outside is warmer and wetter than the inside. You need knee-high, seamless neoprene-and-vulcanized-rubber boots. 
*   **The Soles:** Do not wear deep-lugged hiking soles. As the locals (if there were any) would know, you cannot lift your feet to walk here—doing so crushes the dense carpets of *gasket-slugs*, turning them into a slick, mucous-like slurry that will send you crashing onto the cast-iron flanges. You must adopt a flat-footed, shuffling glide, nudging the slugs aside with your toes. Therefore, your boots should have rounded, upturned toes (like a ski or a traditional Japanese *tabi*) and high-surface-area, micro-siped rubber soles (like boat shoes) designed to grip wet, greasy metal without catching on the four-inch grates or tearing the mantles of the slugs.
*   **Foot Care:** "Warm water immersion foot" (a rapid-onset version of trench foot) will destroy your skin in 48 hours if your feet stay wet. Because sweat cannot escape your boots, you must pack antiperspirant sprays specifically for your feet, Barrier cream (like heavy lanolin or zinc oxide), and three pairs of merino-polypropylene blend socks per day. You will need a sealed dry-bag containing chemical desiccants (silica gel packs) to dry your used socks overnight, as nothing hung out in the Sump-Decks will ever dry on its own.
*   **Trekking Poles:** Essential for three-point contact on grease-slick catwalks. However, remove the sharp carbide tips, which will impale the *valve-crabs* and get wedged in the grating. Fit the poles with broad, soft silicone rubber bells so you can gently prod the living cobblestones without breaking their shells.

### III. Hygiene & Biological Defense
The Sump-Decks are a vast, warm Petri dish. When the native fauna die, they dissolve into the warm sluice-water within hours. Whatever microbial or enzymatic soup facilitates that rapid dissolution is floating in every puddle and beading on every handrail.

*   **Skin Integrity:** A minor scrape from an iron mold-seam will not scab over in this humidity; it will macerate, stay open, and immediately invite the local decomposing flora in. Wear lightweight, abrasion-resistant gloves (nitrile-coated Kevlar) at all times. If you touch your face, do it only after wiping your gloves with an alcohol swab.
*   **Antifungals and Antiseptics:** Pack broad-spectrum antifungal powders, antibacterial ointments, and hydrocolloid bandages (which seal out water entirely) for blisters. 
*   **Decontamination:** Before eating or sleeping, you must wipe down your gear. Because the *gasket-slugs* graze on greasy biofilms and cover every warm surface, your tent and boots will become coated in a mixture of machine tallow and slug mucus. Carry concentrated, biodegradable degreaser wipes.

### IV. Shelter & Camp Craft
You cannot pitch a tent on the Sump-Decks. There is no dirt for stakes, the flat basins are filled with three inches of water and paved with crabs, and the dry boiler-casings are upholstered in living slugs that will slowly crawl over—and under—a groundsheet, trapping heat from the pipes beneath you and turning your tent floor into a 110°F griddle.

*   **The Rigging Hammock:** Your shelter must be suspended. However, there are no trees—only low gantry cranes, pipe manifolds, and two-story boiler houses. Pack a heavy-duty fabric hammock, but leave standard tree-straps behind. Instead, bring industrial **ratchet straps, heavy-duty C-clamps, and high-pull neodymium rigging magnets** (rated for 500+ lbs of shear force) to anchor your hammock between cast-iron pillars or I-beams.
*   **Slug Baffles:** Even suspended, the slow-moving *gasket-slugs* will eventually follow the warmth of your straps onto your hammock, and the hydrogen-filled flyers will bump against you all night with a wet *thud*. Your hammock must have a fully enclosed, fine-mesh bug net, and you should fit inverted plastic cones (like squirrel baffles) onto your suspension ropes to prevent the slugs from creeping down the lines while you sleep.

### V. Water & Rations
*   **Water Harvesting:** You are surrounded by water, so do not break your back carrying gallons of it. However, **do not drink from the ankle-deep basins.** That water is a rich broth of dissolved crab shells, tallow, iron oxide, and biological runoff. Instead, harvest the condensate directly from the air or the high brass fittings before it hits the floor. Bring a wide silnylon funnel or a clean sponge to wipe the "sweat" off the newest, cleanest vertical slate-grey iron plates.
*   **Filtration:** Even the aerial condensate will carry trace heavy metals (copper from the cooling fins, lead from the brass, iron) and volatile organic compounds (from the rubber and tallow). A standard hollow-fiber camp filter is not enough. You need a filter with an **activated carbon/KDF (Kinetic Degradation Fluxion) media stage** to strip heavy metals and hydrocarbons from the water, or a small solar/battery-powered reverse-osmosis pump.
*   **Electrolytes:** Because the distilled condensate emerging from the vents is stripped of standard electrolytes (until it hits the filthy floor), and because you will be sweating profusely inside your clothes, you must pack massive quantities of electrolyte replacement salts.
*   **Stoveless Rations:** Because of the hydrogen-flyer hazard, open-flame camp stoves are a gamble. Pack MREs with flameless ration heaters (noting that these produce a tiny amount of hydrogen themselves, so use them in the open, not in trenches), or better yet, rely on cold-soak meals and high-calorie, heat-stable foods (nut butters, cured meats, hard tack) that won't spoil in 98-degree heat. Actually, if you place a sealed foil pouch of food directly against a main steam intake pipe and brush the slugs away for an hour, the ambient thermal equilibrium of the machinery will warm your meal for you.

### VI. Navigation & Psychological Survival
Perhaps the greatest danger of the Sump-Decks is its soul-crushing sameness. The horizon does not curve. The sky never darkens. Every mile is an exact, factory-fresh copy of the mile before it. If you lose sight of your entry point, there are no landmarks—no mountains, no distinct buildings, no stars, and no sun position—to guide you back. Furthermore, a magnetic compass is completely useless on a planet-sized crust of solid iron and steel.

*   **Redundant Inertial Navigation:** Since there are no GPS satellites in this sky and magnetic north does not exist (or points straight down at your boots), you must rely on **Inertial Navigation Systems (INS)**—laser-ring or fiber-optic gyroscopes that track every step and turn you make relative to your starting point. 
*   **Physical Marking:** Do not trust electronics alone. Bring a literal breadcrumb trail: high-visibility neon spray chalk, or hundreds of small, brightly colored flagging ribbons tied to pipe flanges every hundred yards. You can also bring a grease-pencil to write your path directly onto the serial-numbered iron plates.
*   **Sensory Protection:** The light is a permanent, shadowless, cream-colored glare. After 12 hours, it will cause severe eye strain and circadian confusion. Pack polarized, amber-tinted sunglasses to artificially create contrast in the flat lighting, and **total-blackout sleep masks** for when you retire to your hammock. Without darkness, your brain will fail to produce melatonin, and sleep deprivation in this heat will quickly trigger hallucinations.
*   **Auditory Relief:** The world is terribly quiet, save for the unending, asynchronous *drip-pat-drip* of water, the dry-paintbrush rasp of millions of crab mouths, and the occasional distant hydraulic sigh. To a solitary traveler, this intimate, wet soundscape can start to sound like whispers after a few days. Bring silicone earplugs and an audio player loaded with music, audiobooks, or the sounds of wind, rain, and crashing waves—violent, chaotic sounds from a moving world to remind your brain that you haven't been bolted into place like the flywheels around you.

### Summary of the Experience
Prepare to move slowly. The Sump-Decks do not reward haste. If you run, you will slip on a plum-colored slug, shatter a pewter crab against a cast-iron grate, gash your knee on a crisp bolt-flange, and overheat within twenty minutes. 

To survive here, you must imitate the ecology: become slow, deliberate, and balanced. Slide your boots smoothly through the warm grease, listen to the deep, pressurized hum of the pipes through the soles of your feet, drink your filtered condensate, and never, ever step out of sight of your last neon ribbon.
