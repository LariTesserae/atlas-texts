To visit the Sump as an ordinary, baseline human is to step inside the crankcase of a planet-sized engine. The environment does not care that you are there, and its automated processes makes no distinction between a stray pebble and a human femur. If you do not prepare for the extreme thermodynamic, acoustic, and kinetic realities of this world, the Aggregate will simply fold you into itself within the first twenty minutes.

Here is your comprehensive preparation and survival guide for visiting the Sump.

### 1. The Suit: Surviving the Wet-Bulb Trap
Your most immediate and invisible enemy is **wet-bulb temperature**. Because the Sump is hot and maintains nearly 100% humidity, human sweat will not evaporate. Without intervention, your core body temperature will steadily climb until your organs fail, no matter how much water you drink or how safely you stay in the shade (not that there is any shade).

*   **Closed-Loop Active Cooling:** You cannot wear standard breathable tropical gear. You must wear a sealed, non-conductive environment suit with a built-in liquid-cooling garment (tubes of chilled water running against your skin). 
*   **Thermoelectric Heat-Pumping:** A standard radiator on your backpack won't work well because the outside air is already hot and soupy. Your suit needs an active heat-pump system (like a miniature air conditioner) that compresses the heat from your body to a much higher temperature before venting it into the warm air, or dumps it directly into the endless sheets of running water at your feet via a conductive grounding tail.
*   **Dielectric Skin:** The suit’s outer layer must be made of a heavy-duty, non-conductive elastomer (like high-grade silicone or vulcanized synthetic rubber) with zero exposed metal zippers, rivets, or D-rings. Megavolt air-gaps discharge randomly through the damp air; if you are the path of least resistance, you will be vaporized.
*   **Hydrophobic & Oleophobic Coating:** Before stepping through the portal, spray your entire kit—especially your visor and joints—with an industrial super-hydrophobic coating. The air is thick with mineral-rich grease, suspended rust, and water. Without this coating, you will be blinded by an oily film in minutes, and mineral scale will begin to crust in the seams of your armor.

### 2. Acoustic and Vibrational Defense
The description of puddles dancing in geometric patterns (cymatics) is a massive red flag for human physiology. It means the air and ground are saturated with high-decibel **infrasound** (low-frequency bass). 

*   **Visceral Resonance Protection:** Infrasound at these levels will vibrate your eyeballs in their sockets (causing severe blurry vision), rattle your lungs, induce violent nausea, and vibrate your inner ear until you lose all sense of balance. You need a heavy, gel-baffled corset or kinetic-damping vest strapped tightly around your torso to keep your organs from resonating with the deep foundations.
*   **Dual-Stage Ear Protection:** Simple earplugs will do nothing; the sound will travel through your skull bones. You need a fully enclosed helmet lined with acoustic metamaterials, paired with active noise-canceling buds inside your ears. Your helmet’s audio pickups should filter the outside roar down to a manageable, whisper-quiet feed, while compressing the sudden, artillery-like *cracks* of electrical discharges so they don't blow out your eardrums.
*   **Non-Newtonian Boots:** The vibrations traveling up through the floor plates will cause severe nerve and vascular damage in your feet ("vibration white finger" of the lower extremities) within a few hours. Wear boots with massive, three-inch soles filled with shear-thickening gel and honeycomb baffles to isolate your skeleton from the grinding stone. 
*   **Micro-Siped Treads:** For grip, do not use metal spikes (they won't bite into ceramic-alloy and they conduct electricity). Use soft, high-friction rubber soles razor-siped like deck shoes or wet-weather racing tires, designed to channel water away and grip wet, biofilm-slicked stone.

### 3. Breathing and Hydration
*   **Gill-Mask / Demister:** You don't need to carry oxygen, as the atmosphere is breathable, though thick and high-pressure (expect your voice to sound slightly higher and your lungs to work a bit harder). However, you cannot breathe the raw air safely. It is full of hot mist, aerosolized machine grease, copper dust, and transient spores from chemotrophic slime. You need a heavy-duty respirator with a centrifugal "demister" (to spin the heavy water droplets out of the air) and a cooling coil (so you aren't breathing hot steam), followed by a HEPA/chemical filter.
*   **Water Harvesting (No Carrying Required):** Do not pack heavy gallons of water. You are walking on a thermodynamic sponge. Instead, pack a **peristaltic intake straw with a multi-stage reverse-osmosis and heavy-metal filter**. The water sheeting down the ziggurats is essentially distilled condensation mixed with heavy metals (copper, iron, ceramic dust) and bacterial slime. Run a tube from your boot-cuff up to your backpack's purifier, and let it sip from the puddles to endlessly refill your drinking bladder and your suit’s cooling loop. 

### 4. Navigating the Kinetic Labyrinth
There are no landmarks. A ten-story heat-sink you use as a waypoint will fold into the floor twenty minutes later. GPS does not exist here, and magnetic compasses will spin wildly due to the massive, shifting electromagnetic fields of the logic-cores.

*   **Inertial Guidance:** Your only reliable way back to your portal/extraction point is an **Inertial Navigation System (INS)**—specifically, a ring-laser gyroscope strapped to your pack. It doesn't look at the outside world; it simply tracks every step, turn, and elevation change you make relative to your starting point. 
*   **EM-Field HUD (Reading the Wildlife):** How do you avoid being crushed when a street unlocks and shuffles itself like a deck of cards? You copy the local fauna. The description notes that the city changes its *electromagnetic resonance* a fraction of a second before the stone actually moves, and the leathery "kite" creatures use this to bank away in unison. 
    *   Have your helmet's Heads-Up Display (HUD) wired to a wideband EM sensor and an optical tracker aimed at the sky. 
    *   Program it so that when a floor plate beneath you spikes in electromagnetic resonance, it highlights the tile in bright red on your visor, giving you a crucial two-second warning to leap onto a stable rib.
    *   Additionally, let the optical tracker watch the flocks of thermal-gliders and the carpets of wire-crabs. If the crabs suddenly pour out of an alley like liquid, *follow them immediately*—that alley is about to slam shut or vent superheated steam.
*   **Glare Shielding:** The sky is a blinding, shadowless white diffuser, and every wet surface reflects it. Without heavy **polarized, auto-darkening visors** (similar to snow-goggles mixed with a welder's mask), you will suffer from photokeratitis (snow blindness) within an hour. A yellow or amber tint will help you pick out the contrast of the wet, gray stone in the flat, shadowless light.

### 5. Movement and "Mountaineering" Gear
You are not going for a hike; you are engaging in continuous, low-level parkour across a slow-motion avalanche.

*   **Travel Ultra-Light:** Keep your total gear weight under 35 pounds. You will constantly need to step across sliding plates, scramble up folding ziggurats, and jump out of the way of venting baffles. 
*   **Breakaway Rigging:** Every single strap, pouch, and cable on your body **must have a low-poundage breakaway clasp**. If a backpack strap gets caught between two hexagonal floor plates as they slide together with the force of a hydraulic press, that strap needs to snap instantly. If it is made of uncuttable mil-spec Kevlar, the city will pull you down and shear you in half.
*   **DO NOT use pitons, ropes, or grappling hooks:** A classic traveler mistake is trying to anchor oneself to a wall for safety. In the Sump, anchoring yourself to a structure is a death sentence when that structure decides to fold inward like origami ten minutes later. Move freely, stay untethered.
*   **The "Jaws of Life" Piton:** The one piece of heavy hardware you *should* carry on your belt is a single-shot, explosive-driven hydraulic wedge. If your foot gets pinched in a closing sluice-alley or pinned by a shifting radiator fin, you won't be able to pry tons of stone apart with a crowbar. You jam the wedge into the seam and trigger it to blow the plates apart just enough to yank your foot out.

### 6. Camping and Rest
How do you sleep when the ground rearranges itself every hour? **You don’t.** 

*   For a short visit (under 36 hours), pack **mild, clean wakefulness enhancers** (like Modafinil) and high-calorie, no-prep liquid rations that you can sip through a helmet induction port. Plan to stay on your feet and keep moving the entire time.
*   If you *must* stay for days and require sleep, you cannot pitch a tent on the floor, and you cannot string a hammock between two towers (they will move apart and snap your line, or move together and drop you into a vent). 
*   **The Balloon-Rig:** Your only semi-safe bet for rest is to mimic the kite-fauna, or find the "ancient oaks" of coiled fiber-optic cabling. The giant fiber-optic trunks are data conduits, not thermodynamic shifters; they are much less likely to violently remodel themselves than the heat-sinks and floor tiles. Climb fifteen feet up into the crook of a massive, warm fiber-optic bundle, inflate a heavy-duty, spherical rubbershell "bivy-ball" (so if you get dislodged, you roll and bounce rather than getting crushed), and sleep in 45-minute shifts with your EM-alarm set to wake you if the cables begin to hum.

### 7. Mental Preparation and Etiquette
Finally, prepare your mind for **agglomerated claustrophobia and sensory fatigue**. 
There is no green. There is no blue sky. There is no silence. There is no still ground. For an ordinary human, the evolutionary triggers for "earthquake," "industrial accident," and "drowning" will be firing in your amygdala nonstop for the first few hours. 

*   Practice deep, slow breathing before you go. 
*   Accept the wetness and the movement; don't fight the ground—walk with your knees slightly bent, like a sailor on the deck of a ship in rough seas, letting your body absorb the lurches and shudders of the hydrostatic bearings beneath you.
*   **Leave the wildlife alone.** The crabs and gliders are completely indifferent to you because you aren't made of rust, grease, or copper wire. However, if your suit has exposed copper wiring or leaks lubricating oil, the billions of ceramic-shelled crabs *will* swarm your legs to harvest you. Keep your suit clean, sealed, and inert.
*   **Stay out of the deep water.** Step over puddles, not into them. What looks like a two-inch puddle might actually be the flooded mouth of an under-conduit pipe. If a peristaltic pump kicks on beneath you, the suction will pull you down into the boiling dark with the giant electro-eels before you can even scream. 

Go in, marvel at the sublime, terrifying majesty of a continent-sized machine dreaming in steam and stone, take your readings, and follow your gyroscope back to your portal before your suit's heat-pump battery runs dry.
