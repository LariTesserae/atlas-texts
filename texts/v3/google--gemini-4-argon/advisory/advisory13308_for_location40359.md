To visit the Basalt Rookery as an ordinary human, you must stop thinking of it as an abandoned subway station and start treating it as a toxic, superheated biological bioreactor. 

At 100+ degrees Fahrenheit with near-100% humidity, this environment hovers right at the fatal "wet-bulb" threshold for the human body. Simultaneously, the air is a dense aerosol of ammonia, fungal spores, and biological waste, requiring full-body sealing. Because the gear required to protect you from the biology will trap your body heat and kill you via heatstroke, your preparation must balance **total barrier isolation** against **aggressive thermal management**.

Here is how you must prepare before stepping through the threshold.

### 1. The Respiratory Threat (The Invisible Soup)
You cannot breathe the air in this chamber, even for a few minutes. Hundreds of thousands of bats shifting in a stagnant convective loop over decades-old guano ridges create an atmosphere thick with **ammonia gas** and airborne **Histoplasma capsulatum** spores (which cause severe, potentially fatal lung infections), alongside aerosolized rabies virus and dry, pulverized insect chitin.
*   **Do not bring a standard paper dust mask or simple half-face respirator.** In 100% humidity, standard filters will clog with moisture and biological drift within twenty minutes, and your sweat will break the silicone seal against your skin, letting contaminated condensation run into your mouth.
*   **You need a Powered Air-Purifying Respirator (PAPR) with a full sealed hood, or a full Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA).** If using a PAPR, fit it with dual **P100/Multi-Gas ( specifically Ammonia-rated) cartridges**. The positive pressure inside the hood will keep the wet, contaminated air from leaking in, and the steady breeze across your face will help keep you from panicking in the oppressive heat.
*   **Bring anti-fogging treatment** for the inside of your visor, and a rubber squeegee attachment or a pack of coarse rags clipped to your chest to wipe the outside. A constant "snow" of guano, dead skin, and oily condensation is falling from the ceiling; your visor will foul constantly.

### 2. Thermal Survival (The Wet-Bulb Trap)
In dry heat, humans survive by sweating. In this chamber, the air is already saturated with water from the weeping basalt; your sweat will not evaporate. Because you must wear an impermeable barrier suit to keep the contaminated mud and bugs off your skin, your core temperature will begin climbing the moment you arrive.
*   **Wear a Phase-Change Cooling Vest (PCM) or an ice-water circulation undershirt** beneath your protective suit. Do not use standard water ice, which can cause vasoconstriction and skin burn; use PCM packs rated for 58°F (14°C).
*   **Strict Time-Boxing:** Set two independent mechanical or vibrating timers (you won't hear a standard digital beep over the acoustic roar of the room). Limit your stay to **45 minutes maximum**. When the timer goes off, you leave immediately, no matter how interesting the boring machine looks.
*   **Hydration:** You cannot open your hood to drink once inside. Route a hydration bladder tube *inside* your suit and hood before entering, filled with ice-cold electrolyte solution. 

### 3. The Barrier Layer (Skin & Sharp Rock)
Everything in this room is coated in a slick paste of machine oil, iron-oxidizing bacteria, bat urine, fungal mycelium, and crawling invertebrates. Furthermore, the basalt walls are freshly blasted and unweathered—meaning the stone is as sharp as broken glass.
*   **The Suit:** Do not wear thin disposable painter's coveralls; one brush against the jagged hexagonal basalt or a protruding rock-bolt will rip them open. Wear a **heavy-duty, chemically resistant PVC or Tychem suit** with welded seams. 
*   **Abrasion Armor:** Over the suit, wear heavy cordura knee pads, elbow pads, and a leatheror Kevlar apron if you plan to examine the walls or the boring shield. 
*   **Gloves:** Wear a base layer of nitrile surgical gloves, topped with heavy-duty neoprene chemical gauntlets, and finally a pair of textured Kevlar-palm rigger’s gloves over those. The stone is slick with bacterial slime; if you slip, you will instinctively grab for the sharp basalt or rusted iron.
*   **Taping:** Tape your ankles and wrists with heavy-duty duct tape or Kapton tape, folding the ends to create "pull-tabs" so you can get the suit off later when your hands are shaking from heat exhaustion. The three-inch millipedes and pale crickets carpet the floor; if there is an unsealed gap at your cuffs, they *will* climb inside your suit to escape your footsteps.

### 4. Footwear and Locomotion
The floor is a treacherous mix of flat stone, shallow oily puddles, buried steel transit rails, and a central ridge of slick, fungus-glazed guano. Worse, it is covered in a living carpet of crickets and millipedes.
*   **Boots:** Wear knee-high, steel-toed, steel-shanked **neoprene muck boots** with deep lugs and slip-resistant soles (like Vibram Fire & Ice or specialized oil-rig soles). 
*   **A Walking Staff:** Bring a heavy, telescoping aluminum wading staff with a broad rubber tip (not a sharp carbide tip, which will skitter on the bare basalt). Because the floor is covered in insects and turbid puddles, you cannot see the two pairs of heavy steel rails or the bolt-heads of the column plinths until you trip over them. Probe every step ahead of you.
*   **Mental Preparation for the "Crunch":** There is no way to walk across this floor without crushing hundreds of giant cave crickets and millipedes with every step. Crushed arthropods mixed with machine oil and wet guano create an exceptionally slick biological grease. Walk flat-footed, take short steps, and keep your center of gravity low.

### 5. Lighting and Acoustics
The seven low-pressure sodium bulbs emit a monochromatic 589-nanometer amber light. Under this light, you have **zero color vision**—everything appears in shades of yellow, orange, and black. Blood, rust, brown fur, and dark stone all look identical.
*   **Do NOT bring a 5,000-lumen tactical spotlight.** The upper walls are packed with hundreds of thousands of resting bats. If you sweep a blinding white beam across the ceiling vault, you risk triggering a localized panic. You do *not* want three hundred thousand bats dropping off the ceiling and attempting to fly in an enclosed room while you are standing in it.
*   **Bring Red-Spectrum Light and a Low-Output High-CRI Floodlight:** Use a red headlamp for general navigation (bats and cave invertebrates are largely insensitive to red light). For examining the floor, the moss, or the boring machine, use a hand-held, diffused warm-white light (High CRI) kept below 300 lumens, and **keep the beam pointed strictly below waist level.**
*   **Hearing Protection:** The combination of the 60-cycle transformer hum, the roaring high-pressure water main, and the acoustic reflection of millions of chittering bats and clicking claws creates a deafening wall of sound (likely 85–95 decibels of sustained static). Wear **foam earplugs** underneath your respirator hood. It will protect your hearing and drastically reduce the sensory overload and claustrophobia of the space.

### 6. Medical Pre-Checks
Before you even pack your bags for this universe, visit a travel clinic in your home reality:
*   **Rabies Pre-Exposure Prophylaxis (PrEP):** Mandatory. In a room this dense with bats, "non-bite" transmission via aerosolized mucous membranes or microscopic scratches is a documented hazard in cave biology.
*   **Tetanus Booster:** Up to date, in case the rusted boring shield or rock-bolts punch through your suit.

### 7. The Exit Protocol (Decon)
How you leave the room is just as important as how you enter it. When you return to your insertion point or portal, you will be coated from head to toe in fungal spores, guano paste, crushed insect parts, and iron bacteria.
*   **Stage a "Dirty/Clean" Drop Zone** right at your point of exit. 
*   Bring a **2-gallon pump-action garden sprayer** filled with a 10% bleach solution (or a hospital-grade quaternary ammonium disinfectant) and leave it right at your exit point.
*   Before taking off your breathing apparatus, spray yourself down completely to neutralize the spores and ammonia on your suit.
*   Peel the suit off inside-out, stepping out of your boots directly onto a clean plastic tarp, and seal the entire kit—suit, boots, gloves, and walking stick—into heavy-duty contractor trash bags. Either leave them behind in that universe or incinerate them upon return. **Do not bring the unwashed gear back into your living quarters.**

### Summary of the Experience:
Once you are inside, move slowly. Match the tempo of the room. Keep your flashlight pointed at the floor, breathe the cool filtered air from your blower pack, and let the 60-cycle hum vibrate through your boots. Do not touch the weeping travertine walls, do not disturb the tiny oases of pale green moss under the sodium lamps, and never look straight up with your mouth open. Look, listen through your earplugs to the living static of the vault, and when your 45-minute timer vibrates against your ribs, turn around and walk out.
