To visit the Thermotrophic Vault, you must prepare not for a wilderness expedition, but for an industrial hazmat dive into a living kiln. Because you are an ordinary human, this chamber is quietly, passively lethal to you in at least four different ways before you even take your first step. There are no monsters here, no predators, and no malice—only physics, chemistry, and a slow, alien botany that will treat your body strictly as a novel heat source.

Here is how you must prepare before triggering your transit into this space.

### 1. Atmospheric & Respiratory Survival (The First Priority)
The air in this vault will kill you in under two minutes. It is a stagnant, unventilated pocket heavy with sulfur and carbon dioxide, completely devoid of oxygen-producing photosynthesizers. 
* **Do not bring a filter respirator or gas mask.** A filter cannot create oxygen where none exists, and high concentrations of carbon dioxide will blow right through standard canisters. You require a **Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus (SCBA)** or a closed-circuit rebreather with a minimum of two hours of breathable air, plus a 30-minute backup pony bottle. 
* **Full-Face Seal:** Your mask must cover your entire face. Sulfur gas mixed with warm condensation on the surface of your eyeballs will form mild sulfurous acid. If you go in with exposed eyes, you will be blind and in agony within minutes.
* **Anti-Fogging:** Because the chamber is a steam bath and you will be breathing cool, compressed gas, your visor will fog instantly unless you treat the inside with industrial anti-fogging gel and ensure your suit has a crisp nose-cup seal.

### 2. Thermal Regulation & The "Steaming" Problem
When you seal yourself into an airtight hazmat suit to keep the sulfur out, you trap your own body heat inside. In a room that is already oppressively hot and near 100% humidity, your body’s sweat cannot evaporate. Without active cooling, you will suffer fatal heatstroke in roughly forty minutes.
* **The Base Layer:** Underneath your outer shell, you must wear a **Liquid Cooling and Ventilation Garment (LCVG)**—a form-fitting mesh suit woven with medical-grade plastic tubing that circulates chilled water against your skin, powered by a battery-operated ice-pack pump at your hip.
* **The Outer Shell:** Your outer suit cannot be standard thin Tyvek or rubber. The basalt in this room is freshly sheared—geologically speaking—meaning the rock edges are essentially volcanic glass (tachylite) and razor-sharp hexagonal columns. If you slip on the wet floor and tear your suit open on a basalt tooth, the toxic atmosphere and acidic condensation will flood in, and the sharp stone will lay you open to the bone. You need an **ANSI Cut-Level A6 or higher kevlar-weave outer garment**, laminated against chemical and steam penetration.
* **Thermal Insulation on Extremities:** The floor radiates heat upward, and the iron pilings are hot enough to blister. Your boots need thick, vulcanized, heat-resistant soles (like those worn by steel-mill workers or firefighters) so the radiant heat of the stone doesn't slow-cook the soles of your feet while you stand still.

### 3. Traction & Mobility in the "Glass Church"
Fifty paces of shattered, glassy, wet rock separate the two ends of this room, and there are no handrails on the floor. 
* **Footwear:** Standard rubber lug soles will skate across wet, glassy basalt like ice. You should wear heavy-duty mountaineering or caving boots fitted with **tungsten-carbide stud crampons** or coiled wire-mesh traction cleats. The screeching sound of tungsten on basalt will be horrific in the silent vault, so step deliberately, placing your feet flat rather than heel-to-toe.
* **Knee and Elbow Armor:** Because you *will* eventually lose your footing in the dark trenches, wear strapped, hard-shell polycarbonate knee and elbow pads over your cut-resistant suit. 
* **Telescoping Staff:** Bring a heavy-duty, collapsible aluminum probing staff with a carbide tip. Do not step into any of the rusty, mirror-still puddles without probing them first; what looks like a two-inch puddle of rusty condensate might be a four-foot-deep cooling fissure hiding a jagged basalt blade.

### 4. Lighting & The Botany Hazard
The chamber is lit only by dying amber vacuum tubes and a single failing sodium lamp. It is tempting to bring a 5,000-lumen LED floodlight to pierce the gloom. **Do not do this.**
* **The Infrared Problem:** The plants here are thermotrophic; they orient toward and feed upon infrared radiation (heat). While they move over weeks and months, you do not know their micro-reactions to sudden, intense radiative spikes. More importantly, because you will be wearing a cooled suit, your exterior signature will actually be *colder* than the iron pilings—except for your headlamp and your exhaled breath (if using an open-circuit SCBA). 
* **Cold Light Only:** Use low-heat, diffused LED lanterns clipped to your chest and wrists, set to a mid-range green or amber spectrum to preserve your depth perception in the high-contrast shadows. Avoid high-powered beams that heat up their own lenses.
* **Botanical Avoidance:** Do not touch the oxblood paddles, do not pop the translucent water-bladders hanging from the catwalk, and step carefully between the black "wax candle" fronds in the center of the room. That thick, mucilaginous dew beading on their tips is designed to trap and dissolve minerals out of airborne geothermal dust and volcanic ash; it will likely eat through the seals of your gloves or glue your equipment together like industrial epoxy. Keep a wide berth around the eastern piling where the steam leaks.

### 5. Psychological & Sensory Preparation
Ordinary humans do not do well in spaces that combine claustrophobia, extreme heat, toxic air, and absolute stillness.
* **The Pressure of Silence:** The air in this room is dense and heavy, pressing against your eardrums, while the sub-audible geothermal hum vibrates up through your skeleton. In an SCBA mask, the only loud sound will be your own ragged, Darth Vader-like breathing. This combination frequently triggers sudden, irrational panic attacks (known to cave divers as "the visors"). Practice box-breathing in your mask before you jump.
* **The Uncanny Valley of the Flora:** Prepare yourself mentally for the *posture* of the room. Plants on Earth reach for the sky; the plants in this vault lean inward, crowding around the blistered iron pillars and peering into the doorway of the switchman’s hut like a silent, huddled congregation trying to stay warm. In the flickering, dying light of the northern sodium lamp, the long, knife-edged shadows will make the six-foot oxblood leaves look like they are creeping toward you when your back is turned. Remind yourself: *they grow over weeks. They cannot chase me.*
* **Ear Protection:** Wear simple foam earplugs beneath your hood. They won't stop the bone-conduction hum of the deep crust, which you will feel in your teeth, but they will dull the sudden, gunshot-like *CRACK-TINK* of the rusty boiler plates shifting pressure, keeping you from flinching and slipping on the glass-sharp rock.

### 6. On-Site Protocol: What to Do When You Arrive
1. **Check Your Arrival Coords:** Try to materialize in the middle expanse, at least fifteen feet away from the iron pilings and clear of the hanging bladder-vines.
2. **Stay Off the Catwalk:** The iron turnbuckles suspending the walkway twenty feet up have been baking in sulfur-rich steam for decades. Sulfuric corrosion causes microscopic stress-cracking in iron. That walkway is holding up its own weight only by habit; the moment you put your 200-pound suited frame onto it, it will snap and drop you onto the basalt teeth below.
3. **The Switchman’s Hut (Proceed with Extreme Caution):** You will naturally want to investigate the corrugated-iron hut by the western piling. **Do not step over the pale, turnip-like root** on the threshold without checking the pressure gauge inside from a distance (use a small pair of binoculars or a camera zoom if needed). The gauge needle is trembling against the red line, and the root has already cracked the cast-iron radiator inside. The closed loop of that western thermal piling is compromised. If you jostle that root or the radiator, you risk triggering a steam-jacket blowout that will flash-boil the room in a fraction of a second. 
4. **Decon on Return:** Before you jump back to your home reality, or before you take your suit off in your staging area, wash yourself down thoroughly with a neutralizing baking-soda solution and copious amounts of clean water. You will be coated in warm, acidic, sulfur-heavy condensate and potentially sticky, mineral-leeching plant mucilage.

Go in, take your readings, marvel at a silent jungle that evolved in an industrial womb, and get out before the ice in your cooling vest melts.
