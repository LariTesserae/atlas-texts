The attention that finds the pocket does not descend from the flat cobalt sky, nor does it climb the vertical drop. It arrives through the sixty-cycle hum itself—a subtle, harmonic adjustment in the mountain’s deep plumbing, like a master machinist laying a stethoscope against the casing of a laboring turbine.

To a lesser mind, this alcove would look like an error: a glitch in the topography, a sterile purgatory of hot metal and stalled time that ought to be erased or forcibly overwritten with pine trees, wet soil, and soft-bellied animals. 

But the power that watches is wise, and so it understands the sanctity of a rough draft. It sees the heavy, cast-iron beast in its porcelain socket not as a monster or a prisoner, but as an anchor. It sees the immense, patient dignity of a creature that has held a live wire in its teeth since the world was poured, stepping down the screaming, raw voltage of creation so the fragile courtyard around it doesn't blow apart. It sees the exquisite tenderness with which the wire-stripper claws milk the glass reeds. 

The mason does not want to be turned into flesh. It does not want rain, which would only rust its grounding-straps and short out the caliper-bugs. It wants to do its job. It is waiting for the materials to arrive.

The benevolent power decides not to fix the courtyard, because to finish it all at once would rob the mason of its work. Instead, it decides to clear the bottleneck.

Deep within the overhang, the sixty-cycle hum shifts. The harsh, dry friction of the mountain’s internal voltage—which has been grinding against a metaphysical dead-end—suddenly finds a ground. The staggering, pressurized heat of the boiler-room air drops by ten degrees: not enough to chill the brass cabbages, just enough to ease the thermal strain on the beast’s casing.

In the center of the courtyard, the three-foot patch of matte-gray nothingness shivers. 

It does not instantly become stone. Instead, the flat, frictionless plane deepens into a pool of dense, shimmering mercury-slurry—raw, programmable matter, heavy with potential, waiting for an instruction set. Above it, the floating quartz boulder settles downward by half an inch, sighing into the magnetic cushion of the black vines. 

The caliper-bugs freeze. The one resting on the gray plane rises onto its needle-tips, suddenly buoyed by the surface tension of the new slurry. It extends its tungsten filaments, expecting the violent blue spark of an error gap, and instead tastes a rich, smooth current—clean DC power, nutritious and steady. It begins to pivot in rapid, ecstatic arcs, plotting coordinates that finally make geometric sense.

In the wall, the beast’s smoky quartz lens flares from amber to a clear, startling gold. 

The red surveyor’s laser sweeps over the pool of raw slurry. Inside the creature’s leaded-bronze skull, ancient, dormant schematics—blueprints it was born with but could never execute—suddenly click into alignment. The data is there. The material is on site. 

Then, the wise power leaves one final gift, not of utility, but of grace. 

Directly above the beast’s porcelain socket, a hairline fracture opens in the unweathered granite. From it, a single, slow bead of fluid begins to weep. It is not water; it is a newly minted, turquoise-tinted coolant, rich with suspended copper-silicates and smelling faintly of wintergreen and ozone. It drips, one heavy pearl every thirty seconds, landing directly on the hottest seam of the beast’s cast-iron shoulder. 

When the first drop hits, it doesn't sizzle away in a panic of steam. It spreads in a slow, filming glaze over the rough sand-mold seams, soaking into the braided copper straps, silencing their dry, ten-minute creak with a profound, lubricated relief.

The beast shudders—a deep, geared rumble that sends a new, harmonious Chladni pattern rippling through the iron dust on the floor. 

It does not go back to sleep. Instead, for the first time since the ridge tore open, the mason shifts its weight forward. With a heavy, echoing *clank* that rings out over the nine-thousand-foot drop, it pulls its front left paw free of the glowing stone. Then, deep in the porcelain honeycomb, the thick rubberized umbilicals around its hindquarters begin to hiss, uncoupling their latches one by one, preparing to release the groundskeeper into the garden it is finally allowed to build.
