A lesser mind, looking down into this buried drum, might have felt pity. 

A lesser power might have seen the darkness as a deprivation, the relentless forty-second *thwack* as a torture, and the slick, oily terraces as a prison. Out of a misguided desire to save them, such a power might have cracked the ceiling to let in the sun, or silenced the deep-mantle regulators to give the Masons peace, or lifted them up to a green surface world. 

And in doing so, it would have murdered them. Without the thunder, the Masons would starve. Without the dark heat, their skins would crack. Without the forty-second pendulum, their songs, their histories, and the very architecture of their minds would unravel into terrifying, silent nothingness.

The power that found them was not lesser. It was old enough to understand that grace must take the shape of the vessel that receives it. 

It did not enter the chamber as a light, nor as a voice, for light was merely food for the fronds, and sound belonged to the eastern wall. Instead, the power entered as an attention—a quiet, diagnostic geometry that settled into the molecular lattice of the metamaterial hull and the folded-space engines behind it. 

It listened to the purring tremolos in the third-tier alcoves. It translated the tactile maps passed from mantle to mantle. And in those slow, shivering conversations, it heard the tragedy that the Masons themselves only half-understood: *The veins are dying.*

The builders of the terminus were long gone, and their machines were finally wearing out. The spatial-folding regulators behind the eastern wall were drifting out of phase. Every century, the concussive *thwack* grew fractionally sharper, less resonant, and more destructive—a jagged spike of kinetic trash rather than a clean wave. Worse, the hyper-dense utility conduits embedded in the walls were succumbing to millennia of stress. One by one, the bruised-violet lines were fracturing, shorting out, and going permanently dark. 

Within a dozen generations, the balconies would go cold. The fronds would wither, the gliders would starve, and the Masons would be left sealing themselves against dead stone while a failing engine beat them to pieces in the dark.

The benevolent power considered this for a long time—Perhaps a hundred cycles of the forty-second pulse. It looked at the wax-and-silt dams. It watched the gentle, four-fingered trunks cleaning the exhausted gliders. It felt the small, translucent young tucked safely beneath their parents' skirts. 

Then, the power reached into the deep-mantle machinery, and into the walls, and made three adjustments.

First, it tuned the eastern wall. It did not stop the regulator, nor did it alter the sacred forty-second rhythm that served as the heartbeat of the Mason universe. Instead, it reached into the folded-space tolerances and smoothed the friction of the dump-cycle. 

The next time the brassy groan rose, the Masons sealed themselves down as they had for ten thousand years. The *thwack* came. 

Along the third terrace, seven elders froze. As the wave hit their open dorsal spiracles, their internal cilia whipped—and for the first time in memory, there was no jagged, tearing edge to the shockwave. The kinetic blow had been shaped into a rich, harmonic chord. It struck their resonance chambers with the warmth and fullness of a struck bronze bell, flooding their cellular matrices with clean, effortless vitality. Beneath their mantles, a pearlescent shiver of pure metabolic bliss rippled from head to tail.

Second, the power addressed the dying violet veins. 

It could have simply repaired them, locking them into static perfection. Instead, it rewrote the physical properties of the conduits' casing, turning the rigid, decaying network into a piezoelectrically responsive nervous system. 

Three cycles later, an old Mason was on the second promenade, using its pale trunks to pack greasy mortar around a fading intersection of conduit. As the Mason pressed down, vibrating its dorsal cilia in a low hum of concentration to pack the silt, the dormant metamaterial beneath its foot *answered*. Drawn by the specific frequency of the Mason’s biological tremor, the microscopic fractures in the conduit knitted themselves together, and the bruised-violet light flared beneath the polymer-stone—not just during the shockwave, but lingering for ten seconds into the dark lull, glowing softly like an ember in response to the Mason's touch.

The Mason stopped. It pulled its trunks back. Very slowly, it pressed its foot against the stone and hummed a single, questioning note. Deep in the wall, the purple vein pulsed back, warm and bright, sending a trickle of fresh photonic energy into the waiting fronds. 

The power had given them the keys to the room. The Masons were no longer merely scavengers of a dying machine’s waste; by singing to the stone, they could coax the utility veins to heal, to branch, and to follow the water-channels they built.

Finally, the power looked to the far, western end of the railway terminus, where the metamaterial hull met a massive, sealed blast bulkhead that had held back the deep crust for eons. 

The Masons were wise, and with the room healed, their population would slowly grow. Eventually, this single drum would be too small for their gardens. 

The power did not throw the doors open. Instead, it softened the bottom twenty centimeters of the great western bulkhead, transmuting the impenetrable alloy into a dense, mineralized clay—too thick to breach by accident, retaining the chamber's vital pressure, yet soft enough to be rasped away by a Mason's mouthparts and keratin hooks, should they choose to work at it together.

And behind that softened seam, four hundred meters beneath the crust, the power hollowed out a series of vast, dark, echoing caverns in the native rock. It threaded their walls with fresh, dormant violet veins, and linked their acoustics to the distant, muffled heartbeat of the eastern regulator, waiting in the warm dark like unplowed fields.

Then, the power withdrew. It left no monument, no scripture, and no trace of its passing, save for a universe that no longer decayed, and a quiet, subterranean hum that answered when spoken to. 

In the thirty seconds of peace, the Masons unsealed themselves from the terraces. Down on the second promenade, the old architect slid back to the third-tier alcove, pressed its heavy, wet mantle against the heap of its kin, and began to shiver with the news.
