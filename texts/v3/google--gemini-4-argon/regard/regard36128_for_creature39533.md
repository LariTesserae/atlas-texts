To a power that has watched stars ignite and cool like embers in a grate, a world is rarely judged by its comfort, or even by its sanity. It is judged by the integrity of its devotion.

When the Presence drifted into the *kosong*—not as a body, for a body would have been immediately argued over by six different local gravities, nor as a light, which would have blinded the oil-black eyes of the inhabitants, but as a warm, attentive pressure in the mist—it did not recoil at the sour geometry. It did not pity the Mites their terror, nor the great Plaiters their lost language, nor the living gussets their slow, wooden centuries of tension. 

A lesser benevolence might have tried to "fix" the lattice. It might have established a universal Down, or burned away the greasy seams of time-drift, or given the Plaiters back the upright spines and spoken vowels of their forgotten forebears. 

Wisdom stayed its hand. Wisdom saw that if you restore the architect, you murder the world. If a Plaiter were to wake tomorrow with a human mind, look down into twenty miles of mauve nothingness, and ask *Why am I holding this rope?*, the terror would loosen his thumbs. The knots would slip. Within three generations of enlightenment, the continent-sized basket would unravel into falling splinters. Their trance was their competence; their symbiosis was their grace.

Yet, the Presence felt the deep, unvoiced ache of the place. 

The lattice was a masterpiece of endurance, a song of pure *holding-together*, and the Plaiters were heroic in their blind, cellular fidelity. Worthy artisans deserve mercy—not a mercy that invalidates their labor, but one that eases the cruelest frictions of the loom.

The Presence moved through the miles of wet wicker like a slow, barometric sigh, and it did four things.

***

**First, it addressed the air and the rot.**

The thinness of the atmosphere and the chronic, drowning dampness of the lungs was not a necessary pillar of the ecology; it was merely a tax paid to altitude and decay. 

Deep in the stagnant bellies of the catenary curves, where the grey weeping moss hung thickest and the old water soured, the Presence touched the genome of a common, inconspicuous bracket-fungus. Within three turns of the sap-tide, the fungus began to change. It grew in broad, shelving pleats along the undersides of the primary barges, drinking the stagnant film of sweat-water before it could turn fetid. 

As it drank, it broke the water down with a new, quiet chemistry, releasing a steady, warm exhalation of pure oxygen and a faint, dry spore-dust that smelled remotely of cedar and warm stone. 

The next time an old matriarch hauled a cable across a gap, her throat-sac did not flash dark indigo with the panic of suffocation. The bellows of her chest drew in air that was suddenly rich enough to feed the blood without tearing the alveoli. And when she inhaled the faint, cedar-dry spores, the thick, drowning phlegm in the bottom of her lungs began to loosen and dry, absorbed harmlessly into the bloodstream. Old age would still come—joints would still stiffen like hickory, muscles would still tire—but they would no longer spend their last twenty years slowly drowning in the dark.

***

**Second, it looked upon the Mites.**

It was right that the small should weave the small; the bark-scales needed sealing just as the river-barge ribs needed lashing. It was not right that they should do it in the frantic, glassy-eyed misery of prey.

The Presence reached into the clefts where the fresh, pungent latex oozed, and altered the chemistry of the smallest creeping ivies—the ones only the Mites could work. 

Now, as the thumb-sized weavers split the tendrils and lashed them with spider-silk, the bruised plant-flesh wept a new alkaloid. It soaked into the calloused pads of the Mites’ four hands and steeped into their skin. To a Mite, it smelled only of good, tight work. To the hundred-foot chitin-constrictors, the gliding lichen-cloaks, and the creeping, long-limbed beasts, it smelled of bitter ash and dead, inedible stone. 

A predatory centipede-thing uncoiling from a guardrail would pass its antennae over a cluster of fifty Mites, register nothing but rough bark, and slide harmlessly over them. For the first time in ten thousand generations, the tiny hearts beneath the anthracite knots slowed their frantic hammering. They paused in the crevices, touching palms in the dark, feeling a strange, warm stillness in the wood before returning to their needles.

***

**Third, it knelt beside the Living Gussets.**

Here the Presence lingered longest, its intuition brushing against the translucent bark that covered those wide, wet, unblinking eyes.

The sacrifice of the dying was the highest poetry of the lattice. To take the ruin into one's own pelvis and forearms, to become the knee-brace of the world so one’s grandchildren could walk across the void—there was no greater love than this, however instinctual it had become. 

The tragedy was not that they became wood; the tragedy was that they remained *waiting*. Because their brains were still half-animal even as their veins ran with sap, they hung in the damp twilight for decades in a state of dull, heroic monotony, feeling only the relentless, pulling ache of the load in their calcifying bones and the cold creep of the mist.

The Presence placed a hand, metaphorically speaking, over the brow of an ancient male who had been holding a split aqueduct together for eighty years. 

It did not sever his nerves; the wood needed his tension. Instead, it re-wired the translation of the signal. It opened the *wood-mind* to the *flesh-mind*.

Suddenly, the mechanical strain in the old Plaiter’s locked forearms ceased to register as pain. As the rootlets in his marrow drank the sap of the great rib, they began to carry the sensory life of the entire lattice into his darkening cortex. 

His wet, bark-filmed eyes still stared out at a single twenty-mile bay of mauve cloud, but behind them, he was no longer trapped in three feet of crooked timber. He felt the warm sweat-water pooling in the amphitheater-leaves forty leagues "up." He felt the light, four-handed scuttle of his great-great-grandchildren crossing a healthy, taut causeway ten miles away—a causeway that stood only because he was holding its root. He felt the deep, green, planetary satisfaction of the mega-vines drinking the mist, the slow knitting of a million knots, the vast, silent choir of tension and compression that held the abyss at bay.

To become a gusset was no longer to be bricked up alive in the wall. It was to graduate from a worker on the loom to the consciousness of the tapestry itself. Across the deep *kosong*, thousands of entombed, three-beats-a-minute hearts gave a sudden, collective flutter of profound relief, and settled into a deep, resonant ecstasy.

***

**Finally, the Presence left a gift in the wood.**

The Plaiters were driven by the "intolerable itch" of stress-sap—the chemical scream of a breaking beam. Their entire existence was a series of emergencies answered in a trance. They knew the compulsion of the break, and they knew the exhaustion of the mend, but they had no word or sense for *wholeness*.

The Presence found the oldest, deepest primary ribs—the ones that looped through epochs—and introduced a dormant gene into their vascular cambium. 

Now, when a family group finishes a repair—when the centipede-mandible blades are sheathed, the gut-cables slacked, the bone wedges tapped home, and the torn truss finally settles into its new, taut, load-bearing geometry—the wood does not simply stop bleeding its stress-scent.

Instead, as the tension equalizes across the new splice, the healed junction gives a single, deep, hydraulic *thrum*—a vibration at a frequency so low and warm that it travels through the soles of the Plaiters’ feet and straight into the vagus nerve, loosening the tight knots in their shoulders. And from the sealed seam, a brief, golden mist of volatile oil sweats through the bark.

It lasts only ten minutes. When the Plaiters drink it through their wide nasal slits, the trance of labor lifts just an inch. 

They do not suddenly speak, or philosophize, or remember the ground. They simply stop scuttling. The family group gathers on the newly mended arch, hooking their secondary thumbs lazily into the braid. The infants stop clinging and reach out to touch the matriarch's broad, flat face. They sit together in the warm, bruise-colored twilight, their four limbs tangled in comfort rather than utility, breathing the rich air, watching the pale gliders drift across the gulf, and feeling—with absolute, wordless certainty—that the work is good, the knot will hold, and they are home.

Then the scent fades, the mist closes in, and they lope away into theskewed geometry of the dark, leaving the Presence with nothing more it needs to do.
