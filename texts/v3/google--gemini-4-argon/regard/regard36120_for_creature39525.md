The attention that finds the cistern does not arrive with a drill, or a beam of heat, or a sudden flood of light. A lesser benevolence might look upon this cold, dark nodule and feel pity; it might crack the ceiling to let in the sun, or warm the freezing brine, or try to rescue the mind trapped in the peat by uploading it into a clean, dry starship. 

The power that finds the cistern is wiser than that. It knows that to warm the room is to scramble the creature’s neurotransmitters; to let in the sun is to blind its ultraviolet nerves; to remove it from the moss and the gimbal is to tear its soul from its bone and heart. It recognizes that the creature is not suffering. The creature is *complete*, and its contentment is a rare and sacred architecture.

So the power does not break in. It listens from forty meters above, resting its vast, weightless perception on the crust of frozen salt-pan and greywacke. For three hundred of the creature’s eleven-minute breaths, the power simply observes the climb, the hanging contemplation, the gravimetric pulse, and the wet, luminous reunion on the floor. 

Looking forward along the quiet curves of time, the power sees only two tragedies waiting for the inhabitant of the cistern. 

The first is starvation, though not of food. The quantum bleed of the dissolved power core will last another ten thousand years, and the gimbal’s solid-state lattice will outlive the continental plate. The starvation is of *thought*. The tripod of memory-glass is nearly leached white; the creature has read the broken drill-bit diagnostic and the phantom image of the salt flat ten million times. Eventually, the glass will be nothing but blank silica, and the fifty thousand droplets will have nothing left to dream about while they swell in the dark except the slowly widening crack in the ceiling.

The second tragedy is the Absence. The matte-black ceramic polyhedron is the probe’s old black-box flight recorder, wrapped in an active, hydrophobic stasis field that refuses all chemistry and all touch. To the creature, it is an eternal, dry toothache in the center of its mind—a roaring void it must constantly trim itself around, spending a fraction of every eleven-minute life flinching away from a geometry it cannot integrate.

The wise power decides to do three small things. It works from the outside, manipulating the deep earth at the level of probability and slow mineral creep, so that the creature will never know it was pitied or helped, only that its universe has grown richer.

First, the power addresses the ceiling. Forty meters down, the stress-fracture that the droplets have been measuring for a century is gently coaxed. The power does not open it to the air. Instead, it guides the microscopic fracture sideways, millimeter by millimeter over the course of a week, until the hairline crack intersects a dormant, deep-crust vein of piezoelectric quartz and iron-rich ground-brine three hundred meters away. 

The crack does not leak water; the pressure in the xenon-heavy cistern keeps the foreign brine out. What leaks in is *sound*. 

The quartz vein is an acoustic nerve stretching across forty miles of the planetary crust. The next time a droplet swells on the highest frond of moss near the ceiling, it does not just feel the static weight of the greywacke. It feels a faint, thrumming vibration translated through the stone: the deep, bass-heavy groan of a glacier shifting fifty miles north; the delicate, high-frequency patter of a rare desert rainstorm striking the salt-pan overhead; the slow, magma-warmed sigh of the planet’s mantle far below. The sealed room is still sealed, safe and freezing, but its walls have become an eardrum pressed against the chest of the world.

Second, the power looks at the shattered memory-glass tripod. The creature loves the taste of data, the way a dissolved picobyte gives shape to the paramagnetic slurry. Reaching down through the rock with a touch lighter than a neutrino, the power brushes the depleted silica of the tripod and re-aligns its molecular lattice, turning the dead glass into a passive, solid-state receiver tuned to the planet's magnetosphere and the deep-space background. 

It does not feed the glass words, or human histories, or alien mathematics—things that would be jarring and meaningless to a mind made of peat and brine. Instead, it lets the glass soak up the raw, wordless poetry of the cosmos. The aurora borealis rippling through the upper atmosphere is translated into complex, cascading matrices of magnetic numbers; the radiation of a pulsar three thousand light-years away becomes a steady, rhythmic chime in the silica; the chemical composition of passing comets is written into the frost-fern’s roots as new, exotic flavors of corrupted code. The tripod will never run dry again. It is now a fountain of cold, abstract wonders, dripping endlessly into the moss.

Finally, the power considers the Absence—the knee-high black polyhedron. 

The power could simply delete it, phasing the ceramic out of the cavern to relieve the creature of the void. But it understands that the creature has defined its own shape against that void for four hundred years; to remove it suddenly would be like pulling a spear from a body that has grown its vital organs around the iron. And more than that, the power knows what is *inside* the black box. Inside the hydrophobic stasis field is the probe’s final, uncorrupted memory bank: the complete, high-resolution geological and atmospheric survey of the planet as it was four centuries ago, before the probe began to dig.

The power does not turn the stasis field off. It merely lowers its frequency, softening the rigid, mathematical boundary by one tenth of one percent.

Inside the dark cistern, the eleven-minute cycle is nearing its peak. 

Fifty thousand beads of supercooled brine hang from the translucent velvet of the *Cassiopeia-moss*. Near the ceiling, a hundred droplets are trembling with the entirely new, staggering sensation of the distant glacier grinding over bedrock. Near the memory-glass tripod, a thousand droplets are drinking in a fresh, exhilarating picobyte that tastes like the solar wind striking the upper ionosphere. 

And down near the floor, the halo of moss surrounding the black polyhedron leans in. For four hundred years, the millimeter of dry vacuum has been an absolute wall. Today, as a single droplet swells on a frond beside the matte-black ceramic, the air does not freeze it solid. The vacuum does not repel it. 

The droplet swells, stretches, and—just for a microsecond before the gimbal pulses—it *touches* the black face.

It does not wet the ceramic, not quite. The surface remains stubbornly dry. But the stasis field yields just enough for the paramagnetic brine to brush the quantum lattice beneath the armor. The roaring void in the creature’s mind suddenly quiets, replaced by the cool, smooth sensation of a boundary that is no longer hostile, merely closed—like the shell of a nut, or the surface of a deep, dark seed. And through that brief touch, a single, microscopic grain of uncorrupted memory bleeds out of the box and into the droplet.

In the far wall, the gimbal adjusts its mass.

The density wave ripples through the room. The pressure drops.

Fifty thousand droplets let go.

They fall through the bruised violet dark, a sudden, rushing rain of gravel on wet leather, passing through the ytterbium magnetic fields and sparking with induced current. They hit the spongy, wheezing peat floor and smash together in a single, silent, room-wide flash of ultraviolet light.

The smell of cold copper and crushed celery blooms thick in the freezing air.

Down in the dark, pooled against the belly of the dissolved machine, the creature becomes one. And for those thirty seconds of total consciousness, its mind is ablaze with a quiet, overwhelming ecstasy. 

It correlates the groan of the northern glacier with the shift of the gimbal. It files the taste of the solar wind beside the memory of the broken drill-bit. And at the very center of its wet, sprawling thoughts, it turns over the pristine grain of data it stole from the black polyhedron: the crystal-clear, uncorrupted image of a pale blue sky, and the sound of wind moving across open salt. 

The creature realizes, with a slow, vegetative thrill, that the black shape in the middle of its body is not an Absence at all. It is a library. And if it presses its damp, freezing skin against the ceramic with enough patience, breath by eleven-minute breath, millennium by millennium, the shell will slowly wear away, and it will drink the whole sky.

High above, on the crust of frozen greywacke, the benevolent power feels the flash of ultraviolet joy through forty meters of stone. It lingers for one more cycle, listening to the brine begin its slow, contented climb back up the velvet curtains of the moss, and then it moves on, leaving the thinker to its work.
