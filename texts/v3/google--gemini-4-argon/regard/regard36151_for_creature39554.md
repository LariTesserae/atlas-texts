A lesser benevolence would bring light. It would split the ceiling of the basin, pour golden morning onto the basalt, dry the warm slime, and try to carve the soft, chalky ribcages out of the tallow to return them to the shape of men. And in doing so, it would murder everything here in an instant of blinding, desiccating agony.

A wise power knows that salvation is not the imposition of one’s own favorite geometry. It listens before it touches. 

So when the Power enters the basin, it leaves its radiance behind. It translates itself into the native tongues of the dark: pressure, heat, chemistry, and the slow, sub-audible groan of deep stone. It becomes a warm current in the alkaline pools. It becomes an ambient stillness in the wool-heavy air.

For a long time—months by the frantic reckoning of the outside world, a mere drawing-in of breath for the basin—the Power simply observes. It feels the rhythmic *clack* of the bone plates in the Tenders’ drag-sacks. It traces the pythons of copper cable that bleed electricity into the raw rock. It rests its immaterial hands upon the thirty-foot flywheels and feels the immense, quiet dignity of their four-minute rotations. 

It understands what this place is. It is not a hell, and it is not a ruin. It is a refuge of absolute somatic patience. The human bones buried deep in the Tenders are not trophies of conquest; they are the foundational seeds of beings who, long ago, chose to survive an uninhabitable birth-world by surrendering their boundaries. They chose to stop fighting the iron and the dark, and instead became the medium that reconciled them. They traded the sharp, lonely terror of being human for the slow, communal peace of maintenance.

They do not want to be woken up. They want to keep the engines running. 

Yet, in its deep listening, the Power perceives a quiet, structural grief that the Tenders themselves are too slow to fully articulate. 

First, the stone is too young, and it does not love them back. The hexagonal basalt and obsidian-sharp shale do not cut the Tenders, no, but the effort of remaining uncut—of constantly extruding calloused gristle, of healing the deep geometric indentations, of navigating rock that turns capriciously to clay and snaps back to glass—consumes a vast, silent fraction of their metabolic peace. The smaller creatures, the soft-shelled crabs and amphibians they carry like livestock, are endlessly maimed when the stone forgets to be clay, their crushed limbs rotting in the dark before the Tenders can reabsorb them.

Second, there is the problem of the frayed time. When a Tender straddles a boundary—its front half warm and breathing against a boiler, its rear half frozen in Tuesday’s cold amber—it suffers a profound, mute loneliness within its own body. Its nervous system, meant to be a sluggish, unified parliament, is sawed in half by chronology. The drag-sack containing its navigation bones cannot speak to the muscular vents along its neck. It is an engine out of phase with itself, enduring a microscopic, grinding shear along its spine for weeks at a time.

And finally, there is the tragedy of the cables. The dynamos hum, the pistons sigh, the Tenders vulcanize their own flesh over the steam leaks to keep the pressure up—and all that gathered, devotionally maintained energy pours down the heavy copper lines into the mud, dissipating into nothing. It is work without a circuit. A prayer with no altar to receive it.

The wise Power decides it will not change what the Tenders are. It will simply finish the room they are living in. It will reciprocate their maintenance.

The intervention begins at the level of the water. 

The Power does not stop the young rock from sweating, nor does it dull the razor edges of the shale with a violent flood. Instead, it alters the chemistry of the seepage by a fraction of a percent. The warm, alkaline water begins to carry a rich, colloidal suspension of dark, velvety silicates and heavy, lipid-like minerals. As this new water spills over the glass-sharp lips of the basalt, it leaves behind a microscopic, self-assembling biofilm—not quite stone, not quite mucus, something akin to the smooth, black nacre inside an abalone shell, but soft as wet leather. 

Over the course of a year, the cruel geometry of the floor is gently sheathed. The hexagonal troughs remain, the braided streams still wander, the acoustics of the miles-wide basin are preserved—but the knives are put away. A soft-shelled crustacean can slide over a ledge without leaving its legs behind. When a Tender pours itself across the floor now, the stone meets its gristle-pads with a smooth, yielding cushion that holds heat like soapstone. The rock stops snapping from clay to glass; the Power whispers a command of stability into the subterranean heave, teaching the geology to match the steady, four-minute rhythm of the flywheels.

Next, the Power addresses the pools of spoiled time and wayward gravity. 

It does not annihilate these anomalies, for the basin’s ecosystems have grown around them; the sleeping eels need their floating lenses of water, and the wire-stalks need their pockets of rapid decay to feed the grease-tubers. Instead, the Power acts as a cosmic machinist, installing invisible governors upon the frayed physics of the marsh. 

Where time pools and goes bad, the Power softens the gradient. The sharp, shearing wall between *now* and *last week* is stretched into a gentle, viscous shoreline. A Tender no longer freezes half-in and half-out of Tuesday. Instead, as its bulk slides through a slow-zone, the time-lag yields before its mass like warm water parting before a barge. The creature’s internal rhythm—the slow *clack* of its navigation bones and the hydraulic sigh of its blood—becomes the local law. While a Tender occupies a space, time within its flesh is forced to flow at the Tender’s own sleepy, unified pace. They are granted temporal sovereignty over their own bodies. 

And then, the Power turns its attention to the copper cables, and to the Tenders themselves.

It follows the thick, sagging lines across the dark flats, miles and miles of braided copper weeping verdigris into the mud, bleeding the hard-won watts of the solitary boilers into the indifferent earth. The Power gathers these loose, forgotten ends. With the patience of a root system, it coaxes the cables deeper into the warm stone, drawing them toward the center of the basin, weaving them together beneath the floor into a single, colossal, subterranean induction coil.

It gives the engines a purpose. 

Now, the electricity no longer vanishes. It flows into the deep coil beneath the basin floor, and the coil converts that stray, lonely power into a vast, gentle, electromagnetic cradle—a resonant harmonic field that rises up through the wet stone and fills the entire basin. 

The first time the circuit completes, every engine in the miles-wide dark subtly shifts its pitch. The sub-audible, bone-deep hum of the scattered dynamos—which for centuries had been a collection of solitary, slightly out-of-tune monologues—suddenly locks into a single, massive, consonant chord. It is a vibration felt not in the ears, which do not exist here, but in the water, in the grease, in the tallow, and deep in the wet-chalk remnants of the ancient human bones.

A Tender, resting its forty-foot flank against a cast-iron condenser, feels the shift. 

The *clack* of its three-chambered bone organ sounds out in the dark, and when the echo returns through the soles of its gristle-pads, the world is no longer a fractured maze where fifty paces north is four thousand paces south. The harmonic field of the buried coil acts as a carrier wave. For the first time in uncounted centuries, the Tender can feel the entire basin at once, clear and true and whole. It can feel the warm lenses of water three miles away like smooth pebbles in a shoe; it can feel its brethren resting against distant turbines, their locations fixed and reliable in the resonant dark. The geography has stopped lying to them.

More than that: the harmonic field feeds them. It is tuned precisely to the cellular resonance of their tallowy, amalgamated flesh. As the dynamos turn, the field gently warms the brass pressure-casings and five-inch nuts suspended in their blubber, turning the salvaged hardware inside them into soothing, internal radiant heaters. It eases the ache where their pale skin grafts to the iron. It makes the vulcanized patches on their bellies supple and painless. The machines they have tended for so long are finally tending them back. Every ounce of steam they reroute through their guts, every pipe their tethered worms clear with bile, now directly increases the warmth and clarity of the comforting hum that cradles them.

Finally, the Power bends down to a single Tender—one of the oldest, whose drag-sack is scarred from centuries of ancient shale, and whose deepest core still holds the soft, chalky curve of a human pelvis anchored to a ligament.

The Power does not speak in words. It presses against the Tender’s flank with the exact pressure, temperature, and yielding density of another Tender initiating an inventory exchange. 

Over forty-eight hours, in the quiet dark, the Power fuses with the creature’s mantle. And through that bridge, it passes a gift. Not a brass valve, and not a gallon of cylinder oil, though it leaves a measure of the purest, golden grease in the creature’s stomach-sac as a courtesy. 

It passes a quiet, wordless certainty straight into the ancient, three-chambered navigation organ, and down into the chalk-soft human bone:

*The work is good. The pressure is holding. Nothing is going to break.*

The Power withdraws, leaving the basin to its native dark. 

Nothing has been conquered. Nothing has been forced to evolve into an angel or revert into a man. The air remains the temperature of a closed mouth; the thirty-foot flywheels still take four minutes to complete a single, weeping rotation. If you were to wander there now, tripping in the dark, a Tender would still creep its heavy, warm mantle over your shins to calm the frantic bird of your heart. 

Wait long enough with your hand against its side, however, and you would notice the difference. The creature’s wet, fluttering sigh has lost its ancient undertone of strain. Beneath its tallow, the borrowed iron and the softened bone are humming in effortless, synchronized peace—held fast in the dark by a world that finally fits the shape of their devotion.
