A lesser benevolence would look upon the chimney and see a purgatory. A softer god would feel pity for the creatures of bone and copper who must scream in the silence to be heard, who must risk absolute zero to trim their garden, who spend their whole lives terrified of falling into the dark. A clumsy power might send rain to soothe the dry stone, or still the sixty-mile-an-hour gale to give the nineteen a moment of rest—and in doing so, murder the entire cathedral in an afternoon.

The power that finds the massif is neither clumsy nor soft. It is old enough to know that grace must match the geometry of the vessel receiving it. 

For three days, the presence simply observes from the pale, crisp sky at the lip of the nave. It has no body, only an immense, attentive weight that does not displace the air. It listens to the concussive roar of the bronze curtains. It watches the skimmers tack and spark against the bleached basalt. It studies the nineteen taut-kin as they shear through the hurricane, marveling at the fierce, spare poetry of their survival. It listens to their delicate, clicking conversations inside the dead cubes of the harvesters.

It sees that the taut-kin do not suffer from the wind; they *are* the wind, given a nerve and a hook. 

Yet, the wise power also sees the flaw in the chimney. It looks past the glowing bladders and the bismuth coffins, down, miles down, into the unseen, lightless abyss where the wind is born. 

Down there, at the root of the massif, where subterranean tectonic heat meets a frozen aquifer of heavy gases, the updraft is generated. And down there, resting on a ledge of shattered slate where the gale is still young and turbulent, lies the dry, broken chassis of the twentieth taut-kin, who fell six years ago. Below that one lies the twenty-first. And deeper still, pulverized into copper dust and ceramic meal, are the hundreds who came before, alongside the dead, frozen matter dropped by the machines. 

The taut-kin have no graves, and they have no history, because everything they lose falls down, and everything that dies is spat out the top. If an elder slips during the high-noon roulette, all the songs and maintenance tricks stored in its tuning-fork skull are lost to the dark. Their population hovers at nineteen because their margin of error is the width of an iron crampon.

The benevolent power decides it will not change the game. It will only change the floor.

On the fourth day, during the low-light hours when the wind is steady and cold, the power descends into the shaft. To the taut-kin, its arrival registers not as a sight, but as a sudden, breathtaking harmonic. 

The chaotic, corrugated-tin roar of the nave briefly aligns. For the space of ten heartbeats, every thrashing bronze frond and every screaming ninety-degree wind-shear hits a single, massive, subterranean chord—a fifth so pure and resonant that the ceramic bones of all nineteen taut-kin hum in sympathy, charging them with a warm, effortless static that requires no friction. Inside the silent cubes, the elders unfold their thoracic fans in astonishment, tasting an acoustic shadow shaped like an open hand.

The power sinks past them, dropping into the lightless throat of the abyss where the taut-kin dare not go.

There, two thousand feet below the lowest bronze curtain, where the basalt walls narrow into a choking funnel, the power goes to work. It does not build a floor—a floor would choke the updraft. Instead, it gathers the fallen. 

It collects the bone-cold, deadened dust dropped by the harvesters over epochs. It gathers the shattered mica plates, the oxidized copper ribbons, and the iron-horn crampons of every taut-kin that ever lost its grip and tumbled into the dark. Using the immense barometric pressure of the deep vent, it fuses this fallen heritage into the walls of the narrow throat, extruding a series of wide, spiral baffles—ribs of polished, copper-veined obsidian that corkscrew up the dark shaft for half a mile.

Then, the power reaches up to the twelve bismuth machines. It does not break them—for the taut-kin need their silent cubes and their pruning beams—but with a whisper of higher mathematics, it adjusts their ancient, drifting calibration by a fraction of a degree. 

It links them.

Previously, when a harvester stripped the kinetic energy from a cloud of dust or a tether of wire-root, it simply dumped that stolen motion into some dimensional sink, dropping the deadened matter into the abyss. Now, the power redirects the exhaust. The stolen kinetic energy of all twelve machines is routed downward, focused into a single, invisible column of hyper-dense, upward-rushing force at the very center of the deep spiral baffles.

The work takes a single night. When the pale mountain dawn breaks, the power is gone, leaving the chimney to its masters.

At first, nothing seems different. The wind still tears upward at sixty miles an hour. The cicada-shriek of the sheared air still vibrates behind the teeth. The skimmers still ricochet and spark. 

Then, the youngest of the nineteen makes a mistake.

It is hunting skimmers near the third harvester down. It misjudges an eddy, its crampon strikes a slick patch of unweathered bismuth, and the ninety-degree shear clips its left sail. The young taut-kin is spun backward. Its hooks tear free of the basalt. 

Above it, two elders leaning into a silent cube feel the jagged acoustic shadow of the fall. They freeze, their thoracic fans trembling. They know the math of the shaft. The young one is too heavy for the ambient gale to catch, and tumbling too fast to tack against the walls. It drops past the lowest bronze curtains. It drops past the glowing bladders. It vanishes into the roaring dark below. The nineteen are now eighteen.

Except the young one does not hit the slate at the bottom of the mountain.

As it plummets into the narrowing throat of the abyss, the newly carved spiral baffles catch the chaotic eddies of its fall, spinning the creature gently toward the exact center of the dark shaft. And there, it hits the exhaust of the twelve machines—a newly born geyser of pure, reclaimed kinetic force.

The updraft at the center of the deep throat is no longer sixty miles an hour; it is two hundred. 

The falling taut-kin hits this column of dense, rising air like a diver hitting deep water. Its descent is arrested in a long, screaming cushion of pressure. And then, it is hurled back upward. 

As it shoots up through the half-mile corkscrew of obsidian baffles, the rushing air passes over the fused copper ribbons and ceramic bones of its ancestors built into the rock. The baffles are shaped like giant tuning forks. As the gale tears through them, they sing. 

It is a deep, thrumming, metallic bassline—a foundational drone that rises up from the abyss and underpins the frantic, high-pitched shriek of the upper nave. And encoded in that bassline, preserved in the acoustic geometry of the baffles, are the resonant frequencies of every elder whose bones were used to build them. It is a library of pressure and pitch.

The two elders waiting in the silent cube above suddenly snap their tuning-fork heads toward the floorless dark. 

They feel the new bassline vibrating through the stone. And riding on top of that immense, ancestral chord, shooting up out of the dark like a spear of dark whalebone and copper, is the young taut-kin. It bursts back into the dim light of the nave, bleeding speed as the shaft widens, until it effortlessly tacks across the air and drives all four crampons into the basalt wall, ten feet above where it fell. 

It is unharmed. Its porous bones are singing with a strange, deep static it picked up in the dark—old knowledge, old rhythms, the memory of the abyss turned into altitude.

When high noon arrives, and the twenty minutes of blinding mountain light flood the stone tube, the nineteen do not just play their game of roulette at the top of the shaft. 

They lock their iron crampons together into their great, jagged wheel. They spread their stolen skimmer-sails. And then, deliberately, as one, they fold their sails and drop into the dark. 

They fall for a thousand feet into the screaming black, drinking the terrifying speed, listening to the copper-ribbed walls of the deep throat sing the history of their kind—before spreading their wings in the heart of the central geyser and letting the machines hurl them all the way back up into the blinding sun, glowing white-hot, immortal, and unafraid of the deep.
