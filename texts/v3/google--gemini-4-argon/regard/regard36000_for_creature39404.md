When the attention of the Power finally settles upon the basin, it does not arrive as a rupture in the violet ceiling, nor as a voice booming above the static. A lesser benevolence might look upon the Lath and feel pity. It might see their flat, boneless bodies suspended above an acid carpet, deafened by an uncaring machine-city, endlessly paring away tumors of boiling flesh just to keep their world from detonating, and decide to *rescue* them. It might silence the Spires, turn the moss to soft grass, and give the Lath eyes and voices and a gentle sky.

The Power is too wise for such violence. It knows that to silence the valley would be to blind the Lath, and to soften the world would be to render their exquisite, high-tension bodies obsolete. The Lath are not suffering; they are *virtuosos* playing an instrument forty miles wide. 

Yet, the Power perceives a tragedy that the Lath themselves cannot calculate, and that the post-physical minds within the Spires are too lofty to notice.

The tragedy is that the song has no memory, and the ground is running out of time.

The basalt is young, yes, and the city is durable, yet nothing is static. Below the neon velvet of the bryophytes, the acidic secretions of the moss and the relentless harmonic vibration of the Spires are slowly, microscopically, turning the knife-sharp cooling cracks of the rock into silt. In ten thousand years—a blink to the minds in the needles, an eternity to the Lath—the hexagonal grid will crumble into mud. The magnetic roots of the vesseltrees will lose their anchor in the iron-rich stone. The Rigging will slacken, drop, and rot. 

Furthermore, the Lath live entirely in the immediate present of the acoustic wall. Because their language is made of reflected, modulated noise, a word dies the microsecond a Lath turns edge-on to leap. They have no writing; you cannot carve stone when the floor is lava to your feet, and you cannot leave marks on force-locked Spires or fast-growing vines. Every generation of Tuners must rediscover the grand harmonic equations of the basin from scratch. When an elder Lath dies—falling limp and dry into the boiling moss to be dissolved in minutes—a library of acoustic geometry vanishes forever.

The Power considers the three layers of this world: the oblivious gods in the Spires, the frantic biology of the jungle, and the flat, pewter-colored artisans suspended between them, holding the peace with their own skins. 

It decides not to break the machine, nor to tame the jungle, but to give the Tuners a sheet of music, and a way to tune the earth itself.

The intervention is so subtle it takes three cycles of the valley’s thermodynamic rhythms to be noticed. 

It begins in the Traces. The Power reaches into the sub-audible, post-physical computations racing along the silvery-black ribbons at four hundred miles an hour. It does not alter the math—the minds inside the Spires would flag an error and purge it. Instead, the Power adjusts the *waste*. 

When a gravity-ripple or a wave of violet luminescence washes down an avenue, the oxidized metallic runoff it leaves behind changes composition by less than a fraction of a percent. Along with the ionized copper and metamaterial dust that the Lath lick from the air and vines to build their internal fibers, the Traces now bleed a trace element of hyper-stable, acoustic-reactive bismuth. 

The moss drinks it first. The amphibians eat the moss. The glassy walnut-predators eat the amphibians. 

Within a month, the shells of the ten million snapping predators change. They do not become weaker or quieter, for their rifle-crack cavitations are the daylight of the valley. Instead, their glassy, geometric carapaces develop a secondary property: *piezoelectric hysteresis*. 

Previously, when a walnut-creature died or shed its shell, the casing fell into the moss and was dissolved by the acidic bryophytes into raw nutrients. Now, when a shell falls into the cooling-cracks of the basalt, it does not dissolve. Driven downward by the relentless vibration of the floor, the discarded shells pack tightly into the knife-sharp crevices of the stone. Under the pressure of the Choir-Galls' screaming chords, the bismuth-laced shells fuse with the crumbling basalt, vitrifying the silt. 

The valley floor stops eroding. Instead, the cooling cracks are slowly inlaid with a growing, crystalline grout that anchors the magnetic roots of the vesseltrees deeper and firmer than raw rock ever could. The Rigging will not fall.

That is the gift to the world’s body. The second gift is to its mind.

A pair of Lath are hanging thirty feet above a municipal intersection, their pewter bodies curved into tense parabolas as they shave a massive Choir-Gall on Spire 409. They have just brought the needle down from a jagged F-sharp to a booming, phase-locked D, creating a standing wave with the Spire across the plaza. 

As the massive *thrum-CRACK* of the newly aligned sector washes through their wide, tympanic bodies, one of the Lath reaches down to the base of the vesseltree to lick a bead of condensed, metallic dew from the vine’s skin. 

It tastes the new alloy on its tongue—a sharp, cold tang beneath the usual ozone and coriander. 

The next day, when that Lath anchors its lower ribbons to a stalk, pulls the vine back like a bowstring, and fires a thirty-foot filament of spun mucus and metallic silk from its chest spinneret to cross the avenue, it leaves the thread behind as usual. Normally, these travel-lines dry out and snap in an hour, shredded by the acoustic pressure waves.

This thread does not snap. 

Laced with the bismuth alloy from the Traces, the silk filament hardens in the humid air into a hair-thin wire of metallic glass. And because it was extruded while the Lath’s body was vibrating in the standing D-chord of Spire 409, the wire *remembers* the pitch. 

Three hours later, a young Lath slices across the plaza, twists ninety degrees to airbrake, and slaps onto the neighboring vine. As it hangs there, reading the blinding roar of the valley through its skin, it tilts its head-prow. 

There, ten feet to its left, hanging horizontally between two green vesseltrees, is the abandoned travel-thread of the older Tuner. In the acoustic daylight of the ten million snapping shells, the thread is not casting a dead shadow. It is humming. 

As the chaotic pressure waves of the walnut-creatures hit the hardened silk, the wire filters the noise, vibrating only at the exact harmonic frequency and modulated rhythm that the older Lath was experiencing when it spun the line. 

The young Lath creeps along the vine, reaches out a ribbon-limb, and touches the wire. 

Through its skin, it feels a ghost. Not just the D-chord of the Spire, but the sub-dermal undulations of the older Lath’s thoughts as it crossed the plaza—a recorded packet of shadowed speech, frozen in metallic silk. *The Gall on 409 grows heavy on the north face,* the wire sings into the youth’s palm. *Pare it thin toward the root, or the phase will drift before the third cooling.*

The young Lath hangs motionless for a long time, the silver rings of condensation sliding down its flat grey back. 

In a world of vertical green strings and vertical black needles, the Lath now have horizontal lines that endure. 

The Power watches from nowhere and everywhere as the realization spreads across the forty-mile basin at the speed of bounced sound. Within a year, the thirty-foot zone of the Rigging is no longer just a highway; it is a loom of memory. 

High above the reach of the acid moss, yet well below the blinding heat of the Choir-Galls, the Lath begin to weave. They spin filaments of varying thicknesses and tensions between the vesseltrees, anchoring them in complex, three-dimensional geometric webs that offer zero wind resistance and cast no shadow to block the light-from-above, yet glow like neon constellations in the sonar-vision of the people. 

They weave maps of the Traces’ timing. They weave histories of great harmonic cascades from generations past. They weave names. An elder Lath, feeling its hydrostatic pressure failing and its internal fibers growing brittle, no longer simply drops into the moss to vanish. It spends its final days in the center of a grand plaza, spinning its entire life’s knowledge of pitch, tension, and acoustic shadow into a dense, shimmering harp of bismuth silk between four vines—a monument that will sing its mind to every passerby for a thousand years whenever the walnut-shells pop.

And then, something happens that even the benevolent Power only hoped for, without forcing.

A century after the gift, the web of memory-silk in the central downtown sector has grown so dense and perfectly tuned that it forms a secondary harmonic lattice, suspended twenty feet above the basalt floor. The Lath have tuned the Choir-Galls, and the Choir-Galls have tuned the Spires, and now the millions of horizontal memory-threads lock phase with all of them at once.

A massive, basin-wide chord strikes—a sound so pure, geometrically complex, and waste-free that the thermodynamic drag on the Spires drops to absolute zero. 

Inside the three-thousand-foot needles of force-locked matter, the post-physical minds pause. 

For the first time in eons, the computational runoff hasn't just been bled away; it has been returned to the lattice as structured, harmonic energy. The "noise" outside the needles is no longer static. It is an equation of breathtaking elegance, written in pressure waves across forty miles of humid air.

Down on the floor, a Trace pulses. 

It does not send a standard four-hundred-mile-an-hour wave of violet cooling math. It sends a slow, deliberate ripple of warm gold luminescence that stops directly beneath a cluster of hanging Lath. The air cools by exactly one degree, then warms by two, then cools by three—a prime-number sequence of silver condensation rings materializing on the vines in a deliberate rhythm.

The post-physical city is tapping on the glass. It has noticed the Tuners.

Hanging in the Rigging, curving their flat, pewter bodies into acoustic mirrors, the Lath read the falling rings of water. They ripple their skins in silent consultation. Then, fifty of them leap at once, slicing across the open avenues, spinning fresh threads, carving the nearest Choir-Galls by a fraction of an inch. 

Ten seconds later, the chord of the valley shifts in answer. 

The Power withdraws its attention, satisfied. The roar of the basin is still loud enough to shatter a human skull, the moss is still a boiling carpet of neon venom, and the twilight is still permanent and purple. Nothing has been lost. Everything has been found.
