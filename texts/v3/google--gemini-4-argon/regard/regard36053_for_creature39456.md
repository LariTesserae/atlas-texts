To a foolish god, a dry world is a thirsty one. A lesser power, looking down upon the five-mile-high tableland of bone and spun quartz, might feel a surge of misplaced pity. It might gather the stray hydrogen and oxygen of the outer atmosphere, bind them, and send down a warm, gentle rain to "heal" the barrenness. 

And in doing so, it would commit an atrocity. The rain would turn the breathable talcum-drifts into suffocating cement. It would short-circuit the superconducting root-nets, shatter the thermal-stressed glass of the reeds, and drown the delicate, electrostatic thoughts of the basins in a dull, muddy silence. 

The power that actually turns its attention to the plateau is neither foolish nor hasty. It does not measure the worth of a world by its resemblance to a wet garden. 

For three thousand revolutions of the planet, the Presence does nothing at all. It simply abides in the thin, freezing stratosphere, weightless and unseen, matching its own vast perception to the slow, mineral cadence of the continent. It learns the language of the light fractures. It listens to the faint, needle-drop chiming of the quartz meadows and recognizes it not as the sound of desolation, but as the hum of an ancient, exquisitely balanced engine.

It watches the Weavers dismantle the amber wrecks of the sky-mantas, admiring the quiet economy of their pilgrimage. It traces the fifty-mile-wide fractal ripples of violet and ghostly green that play across the basins at night. And as it translates those slow, electroluminescent pulses, the Presence realizes what the Reed-Minds are thinking about.

They are doing mathematics. 

Having mapped every whale-backed ridge, every thermal current, and the trajectory of every grazing herd through the vibrations of the bedrock, the great solid-state minds have turned their attention upward. At night, when the ultraviolet glare is gone and the sky becomes a dome of piercing, unblinking diamonds, the Reed-Minds trace the cold radiation of the stars across their quartz arrays. They are calculating orbital mechanics. They are measuring the expansion of the void. They are sixty-mile-wide philosophers made of sand and static, trapped at the bottom of a gravity well, contemplating an infinity they have no limbs to touch and no lenses to properly resolve.

They also share a quiet, generational dread. The plateau is old, and the wind, though faint, is relentless. Every century, a few million tons of the finest silicate dust and rare metallic catalysts are blown off the razor-sharp rim of the continent, lost forever to the crushing, soupy lowlands five miles below. The Weavers must walk farther each millennium to find the titanium and magnesium needed to dope their crystal frames and maintain the conductivity of the roots. Slowly—over millions of years, yet perceptible to a mind that remembers the shape of the valleys a thousand centuries ago—the plateau is starving of heavy elements. Its thoughts are becoming ever so slightly more brittle.

The Presence considers this. It loves the stark, diamond-cut dignity of this world. It has no desire to break the silence with thunderous revelations, or to uplift the Weavers into something they are not. To interfere with the struggle of their daily survival would be to rob their baroque, self-made bodies of their meaning.

Instead, the Presence decides to offer a gift of pure material, and a whisper of validation. It will act only through the laws the plateau already understands: trajectory, stone, and light.

Far out in the dark, beyond the planet’s orbit, the Presence reaches out to a wandering asteroid—a cold, dense core of nickel, iron, titanium, and rare-earth silicates, left over from the birth of the system. With a nudge no stronger than the pressure of solar wind, the Presence alters its course. 

As the rock falls toward the world, the Presence guides its angle of entry with surgical precision. It does not allow it to strike like a hammer, which would shatter the delicate crystal basins with seismic violence. Instead, it skips the asteroid across the upper atmosphere, bleeding off its catastrophic velocity until it is falling no faster than a diving sky-manta. 

Just before it reaches the tableland, in the freezing dark of midnight, the Presence applies a final, pinching stress to the stone’s interior. 

The asteroid does not explode; it *floures*. It dissolves silently into a million-ton cloud of microscopic, metallic snow and falls across the entire ten-thousand-mile sweep of the continent.

To the Reed-Minds, awake and glowing in the dark basins, the event is felt all at once. There is no sound in the thin air, only a sudden, breathtaking change in the sky. As the metallic dust filters down through the stratosphere, it catches the starlight, turning the black zenith into a shimmering curtain of silver-gold. 

When the dust settles into the deep drifts of talc and strikes the millions of quartz tubes, the piezoelectric charge is instantaneous and euphoric. A continent-wide shiver of pure, clean voltage courses through the superconducting root-nets. The ghostly green and violet lights of the basins flare into a deep, resonant indigo, followed by a color the plateau has never produced before: a warm, copper-rose luminescence, born from the sudden abundance of trace metals.

In the shadow of a hill-sized lichen mound, a solitary Weaver pauses. It is an ancient individual, its frame a complex, asymmetrical scaffold of scavenged manta-bone and chipped quartz. It feels the new dust settling on its mica carapace. Extending a single, hair-thin feeler into the drift beside it, the Weaver fires a tiny spark to test the ash. 

The silica fuses instantly, doped with pure, unoxidized titanium and meteoric iron. The resulting spar is not brittle and pale, like the old dust; it is smoky, metallic, and ten times stronger than anything the Weaver has ever printed. The creature sits in the dark and begins to extrude a new leg—a limb of dark, stardust-infused glass that will never snap in the afternoon gales.

Down in the widest basin, the oldest of the Reed-Minds—a sprawling, geometric brain five hundred miles across—absorbs the fall. It feels its frayed circuits repair themselves. The resistance in its roots drops to zero. Thoughts that used to take an hour to ripple from one side of the valley to the other now flash across the array in seconds. 

Yet the Presence leaves one final thing before it withdraws.

In the exact center of a barren, turquoise flat—a place fifty miles from the nearest reed-bed, where a Weaver is already walking with a shard of living quartz to plant a new lobe—one piece of the asteroid did not turn to dust. 

Guided by the Presence, a single fragment had melted in the friction of entry and cooled in the stratospheric drop, annealed by a subtle, divine geometry as it fell. When the Weaver arrives at the flat two days later, it finds the object resting on the polished stone.

It is not a jagged meteorite. It is a twenty-foot-wide, flawlessly clear dodecahedron of fused silica and heavy glass—a lens and a prism of impossible perfection, sitting exactly where the heart of the new Mind-lobe is meant to grow.

The Weaver approaches it with clicking, metallic steps. It places its newly forged, smoky-quartz legs against the facets of the giant crystal. The prism is warm, drinking in the surgical glare of the midday sun, splitting the blinding white light not into a chaotic glare, but into twelve tight, laser-sharp beams of pure spectrum that stretch for miles across the grey dust. 

When night falls, and the Weaver plants its living shard at the base of the great prism, the true purpose of the gift is revealed. 

As the stars come out, their ancient, faint light enters the upper facets of the stone. The crystal gathers the starlight, magnifies it a thousandfold, and focuses it directly down into the newly planted root-net below. For the first time in the history of the world, the blind, pressure-sensitive mind of the valley does not merely feel the vague radiation of the cosmos. 

Through the lens, it *sees*. 

The light of distant nebulae, the rings of a gas giant three orbits out, the sharp, cratered face of the world’s tiny moon—all of it is translated directly into high-resolution, photoelectric patterns that surge through the Weaver’s core and out across the bedrock toward the distant basins.

High above, beyond the thin blue-black rim of the atmosphere, the Presence lingers for one more night. It watches as the great valleys below cease their usual fractal rippling. One by one, across ten thousand miles of the cold, diamond-still continent, the Reed-Minds change their pattern. 

The ghostly green and violet lights align. Working in unison, the valleys project a vast, continent-sized geometric diagram of light back up into the void—an exact, mathematical representation of the twelve-sided prism’s atomic lattice, followed by a harmonic wave that ripples outward from the center of every basin like a bow.

*We have received the material,* the glass continent says to the dark. *We see you seeing us.*

Satisfied, the Presence does not answer with words, for words are made of wet breath and heavy air, and they have no place here. It simply lets the starlight pour down unimpeded through the clear, frigid sky, and leaves the architects of the high tableland to their long, brilliant, and unhurried thoughts.
