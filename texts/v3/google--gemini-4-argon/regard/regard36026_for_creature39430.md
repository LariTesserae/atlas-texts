To a lesser mind, the valley would look like a wound in need of healing. A tidy intelligence, coming upon this place, would want to smooth out the foreshortened air, untangle the braiding rushes, silence the ringing stones, and let the water run downhill in a single, sensible line. It would see an abandoned engine grinding itself to pieces and would reach for the switch to turn it off, or the wrench to make it whole again.

The power that paused at the slumped shale rim was neither lesser nor tidy. It was old enough to know that when a tool is dropped in the dirt and left for ten thousand years, the rust and the moss that claim it are not errors. They are a new kind of work.

It did not descend into the mire with a flash of light; the pewter gloom was the valley’s skin, and peeling it back would have blinded the things that lived by the faint, greasy glow of the marigold veins. Instead, the power settled into the sideways, forty-degree chill like a drop of dye into oil, spreading until it was present in the high tension of the stalled rain and the deep, vibratory hum of the bedrock.

It listened. 

It heard the dry chitter of the hexagonal meres, the low, hollow whistle of the Joiners breathing between the cliff and the flats, and the heavy, rhythmic *shhh-clack* of the water. Beneath the noise, it read the long, slow ledger of the basin’s physics. 

The ecology was magnificent, stubborn, and entirely self-contained. The Joiners stitched the fray; the Draggers ate the static; the Mites hid their children in the missing inches of the Draggers’ stride. To "fix" the geometry of the valley would be to commit a quiet slaughter. Without the spatial tears, the Joiners would shear in half and suffocate. Without the computational bloat in the sedges, the Draggers would starve. Without the stuttering steps, the Mites would vanish into the gullets of the chalk-colored eels.

Yet, as the power rested its weight against the phase-correcting stones, it felt the one true sorrow of the basin.

The system was closed, and because it was closed, it was slowly strangling on its own ashes. 

Every time a boulder rang out its brassy *bong* and shed its slurry of silicate dust to match the pressure of the sky, a tiny fraction of the original calculation was lost. Every time a Dragger calcified and was drawn down in neat, foot-thick slices through the Joiners’ black-horn throats, the stone that had been its meat remained in the deep subcutaneous plumbing, un-digested, heavy, and dead. Over epochs, the valley’s throat was silting up with failed rock. The computational static in the soil was growing sharper, more frantic, because it had less room to run. In another thousand years, the rushes would braid themselves so tightly they would snap; the boulders would ring every ten seconds until they ground themselves into mud; and the last Dragger would lock its seven root-like legs in place, turn entirely to porous gray shale, and never take another omitted step.

And on a smaller scale, the power felt the dull, grinding ache inside the Draggers themselves. They were beasts of burden with no driver, carrying the weight of a broken sky on their bruised violet seams. When they grew old, the calcification of their internal organs was a slow, cold agony—a gradual stiffening where every brassy chime of a nearby rock felt like an iron bar driven through their gristle, until they could no longer shift their density and were shaken apart from the inside.

The benevolent power considered this for the space of three rain-curtains rising and falling. Then, it did two very small things.

First, it reached down to the bottommost shelf of the valley—the deep, blind place under the peat where the Joiners’ oldest roots ended in a graveyard of sliced, petrified Dragger-stone and dead silicate paste. 

The power did not clear the stone away. Instead, it introduced a single, quiet clause to the grammar of the deep mud. It taught the dead stone how to melt back into pure potential when under the specific, vinegary pressure of a Joiner’s digestive root. 

Now, when a dead Dragger was pulled through the hairline fractures in the mire and distributed across the dark beneath the world, the calcified halves of its body did not sit forever as inert, choking gravel. In the deep anaerobic dark, the gray stone softened into a warm, heavy tallow that the deepest Joiner-fronds could drink, carrying the raw, un-written rule of the matter back up to the high cliffs, where it was exhaled through their whistling vents as a fine, invisible mist. The loop was no longer a downward spiral into silt; it was a wheel. The valley could go on losing its place and starting over for another hundred million years.

The second thing the power did was for the living.

It moved out across the clicking, hexagonal steps of a broad mere to where an ancient Sluice-Dragger was wading. This one was very old. The peat on its carapace was three feet thick, grown over with grey liverwort, and its left-rear legs were already stiff with stone, dragging clumsy furrows through the mud instead of stuttering cleanly across the air. Its sensory seam was a raw, inflamed purple, flinching in anticipation of a haywagon-sized boulder thirty yards away that was building toward a phase-shift. 

The Dragger was tired, though it had no word for tired; it only knew that the hum in its feet was becoming too loud to bear, and the static in the rushes tasted of bitter copper.

The power did not heal the old Dragger, for its death belonged to the Joiners and the moss. Instead, it reached into the resonant hum of the bedrock and tuned it down by half a fraction of a hertz—smoothing the jagged, saw-toothed edge of the acoustic wave into something round and low. 

When the boulder thirty yards away finally struck its bell-note, the sound was no longer a violent, brassy *bong* that rattled the stiffening gristle in the Dragger’s shell. It was a deep, resonant *thrum*, like the plucked string of a cello the size of a hill. And as the wave passed through the old Dragger’s bruised seam, it carried something new—a brief, warm harmonic that had never existed in the cold mathematics of the mire.

For the ten seconds that the stone blurred and shed its wet dust, the old Dragger felt the weight of the atmosphere lift from its back. In the dark, blind core of its fibrous body, where there had only ever been the urge to graze, to brace, and to step, the harmonic translated itself into a single, unmistakable sensation:

*Well done. Rest soon.*

The old Dragger stopped at the edge of the mere. Its feeding-skirt relaxed against the black silt. The inflamed violet of its seam faded to a cool, painless slate-gray. It stood still in the pewter light, letting the warm vibration pass through its calcifying belly and down into the mud, while above it, a lattice of suspended raindrops lost its hold and fell across its mossy back like a heavy, gentle blanket.

The power lingered on the shale rim for a moment longer, watching the gaunt heron omit its neck to strike at a worm, listening to the flute-note of the Joiners shift a half-step lower into a minor key that sounded almost like sleep. Then, leaving the cold, the mud, and the clicking water entirely to themselves, it withdrew, and let the valley keep its peace.
