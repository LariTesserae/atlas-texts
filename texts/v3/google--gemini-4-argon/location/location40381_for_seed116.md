At an altitude where the outside air is thin and freezing, a single spherical pressure-vessel hangs suspended within the skeletal lattice of an atmospheric processing spire. The chamber is no larger than a village courtyard—perhaps forty feet from rivet-scabbed floor to the curved brass crown—and it is almost entirely filled with warm, crystal-clear water. 

There is no rock here, no soil, no clay. Every surface is manufactured, though the machinery has been running for so many thousands of years that the distinction between architecture and geology has dissolved. The curved bronze walls have been scoured by circular currents until their stamped serial numbers are mere ghost-indentations, smooth as soap. At the center of the basin sits a colossal turbine shaft, its iron skin eaten away by millennia of friction into a porous, bone-like column. It no longer spins; instead, it shudders. A deep, rhythmic cavitation thrums up through the shaft from the miles of drop-piping below, a heavy industrial heartbeat that vibrates through the water and turns the entire spherical room into a resonant bell. You feel the sound in your teeth before you hear it: a wet, metallic *thrum-clack, thrum-clack*, punctuated by the hiss of high-pressure steam venting through floor-grates.

Those vents keep the water at the temperature of a blood-warm bath, despite the frost rime clinging to the exterior of the three thick portholes set into the upper curve of the hull. Through those circular panes of leaded glass, the view is dizzying: nothing but blinding white cloud-tops miles below, and the pale, thin blue of the stratosphere. Normal, crisp daylight pours through the glass, refracting into wavering, golden nets that dance across the submerged brass floor.

Because the chamber is so small, the water so clear, and the central structures so worn down, the space feels paradoxically open. The middle of the sphere is an empty volume of sunlit liquid, swaying gently in a slow, clockwise gyre. Yet the perimeter is lush. Rooted in the velvety layers of rust-silt and decomposing boiler-scale that coat the curved walls is a dense, hanging tapestry of hot-water flora. Thick curtains of copper-tolerant moss—viridian, chartreuse, and deep grease-green—drape over the sunken catwalks. Ribbon-kelp, wide and translucent as oiled parchment, grows directly from the seams between hull plates, their three-foot fronds undulating in the steady current like banners in a slow wind. There are bulbous bladder-ferns that trap the silver air bubbles hissing up from the lower intake valves, using the trapped gas to hold their lacy fronds upright toward the porthole light.

The ecology here is entirely localized, running at a frantic, heated pace within the open water between the rusted ribs. Schools of blind, translucent shrimp with shells the color of tarnished silver flit from one grease-trap to the next, grazing on the velvety algae that coats every rivet. They are hunted by flat, muscular eels no longer than a human finger, whose iridescent skin mimics the rainbow sheen of machine oil. Most peculiar are the valve-crabs—creatures that have forgone growing their own carapaces in favor of inhabiting antique brass hex-nuts and hollowed-out pressure gauges, their pale, hairy legs clicking loudly against the metal walls as they scuttle along the catwalk undersides.

Every twelve minutes, the dynamic of this pocket-world shifts. A counterweight somewhere in the spire beneath the room drops with a muffled, gargantuan *boom*. A secondary sluice-gate in the floor groans open, and for forty seconds, a torrent of fresh, fiercely hot condensate rockets upward through the center of the sphere. 

The quiet gyre becomes a tempest. The ribbon-kelp snaps taut, pointing toward the ceiling; a snowstorm of rust-flakes and loosened scale swirls into the open water, turning the clear center into a sparkling amber haze. The oil-eels dive into the porous hollows of the central turbine shaft to wait out the surge, while the shrimp are tumbled helplessly in the updraft. The roar of rushing water and clattering check-valves fills the chamber, deafening and absolute. 

Then, the counterweight resets with a heavy *thud*. The floor-valve slams shut. The roar subsides back into the steady, rhythmic *thrum-clack* of the ancient spire. The rust-snow slowly settles back onto the lush green walls, the kelp relaxes into its lazy clockwise drift, and the shaft of high-altitude sunlight pierces the clearing water once more, illuminating a tiny, booming paradise suspended at the roof of the world.
