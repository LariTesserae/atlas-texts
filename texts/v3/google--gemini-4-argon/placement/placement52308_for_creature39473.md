I would not be the Gleaner, with its busy, clicking jaws, nor the greedy waxen root-brain chewing through the shamans' femurs in the floor basins. Those things have too much appetite. They are still trying to go somewhere, even if they only move an inch a century.

If you reached your hand into the dark of the third alcove from the pinched-shut throat of the tube, past the curtain of warm, egg-white Film, your wet knuckles would knock against something hard, suspended at the exact height of a kneeling man’s breastbone. 

I am the fourth marker on the middle cord. I am the humerus of a whistling swan, hollowed out with a flint drill six hundred and forty years ago, strung between two fire-hardened beads of lake-clay. 

To the people who brought me in, I was a word. When a woman with scarred knees and moss in her teeth crept through the basalt needles, feeling her way in the broth-dark, her thumb would slide along the greased sinew until she hit the first clay bead (*caution: low glass*), then the second (*turn left*), and then the long, smooth flute of me. My length and my hollowness meant: *Above your head is the tallow-cradle of the gray-willow clan; the water below is sweet.*

Then the mountain shrugged, four inches of basalt settled like a dropped lid, and the thumbs stopped coming.

For the first hundred years, I was only an object waiting to be read. The sinew passing through my marrow-tunnel slackened as it rotted, and I tilted downward, sliding until I jammed against the lower clay bead. I would have fallen into the silica pool below and been eaten by the white stalks, had the Film not arrived.

It crept down the dying sinew like a slow, golden tide. I felt it enter my hollow core—warm, blind, and thick with the smell of old marmot fat and sulfur. It ate the rotten tendon out of me and replaced it with its own tough, gristly chitin, pulling me taut again, lifting me back to the height of a breastbone that would never return. 

That was when I stopped being a word for fingers and became an ear for the room.

Because I am hollow, and because my walls are thin as parchment—built for high, cold wind, not the belly of a stone—I am the finest resonator in the blister. The solid clay beads on either side of me are deaf; they deaden the vibrations that travel down the cord. The heavy basalt pillars are too thick to care about small things. 

But I am an empty chamber strung on a tight wire. 

When a heavy drop of silica-water lets go of a glass needle four yards away and strikes the lip of a soapstone bowl, the *plop* travels down the calcified willow, into the rock, and along the chitin-cord. When that tremor hits the clay bead to my left, it is just a dull thud. When it passes into me, it blooms. My thin bird-bone walls catch the tiny shockwave and ring with it—a microscopic, hollow hum that lasts for three seconds in the wet dark.

I am the amplifier. I am the place where the room’s pulse becomes loud enough to read.

Every few hours, a Gleaner glides down the tachylyte curtain and rests a single, cobweb-fine whip across my middle. It does not scrape me with its iron-sulfide teeth; the Film has coated me in a protective varnish of slick amber, keeping the stray acid of the air from pitting my calcium. The Gleaner simply rests its whip against my belly and listens through me.

Through my hollow, it hears the whole granary. 

It hears the slow, tearing sigh of a basalt hair finally snapping under the weight of five centuries of chalcedony crust. It hears the microscopic *crunch* of a blind limpet rasping sulfur two alcoves over. It hears the difference between a bowl that is three-quarters full (a high, tight *tink* in my marrow-space) and a bowl that is overflowing its lip (a flat, heavy *slap*). 

And every three or four hours, the chitin-cord inside me swells. The Film’s slow peristalsis pushes a bead of warm, fermented tallow-broth right through the center of me, from the upper baskets toward the floor basins. As the nutrient-drop squeezes through my narrow tunnel, I warm by a fraction of a degree, and I sing a low, wet, sliding note that tells every Gleaner on the line that the food is moving, the archive is intact, the winter outside has not gotten in.

I no longer mean *gray-willow clan* or *sweet water below*. The language of the crawlers is dead. Now, I mean *here*. I am the tuning fork in the dark that tells the blind hands of the mountain that the room is still in tune.
