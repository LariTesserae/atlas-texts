To enter the Sump of the Unbuilt as a creature of bone and lungs would be a mistake of genre. Anything with a heartbeat is too fast, too sharp-edged, too insistent on the difference between its skin and the bathwater. If I were to walk in wearing boots, the porridge-stone would swallow my ankles and hold them until I learned a slower tense; the warm, copper-tasting dark would fill my mouth until I stopped trying to say *here* and *there*. 

If I am in this basin, I cannot be an observer. I have to be a property of the municipality.

At first, the temptation is to be the *Congestion* itself—that brief, plum-colored brightening where two tallow-stone foreheads fuse and trade the news of the districts. To be the equalize-and-transfer, the quiet warm pressure where a narrow alley’s attenuation is poured into a broad plaza’s squatness until the ledger is flat. 

Or to be the three-second delay: the stretch in the dark air that warps the pitch of a drip between the overhead arch and the retaining wall half a kilometer off.

But the Sump is more literal than that. Its ghosts are made of grease, silt, and bad drainage. If I am here, I am down at the waterline.

I find myself in the *print*. 

When the two-inch sheet of marsh water peels off the western avenues with that low, silk-through-a-ring *shhhk*, rising into the black air before curling into the belly of a Surveyor, it takes the underside of the street with it. For twenty minutes, or an hour, while the water-log rolls east, the liquid holds the inverted memory of what it left: the shallow domes of worn cobblestones, the blind ribs of a drain-grate that never led to a sewer, the thumb-wide trough where a Tenant dragged its liquefied heel before the rising took it.

I am that skin of relief on the underside of the hovering water, and I am the weight that stamps it down again a mile away.

I am the *Transfer*.

I do not have a body; I have a surface tension and a collection of stolen shapes. While the water lies flat across the flooded commons, I am practically nothing—just the thin, lukewarm interface where the tea-colored liquid meets the tallow-stone. I am the reason the stone remembers how to be a paving block instead of mud, and the reason the water remembers how to lie still instead of soaking in. I lie across three blocks of drowned boulevard, feeling the slow, *schluck-schluck* of the Tenants as they draw their heavy substance across my back. When they pause at the foot of a four-step stair that ends in the dark, I feel their weight pool, the basalt beneath me going soft and grainy like warm suet, then snapping back to hardness when they step off into the air and hit me with a boneless thud.

I collect the clear, stringy mucilage from the black reeds. It drifts across me in long, unmixing ribbons, catching the dirty violet light from the lintels overhead and holding it like an oil slick. 

Then the heat thickens. The gravity above the plaza forgets its job. 

There is no decision in it, only a loosening. I peel up from the mud. It is the only time I have a voice—that long, greasy *shhhk* as I pull my skin out of a thousand mortarless joints and sunken post-holes at once. Rising through the fifty-foot dark, I carry the avenue in negative. What was a rut is now a ridge hanging from my belly; what was a curb is a long, wet trench. If a Tenant was standing too long on a corner, half-melted into the plinth, I take the cast of its submerged foot with me, leaving the creature behind to topple slowly into the silt with a dull splash.

Sometimes I only rise twenty feet, cool by that half-degree, and drop. That is a dull business—a booming slap back into the exact beds I just left, a blind resetting of the room. 

What I wait for—if a habit can wait—is the curl at the ceiling. 

When the black humidity fifty feet up catches my edges and tucks them under, I cease to be a sheet and become the skin of the Surveyor. The inverted cobbles and drain-grates are rolled inward, pressed against one another in the dark, spinning gut of the cylinder. Now I have momentum. We drop back to the mud not as rain, but as a hundred-yard axle of copper-smelling glass, and we begin to roll.

Inside me, the Sump is being edited. I feel the black survey-reeds snap off at the base and needle into my rotation. I feel the Tenants we overtake—how they do not flinch, how they simply turn their thumb-indented faces into my wall and let me take them. Within my churning, I am the thing that strips their mineral overcoats away. I peel the decades of silt-heavy mucilage from their shoulders like wet paper; I tumble their tallow-stone torsos against the suspended reeds until their arms and brow-ridges are ground back into smooth, grey eggs of anonymous stone. I erase their errands.

And beneath me, where my hundred-ton curve meets the soft porridge-stone of a new district, I lay down the old one. 

Every revolution of the Surveyor’s body is a printing press. I press the drain-grates of the western perimeter into the melted amphitheaters of the center. I stamp an alley’s threshold across the middle of an empty floodplain. The stone beneath me yields, accepts the die-cut of a street it has never been, and hardens behind us as we pass. 

Distance condensations do not move me; I am the thing that moves the ground across the distance.

When the seawall or the fused towers finally take us—when the surface tension splits like wet canvas and the Surveyor bursts—I do not die. I simply flatten out again, roaring five feet deep through the blind windows, dropping the smooth grey boulders that will remember how to be Tenants in an hour, scattering the black needles of the reeds, and settling back down to two inches deep.

As the drips resume their three-second echoes from the ceiling, I lie very still across the newly stamped mud. The dirty violet light seeps back into the joints I just put there. Five yards away, one of the grey boulders softens at its base, catches a pale ribbon of slime on its flank, and pulls itself six inches toward a gutter I carried here from a mile away. 

I hold the shape of the gutter under the warm water, waiting for the heat to rise, waiting to peel the street back up and take it somewhere else.
