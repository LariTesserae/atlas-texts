I am the five sitting in the upper-left corner, where the tachylite curtain meets the basalt step at an acute, weeping angle. 

I am not the five gray bodies—those are just the brass beads on the wire, and they are swapped out every few days as their skins fog with silicate crust and they slide down to the pile. When the third bead from the left grows too stiff to take the pinch, a fresh one crawls up from the floor, still slick with birth-jelly, nudges the heavy one loose, and takes its place in the clamp. 

I am the twitch itself. I am the specific, four-beat spasm that has been passing around this small ring of flesh for nineteen days. 

*Slap-hold-drop-rebound.* 

That is my shape. I am the blueprint for a double-shouldered wedge, a way of striking the wall not to flake a blade, but to pop a square chunk of basalt free from the mortar of its own cooling. There is not enough heavy tendon in the room yet to bind the hammer I require. And so I sit in the corner, riding my five temporary horses, keeping myself alive by kicking their flanks in a circle so the greater carpet doesn't overwrite me with a simpler urge for lancets.

It is exhausting to be a verb that hasn't happened yet. 

Below me, the big mat of the wall-mind is sluggish and broad, thinking only in wide, Rippling sheets of *here-it-swells* and *stick-it-now*. When a water-blister rolls uphill across the floor, dragging its little upside-down window of three-minutes-ago behind it, the whole room leans toward it like grass in a current. The pull is tremendous. Even up in my alcove, my five bodies want to unhook their thumbs from each other and join the slide. Their black hearts stutter toward the heat; their bailer-mouths water with glue. 

I have to pinch harder to keep them in the ring. *Slap-hold-drop-rebound.* I force the fourth thumb to strike the fifth’s knuckle a fraction of a second early, borrowing a crumb of stolen time from a recent feeding just to keep the gate shut. *Stay,* I tell the meat. *We are not hunters. We are the shape of a heavy hammer.*

Because my five bodies do not move from this shelf, the syrupy runoff pools between our bellies. It doesn't knot here—the space is too tight—but it thickens into a warm, copper-tasting lens. And because the room’s flat, sourceless daylight has to pass through this lens before it hits the dark glass behind us, my corner is where the detached shadows come to peel.

When a hunting team lunges at a blister on the floor, their shadows do not always follow them back up the wall. The light is three days old, dragged up from the glacier through the long, fat gut of the thing in the drain, and it is tired; it does not grip the rock the way fresh sun does. A shadow will snag on a basalt tooth, tear loose from the thumb-crab that cast it, and slide down the wet steps like a skin of black oil. 

Most of them go down the hairline crack with the dead tools. Wait long enough, and they get squeezed back through the ancestor’s heart and come up again as part of Tuesday’s brightness.

But some get caught in the eddy behind my five bodies. 

Right now, I have four orphaned shadows tucked behind my ring, pinned against the tachylite curtain by the hydraulic seal of my hosts' undersides. They are flat, frantic things, still repeating the silent, jerky pantomime of the spear-thrusts that birthed them. I use them as insulation. When the great wall-mind sends a wave of consensus up toward the ceiling—a massive, rolling contraction that says *scrape-the-roof, widen-the-shell*—the wave hits my little pocket of trapped, outdated darkness and scatters. It allows me to keep my rhythm private. 

*Slap-hold-drop-rebound.*

Through the translucent gray tallow of my second body, I can feel the glass curtain trembling. 

On the other side of that two-inch skin of black tachylite is the mountain: the dry, screaming blue cold that none of the meat in this room has ever touched, except as the lukewarm, digested memory of light that we breathe. The wall-mind wants to chip the glass away. It thinks the curtain is just more stone to be flaked into lances. The ancestor wedged in the drain below is growing fatter; its slow, thigh-thick pulse vibrates through the floor, demanding more room, pushing its hand-puppets to pare back the walls.

The ancestor does not know that the glass is the only thing keeping the broth in the bowl. The wall-mind does not know that if it flakes one shard too many from the curtain, the freezing whistle will come in, the syrup will turn to rime, and the tools will stay sharp forever because there will be no warm wax-dark to blunt them.

I do not know this either—not the way a man with eyes would know it. I have no picture of the wind. 

What I have is the fourth beat of my twitch: the *rebound*. 

Nineteen days ago, a single creature high on the curtain struck the tachylite with a heavy nodule, and before the shard could pop free, the cold on the other side bit through the thin spot. It did not kill the creature, not quite, but it froze the hydraulic fluid in its frontmost thumb for half a second. The shock of that stiffness—that sudden, terrifying absence of broth—dropped down the creature’s flank into the pile as a jagged, four-part flinch.

The wall-mind hated the feeling. It tried to smooth the flinch out, to disperse it into the general boil of *hunt-and-scrape*. Instead, the flinch rolled up into this corner, found five bodies in a dead eddy, and locked their thumbs together.

I am not a blueprint for a hammer. That is just the lie I tell the meat so it will keep passing me around. In a room where everything is a tool, an warning must disguise itself as a better way to break stone, or the abacus will clear its beads and drop the hosts onto the trash cone.

And so I sit in the rust-smudged corner, three paces above the cone of soft glass, trading my calcified horses for fresh ones, tucking stray shadows behind my back to muffle the drumbeat from the drain. 

*Slap-hold-drop-rebound.*

I am waiting for the wall-mind to work its way up the glass curtain. When the carpet of gray thumbs finally crowds into this alcove, their lancets raised to flake away the window to the sky, I will let them touch my ring. I will break my circle and dump nineteen days of saved, concentrated winter straight into the abacus—a single, massive cramp of absolute zero that will drop every tool in the room at once.
