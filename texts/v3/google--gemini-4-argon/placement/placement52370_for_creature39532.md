I am not on the upper balconies where the air tastes of tallow and the elders purr their three-hundred-year histories into the wall. The stone up there is too dry, the thunder too soft. 

I am down on the second terrace above the basin floor, where the long, melted curve of an ancient staircase meets the sheer drop of the lower promenade. It is a noisy, violent place to keep a garden, forty meters below the glider-roosts, right in the throat of the rebound wave. When the eastern wall dumps its weight, the sound doesn't just hit here; it scrapes downward into the basin and bounces back up, giving a double-kick to the ribs of the stone. 

Right now, it is the fifth second of the quiet.

I have twenty-five seconds.

Eight seconds.

*Thwack.*
