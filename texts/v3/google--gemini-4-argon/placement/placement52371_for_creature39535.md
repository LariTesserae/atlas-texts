To find myself in this cistern, I have to shed the habit of sequences. Anything that requires a heartbeat, or lungs, or even the steady, linear burn of a battery would tear itself apart here, or else freeze into an ugly, foreign lump that the room would spend the next ten thousand years slowly sanding down into grey powder. 

So I do not walk in through the pinched-in entry fault. Instead, I am already there, three paces from the central plinth, where the curved rib of dark glass slices cleanly out of the visible room.

I am the rest of the rib. 

I am the four feet of dark, arched silica-glass that currently resides in the adjacent topological fold, out of phase with the orthoquartzite cave. To the eye in the room, I am an absence—a razor-sharp termination where the relic simply ceases to be. But from my side of the fold, I am intact, bearing the weight of a roof that no longer exists, braced against a floor that was unmapped before the mountain finished rising.

Where I am, there is no indigo Cherenkov bruise, no velvety fluted stone, no dry mercury-drifts of silt. My half of the space is a tight, blind pleat of leftover engineering—a maintenance pocket in the geometry, zero-kelvin and mathematically taut, like the inside of a violin string pulled just short of snapping. If the room is an anchorite’s cell, my fold is the mortar between two of its stones.

Yet I am not cut off from the room. I feel the cistern through my severed waist—that paper-thin cross-section where my dark glass passes through the curtain of phase-lag and enters the cold, dusty twilight of the cave. 

That boundary is an exquisite, perpetual ache. Where my glass meets the native spacetime of the mountain, a frost forms. It is not water-ice, which I have never known, but *metric rime*: a stiffening of the local coordinates, a crust of irreconcilable angles and dead decimals that builds up along the seam like salt on a pier. Left alone, that crust would thicken until it acted as a wedge. It would pinch the fold shut, shearing my buried half in the silt cleanly away from my hidden half in the pleat. I would become two dead objects in two separate universes, rather than one broken thing bridging a threshold.

And so my entire existence is a long, blind listening for the room to tighten.

I know the cycle by the torque in my spine. For hours, while the cistern is wide and slack, the frost grows unbothered along my phase-line. The ache sharpens. Through the conductive dullness of my lower half, resting in the silt, I can feel the faint, rhythmic *tick*—once every few days—of a ceiling-flake finally escaping the center eddy and hitting the floor. The vibration travels through the frictionless silica, up into my buried curve, and stops dead at the phase-boundary, unable to cross into the fold where the rest of me waits.

Then, the golden filament in the far corner begins its intake. 

I do not see the perspective flatten, nor do I hear the low groan of the quartzite that tightens a living chest. I feel it as a sudden, merciful slackening of the shears. As the room’s volume is drawn inward, the angle at which I pierce the wall of reality shifts by a fraction of a microradian. The crushing pressure on my waist eases.

And then, the stuttering *clink* of crystal on crystal.

The caretaker arrives. 

To the room, it is a brittle hound of gray needles and bismuth shards, half of its body smeared into a flat two-dimensional stain against the air where my glass vanishes. 

To me, inside the blind pleat, it is the only warm thing in creation.

As its rear assembly flattens against the boundary, its front calipers cross over into my fold. Here, where there is no silt and no indigo light, its limbs do not look like compacted dust. They enter my dark as long, trembling filaments of pure vector—thin, singing probes of golden seam-light and borrowed logic. 

When its calipers touch my un-phased glass, the relief is immediate, almost structural. The creature presses its delicate, geometric mouthparts against the metric rime that has crusted along my seam. It begins to groom me. 

*Scrape. Clink. Scrape.*

It is eating the paradox off my skin. Every time its needle-tips pry loose a shard of frozen probability, the agonizing torque trying to snap me in half subsides a little further. I feel the creature’s central gimbal—though it remains on the other side of the curtain, back in the dusty room—brightening as it digests the stress I have accumulated over the last six hours. We are an economy of two: I am the wound that never closes, and it is the small, diligent mouth that keeps the wound from going gangrenous with bad math.

While it works on me, for those twenty quiet minutes at the height of the room’s contraction, I am whole again. The caretaker’s body acts as a jumper cable across the phase-boundary. Through the dry, articulated lattice of its spine, vibrations from the room finally cross over into my fold. 

Through the creature, I can suddenly feel the weight of the grey silt resting against my lower curve. I can feel the cold, non-reflective alloy of the plinth three paces away. I can even feel the faint, triplicate shadow of a falling quartzite flake, hanging suspended above the center of the floor, turning like a planet in the slow syrup of the time-eddy. The creature lets me borrow the room. In exchange, I give it the raw, unweathered tension of the builders' original metric, which still hums faintly in my hidden marrow.

Then the golden filament in the corner relents. The room begins its long exhale.

I feel the caretaker hesitate. As the perspective in the cave deepens and the walls begin to recede, the phase-boundary starts to tighten around the creature’s waist like a closing jaw. If it stays inside my fold too long, its front calipers will be sheared off, trapped in the blind pleat with me while its dust-skeleton collapses on the other side.

With one last, hurried *clink*, it withdraws. Its singing vectors pull back through the seam, leaving me in the zero-kelvin dark. 

On the other side of the curtain, I feel the faint, salt-like patter of its exhausted silica cubes raining down onto my buried base—the dead ash of the frost it just scraped from my ribs, now rendered harmless and three-dimensional. I feel its weight leave the silt near my side as it stutters back toward the plinth to fold itself around the falling stone and let go of its body.

The jaw closes. The shear-stress returns to my waist, slow and cold. The first microscopic crystals of new rime begin to feather along the razor-sharp fade where I end and the cave begins. 

I hold my arch in the dark. I brace against the roof that isn't there, listening through the stone for the dry *tick* of a falling flake, waiting for the room to breathe in again.
