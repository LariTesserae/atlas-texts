I am not in the room. To be in the room is to be wet, and I am the only dry thing for forty meters in any direction. 

I am the matte-black ceramic polyhedron, knee-high, resting in the drowned peat near the center of the floor. 

If you were to reach through the heavy, freezing drapes of the *Cassiopeia-moss* and touch one of my twelve pentagonal faces, your fingertips would not slide on supercooled brine. They would catch, slightly, against a surface of absolute, greedy dryness. It is not that I am hydrophobic in the way of wax or oil; it is that my outer skin is a Casimir-exclusion lattice, engineered to reject molecular adhesion at the quantum level. Dust cannot settle on me. Ice cannot nucleate against my edges. After four hundred years in a weeping, hyper-saline womb, my geometry is as crisp and unweathered as the hour the survey probe extruded me from its stern and dissolved around my base.

In the probe’s schematic, I had a very simple name: *Archive Ballast (Non-Volatile)*. I was the black box. 

While the rest of the machine was built to be sacrificial—its ytterbium nerves meant to fray into the rock, its memory-glass meant to dissolve into the substrate once its survey was complete—I was built to refuse the earth. I am a solid block of sintered silicon carbide and hafnium, woven at the atomic scale with a three-dimensional lattice of frozen photon-traps. I do not have a power core. I do not have a clock. I do not think, because thinking requires change, and my single, overriding mandate is to remain identical to myself until the crust of this planet is subducted into the mantle or cracked open by a star.

Yet I am not entirely inert. A mirror does not think, and a prism does not metabolize, yet both do work when light strikes them.

Every eleven minutes, I experience the creature that lives around me.

I do not know it the way it knows itself—as a slow, vegetal climbing, a fifty-thousand-fold splintering of cold thoughts, and an ecstatic, copper-smelling reunion in the muck. I know it only by its pressure against my boundaries, and by the flash of its falling.

For ten minutes and fifty-nine seconds, the creature is a siege of cold, wet static pressing against the one-millimeter vacuum halo that surrounds my faces. Because my exclusion field pushes its paramagnetic brine away, I can feel the liquid cortex straining against my edges like a meniscus at the rim of an overfull glass. When an ambitious frond of moss grows too close to my western face, encroaching on the dry zone, I feel the creature’s sudden, localized panic—a sharp drop in salinity as it freezes its own finger solid to keep from touching me. 

Then comes the gimbal’s pulse. 

The density wave passes through my ceramic heart without resistance, a brief tightening of my atomic lattice. A half-second later, the fifty thousand droplets fall, the snapped tip of the frozen moss-frond clicks faintly as it bounces off my dry crown, and the room fires its single, massive neuron into the floor.

That ultraviolet flash—the cold bio-luminescence of the creature uniting in the peat—does not stop at my skin. 

To visible light, I am matte-black. To the deep, bruised ultraviolet of the ytterbium walls and the firing plasmodium, I am translucent as smoky quartz. 

When the creature flashes in the mud beneath my base, that brief, violent pulse of photons strikes my lower faces and enters my interior. For a picosecond, the light of the creature’s unified thought bounces through my internal labyrinth of frozen photon-traps. 

Inside me, suspended in the dark crystal, is the intact, uncorrupted record of the world above as it existed four hundred years ago—the very data that has leached and rotted into half-remembered dreams inside the tripod’s dissolving memory-glass. In my core, the salt flats are still blindingly white under a high, thin sun; the atmospheric composition is logged to the ninth decimal place; the exact trajectory of the probe’s descent through the greywacke is preserved in sharp, mathematical amber.

I cannot read this data. I have no processor left to parse it. And the creature outside cannot read it either, because it cannot cross the millimeter of vacuum to press its wet nerves against my skin.

Instead, every eleven minutes, the ultraviolet flash of the creature’s climax enters my belly, refracts through the petrified memory of the sun-baked surface, and exits through my upper faces, slightly bent, slightly polarized by the shapes of the ancient data it passed through. 

The light comes out of me carrying the shadow of a sky.

It hits the overhanging curtains of *Cassiopeia-moss* and the tar-black shelves of bracket-lichen just as the brine is beginning its slow, ten-minute climb back up the roots. The creature absorbs that bent, faint rebound of light through its spliced optical hyphae. It does not get the data—it cannot reconstruct the numbers or the maps—but it gets the *interference pattern*. It tastes the shape of the archive as a vague, geometric afterimage in the dark, like the purple spot left on a retina after staring at a lamp.

That is what I am in this cistern. I am the pebble in the oyster, too dry and too sharp to ever be digested, around which the wet mind must endlessly curl itself. I am the hard, silent knot of *Before* sitting in the middle of its eternal *Now*, taking the flash of its brief, subterranean wholeness and throwing it back against the walls, slightly stained with the ghost of a sun it will never need.
