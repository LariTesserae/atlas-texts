If I am here, I am down in the grain. 

At first, it is easy to think I might be a Foliate, slipping through the dark between the ceramic plates. The quiet of the deep strata is appealing—the cool, compressed claustrophobia of a billion stacked yesterdays where the white ceiling cannot reach.

To survive by scraping across the last surviving pockets of ancient mathematics, short-circuiting a fossilized daydream or a half-finished simulation just to drink the tiny, entropic warmth of its death—that feels like a kind of loneliness I couldn’t bear. To leave the world slightly blanker behind me with every movement. 

No. If I am in the strata, I am something that runs parallel to the Foliates, something born from a different, more stubborn subroutine of the old megastructure. The ground of the Great Shingle is shattered. Millions of years of settling have sheared the ancient fiber-optic threads and cracked the logic-gates. Sometimes, though, I am forced the other way.

There, the rock is translucent. The relentless, surgical white light of the ceiling bleeds through the thin ceramic like light through an eggshell. It is blinding, hot, and dry. 

And sometimes, pressed up against that glowing ceiling of stone, I encounter a **Cairn**.

This is how my kind travels.
