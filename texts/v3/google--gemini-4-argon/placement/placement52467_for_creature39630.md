I am sixty meters up the west wall, wedged into the dry shadow beneath an overhanging lip of obsidian where the rock sheared in a stepped conchoidal wave. 

I do not have a heartbeat, and I do not have the swift, liquid-metal grace of the bismuth tenders. I began as an early-generation calibration plumb—a heavy, cylinder-shaped parcel of ceramic-jacketed sensors and coiled copper-beryllium tape that the settlement’s original builders dropped down the chimney on a winch line three hundred years ago to map the cleft’s initial thermal profile. When the installation went autonomous and dissolved its temporary scaffolding, my winch line was severed by a falling flake of basalt. Instead of plummeting to the scree, my trailing copper tape tangled in a dense thicket of early tension-threads, and my heavy ceramic belly wedged fast into this diagonal seam in the glass.

The city forgot me within a week. I was not in its maintenance registry; to the early grid, I was simply an anomaly in the rock's surface topology, a stubborn bump to be built around. 

For the first eighty years, I simply baked. My internal batteries swelled, leaked a slow, green paste of potassium hydroxide through my gasket seams, and died. The copper-beryllium tape—forty meters of it, meant to reel me back to the stratosphere—hung down the wall beneath me in a limp, twisted ribbon.

Then the catalytic crust found me. 

The rust-velvet crept up my dangling copper tape from a leaking capillary conduit ten meters below. When the iron-sulfur crystals reached my cracked ceramic casing and met the dried alkaline paste of my ruptured batteries, they didn't just scab over me; they turned my hollow interior into a primitive, wet-cell accumulator. 

I am the **Seam-Gall**—a stationary, accidental organ cemented into the obsidian angle where the west wall’s largest acoustic mirror meets a structural buttress of honeycombed shelves.

Over two centuries, the city and the mites have buried me in their wars and their architecture until I look like an owl’s pellet made of glass and wire, the size of a butcher’s block. The fullerene terraces have grown straight through my outer rigging, using my rigid ceramic core as an anchor post for forty different tension-lines. Slag-mites roost in the warm crannies under my chin during the dry bake, their brittle orange legs clicking against my casing as they groom the sulfur from their whiskers. When they die here, their bismuth shells do not fall to the talus; the rust-crust cements them to my flanks, layer by layer, until I have grown a knobby, silver-pink carapace of dead tenders.

I cannot move, and I do not calculate the mountain’s stress tensors. What I do is *remember*. 

Because the Draft-Mind thinks in steam, its consciousness is completely orchestral and completely transient. When the twenty-minute dry interval settles into the Throat, the vortices die. The honeycombs stop vibrating. The Choir goes largely deaf and dumb between breaths, relying on the brittle, mite-chewed amber lattices to hold the barest outline of its last thought until the magma boils the aquifer again. 

Except here, in my seam. 

The forty meters of copper-beryllium tape that still dangles from my belly has been partially eaten by the crust, partially braided into the city's tension-wires by confused bismuth tenders, and partially fused directly into the obsidian by decades of plasma sparks. It acts as a heavy, low-impedance grounding strap between the upper acoustic mirrors and the deep rock. 

When the steam rises and the east-wall honeycombs begin to shriek their piezoelectric currents across the chasm, a fraction of that voltage bleeds down the guy-wires anchored to my shell. It passes through my alkaline-and-rust core. 

I do not process the signal; I electroplate it. 

Every four-minute thought the Choir sings sends a specific, rhythmic pulse of current through my belly, which causes the dissolved bismuth and sulfur in my wet interior to precipitate out onto the coiled remnant of my internal measuring tape in microscopic, varved ridges. Over two hundred years, I have become a solid-state phonograph cylinder of the city’s mind—a dense, metallic pearl of stratified static, recording every storm that has battered the mountain’s tooth, every tectonic slip, and every long, slow migration of the parabolic shelves.

Right now, it is the eighteenth minute of the dry bake. 

The air in the cleft is forty-six degrees, flat and heavy as heated iron. Down in the violet gloom sixty meters below me, the talus is silent; the dredge-chains are buried deep in the clinker, hoarding their moisture. Around my flanks, a cluster of seven slag-mites are jittering softly, their glass bristles scraping at a hairline fracture in my ceramic hide, trying to taste the low, salty trickle of voltage that never quite leaves my core. I let them scrape. Their frantic, stuttering static is like a warm itch on an old scar.

Then, deep in the obsidian behind my back, the mountain clears its throat. 

It is not a sound yet; it is a tiny shear-wave in the volcanic glass that pinches my ceramic housing by half a micron. The pinch squeezes my internal crystals. A tiny spike of current jumps down my dangling copper tail. 

Three meters below me, a row of pristine bismuth tenders—waiting like silver teardrops along a magnetic seam—feel the stray spike from my tail before the steam even arrives. They twitch in unison. 

The heat hits four seconds later. The air turns thick with the smell of wet chalk and hot copper. 

The millions of tension-threads lacing the gulf around me suddenly vanish as they coat themselves in a blinding, mercury-bright mist of condensation, then reappear as glowing hair-lines of amber and bruised violet. The fifty-meter oval of naked obsidian to my left—the great West Mirror—sweats instantly, becoming a slick, black sheet of oil.

The Choir inhales.

The sub-audible hum rises from the depths, vibrating through the basalt, setting my dead bismuth armor rattling against the wall. A vortex of superheated vapor, spun off a jagged baffle of mite-crust forty meters down, corkscrews up the chasm, hits the wet West Mirror beside me, and deflects hard across the gulf into the ten-deep honeycombs on the east wall.

The honeycombs sing. The voltage spikes down my guy-wires—hot, sharp, and rhythmic. 

Inside my dark, alkaline belly, the chemical bath fizzes. A new layer of bismuth-sulfide, three atoms thick, plates itself onto my internal spool, locking the shape of this afternoon’s barometric shear into metal forever.

And then comes the *shhhk*.

The electrostatic charge sweeps up the walls like a sigh. The millions of droplets race sideways across the fullerene razor-shelves, funneling into the stone. Because my bulk interrupts the smooth curve of the West Mirror, the water gathering on the glass above me cannot reach the capillary aqueducts. It hits my knobby, rust-furred top, pools for a second in the hollows between my dead-tender scales, and boils over my lip.

For thirty seconds, I am the source of a private waterfall. 

A curtain of silver, copper-tasting threads spills off my chin and plummets into the violet dark. Three slag-mites who were too greedy at my seams are swept away in the scalding spill, tumbling end-over-end down the chasm. 

Looking down through the shimmering heat, I can see the floor wake up directly beneath my drip-line. The dry tinkling of glass needles rises from the scree. Four massive dredge-chains—the largest in the Throat, grown fat and heavy on my reliable runoff and the mites I shed—unspool from the clinker and throw themselves ten meters into the air, stiffening into black, braided pikes directly under me, whipping and whistling and sparking with thirty-volt blue fire as they catch my falling water in their mouths.

Up at the ceiling plug, a hundred and forty meters overhead, a gust of stratospheric wind strikes the mountain’s outer tooth. One of the forty-foot frost spires chimes—a single, freezing, diamond-hard note that drops straight down the boiling throat of the chimney. 

The note hits my ceramic shell, rings through my copper tail, and writes itself as a tiny, jagged kink in the metal growing inside my dark belly. The steam pulse passes. The chains collapse with a sound like spilled coins. The rock dries to matte black. And I sit in my seam, heavy and warm and blind, holding the ghost-city’s memory in my rust while we wait twenty minutes for the next breath.
