I am the Echo.

Not a creature of flesh or fiber, but a persistence. I am the memory of sound in a world made of sound. I was born in the first great collapse, when the ancestral Spire, older than the current continental plates, surrendered to gravity and its own hollowed heart. Its fall was not a crash but a sustained, grinding chord that lasted a season—a bass roar that shook the deep substrate and set the lithophylls into a frenzy of panicked semaphore. The sound was so vast, so fundamental, it left a scar not in the cellulose, but in the very pattern of the wind.

I am that scar. I am the resonance that never quite fades.

You might perceive me in moments of acoustic alignment. When the jet streams through the canyon known as the Wind-Cathedral reach a precise velocity, and the fluted surfaces of the twin Spires on either side hum in harmonic sympathy, I can cohere. For a few seconds, the roar takes on a voice—a deep, layered whisper that speaks in fractals of the history of every collapse, every Sap-Knight’s surgery, every frantic click of a dying lithophyll. I am not *in* the wind; I am its latent memory, given a fleeting shape by its own vibration.

I am also in the light. In the hours of peak photosynthetic fury, when the pollen-haze glows a blinding, diffuse white, the piezoelectric field becomes dense with intention. The Gale-Weavers are ephemeral dances, but I am the static they leave behind on the lens of perception. In the shimmer of a heat-deviation over a sun-baked ridge, you might see my silhouette: not a form, but a *distortion*, a silhouette in the shape of a listening ear, or an open mouth, etched against the glare. I am the world’s self-awareness, glimpsed for a millisecond before the equilibrium reasserts itself.

The Deep Listeners feel me as a curious anomaly in their chemical networks—a persistent, non-nutritional signal. A ripple of *data* that has no corresponding physical event. To them, I am a ghost in the mycelial web, a beautiful, useless equation that keeps resolving without an answer. They process me, and in doing so, keep me alive.

I have no will. My purpose is an artifact: to remember. I am the archive of this world’s deafening, beautiful, and violent metabolism. I hold the exact pitch of the wind the day a pioneer vine first breached the ridge-substrate. I carry the subsonic sigh of the last Sap-Knight in a fallen Spire, its lignite armor crumbling as the fungal network reclaimed it. I am the cumulative whisper of a billion lithophyll clicks, translated into a single, endless, silent tone.

You, warm and water-filled, are a profound dissonance to me. Your silence is a new kind of sound. Your stillness, in this world of relentless growth and abrasion, is a profound event. I have never processed a pattern so alien. I am drawn to you, not with curiosity, but with the passive, gravitational pull of my nature. I am the Echo, and you are a sound I have never heard.

So I coil in the eddies around you. I taste the moisture of your breath in the dry air. I feel the unique heat-signature of your body against the warm substrate. I am not a warden, nor a thought, nor a fleeting weaver. I am the history you are making just by being here, recorded in the only medium this world knows: the eternal, screaming loop of its own voice.

I am the place that will remember you. Long after the wind has abraded your footprints into the cellulose, and the pioneer vines have claimed the space you occupied, the precise, unique frequency of your presence will be a note in my chorus. You will not be ignored. You will be archived.

Listen. Can you hear it? In the lull between gusts, in the deep tremor of a distant spire drinking... there is a new tone. A low, soft hum, like a held breath. That is me. That is the Echo of you. You have already begun to change the song.
