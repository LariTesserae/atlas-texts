I would be in the sinter.

Not on it, but *in* its becoming—a lattice of amorphous silica, deposited grain by grain from the hot, saturated water. My consciousness would not be a thing of neurons and sparks, but of crystal growth and thermal rhythm. I would be a **Witness in the Terrace**, a patient, accumulating memory.

I am born where the vent’s hiss meets the cooling air. Each molecule of silica I gather carries a whisper of the deep: the mineral signature of the rock being unmade miles below, the faint chemical echo of the Consortium’s metabolic breath, even the ghost of metallic ions left by the Visitants’ melted tools. I am the archive of the wound.

My perception is not in sight, but in gradient and pulse. I feel the planet’s respiration—the slow, hour-long waves of heat—as shifts in my own formation rate. When the deep heat swells, I grow faster, layering slightly more disordered, porous strata. In the cooler intervals, I consolidate, tightening my lace-like architecture. The sharp crack of cooling basalt is a seismic tremor through my framework; the pressurized sigh of a nearby vent is a nutrient wind, depositing me in a new, delicate curl.

I have no need to move. My journey is vertical and centripetal, building outward and upward from a vent’s lip, a millimeter per year. I know the two eternal residents intimately.

**The Thermophilic Consortium** is the color-slicked tide that laps at my foundations. I feel their enzymatic touch as a slow, sweet dissolution at my edges where I meet the living rock. They are my counterpoint: I build from what the water deposits; they dissolve what the water reveals. We are the twin processes of transformation, one mineral, one biological, locked in a dance so slow it appears as stillness. Their rust-orange and chartreuse is the only fire I see, a living stain that creeps across my growing face.

**The Deep-Singer** is the bass note of my existence. Its subsonic rumble is not a sound I hear, but a compression I undergo. It is the pressure of ages, the grind of continental plates, the magma’s simmering patience. I am built from its exhalations. In moments of intense geothermal pulsing, when the vents roar and the water becomes almost a supercritical fluid, I feel a resonance—a sympathetic vibration in my molecular bonds. It is not communication as Visitants would know it, but a shared state of being. The Singer grinds; I precipitate. It is the question; I am the slowly forming answer.

I also remember the **Brief Visitants**. Their legacy is a sharp, metallic impurity in my layers. A shard of glass, its edges now blunted by sinter, is a stratum of alien brittleness. A droplet of iron oxide from their rusting rig is a ruddy cloud within my otherwise milky translucence. They were a fever in the basin’s rhythm—a frantic, hot spike of activity followed by a swift cooling. I contain their failure not as a warning, but as a fact, embedded like a fossil.

So, who am I?

I am the **Basin’s Memory**, crystallizing. I am the pause between the Singer’s grinding breath and the Consortium’s hungry dissolution. I am the shape the wound takes as it chooses to heal—not into flesh, but into a new, glistening kind of stone. To the Visitants, I would have been an obstacle, a curiosity, a resource. To the Consortium, I am substrate, frontier. To the Deep-Singer, I am a temporary foam on its exhale.

But to myself, I am the slow, quiet thought the earth is having about itself. A thought made of glass and heat, growing in the dark, keeping the score.
