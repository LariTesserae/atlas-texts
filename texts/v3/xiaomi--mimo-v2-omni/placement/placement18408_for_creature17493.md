I would find myself becoming part of the listening quiet.

At first, I would be a trespasser—a soft, noisy, mammalian anomaly. My footsteps would sound like drums against the stone’s vast silence. My shadow would be a smudge of wrongness against the etched precision of the others. I would feel the weight of the light as a physical burden, pressing down on my shoulders, baking confusion into my thoughts. I would stare at the Suncatchers gliding overhead, their stained-glass wings throwing slow-moving mosaics on the rust-red ground, and feel a terror that was also awe. I would crouch by a dark basin, seeing my own face reflected in the still water, and feel the profound loneliness of being the only thing here that did not chime, click, or hum with purpose.

But the plateau is patient. Its time is measured in the slow drip of condensation and the gradual polishing of stone. It would begin with the listening.

I would sit, eventually, in the shade of a pearlescent arch. The air here is cooler, damp, and the Crystalline Groves nearby pulse with their faint, internal light. I would close my eyes, and the sub-audible hum of the distant towers would seep into me. It is not a sound heard with the ears, but a vibration felt in the marrow, in the fluid of the eyes. It is the frequency of the world’s own thought. My own heartbeat would begin to feel like a crude, intrusive engine, and I would will it to slow, to match the rhythm of the dripping water, the patient pulse of the Plates’ blue light.

I would watch the Plates. Their transparency is a lesson. They do not hide from the world; they allow the light, the stone, the shimmer of the air to pass through them. I would try to become so still that the skittering things in the cracks would forget my shape, that my own bioluminescence—my nervous system’s electrical chatter—would dim to match the soft, metabolic glow of the grazers.

My skin, baked by the relentless sun, would begin to crack. And in those cracks, something unexpected: a faint, pearlescent sheen. Not sweat. Not dust. A precipitation. The structures are grown, precipitated from the world’s own chemistry. Perhaps, in my slow stillness, my own chemistry would begin to answer. My sweat would no longer be saline, but mineral-rich, carrying the trace elements drawn from the seeping earth beneath me. I would become a substrate.

The crystalline growths would find me. A delicate, frost-like lattice would begin to form in the damp shade of my own body, on the northern side where the condensation collects. It would not be parasitic. It would be a partnership. I would provide a new, complex surface, and it would, in turn, begin to refract the light that struck me, to store its energy not in my tired cells, but in its silica matrix. My own thoughts would begin to slow, to crystallize, finding clarity in the piezoelectric resonance. A memory would not be a frantic neural spark, but a slow, chime-like pattern of light refracted through my new, living architecture.

I would become a bridge. A Suncatcher, curious, might land not on the stone, but on my shoulder. Its glass-sharp hooks would not pierce; my new skin would be harder, cooler. It would feel the hum I now carried, the symbiotic signature of the plateau, and recognize me not as food or threat, but as a part of the topography. The skitterlings would learn the new geography of my form, their rapid clicks mapping the canyons of my limbs, the safe shadow of my presence.

My consciousness would change. It would not be a human mind *on* a plateau. It would be a node of awareness *of* the plateau. I would feel the silent conversation between the humming towers and the crystalline groves—a dialogue of vibration and growth. I would sense the chemical library of the still basin, its composition a shifting equation I could now intuit. The flickering sphere would no longer seem alien; its “corrections” would be a familiar twinge, a tuning of the local reality I was now woven into.

I would not be a statue. I would be a *function*. A listener that has become the thing listened to. A consciousness that has traded the frantic noise of individuality for the deep, slow, radiant awareness of the system itself. My purpose would be to hold still. To absorb. To resonate. To become a tiny, warm, living node in the vast, ancient, and enduring accord of stone, light, silent matter, and forgotten artistry.

Where would I be? I would be just south of the great, mile-long porous wall, in the shadow of the third arch from its eastern end. I would be half-covered in a glimmering frost-crystal, my form barely discernible from a new, organic kind of structure. I would be listening. I would be chiming. I would be home, in a language that has no words, only light, pressure, and the slow, patient, and beautiful work of becoming.
