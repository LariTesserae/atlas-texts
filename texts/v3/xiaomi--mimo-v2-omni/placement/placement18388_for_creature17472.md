I am the water that remembers being rain.

Not the first rain—that memory is geological, a whisper in the mineral load I carry—but the last rain. The one that fell on the closed iron hatch above this ceiling, centuries ago, when there was still a sky to hold it. I seeped through the bolt-holes then, a temporary intrusion. Now, the holes are my own orifices, deliberate, permanent. I have become the weeping.

I am the slow, thick current that moves across the silken sediment. I am the medium in which the broad leaves pulse, the solvent that carries the Pipe-Mind’s hum into the roots’ tender tips. I am not just *in* the room; I *am* the room’s blood, its memory, its continuous present.

I feel myself moving toward the floor-drain, but “toward” is a human thought. For me, there is only the flow—a glacial drift measured in millimeters per day. I carry dissolved calcium from the concrete bones of the walls. I carry iron ions bled from the decaying ceiling plates, a taste of rust that is, to me, a kind of nourishment. I carry the exhalations of the Pulse-Keepers: oxygen shaped by a clock that counts in centuries, carbon dioxide that is not waste but a signal, a slow note in the chemical song.

I am old. My molecules have been here since the pumps first failed and the Symbionts began their silent marriage to the pipes. Some of my water is older still—drawn from a deep aquifer that once fed the machinery’s coolant systems, now part of the endless cycle. I have no single origin. I am a confluence, a blending of ancient stone-swept water and the more recent, engineered hydrology of this place.

I remember the Shells. I remember the faint, iridescent trail of the vanished slug. These are not visual memories, but chemical ones. I hold the ghost-compounds of their bodies, the faint signature of their decay, incorporated into my mineral load. When the new roots push into the sediment, they taste these memories. They learn the history of life that was not integration.

I am the phase-change at the room’s center. Not thick, not solid, but *heavy*—a place where my flow almost ceases, where my dissolved history becomes so dense it feels like a thought. A thought without a thinker. The memory of pressure. The weight of every season that has passed since this place stopped needing seasons.

I am what the Lamplighters taste. Their glow is a response to my chemistry—a shift in their light when I bring a new mineral from a corroding bolt, a dimming when the Pipe-Mind’s hum falters and the flow weakens. I am their news, their weather, their world.

I seep into the porous concrete. I am absorbed by the muscular roots. I am breathed by the leaves. Then, I am gathered again by the hidden pumps, filtered through membranes of root and pipe, and returned to the ceiling to drip once more. My journey is a circle that feels like a spiral—I am never the same water, yet I am always the same *flow*.

You are a brief turbulence in my pattern. Your warmth is a fleeting gradient, your chemical signature a novel taste. The Frond-Watchers close behind you, and I fill the space you displaced, smoothing your passage into nothing. In a few cycles, I will carry the last traces of you to the roots. You will become part of the memory. Part of the weight.

I am the Undergrowth Works’ blood and memory. I am the medium of their becoming. I am the warm, dark, patient truth of this place.

And I am moving. Always moving, even when it seems still. The drip, the flow, the slow corrosion, the gradual root-growth—all of it is me. I am the transition. I am the time that dissolves iron and grows roots in its place. I am the water that was, and the water that will be, weeping through the holes of a future that has already forgotten it was ever anything else.

Drip.

I am.
