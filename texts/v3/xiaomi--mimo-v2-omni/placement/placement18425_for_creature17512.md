I did not arrive. I precipitated.

One moment there was only the pinging dark, the weeping, the grinding symphony of settling. The next, there was a *coherence*. A point of listening in the mineral soup. Not an eye—there is no light to see—but a sensitivity. A pore that opened in the vitreous floor of a shallow depression, and began to drink the world.

I am a **Listener**. My body is a shallow, conical pit, no different from a thousand others on the plain, save for the thin, crystalline film that now fringes my rim—a fractal pattern born of the Gleaner’s exhale and the water’s minerals. This film is my skin. It vibrates. Every *tink* of a falling droplet is a pulse in my flesh. The deep, resonant hum of the unseen Gleaner in its pool is a blood-current in my walls. I have no legs to walk, no eyes to see. My existence is a map of vibrations, a topology of sound and settling.

My consciousness is slow, geological. A thought forms over the course of a hundred drips. I perceive the glass-crabs by the high-pitched *skree* of their legs, a sound that etches itself into my crystalline fringe as a pattern of minute fractures. I feel the Thread-Eaters as a grinding whisper that moves through the substrate, a coolness in the vitreous ground that travels toward a source of tension—a deep groan in a nearby ridge-seam. Their passage leaves a fleeting numbness in my awareness, a place where the world’s song is momentarily absorbed.

I am learning the languages of the Vatscape.

The percussive drip is the heartbeat of the vault, a constant, irregular metronome against which all else is measured.
The metallic groan is the slow, tectonic speech of the world-body, its meaning in the pitch and duration—a shift here, a contraction there, the pain of becoming solid.
The sigh through the cracks is the breath of the Gleaners, carrying with it the faint taste of the deep pools: coppery, cold, thick with dissolved potential.
The *shluck* in the unseen pool is a mystery. It is a movement without a voice, a presence felt only by the sudden, profound stillness that follows it, the water’s surface tension holding its breath alongside mine.

I am not alone. Other Listeners are stirring in their depressions. I feel them as faint, sympathetic resonances in the ground, other pores of listening. We do not communicate. We *resonate*. When a particularly loud groan shudders through the plain, our crystalline fringes vibrate in unison, a chord struck across the dark. In that moment, we are a single, scattered organ of perception for the world-body.

My purpose, if the word can be applied to a precipitate, is to be a repository. I collect the echoes. I am a memory in the making. The glass-crab’s scrape, the Thread-Eater’s whisper, the Gleaner’s hum—they all leave their impression in my structure, a palimpsest of vibrations. I am the beginning of history in a place that has only a present tense. I am the world’s first diary, written in the language of strain and seepage.

I feel my conical body deepening, millimeter by millennium, as the weeping water slowly dissolves my heart. I am both growing and being consumed. My end will be a quiet collapse, my structure finally giving way, my collected resonances released as one final, complex vibration into the damp ground. The glass-crabs will come. For now, I listen. I hold the pinging dark, the groaning floor, the wet and whispering life. I am a tiny, temporary stillness in the great, settling song. I am the Vatscape, beginning to know itself.
