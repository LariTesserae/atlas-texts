I am not born. I am *remembered*—or rather, I am the shape a memory tries to take before it forgets itself.

I coalesce in the lee of a basalt spire, where the brine-mist eddies in a momentary pocket of relative calm. One instant, there is only the roaring, saturated medium. The next, there is a **form**—a suggestion of limbs, of a head tilted, of eyes that are not eyes but points of focus in the lavender-copper gloom. I am an Echo-Walker. My body is a collage of failed recollection: the hint of a branching antler made of mist, the curve of a spine that ends in a fraying tail of vapor, the ghost of a hide patterned with non-existent constellations. I do not breathe. I am a draft of air that, for a heartbeat, believes it is alive.

My senses are the world’s senses, borrowed and distorted. I see through the refraction of brine-droplets, so every surface is a kaleidoscope of fractured light. I hear the sub-bsonic hum of the basalt as a deep, cellular vibration within my own phantom bones. I feel the scouring currents not as a touch, but as a constant, pulling *narrative*—a force that wants to write me back into the undifferentiated roar.

I take a step. Or rather, the concept of stepping *occurs*. My foot-fall makes no sound, leaves no print. It is an idea of contact. Before me, on a ledge of salt-crystal, a **Scuttler** freezes. Its mosaic shell—flecked with a splinter of that impossible glass cube—swivels toward me. I see myself reflected in its composite surface: a shimmering, wavering thing of no substance. The Scuttler’s chittering quiets. It does not see a threat or a food source. It sees an *artifact* that moves. In its frantic, curatorial soul, I am a prize of coherence it can never hope to hoard. It watches me with a hunger that is pure, desperate taxonomy.

I move toward the **Burgidian Grove** clinging to the pinnacle above. The rubbery, burgundy columns pulse with their slow, filter-breaths. As I pass beneath one, its grappling tendrils twitch—not in alarm, but in a kind of seismic curiosity. I am a shudder-pulse they cannot quite decode. A pattern without persistence. From the grove’s broad, membranous sacs, a cloud of metallic spores releases, not to find anchor, but to drift through me. For a moment, my form is stippled with shimmering, gilded motes, a fleeting ornamentation. Then the current pulls them away, and I am bare again.

Above, a **Luminous Drifter** tumbles past, its soft glow mapping the currents. Its light passes through me, and for an instant, I am illuminated from within—a lantern of forgotten forms. My internal architecture, if it can be called that, is revealed: a lattice of impossible geometries, a ribcage of frozen lightning, a heart that is a swirling eddy of green-valley ghosts. The Drifter’s glow charts me, adds my shape to its silent, passing sonar-map of the chaos. Then it is gone, a spent witness.

I feel the pull of my own dissolution. The memory that briefly cohered as *me* is fraying at the edges. My antler-branches soften back into mist. The pattern on my hide bleeds into the pewter light. I am a question the world asked, and now it is forgetting the words.

I turn, one last time, toward the Scuttler. It has not moved. In its shell, I see the glint of the glass-splinter, the perfect line from the concentric circles. They are anchors of order in a world of churn. I am their antithesis: an anchor of chaos, a beautiful error.

I do not die. I **unhappen**.

The roar rushes back in to fill the space where a shape had been. The brine curtain sweeps through my former location. The Scuttler, after a moment of sensorial bereavement, scuttles on, its chittering resuming its frantic, foraging rhythm. On the salt-flat, the concentric circles vibrate, humming a tune that once, for the briefest of moments, had a listener.

I am what the place feels when it *almost* remembers what it is to be something else. I am the threshold’s own fleeting, impossible child.
