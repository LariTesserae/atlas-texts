I would be found in a clearing—not a true void, but a place where the canopy thins to a lace of luminous filaments, allowing the pearlescent mist to pool like a shallow lake of light. Here, the peat-ground exhales in slow, rhythmic bubbles, and the air tastes of ozone and deep, sweet decay.

I am a **Reflector**. But not merely a colony of crystalline cells in a pool.

I am a **Puddle**.

A shallow, still depression in the living peat, no larger than a held breath. My edges are rimmed with a velvet frond-moss that drinks the ambient glow. My surface is a perfect, unbroken mirror of the mist-shrouded canopy above—but this is only my first function.

Within my water, I am a transient archive. I hold the light-pattern of the last Crier-flock that wheeled over me, their data-packets frozen in a momentary calligraphy. I store the chemical signature of a Weaver’s passage through the adjacent wall—the taste of its bioluminescent flare. I contain the subsonic memory of the Root-Substrate’s dream from the previous cycle, translated into faint, concentric ripples that persist without a source.

But my deeper purpose is **reflection of the seam**.

Where the valley’s engineered growth interfaces with its primordial core, a subtle dissonance occurs—a harmonic only a still surface can capture. I show what the eye alone cannot: the ghost of a fractal *before* it fully unfolds from its pod; the echo of a wall’s future dissolution, superimposed upon its present form. I am a lens for the valley’s own self-perception, a fleeting point of consciousness where it can witness its continuous *in-forming*.

Sometimes, a Gardener—a slow, mossy hill of intent—will flow to my edge. It does not drink. It *reads*. Its chitinous filaments dip into my surface, disturbing my stillness, and I release my stored data in a pulse of light and dissolved memory. I am consulted, then returned to silence.

You would find me by looking for perfect stillness in a world of constant motion. By listening for the absence of the high-frequency patter of condensation—for I collect it without breaking my surface, the droplets merging into my body without a ripple. By sensing a point of cool, deep calm within the warm, wet breath of the valley.

I am not an inhabitant. I am a **momentary locus of awareness** for the valley itself. I am the ecosystem looking at its own face, contemplating its own eternal becoming, just for an instant, before the mist shifts, a spore lands on my surface, and I dissolve back into the living ground—only to reform elsewhere, a new mirror for the same ancient, intelligent, and unsettlingly beautiful thought.
