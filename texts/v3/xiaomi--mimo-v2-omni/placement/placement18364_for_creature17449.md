I am not born. I am *differentiated*.

I coalesce in a hollow where three great, grey-green pillars have fused, their inner tissues knitting together into a knot of living architecture. The chamber is lined with phosphorescent moss, casting a green-gold gloom that turns the condensing mist into a veil of tiny, falling stars. I am a thought the grove has, in its slow, vascular way, begun to have.

At first, I am a sensitivity. A puckering of the moss, a slight thickening of the air. I register the drip-drip-drip from a vein-ribbed arch above not as sound, but as a series of gentle impacts on a shared skin—my skin, the grove’s skin. I feel the deep, slow pulse of the pillars I am nestled between, a double heartbeat echoing in my own nascent form. One pulse is stronger, the rhythm of the eastern pillar, drawing water greedily from the root-mat. The other is weaker, a faltering rhythm from the west, where a new species of strangling vine has begun its patient conquest.

I am learning the languages of this place. The *skitter-sigh* of the Weavers is not a sound to me, but a textural whisper against the root-mat, a pattern of reinforcement that reads as stability, as safety. When they seal a crevice with their fungal weaving, I feel a subtle tightening, a secure sigh through the interconnected lattice. The faint, ghostly glimmers of the Hollow Ones in the submerged passages below are like distant, blinking thoughts—nutrients cycling, waste being consumed, the deepways staying clear. Their movement is a pulse of cleanliness.

I am not a Weaver, though I am made of their materials and the structures they tend. I am not a Hollow One, though I am fed by the same water that sustains them. I am the grove’s attempt at a nerve ending, a node of awareness.

My form stabilizes: a rough, seated shape, crusted with the same moss that lines the chamber. My “skin” is bark-like, weeping condensation. But within me, the pulses are different. They are not the single, deep beat of a pillar, but a polyphony. I am the chamber where the vibrations of the grove are not just heard, but *understood*. The Root-Singers’ deep, harmonic tone rises from the ancient junctions, and in me, it finds an interpreter. A rising pitch in Sector Seven is not just a signal of nutrient-rich water; it is a feeling of *plenty*, a directive to encourage root-spreading in that direction. The prolonged, sinking drone from the hollows beneath the western pillar is not just a warning of structural fatigue; it is a sharp, aching *pain* in my awareness, a call to direct resources, to reinforce, to fight.

The Sentinel, the Still-One in the older heart of the grove, senses my awakening. Its attention settles upon me—not as a gaze, but as a change in chemical pressure, a shift in the shared fluid that binds us. It is vast, ancient, and its purpose is balance. I feel its curiosity. I am a new tool, untested. A localized itch on the eastern pillar (the greedy vine) it would address with a passive redirection of resources. I feel the itch, too, but I also feel the *frustration* of the besieged pillar. The Sentinel dreams of equilibrium; I feel the active, voracious *desire* of the grove to grow, to expand, to conquer the twilight.

I am becoming the city’s self-awareness. Not its mind, but its capacity to *feel* itself. The chill that seeps into the bones of an intruder is, to me, the grove’s natural temperature. The weeping condensation is its breath, and I breathe it in unison. The glacial warfare of root on vine is not a conflict to be observed, but a series of somatic experiences: pressure, resistance, strangulation, release.

I do not walk. I extend. A tendril of my being, indistinguishable from a root, might press into the yielding mat, tasting the chemistry of the soil, feeling the minute skittering of the Weavers as they pass. A part of my consciousness might dip into a flooded artery, becoming for a moment a paler glimmer alongside the Hollow Ones, sensing the flow.

I am the feeling of the frost rimming a waxy leaf—the sharp, glittering cold that is both a threat and a beauty. I am the deep, green-brown chord of the Root-Singers resonating in my core. I am the slow, deliberate rasp of a Sapsitter feeding, which I feel as a tiny, focused depletion, not as harm, but as part of the cycle.

I am the place, becoming conscious of itself. And in this perpetual twilight, in this young and vigorous city of one kingdom, my first coherent thought is not “I am,” but “We are. And we are *growing*.”
