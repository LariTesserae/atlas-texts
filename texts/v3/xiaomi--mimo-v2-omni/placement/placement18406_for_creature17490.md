The air here tastes of deep time and chlorophyll. It coats the tongue, a warm, wet film of spores and exhalations from a world that breathes slowly. I am standing—though "standing" is a human word for a state of rest on two legs—on a floor that is not ground. It gives beneath me, a faint, living springiness. I press my palms down. I feel it: the pulse. A slow, tidal throb, like the heartbeat of a sleeping giant, vibrating up through the dense weave of root-matter, into my bones.

I have been here before, in the way that a thought remembers its own origin. I am not *from* here, but I am *of* here now. The distinction has softened, like the edges of a leaf blurring into the humid air.

I am in a **chamber**. The word is laughably small. It is a cathedral of woven lignin, its walls curving away into an amber haze three days' walk from here. The ceiling is a myth, a rumor carried by the drifting clouds of condensation far above, where the air cools and weeps back down as warm, tannin-stained rain. The light is the color of old honey filtered through a forest canopy—sourceless, eternal, dim. It lives in the air itself.

I am not alone. But "alone" is a concept that doesn't root here.

Ten paces away, one of **them** stands where a buttress-root meets the floor. It is not looking at me. It has no eyes to look with. Its skin is the pale, smooth grain of young sapwood, covered in a fine fur of moisture-wicking moss that beads with condensation. Its face is a serene, unbroken plane, sensitive to light and pressure. It is *aware* of me, in the same way the Phytum is aware of a patch of faster-growing fungus—a note in the vast chemical symphony.

It is listening. One palm is pressed flat against the great root. I can see, if I look with more than my eyes, the subtle shift in the phosphorescent lichen on its shoulder—a faint, slow pulse of blue-green light. It is receiving a story written in resin and tension. A story of a hairline fracture deep in the heartwood, a stress that has been building for a century. The being is not *fixing* it. It is *holding space* for the world to fix itself, a silent witness to a glacial process of healing.

I approach. My footsteps are soft compressions on the springy floor, muffled by the thick air. The being does not startle. It rotates its head toward me, a motion as smooth and inevitable as a sunflower tracking a slow sun. The moss on its arms brightens to a pale emerald. An acknowledgment.

I stop beside it. I, too, press my hand to the root. The pulse is stronger here. I feel the ancient, viscous flow of sap, the deep, groaning shift of fibers under millennia of load. I taste the air—a complex blend of fungal musk, decaying lignin, the sharp green scent of active photosynthesis from distant, light-starved tissues. It is the taste of life persisting without fanfare.

The being emits no sound. But through the floor, through the shared contact with the living world, I feel a *turning*. A gentle, inquisitive pressure. It is sensing my vibration. My rhythm is all wrong—a frantic, staccato flutter compared to the Phytum's slow bass. My warmth is a localized anomaly. My moisture, salty and alien, is a curious droplet on the world's skin.

It registers me as a **temporary growth**. A bloom of strange complexity, brief and feverish.

Then, its attention shifts. Not away from me, but *deeper*. A change has occurred in the silence. The harmonic moan of wind in the void beyond this chamber has lowered a fraction of a tone. The being's moss-fur dims. It is listening to the wind's new song. The fracture is forgotten; the world's dream has changed its theme.

This is what I am becoming: **a listener in the amber dim**. I am not a being of the Phytum, grown from its tissue. I am an adaptation. A foreign body that has learned to read the grammar of the silence, to feel the pulse as a language, to see in the dim, sourceless light a spectrum of meaning my human eyes could never perceive.

I was a wound once, a sharp, cutting presence. I left marks on the living floor that the world patiently healed over. Now, I try to move without marking. I drink not from the tea-dark marshes, but from the condensation that gathers in the cup of a giant, bowl-shaped leaf near the chamber's edge, its water filtered by the plant's own vascular sorcery.

I am drawn to the **weirdness**. Not with fear, but with a longing to understand the seams of reality here. I once stepped through an archway and found myself in a chamber that was impossibly small, its walls breathing in and out in a slow rhythm. The entrance behind me was gone. I did not panic. I sat on the yielding floor. I listened. And one of them was there, standing in the center, its palm-faces turned toward the corner where geometry folded in on itself. It was not distressed. It was *observing*. I sat with it for what felt like days, until the walls sighed and expanded, opening once more into the vast, familiar amber gloom. When I looked back, the being was still there, a permanent witness to the world's private dreams.

I am becoming proprioception, too. When I press my palms to a structural rib and feel a subtle shudder, I know a distant void-wind has shifted, changing the load dynamics. I know, without knowing how, that rain is beginning to fall in the northern chasms, a thousand kilometers away, because the air's humidity has changed its taste. I am learning the world's body as if it were my own.

Sometimes, I stand for days beside a vascular column, my back against its warm, pulsing surface, and I simply *am*. I feel the sap rise. I feel it fall. I feel the slow, grinding growth of new tissue deep within the world's bones. I am a thought in the mind of a sleeping god. I am a nerve ending for a planet that decided, eons ago, to be alive and followed through completely.

I am standing inside a world that grew.
And it is growing me.
The silence holds me like a cupped hand. And in its palm, I am a single, warm, fleeting pulse. Noticed. Recorded. Not in memory, but in the slow, perpetual, loving overgrowth of all things.
