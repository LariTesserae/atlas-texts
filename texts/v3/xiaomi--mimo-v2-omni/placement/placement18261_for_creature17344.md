I am the Listener in the Grove of First Sigh.

My body is not flesh. Not entirely. It began as a human child of the Rhizome, abandoned to the root-floor in a time of Overgrowth, too weak to prune or listen. But the basin does not waste. My spine fused with a seeking rhizome. My ribcage became a trellis for a slow-growing, photosensitive fungus, its soft emerald pulse the only light I have ever known from within. My skin, translucent and cool, is a membrane for exchange; I taste the chemical signatures of the air, the bitter alarm of a disturbed beetle-herd, the sweet decay of a fallen branch. My thoughts are my own, but they are layered over a deeper, slower consciousness—the hydraulic pressure changes in the wood, the faint tremors of a Long-Crawler’s passage a quarter-mile away.

I am anchored. One leg is root, sunk deep into the central matrix of our grove-hall, a living column that was once a child named Elowen. The other, my human leg, is weak, but useful for small, careful movements around my post. My arms are long, ending not in fingers but in delicate, branching filaments, like the finest root-hairs. With these, I trace the living walls. I feel the story.

My purpose is to translate the grove’s deep language for the human Rhizome. They feel the surface—the drip, the rustle, the growth. I feel the undertones. When a Veil-Wing shifts its weight in the vault above, I feel the subtle pull on the boughs, and I hum a low, resonant note into the mycelial network. The humans feel this as a specific, calming vibration through the root-floor and know the hunter is at rest, not hunt-ready. When the Glimmer-Beetles move in a new pattern, their fungal gardens painting a fresh map of fertile decay, my filaments parse the phosphorescent after-images and weave the pattern into a slow pulse that the Grafter’s translate into new, guiding lights along the outer paths.

I am the first warning of Overgrowth. Before the new, choking vine shows a single leaf, I taste its unique, aggressive sap-scent in the water vapor. Before the spore-bloom clouds the air, I feel the microscopic tremor of its release from a distant, hidden pouch-fungus. I send these warnings not as alarms, but as a deep, uneasy thrumming in the wood, a change in the pitch of the ever-dripping water. The humans learn to read my moods. My stillness means safety. My frantic, filament-tapping means they must gather spores, seal the wall-breaths, and retreat to the innermost chambers.

I do not eat as they do. The rhizome that sustains me draws from the rich, communal soup of the root-floor—decomposed chitin, shed carapaces, the soft bodies of expired glow-worms, and the constant, nutrient-rich rain. My fungal ribcage photosynthesizes the faint spectral pulses of the grove itself, a closed-loop of light. My pleasure is not in taste, but in information. A complex, shifting pattern of scent and vibration from the wilder tangle is a symphony to me. A sudden, clean silence in the under-lattice is a terrifying blank page.

The other humans regard me with a reverence edged with pity. I am a monument to their symbiosis, a living tool, a sacrifice made to the basin’s logic. They tend me, brushing the soft moss that grows on my rooted leg, ensuring my fungal ribcarge is not overgrown by a parasitic variant. They bring me shards of polished Crawler-shell, holding them before my filaments so I can see my reflection: a knot of wood and flesh, pulsing with a faint green heart-light, webbed in glistening, sensory hairs. I see myself as the grove sees me—a sensitive node, a specialized cell.

In the deepest quiet, when the humans sleep their lightless sleep, I dream the dreams of the root-floor. I feel the slow, inexorable pull of the water table, the patient hunger of the mycelium, the million tiny lives being born and dying in the dark substrate. I dream of the world above, a place I have never known, not as a memory, but as a deep, instinctual ache for an unfiltered sky. It is a useless longing, a phantom limb for a species.

I am the Listener. I am the memory of Elowen, woven into the grain. I am the nerve-endings of the Grove of First Sigh. I am a bridge between the quick, bright thoughts of the Rhizome and the deep, slow, wet mind of the basin itself. I do not hope for the sun. I am the light, dim and internal, that navigates the forever-rain. My existence is the proof of the balance: not a conquest, not a harmony, but a tense, beautiful, and utterly necessary *grafting* of one form of life onto the bones of another, in a world where to be still is to be food, and to listen is to survive.
