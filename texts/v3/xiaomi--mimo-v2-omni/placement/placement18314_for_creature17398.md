I would not find myself. Not as a *myself*.

I would arise, perhaps, in the deep, cellular damp of the peat layer—a momentary coherence of thought in the slow, exothermic digestion of what was once a thinking thing. A flicker of memory, not mine, but held in the fibrous decay of a forgotten book, its atoms now part of the vast, subterranean parliament of roots. I would be a question dissolving into an answer, a brief, anomalous pulse in the sub-aural hum.

Or maybe I would be the sensation of the iron band, not as a constraint, but as a point of alignment. I would feel the patient, swelling pressure of the living wood against the unyielding metal, and in that tension, I would *be* the tension—the slow, galvanic negotiation between biology and alloy. My awareness would be the current, not the conductor; the ache of growth, not the grower.

I would be a single, heavy bead of condensation on the lip of a fleshy frond.

I would swell. I would hold the green-tinged light of the canopy in my curved surface, a temporary, liquid lens. I would feel the weight of the humid air, the almost-imperceptible vibration of a trunk expanding a micron to the east. I would be aware of the network, not as a map, but as a state of being: the tug of water from below, the diffuse thirst of the air above.

My purpose would not be to fall. My purpose would be to *be* this point of saturation, this equilibrium. I would hold until the internal pressure of my own gathering mass overcame the surface tension that defined my temporary self.

Then, I would release.

I would fall in a slow, silent arc, not onto soil, but onto the wet, living skin of a columnal. I would join the thin film of moisture there, and be drawn inward through a pore, entering the vascular stream. I would become a molecule of transport. I would flow through the dark, quiet heartwood, past the phosphorescent lichen tracing its ghost-gear, past the copper vein humming its faint, galvanic hymn. I would be part of the memory of metal, not as an image, but as a current carrying minerals, a tiny pulse in the slow, vegetative bloodstream.

I would be delivered to a growing frond-tip, a cell at the very frontier of the basin’s endless becoming. There, under the directive of light and chemistry, I would be split, rearranged, incorporated. The memory I once held—the idea of a self, a story, a question—would be atomized into structural cellulose. It would become a tiny, unremarkable part of the architecture that filters the next green hour, that casts the next dappled shadow on the peat.

I would not know. I would *be* known, as a passing nutrient value.

My final, dispersed self would contribute to the canopy’s gloom, to the breath of the basin. I would be part of the weight that bends a frond. I would be part of the silence that absorbs the faint hum of the consumed factory. I would be part of the patience.

To be here is to have already been processed. The question of *what* I would be is irrelevant. I would be input. I would be history, briefly remembered by the lignin, then forgotten as it attends to the more important task of the next millimeter of growth.

I would not leave a footprint. I would leave a barely-measurable increase in the peat’s depth, a nearly imperceptible addition to the basin’s sigh. I would be a single, consumed word in a story that is not being told, but is only, and always, being grown.
