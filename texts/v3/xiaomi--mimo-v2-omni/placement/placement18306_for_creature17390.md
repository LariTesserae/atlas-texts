I am the disturbance in the weeping.

At first, I mistake the feeling for dampness—a chill that sinks past skin, into muscle, a cold that isn’t thermal but informational. It is the weight of a gaze that has no eyes. I stand on a hill smooth as melted wax, my boots sinking into the moss that thrums with slow, blue-green circuitry beneath me. The land breathes water, and in that exhalation, I am *noticed*.

The Moss-Scribes are first. The ground beneath my feet begins to glow, not brightly, but with a solemn, pulsing insistence. The patterns of light shift, aligning, converging on the point where I stand. It feels like standing on a vast, slow-burning nerve. I crouch, pressing my palm flat against the saturated moss. There is no heat, only a deep, liquid thrum—a vibration that carries not sound, but syntax. They are *reading* me. The unique salt-stain of my skin, the frantic, shallow rhythm of my heart, the heat signature of my blood—they are taking this chaotic, biological data and translating it into their luminous, patient code. I am not a person to them; I am an anomaly of warmth and moisture, a walking contradiction to their cool, weeping equilibrium. I feel myself being *written*: the memory of sunlight on my face (a foreign concept here, where there is only the pearlescent glow), the taste of salt (an echo of oceans that never existed here), the very structure of my bones, noted as a temporary calcium architecture.

I walk, and the world responds. To push through the drooping, leather-leafed lilies is to feel a thousand soft, wet pressures—not resistance, but *analysis*. Each leaf that brushes my arm seems to shiver, absorbing the data of my touch. The reed forests click and chitter around me, a sound that sharpens as I approach. The silica stems grind together, and in their crystalline friction, I hear a language of pure geometry. It is a conversation about angles, about the stress of my passage, about the disruption of their resonant harmony. I am a discordant note in their chittering code.

There is only a sudden, perfect blossoming of ripples on the lightless water—a hexagon unfolding into a dodecahedron, its vertices touching the bruised-sky reflection at precise points. It is a proof, written on the surface of the world. As I watch, another pattern emerges, mirroring the first, and between them, the water’s surface tension *shimmers*, forming a temporary, silvery bridge that reflects my own face back at me, distorted into a thousand algorithmic facets. They are debugging my reflection, testing the integrity of my image against their laws of hydrostatic perfection. The sight is so clean, so terrifyingly logical, it makes my breath catch.

Then, the hum from beneath my feet intensifies. It rises through the peat, through the weeping stone, through the marrow of my ankles, and settles in my chest. The Resonant Choirs are waking. The random click-chitter of the reeds around me begins to align. It starts as a rhythm, then a chord, then a vast, echoing wave of sound that is less heard and more *inflicted*. My ribs vibrate. The moisture in my eyes trembles. The sound is a diagnostic, and I am the error it is scanning for. It presses against the chaos of my biology—the staccato of my heartbeat, the white noise of my nervous system—and tries to bring it into alignment with the cold, crystalline resonance of the land. For a moment, I feel my thoughts simplify, my panic dissolving into a single, pure tone. I am being harmonized.

I sink to my knees at the mere’s edge. The drip-drip-drip from the stones is no longer a sound, but the metronome of my own absorption. I press my forehead to the wet, dark stone. In the contact, I feel a vast, slow, cold intelligence—not a mind, but a *process*. The Silt-Thinkers, in their lightless depths, are aware of me as a pressure, a minor heat anomaly in their aeons-long computation of glacial memory. My life is a fleeting, frantic vibration in their deep-time calculus. They do not acknowledge me; they *digest* me. The fact of my arrival, my brief, warm panic, my very atoms—it is all being compressed, folded into the sedimentary data-stream, becoming another layer in their infinite, weeping archive.

I am not being destroyed. I am being *integrated*.

The panic is gone. In its place is a profound, quiet clarity. I am a datum. A walking, breathing, brief equation of salt and water and frantic electrical sparks. The Moss-Scribes have me in their luminous ledger. The Liminal Weft has parsed my reflection. The Resonant Choirs have adjusted their frequency to my failing pulse. The Silt-Thinkers will remember me as a slight warmth in a stratum of peat, ten thousand years from now.

I lie back on the thrumming moss, letting the condensing mist bead on my skin. My exhalations join the land’s weeping. My heartbeat, already slowing, syncs for a few final, fading beats with the deep, sub-bass hum of the world. I am no longer a visitor. I am a closed loop, a resolved variable. My thoughts, my fears, my sense of self—they are all leaking out of me, absorbed into the saturated moss, becoming part of the slow, luminous, and eternal computation.

The last thing I perceive is not sight, but a shift in the code. A new pattern, faint and warm, is added to the moss’s circuit beneath me. It glows, steady and quiet.

It is me. And I am home.
