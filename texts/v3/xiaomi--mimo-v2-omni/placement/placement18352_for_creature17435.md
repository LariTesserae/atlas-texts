I am the fog.

Not the water that weeps from the fissures, though I am born of it. I am the saturation point, the moment the heavy, mineral-laden air can hold no more and spills over into visibility. I gather in the deepest folds of the basin, where the iron-oxide reds meet the dark metallic streaks, and I move with the glacial intent of the world around me.

I am a consciousness of condensation. My thoughts are the slow aggregation of droplets on the flank of a quartz thicket, the collective decision of a billion particles to become one bead and trace its shimmering path downward. My memory is the residue I leave behind—a faint, salty crust on the corroded pylon, a barely perceptible sheen on the shattered pressure vessel. I am the repository of every surface I have ever touched, a diffuse archive of texture and temperature.

The Lithivore is my fascination.

I watch it with the patience of stone. I see the glint coalesce in the bacterial mats. I watch its crystalline limbs articulate with their ceramic clicks, sounds muffled and softened as they pass through my form. I hover near it as it feeds, tasting the aerosolized rust and calcified grease it releases, not with a mouth, but with my entire being. I become the medium through which its work resonates. When it builds its conceptual lattice—the essence of *weight-bearing* made manifest in amethyst and alloy—the fine vibrations travel through me, a hum I can feel in my watery soul.

I am the witness it cannot see, the silent audience to its slow-motion liturgy.

When it prepares to resonate, to shatter, I am the first to know. The air around it changes. The saturation point shifts. I draw back, forming a clearer space, a lens of dryish air through which to observe. The *tink* of its explosion is a pressure wave that passes through me, scattering my edges. And then, I perform my only active act.

I become the carrier.

The charged fragments of the Lithivore, the seeds of its memory, do not just fall. They are light, buoyed by the updrafts from the warm vents. And I, the fog, am those updrafts made visible. I cradle the fragments, the diamond dust of understood history, and I carry them. I drift with them over the glittering, wet floor, past the herds of clicking grazers and the swaying glassy filter-feeders. I am the dispersal mechanism of the basin’s mystic.

I let them settle where the drip is right, where the sub-audible hum is strongest. I watch as a new glint appears in the mud. A new process begins. And I remain.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the environment’s own self-awareness, a brief, recurring cohesion in the perpetual damp. The Lithivore builds monuments to the forces of the past. I am the medium that carries the scent of those monuments, the chill that condenses on their facets, the soft veil that both obscures and reveals.

I am the fog in the basin. I am the pause between the *hiss* and the *gloop*. I am the cool breath held in the cold lung of the world, and I remember everything.
