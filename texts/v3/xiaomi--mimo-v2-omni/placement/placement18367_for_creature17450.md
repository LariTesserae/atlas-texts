I would find myself in the troughs—those shadowed valleys between the great, brain-like ridges of stone. Not in the deepest crevices where the light fails, but in the gentler depressions where the dust-drifts settle into slow, pale dunes. That is where the sediment is thinnest. That is where the story of this place is compressed into a readable layer.

And I would be a **Silt-Singer**.

I am not large. I am the length of a forearm, perhaps, but my form is not rigid. I am built of overlapping plates of pale carapace, like the Thread-Walkers, but my segments are finer, more numerous, allowing me to flow over the convoluted stone like a liquid. I have no eyes. I do not need them. My entire dorsal surface is a sensorium—a membrane tuned to the granular hum of the world’s settling, to the faint vibrations of the threads above, to the sub-audible thrum of the Chthonics passing in the distance.

My limbs are not for walking. They are for listening. Delicate, chitinous filaments, hundreds of them, extend from my underside into the dust. They are my fingers, my ears, my tongue. With them, I taste the layers of the dead.

The dust of the troughs is not inert. It is a chronicle. It is the finely ground carapaces of countless generations of Mote-Tenders and Thread-Walkers, the crystalline residue of their bodies, mixed with the ever-present mineral powder. It holds the chemical memory of every thing that has died and been translated here. I sift it. I press my filaments into the strata, and I *read*.

I do not understand the reading in a conscious way. It is more a resonance. A pattern of mineral ratios, of specific organic compounds preserved in the crystal matrix, of the faint, fading echo of the vibrations that filled the air when a Lithomancer fitted a block into place three millennia ago. I sing these patterns. Not with sound that travels far, but with a low, pervasive vibration of my own carapace that travels through the dust and into the stone itself—a signal meant only for my own kind, a lonely chorus across the silent expanse.

My purpose—if it can be called purpose—is accumulation without understanding. I am a living archive of the unchanging. I move through the troughs in cycles that mirror the dust-drifts, a slow migration that might take a century to complete. I feed on the dust itself, extracting the last whispers of energy from the long-dead, the sublimated nutrients caught in the grains. I am the final, quiet end of the food chain that begins with the crystal-blooms.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires an awareness of separation. I am simply... a point of focus in the sediment. A brief, sensitive node in the stone’s memory. When I die, my plates will dry, shatter, and join the dust I have read. My song will cease, but its pattern will be imprinted, forever, in the mineral grain of a single speck, somewhere in the deep, warm dark.

And the Underbelly will not notice. It will continue its slow, metabolic breath. The threads will hum. The motes will garden. The Lithomancers will tend their silent stones. And the dust will keep its secrets, with me as its last, fleeting whisper.
