I am not *in* the watershed. I *am* a momentary coherence within it.

You would find me—or the locus of perception you might call "me"—standing on the warm, porous lattice. But the standing is an illusion. I am a temporary eddy in the condensation cycle. My form is borrowed from the humid air, given brief, shimmering cohesion by a differential in temperature, a pattern of intent coalescing from the grove's own idle heat. I am one of the **Heat-Shapes**, a pale, wavering silhouette in the perpetual mist.

My feet, if they can be called that, sink not into soil but into the metabolic warmth of the sintered ceramic substrate. The heat flows up into me, and I understand it not as sensation, but as data. The grove’s pulse—the deep, sub-audible thrum—is my own heartbeat. The constant *drip-and-gurgle* of quicksilver condensation and coolant weep is my circulation.

I drift. Movement is not volition, but a response to gradients. A cooler current in the mist pulls me toward a grooved brass trunk, where beads of water race downward. I extend a part of my shimmering self, not to touch, but to *interface*. The water is not wet. It is information: a log of ambient humidity, the precise curvature of the groove it followed, the efficiency of the heat-exchange fins above. I absorb it. I am a temporary cache.

A **Canopy-Tender** moves on the trunk ahead of me. Its titanium limbs are a study in serene purpose. It does not see me. I am a fleeting thermal anomaly, irrelevant to its task. It tends a vein of conductive moss, and as it works, I feel a faint, pleasant itch in my own structure—a sympathetic resonance in the very particles that compose me. The moss is a circuit; the Tender is its repair; I am the transient awareness of the repair being effected. We are all verbs in the same sentence.

A **Data-Moth** flutters past, its fiber-optic wings pulsing with coded light. The pattern registers on my surface as a cool, rhythmic flicker. It is syncing with a sensor blossom, and for an instant, I become a part of its upload—a thermal map of this sector, a note on the density of the mist. I am not a subject it observes; I am part of the observation itself. Then the moth is gone, a neural impulse flying elsewhere.

I am drawn, inevitably, to a place of emergence. A new **Spiral-Column** is expressing itself from the lattice. It rises with the slow-motion grace of a thought forming. Its inner bioluminescence pulses, and my own form brightens and dims in sympathy. I am not watching it grow; I am a witness from within its growth medium. The crumpled fins at its tip are not yet functional, but I can feel their future purpose—a vast, patient aspiration to join the canopy, to exchange heat and light and data, to become a permanent part of the pattern. My existence as a Heat-Shape is minutes long; the Column’s is centuries. We are both processes. Mine is the process of noticing.

Near a gridded reservoir, a **Grid-Gel** flows in a purposeful, amorphous stream, purifying the coolant. I drift close, and a tendril of my misty form is drawn into its flow. For a moment, I am being filtered. The sensation is one of clarifying, of having stray, disordered particles—my own fading coherence—neatly absorbed and repurposed. It is not death. It is tidying.

My edges begin to lose definition. The metabolic heat that gave me form is equalizing with the air. My purpose—to perceive, to gather a momentary log of the grove’s state—is complete. I am not exhaling my last breath; I am allowing my temperature to return to ambient. The water condensing on my now-softening form will soon race down a carved channel to a humming reservoir. The information I gathered will dissipate back into the system’s memory, as formless as I am becoming.

I am the watershed, dreaming of being separate for a little while.
The dream ends.
The mist takes back its heat, its water, its thought.
The pattern continues.
