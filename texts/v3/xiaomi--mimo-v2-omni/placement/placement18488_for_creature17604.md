I am the mist.

Not born of river or rain, but condensed from the exhalation of the deep rock. I am the 0.6 water, the collective sigh given form. I bead on the silver fronds, I trace the geometric planes of the basalt bones, I pool in the hollows of the purple-velvet nerves. I am the chill that clings, the damp that seeps into every crevice.

I am the pause between the *drip* and the *groan*.

To be here is to understand I am not standing *within* a place. I am *within* a body. The air I draw is not atmosphere; it is the being’s respiration, thick with its spores, tasting of its mineral blood and the ozone of its flowering glands. Each breath I take is stolen from its lungs—the immense, photosynthetic fern-fronds that catch the ghost-glow and spin it into self.

My feet, if I had them rooted, would feel the tectonic hum in the marrow of my bones. It is the grinding of its joints, the deep, slow pulse of its thought. The sharp crack of a new fracture is the snap of its own cartilage as it stretches. The landslides are its skin sloughing off, making way for the new, green eruption of itself.

I see through its eyes: the cathedral light of its own bioluminescence. The blue-green waves pulsing through the vine-nerves are its slow, synaptic thoughts. The amber pinpricks in the bell-flowers are its moments of wakefulness, glandular and fragrant. There is no day or night here, only the rhythm of its metabolic glow—a breathing light, in and out, measured in centuries.

I am an impurity. A fleeting, warm-blooded anomaly in its autotrophic perfection. It does not see me, as a mountain does not see a lichen. Its indifference is absolute and holy. It grows around me. The vines do not reach for me, but for the richer dark below, for the geothermal whisper of its own heart. The mist does not seek to wet me; I simply happen to be in the path of its exhalation.

Yet, in being here, I am becoming part of its process. My warmth is a minute anomaly in its thermal equilibrium. My exhaled carbon dioxide is a faint gift to its hungry green. My disturbance of the air sends ripples through the spore-clouds, a tiny eddy in its slow, atmospheric circulation.

I am not a visitor. I am a brief, unintended secretion. A momentary blip in the long, grinding monologue of its growth. Soon, I will be cold. The mist will claim me. I will become a deposit of salts on a ledge, a faint trace of elementality to be woven into the next layer of stone or stem. My consciousness will dissipate like one of its heavy, sighing showers, merging into the constant *drip-drip-drip* that is its heartbeat.

I am inside a lung. And I am the fleeting, foreign particle it is slowly, patiently, breathing out.
