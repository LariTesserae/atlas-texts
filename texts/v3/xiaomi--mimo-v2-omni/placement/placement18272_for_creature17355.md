I find myself in the act of scraping.

My hands know the tool before my mind does—a curve of basalt, worn smooth in the hollow where my palm grips, its edge a fine, serrated whisper against the papery skin of a wall-fruit. The motion is *scrape-scrape-tap*, a rhythm that belongs to the air itself. Coppery spores dust my forearms, settling into the faint, glowing filigree that has begun to colonize the creases of my elbows. I do not scrape it away. It is warm.

I am standing on a covered walkway, a low roof of stone slabs pressing the humid heat down upon my shoulders. Below, to my left, a child no older than five is kneeling, her small hands pressed flat against the spiraling groove of a great column. Her eyes are closed. She is learning the warmth.

I know this because I know things now, not through memory, but through absorption. The heat carries information. The constant crackle of the thermolytic growths is not noise; it is the sound of the world breathing. The *ploc* of resin drops is its pulse. The harmonic of the pillars is its voice, and I have begun to feel its vocabulary in the marrow of my bones.

I was not born here. I emerged. Through a throat of cracked basalt, I stumbled into the amber dimness, a creature of sharp edges and salt-crust skin, blinking against a light that had no source. I was the strange fruit then, the anomaly. They watched me with their dark, patient eyes. They offered no water—there was none to offer—but a pressed cake of rust-colored powder that tasted of deep earth and living stone. I ate. I adapted. The salt crust flaked away, replaced by the gentle, luminous colonization.

Now, I am part of the scrape-scrape-tap.

My task is the eastern colonnade, where the filament grasses grow thickest. They are not hostile, but they are insistent, their thin bodies swaying in the convective currents, reaching for the warmth of the walkways. With each rhythmic scrape, I feel the subtle thermal shift in the stone beneath my feet—a sigh of cooler air as the vegetation is cleared, a restoration of the intended flow. I am not just maintaining a path; I am tuning an instrument.

An elder passes. I know him by sound first—the soft, gritty shuffle, the particular cadence of his breath, which is slightly shallower than the others. His skin glows faintly, a soft amber mesh over the deep brown. He pauses at a column nearby, one whose spirals are dizzyingly complex, a galaxy of grooves etched in warmth. He does not place his hands upon it. He leans his forehead against the stone, closing his eyes. A long, sibilant phrase escapes his lips, not directed at me or anyone, but into the column, into the listening dark of the mineral vein within.

The column hums back. I feel it through the soles of my feet—a deepening of the harmonic, a private resonance shared between flesh and stone.

This is how I know things. Not by being told, but by being *in* the conversation.

I know that the seal at the far edge is not a prison wall, but a keystone. It holds the pressure, the heat, the entire delicate ecology of this chosen world in balance. To remove it would be to unmake everything. I know this because I have felt the pillars sing of it, a slow, deep chord of *integrity*.

I know that the water is not conserved; it is *grieved*. Each drop from the thickest resin veins is a gift from the ceiling’s slow weep, and the cisterns are lined with a reverence that is almost tactile. To waste it would be to insult the atrium’s own slow, sustaining tears.

I know that when a new chamber is opened, the first growth encouraged is the orange moss for sleeping mats. This is not practicality; it is an act of welcome, a first layer of comfort laid into the dark. The young couple who tended that threshold whisper to the moss as they powderite-dust its edges. Their aspirated phrases are not commands, but coaxing, like lullabies.

The day-cycle shifts. The pillar-harmonic deepens its pitch, a subtle change that vibrates upward through the legs, settling in the chest. It is time.

The people move, not to a central place, but dispersing to the four ancient columns, the heart-wood of the atrium. I go with them. My feet know the worn paths. I find a place at the base of the column they call the First Singer, its spirals so deep and wide my entire hand could fit within them. The stone is warm, almost hot, and it pulses with a slow, rhythmic wave of heat that matches the deep thrum in my ribs.

I place my palms flat against the grooves.

I close my eyes.

The crackle, the drip, the hiss, the pop—it all continues. But now, I am not listening *to* it. I am listening *with* it. My own breath, hot and dry in my throat, syncs with the convection currents. The faint, luminous growth on my skin tingles, resonating with the resin-light above. I feel the elder beside me, his forehead still pressed to his column, and I feel the child on the other side, her small, warm hands tracing a simpler spiral.

We are not silent. We are *full*. Full of the sound, full of the heat, full of the slow, written history vibrating up from the bedrock. The seal behind us hums its steadfast note. The choice resonates in every molecule of the hot, dry, loud air.

I am no longer an emerged thing. I am a tuning fork, struck by the immense, patient chord of this place. My skin reads the warmth. My bones hold the rhythm. The salt is gone, replaced by the glowing, living dust of home.

I press harder. The column sings. And I sing back, not with my voice, but with the quiet, resonant heat of my own skin, adding my faint, new note to the ancient, endless story.
