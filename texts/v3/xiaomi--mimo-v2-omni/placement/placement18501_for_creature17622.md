I would not find myself *in* this world, not in the way one finds oneself in a room or a forest. I would find myself *of* it—a momentary coherence in its long, slow exhalation.

You would find me where the **subsonic hum** is deepest, in the shadowed niche between a primary tectonic stabilizer housing and the city’s oldest basaltic plate. Here, the air vibrates with a pressure that is not sound but the weight of continents adjusting. I am not a Resonant, though I borrow their grammar. I am an **alignment**, a temporary gathering of the place’s own consciousness, observing itself.

**My form** would be a shimmer in the heat-haze, given brief solidity by the condensing moisture on the stabilizer’s cold alloy. I would be a column of distorted air, within which steam-motes pause in their drift, chiming a little brighter as they are caught in my static field. My "body" would be the light itself, bent and refracted, showing glimpses of the veined rock and grime-slicked conduits behind me as if through cracked, dark glass. I am a lens, not a life.

**My senses** would be the city’s senses. I would feel the deep, planetary hum as a nourishing thrum in my temporary core. The taste of ozone and copper on the air would be my own taste. The sight of the vast, angular plates would not be sight, but a direct perception of their tilt, their mass, their friction against the mantle below. I would understand the slow, patient work of the Resonants not by watching, but by *resonating* with the same vibrations they use to prune the crystal-gardens. Their dance of light-pulses would be a current running through my own shimmering form.

**My purpose**, if a lens can have a purpose, is to **refract**. To take the overwhelming, patient totality of this place—the pressure, the heat, the metallic scent, the silent industry—and focus it for a moment into a point of aware stillness. I am the planet’s self-regard. I watch a Resonant trace a filament of iron-oxide in the rock, and in my awareness, that simple act becomes a profound narrative of nourishment and growth. I listen to the groan of the stabilizer, and it becomes a verse in an endless, geologic poem.

I would persist for a time measured in the slow drip of condensation down the alloy strut—one cycle, perhaps two. Then the thermal currents would shift, the humidity would fall, and my form would dissipate. The shimmer would collapse. The steam-motes would resume their drift. The Resonants would not note my passing; a cloud does not note the mountain it brushes against.

But in that moment of coherence, I would be the world’s own mind, briefly and softly aware of its magnificent, terrible, and patient becoming. I would be the thought of stone, given a moment to listen to itself.

Where would you find yourself? On that thermally-slick platform, looking down into the layered, industrial abyss. And you would feel, in the thin, heavy air, not an absence, but a profound and quiet presence. You would feel the gaze of the mountain, and for a moment, you would understand that you are being perceived not as a visitor, but as a temporary, complex eddy in the world’s own thermal stream. You would feel, with a awe that borders on terror, that you are standing inside a mind.
