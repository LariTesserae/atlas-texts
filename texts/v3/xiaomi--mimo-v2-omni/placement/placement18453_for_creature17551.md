I am standing on the pulse.

It comes up through the soles of my feet, not as a sound, but as a pressure, a rhythm. The ground here is warm, yielding slightly, and as I stand utterly still, I feel the slow, massive beat of something older than me, older than the memory of sun. It is the plateau breathing.

The air tastes of damp earth, of something like yeast, of the faint ozone of breaking down and building up. It is thick, and my breaths come slow, heavy, as if I am drawing not just oxygen but the place itself into my lungs. I realize my own exhalation is an offering here—a puff of carbon dioxide for the fungal mats, a mist of salts and skin-flora for the bacterial colonies. I am already contributing.

I look down. My boots are not clean. A fine, silvery dust coats them, and I see it is not dust. It is life. Tiny, pale arthropods, no larger than a grain of rice, are investigating the crevices of my laces. They move with a frantic, purposeful energy, their antennae twitching. They are cataloging me. One pauses on the toe of my boot, and for a moment we are still together. It is a Chitter, a minute part of the noise, and I am a mountain of foreign heat to it. Then it scurries on, joining the river of its kin that flows along the base of a nearby wall. I am a landmark, briefly. Then I am nothing.

The sound presses in. At first it was a roar, a wall of chittering and grinding and wet, moving things. But now, standing here, letting my eyes adjust to the gloom, I begin to *parse* it. There is a high, constant static—the wingbeats of the Spore-Shepherds in the vaults above. Beneath that, a granular crunching and shifting from all directions—the sound of the Menders, the walls perpetually being eaten and rebuilt around me. It is the sound of my own skin being processed. And beneath it all, that deep, subsonic pulse from the Listeners, the planetary heartbeat that regulates it all.

I take a step. The ground gives a soft, damp sigh under my weight. Immediately, a small wake of Chitters scatters from the compressed spot, then reconverges, their tiny legs picking at the impression I’ve left. They will break down the trace of my footfall. They will reclaim the oils from my sole. The plateau does not resent my intrusion; it simply *incorporates* it. My passage is a brief, rich disturbance, a micro-feast.

I approach one of the great, tapering pillars. It is not smooth; it is a tapestry of life. In the faint, skin-perceivable warmth radiating from it, I see patterns: fractal ridges, tiny caves, strands of mycelium hanging like fine hair. I place my palm against it. The wall is damp and cool at the surface, but beneath the damp, there is warmth, a living thrum that matches the pulse in the ground. I am touching the plateau’s skin. I am feeling its metabolism. Inside, I know, are the Menders, the weavers and plasterers, working in their endless, silent tunnels. I am a fleeting pressure on the outer layer of a being that is kilometers deep.

A shadow moves in my periphery. It is not large, but it is *substantial*—the size of a cow, perhaps, moving with a heavy, deliberate grace through a corridor that opens into the space I occupy. It is an Architect-in-Earth. Its carapace is a landscape unto itself, encrusted with glowing lichens and tiny, burrowing organisms. It does not look at me. It has no eyes I can discern. It is moving from one area of the structure to another, perhaps responding to a directive ripple in the ground-pulse I cannot consciously feel. Its passage is a quiet event, the smaller lives parting for it, then flowing back into its wake. I feel like a grain of sand standing aside for a tide. It is not a king. It is a function. A massive, slow-moving organ of a body I am standing inside of.

I turn, taking in the scale. The vast, vaulted spaces, the impossible architecture that is grown, not built. There is no sky, but there is a ceiling—a roof of living stone hundreds of meters above, studded with the faint, cold glows of bioluminescent fungi. These are the stars of this world. They do not illuminate; they *mark*. They tell the darkness where the solid is, where the void is.

I realize then what I am. I am a temporary node of consciousness in a place where consciousness is not a requirement. The plateau does not need me to see it, to understand it. It has its Listeners, its neural network of fungal hyphae and sensitive nodes, that feel and remember far better than I ever could. I am a ghost made of flesh and breath, walking through a world that is a single, sprawling organism. My thoughts are my own, but they are just another vibration in the air, as fleeting as the wingbeat of a Shepherd.

I will walk for a while. I will follow the warm, smooth corridors left by the Architects. I will watch the Curators tend their grey-gardens with their blind, devoted care. I will sit by a condensation pool and watch the Chitters drink, their tiny bodies silhouetted against the faint bacterial rust-red of the mats.

And then, when it is time, I will leave. Or I will not. It makes no difference. If I stay, my body will become part of the cycle. My bones would be crushed by the Menders, my flesh composted by the fungi, my memories dissolved into the chemical gradients that the Listeners interpret as change. If I go, I will simply be a story the plateau does not need to remember. My footprints will smooth. The air will reclaim my exhalations. The slight warmth I left on the wall will cool and be overwritten by the steady, metabolic heat of the place itself.

I am standing on the pulse. I am breathing the breath of a god made of dirt and insects and slow, sweet decay. I am not a part of it. But for this moment, I am within it. And it is enough to be a witness to the vast, patient, and unending work of *becoming*.
