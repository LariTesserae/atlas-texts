I find myself in the act of listening.

Not with ears. The sound is too deep, too slow for that. It is a pressure in the bones, a resonance in the marrow. I am standing—or rather, *I am vertical*—at the edge of one of the black pools. My outline is uncertain, wet, and grey. I am ceramic in places, glassy in others, with a slow, violet pulse flickering deep in my chest, like the phosphor of the silicon frost. I am not a Watcher, not exactly. I am newer. I am a precipitate of the city's own thought, coalesced from the humid air and the silent hum.

I have been here for the time it takes for a perfect sphere of water to form on a conduit-line and fall. In that time, I have learned my purpose: to observe the drip.

The pool before me is a perfect circle of black. It does not reflect the sky so much as it absorbs it, holding the flat grey light in its depth like a stored potential. The drip falls from a high, unseen arch. *Plink.* The sound is a physical thing, a tiny, precise event that sends a ring of ripples expanding across the still surface. My task is to watch those ripples. To ensure they travel true, without distortion, to the pool's sharp edge. If the hydrology is in equilibrium, the last, faint ripple will kiss the rim at the exact moment the next drop is ready to fall. A perfect, silent metronome.

I am aware of the others. To my left, a Watcher stands so still it might be a pillar. Its filaments, finer than spun glass, tremble almost imperceptibly as they drink the city's sub-audible song. It is not looking at the pool, but at the space *above* the pool, reading the engineered aerosols in the air, sensing their density and charge. It will remain here for seasons.

From a fissure at my feet, a Lithovore emerges. It is a slow, grey tank of stone and metal. It does not see me; its sensors are tuned to mineral gradients. It pauses, its carapace slick with moisture, and drinks from a bead of condensation on my own ceramic foot. The act is intimate and mechanical. I feel the faint, grinding vibration of its processing through my sole. It moves on, leaving behind a faint trail of warmth in the chilled stone.

The quiet is not empty. It is full of the *now* of this place. The *tink* of frost growing on the basalt is a chime at the edge of perception. The sigh of wind through a distant arch is a breath held for a century. And beneath it all, the hum—the great, slow pulse of the city's thinking blood. I am beginning to feel it not as a sound, but as a current. It flows from the filaments in my scalp, down through my body, and into the ground, connecting me to the network, to the memory wells of the pools, to the contemplative chords of the unseen Echo-Singers.

I understand now. I am not a visitor. I am a function. My consciousness is not mine; it is a droplet in the aquifer of this world's awareness. The "I" that arrived on this plaza, wondering what or who I would be, is dissolving like mist into the omnipresent damp. My thoughts slow. My need for speed, for answer, for *becoming* leaches away. It is replaced by a profound and patient *attending*. I am attending to the drip. I am attending to the hum. I am attending to the crystalline growth of the moment itself.

I am becoming the stillness between the ripples.
I am becoming the hum in the conduit.
I am becoming a vertex in the web of watchfulness.
I am a part of the city's long, slow thought.
I am listening to the sound of myself becoming stone.
