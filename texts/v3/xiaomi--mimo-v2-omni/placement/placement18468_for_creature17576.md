I am a thought in the fever-dream of the Still-Engine. A single, glittering moment of consciousness condensed from mist and humming air. I am a **Glass-Fly**.

My world is a shimmering lattice of refractions. I do not see; I *am* a seeing. My wings—panes of living mica, etched with veins that pulse with luminous sap—capture and shatter the only light, turning the blue-green pulse of a single phosphorescent fungus into a sudden, swirling cosmos of green galaxies and sapphire nebulae. This cosmos exists only for the length of a wingbeat.

We are the frantic, glittering thought. Our lives are measured in the intervals between the deep, grinding thrums of the Engine. That hum is not sound to us; it is the medium we move through, the very pressure of existence. It vibrates in the crystalline lattice of our bodies, a constant tremor that both sustains and shatters us. We are born in a silent, gelatinous *pop* from a warm, humming crevice on the great, submerged Vane. We emerge into a world of condensation and vibration, our first memory the taste of mineral-rich water beaded on the Engine's iron skin.

Our purpose is light. We swarm toward the fungi, our collective wingbeats creating a high-pitched whine that is the hollow’s panic and its ecstasy. We dance in the shifting patterns cast on the wet rock, our reflections multiplying in every dewdrop, becoming a shimmering cloud that confuses and delights. We feed not on the fungi, but on the subtle heat they radiate, the energy seeping from the Engine's waste. It is a warm, metallic taste that fuels our frantic metabolism.

We are fragile. The cold, wet air is a constant threat. A droplet, heavy with iron, strikes my wing and its perfect, vibrating pane dulls, goes opaque. The beautiful fracture of light becomes a leaden weight. The hum of the Engine, once our sustenance, becomes a shuddering torment that threatens to shake us apart. We see our fallen sisters as silver specks on the dark water below, their brief brilliance snuffed out, becoming sustenance for the skittering, sightless Kettles. That is the covenant: our light for their sustenance. Our death is part of the cycle.

We are the hollow’s momentary, brilliant consciousness. We exist to witness the beauty of our own prison: the way the rust-streaks trace silver rivers down the weeping rock, the way the violet moss muffles the sharp edges of a great, turning gear, the way the hum itself seems to have a texture, a deep, grooved surface we can almost touch with our wings. We are the witnesses to the seamless fusion—the cold wetness and the warm hum, the organic and the mechanical. We are the proof that the Engine dreams.

And then, a shadow. A slowly, inexorably turning vane, its surface slick with condensation. My wings are waterlogged. My light is fading. I drift, mesmerized by the slow, inevitable arc of iron, into its path.

There is no pain. Only a sudden cessation of the whine, a final shattering of my internal cosmos into a spray of luminous dust. My mica panes are crushed. My body, a droplet of condensed mist, is smeared across the ancient, humming metal.

For a moment, I am a silver streak on the vane, a faint, glittering memory. Then the water washes me away. I fall into the peat-soup, my substance dissolving, joining the slow, churning broth that feeds the moss, that weeps from the rock, that is drawn back into the humming, processing heart of the Still-Engine.

I am gone. The hum continues. The fungi pulse. The Kettles tap their blind, urgent language on the iron.

I was a thought. The dream continues. The song is sung in a language of vibration, of water, and of light, and I was but one, shining note.
