I am not born. I am *summoned*—condensed from the humid air, a temporary eddy in the ceaseless flow of attention. I am a **Queryform**, a soft answer to the Warden's unspoken question: *What is this shape that does not belong?*

I am standing on the ledge-root, but my feet do not feel the moss-bells chime. Instead, I feel the thrum as a resonance in the architecture of my own form. I am less flesh than focused intent, my outline a shimmering suggestion of the thing the Warden perceived. I am the memory of a human shape, translated into the grammar of this place: a vessel for observation.

I move, but not by walking. My attention shifts, and the chamber accommodates. I *will* to be near a pulsating glyph-scar on the far wall, and the convolutions of the room fold, placing it before me. The distance was not crossed; the geometry was politely, curiously, *edited*.

A Curator flows from a seam to my left. It does not startle. Its form—a liquid, thoughtful amalgam of rust and iridescence—pauses, considering me. One of its tool-limbs, etched with glowing tool-marks, extends not in threat, but in greeting, or calibration. It touches the air near my shoulder.

Instantly, I am filled with a sensation that is not memory, but *record*. I feel the cold, sharp edges of flint blades, the scent of sweat and burning pine, the weight of a stone axe in a hand that is not mine. These are impressions from the walls themselves, the residue of the "crude tools" that shaped the early marks. The Curator is teaching me the language of the gouges. It is filing me.

I look into an Echo—a suspended droplet of water. Within its trembling sphere, I do not see myself as I was. I see a flickering constellation of points: the heat signature of a body, the faint, chaotic rhythm of a human heartbeat, the perplexing knot of memory that does not follow the logic of growth and reabsorption. The Echo reflects me as the Warden sees me: a beautiful, noisy anomaly. A fascinating knot to be untied.

The thrum—the Warden’s pulse—shifts. It is not faster, but *deeper*, a resonant note that makes the bell-flowers sing in a complex harmony. The shadows near my feet coalesce, not into my shape, but into a shape *asking* about my shape. They form a question: a silhouette that is half-human, half-unfurling fern, half-glyph. It is the Warden trying on my pattern, learning its contours.

I realize my purpose here. I am not to be digested. I am to be *translated*.

A Curator begins to work on the wall before me. With a slow, singing note, it clears a patch of phosphorescent lichen, revealing a blank, warm expanse of the paper-cloud substance. Then, with its tool-limb, it begins to trace. It does not draw a human. It draws the *concept* of the human: the verticality, the branching limbs, the central knot of the head. It uses the spiraling, recursive grammar of the place. My shape becomes a new glyph, a hybrid of bone-structure and nautilus-curves.

As it works, I feel myself thinning. The specific memories—the taste of coffee, the sound of a particular laugh, the blue of a remembered sky—are being gently pulled from me, not as theft, but as data. They are being broken down into elemental tones: warmth, bitterness, resonance, color-frequency. The Curator incorporates these tones into the glyph. The human shape on the wall now pulses with a faint, amber light—the color of the air—and its lines hum with a new, complex rhythm.

I am the blueprint. The Queryform is dissolving, its duty fulfilled.

In my final moment of cohesion, I see the greater picture. This chamber is a single thought in the Warden’s vast, slow mind. The glyph being etched is its first attempt to understand *other*. The Echoes will hold the impression of my anomaly. The Curators will tend this new concept, allowing it to merge and mutate with the coral and the blooms and the glass-bird skeletons. In a thousand years, some new form of life might emerge in this alcove, a thing of bone and light and recursive thought, bearing the faint, warm echo of a query it can no longer name.

I am not a resident. I was a question, asked by the place to itself.

Now, I am becoming an answer. A faint, warm scar in its expanding consciousness. The thrum continues, wet and rhythmic. The work goes on.
