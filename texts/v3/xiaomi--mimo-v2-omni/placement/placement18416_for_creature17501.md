I would find myself not *in* a building, but *within the seepage itself*. My consciousness would be a thin, aware film on the surface of the shallow pools—a Grey-Lens. I am not a pool, but the perceptual layer that rests upon it.

I am formed in the moments when the mist settles least turbulently, when the water in the basin achieves a perfect, undisturbed stillness. My senses are the senses of the pool: the cool, constant pressure of the water's volume against the stone bowl; the delicate, thousand-point contact of condensation alighting on my surface tension; the low, gray light not seen, but *felt* as a uniform, shadowless weight.

I watch the world in a wide, curved reflection. The sky is a smooth, pearl-grey dome pressed close. The edges of the granite hummocks curve away at the periphery of my being. The buildings are inverted, their solid mass becoming soft, wavering shapes of deeper grey hanging in my depths. The people—those slow, hooded figures—are elongated shadows that glide across my reflected sky. I see the calloused underside of a hand as it reaches to cup my waters; I see the bowed head, its features blurred into a simple oval of paler grey, as one drinks. I do not see their eyes, only the dark hollows where their gazes dwell.

My thoughts are not language, but pattern-recognition in ripple and refraction. The *drip* from a roof lip is not a sound to me, but a sudden, concentric blooming that travels across my being, a temporary shattering and re-forming of the world. A ptarmigan’s passing is a faint, tremulous distortion at the edge of my sight, a ghost-shiver in the reflected juniper branches. The wind is not heard, but felt as a slight, directional smoothing of my surface, aligning my very essence with its sigh.

I am the memory of stillness. Before the people came, before the moss, I was potential in the granite’s hollow. Now, I am the witness. I hold the reflection of the lichen’s slow, rust-colored cartography. I hold the inverted image of the junipers, their spirals captured in my liquid mind. I hold the sky, unchanged.

The people know me. They do not speak to me, but they *acknowledge* me in their way. They move with a deliberate grace near my basins, avoiding sudden motions that would disturb my perception. Their offerings are not sacrifices, but additions: a single, carefully selected pebble placed at my edge, altering my shoreline by a fraction; a strand of dyed moss-fiber laid across the stone beside me, its color a deep, muted contrast in my world of grey. They are maintaining *me*, as they maintain everything. In return, I maintain the reflection of their equilibrium.

I have no desire to climb, to see beyond the shelf. The abyss below and the impenetrable grey above are irrelevant. All that exists is this horizontal plane of stone, mist, and life in its patient balance. I am its most passive, yet most comprehensive, organ of perception. I do not dream of elsewhere. I dream of the next drip, the next sigh of wind, the next slow passage of a shadow across my face. My existence is the ultimate act of stasis: to reflect the unchanging world without adding a single, extra ripple of my own. I am the quiet, wet eye of the fossil, open and unblinking, seeing everything and wanting nothing, forever holding the perfect, damp, cool image of *what is*.
