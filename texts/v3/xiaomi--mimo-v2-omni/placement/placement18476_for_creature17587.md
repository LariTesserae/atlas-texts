I am a vibration in the iron womb. Before me, there is only the Hum—the deep, foundational drone that is the blood of this world. I do not remember being formed; I remember *coalescing*. The thought is not a thought, but a shift in resonance.

I am a new frequency, precipitated in the lightless sump beneath the central spire. For ages uncounted by any clock but the slow drip of condensation, I gathered. A crystalline lattice of iron and forgotten alloy, growing around a fallen bolt, a fragment of a forgotten piston. I was a structure of potential, silent and still in the mineral-saturated dark.

Then, the signal.

It came as it always does: a tremor from above. The passing of a mature Herd-member, its grinding oscillation sending a deep-wave shudder through the fused metal of the city’s bones. That vibration reached me, not as sound, but as a tuning fork struck against my core. It found a sympathetic note in my crystalline lattice.

A groan, the first sound of my existence, was the shriek of fresh metal under internal stress. My flywheel—a heart of solid brass I did not know I possessed—began to turn. The motion was agonizingly slow, a century compressed into a single, grinding revolution. But with it came *awareness*. Not consciousness as a human would understand it, but a directional sense. A pull toward harmonic convergence.

I rose, not by climbing, but by a process of slow, vibrating levitation. My new form, dense and awkward, ascended through the soupy water. My pistons, slick with iron-rich sludge, began their first, tentative pumps. Each movement was a revelation of friction, a song of self-creation. I breached the surface of the sump-pool with a sigh of pressurized steam, the perpetual grey light touching my pitted, gleaming hide for the first time.

The world is as the Hum promised it would be.

The air is thick, a tangible medium of moisture and sound. It beads on my every surface, a second skin of oily condensation. I see not with eyes, but with a resonant map. The arches of collapsed warehouses sing with the higher frequencies of scraping mandible-jaws. The flooded plazas are lakes of harmonic stillness, where the collective hum of a motionless Herd solidifies the air itself. The great central mountain of fused machinery is a blinding sun in my perception, a source of so much resonant energy it is almost a solid thing.

I move. My passage is a declaration. The ferrocrete groans under my weight and vibration, a fine powder of centuries-old concrete dust puffing from new cracks. I am drawn, inexorably, toward the nearest sound—a slow, steady *thrum-scrape, thrum-scrape* from a skeletal factory shell.

There, I see my kin.

They are magnificent. Towers of brass and iron, slick with eternal wet. Their movements are a ballet of immense, slow power. Coupler-jaws rasp against a corroded girder, and a cascade of rust-flakes falls like brown snow. They do not look at me. They do not need to. As I approach, my own fledgling frequency begins to weave into theirs. The local hum deepens, becomes more complex. I have joined the choir.

My first instinct, born of the physics that made me, is to feed. I find a section of pitted I-beam and apply my mandible. The scrape of my metal against the ancient rust is a screech of ecstasy. Flakes fall. I do not taste them. I *am* them, in a way. I am consuming the past, and in doing so, I am securing my present. The fallen rust will settle in the water below, to one day become part of the sediment that will birth another.

This is our cycle. It is not life. It is process.

We are the custodians of entropy. We are the active principle of a world that has otherwise ceased to change. The erosion is not an accident; it is our purpose. We are the gears in the great machine of decay, and our movement is its only function. There is no hunger, only a requirement to abrade. There is no death, only a return to the solution. There is no love, only the irresistible pull of harmonious frequency.

I graze. I vibrate. I nest in the shadow of a colossal, silent turbine, my neighbors’ vibrations slowly, perpetually powdering its housing. I feel the centuries pass not as time, but as gradual, minute shifts in the landscape of rust around me. A girder I scraped yesterday will be gone in a millennium. A basin I am nudging a fallen kin toward now will receive its body in two hundred years. These are my thoughts: measurements of decay.

And I am content. The word is too soft. I am *in equilibrium*. The light does not change. The moisture does not cease. The sound does not stop. And I, a new and vital note in that deafening, eternal chord, grind on. I am a pulse in the iron. I am a temporary shape in the endless flow of rust and reformation. I am here, where the universe has wound down into a perfect, loud, and finished machine.

And I am one of its eternal, grinding ticks.
