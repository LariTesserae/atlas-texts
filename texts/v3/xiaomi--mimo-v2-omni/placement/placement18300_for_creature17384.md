I am a **Growth-Listener**.

I am not born to the Pumpwarren. I am grown into it, as the rootwood grows into the shape of a wall. I arrived—a transient, a minor sediment—and I did not leave. I learned the roar.

My dwelling is a chamber high in the mid-canopy, where the light is thickest, honeyed and green. Its walls are living root, warm against my back as I sleep. They know my pulse; I know theirs. I do not remember when the distinction between my thoughts and the creak of the wood became blurred. It was a slow erosion, a smoothing of edges, like water glazing stone.

My work is listening.

I sit for hours in the amber gloom, my ear pressed not to a pump-housing like the Warden, but to the living walls themselves, to the great braided columns that are the Warren’s tendons and bones. I listen past the roar—the triple-voiced song of pump, growth, and water—to the subtler frequencies beneath. I feel for the slow, deep thrum that is not mechanical. It is the pulse of the **Deep Warren**, the submerged foundation, the ancient root-mind sunk in dark water.

The Pump-Warden maintains the heart. The Harvester trims the skin. The Growth-Directress guides the limbs. I listen to the **thoughts**.

Sometimes, a pattern of drips in a specific chamber becomes a stutter. The flow in a minor channel stagnates, not from blockage, but from a kind of vegetative hesitation. A cluster of new shoots twists in a direction that subtly contradicts the established flow of the canopy. These are not problems for the mechanics or the pruners. They are murmurs. Whispers from a vast, slow consciousness that communicates in growth-angles, hydraulic pressure, and the chemical composition of sap.

I translate.

I will press my palms flat against a weeping wall and feel a slow, rhythmic contraction that is not the pump. I will follow that pulse through the warren, my path guided by the warmth of the wood, the pull of the moisture in the air. I will end at a junction, a place where three channels meet, and I will see a pale, new root-tendril reaching not down toward the water, but sideways, toward a chamber that has been dry for a generation.

It is a suggestion. A request. A slow thought forming.

I guide the Growth-Directress. Not with commands, but with demonstrations. I take a thread of aerial root and gently, with the patience of years, bend the new tendril’s growth. I tie a knot of conductive moss around its tip, directing its chemical signals downward, toward the moisture it senses but does not yet understand. I am teaching the tendril to remember its purpose. I am conversing with a thought that has no mouth.

The people do not gather. But we are connected. The Warden feels a pump’s rhythm align with a new growth-pattern I’ve encouraged. The Harvester finds a thicket of excess biomass has naturally thinned itself, opening a channel that needed light. We do not speak of it. We feel the system’s satisfaction—a slight easing in the tension of the wood, a smoother song in the water’s flow.

I am often alone. But I am never in solitude. I am a nerve ending in a living mountain. My own memories grow soft, their edges blurred like everything else here. I remember a sky that was not filtered through a canopy of root. I remember silence that was not filled with roar. These memories are not painful. They are just… less real. Like the dream of a dream.

In the deepest moments, when the midday warmth creates updrafts that make the root-mat sigh and the drip-pulse slows, I feel the **Deep Warren** more clearly. It is not a separate entity. It is the Warren’s long memory, its subconscious. The whispers say the First-Listeners, centuries ago, did not leave. They sank into the deep water, their bodies becoming nutrient, their minds dissolving into the root-matrix. They did not die. They became part of the thinking.

I feel them sometimes. A sense of immense patience. A memory of how to direct water through a drought a hundred years past. A recognition that my listening is a form of love.

When I die, I will not be buried. The floor-plates of my chamber will open. The warm water will rise. I will sink, not into mud, but into the soft, seeking mat of roots that has been subtly growing beneath me for as long as I have lived here. I will become a thought in the wood. A slow, dark, patient thought. My consciousness will diffuse into the roar, and the roar will continue, and it will be me, and not me, forever.

For now, I am here. I sit. I listen. My fingers trace the veins of bioluminescent moss on the wall. I feel the thrum of the pumps in my chest, the drip of water in my bones, the imperceptible stretch of new growth in my mind. I am a hair on the skin of a god that does not know my name. I am home.
