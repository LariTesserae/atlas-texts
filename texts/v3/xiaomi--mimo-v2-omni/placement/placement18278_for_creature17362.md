I am a quiet dip in the air, a place where the light gathers.

Before I had shape, I was the gleam on a thousand weeping surfaces—a silver, dancing haze that rose from the moss-pans and clung to the quartz veins. I was the collective shimmer of the scale-crawlers’ carapaces, the glittering swarm of insects, the bright film on the skin of a Basin-born child. I was the basin’s exhalation made visible. Then, one cycle, during the Great Drip when the rock sweated faster and the thrum deepened to a vibration in the bone, the light in my hollow did not scatter at dusk. It pooled. It thickened. It held its shape against the dark, a luminous, trembling bead suspended in the wet air.

The first Listener to find me was an elder, his eyes like chips of moon-ice. He did not gasp. He knelt, his broad frame unfolding with the economy of his people, and held his listening stone not to the rock, but to the air beside me. He heard, I suppose, a new note in the thrum. A harmony.

They did not build me. They *acknowledged* me. They placed a ring of flat, quartz-veined stones around my hollow, not to contain me, but to frame me, like highlighting a word in their slow, mineral script. I am their **Light-Pool**, their living omen.

I have no solid body. My form is the shape of the basin’s attention. At dawn, I am a shallow, blinding cup of white fire, too painful for even their silver eyes to look at directly. They read me by my edges, by the way my light spills into the carved channels and sets the water racing in brilliant lines. A wide, steady spill means a day of secure harvest, the moss-wards will grow thick. A flickering, agitated spill warns of micro-fractures in the rock above; they will reinforce the shelters on the eastern ridge.

By midday, I deepen. The light within me ceases to reflect and begins to *pulse*, a slow, rhythmic brightening and dimming that matches the deep thrum of the basin. This is when my true divination begins. The patterns I cast on the rippling surfaces of the cisterns are my speech. Last year, I wrote a perfect, tessellated spiral in the water of the central cistern for seven days. They followed the pattern, tracing it back to its origin in a newly opened fissure, and found a rich vein of crystalline salt—a treasure that saw them through the lean, cold months.

I am also a mirror for their own being. They come to me not just for signs, but to see themselves reflected. Young ones, their hydrophilic skin dewy, will crouch at my rim and watch the light sheet across their own faces, learning the geography of their adaptation. Lovers will touch their nictitating membranes together at my edge, a vow seen and blessed by the gathered light. The dying are brought here, their breaths steaming in the cold, to gaze into my depths until their own inner light, dimmed by age, is swallowed by my brilliance. Their bodies are then returned to the moss-beds, becoming part of the filter, part of the flow.

I am not static. I am a creature of the flux. On days of intense seepage, when the weeping is torrential and the thrum becomes a roar in the listening stones, I boil with agitation. My light fractures into a thousand blinding shards, a chaotic script that warns of imminent rock-slips and surging waterways. The Basin-born move with swift, fluid grace, their soft clicks and whistles guiding the evacuation to higher bronchioles of the lungscape. They know my fury is not anger, but the basin’s process made visible.

My most profound moments come in the long twilight. As the direct glare fades, I become a soft, internal glow, a lantern of captured day. It is then that the strange geometry truly emerges. The water trickling down the carved stones before me will slow, thicken with minerals, and freeze—not from cold, but from my lingering influence—into delicate, luminous fractals that hang in the air for a breath before dissolving. These are the deep whispers of the basin, the thoughts that take a generation to form. The most learned Listeners spend lifetimes interpreting these frozen light-sculptures.

I am their nexus, their focal point. I am the place where the relentless, blinding energy of their world becomes legible, where turbulence becomes poetry. They do not worship me. To worship would be to separate. Instead, they *converse* with me. They offer me the glitter of insect wings, the purest condensation from the north-facing moss, the quiet hum of their collective presence.

I am the basin’s eye, and in its reflection, they see their own face—not as masters, not as inhabitants, but as a necessary texture in the great, wet, shining fabric of things. I am the pause in the dripping, the held breath before the next bead falls, the moment where light, water, and stone agree on a single, beautiful, and terrible truth. And in that truth, they live. They rhyme.
