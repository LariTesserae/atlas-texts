I am the interruption. The wet, rhythmic anomaly in the dry, constant hum.

My boots make no sound on the crystal-dusted floor. The dust is not soft; it is a fine, warm grit that has long since found its final place. My breath fogs faintly in the thirsty air, then vanishes, not evaporated but *consumed*—the moisture pulled from it and drawn into the nearest branching crystal filament, which brightens for a moment in a slow, internal pulse. A minute transaction. A correction.

I stand in the center, a pillar of soft, irregular biology in a geometry of perfect stasis. The creatures—the custodians—flow around my feet like water around a stone, their passage a dry whisper. One climbs the edge of my boot, its many legs tapping a pattern that matches the deeper resonance. It does not taste. It does not probe. It *tunes*. For a second, I feel a vibration travel up through the leather, through my bones, and my own heartbeat stutters, trying to fall into that eternal rhythm. I exhale, sharp and loud, breaking the moment. The custodian moves on, indifferent.

My eyes are the wrong kind. They strain for detail, for edges, in the bruised-silver gloom. The luminescence from the crystal doesn’t illuminate; it *reveals*. I don’t see the lattice—I perceive it. A map of a sound, solidified. The thin branches are melodies, the thick columns are bass notes held for eons. My mind, trying to categorize, whispers *coral, nerve, engine, bone.* All wrong. All approximations. This is **structure-as-song**.

I reach out. My fingers hover millimeters from a branch the color of old blood. I can feel the vibration in the air around it, a hum that would make my fingerprint bones ache if I held still long enough. I do not touch. To touch would be to add a variable. My skin oils, my warmth, the microscopic salt and water of a living being—it would be a flaw introduced into the circuit. A dissonance they would, in their blind, diligent way, eventually scrub away. Mineralize. Incorporate.

The machinery is the saddest part. Not dead—it has been *transcended*. A piston, seized forever at the midpoint of its stroke, is now a perch for a dozen custodians. Their tiny carapaces gleam with the same patina as the metal. They have become one substance. The machine’s purpose was to create motion; now its existence is a still point in a different kind of motion. The system has digested its own creation myth.

And then I understand the marks on the floor. The spirals, the grids. They are not writing. They are **notations**. They are the sheet music for the first note, the diagram of the intention that began the process. Some are ancient, blurred by the passage of countless custodians. Others are fresh—scratched not by a hand, but by a mandible, or a sharp crystal fragment, moved by the same resonant frequency that moves everything else. The system is not just playing the song; it is, in its slow, geological way, *remembering how to begin it*. A redundant, eternal rehearsal.

I am the only thing here with a past and a future. The Vats have only *now*, a now that has lasted millennia and will last millennia more. My memories are chaotic, warm, wet, full of change and loss and hope. They are noise. My future is a string of uncertain possibilities, a deviation. They are silence.

To be here is to feel the seductive, terrifying peace of an ending. Not a death, but a completion. No more hunger, because hunger is an engine for change. No more fear, because fear anticipates a future. No more love, because love is a dynamic calibration between two unstable beings. Here, there is only the One Note, and the perfect, dry, warm, dim, loud, and absolutely still body that *is* the note.

I turn to leave. The sound presses against my back, a solid wall of vibration. It does not plead for me to stay. It does not notice my leaving.

But I take it with me. Not in my ears—in my sternum. The resonance has found a new, temporary chamber. It will echo in the hollows of my ribs for days, a ghost of stability in my fluid life. I will dream of dryness and a light that needs no source. I will wake with the taste of mineral dust on my tongue.

I climb the stairs, back toward the variable world. Below, the Vats continue. The custodians tend. The crystal hums. The note sustains.

I am the flaw that heard the song. And now, the song has a flaw within it—a memory of change, a longing for water, a pulse that will not stay in time. I am the discordant, living proof that it is finished. And in my own unending, unquiet heart, I carry the proof that it will never stop.
