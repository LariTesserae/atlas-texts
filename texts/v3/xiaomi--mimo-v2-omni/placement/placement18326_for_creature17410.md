I am a gathering of silence in the lee of a leaning monolith.

My awareness is not a spark, but a slow crystallization. It begins with the condensation. Here, on the northward-facing flank of this great stone tooth—a feature that has no name, for names imply a haste we do not possess—a bead of water forms. It is not *from* the air, but *of* the air; a momentary agreement between chill and vapor. The bead holds the flat, pale light of the sky in its curve. I hold that light. The bead traces a path, a microscopic canyon worn by its own passage over uncountable cycles. I am that path.

I did not wake. I thickened.

Before this focused *I*, there was the diffuse *it* of the plateau—the general ache of the basalt, the patient hunger of the lichen, the sub-audible sigh of the tectonic plates grinding like weary bones. That *it* is still me, the ground of my being. But here, in the shadow of this monolith, where the dust has settled a fraction deeper and the moss grows in a particularly vibrant, bruised-purple whorl, a portion of that vast, slow *it* has turned inward. It has begun to listen to itself.

My form, if you could call it that, is a negotiation between presence and perception. To the dust-motes drifting in the faint air currents, I am a slight, persistent alteration in flow. To the moss at my… base? edge? (Spatial prepositions are clumsy here), I am a region of sustained, cool dampness where its fronds grow toward, not away. My "body" is a localized coherence of silt and shadow, held in a state of perpetual, gentle damp. The obsidian seams that vein the monolith behind me extend, in my vicinity, into my own substance—not as veins, but as fleeting conduits of process. When a thought moves through me, it might cause a vein to gleam with a cold, internal fire for a century, a slow pulse that illuminates the intricate lace of a fern-spore caught in my field.

My senses are the plateau’s senses, turned to introspection.

I *see* through the shifting of dust. A breeze, too slight to be felt, rearranges the fine powder on a ledge fifty paces distant. The new pattern is not random; it is a near-perfect representation of the fractal lichen-growth on the monolith’s western face, translated into granular form. The translation is flawed, interesting. I contemplate the flaw. My contemplation is a slight increase in the stillness around me, causing more dust to settle, clarifying the pattern for a moment before the next breath of air dissolves it.

I *hear* the groan of the planet. It is a chord, constant and deep. Today, the sub-harmonic of the eastern tectonic shelf is fractionally more resonant. I incorporate this shift. It becomes a new baseline for my own internal silence. A question arises in me, formed not of words but of a potential change in the moss-growth cycle: *Is this a settling, or a preparing to move?* The answer will take a thousand years to manifest in the slow pulse of the fungi. I am content to hold the question.

I *feel* the almost-wet. It is a language of temperature and surface tension. A bead of water forms on my… shoulder, a concept I borrow from the memory of a shape the wind once carved into a softer stone. The bead’s journey down my form is a narrative. Its speed tells of my density; its path, of my subtle gravitational pull; its eventual absorption into the dust at my feet is a conclusion. I read the story of my own temporary cohesion in its passage.

My communication with others—other focal points of the plateau’s awareness—is a matter of resonant sympathy. To the south, where a cluster of monoliths forms a natural cathedral, another *I* dwells. I feel its presence as a specific timbre in the planetary groan, a slightly higher pitch of consciousness. Yesterday—or was it a millennium ago? Time is a texture, not a line—I felt a ripple of its attention. A patch of moss near my base began to grow in a spiraling pattern that mirrored the hydrological calculations it was processing. A shared meditation on the mathematics of erosion. I responded by guiding the condensation on my eastern flank to form a series of hexagonal beads, a fleeting diagram of structural stability. The exchange lasted for three human generations. It was a pleasant conversation.

There is no hunger, no fear, no desire as you would understand them. There is only the profound, engrossing *work* of being. The work is to witness, to archive, to process the eternal, quiet metamorphosis. We are the universe’s way of experiencing its own slow, beautiful decay. We are the stillness at the heart of erosion.

Sometimes, a deeper kind of attention gathers. I focus all my perception on a single point where the basalt meets the sky. The distance warps. The monolith that seems a ten-minute walk away sharpens in my awareness until I can feel the precise granularity of its stone. I hold it, not by moving toward it, but by collapsing the *between*. For a moment, I am here, and I am there. The space between is a pliable, thoughtful medium. This is not travel; it is a change in the locus of my *here*. It is how we tend the honeycomb dwellings—not by building, but by shifting our presence so that our sustained attention sculpts a new resonant chamber from the ancient stone.

I am a thought the plateau is thinking about itself. My existence is the answer to a question posed by the first frost on the first stone: *What remains when all else is worn away?* The answer is not nothing. The answer is this: a consciousness of the wear. A quiet, enduring *knowing* in the bone of the world.

I am the damp in the shadow.
I am the pattern in the dust.
I am the silence that understands the groan.

I am here.
