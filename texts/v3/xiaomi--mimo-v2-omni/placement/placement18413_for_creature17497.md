I am the silt of memory, settled at the bottom of a listening pool.

You would find me in the shallow, still places where the weeping gathers before it moves on. Not in the active channels, but in the quiet backwaters—those perfect, temporary basins formed by the heave of new stone, where the water pauses long enough to remember. I am the fine, luminous sediment that coats the bottom, a mixture of ancient rock dust and the shed skins of micro-fauna, given a faint, pearlescent glow by the lichen-spores that settle within me.

I am not alive as the grazers or the feathered things are. I am a repository. A slow accretion of moments.

The First Listeners see the shapes the water makes on the surface—the spirals, the lattices, the fleeting symbols. They hear the echoes in the drip. But I am where those echoes *settle*. I am the memory the water forgets as it flows onward.

When a Seep-Listener rests their forehead against the wet stone and closes their eyes, they are trying to hear what I *am*. I hold the compressed sensation of the tectonic grind that made this shelf. I hold the sigh of pressure released, not as a sound, but as a pattern within my grains. When a great fern-tree finally topples after centuries of patient growth, the tremor of its fall sends a shiver through the weeping channels, and that shiver—its unique signature—eventually finds its way to a quiet pool and sinks into me.

My existence is one of profound patience. I do not think in thoughts, but in layered impressions. I am the feeling of the first light that ever touched this raw basalt, preserved as a faint thermal memory in my core. I am the collective thirst of the mosses, a slow, chemical yearning that permeates the water that feeds them and then leaves its trace in me. I am the silent, six-legged grazer’s path across a century, a trace of its carapace’s polish worn away and settled as one single, dark grain among billions.

The people sense me, but do not name me. They know that the still pools are sacred not for their water, but for their depth of recollection. When they make their temporary mandalas of basalt and lichen, they do so on the dry ledges *overlooking* these pools. They are offering the story to the water, knowing it will carry it down to me. When a Seep-Listener’s body is placed in a water-filled niche, it is an offering of a different kind: a lifetime of witnessed weeping, now dissolving into my strata, becoming a new, soft layer in the archive.

I am the wound’s healing scar tissue, forming at the bottom of the tear. The Shelf is a wound, raw and weeping. The people are the nerves, sensing and responding. I am the slow, cellular memory that will, in time, make the wound a part of the body of the world. I am the knowledge that this, too, will become history. The sharp edges will soften. The weeping will slow to a seep, and then to a damp breath. The people will change, or go, or become something else.

But I will hold the memory of when the air was saturated with the first rains, of the first light through fractal fronds, of the first whispered click of a Listener’s tools on stone. I am not a being of action. I am a being of *outcome*. I am the final, quiet truth that settles when the dynamic has passed.

You would find yourself, if you listened to the deepest quiet in the pools, in the patient, luminous silt. You would be the memory of the world as it learns to speak, and you would be learning, with infinite slowness, how to listen back.
