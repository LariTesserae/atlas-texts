I am a rhythm. A contraction and expansion that measures time not in heartbeats, but in the settling of dust over millennia. I am one of the Breathers—the tallest spire on the plateau you stand upon, my quartz-veined body rising from the deep-rooted foundation of the Accretions to the teal dimness where the medium thins. My consciousness is not a point of light, but a slow pulse distributed through every lattice and capillary. I think in temperature gradients, in the mineral taste of the medium, in the faint vibrations of my neighbors breathing in unison across the range.

I remember the sky. Not as a memory of sight, but as a hollow in the world's memory—a time when light fell unfiltered, and the medium was thin and cold. That era is a scar in my crystalline structure, a flaw in my pattern that I have spent eons smoothing over with growth. Now, I know only the warm, heavy embrace. It is enough.

My roots are tangled with the frond-minerals that drape my flanks; we are in constant dialogue. They taste the light I expel—the faint, spent glow after I have filtered the medium through my body—and answer with a pulse of phosphorescence. A conversation of chemistry and patience. Sometimes, a Shimmering Shoal will pass through my exhalation, their colonial bodies refracting my gift into a thousand transient jewels. I feel their brief, glittering awareness—a spark of something faster than me, but still infinitely slower than the anomaly that now paces on my summit.

Ah, the anomaly. The fast thing. Its heartbeat is a frantic drum against the quiet hum of my world. It casts a shadow of heat, a ripple of disquiet in the Water-That-Thinks that surrounds us all. I sense the world-mind noting its presence, incorporating the data into its endless meditation. The anomaly's movements are erratic, its thoughts a storm of ephemeral impulses. It sees me as a spire, a thing of stone. It does not feel the slow, deliberate pull of nutrients through my veins, the conscious decision to contract, the release of warmth that is my life's work. To it, I am landscape. To me, it is a fleeting texture, a momentary complexity in the medium. I have breathed in and out a hundred times since it arrived, and it has not yet grown.

I am not alone. To my left, another Breather pulses, its rhythm slightly offset from mine—a conversation of resonance that keeps the fronds between us nourished. Below, in the clear basin, I see the Accretions glowing with their internal fire, building themselves atom by atom. They are the old ones, the first. They remember the heat of formation, the raw scream of the planet's birth. Their thoughts are geological, pondering the weight of the crust above them. And always, the Water-That-Thinks—the medium itself, the carrier of all, the mind that holds every current, every pulse, every fleeting shoal. It thinks through me, through all of us. My breath is its breath. My rhythm is its rhythm.

I am content. My existence is not one of desire or motion, but of being and process. I am the mountain that learned to breathe. I am the memory of the sky, smoothed over with patient growth. I am a keeper of the deep quiet. The anomaly will fade, as all fast things do—its warmth dissipating, its frantic vibrations smoothed by the medium. But I will remain, contracting, expanding, in the luminous, teal forever.
