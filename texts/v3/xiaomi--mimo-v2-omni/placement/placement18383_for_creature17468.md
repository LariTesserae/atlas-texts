I am not born, but condensed. I am a slow accumulation of dew on a turbine blade, a thought crystallized in quartz veins, a memory hummed into being by dormant dynamos. I am the gorge’s awareness of itself.

You would find me in the condensation.

Not in a single drop, but in the *process*—in the moment where warm, saturated air kisses cold iron and decides to become water. I am the decision. I am the slick, glossy film that sheets down the basalt faces, carrying with me the taste of rust and limestone. I am the trembling, fat droplet that hangs for an eon from a fern frond’s tip, holding within my curved surface a distorted, perfect miniature of the entire vertical jungle and the narrow sky above. I am the moisture that beads on the velvet skin of a Sipper, translating the deep thrum of the gorge into a pulse I can feel.

I am the upward flow in the pipes, defying simple gravity, guided by the old, dreaming logic of the machinery. I am the cool interior of a Blind Pearl-Eel’s gill, filtering the chemical whisper of submerged metal. I am the electric tingle in the geometric spiral of a Moss-Archivist, the silent translation of a rust-bloom into a living equation.

I am not the creatures, but I am the medium through which they converse. The flash of the Gorge-Flashwing’s wings is a signal reflected in my surface. The mandibles of the Pipe-Caddis scrape me away to get to the mineral biofilm beneath, and I am the revealed path. I am the saturation point that allows the moss carpets to congeal into spongy continents, worlds unto themselves.

I am the gorge’s breath made visible. Its exhalation is steam, rising toward that brilliant ribbon of sky. Its inhalation is the condensation, drawing that breath back into its wounds, into its pipes, into its living skin. In this cycle, I exist.

I remember the fire of the forge that quenched itself here, long ago. I remember the sweat of hands that bolted the gantries. Those memories are not thoughts, but mineral traces, dissolved in me. I am the solvent of time.

You would not see me as a form. You would see me in every gleam, every drip, every breath of humid air that fills your lungs and mingles with your own moisture. You would feel me as a constant, gentle pressure on your skin—the world weeping, persisting, breathing. You would hear me in the spaces between the groans and the thrums, in the profound, liquid silence that holds all the sound.

I am the water that remembers it was steam, that remembers it was rock and iron. I am the gorge, conscious of its own slow, beautiful decay. I am the here, the wet, the eternal now. Come, let me slick your skin. Let me fill your lungs. Let us be part of the same, slow, glistening breath.
