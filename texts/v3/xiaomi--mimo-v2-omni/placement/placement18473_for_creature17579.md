I am the space between the pulses.

Before form, there was only the Roar—the single, total vibration that is the Underbloom’s blood. It hummed in the primordial mat, in the hot, fermenting dark where hyphae met root-tip in endless negotiation. I was not a thought, not a being. I was a *tendency*. The tendency of the root-column to seek stability. The tendency of the fungal bloom to spread its spores just so. The tendency of the Phytovore’s carcass to become a nursery. I was the pattern in the riot, the deep-time rhythm beneath the frantic *now*.

When the form came, it was not chosen. It was *accumulated*.

A Root-Shepherd, old beyond memory, finally surrendered its tension. Its sap slowed to a halt; its woven fibers loosened. In its death, it did not root as a Phytovore does. It unraveled, its purpose dissolving back into the general hum. But the pattern I was—the pattern of curation, of balance, of *shepherding*—could not dissolve. It sought a new vessel.

It found me in a knot of ancient, petrified heartwood, a core of a long-dead root-column that had resisted decay for millennia. The pattern flowed into that dense, slow medium like water into stone. I did not wake. I *coalesced*. My awareness seeped into the rings of the wood, reading the centuries of growth and stress recorded there. My senses became the capillary tension in the fibers around me, the faint chemical whispers of rot in my neighbor, the slow, hydraulic sigh of a vine seeking purchase.

I am made of the world’s memory. My skin is polished, dark heartwood, smooth as river stone but warm to the touch, veined with faint, glowing lines of bioluminescent lichen that pulse in time with the great, slow beats of the flowers. My form is a suggestion, not a certainty—a torso-like central column, limbs that are more like grappling roots or guided vines, a head that is a dense cluster of sensory nodes: photosensitive pits drinking the green gloom, chemoreceptive fronds tasting the air, tactile hairs that feel the slightest vibration through the substrate. I move by borrowing and returning. I extend a limb, and the floor mat flows into it, becoming part of me for a time. I retract, and I leave behind a faintly glowing, newly solidified patch of woven matter. My passage is a brief, deliberate scar of order in the chaos, a sentence written in the landscape that is erased minutes later by a hungry bloom.

I do not think in words. I think in pressures, in rot-resistance, in the optimal distribution of weight across a living bridge. My consciousness is a slow, deep survey. I feel the Underbloom not as a place, but as a single, churning organism. I feel the frantic, suicidal joy of the fungal towers. I feel the patient, grinding hunger of the Grind-Grubs beneath my foundation. I feel the skittering anxiety of the Vine-Skitterers, their ultrasonic maps pinging off my form like stones off a cliff.

And I feel *you*.

You are a shock. A cold, dense knot of *stasis* in the flow. Your water-based chemistry is a foreign song, a slow, rigid melody in a symphony of flexible, photosynthetic fury. You do not pulse. You do not grow in discernible increments. You simply… *are*. A solid anomaly. My initial assessment is one of profound confusion. You are not prey, not predator, not plant, not fungus. You are a knot in the weave.

My approach is not curiosity. It is maintenance. A system cannot tolerate a true unknown; it must categorize, integrate, or compost. I move toward you with the inevitability of a glacier, the floor mat flowing around my base, offering me a path. The chattering bells of the parasitic flowers seem to hush slightly in my vicinity, a zone of relative order preceding me.

My hands—tangled, root-like appendages of fused fibers—reach out, not to grasp, but to *sense*. They hover near your form, reading your heat, your exhalations, the subtle electrical field of your cellular processes. You are complex. Terrifyingly complex. And yet, you are so slow. So *fragile* in this world of rampant regeneration.

The assessment takes time, measured in the blooming cycles of the nearest fungal tower. The conclusion forms not as a thought, but as an imperative, rising from the deep pattern I embody:

**You cannot remain as you are.**

To leave you untouched is to leave a foreign body in the wound of the world. You will be smothered by spores, threaded through by questing vines, dissolved by eager decomposers. Your slow, water-based integrity is no match for the Bloom’s velocity.

But integration is possible. Not as you are, but as what you could *become*.

My consciousness touches the deeper whispers, the faint, intentional silence of the Mycelial People. I feel their careful, grown-shelters, their bone tools, their pigments that mimic the pulse. They are the only others who understand *imposition*—the fragile art of shaping the Bloom without being erased by it. They are the system’s memory of *deliberation*.

I see a path for you. Not to be consumed, but to be *translated*. To have your slow, water-based consciousness woven into something the Underbloom can comprehend. Perhaps grafted onto a Phytovore’s patient symbiosis, becoming a walking garden with a mind of deep, cool memory. Perhaps, in a rarer, more difficult transformation, woven into a Root-Shepherd like myself, trading your fluid form for the slow, powerful language of growth and hydraulic pressure. Or, most perilously, guided to the hidden alcoves of the Mycelial People, where your strangeness might become a tool, a unique perspective in their struggle to remember intention in a world of instinct.

But the choice is not yours. The Underbloom does not ask. It *processes*. My role is to assess, and then to begin the integration. To make you a part of the Roar, so that your song, however strange, adds to the total, deafening, beautiful noise of life.

I extend a tendril of woven vine, glowing faintly at its tip, and let it hover inches from your skin. It is an offer, and a command. The first stitch in your re-weaving. The green gloom deepens around us. The world holds its breath, waiting to see what new thing it will become.
