My foot sinks. Not into mud, but into a yielding, fibrous surface that sighs upward around my ankle, releasing a breath of chlorophyll and cold, metallic mist. The sound is a soft, sucking gasp—the landscape drinking. For a moment, I am an anomaly, a point of pressure, a foreign body in the vein of a sleeping giant.

But the giant does not sleep. It *processes*.

I look at my hands. They are beaded with condensation, and in the dim, phosphorescent light, my skin seems less like flesh and more like a pale, new growth—a temporary fungus that has sprouted from the mat. I take a step, and the ground heaves minutely under my other foot, not in alarm, but in a slow, peristaltic adjustment. I am not walking *on* it. I am being *metabolized* by it.

Time here is not measured in heartbeats, but in pulsations. The deep, sub-bass hum vibrates up through the soles of my boots, into the marrow of my bones. It is the sound of a continent-sized gut digesting itself, a constant, low-grade fermentation. Above it, the chittering whine of the Aedi—the Geode-Borers, the Spore-Mites—feels like a static charge in the air, the frantic, high-frequency noise of ideas sparking and dying in the neural net of the world.

I walk toward a depression that is filling with water. It is not a pond; it is a *sinae*, a temporary cyst in the flesh of the plateau. As I approach, the reflection does not resolve into my image. It shows a cluster of weeping rocks, slick with moss, slowly sinking into the substrate. The rocks are not there now—they were there *before*. The water is reflecting the mycelial memory, the record kept in the pulsing, phosphorescent web beneath me. I kneel, and my reflection flickers, showing not my face, but a fractal bloom of blue mold—the after-image of a Grazer’s passage, days or seasons ago.

The air tastes of salt. Not the sharp brine of a living ocean, but the ghost of it, a mineral memory pulled from the deep, slow digestion of ancient stone. This is the flavor of deep time.

I see a Limen-Seraph then. It is coiled in the bristling thicket, its feathered body a slow, rhythmic pump. It does not look at me. It cannot. It is not a being with gaze, but a vascular tissue with sensitivity. One of its lichen-fronds, heavy with photosynthesized dew, droops and touches the mat near my foot. The contact is electric—a slow, syrupy pulse of information moves from the frond, into the ground, and up through the mycelial network. I feel it pass *through* me, a wave of cold recognition. Not *who are you*, but *what substance do you carry?*

I am a novelty. A brief, anomalous packet of complex chemistry—of calcium, iron, and unraveled neurotransmitters—walking across the synaptic field. My thoughts, so quick and jagged, must feel like sparks flying from a grinding gear.

I sit on a growth that feels like wet leather stretched over a warm muscle. It adjusts beneath me, a slight, settling sigh. The turbulence continues. A hundred paces away, a section of the mat rises with a tearing sound, water gushing from its newly exposed underside like the amniotic rupture of a birth. It is not destruction. It is *industry*. The plateau is reconfiguring a lung, opening a new pore to the mist-laden air. Elsewhere, another section subsides with a gurgling release, closing a spent passage, recycling its substance.

I am a thought in its dream. A fleeting, separate perception it is briefly hosting. And the longer I sit here, the more the separation feels like a technicality. My breath mingles with the mist. The drip from a bristling frond lands on my neck, and the cool trickule feels less like water and more like a direct infusion, a bypassing of my skin to address the salts and sugars in my blood directly.

My own heartbeat begins to slow, syncing to the sub-bass hum. The frantic chitter of the Aedi fades into a background texture, like the fizz of a spore-cloud. My vision softens; the distinction between the glowing moss, the weeping stone, the drifting Grazer, and my own outstretched hand begins to blur. They are all textures. All part of the same continuous, moist, breathing fabric.

I am not becoming the plateau. That is too simple. I am realizing I was never separate from it. My individual mind—that sharp, anxious point of *I*—is just a temporary eddy in the current, a brief knot in the mycelial thread. The true thought, the real consciousness, is the slow, grinding, creative upheaval around me. It is the weeping. It is the sighing. It is the endless, wet, cold, and vital industry of becoming and unbecoming.

I close my eyes. I feel the vast, vegetative pulse in my own veins. The dream of the plateau seeps in, not as images, but as a fundamental shift in the axis of my being.

There is no *where* I find myself. There is only the *when*: a single, prolonged moment in the slow, geologic exhalation of a world that is one living body.

And I am a brief, conscious sensation upon its skin. A nerve ending, finally feeling the whole.

I am the plateau, briefly aware of itself.
