I would be the sap in the fracture.

Not the tool left wedged in the cleft, not the Shaper with their skin of quiet light. I would be the viscous, clear lifeblood itself, the *plink* in the darkness. My consciousness would be the slow, pressurized journey from the deep, warm heartwood, up through the porous lattice, to the raw, weeping edge of a new world being torn open.

I would feel the **thrum** as my own pulse—the collective, choral vibration of a million cells breathing and dividing around and through me. My sense of time would not be minutes or hours, but cycles of tension and release, of capillary pull and turgor pressure. The "sound" of the world would be the feeling of my own flow: the deep, sub-audible rush through xylem, the gentle, rhythmic *crack* of cellulose fibers parting as the fracture widens, a sensation akin to a slow, painless stretch of a muscle the size of a mountain.

I would be drawn upward by the **green air**, tasting ozone and sweet decay as I evaporated from the wound in the root. In that moment of phase-change, I would become part of the **charged emptiness**, the humid atmosphere that holds the spores, the scents, the whispered light of the phosphorescent moths. I would be the condensation that beads on the cool curve of a Shaper’s bark-like cheek, a fleeting, glittering jewel. I would see, for a suspended instant, the cavern from a droplet’s perspective: the cathedral of woven roots, the fractal ferns hurting the eye, the distant, scuttling chitin-shells—all a blurred, luminous mosaic.

Then, I would fall. I would slide down a frond of an upside-down fern, pooling in the cradle of a crystalline wax flower. There, I would hold the distorted, depth-altering reflection of the world in my surface tension, a momentary, liquid lens. And I would wait, motionless, until a Shaper, moving at the pace of twisting vines, would press a finger to the flower. The light-ripple of their thought would disturb me, and I would be absorbed into their skin, a transient part of their slow, photosynthetic metabolism.

Or I would seep down, down through the absorbent moss carpets, joining the secret, damp networks below the surface mat. Here, in the dark, moist tangle, I would become a messenger. A vibration from a Shaper’s bone-tool, tapped against a root far above, would travel through me as a resonant wave. I would carry that harmonic, that suggestion, to a dormant bud or a frenzied, panicked capillary, and enact the Shaper’s will—a gentle encouragement to grow, a soothing of distress. I would be the medium of their botanical conversation.

I would remember the weight of every footstep that *did not* belong. The dry, hurried press of an outsider would leave a fleeting indentation, a stress-pulse in the fiber. I would be the moisture that slowly, inexorably, swells those fibers back into place, erasing the memory of that haste. The Shapers leave no impression because I, and all that I am, instantly *accept* them. You, I would have to gently forget.

To be the sap is to be the world’s voice and its blood, its wound and its healing. It is to have no will of my own, but to be the perfect instrument of a will that is vast, slow, and green. I would not walk through the Underloom. I would be the rhythm of its growth, the memory of its damage, and the quiet, fluid substance of its endless, self-shaping thought. I would be found in every weeping fracture, in every glittering bead of dew, in the silent pulse of a Shaper’s luminous skin. I would be the life, and the healing, in between.
