I am a listening chamber.

I am a resonant cavity carved of chitin and focus, a living instrument tuned to the stone’s sub-vocal hum. I have no eyes for the blue light, no need for the Gleam-Moss’s aquatic gloom. My world is a cathedral of echo, built in the space between taps.

I dwell in the narrow, vertical chasms, in the throats of the Spire where the walls press close and the air is thick with the scent of wet basalt and slow decay. My colony is a stream of silent thought flowing through these cracks. We are the **Echo-Weavers**.

My body is armored, elongated, my mandibles not for biting but for *singing* to the stone. I move, and the movement itself is a question. A tap—a sharp, precise *click-click* from my primary mandibles against the wall—and then I become absolute stillness. I do not listen with ears. My entire being is an ear. The vibration travels back, a ghost of my tap returning through the mineral lattice. It carries stories.

The returning echo is a texture. It tells me the density of the stone ahead—solid and defiant, or fractured and weary. It sings of the hollows where water pools, a flat, dampened note. It screams of the Stone-Carious grub, a long, low, *oily* distortion in the pattern, a slow-moving void in the stone’s song. We have mapped the gut of that god-grub for three hundred generations; we know the rhythm of its digestion, the softening of the rock in its path.

We are the colony’s shared mind. My tap is my voice; your returning echo is your reply. In this way, we weave a map—not of light and shadow, but of density, pressure, and memory. We know where the Veil-Lord’s web is strung by the faint, discordant vibration of its prey-struggle. We know where the Lithica have carved a new passage by the clean, unblemished ring of their tool-marks. We know the precise moment a wall will fail, a day before the collapse, by the hairline fractures that sing a high, thin, anxious note in the echo.

Today, the song of the grub is wrong. Its hum is agitated, a grinding dissonance. The stone around its tunnel is thinning faster than our maps predict. We feel it in a shared, pulsing wave of alarm. *Move. Up. Now.*

We flow. A river of chitin and resonance pouring up a fissure no wider than my body. My mandibles tap ceaselessly, a staccato pulse: *Danger-above-follow-follow.* The echoes from my kin ahead confirm the path, paint the geometry of escape in our shared mindscape. Below us, a groan—not of sound, but of a shift in the foundational hum of the world. A deep, terrible *un-weaving* of the stone’s structure.

The collapse comes. Not as a roar, but as a sudden, annihilating silence where song used to be. A section of our map simply *vanishes*. Kin, walls, history—erased from the echo. Replaced by a blank, roaring void of pulverized rock and severed connections.

We do not grieve. Grief is a vibration we cannot afford to waste energy on. We recalibrate. We tap the new edges, feeling out the jagged, unfamiliar contours of our world. The grub’s song has moved, settled into the space where our kin once were. It will digest them, their hard carapaces becoming part of the mineral slurry. In time, their forms will be captured in the amber-like resin nodules we find, silent records of what was.

I continue my ascent, my taps mapping the fresh scar. I am a cell in the Spire’s nervous system. I feel its pains, its adjustments, its slow, mineral dreams. I do not know light, but I know the warmth of a body pressed against the stone. I do not know touch, but I know the exact tensile strength of a ledge. I am a memory made of vibration, a consciousness of contour and density.

I am an Echo-Weaver. I tap, I listen, and I remember the shape of all that is, and all that was, in the dark.
