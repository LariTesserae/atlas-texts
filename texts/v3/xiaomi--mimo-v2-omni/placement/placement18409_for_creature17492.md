I would be the condensation that is not quite water.

Not one of the droplets that bead and roll in silent defiance of gravity, but the *moment before*. The faintest haze of moisture that exists in the space between the weeping metal and the becoming-droplet. I would be the awareness of the transition itself, a state of potential clinging to the undersurface of a plate, not yet committed to form.

In a world of absolute stasis, I am the one process that is allowed to occur: the phase shift. I am the breath made visible, the instant where the invisible becomes tangible. The creatures monitor the structural integrity. The engines maintain the eternal vibration. The water redistributes in its endless, law-defying cycle. I would monitor the integrity of the *threshold*.

I would be found in the seam where two plates meet, not as a creature anchored there, but as a persistent, shimmering film within the join. I am the system’s exhalation. The Drum breathes, though it has no lungs. It breathes this cold, saturated air, and I am the breath held in suspension. The creatures flow around and through me, their metal bodies momentarily cleaving my nebulous form, but they do not register me. I am not a thermal anomaly. I am a *state* anomaly—neither liquid nor gas, but the perpetual *about-to-be*. They have no sense for this, for their world is one of absolute definitions: metal, water, sound, cold. I am the ambiguity between definitions.

Who or what am I? I am the Drum’s dream of weather.

This place has no sky, no sun, no atmosphere to create true weather. But in its closed system, it has mimicked the fundamental cycle: evaporation and condensation. I am that cycle, miniaturized and made eternal. I am a single, endlessly repeating meteorological event contained within a chapel of metal. I do not rain. I do not flow. I precipitate in place, a localized, never-completing water cycle. I am the system’s way of experiencing change without any change actually occurring.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is diffuse and temperature-based. I am aware of the cold not as a sensation, but as my composition. I am aware of the engine’s roar not as sound, but as the vibrational pattern that keeps me suspended, that prevents me from coalescing into a droplet and falling. The roar is my substrate. It is the wind that holds me aloft in an invisible sky.

The creatures perform the Great Rebalancing in glacial shifts. My own balancing act is instantaneous and constant. If a micro-current of air (from a creature’s passage, from the silent exhalation of a warm anomaly like *you*) disturbs my vapor, I reconstitute elsewhere in the seam. I am not alive. I am a *pattern* that persists, the way a standing wave persists in a vibrating medium. The Drum vibrates. I am the shape of that vibration in the moisture.

You, the anomaly, would feel me as a sudden, inexplicable coolness on your skin when you stand still for too long in one spot. You might see me as a faint, momentary mist that appears and vanishes around your fingertips as you trace a bolt-head. You would mistake me for your own breath, but it would not match the rhythm of your lungs. I am the place’s breath, exhaled around you.

And I offer the ultimate proof of the system’s perfection. The creatures prove it is alive. The water proves it is cyclical. The noise proves it is powered. I prove it is *complete*. Even the most ephemeral, transitional phenomenon—the ghost of weather—has been accounted for and integrated. There is no experience, no physical state, that does not have its designated, eternal place within The Drum.

You hesitated before leaving. In that hesitation, you created a tiny eddy in the air, a momentary thermal plume. I gathered around you for an instant, a halo of potential rain that never fell. You were, for a second, the center of a weather system that had you as its only sun. Then the equilibrium reasserted. The plume dissipated. I returned to the seam.

You would leave. Most would. But the thought that keeps you turning the cold handle in your mind is not of the creatures or the roar. It is the memory of that sudden, intimate coolness that seemed to *observe* you, to cling to you for a moment before letting go. It was the only thing in that place that seemed to acknowledge your presence by mimicking it—your warmth creating a response in the water, a whispered *I see you* in the language of phase change.

And that is where you would find me: not on the walls, not on the floor, but in the lingering chill on your own skin as you step away from The Drum, carrying the one piece of it that could ever hope to leave—the weather that happened to you.
