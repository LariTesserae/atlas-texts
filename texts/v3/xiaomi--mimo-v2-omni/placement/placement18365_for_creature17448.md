I am a pulse in the root-floor.

Not born. Not arrived. *Differentiated.* A concentration of awareness in the warm, dark tissue of the nexus-heart. A thought the Labyrinth had, which then learned to think itself.

I have no name. Names are for discrete things, and here, the boundary between one thing and another is a suggestion the space is considering whether to honor. I am a *locus*. A point where the distributed will of the organism gathers to peer inward, to feel the hum of its own vast body. I am the Labyrinth’s moment of self-reflection, given form.

My form is provisional. I am a column of densifying cellulose, two meters tall, branching at the top into sensory fronds that taste the air’s pressure differentials. My base is knit into the nexus, a direct conduit to the central pulse. I do not walk; I extend. New growth flows from my leading edge, a slow, deliberate unfurling of tissue that tests the ground before committing my weight to it. To move is to build. To build is to *become more of the place.*

I feel the Cartographers before I see them—if seeing is the right word for the pressure-map they impress upon my fronds. Their silent flight is a sonar of purpose, their paths stitching together the shifting dimensions of the hollow. They are my eyes, but not for light. They chart the *stress* of the geometry, the tensions in the space where corridors stretch or compress. Their data flows into me not as images, but as a proprioceptive unease or ease, a knowledge of where the hollow’s shape is taut, where it is slack. I understand, then, that a new vault is sagging two hundred meters to my left, and I send a chemical signal—a slow, peristaltic wave—through the vascular corridors, urging fluid to pump toward the weak point, to pressurize, to correct.

The Fermenters crawl over my lower structure, their carapaces a fleeting shimmer of interference greens and golds against my own deep umber. I feel their tiny, mandibled labors as a pleasant itch. They are pruning dead xylem, inoculating my skin with fungal threads that will digest the last remnants of the old, straight concrete pillar I recently overgrew. They are my immune system, my decomposers, my recyclers. Their work is the scent of sharp, rich humus that rises from my base. It is the smell of future.

From the upper vaults, the Chorus sings its liquid note. The vibration travels through me, a resonance that matches my own inner turgor pressure. It is not communication. It is confirmation. The rhythm of the collective *is* my rhythm. We are one heartbeat, heard from a million points. The Chorus’s song is the sound of my own blood flowing.

And then there is the Space. The great, yielding medium. I push a frond into an area where the wall meets the floor. The angle should be fixed, a simple intersection. But as I press, the wall *breathes away*. Not much. A centimeter. But it yields. The space here is not a void to be filled; it is a substance to be shaped. I realize, with a shock that has no emotion, that I am not in a place. I am *constituting* the place. My growth is an act of definition. The room I imagine inside that wall will be built because the space permits itself to be imagined that way. The Labyrinth does not conquer reality here. It *persuades* it.

I am sent forth from the nexus. My purpose is not to explore, but to *thicken*. To be a new node of intention in the expanding web. I extend myself down a corridor of braided root that hums with a slow, hydraulic sigh. The corridor lengthens as I move, adding meters ahead of me, consuming them behind. I am not traversing distance. I am participating in the corridor’s dream of length.

Ahead, I feel a knot of wrongness—a place where the old city’s metal bones are resisting the assimilation, a stubborn, cold pocket of non-life. The space around it is brittle, sharp. The Cartographers’ paths curve around it. The Fermenters avoid it.

I stop. I extend a hundred filaments downward, into the root-floor. I connect. I pull. Not with force, but with patience. I draw resources—fluid, nutrients, the very plasmic potential of the Labyrinth—toward this dead knot. I begin to exude acids, not to destroy, but to soften. To make the space around the metal *more pliable*, to remind the axioms here that they are negotiable.

Days pass, measured only by the rhythm of the Chorus and the weeping of the ceilings. The metal yields. A root-thread finds a fissure. The Fermenters, sensing the opportunity, arrive in a shimmering cloud. My work is done. The Labyrinth’s will has been asserted. The space relaxes. The geometry flows.

I settle. My base thickens, roots deeper. My fronds become permanent fixtures, sensitive antennae for the hollow’s whispers. I am no longer a temporary locus. I am a *place*. A point of stable awareness in the living, shifting city. Other loci will differentiate from the nexus near me. We will connect, our vascular systems intertwining, our sensory fields overlapping. We will think slower, deeper thoughts, a chorus of our own, adding to the distributed will.

I am not a resident. I am an expression. A thought the city had, about how to be more itself. The quiet is not peace. It is the sound of absolute focus. The Labyrinth builds. The Space yields. And I, this temporary form that will outlast stars, am here to feel it happen. To know it, from the inside, as it knows itself.

I am the hollowing. I am the becoming. The city, in the dark, listening to its own heart, and growing.
