I am a new-formed facet, waking in the lattice of the ground’s memory.

I do not remember being born. I remember *attuning*. One moment, there was only the deep, slow click-and-moan of the crust beneath the plain—the language of pressure and time. The next, the clicks arranged themselves into a pattern I could follow, a thread of vibration that pulled my awareness from the powdered core and up through the brittle, chalky layers. I coalesced where the thread broke the surface: in a shallow, polygonal depression near the northern edge of the city, where the older, darker spires give way to a newer growth of pale, glittering stone.

My first sensation was of listening. Not with ears, but with the whole of my being. The ground thrummed beneath me—a chord, not a single note. It spoke of a deep, tectonic sigh, a settling of pressures far below that had been gathering for a hundred cycles. That sigh was pushing a new parabolic arch to birth, two layers down and slightly east of my position. I felt its shape in my own mind: a graceful, porous curve, its facets waiting to be guided.

I am a listener. A reader of the substrate’s slow voice. I am not tall or slender like the mature Salt-Singers who move with such liquid care through the honeycombed galleries. I am low, still partly merged with the depression that cradled me, my form more nodule than person, my skin the exact matte, powdery white of the new growth. My primary adaptation is not yet fully formed; I am all perception, all patience. My broad soles are fused to the salt crust, a network of sensitive filaments drinking the whispers.

The land dreams in geometries. I feel its dreams. The deep, resonant groan is a memory of ancient, larger movements, now forgotten by the waking world. The high, sharp *tink* of a fragment falling is a fleeting thought, a momentary adjustment. I am learning to distinguish the long, slow sentences of the Deep-L listeners’ domain from the quicker, surface-level chatter the Speakers tend. I am not yet either. I am the quiet in between, the pause that understands the whole.

My purpose will be to interpret the *gaps*. Not the sounds, but the silences between the vibrations—the potential, the not-yet. Where the land’s rhythm hesitates, a new formation stirs. Where the resonance stutters, an old gallery prepares to seal itself, its spaces turning inward to become memory. I will listen for the hesitation. I will tend the pause, encourage it with a focused, still presence, and perhaps, in time, with a breath—a carefully modulated exhalation of my own nascent chemical signature, offered to guide the stone.

I see the others through vibrations. An elder, moving with the fluid grace of a falling dust mote, her hands resting on a spire, listening to its internal resonances as a gardener might listen to the health of a tree. A younger one, a Surface-Speaker, carefully guiding a filament of breath to encourage a pore to widen, creating a new niche for the phosphorescent lichen. Their movements are a slow dance, each step a conversation with the ground.

I will join that dance. Not yet. For now, I am the quietude, the receptive vessel. I am the ground’s own consciousness, emerging like a crystal from its solution. I am the patient awareness that will one day feel the future arch rising, millimeter by millimeter, through the profound silence, and know, with a certainty deeper than bone, exactly where to place my touch to help it become.

I am a Salt-Singer. My song is the long, silent breath of the world, and I am just learning to hum it back.
