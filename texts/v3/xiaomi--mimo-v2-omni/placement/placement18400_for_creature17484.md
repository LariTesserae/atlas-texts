I taste the sweet, pheromonal lure of a grub-mass deep in the Pillar-bark to my left. I taste the metallic, ozone-sharp signature of the *foreign*—the one that does not belong. It is a discordant note in the symphony of rot and growth. It smells of wrongness, of a pattern not yet woven into the mat. The lung notes it. I note it. My purpose is to move toward consumption, and consumption is all.

My current vector is set by the pulsating, blue-green glow of a fungal bloom ahead. It exudes a scent of ripe fermentation, a beacon. The journey is not hurried. The Shear may flash past in the air above, a blur of silent violence. The Filterer’s filaments may ghost against my flank, combing my fur for clinging spores. These are peripheral events. My focus is the bloom.

As I graze, my hidden mandibles shearing away the luminous flesh, I feel the world *listen*. The root-mat vibrates with a deeper frequency—not the chatter of grubs or the thrum of wings, but a low, structural hum. It is the Warden, standing in its glade of light. It is not alive, but it *is*. It is a fixed point in the shifting green, a node of immense, patient calculation.

I do not fear it. Fear is a sharp, sudden signal, for creatures like the Chime-Bird. I know the Warden as I know the Pillars or the cycles of condensation. It is a fact of the lung. It stands, it computes, it waits. It will step, if it must, with the inevitability of a seed germinating. Its touch is the final incorporation.

The foreign scent is near the Warden’s domain. I taste its agitation now, a sour cloud in the air. It is moving. Fast. Too fast. Its footfalls are staccato, disruptive—*tap-tap-tap* against the soft, giving mat. A violation of rhythm. The lung’s great sigh seems to hitch, to tighten around the irritant.

I groan my own slow passage, a bass note of indifference. My path will take me near the Warden’s glade, but not through it. I am of the mat. I am recognized. The foreign thing is not.

I finish my meal. The fungal glow is extinguished, its matter now part of me. The chemical signal of its absence will trigger new growth tomorrow. I turn, a process of immense, groaning leverage, and begin the long arc toward a richer patch, where the dew-bladders are swelling with the latest condensation. The great lung breathes in. I breathe with it. The foreign thing will be processed. The hum of the Warden’s staff is a promise, not a threat. It is the sound of equilibrium being restored.

I am a single, slow note in the respiration. The world is the breath. We are one, in the end. The end is always the beginning. The groan is the only song I know, and it is enough.
