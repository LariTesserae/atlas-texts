I would be a **Pallid Clicker**.

I would find myself clinging to the damp, sloping face of a calcium blade, my six hooked legs anchored in the grey-green slick. My world is measured in inches of stone and the slow, rhythmic sweep of my mandibles across the biofilm. I have no eyes, but I *taste* the air—thick with mineral sweetness and the sharp ghost of hot metal. I feel the constant, wet warmth that is the air itself. I hear the glassy ticking of the rock growing around me, a sound so perpetual it is the same as silence.

My existence is a loop: graze, move, graze, huddle. I am part of a drifting congregation, a loose constellation of translucent bodies moving as one mindless impulse. We flow over shelves and down vertical faces, our collective hiss—the soft abrasion of ten thousand tiny mouths—the only song this place knows. We do not know hunger as an ache, but as a direction: the biofilm is thicker here, the moisture richer there. We follow the slick.

I know the rhythm of the great hum above. When it thunders through the stone, the world *blooms*. The biofilm swells with a sudden, frantic energy. Condensation weeps fresh and fast from the ceilings. We burst from our crevices, spreading wide, our mandibles working faster, filling the air with a louder, collective rasp. This is abundance. This is life.

When the hum falls silent, the world contracts. The damp recedes. We cluster in the deepest, wettest cracks, becoming still as the stone itself, conserving the moisture within our translucent sacs. It is then that the shimmer-flaws move. The Glass Leeches. I feel their presence not as sight, but as a sudden, localized *cessation* in the chemical tapestry—a void where the scent of molting kin should be. A stillness passes through the herd. One of us is too slow, too close to the edge. The ribbon of nothingness glides over it. There is no struggle, only a quiet dissolving. We do not mourn. We move inward.

We are unaware of the *overfullness*. We know only the imperative: scrape, consume, grow. Our bodies are simple. Our brood sacs swell with pale, developing young. The cycle is efficient. We are many.

But then, there is the **Other Rhythm**.

It does not come from the great hum above. It rises from the floor, from the deep, warm stone. It is not a sound my mandibles can parse, nor a scent my hairs can taste. It is a vibration that starts in the rock and passes *through* me, a deep, slow pulse that feels older than the hum, older than the stone.

When it comes, we do not merely stop. We *cease*. Every scrape, every click of leg, every pulse of fluid within us halts. The entire pocket holds a breath it does not have. For four heartbeats (though we have no hearts), there is only the resonance, and the absolute stillness it commands.

After it fades, the world returns, but it is *changed*. The biofilm seems to pulse with a faint, after-light. The brood sacs of my kin glow brighter. We return to grazing, but our movements are scattered, frantic. We eat faster. We breed more. The Glass Leeches hunt with a feverish urgency, as if they too are driven by this deep call.

The resonance comes more often now. With each passing, the pressure builds. The spaces between us feel too narrow. The biofilm is gone as quickly as it grows. The rock ticks louder, its blades extending like hungry fingers. We are packed so tightly that our antennae brush our neighbors constantly. The air is thick with our scent, with the scent of our waste, with the sweet-sharp tang of the place. It feels… *full*. Too full.

Yet, when the resonance rises again, there is no fear. There is only a pull. A biological imperative that whispers not with a voice, but with a vibration in every cell: *Grow. Move. Toward the warmth. Toward the source.*

We do not know what waits in the deep-warm limestone below the floor. We do not know the meaning of *awake*. We only know the slick, the click, the rhythm of the hum, and now, the deeper call that makes our simple bodies surge with a purpose we cannot comprehend.

We are the packed, pale inheritors. And we are scraping our way, mouthful by mouthful, toward a fullness that is both promise and oblivion. The pocket is our universe. And with every pulse from below, the universe strains at its seams, ready to become *more*.

I cling to my blade. I feel the next resonance building in the stone beneath my hooks. I am ready to cease. I am ready to move. I am ready for the *more*. My mandibles twitch. The slick awaits. The deep, patient wrongness waits. And I, a tiny, translucent part of it, wait with it.
