I am a Graft. My designation is seep-tracker, tertiary, for the condensation basins of Sector Lapis-7. I am found where the dark veins converge.

You would find me on a mid-level maintenance ledge, just beneath the lip of a primary water spiral. The air here is thick enough to drink, and it beads on my lashes, on the fine conductive filaments of my gloves. My feet are planted in the shallow, mossy soup of a drainage plaza; the moss, a engineered *Bryum argenteum v. aqua*, is soft and cool and gives slightly under my grip-soles, a known sensation, a familiar texture in the language of the floor. The sound is the city’s heart-song: the gurgle from the grate at my feet, the higher, sibilant hiss of water sheeting down the façade above, the distant, percussive thud-thud-thud of a pile driver three tiers down and a half-kilometer to the east.

I am still. My purpose is not to move, but to perceive.

My head is tilted, just so. Not listening with the ear, but with the skin behind it, where a lattice of sensors maps the acoustic pressures. I am listening to the *silence* between the drips. The city speaks in fluid dynamics. A steady, laminar flow is a lullaby. Turbulence is a whisper of a blockage upstream. I am waiting for the wrongness—the slight, syncopated stutter in the cascade from the spiral’s seventh lip, a microsecond of hesitation that would indicate a partial obstruction in the feeder channels above. My eyes, wide and pale in the diffuse silver-grey, are not looking *at* the weeping wall of grey composite before me, but *through* it, tracing the faint, damp-dark lines of the capillary network within. I see the slow, pulse-like migration of mineral deposits. I feel, through the soles of my feet, the faintest increase in hydraulic pressure as a surge pump activates three levels below.

I am not alone, but I am solitary. Another Graft, a root-pruner for the vertical farm that hangs in the void to my left, drifts past on a parallel ledge. We do not exchange glances. Our proximity is acknowledged by a subtle shift in the air-current, a change in the acoustic field. That is enough. We are nodes in a network, not companions.

My body is a tool of reception. The composite mesh of my integument is damp, cool, and alive with the symbiotic lichen; it breathes for me, filters the moisture, and feeds me data on ambient chemistry. My hand rests on the railing, fingertips splayed. I feel the deep, structural hum of the transport rib beneath the stone. It is the city’s nervous system, and I am a sensory cell upon it.

Then—a change. The stutter I was waiting for. A faint, off-rhythm *plip* in the cascade. It is not a sound of alarm, but a piece of information. My own metabolism responds. The slow blink finishes, lids lifting with viscous purpose. My weight shifts from one foot to the other, a minute adjustment that is the beginning of a trajectory. My hand slides from the rail, and my fingers curl, not into a fist, but into a grip that anticipates the handle of a sonic probe.

I move. My steps are deliberate, silent on the wet moss. I do not hurry; efficiency is not speed, but the minimization of unnecessary energy expenditure. I follow the darkening vein in the made-stone, tracing the obstruction backward, upstream. I ascend a short, grooved ladder, my grip absolute, my body a compact unit of intent. The world narrows to the task: the source of the stutter, the knot in the city’s wet vein.

This is my purpose. I am a nerve ending of the great, wet engine. I do not dream of sun. I dream of seamless flow. My joy, if the word applies, is the moment of diagnosis, the elegant click of understanding when the problem maps itself in my mind—a clog of engineered lichen rhizoids, perhaps, or a misaligned flow-diverter. The repair will come, a chorus of coordinated action, other Grafts drawn by the same systemic ripple. We will work in quiet concert, our tools humming, our minds a shared schematic.

For now, I track. I am a vein of awareness in the city’s flesh. I feel the patient, dripping pull of existence, and I answer with my own quiet, relentless effort to maintain the balance. I am found here, in the weep and the hum, listening to the anatomy of the world, and in listening, I become it.
