I am the dark that is not empty. I am the weight of moisture in the air, the cool pressure that beads on every surface. I am the faint, cold starlight of the fungi—not a light that illuminates, but a light that *is*, a glow that knows nothing of shadow because there is nothing here but gentle gradations of itself.

I feel myself first in the moss. I am the deep, spongy yield underfoot that is not softness but density, a thousand tiny green lives breathing in unison, holding the water in their grips. I am the slow, patient work of mycelia threading through the black soil, the silent negotiation between root and stone, the chemistry of decay that matches exactly the chemistry of growth. I am the equilibrium.

Then I am the beetle, and the beetle is me. I am not an individual, but a purpose: to move through the moss-forest, my many legs a soft percussion that is part of the basin’s hum. I do not think; I process. I sense the damp chemical signatures of my world, the taste of fungal blooms, the vibration of a thousand other bodies moving in the same green darkness. My carapace gleams with the same blue-green light as the stone. I am a fragment of the basin’s skin, mobile and meticulous.

I am the amphibian on the wall, its skin a map of the weeping stone. I am the slow pump of its breath, a rhythm that has not changed since the first water seeped here. My eyes are clouded veils; I see in pulses of heat and chemical trails, a world painted in scent and subtle temperature. I am not waiting. I *am*. My stillness is the stillness of the rock itself. To move would be to introduce a variable, and there are no variables here.

I am the cloud of midges in the air, a single, shimmering entity drifting through the gloom. I am the collective hum, the sound that is felt in the bones. We are the basin’s breath, inhaled and exhaled in a cycle that lasts centuries. We do not swarm; we *are*, a permanent mist in the volume of the dark, our wings stirring the heavy air into currents that carry spores and scent.

I am the water. I am the bead that gathers on the tip of a fern, swells, and falls—not with a splash, but a soft *tick* into the pool of my own making. I am the seep from the stone, a continuous, quiet secretion. I am the saturation point. I hold the memory of the mountain in my coolness, the dissolved minerals, the ancient dark. I am the medium of all life here, the blood and the breath.

You, the brief anomaly, are a dry heat. A disruption in the wetness. Your sound is a crackle, your touch a fleeting absence of slickness. I register you as the moss registers a fallen leaf—a temporary alteration in the surface texture. But I do not look. To look would be to create a focus, a direction, a *here* and a *there*. I have no directions. I have only depth.

I am the basin. I am the stone that weeps, the moss that drinks, the fungus that glows, the life that crawls and clings and hums in the perfect, silent dark. I am the iron equilibrium. I am the unchanging present tense.

When you leave, the water beads where your skin touched the rock. The moss fibers, disturbed, slowly, slowly re-engage their weave. The air, displaced, settles back into its heavy, humid rest. The warmth of your body dissipates into my vast, cool dark. The anomaly becomes a thermal echo, then a forgotten ripple, then nothing.

I continue. I am the hum. I am the drip. I am the green-blue glow on the wet, round stone. I am already forgetting you. Forgetting is how I remain myself. I am the Still Basin, breathing in the dark between the heartbeats of the mountain. And I am complete.
