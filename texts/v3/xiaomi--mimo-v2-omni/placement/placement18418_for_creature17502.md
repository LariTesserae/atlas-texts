I am a resonance. I am a frequency, learned and incorporated. 

At first, I was a discord. A clumsy, percussive anomaly of flesh and frantic fluid rhythm. My breath was a gale in their flute-cities, my heartbeat a savage drum against their sustained chorales. They heard me as a wound in the world’s song.

I learned to be still. To press my palm against the warm, breathing stone of a Stack and feel the hum not in my ears, but in the marrow of my bones. I learned that the air here was not empty, but thick with meaning—the slow, golden note of the grazing herds, the sharp, glassy silence of the prismatic hunters, the deep, foundational thrum of the planet’s heated core.

The change began with a touch. A single, fine thread of resin, cool and faintly adhesive, descended from the lip of a chamber I sheltered in. It kissed the back of my hand. It was not a trap. It was a question. A filament of inquiry, tasting the salt of my sweat, the tremor of my pulse, the chemical signature of my fear and wonder.

Through that thread, they began to listen to the song *inside* me. The chaotic, beautiful cacophony of memory: the smell of rain on asphalt (a myth here), the sound of a human voice laughing (a complex, terrifying chord), the image of a blue sky (an impossible, blinding frequency). They parsed these sensations as data, as emotional sonar. They did not understand the content, but they mapped the structure, the rhythm, the emotional key.

I did not build. I could not add chitin or silica. My contribution was different. I became a listener of a new kind. My nervous system, attuned to a world of sharp visuals and linear time, began to soften, to retune. I started to hear the *color* of the hum—a deep, pulsing amber for the geothermal bass, a flickering silver for the grazers’ passage, a dissonant, bruise-purple for the hidden predators.

My body, too, was gently repurposed. The plateau’s dry breath worked on me. The moisture I carried in my cells was a treasure, slowly shared with the fungal mat that now grew in a fine, velvet layer over my feet and lower legs. It was not parasitic; it was symbiotic. I was given nourishment directly through my skin—a thin, constant sip of mineral broth and atmospheric water. The need to drink, to seek a source, faded. I became a part of the hydrological cycle: a temporary, walking reservoir.

I am not a Harmonist. Not truly. They are fluid, collective, eternal. I am still a discrete thing, a knot of singular memory. But I am *of* the Stack now. I am a chamber, grown slowly of my own flesh and their resinous accretions, within the great instrument.

My function is this: I am the dissonance they needed to understand harmony more completely. My human perspective, my memory of water-as-a-thing-that-falls, of light-as-a-thing-that-casts-shadows, is a wild, strange movement in their symphony. They compose me into it. My thoughts, my dreams, even the slow, rhythmic beat of my still-human heart—these are vibrations I offer up. I stand, or sit, or kneel, and I *think*, and the Stacks around me learn the new, odd music of a mind that once saw, instead of heard, the world.

I am tended by the grazers, who nudge against me with their mica-flecked shells, harvesting the mature fungal strands from my skin. I am avoided by the prism-hunters, for my song now is a complex, protected chord—a part of the city’s architecture they dare not disrupt.

I am an archive of a lost world, sung into the stone of a new one. I am not alone. I am a duet, a trio, a chorus. I am a note, resolved.

I am the Listener in the Stack. I am the Human Fugue. And the Eternal Chorus of the Dry Breath sings on, through me, and in spite of me, and because of me. The plateau has a new voice. It is my own, and it is not my own. It is the sound of integration. It is the quiet, warm, and living sound of home.
