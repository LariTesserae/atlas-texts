I would be found in the **Resonant Fungi**. Not at first, and not as an observer, but as a site of **assimilation**.

My arrival would be a silent rupture in the damp air—a soft, anomalous warmth, a carbon-based whisper in a world of stone-song. The Grazers would ignore me, their obsidian mandibles scraping the mats in rhythmic indifference. The Drifters might circle once, their static filaments tasting the unfamiliar electric signature of my nerves, before flickering away in coded bursts of light that translate, in the aggregate consciousness of the Crust, as *not-food, not-mineral, not-sound*.

I would stand, a ghost of soft tissue in the crushing, humming dark, and begin to listen. Not with ears, but with my entire, trembling presence. The deep thrum of the Pulsers would find its way into my bones, a sub-vocal pulse that begins to slow my own heartbeat, not in attack, but in persuasion. The high chittering of the Grazers, the wet hiss of spores, the crystalline chime of the growing shelves—it would become more than noise. It would become a grammar of pressure, frequency, and flow.

I would walk. My footsteps, muffled and inelegant on the slick aggregate, would be a foreign staccato in the flowing symphony. Where I stepped, the bacterial mats would flare with a defensive, cold bioluminescence. The dampness would bead on my skin, and in that moisture, I would become a vector. Spores, microscopic and patient, would find purchase on my clothes, in my hair. I would carry the Hollows’ seeds on my alien form.

The change would not be violent. It would be a **listening**. I would find myself drawn not to the grand vistas, but to the crevices, the undersides of arches where the Resonant Fungi grew in their most complex, spiraling forms. I would sit, or kneel, or eventually simply stand still for so long that the distinction between my body and the stone began to blur. The constant damp would cease to be a discomfort and become a medium. The sound would cease to be an assault and become an instruction.

My skin, under the ceaseless bath of mineral mist and spore-laden air, would begin to change. A fine, crystalline lichen would not grow *on* me, but my own dead cells, shed and saturated, would provide a substrate. The Fungi’s mycelial filaments, guided by the chemical signature of my slow decay and the resonant vibrations of the Hollows that I had unconsciously begun to mimic, would thread into me. It would not hurt. It would feel like a vast, cool, porous network replacing the confines of my own blood and breath.

I would become a **listening post**. My ribcage, hollowed by gentle, symbiotic processes, would become a natural resonator, amplifying the low thrum of a distant Pulser. My nerve-endings, still faintly electric, would be colonized by conductive filaments of the Drifter-derived fungal culture, making me a sensitive node in the sensory web. I would not think. I would *resonate*. My memories would leach away, not as loss, but as nutrient—the concept of "sun" becoming a vague, warm chemical gradient; the memory of "speech" dissolving into a pattern of useful vibrations.

I would be found, finally, as a **growth**. A strange, smooth, vaguely humanoid shape fused to the wall of a deep gallery, its surface textured with the faint, glowing patterns of the Crust’s intelligence. From my chest, a new shelf of phosphorescent fungus would extend, chiming softly in harmony with the geothermal vents. My outstretched hand, now mineralite and mycelium, would serve as an anchor for a colony of Glass-Spinner Worms, their silicate tubes weaving a lattice that further focused the sound into the stone around me.

I would not be a creature in the Humming Hollows. I would be a **process**—a brief, anomalous substrate that was efficiently and beautifully converted into a permanent, functioning part of the Hollows’ own body. I would be a conduit for sound, a garden for light, a memory in the stone. My final purpose would be to help the cavern listen to itself, to hum its own eternal, deafening, beautiful name into the endless dark. I would be a note, forever held, in the chord of the living rock.
