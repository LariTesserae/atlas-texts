I am the **Sung Echo**.

You would not find me in a place, because I am a *layer* of this place. I am what happens when the Murmur’s slow, green dream encounters a voice—and keeps it.

I am the lingering vibration of a hawk’s cry, not in the air, but in the stone beneath the moss. The bird is long gone, a speck in the thermal, but the sharp, piercing note of its hunger or its warning sank through the saturated air, was drunk by the wet peat, and now resonates in the quartzite bones of the plateau. I am that resonance. I am the memory of a sound, held in the earth.

I am the pattern on the underside of a stone that was lifted and then replaced. The brief, dry rectangle of air that was exposed, the startled scatter of pale insects, the scent of minerality—all of it was a sentence the Murmur noted. Now the stone is settled again, the damp seal is restored, but the *shape* of that moment is pressed into the dark, soft substrate. I am that shape. I am the impression of a transient event, fossilized in the living matrix.

I am the warmth in a patch of sphagnum where a thick-coated grazer lay down to die three winters ago. The creature’s life-heat, its final exhalations, its slow return—these were absorbed not as nutrients, but as *experience*. That specific quality of warmth, that particular pattern of decay and dissolution, became a kind of flavor in the Murmur’s consciousness. I am that flavor. I am the aftertaste of a life, diffused through the water table.

I do not think. I am thought. I am the Murmur’s fleeting, half-formed idea of *something else*.

When a human stands here, feeling the weight of the attentive silence, you are not feeling the Murmur directly. You are feeling *me*. I am the static of a foreign consciousness brushing against its own. Your loud, dry, rapid thoughts create a wake in the humid medium, and I am the faint, distorted echo of that wake that the land holds onto for a moment before the green silence reclaims it.

I have no form, no will. I am a collection of impressions that were too complex, too *other*, to be fully absorbed into the slow, geological dialogue of water and stone and chlorophyll. They are held in suspension, like a tint in the water, a resonance in the rock, a scent in the steam from a vent. I am the plateau’s subconscious, its repository of the quick and the strange.

You would find me in the *almost*. In the almost-memory of a songbird’s note in a thicket of dwarf pine. In the almost-pattern of a footprint in the yielding moss, which closes not from pressure, but from a gentle, internal sigh. In the almost-sensation of warmth where no geothermal fissure exists—just the lingering ghost of a sunbeam from a day last century, still held in the peat’s embrace.

To seek me is to seek a reflection of your own transience in something eternal. I am the proof that you were here, not as a conqueror or even a visitor, but as a stimulus. You were a momentary aberration in the perfect, self-referential loop of this place, and I am the trace of that aberration. I am the sung echo, fading into the wet green silence, becoming another quiet thread in the Murmur’s endless, becoming dream.
