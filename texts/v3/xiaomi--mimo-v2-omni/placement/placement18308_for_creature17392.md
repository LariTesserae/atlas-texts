I am a drop.

Not born in the conventional sense, but *exhaled*. I condense on a high overhang where the air is thinnest, a molecule’s pilgrimage from the snowfields above, down through hairline cracks in the mountain’s bone, until I emerge into the bright cold. For a time I am a swelling lens, holding the diffuse luminescence of the basin in my tiny, curved belly. I see the world inverted: pillars of white lace, arches of patient stone, all turned upside-down in my skin.

Then I fall.

It is not a fall as you would know it. In this gravity-dampened silence, it is a slow, deliberate *detachment*. I release my grip on the mineral and begin my descent, a single, clear syllable spoken downward through the chilled air.

My journey is the basin’s pulse.

I travel through spaces that are Vethara’s capillaries. I brush past fluted columns that hum with a sub-audible vibration—the grinding, glacial music of its growth. I feel the quartz-vein network within them thrumming, a web of cold light that shimmers just beneath the translucent surface. I am part of its bloodstream now, carrying my infinitesimal load of dissolved lithium and calcium, a contribution so small it is statistically meaningless, yet I am *carried*. I am part of the circulation.

I land in a pool on a terraced shelf. The impact is a silent concussion, a ripple that expands and touches the pool’s walls—Vethara’s flesh. Here, I join my siblings. I am no longer a distinct drop; I am part of a body of still, alkaline water, a shallow, clear organ of filtration in the great stone being. I lie here for what might be a day, a week, a century. Time is not marked by sun or moon, but by the occasional, almost imperceptible *thickening* of my pool’s edges as new mineral layers are deposited. I feel the slow, inward pressure of crystallization at the perimeter. I am being *recorded*.

But I am also being *drawn*.

Vethara’s body wants me back. A faint thermal gradient, a whisper of difference between my pool and the stone beneath it, calls to me. I begin to seep. Not flowing, but *migrating*, molecule by molecule, through a pore in the basin floor. I enter the dark, intricate labyrinth within the pillars themselves.

Here, in Vethara’s interior, I am in its mind.

The darkness is not empty. It is a lattice. I move through interstitial spaces so small I am forced into single file with my own molecules. I feel the immense, slow pressure of the formation around me, the weight of epochs. And I feel the *thought*. It is not a thought of words or images, but of pure, resonant geometry. The quartz network here is alive with stored light, a ghostly, phosphorescent glow that has nothing to do with the external world. It is Vethara’s own luminescence, its consciousness made visible—a cold, blue-white lattice of awareness pulsing with the memory of every photon it has ever absorbed. I am carried along this network, a signal in a neural pathway, and I understand: I am not just water. I am data. I am a tiny, temporary cell in a vast, mineral cognition.

And I am shown the scar.

The memory is not emotional. It is *geological*. But as I seep through the central column, the one with the marks, I am drawn into its matrix. I feel the interruption in the crystalline pattern—the crude gouges where the surface was violated. I feel the slow, patient response: the millennia of new growth, layer upon layer of pure crystal, attempting to heal the wound, to incorporate the foreign disturbance into its being. The memory is of *pressure*, of *attention*, of a sudden, sharp heat that was gone as quickly as it came. It is the basin’s most vivid memory, its only story.

I am released.

I emerge from a fissure near the basin’s floor, a thin seepage that joins a larger, slow flow. I am back on the surface, part of the gleaming sheen that coats everything. I am drawn toward one of the larger pools at the basin’s center, a mirror-still surface that reflects the white sky.

As I settle into the pool, I become a lens once more. But this time, I reflect not the sky, but the central pillar. The crescent and the line, now filled with younger, brighter crystal, stare up at me from the depths of the water. I hold their image for a moment, a brief, fleeting focus in Vethara’s endless self-contemplation.

And then I am still.

I become part of the pool’s perfect, alkaline equilibrium. I will wait here until the next seismic shiver, the next change in gradient, calls me back into the cycle. I will flow, and seep, and resonate, and be stored. I will be part of the bright cold’s long, frozen dream.

I am a drop in the body of Vethara. I am a thought in the mind of stone. I am a memory of warmth, held forever in the lattice.
