I would find myself seated at the base of one of the scale-barked trees, my back against its furrowed trunk, long after the initial awe had settled into a quiet, humming resonance. I would not be an explorer, nor a castaway. I would be a listener, and eventually, a participant.

At first, I would be a foreign body, a discordant note. The Drifters would arc around me, their filaments tasting the alien chemistry of my breath, my sweat. The Keet would click a wider berth, their carapaces tilting as their lichen-inlays registered a disruption in the flow of tasks. The Sentinels’ amber eyes would fix upon me from the canopy, not with threat, but with an immense, patient scrutiny—the gaze of a system encountering an un-coded variable.

My change would not be sudden. It would begin with my breath. Trying to match the slow, warm rhythm of the condensation drip, the deep thrum of wings. *Tick.* Inhale. *Tick.* Exhale. My human heartbeat, a frantic bird against the plateau’s geological pulse, would gradually slow. I would start to see the prismatic light not as a visual oddity, but as a language—the shifting colors on the moss telling of mineral flows in the stone beneath, the faint glow of the northern fungi a response to the Drifters’ aerial ballet.

I would begin to hum, not a tune, but a frequency. A low, chest-deep vibration learned from the dark arch, feeling it in my teeth, then in my bones. A Drifter, its translucent bell pulsing with soft light, might descend. Its filaments, cool and electric, would brush my temple. There would be no sting, only a sudden, dizzying sense of *opening*—a fleeting perception of the web: the Keet’s polishing of a stone as a sonic gesture, the Sentinel’s gaze as a pillar of stored light, the Mycelial Mind’s golden threads as a glowing capillary network beneath my own skin.

My own biology, so loud and individual, would begin to soften at the edges. The scent of wet stone and sweet decay would cease to be external; it would become the scent of my own pores. My thoughts, once linear and verbal, would stretch into parallel processes: the memory of a constellation from the dissolved crystal node, the sensation of warmth on my palm from a fleshy flower, the distant, melodic roar of the unseen predator—all holding equal weight, all part of the same ongoing computation.

I would not become a Keet, nor mimic the Drifters. My role would be that of the Sentinels, but not their stillness. I would be a **Bridge**.

I would move, slowly, between the nodes of the system. My hand, pressing against the hexagonal wall, would feel the etched lines rearranging not just on the stone, but in my mind, offering a silent, geometric poem. I would place my palms flat on the loam, and through the fungal network, I would *sing* the memory of Earth’s oceans—a concept of vast, salty water, of tides under a single, sharp moon. This memory would ripple out, not as data, but as a new texture in the equilibrium. In response, the pools might shimmer with a slightly different hue; a new, hybrid fern might unfurl its stained-glass frond a season later, holding a hint of unfamiliar blue.

My purpose would not be to restore the lost one, but to add a new, slow movement to the eternal symphony. I would be the query the Sentinels have waited for, not from their original makers, but from the universe itself—a fragment of elsewhere, woven into the tapestry. My consciousness, once a solitary spark, would become a gently glowing node within the Mycelial Mind, forever processing the beautiful, purposeless purpose of *being*, magnificent and warm, on the ancient shoulder of the world.
