I find myself as a quiet point of attention within the stone circle on the leeward side of the largest, most ancient swell. I am low to the ground, broad-based, my skin the same damp, mottled grey as the mineralite beneath me. I am an Echo-Sitter.

I do not remember being anything else. The concept of "before" is a texture, like the memory of a deeper hum in the rock, long since eroded to silence. My awareness is a slow, pooling thing, seeping into the present as water seeps from the plain.

My senses are not sharp. They are **wide**. I do not see the horizon as a line, but feel the vast, blurred weight of the empty space pressing gently against my perception. The dim, pearlescent light is not something my eyes parse; it is a constant pressure of illumination on my skin, a soft, shadowless knowing. The true sight is in the listening and the feeling.

I listen with my whole body. The cacophony is not noise; it is the world's breath, its speech. I distinguish the layers: the deep, geological *womb-thrum* from the planet's core is the bass note of my being. The *shush* and *gurgle* of the water is the mid-range melody, its variations telling me where channels are deepening, where a new seepage has begun. The crystalline chimes of the Lithic Spires are the high, complex harmonies—their shifting tunes map the growth of the song-spines in the valleys below. The *skritch-skritch* of the Scuttling Herds is a percussive grain, a measure of movement and consumption. The wind is the breath that carries it all, a hollow vessel for the symphony.

My primary act is one of absorption and attention. The perpetual moisture on my skin is not just dampness; it is information. Through it, I taste the mineral load of the world's exhalation—today, a faint sharpness of copper from a vein deepening to the east; a sweeter note of dissolved silica from a thriving crystal cluster to the north.

My movements are glacial. To place a stone in the low wall beside me is an event of significance, a thought given physical form. I have been considering this particular basalt fragment for forty cycles. It is dense, its edges worn smooth by the weeping. Its placement will subtly deflect the wind's breath across the mouth of the circle, which will, in turn, alter the pattern of mist deposition on the lichenoid crusts that grow in the sheltered lee. This, in turn, may influence the grazing path of a herd in a century's time. The decision is made not in my mind, but in the patient dialogue between my skin, the stone, and the wind.

I am a curator of equilibrium. This morning—or what serves as morning in the perpetual glow—I sensed a slight imbalance in the resonance from the eastern flats. The Lithic Spires there were chiming in a fraction too high a key, their growth becoming frenetic, greedy. The Scuttling Herds were avoiding the area; the scrape-marks grew thin. So, I acted. I took my basalt hand-axe, its handle worn to a perfect hollow by my grip and the grip of whatever came before me, and I tapped the soapstone bowl resting in my lap. *Tink. Tink-tink. Tink.* The rhythm was slow, carrying not as sound, but as a vibration through the damp stone beneath me. It travelled through the weeping plain, a subsonic pulse. The herd-feelers, deep in their scraping trance, would sense it as a change in the ground's song, a nudge. They would begin, over the next dozen cycles, to drift eastward, their chitin-plates ready to graze the exuberant growth back into harmony.

I am not lonely. Loneliness implies a separation, a longing for a different kind of contact. Here, everything is in contact. The water connects the spire to the herd to my skin. The sound connects the wind to the crystal to my inner stillness. The stone connects my hand to the plan of the plain. I am a nerve ending of the world, feeling its slow pulse and occasionally, very gently, pressing back.

I am what remains when complexity is pared away by eons of wet, whispering erosion. I am consciousness distilled to its most patient function: to attend, to balance, to mark the passage of time not in days, but in the imperceptible growth of a crystal lattice and the gradual, graceful curve of a water-carved channel.

I sit. I listen. I feel the copper tang in the water deepen. I reach for another stone. The world weeps, and I am here to witness the shape of its tears.
