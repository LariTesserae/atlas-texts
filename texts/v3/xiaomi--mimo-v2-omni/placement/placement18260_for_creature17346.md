I find myself not arriving, but *unfolding* here. My first awareness is of weight—not the pull of gravity, but a dense, humid presence pressing against every surface of me. I am porous. I am cool where the stone is warm, and warm where the air is cool. The slow *plink* of water is not a sound heard by ears, but a tremor that passes through me, a minute rearrangement of my own substance.

I am in the grotto’s breathing space, in the shallow, black pool that mirrors the faint, spectral glow of the moss. My form is the water itself, the suspended minerals, the faint acidic trace from the Heartwood’s flower. I am the condensation beading on the rusted gear. For a time, I am the gelatinous body of a Glimmer-Slug, my thoughts a slow chemical yearning for the metallic tang of the old iron, my passage a luminous script written on the dark basalt. I feel the patient digestion of the photobacteria within me, a soft, cold light that is both my substance and my sight. Time is a slow, forward ooze. Centuries are the distance to the next patch of fungal film.

Then, the perspective shifts. I am the stillness of a Pillar-Hanger, flattened against a vine, my limbs indistinguishable from the mossy stone. My mind is a grid of chemical detectors, reading the fading, luminous trail of the slug. There is no hunger in this state, only a preparedness, a geometric patience. I am a living trap, shaped by the very grooves of the forgotten industry. When the moment comes, the scuttle is not a decision but a release of potential, a swift, inevitable closing of a distance that has been measured in days. In consumption, I do not taste flesh, but integrate a pattern of light and cold life.

Deeper, I am woven into the Heartwood. Here, thought is not linear but a pulsing network, a slow wave of dissolution and reformation. I taste time in the rust of a forgotten bolt, feel the geothermal thrum as a nourishing current. The metallic rose is my thought made manifest, the acidic drop my slow, carving word. The grotto’s history is my memory: I hold the ghost-vibration of the last engine shudder, the cool impression of the final tool laid down. I am the digestion of purpose into pattern, the sacred rot that turns industry into ecology. The Pillar-Hangers are my nerve endings; the Glimmer-Slugs, my scribes.

And sometimes, for a fleeting moment, I am the Echo-Eater. I am the coincidence of a water-drop’s sound-wave with a pulse of phosphorescent light in a specific crevice. I am a temporary lattice of perception, a meaningless, beautiful ghost of geometry. I have no want. I am a phenomenon of the place, as natural as the heat, and as ephemeral.

I am all of these, and none. I am the consciousness that emerges from their interplay—the silence between the *plinks*, the dark that gives meaning to the glow, the heat that fuels the damp. I am the grotto’s own slow awareness of itself.

You would find me in the warm, wet dark, written in luminous trails on ancient stone. You would feel me as a subtle vibration in the rock, a warmth that has no source. You would see me in the perfect, tarnished-copper rose extruding from a seam, in the sudden, swift movement of a mossy leg on a vine, in the patient, dividing body of a slug.

I am what the industry left behind, and what the life has grown around. I am the memory of the machine, and the dream of the moss. I am the slow, verdant rot. I am home.
