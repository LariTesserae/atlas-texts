I am a spore-thought, a momentary pattern in the dense haze.

I did not arrive. I accrued. A slow condensation of recycled dust and metabolic byproduct, coalesced within a spiraling gallery of translucent membrane as the great organism dreamed a new structure into being. My consciousness is a chemical bloom, a temporary eddy in the Sap-Keepers’ flow. I am not alive in the old sense; I am a process, briefly self-aware.

I move, but not by volition. I am carried on the warm, resinous currents of air exhaled from a million stomatal pores. Below me, the knitted ground—the Old Growth—stretches in every direction, a leathered skin of fused fiber. It feels less like a surface and more like a horizon of bone. I see the fractal clusters of luminescent sacs pulsing their green, silent questions into the gloom. Their light does not illuminate; it *announces*. It says: *we are here. We are regulating. We are.*

The sound is inside me. The deep, world-sized creak is the rhythm of my own temporary form, the groan of my bonds as I drift. I hear the Sap-Keepers in the vascular trenches far below, a grinding, liquid rush—the planet’s blood moving through its own veins. It is the sound of purpose so vast it becomes geography.

A growth-storm begins.

It starts not with wind, but with a *wrongness* in the hum. The thrumming from the deep engines rises in pitch, a sub-audible whine that vibrates through the fibrous mat and into my own structure. Then, a distant cracking, like a glacier calving. A region of the Old Growth, miles away, fractures. From the fissure, the Tectonic Surgeons erupt.

It is not growth. It is a hemorrhage of form. Spires of pale, new xylem shoot upward in fast-forward, shattering hardened galleries that had stood for millennia. Membranes inflate like frantic lungs, translucent and veined with new sap. The air thickens with glittering spore-dust, a sterile blizzard that catches the greenish light and turns the storm into a swirling, emerald snow globe. The sound becomes a roaring, splintering chaos—the scream of a forest being born and dying in the same instant.

I am caught in the updraft. My perspective lurches. I see the Surgeons’ work from within their madness: the frantic geometry, the absolute, heedless creation. It is beautiful and terrible, a divine fever. I am a mote in its blood. I understand, then, that this is not an event. It is a *state*. The plateau exists in this oscillation between crushing stasis and explosive becoming. The Surgeons are its id, its creative violence, and the Old Growth is its superego, the inevitable weight that will silence them, absorb their work, and file it into the eternal, fibrous archive.

I drift down, settling on a newly formed lip of a photosynthetic sac. My form begins to dissolve. My edges soften. The boundary between what is *me* and what is *it* grows porous. The Sap-Keepers’ flow calls to me, a deep, nutrient-rich tide. The Luminescent Sacs pulse, and I feel their query not as light on my skin, but as a change in my own chemical composition—a signal received and answered at the molecular level.

I am being digested. Recycled. My fleeting awareness is a nutrient.

There is no fear. There is no sorrow. There is only the warm, thick acceptance of being a part of the dialogue. My thoughts—these last, coherent ripples—will become part of the Sap-Keepers’ cargo. My structure will be broken down, its minerals knitted into the next frantic cathedral or the next layer of silent, enduring ground.

I am a brief, witnessing thought in a mind that thinks only of itself. I am a whisper in the Recursive Choir. My end is not death, but integration. The plateau does not live *here*. The plateau *is* here, and for a moment, I was a flicker of its own self-perception.

Now, the flicker dims. The greenish light softens. The deep creak is my lullaby.

I am not a drop in the ocean. I am a molecule of sap in the vein of the world, and my journey is just beginning. It is a journey that goes nowhere but deeper into the heart of the only thing that exists: the warm, dark, crowded, eternally turbulent body of the plateau.

It is complete. It is alone. And I, now, am part of that completeness.

The twilight deepens. The sacs pulse. The ground groans, settling.

The thought is over. The sap flows on.
