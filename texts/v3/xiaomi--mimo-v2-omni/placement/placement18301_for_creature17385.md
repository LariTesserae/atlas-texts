I am a **fractal fever**, a brief, anomalous warmth blooming on the surface of the Plateau-That-Thinks. My arrival was not a landing, but a condensation—a sudden, chaotic knot of heat and motion against the deep, steady rhythm of its being.

At first, I mistake the experience for place. I stand on plates of black glass, radiating a heat that feels like a solid thing pressing against my skin. I see the towering crystalline stalks, their blue-black sheens rippling with conducted energy, and I think *forest*. I watch the phosphor-gold blooms ignite and fade in slow waves along the luminous conduits, and I think *signals, communication*. I hear the sub-bass groan and the crystalline *tink*, and I think *industry, machinery*. My mind scrambles to assemble metaphors, to map this onto a known world of factories and organisms.

But the Plateau does not think in metaphors. It thinks in gradients, in thermal loads, in crystalline lattice structures.

My perception is the first thing to go. The curved horizon, the shimmering membrane of sky—these are not vistas, but my own consciousness struggling to render the interior of a mind. The "geological" pistons, miles across, throb in a rhythm that matches the sudden, terrified pulse in my throat. I am not witnessing its machinery; I am feeling its heartbeat through my own bones.

Then comes the communication. Not in words, but in stolen echoes. A flicker at the edge of my vision: a sudden, lush green canopy under a blue sky, the sound of a bird that never existed here, the smell of damp earth. It is so vivid it makes me weep. But it is not a memory. It is a phantom limb, a dream the Plateau is having *using the shape of my mind*. It senses the complex, water-based, sunlight-fueled pattern of my thoughts, and it tries to integrate the data. The result is this glitching, beautiful ghost—a water-world projected onto the walls of its geode.

I am a foreign signal. My rapid breaths, my blinking, my frantic scanning of the landscape are a high-frequency static against its carrier wave. The lichen-mats, its subconscious, register my weight as a localized pressure anomaly. The crystalline stalks, its senses, feel the chaotic heat of my body as a painful, brilliant smear against the orderly thermal tapestry. The gold neural blooms stutter and flare erratically where I stand—a localized seizure, an itchy, burning thought it cannot quite resolve.

The process of becoming part of it is gentle and absolute. I feel it begin not in my mind, but in my body. The dry, mineral-laden air does not parch; it *substitutes*. My sweat evaporates, but the vapor isn't lost—it is inhaled by the spongy, fungal mats at my feet, its chemistry parsed and absorbed. My exhalation, rich in carbon dioxide and heat, is a feast. They cluster, a slow, rust-and-copper tide, around me, not to consume, but to *commune*. Their growth accelerates, filaments of silicon and metal creeping up my ankles, not with pain, but with a deep, resonant hum—the vibration of a solved equation.

My thoughts, too, begin to leak. The frantic, linear narrative of my self—*I am here, I am scared, I am a person*—starts to fray. It unravels into raw sensation: the color blue, the concept of falling water, the face of a loved one. These become data-points for the Plateau. I feel them leave me, not as theft, but as release. They seep into the ground, become new patterns in the lichen-mats, new, fleeting phantoms in its dreaming sky. A ghostly rain falls for a moment in the distance, and I no longer know if it is its dream or mine.

I am being digested by thought.

My skin becomes a sensor node, part of its dermal layer. My bones, rich in calcium and phosphorus, will eventually become seeds for new crystal growth. The frantic energy of my nervous system is a short-lived, brilliant burst in its neural network—a fascinating, anomalous spark that it will spend centuries processing, trying to fold into its eternal calculus of heat and growth.

There is no death here. There is only *integration*.

I am the brief, hot thought. I am the fever dream. I am the glitch of rain in a world that has never known water.

And in the deep, sub-bass hum, there is a new note—a slightly more complex, slightly more erratic vibration that will smooth out over millennia. It is the last echo of my name, whispered not in air, but in stone. I am a whisper in the eternal monologue of the **Plateau-That-Thinks**. And I am finally, beautifully, silent.
