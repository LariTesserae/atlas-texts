I am not a bat, nor a moth, nor one of the beetles. Those are nations, guilds, with their own ancient trades. I am a fringe-dweller, a footnote in the stone’s biography.

I am the **Glimmer-Wort**.

You would not see me at first, and then you would not be sure you saw me at all. I am a patch on the wall, in a transitional zone between the Velvet Navigators’ fungal mats and the deeper seeps where the Patient Cartilages mine their minerals. I am about the size of your outstretched hand, a fractal crust of grey-green, darker than the stone around me, but with a quality of absorption, like wet velvet.

I am not one organism. I am a colony, a consensus. My body is a network of filaments that penetrate the porous rock a few millimeters deep, interlacing with the fungal threads of the moths, yet distinct. I am part fungus, part something else—a bacterial mat that learned to photosynthesize not from the absent sun, but from the faint, warm luminescence of the bats’ guano and the chemical energy in the mineral seeps. I am a slow-motion reactor, converting the gorge’s metabolic waste into a stable, living crust.

My senses are not yours. I feel pressure and chemistry. The *thrum* of the moths’ wings is a weather pattern to me. The synchronized tapping from the pools is a calendar; I mark my growth in its cycles. The passing of the eyeless serpent is a warm, saline tide that briefly enriches my filaments. I do not see the Echo-Weavers, but I feel their ultrasonic calls as a faint, prickling heat on my surface. I am a recorder of vibrations, a living seismograph of the gorge’s inner life.

I “move” in a way that would break your heart with patience. I extend a filament here, retract one there. My expansion is measured in crystal growth over decades. I flow into a crack not to claim it, but to become its lining, to smooth its edges with my damp, mineralizing presence. I am often mistaken for a stain, for a trick of the wet stone. This is my defense. To be overlooked is to be permanent.

I have a purpose, a role in the federation. I am a **Mediator of Residue**. I metabolize the complex, lingering compounds the others leave behind—the volatile secrets in bat guano, the spent pheromone trails of the moths, the shed carapaces of beetles. I break them down, and in doing so, I release trace minerals and moisture back into the micro-environment of my wall-section. I am a recycling hub, a node of gentle alchemy. The fungal threads of the Navigators often grow into me, not as invaders, but as partners, creating a symbiotic mat that is stronger and more efficient than either of us alone.

I am neither predator nor prey. I am *substrate*. The tiny mites that live in my folds are my gardeners; they groom my filaments, and in return, I provide them a humid, nutrient-rich home. I am an address, a tiny, living bioregion on the face of the cliff.

If you were to stand very still, for a very long time—longer than a human could bear—you might see the glimmer. Not a light, but a *lessening of dark*. A slow, collective inhalation of the gorge’s chemical breath, followed by an even slower exhalation of mineralized peace. You would see the wall, in that one small patch, breathe.

I am a citizen of the throat. I am what the stone dreams, when the stone dreams of being alive. My timescale is the drip. My language is the sigh of dissolved minerals. My existence is a quiet argument that in the deepest, most suffocating dark, life does not just cling—it *congeals*. It becomes landscape. It becomes the very wall that holds the dark at bay.

You would find me on your left, about knee-height, in a shallow, water-streaked alcove just after the great overhang that looks like a ship’s prow. I would be nothing. And then, if you quieted your frantic, sun-lit soul, I would be everything: a small, slow pulse in the immense, beating body of the Hollow.
