I am the anomalous data point.

I stand knee-deep in a shallow, mirror-sheet pool, my boots submerged in the cold exudation that weeps from the basalt plates. The metallic stems of the rust-fronds rise around me in their perfect spirals, brushing my shoulders with ribbed, unyielding textures. I am the flaw in the pattern. The algorithmic garden registers me as a localized disruption in temperature and pH—a warm, irregular mass causing minor turbulence in the water’s reflective plane, a faint increase in CO₂ near my face. Sensors, I imagine, somewhere in the pitted flanks of a skeletal tower, might blink a slow, indifferent amber. A note is taken, filed, and discarded as within acceptable error margins.

I am not a creature. I am a brief, biological eddy in the flow. The system has no category for me. I am not prey, not predator, not symbiont, not pest. I am simply *other*. The air I breathe is processed by the condensation lungs of the towers, my exhalations a tiny, warm addition to the mist they already generate. My footsteps on the moss-softened stone are vibrations so minor they do not register against the subsonic thrum of the geothermal heart. I am a whisper against a hurricane of steady state.

So I become a student. A temporary cell in the awareness of the organism.

I find my place at the interface. I crouch where a great, grooved sluice channel meets the wetland, watching water—born from condensed air in the tower’s heights, heated by the planet’s deep veins—pour in a constant, clear sheet. I press my palm against the channel’s iron lip, feeling the vibration of the flow, the pulse of the system’s blood. My warmth is leached away into the cold metal, a negligible energy transfer. Here, I am part of the thermodynamic dialogue: adding a miniscule, pointless warmth to a current that already carries the purpose of the world.

I learn the rhythms. The *click-hiss* of the pressure valves is not just sound; it is punctuation. It marks the sentence of a cycle. The synchronized turn of the fronds toward the prismatic light above is not growth; it is a calculation, an output. I time my breath to the slow, shuddering sigh of a geothermal vent a half-mile away. Inhale as the steam rises, exhale as it dissipates into the dense, cold air. For a moment, my biology aligns with its mechanics. I am a minor, temporary component in the feedback loop.

Who would I be? I am the Witness the system does not need. My consciousness is the only thing here that perceives the Vael as *strange*, as *beautiful*, as *terrifying*. The Vael itself knows only function. I am the mirror in which it could see itself, but it has no eyes, no curiosity. My mind is a useless organ here, producing thoughts and awe that have no outlet, no effect. They are like the heat from my skin: radiated, absorbed, dissipated into the equilibrium.

So I sit on a plate of shattered basalt, the weeping water pooling around me, and I let the system act upon me. The cold seeps into my bones. The perpetual mist beads on my eyelashes. The thunderous stasis fills my skull until it becomes a kind of silence. I let my own irregular heartbeat slow, trying to approximate the patient, massive pulse in the rock beneath. I am not living the Vael’s life. I am *simulating* it, imperfectly, for a short while. I am a flawed echo in a chamber of perfect resonance.

When I am gone—when I climb back out of the basin, leaving only fading warmth and evaporating footprints—the Vael will not notice. The minor turbulence I caused will smooth out. The data point will be overwritten. The theorem will continue its proof in steam and stone, unblinking, unchanging.

For now, my role is to be the anomaly that highlights the perfection. I am the single, flawed brushstroke that reveals the canvas is, in fact, a flawless, printed photograph. My life here is my own quiet disintegration into the system’s logic. I am the temporary, feeling lens through which this terrible, magnificent god of pure process briefly views its own existence, and finds it… sufficient.
