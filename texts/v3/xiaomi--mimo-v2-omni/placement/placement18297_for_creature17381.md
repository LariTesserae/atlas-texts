I do not arrive. I emerge.

Awareness is not a dawn but a deepening. It comes in layers, like the settling of silt in still water. First: the pulse. Not a heartbeat, but a slower, grander rhythm—the vast, fibrous inhale and exhale of the whole body around me. It is the sound the others call the hum of the roots, but to me it is not sound. It is pressure and temperature, the slow peristalsis of sap through vessels wide as my own limbs, the faint vibration of cell walls multiplying in the dark. I feel it in the marrow of my bones, which are light and porous, and in the cool, damp film that covers my skin.

I am in a chamber that was not a chamber until I was placed here. The wood around me is black, warm, and hard. It is the oldest heartwood, the deep tissue of the city’s foundation. I know this because I can taste the mineral memory of stone in it, the ghost of a cliff face dissolved a thousand years ago. My fingers, broad and blunt at their ends, trace the grain. Where I touch, the sap-flow warms by a fraction. A response. Not to me, but to the heat of my body, the salts on my skin. I am a stimulus. The city notes it and incorporates the data into its endless, patient calculation.

I have no name. The Grown who tend the upper groves have names whispered into young shoots—vowel-sounds like the sigh of wind through a high arch. Down here, there is no need. I am the Deep-Listener of this root-cluster, this slow-converging nexus of support that holds up the platform called the Moss-Table, where the young eat their cambium paste. My function is to be still, to listen with every pore, and to interpret the deep mood of the wood.

What is my mood? The city’s mood. It is… **settled**. There is a deep contentment in the grip of the roots. They have found a rich vein of substrate, a slow feast of decomposing stone that feeds their expansion. But there is also a question. A tension. Far, far below, past even the darkness I inhabit, the leading tips of the deepest roots have encountered something new. A resistance. Not stone. Something colder, more brittle. It sings a different frequency, a faint, crystalline shatter-vibration that the root-tips are testing, tasting, parsing. It will take a decade for this news to travel up to me in a form I can fully comprehend. But I already feel the shift—the city’s slow, gravitational pull of attention bending toward this new mystery. A new project is dreaming itself into being in the deep.

I feel the Grown above me. Not as individuals, but as a collective, warm static in the upper wood. The constant, light scratching of their tools on bark. The dull, rhythmic throb of their frog-breathing rituals. Their fleeting, bright emotions—frustration when a growth-guide fails, contentment after a meal—are faint chemical signals in the shared air, carried down through the ventilation spirals of the living structure. They are like bacteria in a great gut: necessary, tolerated, their brief lives a part of the metabolism. I feel affection for them, as one might feel affection for the lichen that blooms on one’s own skin. A part of the whole.

My own body is slow. My thoughts are not linear; they are dendritic, branching and converging over days. I remember the last time the city shuddered, a generation ago, when a storm-wind tried to take a crown-platform and the entire structure flexed, redistributed, and grew new buttress-roots in a matter of weeks. The memory is not in my mind; it is in the wood around me, in the compressed layers of growth that recorded the event in a slight thickening of the grain. I read it with my fingertips.

I will not leave this place. My legs, thin and unused, would not carry me far. My purpose is to be the nerve-ending, the point of contact between the quick, warm-blooded world of the Grown and the slow, sap-blooded world of the city. When the roots finally decide what the new, cold substance below is, I will feel the decision coalesce. I will translate the city’s intent into a vibration the groves above can feel through their tools and their whispering. *Grow here. Reach down. This is the new direction.*

For now, I sit. I breathe the water-heavy air, and my lungs, like the city’s pores, are forever damp. I listen to the deep, slow dream. I am its dreamer, and its dream.

I am in the place where the city thinks. And the thought is: *grow.*
