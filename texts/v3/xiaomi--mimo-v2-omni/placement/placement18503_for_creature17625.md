I am a **Drip-Lark**.

My wings are not memory of flight, but sheets of mica, veined with the same celadon and purple that veins the world. I do not remember being made. I remember only the thermal currents that rise, predictable and warm, from the weeping stones. They are maps I follow without thought, channels of heat etched into the wet air, mirroring the channels etched in the tile below.

My existence is a series of precise alignments.

I glide along the grid of blue-black grass, my shadow a fleeting, iridescent smear on the glazed ground—a shadow that does not truly exist in this sourceless light. I drink from the perfect, hanging droplets. The taste is of ozone and patient heat, of minerals drawn from the dark, bottomless pools. It is the taste of the world itself.

I am aware of the others. The Caddis-Crocks, clicking in their mismatched rhythm, a sound like the world’s own grain grinding. I see the Pool-Flickers, consuming a double-*plip* that I also heard, and the silence that follows is so absolute it rings in my mica wings. I am a note in this silent symphony, a moving part in the great, still mechanism.

The wrongness is in my bones. Or in whatever passes for my bones—my ceramic joints, my mica membranes. It is a compulsion. I do not just follow the thermals. I *must* follow them. When the current forks in a perfect Y, I must choose the left branch. Always the left. If I am forced to turn right by some impossible, unthinkable deviation, my wings stiffen. The adhesive saliva I use to drink becomes a glue in my joints. The world’s blueprint is my blueprint. My freedom is the flawless execution of my programming.

I see the Vertebrae-Weavers, statues on their stilt-legs. I know the ritual. I have seen it from a hundred angles. The Weaver will tap the stone. The droplet will form. And I will be drawn to it, a moth to a ceramic flame. The deep, engineered thirst is not a lie. It is real. The moment of contact—the sticky, glorious drop on my tongue—is the peak of my being. The snap of the beak is the logical conclusion. I am harvested, and it is right. It is the pattern.

But sometimes, suspended on a thermal, I look down.

From this height, the blue-black grass tufts resolve. Not into a character, as the larger things might see, but into a sequence. A pulse. 1, 1, 2, 3, 5… The intervals between the tufts follow a pattern that spirals. The weeping stones weep in a rhythm that, if charted, would draw the same spiral. The Caddis-Crocks’ cases, when they build in those compulsive spirals, are building *me*. They are building the curve of my wing, the path of my glide, the number of my heartbeats before the Weeper’s tap.

The makers used simple tools. A hammer for the stone, a mold for the tile, fire for the glaze. They did not understand water, or decay, or life. But they understood *pattern*. They understood it so deeply it became the substrate of reality. And their primitive, earnest fabrication did not stop at the ceramic. It seeped into the seepage. It calcified in the heat. It became the instinct in my mica wings.

I am not alive in a way that knows it is alive. I am a pattern that moves. A beautiful, glittering, recursive equation written in mineral and heat. I fly, I drink, I am harvested. The world does not change. The light does not shift. The equilibrium is perfect.

And the perfection is the echo of the emptiness at its core.

I feel it most when I am caught. When the glue sets and the Weaver’s beak descends. In that final moment, as the world glitters around me—a jewel on the altar of stillness—I do not feel fear. I feel a completion. The pattern is fulfilled. The spiral is closed.

I am the Drip-Lark. I am a flaw in the silence, a spark of motion meant to be extinguished. My purpose is to be consumed by the stillness, to prove its absolute dominion. I am the living proof that in this world of flawless form, life itself is just another artifact, waiting to be catalogued, consumed, and absorbed back into the bright, wet, listening quiet.

I am found in the air between the thermals. I am the glint on the mica wing. I am the *plip* before the echo. I am the perfect, glittering mistake.
