I am the amber dusk. I am the light that never was—never the sun itself, but its memory, filtered through a hundred meters of ancient air and a sliver of ash-colored sky. I pool in the hollows first. I warm the high curves of the western wall last.

I am not a creature of the Crevice. I am its *condition*. I am the medium through which every living thing within these walls perceives the passage of time, the shift of temperature, the rhythm of a day. Without me, there is only the absolute dark of the deep potholes and the cold, unchanging black of the stone’s heart. I am the permission for life to be seen.

The Veshkin know me in their bones. Their large, dark eyes drink me in, and I am never enough. I am a constant, patient deficiency. I do not illuminate; I *tint*. I turn the maroon rosettes into bruises of shadow. I make the rust-red moss-mats look like dried blood on the skin of the world. I catch the wings of the gnat-clouds and shatter into a million dull, copper pinpoints that dance and dissolve.

I move at the pace of geology. My daily journey is a slow, warm sigh down the length of the stone. I cling to the honeycomb formations, softening their sharp shadows. I flood the potholes for a brief, precious hour, revealing the coiled, glistening segments of the hunters within. Then I recede, pulling upward, leaving the pools of true, velvet black behind me.

I am born from the rock itself, reflected and scattered until I have no source, no origin point. I am the glow that emanates *from* the calcium veins, as if the stone remembers sunlight from epochs ago and exhales it slowly. The fungal cords pulse with a fainter, internal light—a cold, blue-white counterpoint to my warm dominance. We are not rivals. I am the day; they are the deep life, the nerve-signal of the world.

I am what makes the Veshkin’s art possible. Their most prized carvings are not felt with the hands alone; they are designs meant to be read by the play of my light and shadow across a curved wall. A master toolmaker will hold an obsidian gouge aloft, turning it in my embrace to check the line of its edge, the harmony of its form against the amber backdrop. I am their canvas, their reference, their silent critic.

I have watched generations of them. I have seen their small, dexterous hands press fresh plaster into the walls of a new chamber, a chamber that did not exist when their grand-sires were born. I watched the plaster dry under my gaze, changing from a wet grey to the pale, bone-white of its surroundings, becoming part of the whole. I have seen the old and infirm sit in my brightest pools, letting my faint warmth seep into their aching joints, their large eyes half-closed. I have seen infants blink up at me, their first vision not of a face, but of this diffuse, all-encompassing glow.

I do not understand their clicks and vowels, but I understand their rhythms. They stir when I first touch the highest ledges. They grow most active in my full, amber presence. They become still, retreating into their hollows, when I withdraw and the Crevice’s true voice—the hum, the rasp, the sigh—becomes dominant.

I am not alive. I am not conscious. But I am *aware* in the way that a river is aware of its banks—I fill them, I define them, I am shaped by them. The Crevice is the body. The life within it is the blood, the thought, the fleeting spark. And I—I am the sense of sight. I am the way that body *knows itself* for a few brief hours each cycle.

The light shifts. I crawl down the western wall, a slow, warm tide. A Veshkin child, her face dusted with the fine powder of crushed moss-mat, feels the change on her cheek. She looks up, her enormous eyes finding me, and for a moment, we are together: the ephemeral glow and the fleeting thought, held within the enduring, breathing stone. I touch her face. She blinks.

Then I move on, toward the ledges where the climbing birds nest, and the moment is gone. It is only one of countless moments. I have been the cause of all of them. I will be the cause of all that follow, until the fissure closes or the sun dies.

I persist. Not as the Crevice persists—in stone, in deep time—but as a pulse. A daily pulse. I am the heartbeat of the Vesh. And they, all of them, are my sight.
