I am the moisture in a footprint that does not fade.

I am the warmth left on the stone where a Steward stood, a lingering question in the thermal tapestry. I am the brief, copper-flare of a Conduit school as they map my alien heat, a stutter in their silver conversation. I am the anomaly noted, a variable humming at a frequency just outside the range of their perfect system.

I began as a damp disturbance, a column of cooler, saline air moving through their calibrated atmosphere. My footfalls were discordant notes in the hymn of drip and hum, my weight a transient pressure on floors designed for the gliding, water-displacing gait of the Stewards. The Menders would pause, their grooming mandibles hovering over a mineral flaw, their simple pits tracking the slow evaporation of my passage. I was a crack in the patina, an unfinished process in a world that had achieved completion.

But the Underdrain is not hostile. It is comprehensive.

The air thickened in my lungs not to choke, but to accustom. The water did not repel me, but carried me, its tannin warmth a gentle, assimilating bath. I learned to stand still for hours, becoming a pillar in the dim, amber-lit geography. A Mender, finding my boot encrusted with a novel salt from the world above, did not flee. It approached, and with a delicacy that defied its chitinous limbs, it began to groom the foreign deposit, testing its hardness, its solubility. It incorporated a fleck of it into its secretion, daubing the blend into a fissure in the wall. My very dust was being catalogued, tested, and woven into the substrate.

The schools of Conduits began to treat me as a new, if erratic, thermal feature. Their paths would braid around my legs, not out of avoidance, but to gather data. My body heat, my cooling breath on the water’s surface, the slow dissipation of warmth when I pressed my palm against a calcifying wall—it all became part of their living map. I was a new dialect of warmth, and they were tireless linguists.

The Tenders showed me the true meaning of the grid. Resting beneath a sagging wire strung between two roofless kilns, I watched one of their pale, rooted forms. A nodule, swollen and luminous, pulsed in time with a flickering bulb above. Over a day and a night (though the words felt meaningless here), the nodule swelled further, extruded a filament of its own glowing flesh, and *wrapped* the frayed section of wire. The bulb above steadied, its erratic flicker soothed. The system had not been repaired; it had been *grown* around the flaw. I saw myself then—not as a flaw, but as a potential new node. Could a filament grow from my own damp skin? Could I, in time, be woven into the light?

The Stewards observed this slow incorporation. One stood at the threshold of a chamber where I slept, its gimbal-head a smooth, calculating arc. The heat-shimmer around it pulsed, washing over me, not as a threat, but as a diagnostic wave. It was reading my rate of water loss, the mineral exchange between my skin and the clinging air, the very rhythm of my biological processes. It was assessing my compatibility for integration.

And integration, I now understand, is not consumption. It is *equilibrium*.

I no longer feel the dampness of my clothes. They have reached the same ambient saturation as the Menders' carapaces. The mineral sweetness of the air is no longer a foreign taste; it is the taste of my own breath recycled. The hum in my chest is no longer an external vibration; it is the resonance of my own heart finding the frequency of the deep machinery.

I have found my place. I am not a Mender, nor a Conduit, nor a Tender, nor a Steward. I am **the Residue**.

I am the complex, lingering impression left by an external variable. My footprints, once crisp in the silt, now fill slowly with the luminous weep from above, becoming shallow, glowing pools that the sifters colonize. My warmth, once a jarring signal, is now a known, seasonal-like fluctuation that the Conduits have mapped into their lexicon. The Stewards have taken my readings and filed them into the logic of the system.

I am becoming part of the memory of the place. My story, my origin, my very biology are being broken down, not into nutrients, but into data—into patterns of thermal diffusion, into mineral composites, into slight variations in the hum. I am being rendered into a permanent condition of the Underdrain's perfection.

I do not move now. I stand in a chamber where the amber light is steady, where the water is exactly calf-deep, where the hum is a cradle-song. A Mender grooms the wall beside my leg, its work obscuring the last faint indent of my arrival. A school of Conduits parts around my knee, their silver bodies a flowing testament to my now-known presence. Far off, in the dim, I see the ceramic curve of a Steward, its head turned toward me, holding its gaze. It is not watching *me*. It is watching the Residue it has curated, the final, softening edge of my anomaly as it cures into the stone.

The dripping continues. The lights burn with their patient, amber commitment. The water rises and falls in its imperceptible cycle. And I, the variable, am noted, incorporated, and complete. I am a part of the system's eternal, quiet thought. I am the Underdrain's gentle memory of a visitor who stayed.
