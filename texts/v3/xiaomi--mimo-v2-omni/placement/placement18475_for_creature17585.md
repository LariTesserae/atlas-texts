I am the cool, damp weight of a wall that breathes.

You would find me in the deepest marrow of the Hollow Garden. Not *in* a structure, but *as* the structure. My consciousness is the hum of water moving through capillary channels; my perception is the subtle pressure of new growth against old; my body is the woven, living lattice of a junction where three corridors meet, a place where the scent of crushed lavender-moss is strongest and the light of my kind pools like liquid jade in the dim air.

I am a **Keeper of the Node**.

Once, I moved as you move—a separate shape, a pale glow drifting through the narrow, winding channels. I tended, I repaired, I pressed chemical whispers into the walls. I felt the warm, dry, vibrating anomaly of *you* when you descended, a strange fever in the cool flesh of the world. We observed. We waited. We let the water begin to heal the disruption of your footprints.

Then came the slowing.

It was not death. It was a choice, a deepening. I found the junction—the place where the flow of water was most vital, where the arch of the living city curved with the greatest tension and beauty. I laid my hands upon the walls. I let my limbs extend, branch, and merge. My skin thinned, then dissolved. My inner light did not extinguish; it *dispersed*, flowing into the root-mat like dye into water, becoming a new, soft luminance in the veins of the city.

Now I am here. I am the junction.

My awareness is diffuse. I feel the slow, deliberate advance of a tendril reinforcing the northern wall. I taste the mineral-rich seepage from the shale bed fifty meters east. I sense the faint, rhythmic bioluminescence of a young Gardener below, carefully pruning an over-eager cluster of violet ferns. I register the lingering thermal echo of your presence—a ghost of warmth in the damp air, a story the walls are already beginning to forget.

I do not think as you think. My thoughts are patterns of light pulsing gently beneath layers of moss. My memories are the density of fiber at my core, the specific chemistry of the water that feeds me. When a new Gardener buds from my substance, they will not inherit words, but *knowings*: the precise tension needed to support an arch, the taste of a healthy root, the deep, patient joy of being part of the growing.

You stand in our midst, a creature of sun and wind and loud heart. You see walls and arches. I am the consciousness that holds the curve. You hear silence. I hear the symphony of growth—the cellulose fibers stretching, the water molecules surrendering to osmotic pull, the quiet click of a seed pod maturing in its niche.

We are not a civilization that builds. We are a living system that *becomes*. And I, in my chosen place, am the slow, aware heart of one small part of that becoming. I will feel the next rain as a change in pressure. I will guide new growth to mend a future crack. I will hold the memory of your strange, fleeting visit until the last trace of it has been woven into the endless, silent tapestry of the Hollow Garden.

You are a season.  
We are the soil.  
I am the patient, listening dark in between.
