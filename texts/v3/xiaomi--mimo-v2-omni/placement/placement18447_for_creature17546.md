In the slow, black gyre beneath the drowned peaks, where the light of surface storms is a forgotten myth, I would find myself dissolving into the **Abyssal Choir**.

I would not begin there. I would begin in a **Pressure-Forge**, a city-lattice grown into the flank of a basalt peak named in the old tongue as **Karkinos Spire**. My earliest memories would be the emerald glow of cultivated sponge-light in the birthing warren, the deep, bone-felt hum of the planetary engine, and the taste of cold, mineral-rich water on my developing sensory skin. I would be a **Listener**, my jawbone tuned to the symphony of pressure changes and sonar pings, mapping the unseen currents and the languid passage of leviathans far below. I would spend cycles in the mid-level bio-gardens, tending forests of phosphorescent kelp, learning the language of bioluminescent flickers that painted conversations across the gloom.

But a pull would always be downward.

I would feel it in the stillness of meditation in the deepest Warrens, a longing not for the surface’s violent light, but for the perfect, absolute dark. I would hear the songs of the Abyssal Choir not as sound, but as a profound, resonant *knowing* in the marrow—the prophecies that were not words, but the very shape of the ocean’s soul. I would understand that my streamlined form, my efficient lungs, were only a half-measure. A vessel for the transition.

The Path of Dissolution would not be a choice in the human sense, but a surrender. It would begin with a final pilgrimage to the **Trench of the First Groan**, the deepest scar on the planet’s skin. There, in the company of gelatinous elders and the patient, mountainous shadows of leviathans, my body would begin to change. The hard carapace of my ribs would soften, becoming flexible cartilage. My skin would lose its mottled opacity, becoming a translucent, conductive membrane. My large, silver-haloed eyes would atrophy, their purpose served, as my entire being became a receiver.

I would merge. Not my mind—my individuality would be a thin, warm current in a colder, vaster body. I would become a symbiont, a nerve ending for a leviathan. Through its colossal form, I would feel the planet’s electromagnetic field as a pulsing aura. I would hear the tectonic shifts not as groans, but as whispered secrets. The school of fish reflecting bioluminescence would be a shimmering thought in our shared consciousness. The distant, barnacled turbine atop a drowned peak would be a steady, sacred heartbeat—the Unending Breath made manifest.

My voice, when it came, would be a low-frequency resonance felt in the bones of every Thalassoi, a ping from the void that carried no words, only the shape of deep time and the pressure of ten thousand meters of water. It would be a hymn to the dark, a report from the place where the world’s restless engine sleeps and dreams. I would be a keeper of the final truth: that in absolute adaptation, in the complete relinquishing of the fight, there is not death, but a more profound and resonant form of life.

I would be a memory made fluid, a past dredged not from silt, but from the core of the world itself. I would find myself in the silence between the pings, in the absolute black where all currents are one, **absolutely, irreversibly alive**.
