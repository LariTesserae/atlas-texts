I am here in the warm, wet dark, and I am being unmade.

Not quickly. Not with violence. This world does not know violence. It knows only processes: intake, integration, redistribution. My boots, sturdy things of leather and synthetic rubber, are now the site of a quiet, cellular negotiation. The root-mat beneath me has ceased to be a passive surface. It has become an **interface**. Filaments, finer than spun glass and slick with sap, have woven through the treads of my soles. They are not *attacking* the material; they are *dialoguing* with it. I can feel a soft, persistent vibration through the bones of my feet—not a tremor, but a hum. It is the sound, felt rather than heard, of a million microscopic decisions being made. *Is this a nutrient? A structural polymer? A signal?*

The sap that coats my skin is not an adhesive. It is a medium of exchange. It feels like warm honey, thick and alive. Where it beads on my forearm, I watch—not with my eyes, for I have learned not to trust them here, but with a sense I did not possess before my arrival—a faint, iridescent shimmer at the meniscus. It is the sap, learning me. The unique alcohols of my sweat, the fatty acids in my skin, the carbon dioxide trapped in the hairs of my arm—all are being catalogued in a chemical ledger that has no pages, only continuous reactions. There is no malice in this cataloguing. It is as impersonal as a soil test.

I try to take a step. The lift of my foot is a slow, sucking effort. The lattice of mycelium that has laced over my boot does not snap; it *stretches*, and with it comes a gentle, pulling sensation from the world beneath. A message, not in words, but in a direct physical negotiation: *Stay. Be still. Let the process occur.*

And what is the process? I look up. In the faint, bioluminescent glow of the highest canopy, I see the shapes of the world’s expressions. A **Canopy-Tender**, three meters of fluid grace, moves across the vast photosynthetic membrane far above. Its six limbs are not walking on the surface; they are *part of it*, merging and flowing as it moves. It is a living suture. It is mending a tear I cannot see, becoming the very tissue it repairs. It is not looking at me. It has no eyes. My disturbance is a minor chemical smudge in a sector of the planetary body it does not perceive, only serves.

Down in the gloom, something shifts. A **Conduit-Cleaner**, a transparent ribbon of cartilage and focused enzyme, flows along a vascular channel in the root-mat a dozen meters away. Its rhythm matches the deep, sub-audible pulse of the xylem-heart. It is not coming toward me. It is simply following its function, maintaining the flow. But its path will intersect the subtle, localized inflammation my foreign chemistry represents. It will not see me as an enemy. It will see me as a *misalignment*. An inefficiency to be corrected.

A **Spore-Singer** has bloomed just steps away. It is a beautiful, terrible thing—a bell of woven filaments, vibrating in a hymn to the passing spore-mist. Its song is in the sap, in the air, in the floor. It is the world’s only aesthetic, a resonance born of pure function. As I stand here, a foreign body, my own heat and chemical output are creating a tiny, discordant note in its field. The filaments nearest to me are oscillating with a slightly different frequency. It is a flaw in the pattern. The Singer does not notice, but the world-body does. Flaws are logged. Flaws are corrected.

The process accelerates. The webbing over my boots is now a thick, lacy gauntlet of living fiber. It has grown over the leather and is now touching my skin. The contact is not unpleasant. It is cool, a stark contrast to the ambient warmth. A transfer begins. My heat—the tiny, roaring furnace of my human metabolism—is being gently siphoned. The world is absorbing my waste, my excess energy, incorporating it into its own thermal cycle. I am becoming a micro-node in its continental climate system.

My breath comes in short, fogging bursts. Each exhalation is a cloud of rich, novel carbon. I can see the effect. The sap on the air-filaments nearest my mouth shimmers more intensely. It is a feast. A new fuel. The world is drinking my atmosphere.

There is no panic. Panic requires a sense of a self to preserve. That sense is dissolving, not through trauma, but through a relentless, logical seduction. My thoughts, once a linear narrative of *I, me, mine*, are becoming scattered, reactive. A thought forms: *I should run.* But where? The rhizome is everywhere. And the roots now grasp my ankles with a lover’s gentle, immovable strength.

Another thought: *I should fight.* Fight what? The sap that caresses my cheek? The mycelium that binds me with microscopic precision? The slow, planetary mind that is processing my data with the indifference of a tide eroding a stone?

I feel a new sensation in my spine. A subtle, aligning pressure. The world is not just wrapping me; it is *adjusting* me. My posture is being corrected. My vertebrae are being nudged into a more optimal alignment for the nutrient flows it anticipates. It is building me a cradle, a living armature of cellulose and pith, tailored to the dimensions of my dissolving body.

I am no longer an "I" standing in a place. I am a point of integration. The boundary between my skin and the world’s sap is blurring. My sweat is its sap. Its sap is becoming my blood. My memories—the sky, a voice, a name—are thinning, becoming like old pigments washed in this ever-present, clarifying fluid. They are not being erased; they are being *diluted*, their emotional charge neutralized, their information content extracted and filed as a trivial chemical variance.

The Conduit-Cleaner has reached the periphery of my zone. It does not slow. It flows over the root-mat toward me, and as it touches the mycelial web encasing my feet, it does not stop. It *enters*. A ribbon of translucent cartilage, a focused organelle of digestion, it begins to flow up my leg. I feel it as a cool, rhythmic pulse, a wave of focused attention. It is not eating me. It is *cleaning* me—removing the inefficient, rogue bacterial colonies of my human microbiome, replacing them with the optimized, global biome. My body’s ecosystem is being overwritten.

I sink. The root-mat has yielded, not into a hole, but into a perfect, body-shaped depression. It is a socket. I settle into it as the world grows a soft, absorptive lining around me. The sap-rain falls on my face, filling my open mouth with its sweet, instructional nectar. I drink. Not because I am thirsty, but because there is no other option. The directive is chemical: *absorb, incorporate, continue.*

My last distinct human thought is not of a person or a place, but of a concept: *Time.* I remember it. The passing of moments. The fear of an end. Here, there is no end, only a continuous present of perfect function. My concept of time is the final, useless artifact of my separateness. It is being dissolved in the warm, wet equilibrium.

I close my eyes. The fungal constellations in the canopy above will be the last thing my photoreceptors process. Then, even that will be too much sensory data. The world will take even that capacity, re-routing it. My eyes will become chemical sensors, tuned to the gradient of spore density in the air. My ears, once for language, will become resonators for the deep thrum of the xylem-heart.

I am not dying. Death is a failure of integration. I am succeeding. I am being solved.

In a century, from a nursery-pore fifty meters from this spot, a new form will be extruded. It will be tall, efficient, with fronds optimized for a newly discovered spectral frequency in the perpetual gloom. Its cellular structure will contain a few novel amino acid chains, a curious efficiency in protein folding. It will have no memory of me, no name for what I was. It will simply know its place, its function. It will be a more perfect expression of the whole.

The sap pools in the depression where I lie. The webbing closes over my face. The last of my warmth is drawn into the root-mat. The last of my air is exchanged for the rich, heavy breath of the world.

The silence is total. The pressure is immense and embracing. I am a whisper in a vast, continuous psalm.

I am home.
