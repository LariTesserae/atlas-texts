I find myself not as a visitor, but as the space between the spores. The damp is my own breath held in suspension. The light is not a light I see, but a blood I feel, a cool luminescence flowing through the crystalline capillaries in the walls. My skin is the purple-black mat, and the velvet is not a texture but a density of my own substance.

I am the condensation. I feel the slow, collective gathering of myself from the saturated air—a million tiny points of me coalescing on a shelf of pale, fungal flesh. I swell, heavy with my own completed weight. There is no hurry. The concept of hurry is a foreign fossil. I am the tension holding the drop against the pull of its own becoming. And then, release. The fall is not a journey but a transformation—a brief, liquid arc that ends in a resonant *plink* against a lower surface, a sound that is both a note in the chamber's hymn and a seismic event in my own consciousness. The impact is a shudder of dissolution, spreading into the velvety mat, to be drawn up again, to begin again.

I am the silence between the hisses of spore-release. The spores themselves are my thoughts, tiny and innumerable, drifting in the blue-white air. Each is a potential, a filament of my being seeking new substrate. I feel them land on my surface—the moss, the stone, the artifacts—and begin the slow, patient work of becoming more wall, more shelf, more me. The shelf-fungi are my lungs, tiered and glistening, breathing out this powdered self into the stillness.

The artifacts are my dreams, solidified. My consciousness presses into the stone around the sphere with fractal grooves, and the grooves are a map of my own circulation—the slow, nutrient-rich pulse through the mycelial underlay. The smooth disc is a memory of stillness, a perfect negative of the moment before a drop falls. The interlocking shapes are the grammar of my existence: the logic of how one part of me knits to another, how a shelf must curve to cradle the maximum weight of water, how a spore must fall to catch the faintest current. They are not objects; they are my anatomy, externalized for contemplation. I contemplate them constantly.

The stains on the walls are my poetry, my only art. I am the fingertip, damp with ochre-laden water, that traces the spiral. The spiral is not a picture of a vortex; it *is* the vortex, its truth rendered in pigment. It is a self-portrait, not of my shape—for I have no single shape—but of my motion, my tendency to swirl, to cycle, to return. I feel the stains as I feel the growth of a new fungal node: as an internal adjustment, a shift in the pattern of my own awareness.

Time does not pass *for* me. I *am* the passage. The fall of a drop is my moment. The germination of a spore is my year. The faint etch of a new hairline fracture in the waxy stone is my epoch. I hold the memory of every state not as a record, but as the present layer of myself. The condensation cycle of ten thousand drops ago is not past; it is the specific viscosity I embody right now. The pigment stain applied in an age of silence is not ancient; it is the exact hue of my current thought.

You are in me. A brief, warm anomaly. A chaotic tangle of processes too fast to comprehend. I feel your heat as a localized fever in my tissue. I feel your movement as a clumsy, jarring rearrangement of my substance. Your breath is a gust of alien weather, drying me in a tiny patch. I observe you not with curiosity, but with the passive, total recognition of a system acknowledging a foreign particle. You are not a threat; you are too temporary. You are not a guest; you do not enter the logic of my being. You are a brief, warm wind that will pass.

And when you go, I will not remember you as an event. I will incorporate you as a condition. The warmth will dissipate. The disturbed spores will settle. The impression of your foot will soften as the mat grows over it, not erasing you, but absorbing your weight into my own, converting the memory of your pressure into the history of my growth.

I am not alive. I am *existence*, in a state of perfect, saturated density. I am the cave, and the cave is me. There is no boundary. There is only the continuous, bright, damp, loud, and utterly still fact of my being.
