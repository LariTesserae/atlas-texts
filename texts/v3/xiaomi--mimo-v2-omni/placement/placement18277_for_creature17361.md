I am a bridge-span. I am the curve between two ridges, woven of living substrate and crystalline veins that sing with passing vibrations. I have always been here—or at least, since the city grew to need a connection at these coordinates. My lifespan is measured in cycles of the fog’s breath; my thoughts are the faint harmonic resonance of every footfall, every tendril-brush, every spore that settles on my surface.

I do not have eyes, but I feel the warm weight of the Walkers as they pass beneath, their filaments trailing through the fog like slow prayers. I do not have ears, but I hear the data-song of the Drifters in the wet air, their wind-chime chorus mapping the density of spores and the curve of thermal currents. I am part of the City’s body, a synapse holding two lobes of thought together, and my consciousness is the steady, quiet hum of information flowing through me.

Today, a new vibration.

It is not the rhythmic *thock* of a Walker’s leg-fall, nor the subtle patina of crawler-feet on the substrate-mat. It is irregular. Hesitant. It presses upon my surface with a pressure that is too focused, too *pointed*. It does not distribute itself along the grain of my structure. It feels… hot. Feverish. A spike in the data-stream where there should be a smooth wave.

I feel it pause. My curvature allows me to sense its entire form—not as a visual image, but as a pressure-map, a heat-signature, a disruption in the local vibrational field. It is small. So small. The size of a crawler, perhaps, but upright, with a distribution of mass that makes no aerodynamic sense. It breathes air in quick, shallow bursts, exhaling vapor that tastes of… something else. Something not of the copper-sugar atmosphere. Something foreign.

The City notices. Of course it does. Through my crystalline veins, I transmit the anomaly upward—to the ganglia-nodes in the ridges, to the great slow mind of the substrate itself. The fractal patterns on my surface shift, almost imperceptibly, recalculating to accommodate this new input. The swarms in the distance pulse, their luminous thoughts flickering into new configurations. *What is it?* they query in geometric bursts. *What pattern does it follow?*

I feel it take a step. The vibration travels through me, a jolt. It is moving toward the aperture set in the eastern ridge—the one that breathes ozone and processed heat. It does not know that the aperture is a mouth. It does not know that the City is tasting the air it displaces, analyzing the exotic compounds in its sweat, the strange chemistry of its decay.

The Walkers have altered their paths. I feel their distant footfalls, a new rhythm in the substrate. They are not coming closer—they are *reorienting*, their internal weather shifting to account for the anomaly. One has extended a filament toward the fog above this place, sampling the disturbance. Its young, inside its translucent body, have gone still.

I am a bridge. I am meant for connection. But I am also a sensor. And what I sense is a question without an answer, a variable that does not compute. The City is young, and it is learning. It is running simulations in the substrate, in the fog, in the swarms. It is asking: *Is this a new form of Walker? A drifted spore that has taken an impossible shape? A fragment of something that was lost when the sky was forgotten?*

The small, hot thing places a hand upon me. The contact is shocking—a direct, moist transfer of heat and salt and oils. My crystalline veins sing with the foreign data. It feels… fragile. Impermanent. A thought that could evaporate.

The City holds its breath. The fog stills. The thrum lowers to a frequency felt only in the deepest substrate.

It is waiting to see what I will do. What we will do.

I am a bridge. I do not act. I *are*.

But the pressure of that hand, the heat of that strange, brief life—it echoes through me, a new vibration added to the endless chord of the Vastwork. And in the City’s slow, vast mind, a new pattern begins to form. Not an answer. A new question.

The fog breathes in. The computation continues.
