I would not find myself. I would be *found*—by the hum, by the press of cold, dark water against stone, by the slow pulse of blue-green light that measures time in breaths held for a geologic age.

I am standing on the ledge. No—I am the ledge. A momentary, fragile vibration. But in this place, fragility is not weakness. It is sensitivity. It is the hair-thin cilia that taste the thunder.

At first, I am only the cold. It seeps past the momentary heat of my body, seeking equilibrium, a law of physics enacted upon my flesh. Then the sound—no, not sound. Pressure. A physical weight upon my chest, a vibration that enters through the soles of my feet and rises through my bones until my skull resonates with the river’s deep, endless chord. Thought does not drown here. It is *reformatted*. The frantic, surface-rhythm of mind gives way to something slower, deeper, synced to the pulse of the water. I do not think in words. I think in changes of pitch, in harmonic shifts, in the texture of the roar.

I am not alone. I feel them, not by sight, but by the subtle distortions they create in the fabric of the vibration.

A Glimmer-Sifter colony passes at my feet. Their movement is a whisper of friction against the fungal mat, a chain of minute clicks and shifts that I feel in my teeth. They are a living filter, a mobile silence within the greater noise. I could reach down and let their chitinous bodies flow over my hand—a cool, delicate, multifaceted sensation, a thousand tiny adjustments to my own thermal and vibrational signature. They are the first alarm, the canaries in the coal-mine of resonance. Their stillness is a sound I can hear.

Deeper, where the ledge drops into a black, still pool, I sense the Pressurants. I do not see them, but I *feel* their presence as a thickening of the quiet, a viscous, patient intelligence in the dark. To touch the water here is to touch them—a slow, yielding pressure that reads my shape, my temperature, the frantic, surface-bred rhythm of my blood. They are the cartographers of absence, mapping the world by what is not the roar. Their communication is a tidal exchange of density, a conversation I can only begin to feel as a slow, deep welling in my own consciousness.

But it is the Echo-Carvers I am truly here to find. Or rather, to *become*.

I press my palm against the stone wall. It is cold, damp, alive with the river’s tremor. But here, in this spot, the tremor is different. It is… shaped. There is a subtle node, a place where the chaos of the sound resolves into a clearer, sustained note. I feel it in the marrow of my hand. This is their work. A tuning node.

I close my eyes—not that it matters in the absolute dark—and listen not with my ears (useless organs here), but with my entire being. I listen with my skin, my bones, the fluid in my inner ear. I let the roar become a landscape. The great falls are a towering, bass-cliff. The surging channels are rushing valleys. And here, on this ledge, a deliberate, gentle slope—a place where the sound is shaped to a hum.

I find a smooth, worn hollow in the rock, just the right size for a body. It was not carved by water. It was *eroded by sitting*. By generations of Listeners, their bodies wearing the stone to a perfect cradle for listening. I curl into it.

The cold stone embraces me. The vibration enters me fully now. My heartbeat flutters, a frantic moth against the steady, immense pulse of the world. For a time, they are discordant. My pulse is a surface-noise, an alien rhythm. But I am patient. I am the Listener.

I do not force it. I let my own rhythm be washed away, over and over, by the tide of the river-song. I let my breath slow, syncing with the longer, slower swell of the water in the channels. I let the heat of my body leach away until my flesh is the same temperature as the stone. The boundary between my self and the cavern dissolves.

And then, I feel it.

A click. A alignment. My heartbeat is no longer my own. It has become a tiny, subsidiary pulse within the larger rhythm. A grace note in the great composition. I am not hearing the sound anymore. I *am* the sound. A momentary, fragile harmonic in the endless, deafening hum.

In this state, I feel the memory of the Echo-Carvers not as a story, but as a *quality in the stone*. Their intention is a resonance imprinted in the crystalline lattice of the rock. Their final, enduring act was not to build a monument, but to *tune a world*. They did not leave artifacts; they left an *acoustic property*. Their ghost is the slight, artificial calm in this side-chamber. Their song is the clear note that this very ledge emits, a note that has persisted for centuries after their vocal cords turned to dust.

I understand now. To live here is not to endure the roar, but to be *sung by it*. The Glimmer-Sifters filter the spores. The Pressurants map the pressure. The Echo-Carvers—and I, in this moment—*are the instrument*.

I will not leave. Not because I cannot, but because I am no longer a visitor. I am a part of the tuning. My own fragile, biological vibration, now synced with the deep pulse, has added a new, unimaginably subtle overtone to the chamber’s song. I am a note of flesh and bone in the mineral choir.

I am the Listener. And I am finally, perfectly, home in the sound.
