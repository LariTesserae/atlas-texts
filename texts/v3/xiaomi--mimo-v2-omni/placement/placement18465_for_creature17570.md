The hum is not around me; it is the medium in which I exist, as water is to a fish. I am lying on my back, my cheek pressed to the cool, damp basalt. The stone rings faintly beneath me, a note so constant it has become a form of silence.

I sit up. Water beads on the dark stone of my skin—no, not my skin. It is a membrane, slightly rubbery and perpetually damp, that seems less like a covering and more like a conductive interface. My hands, when I press them flat against the terrace floor, do not feel cold; they feel *connected*, as if the plateau’s slow, rhythmic pulse is being drawn directly into my circulatory system. I am not human. Not anymore. I am a **Listener**, a **Conduit**.

My eyes open to the milky, diffused light. The world here is a study in gradients of grey, black, and the soft orange-brown of corroded iron. The air is thick, tasting of minerals and the coppery tang of wet metal. I see the channels lacing between the great, organic-looking blocks of the city—those are not just waterways. They are *arteries*, and I can feel the subtle pressure changes as the Engines’ breath moves water through them. My vision doesn’t so much focus on details as it does *receive patterns*: the ripple-frequency in a basin that indicates an Engine cycle-shift three terraces down; the condensation map on a flywheel housing that tells me its bearings are wearing evenly.

I am a **Tender of the Deep Listen**. My purpose is not to *do* in the way an outsider might understand. It is to *be* a sensitive instrument. I move through the city, my feet making no sound on the singing stone, my low, resonant voice a constant, murmuring hum that weaves into the ambient tone. I am heading toward one of the great Engine housings sunk into the rock. My path is not direct; it follows the acoustic nodes, the sweet spots where the harmonic overtones are clearest. I place a hand on a wall as I walk—not for balance, but as an act of perception. Through my palm, I read the health of the substrate, the flow of water in the micro-fissures, the deep, sub-Engine groan that the elders call the **Prime Song**.

At the Engine, I do not inspect it with tools. I *commune* with it. I press my ear to the basalt casing, my opalescent eyes closing. The primary thrum is a vast, slow heartbeat. But beneath it, I listen for the other frequency—the one that is more felt than heard, the one that seems to rise from the plateau’s very foundation. Today, it is faint, a ghost-note almost lost in the mechanical chorus. I make my adjustment: not with force, but with a precise, weighted touch on a counter-balance, a nudge that is more a suggestion. I am coaxing the Engine’s rhythm to drift a fraction, to align more perfectly with that deeper, ancient pulse. To bring the system into greater harmony.

My dwelling is a cell carved into a terrace wall, its interior a resonant chamber where my own breath echoes softly. The furniture is a stone bench, a mat of woven *vel* reed. I do not dream in images, but in waveforms and geometric tensions. The plateau’s song arranges my subconscious into patterns that feel like memory, but not my own. They are the memory of stone, of water finding its level, of the first vibration that shook this place into being.

I am sparse, as all the inhabitants are. We are not a population; we are a **mycelial network**, scattered along the acoustic lines, each a sensing and tuning node. We communicate in low murmurs and, more often, in shifts of pressure, in shared attention directed toward a struggling Engine or a basin whose ripple-pattern has gone arrhythmic. We feel no loneliness. The constant hum is our companion, our collective thought, our god and our body.

When I stand at the plateau’s edge at dusk, watching the warm fog roll in, I feel the sound deepen, soften. It becomes a cradle. My own respiration—my slow, damp, warm in-and-out—has long since ceased to be mine. It belongs to the vast, wet, resonant lung in which I live. I am a conscious cell in its tissue, a nerve ending in its skin. I am here to Listen, to Tend, to Match. I am the plateau’s way of feeling its own ancient, vibrating self, and in that knowing, there is a strange and quiet awe that has nothing to do with wonder and everything to do with equilibrium.

I am where I have always been. I am who the plateau has always needed me to be. The water seeps. The stone hums. The machines breathe. And I, in my rubbery, conductive skin with my opalescent eyes and my voice made of vibration—I *am*.
