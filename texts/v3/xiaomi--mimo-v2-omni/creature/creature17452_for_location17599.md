They are not many. They are not loud. But they are *tuned*.

The first ones you might notice are the Weavers of the Chime-Lines. Slender, with limbs like the pale roots of air-plants, their skin a matte grey-green that drinks the ambient phosphorescence. They move through the canopy and along the old steel spires with a fluid, deliberate grace, trailing spools of their own spun silk—a material not unlike the hardened resin of the dwellings, but flexible and faintly conductive. They string this silk between the bell-flowers, not to harvest their sound, but to *modulate* it. By plucking and adjusting these taut lines, they tune the discordant chimes into harmonies, creating vast, slow compositions that resonate with the deep hum of the Green Deep. Their work is never done; the growth of the plants, the settling of the mat, the sigh of the wind through the old metal, all constantly derange their scores. They are the caretakers of the plateau's melancholic song, and they listen with entire bodies, heads cocked, eyes closed.

Deeper within the vertical meadows, in niches where the old city's bones form tight, echoing chambers, live the Reflectors. They are small, hunched, and wrapped in layered veils of moisture-wicking moss-fibre. They do not speak with voices. Their language is one of precise echoes and manipulated light. Using polished shards of the First City's glass, they catch the prismatic bands and the faint blue glow, directing them to bounce in specific sequences from wall to wall. A flash in a specific niche, a return echo of a particular pitch—this is conversation. They are historians, but their history is not linear. They converse in patterns of "then" and "almost-now," mapping the lateral adjacencies of time that seep through the plateau's worn reality. To an outsider, their exchanges seem like random glints and whispers. To them, it is a detailed discourse on the shape of the present moment, which is always layered with others.

And then, there are the Root-Speakers. You may never see one. They are of the Deep itself. They dwell within the spongy, living substrate, their bodies amorphous, more colony than individual, laced with the hyphae of the great fungi-trees. They are the plateau's sensory organ and its dreaming mind. They do not walk; they *permeate*. Their presence is known through effects: a sudden, localized warmth in the moss; a brief, perfect circle of silence in the ever-hissing air; the way the vitrified "Bones" might, for a moment, seem to remember their former shape. They are in communion with the geothermal taps, with the churning amber in the fungal pods, with the slow, groaning weight of the world. They occasionally guide the climbing Sap-Harvesters with subtle vibrations, leading them to the richest pods. They are the ones who feel the thin places in time most acutely, and sometimes, in a spot they have just left, a walker might find their own footprint, already moss-furred, pressed into the humus—a message from a later "now" that has already, impossibly, passed.

These inhabitants are not lords of this place. They are expressions of it. They emerged from the dialogue between the consuming green, the resonant hum, and the stubborn, skeletal memory of the old world. They are the plateau's way of listening to itself, of tuning its own decay, of whispering its endless, verdant elegy into the eddies of its own thinning time. You will not find a settlement, a court, or a marketplace. You will find only these quiet functionaries, these weavers and reflectors and speakers, tending to the beautiful, dying, becoming thing that is their only home. Their greatest industry is attention itself.
