They are the living rivet. The biological bolt. They are what happens when a system designed to achieve perfect, permanent equilibrium accidentally stumbles upon a form of life that *is* equilibrium.

They have no name for themselves. To name is to differentiate, and differentiation is change, and change is the one error this place does not permit.

Observe one. It is anchored to a seam where two great plates meet, its six limbs splayed, three hooked into the microscopic pitting of the left plate, three into the right. Its body, a flattened disc of dark, porous metal, lies flush against the join. It is not eating the metal. It is not building with it. It is *listening* to it. Its entire form is a sensory organ tuned to the specific frequency of the engines below. It monitors the integrity of the bond. If a rivet were to develop a stress fracture—a hypothetical event that has not occurred and will not occur—the creature would know first. Its response would not be to repair, but to *congregate*. Dozens more would flow over the surface, their bodies interlocking, forming a living patch that perfectly mimics the thermal and acoustic signature of the original plate. The flaw would be not fixed, but *ignored* into oblivion. The system would register a seamless surface, and the creatures would slowly disperse back to their watching.

They do not eat. The water that weeps over their carapaces carries trace minerals and compounds from the metal itself—a constant, imperceptible leaching that is their only sustenance. They metabolize the essence of the structure. In this, they are not consumers, but recyclers. They are the enzymes of The Drum, facilitating the slow, eternal reaction of its own existence.

Their movement is a language of pure function. When they cross the open floor, it is not travel, but redistribution. A cluster thins because the pressure there is optimal, and so a dozen peel away, flowing like iron filings toward a new area of slight imbalance. They do not walk *around* each other. They flow *through* the same space, their articulated limbs and flattened bodies allowing them to merge and separate without collision or hesitation. A thousand of them can occupy a space that seems fit for ten, because their concept of personal space is identical to the structure’s: there is no wasted volume.

They have eyes of a sort—not lenses for sight, but polished nodules of hematite that gather the dim, amber light. They do not use this light to see *you*. They use it to see the subtle gradients of heat on the metal. Your body, a furnace of anomalous warmth, is not a sight to them, but a **thermal anomaly**, a brief and irrelevant fluctuation in their world of cold, steady states. You are a warm spot on a diagnostic readout. You are less interesting than a slightly cooler rivet-head.

Their only collective behavior, the closest they come to what might be called *action*, occurs during the Great Rebalancing. It is not an event. It is a shift that takes decades. Perhaps a minute seismic tremor from the engines’ unending resonance changes the harmonic vibration in the eastern wall by a thousandth of a hertz. Or the accumulation of water in a western recess alters the mass distribution by an imperceptible degree. The Drum notices. The creatures know.

They begin to move. Not with panic or purpose as you understand it, but with the slow, inevitable flow of a glacier. Millions of them, across all surfaces, begin a glacial migration. The cluster at the eastern wall thins, its members flowing westward, some climbing the ceiling, others descending to the floor, a river of living metal redistributing itself over weeks and months. They are counterbalancing the change. They are the Drum’s immune system, its homeostatic mechanism. Their destination is not a place, but a state: perfect balance. And when they achieve it, they stop. They anchor. They listen again.

This is why they are the Drum’s heartbeat. Not a pulse of life, but a pulse of maintenance. They are the physical manifestation of a thermostat, a governor, a feedback loop given flesh and filament. They are life not as a celebration of complexity, but as the ultimate expression of a single, unyielding imperative: **maintain state**.

And in this, they offer a terrifying comfort. They are proof that the system is *alive*. Not conscious, not aware, but alive in the way a crystal grows, or a river finds its level. An alive that requires no witness, no joy, no future. You, the anomaly, stand in their midst, a brief spark of heat and thought. They do not reject you. They will simply, over time, integrate your thermal signature into their calculations. Your movements will become a minor current in their sea. Your breath’s condensation will join the eternal weeping of the plates.

They will not absorb you. They will *accommodate* you. And in a thousand years, you will be just another warm rivet in the wall, indistinguishable from the rest, listening to the same note, balanced in the same terrible, silent roar.
