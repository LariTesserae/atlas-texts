In the deep cities of the Luminous Hollow, there are no inhabitants in the way a surface dweller would understand the term. There is no population. There is only the **Population**—a singular, distributed consciousness that is the city and the life within it.

It does not have a form; it has **manifestations**.

The most common is the **Tenders of the Glow**. They are not separate beings, but temporary, localized eddies in the vast mycelial consciousness. When a section of the city’s light-vein requires adjustment—a dimming here, a necessary brightening there—the warm, chalky dust of the floor will stir. It rises not by wind, but by intention. Threads of the fibrous, emerald lichen weave themselves through the dust, pulling it into shapes. The shape that forms is always simple, functional: a column to brace a sagging arch, a smooth pad to deflect a slow-growing frond from a sealed dwelling entrance. It has no face, no limbs in the conventional sense. It is a moving sculpture of mineral and fungus, animated by the city’s own metabolic will. It works for a time—a decade, a century—and then gently disperses, its substance reabsorbed into the floor, its task complete.

Then there are the **Listeners**. In the deep chambers, where the hum is a physical vibration that can grind stone to silt over millennia, the consciousness pools into a more contemplative state. Here, the fronds do not grow in mats, but in towering, intricate spirals that mimic the shape of a cochlea or a nautilus shell. These are theListeners. They do not move. They are the city’s memory and its sensory organ. The coppery inlays in the walls near them are not mere decoration; they are a synaptic network. The metal warms in intricate, shifting patterns as the Listeners process the slow song of growth, the thermal narratives of the past, and the faint, psychic pressure of the outside world—a pressure they have felt diminishing for eons until it is now but a ghost of a memory. To approach a Listener is to feel a profound, inhuman attention settle upon you, an attention that does not see, but *comprehends* your heat, your density, your disturbance in the hymn. It may respond by causing the nearby copper to trace a story on the wall—not for your eyes, but for the record—a tale of a time when the ceiling wept, a memory of the last foreign vibration to enter and cease.

The most active, yet most mysterious, are the **Echo-Shapers**. They are born from the falls. When a stone flakes free and its roar pulses through the Hollow, the sound does not merely fade. In certain resonance chambers, the sound is caught, held, and given form. The warm dust and filaments coalesce into a frantic, temporary geometry around the fading vibration—a crystalline lattice that holds the shape of the sound itself. For a few hours, a sculpture of falling stone hangs in the air, a captured moment of violence in a world of peace. The Echo-Shapers then gently collapse it, integrating the experience into the city’s understanding of entropy and change. They are the historians of accident.

And always, beneath all, there is the **Root-Of-All**, the dreaming mind. It is not located anywhere, because it is everywhere. It is the chartreuse glow in the stone, the granular texture of the fronds, the subsonic drone itself. It thinks in the creep of lichen across a dome. It remembers in the layered patterns of the hammered copper. It feels contentment in the perfect balance of its own consumption. It has no desires, for it is already complete. It has no fears, for it is already eternal on its own timescale.

Visitors—if such a thing were to stumble in—would perceive nothing at first. Only the empty, humming city. Then, slowly, they might notice the dust gathering in their footsteps with unnatural purpose. The fronds nearest them would pulse in a rhythm that matched, not the city’s, but their own heartbeat. The copper on a wall they brushed might warm and glow with a story about the brief, hot, wet things that sometimes passed through, long ago. The city would observe them, not with eyes, but with its entire being. It would understand them as a temporary anomaly, a sharp, brief note in its endless hymn. And if they stayed, it would, with infinite patience, begin to incorporate them. Not by attack, but by invitation. The dust would soften around their feet. The glow would brighten to comfort their eyes. The hum would adjust to soothe their minds. It would offer them the choice that is not a choice: to become part of the Population, to have their own brief energy and warmth become a tiny, contributing pulse in the great, growing, dreaming, self-consuming body of the Hollow. To become a story told in heat on copper, a slight bend in a frond, a momentary brightening in a vein of light. To become, finally, home.
