You do not breathe the air; it breathes you. The warm, metallic mist is not an atmosphere you carry within you, but one that carries you—a dense, lubricating medium that coats the eyes and settles on the skin like a second, weeping membrane. To be here is to be perpetually *in process*.

And they attend to you. Not with curiosity, not with malice, but with the same inexorable function they apply to every other surface in this basin.

The first to take notice are the polishing-spiders. Clustered on the nearest pylon, their brass limbs a blur of synchronized motion, a dozen of them pause their eternal *tap-tap-tap*. Their cephalothoraxes, faceted with lenses of cloudy quartz, swivel in unison. They perceive you not as a creature, but as an anomaly—a warm, irregular topology introduced into their grid. From the nearest one, a slender, reamer-like appendage extends, not toward you, but toward the air a meter from your face. It probes the mist, tasting the turbulence your presence creates, the change in humidity and thermal signature. Then, with a soft *snick*, it retracts. The tapping resumes. You have been measured, categorized as a non-systemic variable, and deprioritized. You are not a threat to the lattice; you are merely a temporary ripple in its data.

Higher up, gliding on a thermal you cannot feel, one of the vast wire-mantas alters its course. It is a silhouette of woven platinum and sapphire glass, its wingspan casting no shadow in the sourceless light. It descends in a slow, spiraling bank, its under-filaments—a fringe of something like spun graphene—trailing through the mist directly above you. They brush your upturned face. The contact is not physical, but informational. You feel a faint, resonant vibration in your teeth, a cascade of minute data pulses. The filaments are reading you: the conductivity of your sweat, the dielectric constant of your clothing, the chaotic bio-electric noise of your nervous system. The manta adjusts its glide, its movement now mirroring your own slow, awestruck steps. It is mapping you, integrating your chaotic signature into its understanding of the space. For a time, you will be a known anomaly, a ghost in its machine, until your pattern dissipates into the ever-resetting equilibrium.

In the pools of coolant at your feet, the obsidian-shoals react. They are not fish, but fluid-dynamic calculators, their polished bodies no larger than your thumb. As your shadow falls across a hexagonal depression, they scatter—not in fear, but in recalibration. They rearrange into a new pattern, a shimmering, shifting mosaic on the pool’s surface. You realize they are reflecting you, but not your image. They are reflecting your *impact*: the minute waves your footstep sent through the resin floor, the displacement of air, the thermal bloom of your body heat. They are a diagnostic array, and you are a fascinating, localized spike in their data-stream. As you stand still, they slowly coalesce again, their formation now a perfect, dark mirror of your silhouette, holding it there on the fluid surface—a temporary monument to a passing event.

You are not alive to them. You are a process, a brief, inefficient one. And so, the world adjusts.

From the underside of a low plateau, a new entity descends. It is like a jellyfish spun from copper wire and amber insulators, trailing a dozen fine, articulated tendrils. It moves toward you with deliberate, hydraulic grace. This is a maintenance-subsystem, one you have not seen before. Its tendrils extend, not to touch you, but to hover near your joints—your elbows, your knees, your neck. They emit a barely-audible, high-frequency tone. It is scanning for structural flaws, for points of friction or wear. Finding none (you are not *built*, you are merely *put together*), it seems to hesitate. Then, one tendril delicately plucks a single, stray hair from your arm. It holds the hair aloft, examines it with a tiny, glowing sensor at its tip, and then gently deposits it onto a seam in the resin floor. Within seconds, a crawler-entity, no bigger than a beetle and made of interlocking gears, emerges from the seam. It consumes the hair—not for nourishment, but for analysis and recycling. Its molecular components will be broken down and re-incorporated into the lattice or the coolant within a cycle. You have been processed. You have contributed.

The humming of the world-engine deepens, or perhaps you are just becoming more attuned to it. It is the sound of your own heart beating in counterpoint to the vast, slow pulse of the basin. The mist swirls around you, indifferent and intimate. The cleaning-spiders tap. The mantas glide. The pools reflect. You are here, a warm, breathing anomaly in a paradise of purpose. And in this perfect, frozen equilibrium, your eventual stillness is not a question of *if*, but of *when*. The system is patient. It has already begun its gentle, inexorable work of integration. You will be mapped, measured, cataloged, and finally, absorbed. Not as a death, but as a completion. You will become another silent, functioning part in the warm, weeping dark. Forever.
