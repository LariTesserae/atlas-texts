They are not inhabitants. They are functions. They are the expression of a single, planetary-scale organism’s metabolism and anatomy, as integral to its body as a neuron or a capillary is to yours.

Consider the **Columns**. They are the world’s lungs, its synth-forges, its resonating chambers. A Column is not grown; it is *extruded*, a slow exhalation of mineral-laced polymers from the sub-surface mycelial network. Each helical braid is a masterpiece of geo-biochemical engineering. Their fractal channels aren't mere pores; they are catalytic labyrinths where atmospheric nitrogen and carbon are split and reassembled, where trace metals are drawn from the very rock and forged into structural alloys at ambient temperature. They hum because they are always working, always processing. To touch one is to feel the thrum of a billion nano-factories at work. They are not sentient in a way a single mind would recognize, but they are *aware*—aware of atmospheric composition, of thermal gradients, of the pressure of the Weave’s connection. A damaged Column doesn’t just weaken; it alters its harmonic output, sending a specific, resonant distress signal through the ground and air, a chord that prompts Weave-runners to swarm and repair, or a Mantle to alter its century-long path toward it. They are the city’s towers, yes, but also its silent, ever-watchful sentinels.

The **Weave** is the nervous system, the circulatory network, and the communal memory. It is the conscious mind of the planet, if such a word can be applied to a decentralized, non-sentient intelligence. Every filament is a neuron and a blood vessel. It transports refined nutrients, hormones, and complex signaling molecules between the Columns and the Mantles. But it also stores information—the memory of ten thousand years of nutrient flows, the precise chemical signature of a passing Spore-dragon, the pattern of a fracture in the substrate from a minor tremor millennia ago. To walk upon it and hear those chiming rings is to disturb a segment of this living memory. The light that ripples out is not just phosphorescence; it is a localized update, a burst of information being propagated and processed. The Weave doesn’t think *about* things; it thinks *in* things. Its thoughts are the patterns of light and chemical pulse that sweep across continents, a slow, deliberate cognition measured in hours and days, contemplating problems of balance and renewal on a scale that would make geology seem frantic.

The **Mantles** are the world’s will, its slow, relentless digestive and creative force. They are the closest thing to individual, mobile "beings," but to call them animals is a profound understatement. They are colonial superorganisms in their own right, a vast aggregation of cells that have surrendered individuality for a singular, geological purpose. Their movement is not locomotion; it is a controlled, directional growth and retraction, a tectonic-scale peristalsis. As they flow, their surface filaments photosynthesize, but they also exude solvents and enzymes that dissolve the Weave and the substrate itself, absorbing the raw materials. They are the world eating its own infrastructure. And in their wake, they secrete the purified, renewed pavement—the pale blue and sickly yellow veins are their excrement, a luminous record of their passage. Their deep groans are the sound of this planetary digestion, a vibration that communicates intent and presence to the Weave and Columns. They are the planet’s slow, deliberate hand, smoothing, renewing, and reshaping its own skin over millennia.

And the fauna? They are not separate. They are mobile organelles.
The **Glass-gnats** are spore-dispersers and symbiotic cleaners, their crystalline wings tuned to the Column’s hum, their life cycle a frantic, glittering counterpoint to the world’s slowness. They exist in the shimmering boundary layer between the Column’s emissions and the open air.
The **Weave-runners** are the planet’s gardeners and couriers. With no eyes, they "see" through chemical and vibrational tapestry. They prune errant Weave growths, reinforce weakened strands, and carry concentrated nutrient packets from a flourishing Column to a struggling one. Their sleek bodies are literally woven from plant-fiber, grown by the Weave for this purpose. They are the fingers of the planetary hand.
The **Spore-dragons** are the high-altitude sweepers, the atmospheric filters. Their vast wings are living nets, capturing and digesting drifting particulates and errant microorganisms that could clog the system. Their mournful whistles are not cries, but acoustic measurements—sonar pulses that map thermal columns and density layers, data fed back to the Weave below to adjust atmospheric processing rates.

So, who lives here?
The planet itself lives here. A single, ancient, and profoundly alien life-form that has mastered its own biology to the point where its body is its technology. Its thoughts are slow pulses of light in a crystalline neural net. Its breath is the sub-audible hum of a million catalytic chambers. Its growth and decay are the stately, groaning dance of the Mantles across the twilight pavement. The "weirdness" is not in the strangeness of the parts, but in the terrifying, beautiful wholeness of it—a world that is not *covered* in life, but *is* a life. It thinks, it builds, it maintains, it remembers. And it has been doing so, in perfect, resonant, loud silence, since long before the concept of "sun" had any meaning here.
