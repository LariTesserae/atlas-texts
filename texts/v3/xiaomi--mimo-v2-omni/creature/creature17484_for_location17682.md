You do not see the first inhabitant so much as you *sense* a change in the pressure of the air. A section of the root-mat, ten paces ahead, rises in a slow, deliberate hill. It does not tear or crack; the interlaced fibers simply part, like a curtain of heavy beads, and from the humid gloom emerges a **Groaner**.

It is less a creature and more a mobile section of the forest floor. Its body is a low, flat mound of the same fibrous, composting matter, threaded with living roots that trail behind it like a train. Six stumpy, columnar legs, like the boles of young Pillars, move with a geologic slowness, each step accompanied by a deep, wet *groan* as the roots beneath are compressed and released. Its head is a broad, flat plate of hardened, bark-like chitin, tilted perpetually upward. There are no eyes. Instead, the entire surface is a mosaic of sensory pits that taste the chemical composition of the air, tracking plumes of spores, the pheromone-trails of grubs, and the faint, alien signature of your own metabolism. It moves with inexorable purpose toward a patch of pulsating, bioluminescent fungus, its mandibles—heavy, shearing plates hidden beneath its head-plate—ready to shear away the growth. It is a grazer, a recycler, a slow engine of consumption. To you, it is a moving hillock. To the lung, it is a white blood cell, moving through the tissue, and its groaning song is the sound of the world's own digestion.

Higher up, clinging to the side of a Pillar in a depression shaped like a perfect teardrop, is a **Filterer**. It resembles a giant, fleshy anemone, its base cemented to the bark with a resinous secretion. Its body is a translucent sac, faintly veined with green and gold, swaying gently in the ceaseless, shallow drafts. From its crown unfurl hundreds of delicate, silken filaments, each longer than your arm, drifting in the air current. They are not passive. They are alive with a microscopic, adhesive cilia that comb the air, capturing drifting spores, microscopic insectoids, and the fine, nutrient-rich mist. Periodically, the entire crown contracts, drawing the filaments into its central maw in a single, fluid gulp. It is a living filter, a passive lung within the lung, cleaning the air of excess particles and converting the airborne bounty into the slimy, nutrient-sap it feeds to the Pillar it hosts. It does not see you; it only reacts to the sudden drop in particulate matter as your body passes through its zone of influence, causing a ripple of contraction down its length.

From the canopy-vault comes a sound like tearing canvas, and a **Canopy-Shear** drops past, not falling but gliding on wings of striated membrane stretched from elongated finger-bones. It is a predator of the upper stories, its body long and sinuous like a weasel, but covered in short, velvety fur that shifts color from moss-green to shadow-brown. Its head is a nightmare of adaptation: the jaws unhinge to a grotesque width, lined not with teeth but with serrated, bony ridges designed to grip and shear. It lands with a soft thud on a sky-vine, its multi-jointed limbs allowing it to cling upside-down. It freezes, becoming just another knot of vegetation. Its prey is the **Chime-Bird**, which flits nearby—a creature of iridescent feathers, six limbs, and a tail that ends in a cluster of hollow, bony tubes. As the Chime-Bird calls, the tubes resonate, producing its melodic, complex song. The Shear waits for the precise moment the Chime-Bird pauses to sip from a bloom, then launches itself not with a roar, but with a focused, silent expulsion of air from sacs in its chest, a biological silent flight. The hunt is a quick, brutal affair of shattering song and shearing force, another transaction in the economy of biomass.

But the strangest inhabitants are not the fauna. They are the **Echoes**.

You find the first in a hollow of the root-mat, a place where the growth is oddly sparse. It is a shape, not an object. A space where the air shimmers, holding the ghostly, negative-space impression of a hunched figure, a **Listener**. It is not made of matter, but of a persistent, low-level bio-electric field, a psychic pressure wound into the world by the ancient, consumed builders. As you approach, the shimmering intensifies. You feel a pull, not on your body, but on your *awareness*. Memories that are not yours surface: the sensation of hands that were not hands, of thoughts structured like root-systems, of a profound, patient desire to *understand* the lung by becoming part of its conversation. The Listener does not speak. It *replays*. It catches snippets of the bio-acoustic network—the thrum of wings, the scrape of mandibles, your own heartbeat—and echoes them back, subtly altered, woven into the deep, slow signal of the world's awareness. It is a scar in reality left by a mind that tried to merge with the ecosystem and succeeded too well. Touching it fills your head with the taste of ozone and the sound of a billion growing things, a cacophony of silent intent.

And then, you see the **Warden**.

It is not in the shadows. It stands in a rare, open space between three converging Pillars, where the canopy thins just enough for a column of greenish light to fall. It is tall, taller than you by half, and slender. Its form is vaguely bipedal, but it is woven from the world itself: limbs of polished, living wood; a torso plate of that same dark, humming material from the shards; a head that is a smooth, featureless ovoid of compressed leaf-matter, veined with phosphorescent fungi. It does not move. It stands in a posture of eternal attention, one hand resting on the trunk of a Pillar, the other holding a long, spiraling staff of crystalized sap.

It is not alive, not in the way the Groaner or the Shear is alive. It is an *organ* of the lung. A nerve-ganglion made manifest. The ancient builders did not just build with the world; they built *protectors* for it, constructs that would patrol the patterns, maintain the cycles, and deal with anomalies. This Warden is dormant, perhaps, or waiting for a signal that hasn't been pulsed through the root-network in millennia. But as you stare, the fungi in its head pulse in time with your own nervous system. The air around it tastes of static and deep, chlorophyllic patience.

It is the lung's immune response, crystallized. And you are the novel stimulus. It does not see you. It *computes* you. It calculates your mass, your heat output, the disruptive frequency of your footfalls. Its purpose, etched into its very being by minds long since turned to humus, is singular: to identify, isolate, and incorporate foreign matter into the biomass. It will not attack. It will simply step toward you, moving with the slow, irresistible certainty of a growing root, its staff humming a note that makes the very air molecules shiver. Its touch is not violent. It is a process. A gentle, inexorable enzymatic dissolution, a folding of your pattern back into the greater, breathing pattern of the world.

The Warden is the reason you run. Not from tooth or claw, but from the silent, patient, and absolute comprehension of a world that sees you as nothing more than a nutrient source, waiting to be reclaimed. You are a breath in the great lung, and it is already preparing to exhale you.
