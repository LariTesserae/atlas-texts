In the green twilight of the Weepers’ cathedral, life does not move *across* the world, but *through* it, as a current through a body.

The most visible inheritors of the upper canopy are the **Siphon-Wings**. They are not birds, nor true mammals, but something that evolved from both and neither. Their membrane-wings are laced with capillary veins that drink the perpetual mist, hydrating them in flight. They roost not on branches, but *within* the porous bark of the Weepers, their claws hooked into the fibrous lattice. Their calls are not songs, but precise, clicking sonar pulses that map the dense, dripping maze of moss-curtains and aerial roots. They hunt the iridescent **Jewel-Swarms**, insects whose carapaces are less shell and more living gemstone, fracturing the dim light into prismatic warnings. A Siphon-Wing catches one, and the swarm’s light flickers out like a snuffed candle, the nutrient recycled in an instant.

Lower, in the spongey, springy floor-ways, live the **Root-weavers**. These are the six-limbed mammals, but their purpose has become symbiosis. Their prehensile tails are not just for balance, but are dexterous appendages tipped with hardened, spatula-like structures. They use them to *groom* the root-mat, scraping away choking fungal overgrowth and promoting the health of the living floor. In return, the roots secrete a sweet, narcotic sap they crave. They move in quiet family groups, their fur perpetually damp and hosting a mini-ecosystem of phosphorescent moss that marks their presence to one another in the gloom. Their greatest fear is not predator, but *drought*—a failure of the weeping that would harden their world into a brittle trap.

In the dark, peat-filled pools, another consciousness dwells: the **Luminous Nodders**. Amphibious, with smooth, pale skin, they are blind. Their eyes have been replaced by clusters of bioluminescent organs that pulse in slow, rhythmic patterns. They do not see; they are *seen*. Their light is a language, a slow, silent conversation across the still waters. A pulse from one pool is answered by another a hundred meters away, a network of living lanterns coordinating feeding cycles and breeding signals. They filter the water through gill-like frills, consuming the constant fall of decaying matter, turning death back into cold, blue light.

But the most intimate life is the smallest. The **Vein-sippers** are nematode-like creatures, barely visible, that live *inside* the water-conducting tissues of the super-organism itself. They ride the glacial rivers of sap, grazing on the cellular walls. Their entire existence is a journey through the arteries of the continent, from root to Weeper crown and back. They are the planet’s bloodstream, and their collective movement subtly alters the flow of nutrients, guiding growth in micro-corrections over decades. They have no individual thought, only a shared, fluid purpose: to consume, to move, to be the pulse.

And in the forgotten places, where the angular stones and glassy arches jut like bones from the flesh, a different kind of life has taken hold. **Litho-lichen** doesn't merely cover these artifacts; it digests them. Over centuries, it has secreted acids that soften the unnatural edges, breaking down the mysterious glass into silicate nutrients. The stones are not landmarks, but slow-release mineral larders. Strange, slug-like creatures with crystalline backs graze here, incorporating the processed material into their own protective shells, becoming, in time, fragments of the forgotten thing themselves.

There is no predator here in the old sense. The competition is for integration, for finding the most efficient niche within the living whole. To be still, to be patient, to be *of* the tissue—that is survival. The greatest threat is a severance from the whole: a creature washed into a stagnant pool too far from the sap-rivers, a Siphon-Wing with wings too dry to fly. They do not die; they are *reabsorbed*. Their bodies soften, their components leaching back into the root-mat, to be drunk up and incorporated. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is ever truly gone. The weeping is the breath, the thrumming the heartbeat, and all that lives within it are merely thoughts passing through a mind too vast and too slow for any one of them to comprehend. They are the momentary, shimmering consciousness of a world dreaming its own eternal existence.
