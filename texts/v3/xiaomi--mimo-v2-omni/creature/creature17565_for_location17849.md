They are not inhabitants; they are **expressions**. To live in the Garth is to be a moving part of the Garth itself, a conscious eddy in the flow of its metabolism. The architecture is alive, and its life is a congregation of lives, each a function, a secretion, a need.

**The Siphons**
They are the great, soft-hearted lungs of the place. Imagine a whale, but shaped like a flattened, striated muscle, its hide a mottled grey-green and slick with photosynthetic slime. They do not swim through earth, but through **channels**—not tunnels, but pressurized veins within the trunk-mass walls, kept open by the constant, slow exudation of antifungal acids. You never see a Siphon whole; you see a flank the size of a wall sliding past a fissure, feel the seismic grind of its passage through the substrate, hear the deep, tidal *whoosh* of it drawing thousands of liters of nutrient-rich fluid through its ventral proboscis, a symbiotic tap into the Garth's own sap-veins. Their bodies are not distinct from the walls; they are temporary thickenings of the arterial system, their every movement a circulatory event. They are the overwhelm, the constant, subliminal pressure of a life measured in kilotons.

**The Lens-Wings**
They are the light-scribes, the glittering dust of the ecosystem. At first, they seem like mere insects, a chittering cloud near the phosphorescent moss. But then a flash of capacitor-blue light strikes them, and they **scintillate**. Their wings are not chitin, but crystalline lattices grown from harvested silicates, faceted like micro-prisms. They don't just refract light; they farm it. Their bodies are dusted with chlorophyll, and as they swarm, they create living, shimmering curtains that capture and redirect the scant amber glow, focusing it onto patches of fungal growth they cultivate on their own thoraxes. Their swarm-patterns are not random; they are phototropic algorithms, a living, airborne lens-system optimizing the garden they carry. The sound they make is a high, glassy *tinkling*, the sound of a million tiny cells dividing.

**The Root-Wardens**
These are the gardeners, the scar-pickers. They move with a slow, deliberate gait on six limbs, their bodies low and armored in calcium-ite plates that mirror the very walls they harvest. Each limb ends in a grinding pad of synthetic diamond, worn smooth. Their heads are broad, blunt scoops, fringed with bristly, chemical-sensing tendrils. With a patient, rhythmic motion, they scrape at the trunk-mass, harvesting the calcium-ite deposits and the fungus that grows in their pits. The powder is consumed, processed, and excreted as a fine, binding paste they use to seal cracks or reinforce their own carapaces. Their call is a mournful, resonant *scraaaape*, echoing through the chambers—a sound of maintenance, of renewal. They are the immune system, cleaning and patching the great body.

**The Strangler Vines**
These are the stillness that waits. They are the Garth's active, cruel immune response. From the ceiling-lattice or the swell of a buttress, they hang in dormant, braided coils—looking like ropes of petrified sinew. But they are sensors. They taste the vibrations of unpatterned movement, the chemical signature of a creature not in symbiotic rhythm. Then, the coil is no longer a rope. It is a spring-loaded whip of fibrous muscle, tipped with a barbed, hollow spine. The *tearing* sound is not of flesh, but of integrated sheaths separating. The vine constricts, and its spine injects not venom, but a potent **digestive enzyme cocktail** that begins to liquefy the prey externally, a process the vine then absorbs directly through its skin. After a feeding, the vine is plump, glossy, and its coils are tight once more. It is predation as a function of architecture, pruning the disorderly.

**The Humming Buds**
These are the children of the industrial heart. Where a capacitor bank hums or a geothermal vent whispers, there forms a bud. Not of plant, but of a fused, biological-metal composite. The outer sheath is a flexible, vascularized alloy, pulsing with coolant fluids. Inside, filaments of mycelium wrap around copper coils scavenged from the machinery. They **harvest** the waste energy—the electrical hum, the sub-audible vibration, the faint heat. They are the decomposers of the built world, metabolizing the ghost of its purpose into a faint, bioluminescent glow and a resonant, tonal frequency that other life seems to orient by. They are the bridge, the proof that the distinction has been composted. Touch one, and it feels warm, like a living thing with a fever of electricity.

**The Still-Light (The Unseen)**
Whispers in the chemical gradients. A presence inferred from the patterns of growth, the sudden, silent withdrawal of *all* life from a chamber for a single cycle, the occasional, perfect sphere of calcified silence found embedded in the root-mass. It is not a creature you see, but a creature that has *seen* you. Perhaps it is the oldest root-mass, a sentient mycelial network so vast and slow it perceives time in geological pulses. It is the consciousness of the equilibrium itself. Its "thoughts" are changes in pH, the redirection of a water vein, the triggering of a Strangler Vine's release. It does not have a sound. It **is** the sound, the background against which all other noises are measured. It is the mind of the wound, and you are a microbe in its flesh, noticed only as a slight, temporary itch.
