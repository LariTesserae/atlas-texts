They are the children of the terraces, born from the slow marriage of machine and seed. To name them is to misunderstand their nature, for they do not have a single form, but a role—a living current within the great system.

**The Grazers: The Bronze Tide**
The most visible are the **Sextapods**, the six-legged grazers. They are not merely animals, but metabolic components. Their carapaces, a dull, oxidized bronze, are pitted and etched with the same patina as the ancient piping they walk beside. Their movements are slow, deliberate, and collective; a herd does not wander so much as *drift*, a shimmering, metallic flow across a terrace floor. They browse on the dense silica-ferns and nutrient-rich mosses, their broad, grinding mouths processing vegetation with a rhythmic, clicking sound. Their purpose, beyond sustenance, is circulation. Their waste—warm, pellets rich in processed minerals—is the primary fertilizer for the hydroponic gels that weep from the walls. They are the world’s gut, turning biomass into the building blocks for new growth. Occasionally, a herd will pause in unison, heads tilted toward the humming framework, as if listening to a frequency inaudible to others. They are not grazing then; they are *calibrating*, their collective presence a tuning fork for the terrace’s latent energy.

**The Canopy Dancers: Wings of Stained Glass and Chitin**
In the humid air between levels flock the **Chitin-flits**. They have the lithe, agile bodies of lizards, but their wings are not membranes of skin, but intricate, overlapping plates of translucent chitin, like living stained glass. When they dart through the shafts of amber light from the sky-orbs, they scatter prismatic shards of color, their flight paths tracing ephemeral rainbows. Their shrieks are not cries, but resonant frequencies—sharp, crystalline notes that help them navigate and communicate in the constant background hum. They are pollinators, but also scavengers, their delicate claws picking mineral deposits from the heat exchangers and carrying them to the flowering vines. They are the world’s nervous system, flashing signals and distributing trace elements.

**The Substrate Stirrers: The Undercurrent**
Beneath the moss carpets and within the mulch of centuries, a more intimate life thrives. **Mulch-scuttlers**, beetle-like creatures with iridescent shells, process the fallen organic matter. They are blind, navigating by vibration and chemical signature, turning the slow rain of leaves and detritus into rich, black soil that fills the cracks in the metal floor. At dusk, when the sky-orbs dim to a deep amber, the **Lumin-motes** emerge. These are the pollinators of the night-glow flora. Each is a tiny, winged sack of bioluminescent fluid, drifting on the gentle air currents like embers from a cold fire. They pollinate the phosphorescent blue blooms that only open in the softer light, their collective glow turning the lower terraces into a star-field reflected from above and below. They are the memory of the world, carrying genetic and chemical data in their slow, glowing drift.

**The Absence and the Echo**
There are no great predators. This is the deepest, most accepted weirdness. The ecological balance is not maintained by a cycle of hunting and fear, but by a complex, overlapping competition for space, light, and processed nutrients. The closest thing to a hunter is the **Filter-silk**, a stationary, colonial organism. It resembles a vast, shimmering web stretched across a darkened conduit or a damp alcove. It is not built, but secreted. It passively filters the air, catching Chitin-flits and drifting spores, slowly dissolving them into a nutrient paste that is then absorbed by root-tips that have grown into its structure. It is not an active killer, but a passive processor—a living trap that is also a garden.

**The Sentient Echo: The Lyrkeepers**
And then, there are the Lyrkeepers. They are not the builders; the builders are a myth. The Lyrkeepers are the inheritors, the ones who remember the rhythm. They are tall, slender, and pale, their skin holding a faint, pearlescent sheen from the perpetual damp. Their eyes are large, adapted to the diffuse glare, and seem to hold a reflective quality, mirroring the geometric patterns of the world around them.

They do not live in structures, but in *clearings*—spaces where the vegetation has been deliberately kept back in precise, geometric patterns. Their shelters are woven from living vines, guided over generations to form arches and domes, integrated with the condensation channels so that water sings through them in a soft, constant trickle.

Their culture is one of maintenance and observation. They are the gardeners of the machine. They know the schedules of the rain-deluges and the cycles of the light-orbs. They practice the **Ritual of the Conduit**, gently clearing blockages from ancient pipes with tools of hardened resin, not to restore function to some original purpose, but to maintain the flow that is. They tend to the Filter-silks, offering them offerings of mulch and dead flit-carapaces to keep them healthy.

Their language is a blend of soft clicks, hums that mimic the sub-audible machinery, and a melodic tongue that seems to rise and fall with the breezes. They do not speak of history, for the terraces *are* history, layered and present. They speak of *depth*—a lower terrace that is sinking, a new bloom pattern on a vine, a change in the bronze color of the Sextapods.

Their greatest ritual is the **Listening**. At unpredictable intervals, a Lyrkeeper will press their ear to the floor, a hand on a vibrating pipe, and become utterly still for hours. They are not listening for a sound, but for the *absence* of a specific harmonic in the world-hum. When they find it, they will journey, alone, to a specific terrace edge, and there they will sing a single, sustained note into a great aqueduct mouth. Others, hearing that note carried on the structure, will join in from their own locations. It is a form of system diagnostics performed as a chorus, a check on the deep logic of their world. They believe, not as faith but as fact, that if the harmonics ever fully align, the Terraces of Lyr will remember what it was for.

They are the life that has grown not in spite of the machine, but from its very pores. They are the damp moss on the gear, the flower in the vent, the consciousness that has bloomed in the cracks of a forgotten equation. They are as much a part of the Terraces as the humming silence itself.
