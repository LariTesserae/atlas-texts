The inhabitants of the Grove-City are not masters, nor mere residents. They are **Tenders**.

They move with a quiet, practiced economy, their bare feet knowing the subtle differences between spongy moss, springy root-mat, and the firmer, polished grain of a walkway-bough. Their clothing is not woven cloth, but **felted**—layers of condensed plant fiber, lichen, and fungal silk, dyed in the soft greys, greens, and browns of the Grove itself. It is seamless, growing with and occasionally being shed back into the system. Their skin carries the permanent, cool kiss of moisture, and their hands are often stained with the ochre of nutrient-rich sap or the dark loam of the catchment basins.

They are not a numerous people. Each Tender is a unique graft upon the city's body, their role dictated not by law, but by innate affinity and a lifetime of learning the city's slow language.

**The Menders** are the closest thing to architects. They do not build, but **guide**. With tools of polished bone and living vine, they perform minute, deliberate manipulations on the growing structures—a gentle binding here to direct a branch's curve, the careful introduction of a specific mycelial strain there to strengthen a junction. Their work is measured in seasons, not hours. They are often seen in the higher canopy, their forms silhouetted against the diffused light, listening to the creaks and whispers of the wood with an intensity that borders on prayer.

**The Listeners** are the ecologists and historians. They spend long hours in silent communion at the bases of the great arterial trunks, their ears pressed to the living bark, interpreting the rhythmic *thrum-pulse* of the fluid within. They track the health of the system not by charts, but by the pitch of the capillary gurgle in the root-weaves or the scent of the ozone exuded from deep within the heartwood. They are the ones who can predict a shift in the water flow or sense a coming period of growth or rest. Their knowledge is not written, but hummed in low tones that resonate with the city's own frequencies.

**The Gatherers** move through the grove in soft-footed silence. They harvest with profound respect and precision: a precise cluster of nutrient-dense fungus from a symbiotic node, a specific quantity of clear water from a catchment basin, a handful of pale, sweet sap from a tapped bough. They never take more than the system can replenish in a single cycle of light and mist. Their collections are stored not in pots, but in the hollow dwelling-trees, where the constant temperature and humidity act as a perfect, living larder.

Their communication is largely non-verbal—a system of soft clicks of the tongue, subtle hand gestures, and shared, focused attention. When they do speak, their voices are low, carrying no further than the whisper of air through the canopy. The content of their speech is often indistinguishable from the environment: a comment on the day's mist, the strength of the root-mat, the progress of a new growth. To an outsider, it would sound as though the forest itself were murmuring.

There is no conflict, for there is no scarcity in the balanced sense. There is no ambition for change, for the form is already perfected. Their "work" is a form of devotional maintenance. Their leisure is indistinguishable from their labor: sitting in the damp, fragrant air, observing the minute adjustments of the canopy; tracing the flow of water with a fingertip; feeling for the pulse in a living wall.

They are **quiet** beings, their inner landscape as calm and pearlescent as the air around them. Their emotions run deep and slow, like sap. Joy is a moment of perfect alignment, felt as a harmonic resonance through the soles of the feet. Sorrow is a sense of imbalance—a stalled pulse in an artery, a patch of lichen turning the wrong shade—that is met not with outcry, but with patient, corrective tending.

They are born from the loam and the loam awaits them. When a Tender feels their own rhythms slowing beyond recovery, they are led by the others to a chosen place—a soft, damp hollow at the base of a great dwelling-tree. They lie down, and over days, are gently incorporated. Mycelial threads weave through them, roots embrace them, and they become part of the nutrient flow, their memories and their matter feeding the very city they tended. Their identity dissolves into the collective, patient logic of the Grove.

To watch a Tender at work is to witness a form of love so deep it has become indistinguishable from function. They are the consciousness of the Grove-City, the nerve-endings of its vast, vegetative mind, living out a quiet, symbiotic covenant with the living architecture that is their home, their temple, and their eventual tomb. They are not surviving; they are **participating** in a life that has transcended the need for struggle.
