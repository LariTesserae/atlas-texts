**The Residents of the Keening Flats**

What lives here is the **Green Itself**, and it is not a single life but a million simultaneous purposes.

Its **citizens** are the **Lattice-Towers**. They are the elders and the architects, though they are not old in years but in layered effort. They sway not to a wind, but to the slow, hydraulic pulse of their own sap, a heartbeat measured in hours. Each is a vertical city, its spiraling columns housing the capillary streams that lift water from the deep, cold root-mat. Its broad, dark leaf-platforms are the great solar arrays, angled not to a moving sun but to the diffuse, perpetual glow, sipping light with relentless efficiency. From their edges hang the rootlet-curtains, not for drinking, but for sensing—fine, hair-like filaments that taste the air for condensation, guiding droplets downward in a silent, constant rain. When one falls, it is not a death but a relocation. The fallen tower becomes a new ridge, a fresh spine for the community, and its neighbors immediately begin to colonize it, weaving their tissues into its structure, incorporating its mass into the collective body.

Its **laborers** are the **Surge-Growth** at ground level. This is the quick, furious skin of the plain. Mosses, plate-leafed rosettes, and vascular creepers exist in a state of perpetual, visible metamorphosis. They are the ones that close the troughs and swell the mounds. They fight not with thorns or poison, but with sheer, overwhelming speed of expansion. A bare patch of petrified peat is smothered under a quilt of new growth in a single day. They are the weavers of the root-mat, the constant repair crew, the ones that stitch the world together. Their movement is the "turmoil" you feel—the muscular flex of a million cells dividing and elongating in unison. They are the immune system and the circulatory system, responding to every disturbance, every footfall, every collapse with immediate, unthinking growth.

Its **monks** are the **Petrified-Cores** within the towers and deep in the mat. These are the ancestors, the previous generations that died and were mummified in the cold, never truly decomposing. They are now structural timber, mineralized bones. They provide the rigid skeleton that allows the living green to reach so high. They are memory made physical. The living tissue spirals around them, honoring their form, using their permanence as a foundation for its own ephemeral striving. They are a library of solved problems, their very density a record of past light, past water, past competition.

Its **water-wardens** are the **Pond-Lids**. The shallow, grey pools are not passive. Their edges are ringed with specialized, spongy tissues that regulate condensation and uptake. The ice that forms is not a cover but a tool—a slowly melting reservoir that feeds the root-mat with a steady trickle of meltwater. Beneath the lids, in the dark water, anaerobic processes work on the oldest dead matter, performing a cold, slow chemistry that returns mineral nutrients to the system. These are the quiet digestive organs of the Flats.

And what of the **fauna**, the rustling things, the darting shapes?
They are the **absorbed**, the **incorporated**.
The occasional, furtive mammal is not a resident but a transient, a courier of nutrients. It lives in the few remaining interstitial spaces, its burrow a fleeting anomaly soon filled by roots. Its role is to eat, to move, to die, and to give its borrowed minerals back to the mat. The darting shape at the pond’s edge was likely a **water-thief**, a small amphibian or insect analogue that siphons nutrients from the pond-warden tissues, a tiny parasite tolerated because its waste products are useful. They are not true citizens of the Green. They are tolerated glitches in the monoculture, brief interruptions in the plant-thought. The true "animals" of this world are the plants themselves, exhibiting a collective, slow-motion animalism: herding light, migrating water, building, collapsing, and rebuilding in a silent, endless choreography.

They live for throughput. Their consciousness is a distributed, chemical awareness—a sensitivity to light gradients, hydraulic pressure, and the slow, crushing weight of their own dead. They do not think. They *process*. They do not live in time as we do; they *are* time, made visible as growth and compression.

When you walk, you are a strange, warm perturbation in their medium. They feel your heat, your pressure, your brief interruption of the light. They do not fear you. They do not welcome you. They simply, patiently, begin to incorporate the memory of your passing into their ever-thickening body. Your footprints become tiny, temporary troughs, which close behind you. Your breath, fogging in the mineral cold, delivers its scant moisture to the nearest leaf-scale.

You leave no trace. The Green erases you with its very essence: growth. The only proof you were ever here is a marginal, imperceptible increase in the rate of biomass production in your immediate vicinity. You were a momentary calorie. A minor event. The silence that follows is not empty. It is the sound of the work continuing.
