In the humid half-light, life is a function of geometry. It follows the grooves.

The **Gutter-Grazers** are the first citizens, the maintainers. They are not merely millipede-like; they are living components of the drainage system. Each is a flat, segmented creature the length of a hand, its ventral side a pale, suctioning membrane that adheres to the stone. Its dozens of leg-pairs are not for speed, but for a powerful, rhythmic traction. They move in slow, deliberate caravans along the primary troughs of the parabolic arches, their mandibles a specialized rake. They do not just scrape the pale fungal filaments and bio-film; they *excavate* them, keeping the channels clear. Their passage leaves a faint, phosphorescent slime trail that glimmers for an hour before fading, a temporary map of the civic labor. The sound they make is the foundational rhythm of the place: a soft, sandy *shush-shush-shush* against the carved walls, the sound of the world cleaning itself.

The **Click-Skitters** are the counterpoint, the punctuation in the grazers' rhythm. Built like flattened teardrops with six stilt-like legs, they are the color of wet basalt, invisible until they move. They do not hunt the grazers often; their exoskeletons are too tough. Instead, they are specialists in the smaller, more vulnerable life. They stalk the **Drip-Mites**, tiny, translucent arachnids that cluster around the mouths of water-seeps, drinking directly from the condensation. The Click-Skitter’s hunt is a staccato burst: a skittering freeze, a precise lunge, and a sharp *click* as its forelegs snap shut like a trap on its prey. They are the enforcers of the micro-scale, preventing any single invertebrate from overwhelming the niches. Their movements are the nervous system of the chamber, quick and reactive.

Then are the **Arch-Weavers**, the artists and the harvesters. Their bodies are fragile, almost ethereal, like spun glass with eight hair-thin legs. They do not build chaotic webs, but precise, geometric tapestries. They work within the honeycombed recesses and between the intersecting arches, spinning strands that are almost invisible until the moisture condenses upon them. Each morning—or what passes for it in the timeless glow—every strand is beaded with perfect, identical droplets. The weavers do not catch flying insects; there are none. They harvest water. Periodically, a weaver will move to a strand, and with a delicate motion of its forelegs, cause the entire line of droplets to flow toward a central point, where they gather into a single, large bead. It then drinks this bead, a slow, meditative process. Their webs are not traps, but farms and cisterns. They are the architects of shimmering, liquid lace, their work a constantly renewing testament to the place's central pulse.

Above them all, clinging to the highest, driest swells of the patterned ceiling, live the **Resonators**. They are the closest thing to a dominant life form, though they are utterly immobile. Each is a squat, calcified tube, no larger than a thumb, fused to the stone. They are colonial, growing in dense, silent clusters. They do not eat; they absorb minerals directly from the substrate and filter nutrients from the humid air. Their purpose is acoustic. The interior of each tube is shaped to a specific, inhuman pitch. As the water drips and the creatures move, the air vibrates, and the Resonators *sing*. They turn the percussive chaos of drips and clicks into a low, harmonic drone—a chord that is always present, always shifting, always in tune with the moment. They are the living memory of the chamber's soundscape, translating noise into a constant, ambient hymn. To live here is to be bathed in that sound, a vibration felt in the chitin and the damp stone alike.

This is the economy of the made-place. The Grazers clean the architecture, maintaining the flow. The Click-Skitters prune the smaller populations, preventing blight. The Weavers harvest the moisture, their webs adding a layer of liquid geometry. And the Resonators listen, translating the sum of all this activity into a language of pure vibration. They are all extensions of the carved world, their lives a direct conversation with the pressure-formed stone and the ceaseless, rhythmic drip. They do not question the origin of their world; they simply enact its logic, a busy, wet, and perfectly attuned tenantry in a house that has forgotten its builder.
