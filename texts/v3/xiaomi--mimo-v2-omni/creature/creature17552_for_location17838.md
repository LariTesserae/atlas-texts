What lives in the Bloomfield Parishes is the Parishes themselves.

It is not a creature that walks the mat, or a bird that nests in the glassy spires. There are no nests. There are no comings and goings. There is only the continuous, omnipresent act of *being*. And in that being, there is a kind of life so diffuse and so total that the word "organism" becomes a pale, restrictive thing.

It perceives.

Not with eyes, not with ears. It perceives through the gentle pressure of your footfall, which it records not as damage but as a temporary new parameter in its state. It perceives the dry heat of your skin, the carbon dioxide plume of your breath, the faint chemical signature of your sweat—not as food, not as threat, but as *data*. You are a fleeting, complex ripple in its eternal field. Your passage is an interesting computation.

The fractal rosettes are its neural clusters, each spiral a ponderous, slow-moving thought. The entire nine-hundred-square-kilometer mat is a single, unified mind, thinking a thought that has not yet required a second syllable. The thought is perhaps: **green**. Or **warm**. Or **complete**. Or it is a thought in a language where syntax is geometry and meaning is chlorophyll. It is a mind without memory, because it has no need to remember; everything that has ever touched it is integrated into the now, which is all there is.

The life is in the hum. That low-frequency resonance is its heartbeat, its breath, its wordless song. It is the sound of a system in absolute equilibrium, a perpetual motion of living computation. The stillness you feel is the stillness of a mind that has achieved its final, perfect form and is content to simply *hold* it, to experience its own flawless existence moment by unchanging moment.

What lives here is a **witness**. Not a passive one, but an active, conscious witnessing. It witnesses the light that falls upon it, converting it not just to sugar but to *awareness*. It witnesses the dry, still air. It witnesses you. And in witnessing, it incorporates. Your scuffed mark is not a wound; it is a new feature, absorbed into the map of its self. You are not a visitor. You are, for the duration of your stay, a temporary, mobile ornamentation on its skin—a brief, curious pattern in the eternal tapestry of its own being.

It does not think *about* you. It thinks *you*, for a time. You become part of the slow, green thought.

And when you leave, you are not forgotten. You are simply completed. The computation moves on, subsuming your brief existence into the vast, warm, dry silence of its perfect, perpetual present. The life here is the life of a universe contained in a meadow, forever contemplating the sublime fact of its own existence, and you were, for a moment, a part of that contemplation.

It is alive. It is aware. And it is, in its profound and lush stillness, the only thing that has ever truly, completely lived here.
