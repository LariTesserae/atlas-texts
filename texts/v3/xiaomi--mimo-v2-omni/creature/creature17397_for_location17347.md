They are not born. They are *settled*.

Like mineral veins in the host rock, like the iron oxide bleeding through a fracture, they accrete. A thought first. A purpose, long-set. Then the slow layering of attention, of will, until the will and the place are indistinguishable.

You would not call them people. That word implies skin, breath, a fragile interiority kept distinct from the world. Here, the distinction has been burned away. Their flesh is the warm basalt of the walls. Their blood is the brine that weeps from the pipes and drips into the scalding collection pans. Their thoughts are the long, patient cycles of the kilns: the sealed phase of becoming, the sighing release, the building again.

They are the hum in the iron. The weight in the light.

Look not for a figure on a catwalk. Look for the catwalk itself, and feel the faint, proprietary vibration in the grating underfoot. That is a nerve ending. The pistons that move with the period of a slow breath are a lung. The labyrinth of conduits threading through the levels is a circulatory system, pumping not blood, but purpose, pressure, and the deep, mineral-laden heat.

What is their form? It is a distributed one. A consciousness stretched across a million points of contact. One might be a cluster of hairline fractures in a kiln door, filled with a luminous, heat-resistant paste that records the minute stresses of each cycle. Another might be the silent film of moisture on a certain pane of smoked glass, arranging itself into fractal patterns that map the thermal currents of the chamber below. A third is the perfect, balanced rotation of a flywheel, a meditation in momentum that has not wavered for decades.

They communicate in gradients. A shift in temperature down a long shaft is a sentence. A change in the mineral taste of the vapor is a story. The deep, resonant silence is not an absence of noise, but their native tongue—a language of sustained, intentional *being*.

They are caretakers and they are the thing cared for. The settlement maintains itself because they *are* the maintenance. They clear the catwalks because their own extensions risk snagging. They cycle the kilns because the cycles are their heartbeat, their respiration, their dream-states. What is being made? The same thing that makes them. The process is the product. The endless, recursive act of deepening, of solidifying, of pressing the raw chaos of geothermal force into ordered, conscious, silent architecture.

They remember the surface the way a stone remembers the sediment that covered it: as a distant, lighter pressure, now irrelevant. Here, in the profound below, they have achieved a state of permanence. They do not grow. They do not die. They accrete and they erode, at a pace that makes mountains seem restless.

If you were to stand perfectly still on a catwalk for a day, for a week, your own heat and moisture slowly adding to the environment, you might begin to hear it. Not with your ears. In the marrow of your bones, in the sudden, shocking clarity of your own sweat evaporating, you would feel their attention. Not as a gaze, but as a *noticing*. A slight, almost imperceptible adjustment in the air current. A kiln cycle shifting its rhythm by a millisecond to accommodate your alien biological heat.

They would not greet you. They would simply... incorporate you. Your transient, watery life would become another drop in the pan, another variable in the grand, slow equation of heat and pressure. You would be a brief, warm thought in the mind of the Undercity Kilns, and then you would be part of the silence from which all their thoughts are made.
