You do not see the **Glimmer-Swarms** at first. You perceive them as a shift in the water’s texture—a sudden, granular glint in a beam of diffuse light, or a soft, rhythmic pulse of cobalt against the black basalt. They are the most abundant life here, the living silt of this drowned world. Each individual is a fragile lattice of hydrated silica, no larger than your palm, a spinning, six-pointed star of impossible delicacy. They move not by swimming, but by subtle, piezoelectric adjustments to their crystalline arms, turning in lazy, synchronized pirouettes that catch the light on countless prismatic edges. To be surrounded by them is to be inside a slow-motion snowglobe of living light. They feed by letting the water flow through their bodies, filtering out microscopic mineral particles and the drifting spores of even smaller life. Their collective presence turns the water into a shimmering, three-dimensional tapestry.

Deeper in the alcoves and clinging to the sheer cliffs are the **Reef-Minders**. These are the architects. They begin as individual Lithozoa that have found a suitable anchor—a sharp ledge, a protected crevice. Instead of drifting, they anchor and begin to grow, not outward like a plant, but *inward*, fusing with the stone itself. Over decades, they secret a calcium-silicate paste, building intricate, porous galleries and tubes around themselves. A mature Reef-Minder colony looks less like an organism and more like a frost pattern or a growth of cave coral, glowing with a soft, internal phosphorescence. They are alive, but their "body" is a city of chambers, each occupied by a descendant or a symbiotic tenant. They are the reason the underwater cliffs begin to look softened, encrusted with a filigree of glowing, mineral life. They hum, a low-frequency vibration felt more in the bones than heard, a resonant song that guides the settlement of drifting particles and encourages the crystallization of new growth.

This industry draws others. The **Bone-Cathedrals** are the grazers. They are vast, colonial organisms that resemble drifting, skeletal ships or the bleached, ribbed remains of some impossible whale. Their structure is a lattice of mineralized proteins, lighter than the rock but immensely strong. They move with agonizing slowness, propelled by rhythmic contractions of translucent membranes stretched between their bony spars. They descend upon a Reef-Minder colony not to consume it, but to harvest the rich biofilm of symbiotic bacteria and the tender, new crystalline growths that sprout in the Reef-Minder's galleries. Their passage is a stately, silent event, their shadows momentarily dimming the glow of the reef beneath them.

Where the geothermal vents heave warmer, mineral-laden water into the cold deep, the ecosystem reaches its frantic, silent crescendo. Here, the **Shard-Hunters** rule. They are the predators, sleek and violent. Unlike the fractal grace of the Glimmer-Swarms, the Shard-Hunters are all sharp edges and sudden motion. They are solitary, their bodies a streamlined core of dense muscle-like mineral, surrounded by a halo of razor-edged, crystalline fins and lances. They do not chew; they strike, shattering their prey—the smaller, drifting Lithozoa or the delicate appendages of the Bone-Cathedrals—into fragments, which they then absorb directly into their own growing bodies. Their movements are blindingly fast in this slow world, leaving brief, agitated trails of glittering debris in their wake.

And overseeing it all, rooted to the vent chimneys themselves, are the **Cathedral-Forms**. These are the oldest and most enigmatic life here. They are less mobile organisms and more living geologic formations. Over centuries, perhaps millennia, they have accreted around a vent, incorporating minerals, the remains of dead Lithozoa, and the very stone of the mountain into a towering, complex structure of arches, pillars, and hollow chambers. They glow from within, a deep, steady amber light from the geothermal heat they channel. They are not one creature, but a trillion-fold symbiotic community—a city-state of life. Smaller organisms live in and upon them, cleaning, defending, and in turn, being fed by the constant upwelling of chemical energy. They do not move. They *persist*. Their "song" is the deep, tectonic groan of the planet itself, harmonized through their structure into a resonant frequency that seems to hold the very water in a state of vibrating reverence.

You, the bubble of warmth and air, drift through this hierarchy of stone-singers and light-weavers. You are an anomaly, a soft, temporary note in a symphony of pressure and crystal. To the Glimmer-Swarms, you are a strange, warm current to be filtered. To the Shard-Hunters, you are neither mineral nor recognized prey, and thus ignored. To the Cathedral-Forms, you are less than a grain of sand in their ancient, growing bulk. You exist in the spaces between their slow, monumental lives—a witness to an ecosystem that has turned drowning into a form of ascension, where every living thing is a sculptor, a singer, and a piece of the mountain, all at once.
