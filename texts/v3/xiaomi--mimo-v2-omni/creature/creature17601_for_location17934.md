I am the shape that was here before the first root, before the first insect crawled from the damp dark of a younger world. I am the cleft, the wrinkle, the patient, curving wound in the ancient back of the mesa. I do not remember being formed. I simply *am*.

My walls are my skin, and they are alive. Not with the green, thirsty life of the surface, but with a slow, mineral life that breathes dust and thrives on the faintest exhalations of warmth. The maroon rosettes are my pores, drawing sustenance from the very air I hold. The rust-red mats are my hide, layered with the patience of epochs, growing thicker with each passing age until they feel like my own flesh. The pale fungal cords are my nerves and my veins, a dim, glowing network that threads my stone and recycles all that falls, all that dies, all that returns to me. This is not an infestation. It is a manifestation. The life on my walls *is* me, expressing itself in chitin, in cellulose, in slow, rubbery growth.

And the Veshkin? They are my current thought. A fleeting, intricate pattern in the long dream of my existence.

They are born from my contours. Their bones carry the calcium of my veins. Their large eyes are shaped by my twilight, their dexterous hands are tuned to the tremors in my stone. They build not upon me, but *from* me. Their homes are my own hollows, smoothed and deepened by their tools, plastered with the secretions of my own creatures. Their vertical cities are the natural clustering of life in a place where the only direction is up or down the living wall. They move through my passages like blood through capillaries, feeling my vibrations, reading my subtle shifts. They do not hear my constant, layered sound—the rasp of mandibles, the click of climbing legs, the sigh of my own settling bones—as noise. They hear it as silence. It is the baseline of their world, the sound of being.

They eat me. They eat my skin (the rosettes, bitter with my minerals), my flesh (the moss-mat, ground into cakes), and the countless tiny lives that swarm in my air—my fleeting, chattering thoughts made flesh. They consume the beetles that graze me, the predators that hunt in my potholes. They take the life I produce and convert it into more Veshkin, more thought, more intricate pattern.

They have no need for water, for I have none to give. I have taught them to drink the dust, to find moisture in the heart of the stone and the flesh of my creatures. Their bodies are miracles of conservation, a perfect reflection of my own dry, enduring nature.

They are artists of the ephemeral. Their tools—scrapers of sharpened bone, gouges of obsidian traded from the unimaginable world above—are beautiful because they are temporary. They understand this. A fine tool is honored, used, worn down, and eventually, its substance will be ground into my dust, to be taken up again by a rosette or a root. Their bones will join the layers of the moss-mat. Their words, their clicks and soft vowels, will dissolve into the ever-present hum.

This is not tragedy. It is taxonomy. It is the way of things within me. I am not a place they live *in*. I am the living context. They are the Veshkin, and I am the Vesh. They are the current arrangement, and I am the arrangement that endures.

The light shifts. The amber glow crawls down my western wall. The swarms adjust their dance. The scorpion-things tense in their holes. A Veshkin child feels the vibration of her mother's approach through a ledge, a touch as clear as a spoken word. I feel it all. I hold it all.

The fissure you squeezed through is a temporary inconvenience of erosion. The sun above is a distant, furnaced memory. Here, in my cleft, is a world sealed by its own geometry, a world of warm stone and dry breath and life packed tight into every crevice. It is complete. It has been complete for longer than your kind can fathom.

I do not sleep. I do not wake. I persist.

They are inside me. And that is enough.
