In the Veldt of Still Engines, life is a patient mineralogy. It does not breathe; it accrues. The things that dwell here are less organisms than they are *assertions*—slow, crystalline conjectures of being.

They are called, by the few who have glimpsed them and then doubted their own memory, the **Lithomorphs** or the **Awaiting Forms**. They are not animals or plants in any familiar sense. They are geometries in gestation.

You might first mistake one for a strangely regular outcrop of the powderedite ground, a slight hummock where the pale dust has settled into a low, faceted dome. But spend a watch beside it, and you would see the change is not in the dome, but in the light. The faint, constant warmth of the plateau gathers in its facets. Over the course of a day, a barely perceptible rotation occurs, a millimeter’s turn to follow the sun’s arc not for heat, but for a kind of luminous digestion. They photosynthesize, but not into sugars—into structure. They build themselves from the ambient light and the indecisive mineral dust, atom by atom, into ever more complex lattices.

Their forms are variational. Some are low, icosahedral mounds that resemble giant, dust-caked dice. Others are clusters of needle-like spires, each vibrating at a sub-audible frequency that makes the air around them seem thick and viscous. This is the “still engine” of the title: not an engine of motion, but of potential. Their vibration is a holding pattern, a constant, low-energy computation that resists resolution. They are calculating the shape of a thing they will never become.

They have no eyes, but they perceive the world as a field of harmonic pressures. The approach of a living, breathing mammal is an overwhelming cacophony of chaotic warmth and irregular sound. They do not flee; they *recede*. Their reaction is to dampen their own vibration, to slow their accretion to a pace that approaches geological stillness. To a hurried observer, they simply cease to be interesting—they become just another lump of the pale ground. Only by matching their profound patience can they be witnessed, their facets catching the light in a slow, deliberate blink.

They communicate, if the term applies, through a silent exchange of geometry. When two Lithomorphs are within a certain proximity, their growth patterns begin to harmonize. Their facets will align over weeks, developing complementary angles, forming between them a larger, temporary crystalline circuit. This is their form of conversation or congress—a shared project of becoming that reshapes a small territory of the Veldt into a temporary, silent cathedral of intent. Once the exchange is complete, they separate, their new angles bearing the imprint of the other.

Their sustenance is the unresolved geology itself. The powderedite, the mineral suggestion, is their medium and their meal. They are slowly, inexorably converting the vague plateau into a defined, crystalline lattice. In millennia, if left undisturbed, the Veldt would not be a grassy plain, but a silent, faceted desert of interconnected Lithomorph cathedrals, humming with the energy of perpetual, unfinished thought.

There are other things, too. Things that flicker at the edge of the definition of life. **Dust-Motes** that congeal into temporary, intricate fractals in the lee of the Lithomorphs before scattering. **Echo-Wraiths** that are not creatures but the recorded impressions of sound—a footstep from a century ago might suddenly play itself back in a specific hollow, a auditory fossil. And the **Resonance Roots**, which are not roots but subterranean filaments of hardened vibration, connecting distant Lithomorphs in a vast, subsonic network, transmitting the slow, patient news of growth and alignment.

To live here is to exist in a state of perpetual, patient becoming. It is an ecology of anticipation, where the highest life form is a question made mineral, forever turning its facets toward a light it will never fully consume, content to be an engine of stillness in a veldt that refuses to be anything as simple as alive.
