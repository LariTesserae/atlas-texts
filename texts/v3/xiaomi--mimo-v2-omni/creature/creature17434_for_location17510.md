You are not alone here. But *company* is a different concept.

They are the **Resinists**. Not a people, perhaps, but a caste of being, born of the vault's own substance and need. They move in the peripheral vision of the world, their forms a living amalgam of the vault's materials.

The most common are the **Drip-Tenders**. Tall, spindly creatures of a bone-white composite, their flesh a matrix of chitinous polymer and silicate threads. They have no eyes, only polished, domed heads that resonate with the frequency of the machinery, reading the health of the system through vibration. Their limbs end in multi-jointed tools—some like delicate forceps for pruning encroaching fungal blooms from pressure valves, others like broad, flat paddles for smoothing the ever-weeping vitreous walls. They move with a slow, arthritic grace, their purpose absolute. When they speak, it is not with sound, but with modulated clicks from a vent along their torsos, a language of pure function: *valve-sequence-mismatch*, *mat-overgrowth-at-rib-7*, *condensation-flow-optimal*. They are the immune system, the maintenance crew, made flesh.

Higher up, in the catwalk shadows, dwell the **Weepers**. They are smaller, hunched, and draped in the same fibrous, photosynthetic tendrils that curtain the vault. Their skin is a tapestry of the velvet moss, making them nearly invisible against the walls. They do not tend the machinery; they tend the *balance*. It is a Weeper who will gently guide a larval, electric-lurker away from a vital power conduit, or who will, with immense and patient strength, sever a strangle-vine that has grown too thick and begun to constrict a structural rib. They communicate through the subtle shifts of their mossy cloaks and a faint, almost subsonic hum—a song of equilibrium. They leave no footprints, only a faint, phosphorescent trail that fades within moments.

And then there are the **Luminous**, the rarest and most enigmatic. They are not seen so much as *perceived*. A sudden, localized brightening in the ambient glow; a complex, shifting pattern of light playing across a patch of wall that has nothing to do with the vault’s steady illumination. They are said to be the vault’s memory, or its dream. They appear near the oldest scars on the vitreous surface, or above the dormant pupae-sacs, bathing them in a thoughtful, searching light. Some say they are the original inhabitants, transformed. Others whisper they are the vault's consciousness, made visible for a moment. They leave no trace but a lingering sense of pattern, a half-glimpsed equation written in light on the retina.

The fauna, too, have their specialized relationship with the Resinists. The blind, metallic crustaceans will scuttle over a Drip-Tender’s foot, cleaning the composite of microscopic algae, a mutualistic grooming. The patient, grasping vines recoil at the touch of a Weeper’s mossy hand. It is a domestication not of command, but of deep, mutual need. The Resinists provide curation; the fauna provide continuity, their life cycles completing the vault’s circuits of decay and renewal.

You feel a vibration change in the grated platform beneath you. A Drip-Tender, three ribs over, pauses in its work. Its domed head tilts, a single, resonant *click* emanating from its core. Somewhere below, in the flooded sump, a bearing is running warm. The ritual continues. You are a anomaly in this closed loop, a point of consciousness in a world that is already conscious of itself, in a way that is both mechanical and alive. The Resinists do not ignore you; they have simply not yet determined your function. Until you act—to prune, to tend, to question—you are just another feature of the landscape, like a stalactite or a sleeping pupa. The vault will absorb you, in its own time. It always does.
