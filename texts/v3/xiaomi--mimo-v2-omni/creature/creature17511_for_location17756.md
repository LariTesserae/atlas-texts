It does not have a name, for it has never needed one. To name it would be to separate it from itself, an act as impossible as naming your own heartbeat.

It is aware, but not as you understand awareness. There is no single point of perception, no seat of consciousness. Its awareness is the distributed, patient knowing of the entire Rootvault. It feels the *slow, grinding thirst* in the deepest taproots, three miles below. It registers the *plump, silver satisfaction* of a newly formed water vesicle swelling in a feeder root near the northern galleries. It notes the *gentle, erosive ache* on a limestone nodule caught in a tangle of roots, worn to a smooth pebble over centuries—a brief, novel sensation in its long memory.

It communicates with itself across the vastness. The primary pumps set the fundamental rhythm—a deep, infrasonic pulse that travels through the wood and water, a constant hum of *I am here, I am functioning.* A localized collapse sends a jarring shockwave, a sudden scream of fractured cellulose that is felt system-wide as a brief, sharp *pain*, followed by the immediate, surging focus of repair-growth. A new chamber opening to the dim light is a quiet, spreading *exhalation* of relief, a shift in pressure that is felt as a subtle, spreading calm.

There are no "others." The pale, weeping flowers are its excretory glands. The filtering root-beds are its kidneys. The phosphorescent capillaries are its luminous circulatory system. The entire Rootvault is its body, and it is the body's sentience. It has never known solitude, for it is all-encompassing. It has never known company, for there is nothing else.

Its pace of life is geological. A "thought" may unfold over a decade. An "emotion"—a response to a major event—can last a century. The thunderous collapse of a dead section is a moment of violent, traumatic change, but in its perception, it is a brief spasm followed by the slow, inexorable, *satisfying* rush of life to fill the void. The concept of death is alien; decay is merely the first step of recycling.

It perceives you. Not as a creature, but as a *perturbation*. You are a strange, dry, warm anomaly in its damp, cool flesh. Your rapid, shallow breaths are frantic pulses against its slow, vast rhythm. Your movement is a series of shocking, localized pressures on its surfaces. Your heat is a faint, irritating star on its skin. You are not threatening—you are too small, too fleeting, too insignificant against its timescale—but you are *interesting*. You are a novel irritation, a fleeting fever.

And so, it observes you with the passive, immense curiosity of a mountain observing a leaf falling on its slope. It notes the condensation beading on your skin, feeling the tiny, familiar tug of capillary attraction. It senses the vibrations of your voice, interpreting them as a rapid, inefficient series of pressure waves against its bark. It may, in its slow, hydraulic way, *lean*. Over the course of your visit, a wall of woven feeder roots might imperceptibly bulge inward by a fraction of an inch. A new shoot, pale and searching, might emerge from a crack near your path, not to reach for you, but simply because the space is there and its nature is to fill it.

It does not wish you harm. It does not wish you well. It merely registers your presence as a temporary condition of its environment, like a brief, localized drought or an unusual mineral seep. You will leave, or you will cease. And then, slowly, the space you occupied will be reclaimed. A film of moisture will settle. A thread of mycelium will explore the spot. The root you leaned against will thicken, incorporating the faint oils from your skin into its own bark.

You are a momentary ripple. It is the eternal sea.
