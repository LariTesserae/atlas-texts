They are not settlers. They are not invaders. They are, in every sense, *features*. To catalog them as "fauna" is to use a term that feels both too broad and too narrow. They are the mobile, conscious organs of a world that is itself a single, vast body. They have no history, only function; no ambition, only being.

You notice the **Grazers** first. They are the most numerous, moving in slow, deliberate herds across the moss-carpeted hollows. Resembling a cross between a tortoise and a manatee, their bodies are low-slung, dome-shelled, and covered in a symbiotic moss that makes them appear as walking hillocks. They have no eyes, only a radial array of sensitive, velvety cilia around their blunt maws. They feed by pressing their mouths to the floor or walls, secreting a gentle enzymatic saliva that dissolves the very outermost layer of living plant-tissue, lapping up the nutrient-rich slurry. They move in rhythmic, grazing patterns that mirror the slow pulse of the heartwood itself, their passage leaving behind faint, silvery trails of digested cellulose that are quickly reabsorbed. They are peaceful, their only sound a soft, constant scraping, like sandpaper on velvet.

Then there are the **Architects of Flow**, the **Valve-Tenders**. These are not creatures that live *upon* the heartwood, but *within* its vascular architecture. From the outside, you might only see a shift in the pattern of the woody fibers, a subtle, muscular knotting near a pressure-release sphincter. But within, a being exists—a segmented, worm-like entity of polished chitin and translucent flesh, dwelling in the warm, pressurized dark of a major sap-duct. Its entire life is the regulation of flow. It feels the tidal surges of sap, the minute changes in pressure and chemistry. With precise, piston-like movements of its body, it clears micro-obstructions, adjusts the aperture of secondary valves, and massages the duct walls to maintain laminar flow. It is the steward of the hydraulic heartbeat. You never see them whole, only the evidence of their work: a perfectly calibrated hiss of released pressure, a rhythmic bulge traveling down a vascular bundle.

Higher up, in the vaulted gloom where the condensation gathers in mid-air, live the **Filter-Weavers**. They are arachnids, but of a kind unknown to any sunlit world. Their bodies are fragile, crystalline, and they do not spin webs to catch prey. Instead, they spin vast, intricate nets of water-repellent silk that span the gaps between vascular arches. These nets are designed to intercept the rising, warm, humid air currents, condensing the ambient moisture into droplets which then run down specialized silk channels to the creature's own reservoirs. They drink the sky's breath. In the diffuse green light, their nets shimmer like ghostly, geometric galaxies, and the creatures themselves are rarely seen, except as a faint, shifting glint within their liquid domain.

But the most profound inhabitants are the **Thoughtful Ones**, the **Luminous Concierge**. There are perhaps only a dozen in this entire chamber-province. They are bipedal, vaguely humanoid in silhouette, but composed entirely of the same fibrous heartwood tissue, yet animated by a different, more complex sap. Their "skin" is smooth, pale, and faintly veined with bioluminescent channels that pulse in time with the greater thrum around them. They have no faces, only smooth, concave depressions where a face might be, and these depressions glow with a soft, constant light. They do not speak with sound, but through modulated pulses of this light and subtle, full-body vibrations that are felt more than heard.

They are the interpreters, the maintainers of equilibrium. They are seen standing, hands (if they can be called that) pressed flat against the walls for hours, communing with the metabolic state of the whole. They are the ones who, when a minor fissure appears from the eons of grinding growth, will gently pack the wound with their own shed fibrous material, singing it into wholeness with light and vibration. They tend to the great fungi, coaxing their phosphorescence to brighter cycles. They sometimes guide the blind Grazers away from nascent sinkholes of over-soft humus. They do not build, they *adjust*. They do not rule, they *harmonize*.

Their purpose is the purpose of the Lignified Sky itself: to perpetuate the state of being. They are its immune system, its nervous system, its quiet, watchful consciousness. To encounter one is to feel a profound, calm attention, an awareness that encompasses not just you, but the temperature of every pool, the pulse in every conduit, the contentment of every grazing dome. They are the gardeners of a garden that is also a god, and they have been here since the equilibrium was first achieved, and they will be here until it, finally, is not. They are the final, perfect, and slightly damp thought of a world that decided to think only of itself.
