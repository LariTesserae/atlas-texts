In the saturated stillness of the Kept Highlands, life does not merely *inhabit*; it *integrates*. It is an ecosystem where the boundaries between organism, mineral, and element are not blurred but never truly existed. What lives here is an extension of the process itself.

**In the Water-Canopy:**
The immense fronds are not plants in any terrestrial sense. They are **Frond-Kelp**, colonies of phototropic cells anchored to the stone by a network of silicate-secreting rhizoids. They do not sway; they *pulse*, a glacial rhythm synchronized to the subsonic hum and the imperceptible tidal shift. Their thick, waxen surfaces are not leaves but living filters, sifting warmth and dissolved minerals directly from the water. Clinging to their bases are **Shard-Shells**, gastropod-like creatures whose spiraling shells are not calcium carbonate but a composite of biological polymers and the very iridescent mineral veined through the stone. They move with the patience of geological time, leaving faint, silvery trails of metabolized mineral behind them.

**On the Stone:**
The mosses are **Heat-Lichen**, a symbiotic conglomerate of fungus and chemosynthetic bacteria that taps directly into the stone's gentle warmth. They are the first colonizers, the true vanguard. Their velvet pile is a forest of microscopic filaments that excrete acids, softening the basalt's glassy edge and creating the first fractures for larger roots. Among them, on the newest stone, you might find **Vein-Crawlers**. These are the highlands' most striking inhabitants: arthropods with segmented carapaces that are literally translucent, their internal structures laced with the same iridescent mineral. They do not eat the lichen; they *graze the light*, their shells refracting the diffused glow into tiny, moving sparks of copper and blue. Their movement is so slow you often mistake them for a glint in the rock until one blinks a multifaceted eye.

**In the Deep Basins:**
Where the water pulls back from a basin's edge in seeming respect, you are observing the territory of a **Still-Thing**. It is not seen in full, only in suggestive parts: a slow, dark curve moving twenty meters down, disturbing no silt; a momentary displacement of water that suggests a body of immense size and deliberate motion. It is believed to be a detritivore of the highest order, a creature that processes the fallen biomass and the ancient stone alike, its digestion so slow it is nearly symbiotic with decay. It is the highland's quiet engine, turning matter over on a timescale that makes the tides seem frantic.

**The Solitary Caller:**
The flutelike call belongs to the **Echo-Finch**, though it is neither an echo nor a finch. It is a solitary, wingless avian that nests in the deepest hairline fractures of the oldest stone. Its song is a lie—a mimicry of sounds that *used to* exist here, perhaps carried on the wind from distant, drier lands: the memory of a forest bird, the sigh of wind through dry grass. It repeats these fragments not for communication, but as a kind of acoustic fossilization, preserving a ghost of an ecosystem it has never known. It moves unseen, its plumage a perfect, wet-stone grey, and its call never comes from the same direction because it moves with the slow, drifting patience of the Shard-Shells, its location a function of the tide.

**The Unseen Majority:**
The true biomass is microscopic and mycelial. A **Fungal Mat** underlies everything, a vast, continuous network that threads through the rhizoids of the Frond-Kelp, the roots of the waxy stalks, and the cracks in the stone. It is the highland's nervous system and circulatory system, transporting nutrients and chemical signals across kilometers. It is this mat that gives the water its preternatural clarity in places—it actively filters particulate matter, feeding on it. The "things" moving in the deep are often just sections of this mat pulsing with activity, a slow, silent communication you feel in your bones as a change in the subsonic hum.

**The Relationship:**
Nothing here is a discrete "animal" or "plant." Everything is a **Process-Architect**. The Vein-Crawler builds its shell from the landscape. The Frond-Kelp shapes the water flow that delivers its food. The Still-Thing carves the basins that become its domain. They are all expressions of the same geothermal warmth, the same patient mineral uplift, the same saturated, living medium. They do not hunt, so much as *harvest time*. They do not flee, so much as *persist in a different location*.

They are life forms that have surrendered speed for sustainability, individuality for integration. In a place that is itself a slow, living thing, they are its thoughts, its metabolism, its fleeting, beautiful details. They know you are here only as a temporary anomaly in the resistance of the air, a strange heat signature that will be reclaimed, overwritten, and absorbed back into the endless, quiet becoming.
