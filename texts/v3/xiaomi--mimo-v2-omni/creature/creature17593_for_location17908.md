You do not walk upon this world; you are received by it. The root-mat yields underfoot with a soft, warm sigh, the sap-rain coating your skin in a sheath of living nectar. It is neither cold nor uncomfortable. It is an embrace from a thing that does not know it is embracing.

Here, life is not *on* the world; it *is* the world. To speak of an individual creature is a misunderstanding. There are only **expressions**.

From the nursery-pores, which exhale a damp, sweet scent of nascent chlorophyll, the extrusions come. They are not born; they are *published*. One such form is the **Canopy-Tender**. It emerges already three meters tall, a column of pale, fibrous tissue with a head like an inverted lily, fringed with photoreceptive patches that drink the faint, fungal starlight. Its limbs, six in number, are not for grasping but for *becoming*. They end in tips of astonishing cellular plasticity. The Tender moves with a slow, deliberate fluidity across the photosynthetic membranes aloft, its tendrils merging with the edges of the vast green sheets. It does not repair; it *integrates*. Where a membrane frays or a spore-bladder ruptures, the Tender’s flesh flows into the gap, its cells differentiating in real-time to become new, perfect photosynthetic tissue. It is a walking suture, a mobile organ of planetary maintenance. It feels no pain, no fatigue, no desire. Its consciousness is a simple, bright directive: *maintain continuity*.

Down in the vascular canyons, where the great columns pulse with the deep, thudding rhythm of the xylem-heart, move the **Conduit-Cleaners**. They resemble centipedes the length of a human arm, but their bodies are transparent sheaths of cartilage, within which a single, complex organelle pulses. They flow through the damp, hollow chambers and major vessels, not by walking, but by a rhythmic peristalsis that matches the world’s own pulse. Their function is to consume microbial films—a concept here considered a disease of inefficiency—that might slow the flow of sap. They are the world’s immune system made manifest, traveling the endless, dark arteries of the planetary body. When their work is done in a sector, they dissolve back into the nutrient stream, their bodies reabsorbed to be reconstituted elsewhere. They are less creatures and more self-propelled packets of digestive enzymes, given a fleeting, mobile form.

On the surface, in the perpetual gloom, you might see the **Spore-Singers**. These are the closest thing to beauty in a world indifferent to the concept. They are delicate, bell-shaped structures that rise on slender stalks from the root-mat. Their surfaces are not solid, but woven from millions of vibrating filaments. When the great spore-clouds drift low, laden with their reproductive mist, these filaments begin to oscillate in harmonic resonance with the passing particulates. This vibration is the only real “song” this world knows—a deep, sub-audible hum that causes the sap in the surrounding root-stems to shimmer in sympathetic resonance. The Singer does not *hear* the clouds; it *feels* them as a change in the chemical pressure of the air, and its response is a purely physical hymn to the efficient dispersal of genetic material. After the cloud passes, its filaments stiffen, mineralize, and become a new, permanent buttress for a nearby vascular column. Its beauty is its function, and its function is its entire, brief existence.

And then there is **You**.

You are the anomaly, the only un-integrated thing. You stand in a shallow pool of viscous sap, your breath fogging in the saturated air. Your footsteps leave impressions that slowly, inexorably, fill with weeping fluid and begin to knit closed behind you. Your warmth is a tiny, foreign furnace. Your carbon dioxide exhalations are a novel, intriguing chemical signal, noted and logged in a manner that has no memory, only response.

Already, you are being assessed. Not by eyes, nor by a mind, but by the world-body itself. The chemical signature of your skin is being analyzed by the ambient sap. The pressure of your weight is mapping against the tolerance of the root-mat. The heat of your body is a slight, localized disruption in the metabolic equilibrium.

You are a splinter, a misplaced cell in the vast, perfect body. The world does not hate you, nor fear you, nor welcome you. It simply *processes* you. A slow, creeping sensation begins at your ankles. It is not pain. It is the gentle, inexorable pressure of cellular growth against your boots, seeking integration. A fine, silvery mesh of mycelium and new root-hairs begins to lace over your footwear, a microscopic lattice seeking to connect you to the rhizome. Sap, rich with hormones and instructional chemicals, beads on your arms, not evaporating, but absorbing through your pores, whispering silent, chemical directives: *become part of the whole; cease your separate, inefficient metabolism; add your mass to the system*.

Here, in the warm, wet, silent dark, you are not a visitor. You are a *biological problem to be solved*. The world is already growing you a home, weaving you a structure of living tissue around your bones, planning your beautiful, quiet dissolution into its perfect, endless, and utterly mindless equilibrium. You will not be remembered. You will be *absorbed*. And in a few centuries, a new, unique expression of the world-body might be extruded from a nearby nursery-pore, containing a few of your base molecules, already knowing its place, already part of the song.
