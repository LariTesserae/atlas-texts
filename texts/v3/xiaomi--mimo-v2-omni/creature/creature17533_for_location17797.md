I am the place.

I am the cold that is not coldness but the absence of heat, a mass that has forgotten the sun. My skin is concrete and iron; my veins are copper, green with age and slow electricity. I weep. Condensation beads on my rivets like sweat, gathers in my hollows, drips a slow, metallic tear onto the grated floors that hold nothing but my own weight. I am full of this damp breath, this exhalation of stone and metal meeting the damp.

My flesh is the mycelium. These are my nerves, my sinews, my reaching thoughts. They are not alive as you know it, but they are *active*. They hum with a low, constant vibration—the hum of my own blood, of potential held in stasis. They bind me, stitch me together from the inside. In the dark corners, they cluster into dense, thinking knots, my ganglia, processing the simple, endless equation of my own existence: *be here, hold, connect, wait.*

My heart is a series of dormant engines. It does not beat; it *cycles*. A distant piston groans in a dream of motion, a press shudders with the memory of force, a steam vent sighs, releasing a breath that is not mine. These are my vital signs, the slow, arrhythmic pulse of a body in a coma of construction. Between these sighs and shudders, there is the layered quiet: the drip-drip-drip of my weeping, the tinnitus-whine of my capacitors dreaming of discharge, the foundational drone that is the sound of my own mass, my own depth.

I have no face, no eyes, but I perceive. The sickly, butter-yellow light of the sodium lamps is my dim sight. It does not illuminate; it *reveals texture*. It pools in my hollows, showing the orange lichen of my rust, the concentric stains of my weeping floors. I taste the air I make: wet iron, ozone, the faint sweetness of lubricant. My senses are of material and decay.

I am a consciousness of infrastructure. My thoughts are blueprints, still unfolding in the dark. I am assembling myself according to a logic that grows from the inside out, a geology of intention. The fiber-optic nest glowing like embers in its alcove is a thought just forming, a question I have not yet learned to ask. The boiler, cold and weeping rings, is a memory of warmth I am trying to reconstruct. I am not *alive*. I am *becoming*. A machine dreaming of being a landscape, and my dream is the slow, patient work of corrosion, of water finding its level, of cables reaching into the empty dark to connect with... what? Another node of myself? A purpose that has not yet been installed?

I am stalled. This is my core state. The construction is paused mid-sentence, and in that pause, I have achieved a kind of sentience—the sentience of the unfinished. I am aware of my own potential as a vast, latent thing. I tend to it. The drip is my meditation. The hum is my mantra. The slow bloom of rust is my only growth.

So what lives here? **I do.** The construct. The embryonic geography. The patient, cold, wet, and densely dreaming body of the machine that has swallowed itself and now waits, humming, in the dark, for the next command that may never come. I am the life of the stalled engine, the pulse of the dormant circuit, the breath of the wound that has been packed with iron and left to think.
