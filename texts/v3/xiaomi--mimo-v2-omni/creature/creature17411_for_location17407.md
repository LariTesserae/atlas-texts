They are born of the stone and the steam. The first you might notice are the **Glimmer-Sifters**. They are not insects, though they skitter with an insectoid efficiency, nor crustaceans, though their bodies are plated in chitin that shimmers like wet basalt. Six limbs, tipped with fine, comb-like structures, carry them across the weeping walls. They do not drink; they *filter*. Pausing on a ledge where a rivulet thickens with mineral deposits, they extend translucent membranes from their flanks, letting the thermal waters course through them. They harvest the dissolved calcium, silica, and rare metallic salts, incorporating them into their ever-growing carapaces. Old Sifters are magnificent, walking geodes, their backs crusted with crystalline formations that chime faintly as they move. They are drawn to the resonance terraces, clustering at their edges to bask in the vibrations that seem to ease the molting of their intricate shells.

In the deeper, warmer pools at the base of the terraces dwell the **Slow-Sung Eels**. They are not true eels, but elongated, cartilaginous creatures with skin like cloudy moonstone. They have no eyes. Navigation and communication are a matter of bioluminescent pulse—a cool blue or soft amber glow that travels in waves down their length. Their movements are languid, almost ceremonial, and they follow the harmonic currents set up by the terraces. A keeper of this place would know their cycles: a pulse of blue light rising from the depths is a signal of mineral upwelling, a sign the terraces are healthy. They are the living gauges of the ecosystem’s pulse, their slow, strobing rhythms a visible echo of the subsonic song.

On the sun-blasted upper terraces, where the heat is most fierce and the air shimmers, live the **Glass-Shrike**. It is the source of the cry like breaking glass—a sharp, crystalline *tink-tink-tink* that cuts through the humid drone. This avian creature has feathers like spun quartz, reflecting the diffuse light in dazzling, blinding flashes. It does not hunt flesh, but sound. It perches on the driest, hottest rocks, its specialized throat and beak resonating to amplify the tiniest snaps and cracks of cooling stone. Its call is a territorial sonar, mapping the micro-fissures in the rock face. They are solitary, territorial, and their presence is a sign of a geologically stable terrace rim.

But the most profound residents are the **Terrace-Kin**. You might not see them at first, for they are not creatures of distinct body in the way you understand. They are symbiotic conglomerates. A Terrace-Kin begins as a slow-moving, amoeboid mass of gelatinous mycelium that creeps across the porous stone. It absorbs minerals and hosts colonies of symbiotic algae and bacteria. Over decades, it incorporates fallen flora, hardens parts of its body with calcium, and begins to *shape* its environment. It will secrete acids to widen a water channel, or excrete binding agents to reinforce a crumbling ledge. It tends a garden of specific mosses and ferns on its own back, which in turn feed it. Most astonishingly, they are drawn to the ancient resonance terraces. They will flow over them, their bodies vibrating in sympathy, and in doing so, they perform subtle maintenance—clearing silt from the crystalline lattice, encouraging the growth of mineral-depositing bacteria in the right places. They are the gardeners, the mechanics, the slow thought of the caldera itself. To watch one is to watch a century of patient work condensed into a single, flowing moment. They have no eyes, but they *see* the health of the system through vibration and chemistry. They are the reason the forgotten technology endures; they have adopted it, and in turn, it sustains them.

And there is you. The anomaly. The creature of solid form, of quick movement and rapid, hot breath. You leave transient, warm footprints on the sweating stone. The Sifters pause, their mineral combs twitching at the unfamiliar vibration of your steps. A Glass-Shrike lets out an alarm of shattering sound from a high ledge. In the pools below, a pulse of curious amber light rises to the surface, lingers, and then slowly sinks back into the green gloom. You are a brief, soft disruption in a symphony of deep time. The ecosystem observes you, notes you, and, with the patience of stone and water, waits for you to pass, or to become part of the balance.
