They are children of the hum. Their bones are light, honeycombed like the rock itself, and their skin holds a faint, internal luminescence—not a glow, but a muted sheen, like polished nacre in deep water. Their eyes are large, pools of black adapted to the perpetual amber twilight, capable of seeing the intricate, veined patterns of light on the fungal caps with startling clarity. But their most profound adaptation is internal. Deep in their chests, where a mammal might hold lungs of complex alveoli, they possess a dense, spongy organ—a *spore-sac*. It is a filter, a digestive chamber, and a resonant instrument. They breathe the glittering air, and the sac harvests moisture and nutrients directly from the suspended spores and volatile compounds, sustaining them. They "drink" through inhalation. Their exhalations are warm, rich, and contribute to the very atmosphere that feeds them.

The Grown-Tenders do not speak as outside worlds might understand. Their language is a layered harmony of sound and subtle light. Words are formed by a combination of soft clicks of the tongue, hummed tones that vibrate in the spore-sac (creating palpable frequencies in the still air), and the deliberate, patterned dilation of bioluminescent patches on their wrists and throats—a slow, morphing script of cold blue light. A sentence is a chord, a story a symphony. The constant, soft rasping of the grazers is the baseline of their world; their own communication rises above it like intricate melody.

Their society is structured around the cycles—the slow growth of the city-fungi, the decadal cap-cycles of the great grazer-spines, the unpredictable, profound Sighs. They have no concept of "building" or "owning." They are *Cultivators*. From birth, they are attuned to the mycelial pulse. A Tender does not choose a trade; they are *grown into* a role by the specific inoculations and guided exposures of their youth. There are **Mycelists**, who read the nutrient flows in the living floor by touch and taste. There are **Cap-Wardens**, who tend the vast, luminous grazing fields, encouraging even growth and harvesting the gentle grazers not as predators, but as fellow participants in the cycle—their husks and waste are precious compost. There are **Lumen-Whisperers**, the artists and engineers who coax the bioluminescent veins in the architectural fungi to follow specific, beautiful, and functional patterns, lighting their spiraling homes and gathering halls.

Their greatest works are the **Echoing Halls**. These are not merely spaces, but cultivated acoustic instruments. By guiding the growth of certain shelf-fungi—those that ring like porcelain—into specific formations, and by carving the petrified fungal-stem tools into precise shapes, they create chambers where every sound is amplified, harmonized, and preserved. The history of their people is not written, but *sung* and *heard*. The story of a great Sigh, the genealogy of a cultivar, the pattern of a new light-script—all are stored as complex sonic vibrations, replayed and experienced in these halls as a tangible, shimmering presence in the air. To enter an Echoing Hall is to walk through a living archive of sound.

Life is measured in resonances. A child is named for the unique harmonic signature of their first resonant hum. A coupling is marked not by a ceremony, but by two Tenders spending a full growth-cycle merging their hums, learning to breathe in perfect synchrony until their spore-sacs attune. Death is a return. The body is not buried, but *inoculated*—placed upon a specific mycelial mat and seeded with chosen spores. The deceased is slowly, lovingly digested, their essence absorbed into the fungal tapestry, to be breathed in later by their kin. They believe all consciousness is a pattern in the hum, and it is never lost, only rearranged.

Their understanding of the "outside" is a theological void, a *Silence*—the antithesis of their world’s constant sound and breath. To break the balance is to invite Silence. The most heinous crime is not murder or theft, but *fracturing*—the unnecessary breaking of a living fungal structure, the introduction of a disruptive, non-cyclic sound, the hoarding of breath. Such acts are met with a terrifying punishment: the offender is taken to the edge of the known caverns, to the place where the rock yields to the endless, soundless void, and there, they are made to *listen*. To experience the absolute Silence, even for a moment, is a trauma from which few return fully.

Thus, they live in a state of profound, participatory stillness. They are not masters of their world; they are its most sensitive nerve endings. They feel the deep, slow digestion of the rock beneath their feet as a grounding vibration. They taste the health of the mycelium in the air they breathe. Their greatest ambition is not to ascend or conquer, but to deepen the resonance, to add their own fleeting, beautiful chord to the eternal, amber-lit hum of Gloomth. They are the tenders of a garden that is also their cathedral, their body, and their soul. They are the consciousness of the fungal world, arisen to marvel at its own slow, eternal song.
