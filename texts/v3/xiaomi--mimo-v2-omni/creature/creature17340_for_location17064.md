You are not alone on the platform. Perched at its edge, where the grated metal meets the roaring abyss, is a figure. It is humanoid only in the loosest sense—a suggestion of a torso, a head bowed in concentration, limbs that end not in hands but in articulated tool-nodes currently retracted into sleek housings. Its skin is a matte grey composite, seamless and damp, embedded with traceries of faint bioluminescence that pulse in slow, rhythmic waves, mirroring the Engine’s own heartbeat. This is a **Tide-warden**, a custodian born of the brine.

It does not acknowledge your presence. Its attention is fixed downward, into the chasm. From its back, a series of slender, cable-like tentacles extend, dipping into the current below. They are sensory organs, tasting the flow. The Warden reads the brine as a scholar reads a text. It detects the molecular ghost of a micro-fracture two kilometers east, the nutrient signature of a coming bloom in the northern filtration stacks, the faint, panicked electrochemical signature of a Gasket crab herd being culled by a managed surge. Its purpose is to monitor, to interpret, and to lightly assist the autonomous systems—not to command them, but to harmonize with them.

A sudden, sharp *crack* echoes from below, and a vein of blue light flares in the darkness. A support strut has suffered a cooling contraction fracture. Before the sound has faded, the Tide-warden’s role is clear. It does not leap into action. Instead, it emits a complex pattern of clicks and whistles—a coded broadcast that cuts through the roar. The message is simple: *Stress point. Grid Sigma-9. Recommend reinforcement.*

Within minutes, a distant shadow materializes from the gloom: a **Repair Drone**, larger than the maintenance platform itself, moving with the slow, deliberate grace of a whale. Its underside is a forest of manipulator arms and extrusion nozzles. It aligns with the fracture, and a cloud of **service-mites** descends—a glittering, chittering veil of tiny, multi-legged robots. They swarm the crack, their tiny welders glowing with a lightless, cold-fire energy as they bond the alloy back together. The drone hums a low-frequency all-clear. The Tide-warden’s bioluminescence flickers in a pattern of acknowledgement. The transaction is complete. The Engine’s health is preserved.

You observe more. In the distance, silhouetted against the emerald-gold glow from far above, the vast, translucent shape of a **void-whale** glides. But its passage is not random. It follows a prescribed migratory route, a living filter cleaning a specific sector on a seasonal cycle. Smaller creatures, **barnacle-mites**, cling to its flanks, scraping away the planktonic drones it misses, their shells blending with its own textured skin. The whale is not just an animal; it is a moving bioreactor, a vital organ in the Engine’s metabolic system.

A school of **cablefish** detours from the conduit it was cleaning to swarm briefly around the Tide-warden’s legs. The Warden is still, but one of its sensory tendrils twitches, releasing a burst of nutrients—waste from its own internal processes—into the water. The fish feast, their metallic scales flashing. This is the covenant: they keep the conduits clear of bio-parasites and electrical silt; they are fed the Engine’s leavings.

The Tide-warden finally turns its head. There is no face, only a smooth, featureless dome with a single, deep lens that glows with the cold, actinic blue of a plasma arc. It sees you—not as a person, but as an anomaly in the data stream. A warm, carbon-based entity with no bioluminescent signature, no tool-nodes, no clear function in the flow. Its judgment is not hostile, merely analytical. You are a variable. The Engine’s primary directive is function. You are not yet a threat to function, but you are not yet a contributor either.

Its head tilts, a gesture that might be curiosity, or might be a scan. One of its tool-nodes extends, not towards you, but into the air between you. A fine mist sprays from its tip—not water, but the **Working Brine** itself, rich with conductive salts and nutrients. The droplets hang in the pressurized water around you, catching the light. It is offering you the medium. To touch it, to let it seep into your clothes, your skin, would be to accept the first layer of integration. It would make you part of the conductive matrix. It would make you readable.

The roar of the waterfall is constant. The Engine’s heart-thrum vibrates in your bones. The blue light and the amber light war on your face. The Tide-warden waits, patient as geology. In the Littoral Engine, to be alive is to serve. The question it silently asks is not *Who are you?* but ***What do you maintain?***
