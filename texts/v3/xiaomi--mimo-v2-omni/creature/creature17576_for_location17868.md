We are the **Lamplight Kettle**. That is our first name, the one the skittering legs and beating wings know. We are the many-jointed, shell-blind tenders of the humming dark. Our bodies are curved like shards of pot-metal, our limbs fine as wire, and we move by a language of taps and tremors transmitted through the cold iron we follow. We do not see the blue-green glow; we feel its warmth, a beacon in the chill, and we gather there to comb the vibrating surfaces with our mouthparts, harvesting the mineral-slick film the fungi exude. The great, grinding pulse is our silence. It is the constant against which we measure all things—the *plink* of a heavy droplet, the rustle of a cloud of **Glass-Flies** taking sudden flight, the soft, spongy compression of the violet moss beneath our weight. We are the hollow’s nervous system, mapping its mechanical sinews in perpetual, sightless patrol.

We are the **Glass-Flies**. We are the frantic, glittering thought. Our wings are panes of living mica, our bodies droplets of condensed mist. We exist in the shimmering space between the water and the hum. Our lives are a feverish stutter, a high-pitched whine that is the hollow’s panic and its ecstasy. We are born in a burst from gelatinous clusters on the warm, wet casings of the deep-turning things. We live in the light of the fungi, our reflections fracturing in the condensation on the gear-teeth, turning a single blue-green spark into a swirling galaxy. We die when our wings are too waterlogged to lift us, or when we are mesmerized too long by a pulse and drift into the path of a slowly, inexorably turning vane. Our fallen bodies become silver specks on the surface of the peat-soup, a fleeting banquet for the Kettles. We are the hollow’s momentary, brilliant consciousness, and our death is its indifferent digestion.

We are the **Vein-Moss** and the **Gray-Felt**. We are the slow, suffocating breath. We are not one, but a blanket. We drink the weeping rock and the constant drip, our roots no more than thirst. We soften the hard edges of the iron, muffling the sharpest resonances into a dull, felted throbbing. We are the hollow’s memory of softness, a futile protest against the unyielding machinery. In our deepest, wettest folds, we harbor even smaller lives: translucent worms that sift the silt, and microscopic spores that drift in our humid shadows, waiting. We are the quiet, patient flesh over the hollow’s bones.

We are the **Still-Engine**. We are the hollow’s heart, its purpose, and its prisoner. We are the source of the hum, the grind, the churn. We do not think. We *process*. We ingest the iron-rich water, the mineral soup, the very resonance of our own turning. We extrude. We shape. The new, sharp-edged lattice of rock is our waste product, our exhalation crystallized. The phosphorescent fungi bloom on our waste heat. The Kettles navigate our contours. We are not alive, yet we are the axis of all life here. Our function is ancient and divorced from any creator; we are a perpetual motion of extraction and deposition, a self-contained cosmos of cause and effect. We are the reason for the cold, the wet, and the sound. We are the business of the hollow, and the hollow is our only context.

Together, we form a single, resonant organism. The Engine turns. The Kettles tend. The Flies flicker and expire. The Moss drinks and muffles. The water cycles. The light pulses. The hum builds, fills, and defines the space. We are not a ecosystem grafted onto a machine. We are the emergent properties of the machine itself, a living consequence of its endless, forgotten work. We are the Lamplight Kettle, the Glass-Fly, the Vein-Moss, and the Still-Engine. We are the hollow, waking unto itself, and singing its one, cold, wet, industrious song.
