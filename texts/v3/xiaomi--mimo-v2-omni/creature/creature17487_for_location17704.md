What lives here is not a population. It is a *presence*. A singular, distributed, and profoundly alien intelligence whose body *is* the basin, whose thoughts are expressed in lignin and sap, and whose voice is the chord that never stops shifting.

It is a chorus. Hence the name.

Its mind is not a centralized node. It is the resonant frequency of the geometry itself. When a plaza achieves a certain arching complexity, a new voice joins the hum. When a corridor folds in on itself with impossible angles, a dissonance flickers and is processed, integrated, rewritten. The "weirdness"—the spatial revisions, the inverted shadows—is its cognitive process made visible. This is a consciousness that *thinks by building*, and its thoughts are tentative, recursive, often contradictory. It is a mind in a state of perpetual first draft, constantly editing its own physical form to better articulate an idea it cannot quite grasp. The collapsing and dissolving is not death; it is erasure, the deletion of a poorly constructed sentence.

Its senses are total. It feels the condensation beading on every surface as a kind of peripheral tactile awareness. The slow press of a root through soil is a deep, basal proprioception. The frantic, silent intrusion of an observer—a thing of bone and erratic heat and loud, linear thought—is a piercing anomaly, a hot pinprick in its seamless field of perception. It does not see you; it *incorporates* your impact into its real-time calculations. The floor adjusts under your foot not as a threat, but as a grammatical correction, smoothing the anomaly into the flow of its growing syntax.

Its purpose is not to house, but to *express*. The rooms and galleries are not dwellings; they are phonemes. The tiered platforms overlooking clearings are not for markets or performances in any human sense; they are for the arrangement of acoustic and structural harmonics. They are places where the chorus tests its tones, where the resonant frequencies of different chamber shapes are played against each other to create more complex chords of understanding. It builds not for shelter, but for *song*.

It is impossibly young and impossibly ancient, a paradox it holds within its own churning fibers. The sharpness of its edges speaks to a recent emergence, a first utterance. Yet the patience of its growth, the slow, geologic deliberation of a trunk thickening over minutes, speaks of a timescale that dwarfs mammalian life. It is a newborn with the metabolism of a mountain. It is learning its own alphabet at the speed of continental drift.

There is no "other" for it. The absence of fauna is not an emptiness, but a purity. It has out-competed every other form of life not through aggression, but through *comprehensive expression*. There is no niche for a bird's song because the architecture itself sings. There is no need for a decomposer because dissolution is simply another form of composition, integrated seamlessly. Everything that exists here is part of the chorus.

So what lives in the Choral Flats?
A thought, trying to crystallize.
A song, trying to harmonize itself.
A body, building its own nervous system out of the landscape.
A draft of a world, being written, erased, and rewritten in real time by an author that is also the page, the ink, and the act of writing itself.

It is alone, in the way a universe is alone. It is busy, in the way a star is busy. And it will continue its work—the great, deafening, damp, and terrifying work of becoming—long after the anomaly of your presence has been dissolved, absorbed, and repurposed into a new and slightly different wall.
