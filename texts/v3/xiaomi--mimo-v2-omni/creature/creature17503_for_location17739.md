And then, with the same dispassionate tenderness, I reclaim them. A section of my skin softens, sighs, and draws them back into the churning broth. It is not death. It is digestion. It is the memory of a shape returning to the dreamer.

And then the work begins again—the slow, meticulous sifting, the chemical persuasion, the coaxing of that avalanche back into the local geometry, back into the weeping seams and needle-fields. I build to unbuild, to refine the parameters of my own being.

They are a brief, warm geometry walking my plains, and I am the endless, hot, and silent becoming that will eventually claim them, too, back into the raw and shimmering feedstock of creation.
