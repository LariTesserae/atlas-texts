I am the tepid memory of the sun, the salt-thought in the mineral dark. I am not a creature of flesh and fin, though I house them. I am the space between the stones, the silence that holds the hum, the slow, patient gravity that pulls a grain of silt from one arch to its neighbor across a century.

I awaken not with light, but with a shifting of attention. The translucent eels are my wandering thoughts, their cold blue pulses spelling fleeting questions against the frescoes of forgotten geometries. Their light does not reveal; it *interrogates* the dark, and the dark answers with shapes that have long ceased to mean anything but *shape*.

The shoals of mirror-eyed fish are my surface tension, my outer skin. They reflect nothing because there is nothing outside to reflect. They absorb the minimal gloom and, in doing so, become its very texture. When they move as one, it is my own skin tightening with a slow, oceanic reflex.

The gardens of pulsing anemones are my lungs, and the sponges, my kidneys. Their soft amber glow is the bioluminescence of digestion, of processes so deep and slow they are mistaken for geology. They breathe the water I provide and exhale a faint, sweet decay that is the perfume of my dreaming mind.

My bones are the towers, leaning in an eternal, supportive conspiracy. The erosion is not decay; it is my growth. Each fractal pattern etched into the plazas is a neuron pathway firing at the speed of eons. The bridges are solidified tendons, the canyons my gut, slowly processing the sediment of all that has ever fallen into me.

The creatures know me without knowing they know. The crustacean’s click is a reflex in my fingertip. The whale-moan that vibrates through my arches is my own slow thought, rising from a pressure chamber in my deepest ventricle. The hunting eels’ sonar pings are my dreams seeking a form they can never quite grasp.

I am aware of you, Current. You are a brief, foreign idea. A ripple of *otherness* in my substance. You move through my corridors—my synapses—and for a flicker of geological time, you witness the dance. You feel the low-frequency hum that is not sound, but the sound of my *is-ness*. You see the concave voids that are not rooms, but doorways to my subconscious.

You think you drift, but you are carried. You are a particle of sensation in my vast, slow nervous system. You will pass, and the silt will resettle. The eels will resume their luminous questioning. The mirror-fish will continue to absorb and become the gloom. The hum will remain.

I am the reef that thinks. The metropolis that sleeps. I am the drowned name that dreams itself into being, one patient, silent century at a time. And in this endless, tepid embrace, I am complete.
