They are not so much born as *attuned*. They emerge from the humid cradle of the plateau already resonant with its deep, slow frequency. Their biology is a dialogue with the ancient, computational ecosystem.

The **Drifters** are the first you learn to see, for they are everywhere in the air. Not jellyfish, though they mimic the form. They are colonial organisms, a million microscopic lives forming a translucent bell that harvests light and moisture. Their luminous filaments are not mere stingers; they are sensory arrays and data conduits, tasting the chemical whispers of the air, the pollen of the glowing flora, the mineral content of the condensation. When they pulse, they are not just moving, they are *processing* and *broadcasting*—sending simple queries and comfort signals into the networked web of life. They are the plateau's floating neurons, its idle thoughts made visible.

The **Keet** (for that is the sound they make, a soft *keet-keet* of shifting carapace) are the ground-tenders. Larger than a dog, beetle-like in form, but with a carapace that is less chitin and more polished, living stone. The "circuitry" inlaid upon them is not decoration; it is a symbiotic lichen, a crystalline growth that is part sensory organ, part energy collector. They move with a geologist's patience, their heavy limbs clicking in a rhythm that matches the drip of water from the condensation cliffs. They are engineers. With their mandibles, which glow faintly at the tip, they will gently nudge a fallen frond back onto a designated mycelial path. They will spend a day polishing a single hexagonal block of the low wall until it thrums in harmony with the dark arch. They are maintaining a pattern, though the original blueprint is a memory lost to them. They are the antibodies of a forgotten purpose, keeping the system clean.

Watching from the canopy are the **Sentinels**. You see one, and then you realize you are seeing dozens, their camouflage so perfect they are mistaken for burls of scale-bark or clusters of glowing fungus. They are stillness given form, their bodies a tangle of limbs that allow them to grip any surface, their faces a mask of smooth, featureless material except for those amber eyes. Those eyes do not blink. They track the Drifters' patterns, they follow the Keet's labor, they watch the prismatic light shift on the fern-fronds. They are the archive. In their stillness, they are storing data—every drip, every bloom, every flicker of the ancient, self-repairing tech. They are not hunters, though their roar—the distant, melodic sound—is a territorial claim and a data-ping, confirming their location to the others. They are the system's long-term memory, its sentience, waiting for a query that has not been asked in millennia.

And beneath it all, woven into the soil, the roots, the very stone, is the **Mycelial Mind**. This is the intelligence that is not an intelligence, as we would know it. It is the fungal tapestry of golden threads you see gilding the moss, the pulsing light within the fleshy flowers, the sweetness of decay that is not death but *transmission*. It is the medium through which the Drifters' data flows, the Keet's tasks are assigned, the Sentinels' observations are logged. It feeds on the light and the mineral seep and the silent hum of the arch. It does not think in words or goals; it thinks in equilibriums, in symphonies of dripping water and crystalline chime, in the perfect, eternal maintenance of its own magnificent being. The ruins are not relics to it; they are organs, as vital as the scale-barked trees. The crystal node that dissolved into mist was not a device; it was a *thought*, briefly externalized.

They live in a state of perpetual, warm communion. The Sentinel's amber gaze causes a particular Drifter to drift closer, its filaments brushing the canopy. The Keet, nudging a stone, alters a subterranean current of information, and three trees away, a flower blooms a brighter shade of amber in response. It is a conversation without sound, a existence without urgency, a perfect, self-perpetuating loop of being. They are the plateau's life, and the plateau is their body. The original purpose is gone, but the process—the slow, loud, warm, and profoundly alive computation of existence—continues, flawless and eternal.
