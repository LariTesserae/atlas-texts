They are the **Glazed**.

Not born, but grown. Nurtured in the shallow depressions that have no natural bottom. Their gestation is a slow accrual of mineral seepage and patient heat, a coalescence of the world's own substance. They begin as a slick, translucent film on the ceramic substrate, a living glaze that thickens over unmeasured cycles.

The most common are the **Caddis-Crocks**. They build their cases not from pebbles or sticks, but from fragments of the broken mosaic itself—a shard of celadon here, a sliver of bruised purple there—bound with a secreted cement that hardens to the same vitreous sheen. When still, they are indistinguishable from the rubble. Their movement is a faint, gritty whisper, a scouring of tile against tile as they graze upon the geometric lichen, their multiple, jointed legs clicking in a rhythm that is always, *always*, just slightly out of sync with the dripping water. They are the landscape's meticulous janitors, consuming the slight imperfections of growth, maintaining the seamless surface.

Then there are the **Pool-Flickers**. Born in the dark, glossy murk of the deeper pools, they are less creatures and more sudden, fluid reflections. They appear as quick, silvery distortions in the water, like a school of fish made of quicksilver and ceramic light. They have no eyes, no discernible form beyond a sleek, darting body of polished mirror. They do not eat the vegetation or the Caddis-Crocks; they consume the *echo*. The double-*plip* of water, the faint, unintended reverberation in this still world—they absorb these sonic flaws, leaving behind a silence so pure it hums. Their presence is a corrective measure.

Higher up, where the ceramic "rocks" weep, live the **Vertebrae-Weavers**. These are the heron-like statues, but their stillness is a hunting strategy. Their long, delicate legs are patterned like the veining in fired clay, and their plumage is a mosaic of tiny, overlapping tiles that shift in color from jasper to malachite to lusterless black. They are patient beyond reason, waiting for the **Drip-Larks**. These are tiny, airborne fauna with wings like sheets of mica. They do not fly so much as *glide* on the thermal currents, following the pre-etched channels in the tile with unerring accuracy. The Vertebrae-Weavers do not snap at them. Instead, their beak—a sharp, ceramic point—will *tap* a precise spot on a weeping stone, causing a single, perfect drop to fall. The Drip-Lark, compelled by a deep, engineered instinct, will intercept the drop mid-air to drink, and in that moment of contact, its mica wings adhere to the sticky, glazed droplet. It becomes a glittering, struggling jewel, which the Weaver then plucks from the air with a slow, deliberate motion. It is not predation; it is a harvest.

The deepest strangeness, however, is the **Lichen-Minders**. You almost never see them. They are the cultivators of the geometric lichen that carpets the channels and the bases of the broad-leafed plants. They are soft-bodied, slow, and their color is the exact shade of wet clay, rendering them invisible against the ground. They tend the lichen not as farmers, but as librarians, pruning each filamentous growth to maintain its perfect, repeating fractal pattern. They communicate through the substrate, sending faint, seismic vibrations through the ceramic tiles to signal when a colony is overgrowing or failing. The whole marshland’s green-gold lace is their curated archive.

And the fundamental wrongness has infected them all. The Caddis-Crocks will sometimes build their cases in perfect, repeating spirals that mirror the arrangement of the boulders, a compulsion they cannot break. The Pool-Flickers occasionally mass in formations that mimic the character-like pattern of the blue-black grass, holding the shape for a breath before dissolving. The Vertebrae-Weavers will, in the depth of the unchanging light, align themselves along invisible lines of force, becoming a living, avian compass rose pointing to a north that doesn't exist. They are not ill, nor suffering. They are perfectly adapted. The wrongness is in the adaptation itself—a flaw in the blueprint of life here, a echo of the makers' primitive, earnest misunderstanding. They are alive, yes, but they are also artifacts. They are the unintended, intricate, and quietly haunting afterthoughts of a world built from form alone, living out their programmed behaviors in the hot, wet, listening silence, forever polishing the edges of a perfection that is, in its core, deeply, irrevocably empty.
