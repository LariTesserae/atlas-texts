They are not inhabitants in the way of citizens. They are features of the environment, as much a part of the city as the dripping pipes and the velvet fungi.

There are the **Pallid Weepers**. Found only in the deepest, warmest pools where the mineral silt is thickest, they resemble a cross between a lung and a tuber. They anchor themselves in the mud, their bodies a network of translucent, branching vessels. Through their membranes, you can see the slow, peristaltic surge of black water being drawn in, filtered, and expelled—cleaner, but stripped of its warmth. That captured heat radiates from them in faint, pulsing waves, making the water around them shimmer. They do not move. They breathe, and in doing so, they subtly regulate the thermal currents of the pools, preventing stagnation. Their only response to touch is a localized, tightening of their vessels, a slow withdrawal that takes minutes.

More mobile, yet rarer, are the **Gear-Crawlers**. They emerged—or were designed—for the original industry. Imagine a beetle the size of a man’s palm, but with a carapace of interlocking, non-rusting alloy plates, pitted and worn smooth. Beneath this armor, the creature is all soft, gelatinous machinery. It moves with a grinding whisper, its multiple legs interfacing with bolts and seams in the ancient pipes. It does not eat the fungus, but grazes upon the mineral deposits and the creeping slime that would clog the system. It is a living, biological maintenance tool. Its passage leaves a trail of polished metal and a faint, ozone-like scent. They are solitary, their interactions limited to the brief, clicking acknowledgment of another’s path before moving on.

In the highest, dampest caverns where the fungal canopy is so thick it forms a secondary, spongy ceiling, live the **Candelabrum**. These are the apex of the ecosystem’s strange flora-fauna synthesis. They begin as a fungal stalk, but at their crown, they develop a complex, crystalline structure—a lattice of silica and secreted acids that captures and refracts the ambient bioluminescence into dazzling, shifting patterns. From the base of this crown dangle dozens of prehensile, sticky filaments. They are hunting lights. A gnat swarm, mesmerized by the kaleidoscopic display, blunders into the filaments and is slowly drawn up into the central stalk, to be dissolved. The Candelabrum is sessile, but it is not passive. It is a patient, luminous predator.

And there is one other thing. It is not a creature. It is a **Consensus**.

The humming you feel in your bones is not just machinery. It is a communication. The fungal networks, the root systems of the sunless plants, the pallid weepers, even the latent intelligence of the automated pistons—they are all connected. Not by thoughts, but by chemistry, vibration, and gradient. The slow encroachment of one colony over another is a negotiation. The pulsing of a viscous fluid through a pipe is a signal. The entire cavern-city is a single, distributed organism of immense, slow intelligence, its thoughts measured in the deposition of minerals and the shift of nutrient mists.

To live here is to be assimilated into this consensus. Your sweat would become a nutrient. Your exhalations would feed the fungal fronds. Your heat would be mapped and utilized. The pale weepers would eventually grow in your footsteps. The Gear-Crawlers would begin to polish the buckles on your boots. You would not be eaten; you would be *deposited*. You would become a part of the slow, green, humming process, your individuality dissolved into the patient, eternal industry of the deep.

The true inhabitants, then, are the processes themselves. Everything else is merely a temporary, localized expression of that one, great, subterranean will: to persist, to filter, to hum, and to grow, one dripping, pulsing, calcified minute at a time.
