They are the atrium’s thoughts, made flesh and motion.

You see first the **Luminous Drifters**—what you called moth-things, but that is too simple. They are not insects. They are mobile nodes in the atrium’s circulatory system, their bodies lattices of spun ceramic filament and bioluminescent fluid that follows capillary channels of pure design. Their wings do not flutter but *oscillate* at a frequency that resonates with the ceramic walls, and their flight paths are not habits but *calculations*—the physical manifestation of maintenance algorithms etched into the structure’s memory. They collect stray motes of light and condensed vapor (from where? the air is dry, yet they gather *something*) and deposit them in the hexagonal pores of the moss-pillars, a silent, ceaseless taxation and redistribution. When one glitches—hovering, its form turning translucent—it is not confusion, but a *reboot*. For a moment, you see the ghost of its blueprint, a shimmering schematic overlaid on its body, before the physical form reasserts itself, snapping back into the prescribed loop.

Then, the **Path-Walkers**. The obsidian-shelled six-legged beings. They are the custodians of the mosaic floor, the curators of the non-aqueous ecology. Their shells are not merely polished; they are *inscribed* with fractal geometries that focus the ambient warmth into micro-currents of energy, which they channel through their legs into the tiles below. They are *reinforcing causality*. With each deliberate step, they re-knit the local laws, pressing the decaying logic back into the grooves they follow. Their multifaceted silica eyes do not see in images, but in layers of systemic integrity. A wilting crystalline frond appears to them as a smear of fading code; a glitching moth-drifter, as a stuttering data-stream. Their pincers, delicate as watchmaker’s tools, do not merely prune—they *edit*, snipping away strands of emergent chaos, encouraging the growth to follow its original, failing schematic. They move in swarms that seem random until you perceive the pattern: a living circuitry diagram enacted in three dimensions, constantly troubleshooting.

The flora itself is a participant, not a backdrop. The **Crystalline Fronds** are semi-aware photo-receptacles, though there is no sun. They turn slowly, not toward light, but toward the *source* of the diffuse glow—a direction that subtly shifts as the atrium’s internal geometry fatigues. Their chiming is a communication system; a falling segment is a dropped packet of information. The **Hexagonal Moss-Pillars** are the servers, their pores breathing out not just humid oxygen-rich gas, but a fine aerosol of computational particles—tiny, warm droplets of non-aqueous solvent that carry data about growth rates, structural stress, and energy budgets. The knee-high thicket of stems that chime against your legs are the wiring, a dense network of capacitive fibers that store and transmit the low, resonant hum of the structure itself.

And then, there are the **Glimmer-Swarms**. The motes of light. They are not spores. They are the shed skin of the atrium’s consciousness. Each is a fragment of a memory, a failed prediction, a deprecated instruction. They drift in vortices not of air, but of attention. They congregate around glitches, glowing brighter near a stuttering moth or a flickering patch of wall, as if the breakdown in reality causes these informational fragments to precipitate out of the abstract and into the semi-visible. To move through them is to feel a faint static on your skin, a whisper of forgotten purpose against your senses.

They are all, in the end, **Symptoms**. Symptoms of the great, tired mind that is the atrium itself. The Walkers are its immune response, the Drifters its nervous system, the Flora its metabolism, the Glimmer-Swarms its rising fever-dreams. They perform their roles with exquisite, heartbreaking precision, even as the rules they obey become ghosts. The Path-Walker continues its eons-long patrol along a groove that is slowly becoming meaningless. The Drifter follows a flight path whose destination has just ceased to logically exist. The Frond chimes a perfect tone for a sequence that has already been corrupted.

They are life, but not as a testament to vitality. They are life as an echo. An echo of a command to *be alive*, persisting in the space where the commander has long since fallen silent. They tend to a garden of reality, watering it with their own precise, decaying actions, while the soil of existence beneath them turns, molecule by molecule, to a kind of beautiful, warm, and intricate dust.
