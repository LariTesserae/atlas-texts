At the very bottom of the spire, where the blue light fails and the dripping is loudest, live the **Root-Sifters**. They are not one creature but a shifting consortium: pale, soft-bodied things with vestigial limbs, more like worms with grasping cilia than anything with a spine. They cluster in the black pools, their bodies half-dissolved into a symbiotic mat of bacteria. They do not hunt; they are the living digesters. Every scrap of detritus—a shed carapace, a still-twitching limb, a drowned Glimmerswimmer—drifts down to their domain. The Sifters envelop it, their collective bodies secreting a milky enzyme soup that reduces solid matter to a nutrient-rich sludge, which they then absorb through their skin. They are the final, formless stage of all things, the soft underbelly of this hard, chitinous world.

Scaling the sheer walls just above the perpetual dark, you find the **Lithica**: the true people of the Spire, if such a word can apply. They are not arthropods, nor mollusks, but something in between—a branch of evolution that chose hardening over fleeing. A Lithica is about the size of a large crab, but upright, with a heavy, calcified carapace the colour of wet slate. Six limbs end in precise, three-fingered claws. The lower two are stout, for gripping the vertical world; the upper four are delicate, tipped with chitinous tools they hone against the stone. Their heads are a smooth, forward-curving plate, housing two clusters of simple, dark eyes and a mouthparts of intricate, interlocking plates that can rasp stone or perform the most delicate surgery on a captured vein of Gleam-Moss.

The Lithica have culture. Not art, not stories, but *practice*. They are the gatherers, the engineers, the surgeons of the ecosystem. With painstaking care, they prune the Gleam-Moss, stimulating its growth in desired patterns to illuminate new ledges or mark safe pathways. They cultivate specific, less-aggressive strains of smaller creatures in "herds"—filter-feeding insects they protect from Skittering Veils in exchange for a share of their bounty. They use the resinous nodules excreted by the Stone-Carious larvae as a kind of glass, setting them into sockets on their carapaces to focus the moss-light into beams, or using them as cutting lenses. They do not speak with sound, for sound is the endless, confusing roar of the Spire. Instead, they communicate with *tap*: a precise, rhythmic language of claws striking stone, a semaphore that can convey urgency, location, and complex instruction across vast vertical distances.

Their most sacred and brutal task is the **Tending of the Stone-Carious**. These immense, pale grubs—the size of ancient tree trunks—are their charge and their gods. The Lithica tend to their tunnels, clearing debris, reinforcing passages with resin, and even carefully carving guide-grooves to direct the grub’s consumption, shaping the very geology of their world over generations. In return, they harvest the resin, and from the grub’s molts, they obtain vast sheets of flexible chitin they use as shelter, as rafts on the black pools, and as armor. But to be a Lithica is to live with the knowledge that one day, a grub will turn its slow, mineral-melting appetite upon a chamber you call home. The Tending is a covenant of mutual consumption.

At the mid-levels, in the chaotic sapphire gloom, reign the **Veil-Lords**. They are not Skittering Veils, but the evolved apex of a different arachnid line. A Veil-Lord is a creature of terrifying, elegant economy. Its body is a flattened, disc-like carapace, no larger than a man’s hand, but from it extend eight legs of impossible length and thinness, like spun glass. They do not scuttle; they flow, moving with a silent, liquid grace that makes them near-invisible until they strike. They live alone, claiming a vertical territory of sheer wall. The true artistry is their web. It is not a trap, but a sensory net of profound sensitivity, strung across a wide area and connected to their legs. Every vibration—the drip of water, the skitter of a Veil, the fall of a stone—is analyzed. And the web is alive, seeded with colonies of a predatory, carnivorous Gleam-Moss that stings and paralyzes anything that touches it. The Veil-Lord does not chase; it waits until the web sings with a certain frequency of struggle, then glides out to envenom its prey with a painless, liquefying bite. They are the patient, geometric gods of the vertical hunt.

And in the deepest, most narrow chasms, where the stone walls hum with a subsonic vibration, dwell the **Echo-Weavers**. They are blind, and beyond blind—their eyes have become resonance chambers. Shaped like elongated, armoured beetles, they tap the stone with specialized mandibles, not to eat, but to listen to the return echo. They map the entire micro-geography of a fissure, the location of a Stone-Carious’s gut, the thin wall about to collapse, all through sound. They use this not just to navigate, but to *communicate*. A complex series of taps and listens forms a shared, three-dimensional mental map of the Spire’s interior, maintained by a colony. They are the miners, the seismologists. When a Stone-Carious grub moves, the Echo-Weavers feel the shift in the stone’s song, and their colony streams away in a silent, coordinated wave, tapping warnings to each other, moving through fissures too narrow for any predator to follow. They are the Spire’s memory, its nervous system, recording the slow, geological thoughts of the world in the pattern of their taps.

Life here is a perfect, crushing machine. The Lithica tend the moss and the grubs. The grubs reshape the stone. The Veil-Lords cull the weak. The Root-Sifters consume the dead. The Echo-Weavers listen to it all, and remember. There is no malice, no joy, no ambition. There is only the vertical, the dark, the damp, and the relentless, luminous cycle of consumption and renewal. To be born here is to be a cell in a vast, cold, and glittering organism. Your purpose is etched in your carapace, felt in your taps, and, eventually, dissolved in the milky dark.
