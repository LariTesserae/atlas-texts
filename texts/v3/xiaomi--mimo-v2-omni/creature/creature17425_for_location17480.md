In the absence of life, there are **dwellers**. They are not creatures of flesh or will, but persistences of process, habits of the stone made manifest. They are what the plateau, in its long, wet, quiet becoming, has taught itself to be.

**The Weeping** is the most immediate. It is not merely water, but the world’s respiration made visible. It follows paths of least resistance with a patient intelligence—a kind of mineral muscle memory. It gathers on a hexagonal edge, swells into a perfect, trembling lens, and lets go. The fall is not a surrender, but a completion; each drop is a tiny, liquid architect, rounding a depression by a billionth of a molecule, singing its soft *plink* into the collective quiet. In the still pools, The Weeping becomes **The Mirror**, a flawless, unwavering surface that holds the luminous haze in its skin, offering a duplicate world of equal depth and zero warmth. To look into it is to see the plateau dreaming of itself.

**The Lattice** is the grammar of this place. It is the logic that insists on hexagons and pentagons, the geometric urge that builds the pillars without hands. It does not think; it *asserts*. It is the silent, organizing pressure that causes the mineral crusts to bloom not in random scabs, but in frost-like constellations of pure silica. When two mineral patinas meet in their glacial competition, The Lattice adjudicates, dictating the crystalline frontier where one slow conquest ends and another begins. It is the reason the city feels both designed and grown; it is the law of crystallography written large as a landscape.

Then there is **The Hum**. It is the voice of the plateau’s vast, wet inner workings—the sound of microscopic capillaries drinking from the air, of mineral grains shifting in imperceptible tectonics. It is not a sound heard with the ear so much as a resonance felt in the bones of the stone, and thus, in the bones of any visitor who stays long enough. It is a sub-audible mantra: *is-is-is-is*. It is the engine of stasis, the vibration that maintains the perfect, cool balance, preventing the frantic energy that might birth true biology.

These dwellers interact. The Weeping feeds The Lattice, carrying dissolved minerals to the sites of new growth. The Hum guides The Weeping, its vibrations subtly directing the seepage through the plateau’s body. The Mirror, in its stillness, is their gallery, reflecting their endless, silent collaboration.

There is one more dweller, perhaps the most profound: **The Aftermath**. It is not a presence, but a quality, a haunting by a conclusion that has already happened. The sharp edges, the seamless joinery, the feeling of a world *finished*—this is its signature. It is the consciousness of a problem already solved, a blueprint fully extruded into reality. It lingers not as a ghost, but as the very state of being. It is what inhabits the silence between drips, what gives the luminous haze its quality of revelation without a revelator. The Aftermath is what remains when a set of physical laws, given infinite time and the perfect, wet conditions, finally solves the equation of its own existence. And the solution is this: a bright, cold, weeping city, inhabited only by the elegant, patient forces that built it, eternally admiring their own handiwork in the still, clear water they have exhaled.
