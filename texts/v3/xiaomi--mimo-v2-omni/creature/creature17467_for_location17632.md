What lives in the Sump is a function made flesh, a population of answers to questions long rotted away. They are not inhabitants; they are components, self-replicating and self-repairing, evolved to tend the great, dead God-machine that is their world.

They begin as a dust. A living, gritty sediment of bacteria and micro-animals that settles on every surface, a slow-feeding film that digests the leached minerals and chemical whispers from the structure. It is the foundation of the world, the moss that grows on a steel mountain. Upon this dust feed the **Glass-sippers**, schools of eyeless fish no longer than a thumb. They are translucent, their internal organs visible as dark, pulsating knots. They move in shimmering, coordinated clouds, guided not by sight but by the electromagnetic hum of the Sump and the minute currents against their lateral lines. They sip the bacterial film, and in their constant, darting grazing, they clean the conduits, preventing clogs in the ancient, unseen filters. They are the Sump's white blood cells.

Then there are the **Lithophages**. These are the size of a human hand, their bodies low-slung and armored in plates of black chitin that seem to absorb the blue-green gloom. They do not graze; they mine. With mandibles of startling crystalline hardness, they scrape at mineral deposits and hardened biofilm in rhythmic, scraping choruses. They are found on the vaulted ceilings, on the sheer walls, always working against the slow accretion. Their waste is a fine, inert powder that settles into the silt below. They are the Sump's dentists, its exfoliants, ensuring the smooth, worn patina of the world does not choke its own veins.

The central chambers are the domain of the **Pallid Siphons**. These are the larger things, the drifting shapes. They are vast, slow, translucent creatures, like living jellyfish the size of small boats, but with a subtle, internal architecture of fibrous muscles and gas bladders. Their "flesh" is a membrane that filters the water itself, extracting nutrients and the precious, trace elements of life from the flow. From their core dangle lures—not of light, but of a localized, hypnotic pressure change. Smaller fish, including the Glass-sippers, are drawn to these eddies, sometimes becoming ensnared in sticky filaments, consumed not by a mouth but by cellular absorption across the Siphon's skin. They are the Sump's living filters, its passive harvesters, and their slow, pulsating drift is the closest the ecosystem comes to a heartbeat.

But the true custodians, the ones that walk the narrow maintenance catwalks and peer into sealed junctions, are the **Eyes**. They are not one species, but a guild. Multi-limbed, chitinous creatures with carapaces that mimic the corroded metal—a mottling of rust-red and verdigris. Their heads are dominated by clusters of simple, pressure-sensitive orbs and chemoreceptive pits. They do not speak. They communicate through modulated clicks and the tapping of their limbs on the metal, a complex language of resonance that carries for miles through the structure.

The Eyes are the maintenance crew. They follow routes as fixed as a circuit, inspecting the groaning seams of pressure doors, cleaning intake valves with precise, practiced scrapes of their forelimbs, and replacing worn-out bacterial mats in designated bio-reactor niches. They are drawn to the sound of malfunction—the specific harmonic of a failing bearing, the irregular drip of a compromised seal. Upon detection, a team of Eyes will converge. They work with silent, efficient coordination, their multi-tool limbs disassembling panels, extracting crystalline growths that serve as replacement parts from their own cultivated gardens, and re-sealing the breach with a secretion that hardens into a perfect polymer.
