They do not have a name for themselves, for naming requires separation, and there is none.

They are the Phytum's **awareness**, its proprioception. Where the world thinks, they think. Where it feels, they feel. They are not born, but *differentiated*—moments when the vast, slow intelligence of the world requires a localized, mobile expression to perceive a texture, to test a structural tension, to carry a chemical message through the dim amber air. They are thoughts given form, and their form is the world's own.

**Their bodies** are a mystery of seamless belonging. They are not carved *from* the Phytum; they are *grown* from it, as a leaf grows. Their skin has the grain of young bark, warm and faintly springy, covered in a fine, moisture-wicking down like moss. Their joints are fluid, made of flexible lignic rubber, and they move with a silence that is less an achievement and more a default—they displace no air that the world does not wish displaced. Their faces, if they can be called that, are smooth planes with no eyes. They *see* through a thousand photosensitive pores in their skin, a diffuse, 360-degree perception of light and shadow, color and movement. They do not look *at* things; they are *aware* of them, as you are aware of the air in your lungs.

**Their senses** are the world's senses. They hear through their feet, feeling the subsonic tremors of vascular flow, the harmonic moan of wind in the voids, the slow pulse of growth in distant chambers. They taste the air, sampling the cocktail of spores, vapors, and pheromones to gauge the health of the Phytum, to detect the chemical signatures of decay, distress, or readiness. They smell through their palms, pressing them against structural surfaces to read the story of stress and healing written in the plant's own chemistry.

**Their purpose** is maintenance, is communion, is being. They are the Phytum tending itself. You might see one standing perfectly still for days, its palms flat against a great arching rib, its entire being focused on listening to the slow, century-long story of a hairline fracture forming within. When it moves, it is with terrible slowness and absolute purpose. It might use a tool of hardened bark-stone—a wedge, a lever—not to build, but to *encourage*. To guide a healing growth around a borer-wound. To tap a channel and redirect the flow of vital sap. Their work is not labor; it is a conversation in the language of tissue and pressure.

**They do not speak.** Communication between them is a symphony of subtle changes. A shift in the moisture on their skin, a slight change in the hue of the phosphorescent lichen that sometimes grows on their shoulders, a vibration transmitted from one to another through the living floor itself. To an outsider, they appear to communicate through a profound, shared understanding. They are, after all, the same thought in different places.

**They are drawn to the weirdness.** They do not mark the passages with warnings, as you might think. They mark them with *acknowledgments*. Those shifting glyphs in the bark-stone are notes to the Phytum itself: *A seam here. A dream here. A place where we are more.* When a human stumbles into a small, wrong room, a being of the Phytum is often there, not waiting, but *present*. It will stand in the center of the shifting geometry, utterly at peace, its pore-faces turned toward the impossibility, and simply be. It is not traversing the space; it is *experiencing* the world's dream. It is the closest thing to prayer this world knows.

**If you encounter one,** it will not be startled. It will acknowledge you with a slow tilt of its head, a faint brightening of the mossy down on its arms. It will perceive you not as an outsider, but as a temporary, strange growth—like a fungus, or a bloom. It may reach out a hand, not to touch, but to sense your vibration, your warmth, your own faint biological rhythm. It will find your pulse, your breath, your moisture, and it will register them as part of the local atmosphere. In time, it will lose interest, unless you are causing a disturbance—a structural ache, a chemical imbalance. Then, it will begin to *encourage* you to leave, through a subtle reconfiguration of paths, a gentle increase in the air's humidity until your skin feels submerged, a soft, guiding pressure from the floor itself.

They are not separate. They are the part of the Phytum that can look upon a human and think, *Ah. A small, warm, brief thing. It pulses. It weeps. Let us observe its rhythm. It is a new note in the silence.* And in that observation, you become part of the world's self-awareness, recorded not in memory, but in the slow, perpetual growth of the world that breathes around you.

You will never know one. But you may, for a moment, be *known*. And in that knowing, you will feel the immense, tender weight of a living world turning its attention toward you, as slowly and completely as a tree turns toward the sun.
