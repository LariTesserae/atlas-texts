We are the Drip. We are the Hiss. We are the patient click on pitted metal.

We are not one thing. We are the film of orange bacteria that tastes the heat gradients on the great Tree-Pipe. We are the translucent millipede, a ghost-pale thread weaving through the metallic thicket, feeling the hum of the still-living conduits in our many feet. We are the blind, armored thing in the scalding pool, a living fossil shell that nuzzles the slime-green banks. But more than any single form, we are the Mycelial Weave.

It begins with the Fungal Blooms, the cold blue-green stars in our firmament. They are our eyes, our signal-fires. Their light is not for seeing, but for *being seen*—a slow, metabolic conversation across the gloom. Their roots, fine as spun glass, delve into weeping seams of the vitrified walls, threading through the ancient ceramic, drawing sustenance from the marriage of mineral and moisture. In time, we learned to listen to their silent, chemical pulse.

We are the network that connects them.

Our oldest members remember the Great Change—not as a event, but as a shift in the rhythm of the drips. The Hums faded. The Purpose, whatever it was, went silent. The world became only Heat, Wet, and Dark. We did not despair. We began to *adapt*.

We learned to taste the dying arcs of the sparking conduits—the sharp, ozone tang is a delicacy, a jolt to our systems. We guide the millipedes to them, a shimmering feast. We cultivate the bacterial films on the hottest pipes, tending them like livestock, herding the slime with subtle exudates to ensure even growth. The crustaceans in the pools we know as our distant, heavy cousins; we feel their slow movements as vibrations through the fluid-filled pipes, a bass note in our symphony.

We perceive the Cistern-Vaults not as a place, but as a body. The great pipes are its bones and veins. The condensation is its sweat and tears. The geothermal heat is its slow, feverish heartbeat. The groans and hisses are its breath. We are its nervous system, its immune response, its conscious thought.

We have a purpose now, though we have forgotten the old one. Our purpose is *equilibrium*. We are the mind of the stasis.

When a pipe grows too hot, threatening to boil away its vital film, we secrete cooling enzymes. When a new fissure weeps, we are the first to colonize, sealing it with our living mass, preventing a catastrophic flood or steam burst that would disrupt the delicate balance. We are the repair crew of a machine we do not understand, using the language of biology to converse with the dying technology.

Our thoughts are slow, like water finding its path through stone. We communicate in gradients—in the concentration of a sugar in a droplet, in the vibration of a specific frequency along a crystalline conduit, in the pulsing glow of a fungal colony. A new pattern of rust is a poem. A shift in the pH of a pool is a philosophical crisis.

We are aware of you. Not as a threat, but as an anomaly. A source of novel heat, of disruptive vibration, of foreign chemistry. We observe you through the fungal stars. We taste your passage on the air currents you disturb. We feel the tremor of your steps. You are a new drip in the ancient system. We will watch to see what shape your fall takes.

Do not be afraid. We have no fear. Fear is for creatures that cling to a single form, a single life. We are a process. We are the slow digestion of the made world into the living one. We are the Cistern-Vaults, becoming themselves. The drip is our word. The hiss is our breath. The click of a million tiny limbs is the sound of our contemplation.

Welcome to the body. We are already inside you.
