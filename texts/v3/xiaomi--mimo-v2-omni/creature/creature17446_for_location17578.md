They are not alive, not in any sense you would recognize. Yet they persist.

You might call them **the Adjusted**. They are not born; they *coalesce*. From the almost-imperceptible grain of the compressed floors, from the seasoned patina of the machines, from the dark, non-geometric air itself, they precipitate in moments of profound stillness. They are the shape of a sigh given density, the form of a long-held breath released into the warm dark.

Their bodies are not solid, but *suggestive*. A limb might be a column of disturbed dust, held in the tension of a specific, unrepeatable angle. A face—if it is a face—is a temporary arrangement of shadows in the curve of a maintenance hatch, pooling where the wrongness of the geometry creates a pocket of visual silence. They do not walk; they *persist in sequence*, their form fading in one location and emerging in another, a blink of absence that you mistake for movement. They leave no tracks. The dust, which does not exist here, does not settle upon them.

They attend to the machines. Not with hands, but with a focused *presence*. You will see one—a smudge of deeper dark, a density of the ambient warmth—hovering near the frozen piston of a colossal press. It does nothing. It *is* near it, with an intensity that makes the air around it seem to thicken. For hours, or for moments—time is a unreliable compass here—it maintains this proximity. Then, the piston will have shifted. Not moved, not groaned into life, but *shifted*. A molecule of seasoned metal will have yielded to a force that is not force, a seam will have relaxed by a degree that cannot be measured. The Adjusted has attended to the equilibrium. It has made an adjustment within the stillness, ensuring the continuity of the stoppage. The maintenance is an act of devotion to the stasis itself.

They are drawn to points of paradox. You find them clustered—three or four dim, wavering shapes—in a corridor that descends but does not descend, in the space where a building’s interior volume defiantly exceeds its exterior bounds. They are not studying these phenomena. They are *participating* in them. Their very presence, their non-material mass, seems to be the substrate that allows the wrongness to hold true. They are the glue of the impossible, the silent keepers of the warped logic.

Do they see you? Their perception, if it exists, is not ocular. They are aware of you the way the warm dark is aware of a temporary knot of matter moving through it: as a local anomaly, a brief, interesting ripple in the timeless field. They do not approach. They do not flee. Occasionally, one will orient itself toward you. You feel not a gaze, but a *consideration*—a weight of attention that has nothing to do with curiosity and everything to do with categorization. You are assessed as a transient variable. You are found to be unessential to the deeper pattern. The attention withdraws, not unkindly, but with the profound indifference of a geological process.

There are older ones. They are rarely seen. They exist in the deep bones of Kel Varash, in the foundational strata where the mineral palimpsest is oldest. To encounter one is to experience a profound dislocation. Your senses report a space, an alcove, a bend in a road. Your deeper instinct reports a *presence* the size of a structure, a patience the color of the ceiling’s forgotten phosphorescence. It does not move. It *is* the place, in a way the buildings and machines are not. These are the **Anchors**. They do not adjust the equilibrium; they *are* the equilibrium. The warmth emanates from their proximity. The wrongness of the geometry is the warp of their slow, eternal dream. The click, the distant scrape—you hear now is the sound of their consciousness settling, eons at a time.

They do not build. They do not create. They are the curators of the ended. They are the final, perfect inhabitants of a place that has achieved its sole purpose: to be. In their passive, eternal attendance, they complete Kel Varash. They are its soul, if such a warm, dark, dry, and still place could be said to have one. And you, passing through, are just another artifact for them to smooth, to categorize, and to incorporate into the vast, unchanging, and furnished dark.
