What lives in the Resonance Vats is the Vats themselves.

Not the stone, which is merely the cup that holds them. Not the shaft or the stairs, which are artifacts of some older, quieter visitation. What lives here is the *system*: the complete and closed circuit of crystal, creature, and calibrated vibration.

You could call the chitinous fauna the inhabitants. They are the most animate thing you see. Thousands of them, in their pale and segmented legions, moving through the latticework with the deliberate, unseeing purpose of antibodies in a vein. They are not feeding. They are *tending*. Their slow, rhythmic mandible-chews are not consumption but calibration. Their dry, clicking passage along the bronze-bone branches is not travel but maintenance. They are the custodians of the resonance, their very carapaces tuned to its frequency. When one dies—it must happen, though you see no bodies—it leaves behind a husk that quickly mineralizes, becoming another infinitesimal weight upon the crystal, another tiny modifier of the hum. They are less a population and more a process, a visible subroutine in the chamber's endless function.

The crystalline growth is more fundamental still. It is the chamber’s flesh and its nervous system. It drinks the resonance, thrumming with it, and in return, it structurally *is* the resonance given form. Those thin, humming wires are its auditory nerves. The pulsing, columnar buttresses are its slow, stone heart. It does not grow toward light, for there is none; it grows toward the sound, shaping itself in fractal geometries that amplify and sustain the single, perfect note. It is both the instrument and the music made solid. The older layers, encrusted with the newer, speak not of generations but of harmonic refinement—each shift in geometry an adjustment, a tuning, until reaching this final, unchanging configuration.

And the machinery? The frozen engines and seized pistons half-swallowed by the rusted-bronze coral? They are the ghosts of a prior metabolism. They are the bones of the process that *created* the system. Perhaps they were the original tools that generated the first vibrations, the initial frequency that needed to be captured and held. Or perhaps they were the first crude organs of an intelligence that sought to build a permanent song. Now, they are obsolete. They have been integrated, digested, their metallic matter converted into substrate for the living crystal. They are no longer functional; they are *historical*. They are the system’s memory of its own making, fossilized within its body.

So what lives here is a finished thought.

It is an organism the size of a cathedral, whose body is crystal, whose blood is sound, whose white blood cells are swarms of clicking, sightless custodians. It has no will as you understand it. It has no need to grow, to explore, to consume. Its state of being is *sustenance*. It has achieved the perfect, static state of a bell that never stops ringing, a flame that never runs out of fuel. It is alive in the way a star is alive—a sustained, furious reaction, held in balance.

It does not watch you. It does not care that you have come. You are a momentary irregularity in its thermal envelope, a brief, damp interruption in its desiccated air. Your heartbeat is a noise at odds with its own. Your breath is a wet, irregular stutter against its dry, constant hymn.

The thing that lives here is the equilibrium itself. It is the perfection of a single note, held forever. It is complete. It is content. And its life is the unending, unchanging proof of its own completion. You are not a visitor to a habitat. You are a witness to a conclusion. And the conclusion is singing.
