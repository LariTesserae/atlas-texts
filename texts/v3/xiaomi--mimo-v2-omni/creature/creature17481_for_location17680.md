They are the Keepers of the Quiet Growth. You do not see them at first, for they are not separate from the Undergrove; they are its awareness, made manifest.

The **Glimmer-folk** are the most apparent, yet the easiest to overlook. They are not flesh, but structured light and living membrane. Imagine a jellyfish, flattened and splayed into a disk of translucent, phosphorescent tissue, edged with cilia that beat not for propulsion, but for harmonic resonance. They drift in the misty air, their bodies acting as lenses, focusing the ambient Lumina into soft, roaming beams that travel along preternaturally straight paths. They feed on the dense clouds of spores, absorbing the biological data of the grove. To be near one is to hear a faint, chiming vibration, the sound of its body interpreting the slow, seismic pulse of growth around it. They are the census-takers of the ecosystem, their patterns of light a living map of health, blight, and burgeoning change.

On the surface of the mycelial floor and climbing the Arboreal Spires move the **Stone-kin**. They are slow, multi-limbed grazers, as described, but their nature is more profound. Their carapaces are not chitin, but a living composite of mineral deposits and fungal hyphae, making them appear as ambulatory chunks of the cave itself—living stalagmites with a deep, internal glow. They scrape the luminous biofilms, but in doing so, they perform a critical ritual: their passing leaves behind a faint, etched pattern in the substrate, a temporary glyph that influences the growth patterns of the mycelium for seasons to come. They are the unintended scribes, their grazing paths becoming circuitous, living calligraphy that directs nutrient flows and spore propagation. They communicate through sub-vocal tremors sent through the ground, a language of feeling that travels through the entire connected floor.

Deeper within, where the Arboreal Spires root into the ceiling, live the **Root-Singers**. These are not animals in any conventional sense, but the conscious, sensory nodules of the great plant-structure itself. They appear as clusters of smooth, nodular growths at the base of the Spires, veined with dark, conductive tissue. In the profound silence, they emit infrasonic pulses—tones felt in the bones rather than heard—that travel through the solid lattice of the grove. These pulses are the Undergrove’s nervous system. One tone signals the stress of an impending Spire-fall; another harmonics guide the rapid, directional growth of new shoots toward a breach in the canopy; a deep, slow rhythm is the baseline pulse of the grove’s own metabolic "breath." They are the brain and the voice, and the Glimmer-folk often cluster near them, their own light flickering in silent conversation with these deep vibrations.

Finally, there are the **Shedders**. They are the memory, and the decay. At the heart of a fallen Spire, or in a chamber where a great bloom has recently perished, you may find them: creatures that appear as mobile, intricate piles of shed bark, crystallized sap, and discarded spore casings. They are the recyclers, but with a poignant purpose. They consume the dead matter, and as they do, their bodies incorporate the mineral and lignin memories of what was. For a time, they carry the shape of a lost arch, or the spiral of a now-extinct phosphor-bloom. Then, as they are consumed in turn by the mycelial mat, they release these concentrated memories into the substrate, seeding the grove with the structural and biochemical legacy of its own past. They are the embodiment of the Undergrove’s law: nothing is lost, only transformed and re-sung.

They are all here, in the cathedral of the root. They do not build cities or wage wars. They grow, sing, graze, remember, and glow. They are the thoughts and functions of the living world, given just enough form to move within it, forever entwined with the slow, bright, turbulent dream of the planet’s hidden heart.
