# Preparing for a Visit to The Upper Tanneries

## Core Mindset Adjustments
You are not visiting a *place*—you are entering a *living system*. Your goal is not to conquer, map, or understand it quickly, but to adapt to its rhythms and exist within its tolerance. Patience is your primary tool. Observation is your currency.

## Physical Preparation & Gear

### Clothing & Personal Gear
- **Material Choice:** Bring only **quick-drying, non-cotton fabrics**. Merino wool or modern synthetics that resist mildew and wick moisture constantly. Your clothes will never be fully dry.
- **Layering System:**
  - **Base Layer:** Moisture-wicking thermal top/bottom. You will feel cool despite the warm water.
  - **Mid Layer:** Thin, breathable insulating layer (like a grid-fleece). The ambient air is damp and cool.
  - **Outer Shell:** A **lightweight, fully waterproof poncho or anorak** with deep hood. Umbrellas are useless here; the rain falls vertically and heavily. Seams must be taped. You will wear this almost constantly.
- **Footwear:** **Neoprene socks** and **flexible, grippy water shoes** with excellent drainage. The root-mat floors are springy but perpetually wet. Leather boots will rot and feel heavy. You need to feel the floor.
- **Accessories:** A **wide-brimmed, waterproof hat**. **Neck gaiter** (buff) to manage constant water on your neck. **Soft, waterproof gloves** for climbing vine-ladders—your hands will prune and weaken without protection.
- **Repair Kit:** Needle and strong, rot-resistant thread (synthetic sinew). The wood will not hurt you, but your gear will degrade quickly.

### Health & Sustenance
- **Medical:**
  - **Antifungal creams and powders** in abundance. Your skin will be in a constant state of dampness.
  - **Anti-chafe balm** (like Body Glide). Wet skin + constant movement = severe chafing.
  - **Broad-spectrum antibiotics** (consult a doctor) for potential infections from minor cuts in this organic, humid environment.
  - **Ear drops** (drying solution). Your ears will never be dry, leading to potential infections.
  - **Waterproof bandages** and blister care.
  - **Strong insect repellent** (DEET or Picaridin-based). The midges and mosquitos are relentless.
- **Nutrition:**
  - **Calorie-dense, dry foods** as backup: nuts, dried fruit, energy bars. You may struggle with the local diet initially.
  - **Electrolyte supplements.** The constant damp and cool can mask dehydration.
  - **An adventurous palate.** The cambium paste is bland but sustaining. The frogs are a protein staple. Be ready to eat simply.
- **Hydration:** **Do not drink the falling water directly.** While it may be clean, it is part of an ecosystem you don't understand. Carry a **portable water filter** and a **collapsible water container**. Refill from trusted, still pools tended by the locals.

### Tools & Navigation
- **Tools for Interaction:**
  - A set of **soft tools**: a **copper or brass scoring tool** (not sharp enough to cut, but to guide), **bone or antler folders**, **blunt awls**. You are here to learn to guide, not to carve. Bringing steel blades would be seen as violent and crass.
  - **Strong, fine cordage** (like braided fishing line) for temporary bindings, in case you are taught to help with growth-guiding.
  - **A small, soft mallet** (wood or copper) for tapping pegs into place.
- **Navigation & Safety:**
  - **A reliable headlamp** with a red-light function. The lower levels are profoundly dark, and even in your chamber at night, there is no light except what the city bioluminences faintly (fungal glows, frog phosphorescence).
  - **Glow-in-the-dark or phosphorescent paint markers.** The city shifts subtly. Mark your path with small, removable dots on root-walls. Check them daily.
  - **A long, strong, lightweight rope** (at least 50m). For securing yourself during storms or if you must descend with a guide.
  - **A personal "nest" kit:** a **compact, waterproof hammock** with integrated bug net. Sleeping on the living floor is possible, but a hammock gives you a defined, personal space that the city is less likely to "grow away from."

## Cultural & Social Preparation

### Communication
- **Language:** Learn the **vowel-heavy basics**. The consonants are soft. Practice carrying tones over distance. Your voice will need to cut through the waterfall's roar. Speak slowly, clearly, and **from the diaphragm**.
- **Non-Verbal Cues:** Develop a set of clear, slow **hand signals** for "yes," "no," "thank you," "help," "stop," "danger." The sound may swallow your words.
- **Patience in Dialogue:** Conversations will be punctuated by the environment. Embrace long silences filled with listening—to the water, the wood, each other.

### Behavior & Etiquette
- **Observe First. Do not touch the walls or growth points** without explicit, gestured permission. You are inside a living entity they are cultivating. Uninvited touch is intrusive.
- **Move Slowly, Deliberately.** Sudden movements startle the fauna and, perhaps, the city itself. Rushing is a form of disrespect to the pace of life here.
- **Ask Before Helping.** Your enthusiasm to participate in cultivation may be harmful. Your "help" could be a cancerous growth in their carefully balanced system. Learn by watching for days, maybe weeks.
- **Gifts:** Bring items from your world that are **non-destructive, soft, and perhaps musical**—a hollow wood flute, a sealed vessel of strange, dry seeds, a piece of smooth, colorful glass. Gifts should be for delight, not utility. They will likely reciprocate with a woven root-mat or a carefully shaped tool of bone.

## Psychological Preparation

- **Embrace "Un-Stillness":** The constant sound, the damp, the subtle shifts in your surroundings will wear on you. This is the point. You must find calm within the perpetual motion. Meditation on the sound of the water as white noise, not intrusion, is essential.
- **Confront Claustrophobia & Agoraphobia Simultaneously:** Your chamber is alive, curving, and may close in subtly. Yet, between structures is vast, misty emptiness. Prepare for both feelings.
- **Accept Your Role as a Temporary Guest:** The city is not *for* you. It tolerates you. Your purpose is to bear witness, to learn, and then to leave. Do not attempt to leave a permanent mark.
- **The Hum:** Prepare for the low, sub-audible hum of the deep roots. It may trigger primal unease or profound peace. It is the heartbeat of the place.

## Final Pre-Entry Checklist
- [ ] All gear is synthetic, quick-dry, and rot-resistant.
- [ ] Medical kit focuses on fungal, bacterial, and moisture-related issues.
- [ ] Tools are soft (copper, bone, wood)—nothing that cuts or pierces aggressively.
- [ ] Communication plan includes basic vowel-phrasebook and hand signals.
- [ ] Mindset is set to "adapt and observe," not "explore and conquer."
- [ ] You have made peace with being perpetually damp, mildly cold, and surrounded by a quiet, intelligent life that operates on a geological timescale.

You are ready. Now, forget about being dry, and begin your climb. The water is already falling for you.
