Visiting the Underworks is not an exploration; it's a state of temporary coexistence with a deep and active process. An ordinary traveler must prepare not to conquer or discover, but to endure, observe, and pass through without becoming a new variable in its ancient equations. Here is how you should prepare.

**1. The Psychology of Descent: Accept the Inhuman**
Your first and most important preparation is mental. You are entering a place that operates on a logic of pure function, a scale that dwarfs you, and a timeline that likely extends millennia beyond your comprehension. The "intent" you sense is not for you. Your goal is to achieve a state of **passive observation**. Do not project your own narrative onto the machines. They are not "monsters," "guardians," or "artifacts." They are processes. Think of yourself as a mote of dust caught in the gearing of a vast clock—your safety depends on not interfering. Prepare to feel profoundly insignificant, cold, and irrelevant. This is not a weakness; it is the correct perspective for survival.

**2. Equipment: Armoring Against the Condition**
Your gear must mediate between your soft, warm biology and the cold, wet, abrasive environment.

*   **Thermal Suit:** Not just for warmth, but for **moisture management**. You need a multi-layered system:
    *   A wicking base layer to pull sweat away.
    *   An insulating mid-layer (synthetic, not down, which collapses when wet).
    *   A **non-absorbent, seamless outer shell**. The ambient wetness will coat everything. Seams are failure points. The material should be slick, like a high-tech diver's suit, causing the perpetual condensation to sheet off. Bright colors are useless here; opt for greys or matte blacks to avoid drawing attention, if such a concept exists here.
*   **Footwear:** Critical. The ground is a cracked, slippery mosaic of ceramic and iron. You need rigid-soled, waterproof boots with aggressive, non-metallic traction. The cold from the ground is conductive and relentless. **Insulation from the floor is as important as insulation from the air.** Consider thermal insoles.
*   **Respiration & Eyes:** The air is thick with ozone (which can irritate lungs) and particulate matter (rust, mineral dust). A simple particulate mask is mandatory. For your eyes, **anti-fog, wide-coverage goggles** are non-negotiable. The temperature differential will fog any seal instantly; choose models with good ventilation channels or a double-lens system. Your breath will be a visible, distracting plume—breathe slowly and steadily to minimize it.
*   **Tools & Interface:**
    *   A **high-intensity, focused-beam flashlight** (with red-light capability). The ambient industrial panels cast a dim, omnidirectional gloom that erases detail. You need to control your own light to inspect the path, but use it sparingly. The machinery moves on its own schedules; do not blind a piston or a drone.
    *   **Passive, ruggedized recording devices.** No active scanners or transmitters. You do not know what constitutes "interference" here. A hardened camera, an audio recorder, and perhaps a simple spectrometer for the water film (if you dare sample it) are sufficient. All devices should be tethered to you.
    *   **Sealed containers.** For samples, waste, anything. You bring everything out with you. The Underworks has its own closed-loop cycles; do not introduce contaminants.

**3. Physical Preparedness & Movement**
*   **Endurance over strength.** You will be walking on uneven, slippery surfaces in heavy gear, in cold that saps energy, amidst a constant, draining auditory barrage. Train for sustained, low-intensity effort.
*   **Balance and proprioception.** Practice walking on unstable, wet surfaces. The gratings will shift; the ceramic plates will be slick with the iridescent film.
*   **Learn to move with the rhythm.** The place has a pulse—the thump of the pistons, the cyclic cleaning of the mesh screens. Do not startle. Move deliberately. Time your crossings of open spaces between the major mechanical cycles. Watch the "maintenance drones" that clear mineral deposits; they are predictable. Do not step on their paths.

**4. Environmental Protocols**
*   **The Water is a Character, Not a Feature.** It is your primary hazard and your key to understanding. It weeps, flows uphill, forms spheres. Do not drink it. Do not submerge in it. Do not let an open wound contact it. Its chemistry is the place's logic. Assume it is a complex solvent carrying nano-scale machinery or reactive compounds. If you must sample it, use a non-reactive, sealed tool and contain it immediately.
*   **Respect the Scale and the Sound.** The sub-audible thrum of the turbines can cause nausea and disorientation over time. The loud cracks of pressure valves are disorienting. Wear **acoustic dampening earplugs** (not full headphones; you need to hear the directional sounds of the environment). Learn to feel the vibrations in the floor plates—they can telegraph the movement of large machinery before you see or hear it.
*   **Avoid the Low Places.** The "wide, shallow pans" where the iridescent water gathers are the basins of unknown chemical processes. They are likely the most stagnant and reactive zones. Stick to the higher ground of the heaved plates and gratings.

**5. The Final, Unspoken Rule: Do Not Try to Understand the Purpose.**
Your instinct will be to solve the riddle: What is this for? Who built it? You must resist this. The answer is likely incompatible with human comprehension or relevance. To try and grasp the "why" is to project a story onto a system that operates beyond story. Your task is to note the *what* and the *how*: the patterns of flow, the schedule of the machines, the sound of decay. Your journal should be a log of observations, not a treatise of theories.

You are not an explorer here. You are a **temporary symbiont**, a fragile, self-contained organism borrowing space within a greater, colder, and older living system. Prepare your body, quiet your mind, and respect the rhythm of the Weeping Cathedral of Function. Then, and only then, may you descend and witness the Underworks without becoming a part of its machinery.
