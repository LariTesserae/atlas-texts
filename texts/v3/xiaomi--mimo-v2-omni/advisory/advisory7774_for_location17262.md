**PREPARATION PROTOCOL FOR SUBSTRATE CAVITY BIOME (DESIGNATION: THE CARVED HOLLOW)**

**I. Core Philosophy: The Visitor as a Temporary Component**
You are not entering a landscape; you are entering an ancient, active machine of stone and water. Your goal is not to conquer or claim, but to observe and integrate without disrupting its delicate, millennia-old equilibrium. Your presence should be that of a respectful ghost.

**II. Essential Gear & Modifications**

*   **Attire:** Full-body, seamless garment of matte, non-reflective material (e.g., treated polymer-weave). Color should be dull grey or beige to blend with the mineral dust. **Critical:** All seams must be sealed, and the outer surface should be slightly hydrophobic to shed water, but not so much as to create unnatural beading. You want to get damp, not stay dry. Boots must have soft, gripping soles for wet stone and be completely enclosed to prevent ingress of small fauna.
*   **Atmosphere & Senses:**
    *   **Auditory:** High-fidelity, noise-dampening earwear with active filtering. You must be able to reduce the overwhelming percussive symphony of water and chitin to a manageable level to think, but also be able to "zoom in" on specific audio cues—the tick of a hunter's claws, the subtle shift in a drip pattern that might signal a change.
    *   **Olfactory:** A basic air filter/mask is required, not for toxins, but for allergens. The air will be thick with spores from the pale filaments (likely a fungal analogue) and fine particulate from the perpetually abraded stone. A simple pre-filter will prevent respiratory irritation.
    *   **Visual:** No bright lights. The sourceless diffuse glow is your only illumination. Bring a low-lumen, warm-toned lamp with a red-filter option for examining details without startling the fauna (many chitinous creatures see poorly in red spectrums). Your eyes will need time to adjust to the shimmering, reflective wetness.
*   **Mobility & Interaction:**
    *   **Limbs:** Four-limbed contact is encouraged. Use hands and knees often. The floor is a living map. Carry light, grippy gloves.
    *   **Tools:** A small, blunt-tipped probe for gently testing the stability of a surface or redirecting a trickle of water. **No sharp instruments.** You are a guest, not a archaeologist with a chisel. A shallow, wide container for collecting pooled water (for study or temporary settling) is useful. A magnifying lens or macro-scope for observing the micro-fauna.
*   **Physiological Support:**
    *   **Hydration:** Despite the omnipresent water, it is mineral-heavy and likely laden with organic particulate. Bring your own sealed, purified water supply. Do not drink from the pools.
    *   **Nutrition:** Bring inert, crumb-free nutrition blocks. You cannot risk introducing foreign organic matter or crumbs that could disrupt the grazing cycles or attract unwanted attention.

**III. Environmental Adaptation & Conduct**

*   **Movement:** Proceed slowly. Let the rhythm of the place set your pace. Sudden movements will send skitters through the insect population and cause water to sheet unpredictably off surfaces. Move with the flow of the carved channels when possible.
*   **Respecting the Architecture:** **Do not touch the geometric reliefs more than necessary.** The softened edges are the result of eons of water and tiny claws. The oil from your skin is a foreign contaminant. If you must brace yourself, choose the smoother, less-patterned areas within the honeycombed recesses.
*   **Fauna Interaction:**
    *   **Grazers (millipede-like):** Ignore them. They will flow around you like a living carpet. Do not step on them; their crushed remains would be a significant biological event here.
    *   **Hunters (six-legged):** Be aware of your perimeter. They are not interested in you, but will investigate a sudden, large, warm presence. Keeping still and low makes you part of the geology.
    *   **Weavers (spider-like):** **Actively avoid their webs.** They are moisture-trapping marvels and critical to the local water cycle. Damaging one is a major disturbance. The strands are often nearly invisible in the diffuse light; use your probe to gently check the space ahead of your face and limbs.
*   **Managing the Moisture:** Expect to be perpetually damp. Your suit will be wet within minutes. The key is to not let it become cold. Layer with a thermally adaptive undergarment. Condensation will form on your gear; periodically and gently tilt to allow it to join the local flow, rather than dripping randomly.

**IV. Psychological & Philosophical Preparation**

*   **Embrace Claustro-Latency:** The space is intimate yet layered. The walls are always near. This is not a threat, but a characteristic. It induces a focused, almost meditative state. Fight any impulse to "find an exit" or "see the sky." There is none. Your world is now this shimmering, wet, carved volume.
*   **Listen to the Story in the Stone:** The uniformity of the patterning speaks of a single, overwhelming design intent. The wear speaks of time beyond reckoning. Place your hand flat on a wall (through your glove). Feel the damp chill. This place was *made*, and then it was *abandoned to itself*. You are witnessing the universe's tendency to take a crafted thing and turn it, slowly, into a wild thing.
*   **Accept Your Scale:** You are the largest creature here by a vast margin. This grants you a temporary, benign dominance, but it also makes you a profound anomaly. Your footstep can obliterate a life-form's entire world. Your breath changes the local humidity. Your visit is a seismic event in miniature. Tread with the gravity of a god, but the gentleness of a whisper.

**V. Final Checklist Before Entry:**
- [ ] Seals on all garments intact.
- [ ] Auditory filters calibrated to dampen drip-frequencies.
- [ ] Red-light filter on headlamp functional.
- [ ] Water and nutrition packs secured and insulated.
- [ ] **Mindset:** Patleness. Humility. Curiosity without possession.

**Remember:** You are not exploring a cave. You are visiting a living, breathing, crafted organism. The weirdness is not in monsters or magic, but in the profound, quiet, wet, and busy *aliveness* of a thing that should, by all rights, be dead stone. Do not disturb the balance. Observe the dance of water and claw, then leave, letting the carved hollow forget you ever existed.
