### **Phase 1: Mental & Philosophical Preparation**

*   **Abandon the Concept of "Land":** You are not visiting a place; you are entering a living, continental-scale organism. Think of yourself as a microbe visiting a lung or a skin. Your goal is not to conquer or traverse, but to be tolerated. Every step is on tissue.
*   **Embrace Slow Time:** The rhythm here is geological. Patience is your primary tool. Sudden movements, loud noises, and hurried decisions will mark you as a foreign irritant. Move with the drip, not against it.
*   **Accept Symbiosis:** You will be colonized. Spores will settle on your gear, dampness will permeate your core. Your objective is to manage this, not prevent it. See it as the price of admission and a potential means of camouflage.

### **Phase 2: Physical & Gear Preparation**

*   **Dermal Seal:** Your skin is the primary barrier, and it will be tested. Apply a base layer of medical-grade, breathable barrier cream (like a high-tech version of lanolin) before suiting up. This isn't to keep you dry—you will get wet—but to prevent constant maceration and fungal/bacterial invasion of your skin.
*   **Clothing System:**
    *   **Base Layer:** Merino wool or synthetic wicking fabric. Cotton is a death sentence; it holds moisture and chills.
    *   **Insulating Layer:** Light fleece or aerogel-infused vest. The air is humid-warm, but the constant dampness creates a profound *chill* over time.
    *   **Outer Shell:** This is critical. You need a **breathable, yet absolutely waterproof** suit with sealed seams. Gore-Tex Pro or equivalent. It must have a high water column rating. A hood is non-negotiable.
    *   **Footwear:** High-ankle, fully waterproof boots with a thick, flexible sole. The terrain is springy and uneven; you need grip and ankle support, but also the ability to feel subtle shifts in the substrate. Carry spare laces; organic acids may degrade synthetics.
*   **Respiratory Protection:** A simple particulate mask (N95) is the minimum. The air is thick with spores, pollen, and volatile organic compounds from constant decay. For extended stays, a lightweight respirator with organic vapor cartridges is advised. This also helps manage the overwhelming smell, which can induce nausea or disorientation.
*   **Equipment:**
    *   **Multi-spectrum Light:** A headlamp with a **red-light mode** is essential. The native fauna's eyes are adapted to the green twilight; white light will blind and startle them. Use red light for navigation at "night."
    *   **Sound Discipline:** Use hand signals and low-frequency, dampened comms. Acoustic carry is strange here; a whisper can seem close, a shout can be swallowed.
    *   **Navigation:** GPS will be unreliable under the canopy. Use a combination of inertial measurement unit (IMU) and marked trail system (see below). Carry a compass, but understand magnetic readings may be skewed by mineral deposits in the ancient roots.
    *   **Sampling & Sealing:** For any sample taking, use hermetically sealed containers. Do not expose native biology to your world, and vice versa. Assume all fluids are reactive.

### **Phase 3: On-Site Protocols & Behavioral Adaptation**

*   **Movement:**
    *   Walk with a rolling, flat-footed gait to distribute weight. Avoid sharp impacts.
    *   Test questionable ground (darker, wetter areas) with a **long, probing pole** before committing your weight. These are likely peat pools or thin root mats over hidden voids.
    *   Never cut or break living structures for pathfinding. It is an act of violence against the whole. Use pre-existing animal paths or follow the subtle, smoother trails of vascular bundles.
*   **Hydration & Sustenance:**
    *   **DO NOT DRINK ANY WATER DIRECTLY.** The weeping moisture and pools are laden with bioactive compounds, sedatives, and potentially psychoactive alkaloids from the plant-mind. Use a multi-stage filter (particulate, carbon, UV) **and** boil the result.
    *   Bring all food. Forage is extremely high-risk. What is nourishing to a six-limbed mammal may be toxic or parasitic to a human digestive system.
*   **Interaction with Fauna & Flora:**
    *   **Observe, Do Not Touch.** Many creatures have symbiotic relationships with the flora; your touch disrupts chemical signals.
    *   **Bioluminescent pools are not decorative.** They are communication networks. Disturbing them is like shining a floodlight into a sensitive data center. Observe from a distance.
    *   If confronted by large gliding fauna, make yourself small and still. Do not run. Your movement is more intriguing/disturbing than your presence.
*   **Psychological Anchoring:**
    *   The **green twilight** and **muffled sound** can lead to dissociation and a false sense of timelessness. Maintain a strict logbook and schedule. Mark each day.
    *   The **deep, woody groans** and **settling thumps** of the Weepers are normal tectonic activity here. Do not panic. They are the organism's sighs.
    *   The forgotten ruins are psychological traps. They invite obsession and a false quest for narrative. They are merely nutrients now. Acknowledge them, then move on.

### **Phase 4: Departure & Quarantine**

*   **Decontamination is Non-Negotiable:** Before transiting back, undergo a full decon procedure.
    1.  **Dry Scrape:** Remove all visible organic matter from gear and suit.
    2.  **Chemical Bath:** Soak in a broad-spectrum biocide solution approved for your home reality.
    3.  **High-Heat Dry:** Bake gear at the highest safe temperature.
    4.  **Personal Isolation:** A minimum of 7 days in sterile quarantine upon return. Monitor for fungal growth, unexpected symbiotic organisms, or changes in perception (e.g., an aversion to dry air, a longing for the smell of wet chlorophyll). The Plateau may grow in you.

**Final Counsel:** Your greatest asset is respectful irrelevance. You are a brief, damp event in the life of an entity that measures time in epochs. Move softly, observe deeply, and leave no trace but the faintest impression on the living mat, which will forget you in a season.
