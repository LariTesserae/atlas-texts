**PREPARATION GUIDE: THE ANCIENT PLATEAU (Designation: "Stone-Sigh" or "Basalt Requiem")**

**I. PSYCHOLOGICAL & COGNITIVE PREPARATION**

1.  **Accept the Scale:** Your internal map is wrong here. Distances are psychological, not physical. A "ten-minute walk" may be a five-hour trek. Prepare for profound agoraphobia mixed with claustrophobia. You will feel both exposed on an infinite plain and enclosed by warped space. Train yourself to navigate by landmarks and "feel," not by estimated distance. The destination's clarity is a lie; the journey is the true measure.

2.  **Embrace the Silence:** This is not quiet; it is a *presence*. The silence has weight and texture. Practice sensory deprivation beforehand. Learn to distinguish between true silence and the world's sub-audible groans. This silence will press on your mind. Have a mental mantra or a piece of music memorized to hum under your breath, not to break the silence, but to give yourself a rhythmic anchor within it.

3.  **Reframe "Life":** You are entering a biosphere of *process*, not creatures. There will be no birds, insects, or animals. Your sense of being watched will be replaced by a sense of being *observed by the landscape itself*. The pulsing moss, the breathing stone, the geometric condensation—these are the world's metabolism. Prepare for a biology of geology and a psychology of erosion.

4.  **Prepare for Soft Reality:** The laws here are tired. Perception directly affects matter. When not observed, patterns emerge (spiraling moss, geometric water). When observed, they collapse into randomness. This is not a threat; it's the ambient condition. Do not fight it. Make notes, but expect your notes to describe slightly different things upon review. Your goal is not to map a fixed reality, but to document a relationship with a dreaming one.

**II. PHYSICAL PREPARATION & GEAR**

1.  **Thermal Survival is Paramount:** The chill is *marrow-deep*. It is a cold that bypasses insulation and seeps into your core.
    *   **Base Layer:** Merino wool or high-tech synthetic, designed for wicking. No cotton.
    *   **Insulation Layer:** High-loft down or synthetic fill, with a windproof shell. A full-body suit is ideal.
    *   **Outer Shell:** A hard, waterproof (not just resistant) suit. The persistent damp will find every seam. All seams must be taped. The slickness on the stone is not mud; it's a near-invisible glaze of condensed moisture.
    *   **Extremities:** Vapor-barrier liner socks, insulated waterproof boots with *aggressive, soft rubber soles* for gripping slick stone. Mittens over gloves. A balaclava or full-face buff is non-negotiable.

2.  **Respiratory System:** The air is thin and cold. Expect shortness of breath, accelerated fatigue, and potential altitude sickness.
    *   Carry a lightweight, portable supplemental oxygen system for emergencies and acclimation periods.
    *   Medication like Acetazolamide (Diamox) should be considered, under a doctor's guidance.
    *   Cover your mouth and nose with a filter gaiter; it will warm and humidify the air you breathe slightly.

3.  **Navigation & Gear:**
    *   **Forget GPS/Compasses:** Potential reality warping and magnetic anomalies (implied by the strange shadows) make them unreliable.
    *   **Primary Nav:** A high-quality, wide-angle monocular or pair of binoculars. Your eyes are your best tool. Mark landmarks obsessively in a physical journal.
    *   **Tethering:** Carry 100m of high-visibility paracord. In low-fog conditions or when spatial distortion is severe, tethering to a known monolith can prevent disorientation.
    *   **Light:** Multiple headlamps with red-light settings (to preserve night vision and not disturb the luminescent fungi). A high-lumen flashlight to cut through fog. The ambient light is flat and shadowless; artificial light will be your primary tool for depth perception.
    *   **Footwear Traction:** Even with good soles, carry lightweight micro-spikes. The "almost-wet" glaze can be treacherous.

4.  **Sustenance & Hydration:**
    *   **Water:** **DO NOT DRINK** from condensation or any surface. The moisture may carry unknown particulates or microorganisms adapted to this geochemistry. Carry all water and a robust filtration system (ceramic or hollow-fiber membrane).
    *   **Food:** High-calorie, easy-to-eat rations. The cold and thin air will burn calories rapidly. Prepare for a complete lack of edible local flora. The fungi are likely toxic or psychoactive to baseline humans.

**III. ON-SITE PROTOCOLS & BEHAVIOR**

1.  **Movement:** Walk slowly. The silty dust will puff up and coat everything. Sudden movements feel sacrilegious here. Test every step on moss (it can be slick) and stone (it can be glaze-coated). Use a walking staff for stability and to probe the ground ahead.

2.  **Observation Rituals:** To counteract the reality softness, engage in structured observation.
    *   "I am observing this moss patch at 14:32. It forms a ragged edge."
    *   Sketch it. Return in an hour. Note the change. The act of recording creates a stabilizing anchor.
    *   The buildings are eroded, not built. Touch them gently. They are part of the landscape's body. Do not chip or remove samples without deep consideration. This is a cemetery, a cathedral, and a living organism all at once.

3.  **Dealing with the Weirdness:**
    *   **Geometric Condensation:** If you see hexagons or lines forming in moisture, do not touch them. Observe and record. They are the world's fleeting thoughts.
    *   **Shadow Anomalies:** Do not trust shadows for direction or timekeeping. They are independent actors here.
    *   **Spatial Warping:** If a path seems to vanish, stop. Sit. Wait for the fog of perception to shift. The path exists for the mind, not the foot. Your intent to reach a place is part of the navigation.

4.  **Communication:** Assume you are alone, but not unobserved. Whistling or shouting will feel violently intrusive. Use a low-power radio for communication with your team (if any), set to a low volume. The sound will carry strangely.

**IV. PHILOSOPHICAL KIT**

Bring a blank journal, a set of fine pencils, and a willingness to contemplate entropy not as an end, but as a form of patience. This world is a masterclass in slow transformation. Your visit is a single, frantic heartbeat in its aeons-long sigh. Your role is not to conquer, but to witness. The greatest risk is not physical harm, but returning with a permanent sense of temporal dislocation, forever remembering a place where time moves like glaciers and stone dreams in the rain that never falls. Prepare to be humbled by the profound, quiet beauty of a world that has already finished, and yet, in its finishing, has found a way to endlessly begin again.
