**Multiverse Traveler’s Advisory: Mineral Symbiosis World (Designation: "Litho-City")**

**Primary Hazards:** High humidity and ambient heat; airborne mineral particulate; geologically active terrain; extreme scale; psychologically disorienting biosphere; non-standard fauna behavior.

**Recommended Preparation:**

**1. Physical & Equipment Preparation:**
*   **Thermal & Moisture Management:** The environment is consistently warm and saturated with moisture. Wear layered, breathable synthetics designed for hot-spring or steam-vent conditions. A personal climate-control suit is ideal, but at minimum, use high-wicking base layers and a waterproof, vapor-permeable outer shell. All equipment must be rated for continuous high humidity to prevent fungal or mineral-deposit corrosion. Sealed lenses for goggles/visors are essential; condensation will be constant.
*   **Air Filtration & Respiratory Protection:** The air carries "mineral dust and lithic spores." A fine-particulate respirator (N95/P100 rating or equivalent) is mandatory. Consider a powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR) for extended stays to reduce fatigue. Medical pre-screening for silica-type dust sensitivity is advised.
*   **Audio Discipline:** The soundscape is a deep, resonant industrial-geological hum. Prolonged exposure may cause nausea or disorientation. Bring high-fidelity noise-cancelling earwear with selective pass-through (to hear warnings or communications). Acoustic dampening within your shelter will be critical for rest cycles.
*   **Geological & Engineering Gear:** You are entering a landscape where biology and engineering are fused. Carry tools for non-invasive sampling: a insulated tongs, a portable spectrometer, and a laser microscanner. **Avoid any tool that uses vibration, percussive force, or high heat**—you risk disrupting the slow, delicate mineral accretion/erosion balance, or triggering a geothermal vent. Magnetic boots may be necessary; the mineral lattice could create unpredictable ferrous patches.
*   **Mobility & Scale:** The world is vast and its structures monumental. You will need climbing gear rated for "unknown stone" (it may be brittle or unusually slick from constant seepage). A drone or high-altitude balloon-cam is strongly recommended for reconnaissance; ground-level perspective will be overwhelmingly labyrinthine. Mark your path with chemically inert, mineral-based pigments—avoid metal or plastic markers that could be absorbed or disrupt growth.

**2. Biological & Temporal Preparation:**
*   **Detoxify & Seal:** Before transit, undergo a full decontamination cycle. You do not want Earth microbes interfering with this ecology, nor its spores colonizing you. Expect a full-body sealant application (a breathable, mineral-dust-impervious coating for skin and hair). All waste must be carried out in sealed, sterile containers.
*   **Temporal Patience:** This is the most critical adjustment. The fauna and processes here operate on a *geological* timescale. Your every instinct will scream to move, to act, to *do*. You must suppress this. **Move deliberately slowly.** Sudden motions will mark you as an alien anomaly. Observing the "ambulatory geodes" or the "drifting pollinators" requires hours of stillness, as their movement is measured in inches per day. Consider sedative micro-doses to lower metabolic rate and promote meditative patience.
*   **Dietary & Metabolic Prep:** Pack nutrient-dense, low-residue food. Digestion is a fast, messy biological process that will feel alien here. Hydrate via closed-loop systems only; the ambient condensation is saturated with dissolved minerals and could be toxic or purgative.

**3. Psychological & Philosophical Preparation:**
*   **Reframe Your Concepts:** You are not visiting a "city" or a "wilderness." It is a **single, integrated techno-organic-mineral continuum.** The "buildings" are alive; the "machinery" is geological. To categorize is to misunderstand. Study concepts of stromatolites, deep-sea chemosynthetic vents, and fungal networks beforehand to wrap your mind around a life that is collective, slow, and based on mineral exchange.
*   **Embrace Cognitive Dissonance:** You will feel the "unsettling wrongness of its rightness." It is teeming, yet plantless. It is alive, yet abiotic. This can induce a form of existential vertigo. Prepare a mental anchor—a mantra, a memory of a green, fast, loud Earth—to ground yourself. Journaling is recommended, but write slowly.
*   **The Doctrine of Minimal Impact:** Your greatest danger is your own biology and impatience. Do not touch the "coral-growth." Do not step on the crystalline trails of the fauna. Do not shout, run, or create sudden heat or light. You are a ghost in this world, and to be noticed could trigger a reaction measured in decades—a slow, mineral encasement, a patient, curious investigation by the ambulatory geodes. Your goal is observation, not interaction.
*   **Accept Your Transience:** You cannot comprehend the depth of time here. The engines have hummed for eons. The drizzle has fallen for millennia. You are a spark. This humility is not optional; it is a survival tool. Your visit is a blink. Leave no trace, not even in the memory of the stone.

**Final Advisory:** This world is a masterpiece of settled equilibrium. To visit is a privilege of the highest order. You are not an explorer claiming a frontier; you are a student permitted to sit silently in the classroom of deep time. Prepare your gear, then prepare your mind to be very, very small.
