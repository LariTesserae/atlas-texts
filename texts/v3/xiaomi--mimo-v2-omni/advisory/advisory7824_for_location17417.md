**Preparation Protocol for Grove-City Integration**

**1. Physical & Material Preparation**
*   **Gear:** All equipment must be **inert, non-corrosive, and sterile**. Titanium or polished ceramic containers are ideal—no untreated leather, wool, or natural fibers that could introduce foreign spores or decompose unpredictably. Electronics should be fully sealed; condensation is relentless. A traditional compass is preferred over digital devices; magnetic fields are undisturbed here.
*   **Clothing:** Wear **smooth, synthetic fabrics** (like treated nylon or polyester) in muted greens, greys, or browns. The goal is to shed moisture and avoid snagging on fibrous surfaces. A deep-hooded, seamless rain cape is essential. Footwear must have **soft, non-abrasive soles**—like thin rubber or specialized silicone—to avoid damaging the living loam or root-mats. Consider it like wearing socks in a sacred space.
*   **Sustenance:** Bring all food and water. **Nothing organic from your world may be deposited.** All waste, including crumbs, must be packed out. Caloric needs may be lower; the atmosphere is soothing but offers no nourishment. Hydration packs are better than bottles; the sound of unscrewing a cap is jarringly loud here.

**2. Biological & Contamination Protocol**
*   **Decontamination:** Before transit, undergo a **full bio-scour**. This is not just sterilization; it's a removal of all non-native pollen, fungal spores, and microbial life from your person and gear. You are a potential vector. A double-layered, sealed outer garment worn only for transit and then discarded or incinerated upon return is a wise precaution.
*   **Health:** If you have any respiratory sensitivities, reconsider. The air is thick with benign mold spores and organic particulates. Bring a simple, non-electronic air filter mask if needed. The environment is pharmacologically active in subtle ways; avoid taking any medication that might alter your body chemistry unpredictably.

**3. Behavioral & Psychological Readiness**
*   **Movement:** Practice **extreme physical quiet**. Move slowly, deliberately. Place your feet with intention. The constant *drip* and *creak* of the Grove-City is a baseline; your footfalls, your breath, the rustle of your clothing will be the loudest sounds in your world. You will feel like a bull in a glassblower's studio at first.
*   **Interaction:** **Do not touch.** The architecture is alive and communicates through touch and chemical signals. Your oils, salts, and warmth are a disruptive stimulus. Observe with your eyes. If you must steady yourself, use only the back of your gloved hand on what appears to be deadfall or mature, non-arterial wood.
*   **Mindset:** Abandon the human concept of "wilderness" and "city." This is a **single, conscious entity**. You are not exploring a place; you are entering a being. Your goal is not to conquer or map, but to witness and integrate temporarily. Prepare for a profound sense of being *observed* by the environment itself, not by eyes, but by a vast, distributed awareness of pressure, humidity, and vibration.

**4. Understanding the Rules of Integration**
*   **The Rule of Emptiness:** Respect the spaces between structures. They are not "empty"; they are lungs, light wells, and rain funnels. Do not set up camp or linger in these channels.
*   **The Rule of Flow:** Water is the circulatory system. Do not block, divert, or pollute any catchment or channel. Follow the roots; they will show you where to step.
*   **The Rule of Patient Logic:** There is no urgency here. If you are lost or disoriented, **stop**. Find a high, dry nodal point (like a mature bridge-bough) and wait. The environment will respond to your stillness—not to help you, but to continue its processes around you. Your panicked movement is a stressor; your calm is absorbed.

**5. What to Bring for the Experience**
*   A **small, sealed notebook and a graphite pencil** (no ink, which could spill or leak). The humidity will make pages damp, but graphite will still mark.
*   A **low-light, red-filtered lamp** for any necessary navigation after the perpetual dusk. The canopy filters light to a permanent, pearlescent twilight.
*   **A single, small, perfectly smooth stone** from your own world, to hold in your pocket. It is a foreign object, a reminder of your own solidity and origin in a place where all solids are alive. It is your anchor to self.

**Final Note:** You are not visiting a ruin or a garden. You are entering a **post-human conclusion**. It is a monument to the idea that the highest technology is growth, and the ultimate industry is equilibrium. To be there is to be humbled by a quiet, green, and complete answer to the question of civilization. Go softly. Listen more than you breathe. And understand that your most important preparation is to leave your sense of human agency at the threshold. Here, you are only another kind of rain—temporary, external, and hopefully, harmless.
