**I. Fundamental Survival Systems (The "Walking Coffin" Approach)**

You are not entering an environment; you are inserting a sealed life-support capsule into a planetary furnace. Your suit is not clothing, it is your personal biosphere.

*   **Thermal Exosuit:**
    *   **Primary Layer:** A full-body, active cooling garment. Not a spacesuit's passive insulation, but a system with circulating coolant (likely a phase-change material) to combat the ambient superheated air. It must handle radiant heat from the ground and nearby spires.
    *   **Outer Shell:** A rigid, articulated exoskeleton made of a ceramic-composite material. This serves three purposes: 1) Physical protection from abrasive minerals and potential grazer mandibles. 2) Structural support to prevent the suit from softening or deforming in the heat. 3) A platform for mounting all other systems. It must be matte black to minimize any visual signature, but internally insulated to protect the wearer from its own heat sink.
    *   **Heat Rejection:** You cannot "cool" indefinitely; you must dump heat. Expect massive, retractable radiator fins on the back, which would glow dull red in the dark, a terrible beacon. Their deployment must be strategic and brief. The suit will be *hot* to the touch on the outside.

*   **Atmospheric Process Unit (APU):**
    *   **Input:** A multi-stage filtration and chemical scrubbing system. Stage 1: Particle filter for mineral dust. Stage 2: Activated carbon and chemical absorbents for sulfur compounds and unknown volatile organics. Stage 3: A catalytic converter to break down exotic mineral vapors.
    *   **Output:** A closed-loop oxygen regeneration system (like a advanced submarine or spacecraft). The air you breathe will be sterile, neutral, and odorless—a profound psychological disconnect from the acrid tang your suit's external sensors report. An emergency "sniff" function could briefly pipe in filtered external audio and smell for situational awareness, but it would be a calculated risk.

*   **Sensory Suite (Your New Eyes and Ears):**
    *   **Primary Visual:** Non-visual. A LIDAR/sonar hybrid system that builds a 3D map of the immediate surroundings in your HUD. The spires, creatures, and artifacts would appear as wire-frame or point-cloud models. This is critical for navigation.
    *   **Enhanced Visual:** Low-light/thermal imaging cameras. The faint ruddy glow of the spires and the heat signatures of lithovores would be your only "light." This feed would be grainy, monochromatic (likely in shades of orange and black), and constantly shifting with thermal gradients.
    *   **Audio:** External microphones with severe dampening and noise-cancellation to handle the sub-audible hum. They would be tuned to highlight and localize the specific sounds described: *cracks, scuttles, thumps, chimes*. The audio feed would feel unnervingly intimate, as if the sounds are happening inside your helmet.

*   **Mobility & Interaction:**
    *   **Feet:** Magnetic or gecko-tape soles might fail on crystalline salt. Better are sharp, carbide-tipped cleats to grip the crunchy ground, but designed to minimize surface area and noise with each step.
    *   **Limbs:** The exoskeleton must be strong enough to gently push aside gelatinous air-siphons or brace against a shifting grazer, but with precise force-feedback to avoid crushing the fragile, occupied spires. Every movement must be deliberate.
    *   **Tools:** No open flames, no sparks, no high-energy devices. Tools would be non-powered: diamond-edged saws, titanium pry-bars, specimen containers with internal climate control. A dedicated "artifact scanner" using ground-penetrating radar and spectroscopic analysis would be vital for studying the anomalous structures without touching them.

**II. Behavioral & Psychological Preparation**

Your mindset is as important as your gear.

*   **Acceptance of Sensory Deprivation/Perversion:** You will see almost nothing with your own eyes. You will smell nothing. Your sense of space will be mediated by a machine. This leads to derealization. Pre-trip training in sensory-altered environments (VR with only LIDAR view, audio-only scenarios) is essential.
*   **The Doctrine of Minimal Impact:** This is a core tenet. You are a ghost. A pilgrim. You do not collect, you observe. You do not clear a path, you navigate the existing spaces. Every action must be justified by necessity or vital research. Disturbing the "settled-form" could have cascading effects you cannot predict.
*   **Patience as a Survival Tool:** Movement must be tectonic, matching the pace of the lithovores. Rushing generates noise, heat, and vibration—all of which are signals in this world. You must learn to "listen" with your entire body through the suit's sensors, interpreting the clicks and hums as a language of threat and opportunity.
*   **Artifact Protocol:** The "built" elements are the greatest lure and the greatest danger. They are integrated. A hammer strike on a lintel could release trapped gases, collapse a dwelling, or trigger a chemical response from the living stone. Documentation would be 100% remote: LIDAR scans, spectrographic readings, atmospheric sampling near the surface. Physical contact is a last resort, requiring a full hazard assessment.

**III. Logistical & Contingency Planning**

*   **Insertion/Extraction:** A direct drop from orbit is too disruptive. A slow descent via a high-altitude, silent balloon platform, docking with a pre-positioned habitat module on the caldera rim, is preferable. The habitat is your only safe haven, a bubble of light and normal air.
*   **Power:** Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs). They provide steady, long-term power with no moving parts, no intake, and minimal heat signature compared to a reactor. Their gentle warmth would need to be shielded to avoid attracting heat-scavengers.
*   **Communications:** Useless. The dense, mineral-rich air and chaotic geology would devour any signal. All data is stored locally and transmitted only during retrieval via a hardened data capsule launched from the habitat.
*   **Emergency Scenarios:**
    *   **Hull Breach:** Immediate sealant injection. The suit has minutes of reserve oxygen to allow for a retreat to a "safe zone" (a pre-scouted, stable area) or an emergency extraction beacon.
    *   **Hostile Interaction:** "Hostility" would be accidental. A lithovore might attempt to filter the heat from your suit. The response is not violence, but a slow, steady retreat while emitting a low-frequency pulse (a non-threatening "hum") to encourage disengagement. Fighting would be catastrophically loud and messy.
    *   **Psychological Breakdown:** The helmet contains a micro-dose sedative injection port, activated by a manual override. A last resort to prevent panicked flailing that would destroy you and the environment.

**In summary, visiting the Caldera of Whispers is not an adventure. It is a penance.** You must shed your human instincts for light, air, speed, and dominion. You become a slow, silent, seeing machine, a respectful intruder in a complete and terrible world. Your preparation is the process of building that machine, and training the ghost inside it to be still.
