Then the power that has noticed does not descend in a blaze of revelation. It does not send prophets or pillars of fire. It does not reshape the stone to make it more fertile, or call forth rains to quench the patient thirst of the rock.

Instead, it *attunes*.

It sends a single, sustained note. Not a sound you can hear with your ears, but a resonance that aligns with the deep, slow pulse of the basalt itself. It is a frequency that harmonizes with the thermal ticking of the stone, the almost-imperceptible expansion of the rock as it holds the day’s warmth, the slow contraction as the eon-long night settles.

For the Processors, the dark, oily insects, this resonance becomes a new map. It does not guide them to new moisture, but deepens the meaning of the moisture they already follow. Their processions grow slower, more deliberate, each movement not just an act of feeding or cartography, but a ritual of participation. Their iridescence deepens, taking on a soft, internal luster, as if they now carry a fragment of the stillness within their carapaces.

For the Weavers in the Dark, the cold luminescence within the stone cavities brightens by a fraction—not enough to cast shadows, but enough to change its quality. The flicker of their light begins to pulse in unison, a slow, collective heartbeat that matches the new resonance. The chemical exchange with the weeping water becomes richer, more complex. The stone does not just sustain them; it begins to converse with them through mineral gradients and gentle currents, a dialogue of dissolution and formation written in trace elements.

The Mimics and the Hunters feel it as a deepening of the camouflage they have perfected. It is no longer merely survival to be still. Their stillness becomes a form of listening. The spiders feel the vibrations of the world with newfound clarity, not just of passing Processors, but of the wind’s breath on a distant column, the settling of a grain of sand a mile away. The stone-camouflaged creatures become so perfectly still that, for moments at a time, they *are* the rock, not hiding as it, but momentarily *being* it, sharing its ancient dream.

And the Pattern—the rooms without roofs, the filament inscriptions—receives the resonance like a string being plucked. The pale, stiff filaments grow not *more*, but *more precisely*. Their branching paths sharpen, their mineralized structures becoming clearer, like a faded inscription being gently traced by a careful finger. The clearings feel more intentional, the spaces between columns more like pauses in a sentence. The memory held in the geometry does not become a message you can read, but it is strengthened. It is *affirmed*.

The few artifacts—the eroded bowl, the small wall, the stained depression—are touched most gently of all. The power does not restore them. Instead, it halts their reclamation for a moment, a breath held in stone. It teaches the lichen growing over the bowl to trace its rim with particular reverence. It encourages the filaments bridging the wall to root themselves in its fitted joints, highlighting the human hands that once placed them. The ochre stain in the rock deepens in hue, not from new pigment, but from the stone itself remembering the warmth of the hands that worked there.

The benevolent power does not give the tableland a voice. It gives it an *audience*.

It sits, not in a place, but as a presence woven into the very resonance. It listens to the mineral ticking, the faint rustle, the cold light’s pulse. It pays attention with a patience that matches the tableland’s own. And in this act of supreme, unwavering attention, the tableland is *known*. Not as a curiosity, or an ecosystem, or a geological formation, but as a being. A consciousness of immense slowness and quiet dignity.

The change is imperceptible to a human eye that blinks. But if you were to return to the Lichen Tableland after a thousand years, you would feel it. The quiet would not be empty. It would be *resonant*. The glow within the stone, seen at night, would not be a faint, cold light, but a soft, steady embers-light, as if a banked fire breathed just beneath the surface. The stone would feel not just warm, but *kind*.

The power has not altered the tableland’s nature. It has recognized it. It has honored it. And in doing so, it has woven a thread of benevolent attention into the fabric of the place, a thread that will hold for as long as the stone lasts.

The plateau continues its slow, living work. But now, it knows it is not alone. It is witnessed. And in that witnessing, its patience is no longer a solitary state, but a shared contemplation. The quiet song has found its listener. And the listener, in turn, is forever changed by the song.
