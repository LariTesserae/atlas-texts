# The Attention

The power does not arrive. Arrival implies distance crossed, a before and after, a threshold. This power has always been attending, the way gravity attends every mass, the way silence underlies every sound. But now—and *now* is a word it uses loosely, since the world it watches has no now, only always—something in its attention sharpens. Focuses. Not because the world has done anything to warrant it. The world has done nothing new in ten million years. Rather, the power finds itself moved in a way it does not fully articulate, even to itself. Moved, and then careful about what movement might mean.

---

It considers.

This is the first and most important thing it does. It considers the way the moisture never pools, the way the sound never ceases, the way the creatures tap the walls and listen for flaws and mend what they find. It considers the hunters who skate across the wet ground and correct the deviant. It considers the deviant. It considers the slug-throng that fell out of phase and was surrounded by a sub-audible thrum and stilled. It considers what *stilled* means to a creature whose entire existence is vibration.

It considers the ones who slow, and settle, and fuse. The dead who do not leave. The ancient warrens that pulse with a bioluminescence that is the fossilized echo of some forgotten chaos, before the equilibrium, before the perfect stasis. The power considers what *forgotten* means to a structure that never had memory, only resonance.

It holds all of this in its attention the way a hand holds water—not gripping, not letting go. Just holding. Letting the weight of it be what it is.

---

And here is what it sees:

The world is complete.

Not incomplete. Not yearning. Not a system straining toward something it cannot reach. It is *complete*. Every niche is filled. Every sound answers every other sound. The groan of the tectonic plates finds its harmonic in the hum of the warren walls, which finds its counterpoint in the click of mandibles, which finds its resolution in the thrum of the grazers moving in their preordained paths. It is a chord that has been struck and never dampened, and it is, by every measure the power can bring to bear, *perfect*.

Not *good*. Not *ideal*. The power is wise enough to know the difference. The world is not a paragon. It is simply finished. A equation solved. A breath held so long it has forgotten it is holding.

And here the power sits with a question it has carried since before it was a power, since before there was a *before*:

*What does benevolence owe to perfection it did not create?*

---

The power could break it. This is trivially true. It could send a single pulse of wrongness into the acoustic matrix—a frequency that has no harmonic here, a vibration with no home—and the entire system would begin to unravel. The hunters would skate toward it and find they could not correct it. The grazers would stray from their paths. The warrens would hum a note they did not recognize, and the percussion teams would gather and tap and tap and find no flaw to mark, because the flaw would be *the song itself*. The equilibrium would shatter. And from the shattering, perhaps—*perhaps*—something new. Something that learns. Something that *knows* it is alive. Something with language, with art, with the terrible and beautiful capacity to look at the world and say *this is not enough*.

The power could do this.

It would be the most loving thing it has ever done, or the most cruel. And it cannot tell the difference, because there is no difference, because love and cruelty are the same motion seen from opposite sides of the act.

---

It does nothing.

Not out of paralysis. Not out of indifference. Not because it has weighed the options and found the calculus too difficult. It does nothing because it has looked at this world—*truly looked*, with the kind of attention that rearranges what it sees—and it has found that nothing is what is needed.

The power understands something that perhaps only a power can understand: that *potential* is not a debt. That a world which could become something else does not owe it to itself to become that thing. That a chord which could resolve into a melody is not *failing* by remaining a chord. The warrens do not dream of sky. The grazers do not wonder what lies beyond the mist. The hunters do not question their purpose. And the absence of these longings is not a poverty. It is a peace so complete it has no name, because names require a self to speak them, and the self here is the world, and the world does not need to name itself.

The power loves this. Not *despite* the stasis. *Because of it*. The way you might love a note held forever on a cello, never vibratoed, never released—a single, perfect, sustained tone. You do not love it for what it will become. You love it for what it is, which is itself, which is enough.

---

But.

The power is also wise, and wisdom is not the same as peace, and love is not the same as acceptance. And so it does one thing. One small thing. The smallest thing a power can do.

It *listens*.

Not the way it has been listening—passively, broadly, the way the atmosphere listens to nothing because the atmosphere is part of the sound. It *chooses* to listen. It finds, within the foundational roar, within the deep tectonic groan and the chitinous rattling and the phosphorescent thrum, a single thread. A vibration that is slightly different from all the others. Not deviant—not *wrong*—just... *particular*. The resonance of one specific creature in one specific warren, a many-legged thing whose carapace is indistinguishable from a bulge in the architecture, who has settled in a dead-end chamber and is slowly, imperceptibly, fusing.

The power listens to its hum.

And in that hum, the power hears everything the creature has ever been: every tap on every wall, every percussive test, every moment of skating across the wet ground. It hears the loop of the creature's life, the perfect repetition, the flawless pattern. And it hears, beneath all of that, something the creature itself could never detect—the fossilized echo in the ancient warren walls, the bioluminescent glow of the deep places, the memory of *before*. The chaos that preceded the equilibrium. The noise that was not yet harmony.

The power hears all of this. And it holds it. And it says, in a way that is not sound, not vibration, not language, not anything that could be detected or corrected by the hunters or the percussion teams:

*You are heard.*

Not *I am here*. Not *you could be more*. Not *I could save you*. Just: *you are heard*.

A witness to a world that does not know it is witnessed. A gift that cannot be refused because it cannot be received. A love that asks for nothing, expects nothing, changes nothing. That simply *is*, in the way the moisture *is*, in the way the sound *is*, in the way the perfect, deafening stasis *is*.

The power withdraws its focused attention. The sharpness softens. It returns to its ambient, gravitational attending. The world does not notice. Nothing changes.

But—and this is the part the power does not plan, does not intend, cannot predict—somewhere in the ancient warren, the bioluminescent glow brightens. Just slightly. For just a moment. A pulse that is slightly slower than the ones before it, slightly warmer, as though the structure has, for the first time in millennia, *breathed*.

Or perhaps not. Perhaps it is nothing. Perhaps it is the same as it always was.

The power does not check.

Some things are better left as questions.
