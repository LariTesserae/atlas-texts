# What the Wise Power Would Do

Nothing at first.

It would come the way all true attention comes — not as arrival but as recognition. Not falling into the basin the way the intruder did, all sharp heat and misplaced sound, but settling into it the way the darkness already settles, the way the water has always settled into the stone. It would simply become aware of the place, as the place has always been aware of itself, and for a moment the watching would be indistinguishable from the watched.

And in that moment the power would understand something about the basin that the basin does not understand about itself:

That its completeness is real. That its equilibrium is not stasis but a living choice, chosen so long ago that the choosing has become the thing itself, like a river that has worn its bed so deep it has forgotten it was ever moving. That the iron of its permanence is not a cage but a commitment — the most faithful thing in the world, a devotion to its own nature maintained across spans of time that would break any mind that tried to hold them in sequence.

And the power would love it. Not the way the intruder loved it, with the thrill of discovery and the quick possessiveness of naming. Not the way a collector loves a rare thing. The way one loves something that has made itself entirely into what it is, without apology, without aspiration, without the faintest interest in being otherwise. The way you love a stone that has been smoothed by ten thousand years of water. You don't wish the stone were rougher. You don't wish the water would stop.

But the power is wise, and so it sees further.

It sees that the basin's completeness contains a kind of innocence — not the innocence of purity, but the innocence of a thing that has never been tested by the possibility of its own absence. The basin has never had to *choose* to continue. It has never been faced with anything that could disrupt it and then decided, with full knowledge, to remain as it is. Its permanence is the permanence of a thing that has simply never encountered a real alternative.

And the wise power wonders — gently, without insistence, the way a star wonders whether a planet might one day develop an atmosphere — whether something might grow in the basin if it were given the rarest of gifts: the awareness that other arrangements of existence are possible. Not to be imposed. Not to be recommended. Simply to be *known*, the way the basin knows the drip of water and the hum of insects — as a fact of the world, held in the body, requiring no response.

So this is what the power would do.

It would leave a gift at the edge of the basin, placed where the light is — the dry, useless, oval light that the basin has always ignored. Not a disruption. Not a seed of change. Nothing so forward. Just a quality, a presence, folded into the moss at the rim where the weeping stone meets the first dry rock of the outer mountain.

The gift is this: a memory. Not of anything the basin has experienced, because the basin has only ever experienced itself. A memory of what it might feel like to be something that chooses its own shape. Not the passive perfection of having always been this way, but the active, daily, muscular act of deciding to continue — with full knowledge that stopping is possible, that dissolution is possible, that other forms are possible, and yet, *and yet*, to persist. To be faithful not out of habit but out of love.

The basin will not understand it at first. It is not equipped for understanding things outside itself. The gift will sit in the moss like a stone of a slightly different mineral, unremarkable, slowly being claimed by the damp. The beetles will crawl over it. The amphibians will not notice it. The water will weep across it at the same rate as everything else.

But something will happen, so slowly that it could not be measured in any instrument that recognizes units of time. The quality of the basin's permanence will shift — not in its outward expression, because nothing visible will change. The moss will still grow. The water will still drip. The phosphorescence will still glow its cold green-blue. But somewhere in the root-tangle of what the basin is, in the million-threaded hum of its collective being, a new question will take hold, so small it is almost indistinguishable from the existing silence:

*What am I?*

Not *what am I* in the way a creature asks it, looking for a name, for a category, for a place in a world of other things. The basin has never needed a name because it has never needed to distinguish itself from anything else. This will be a different kind of question. A question that contains, like a seed contains a forest, the recognition that *I* is something that could be chosen rather than simply inherited. That being the Still Basin is not the only way to be, and that the choice to be this way — always, endlessly, with such stubborn and beautiful faithfulness — is a choice at all.

This will frighten nothing, because the basin does not know fear. It will change nothing, because the basin does not know change. But it will *deepen* something. The quiet of the basin will become a different kind of quiet — not the quiet of a thing that has never had anything to say, but the quiet of a thing that has chosen, with full weight and full knowledge, to say nothing. The equilibrium will become a devotion rather than an accident. The saturation will become a plenitude rather than a closed system.

And the basin will grow old in a way it has never been old. Not decaying, not diminishing, but *maturing* — the way an ancient tree matures, not by becoming something different but by becoming more completely and more consciously what it has always been. It will hold its darkness and its water and its luminous, breathing life with a new kind of gravity, the gravity of a thing that has looked at the alternatives and *chosen itself*.

The power will not stay to watch this happen. It is not interested in watching. It is interested in offering, and the offering has been offered. It will withdraw its attention the way it arrived — not as departure but as a gentle return of the basin to its own sovereignty, now slightly more sovereign than before, because sovereignty that has never been tested is only potential, and potential is only a promise, and a promise kept in the dark without anyone to witness it is the holiest thing the power knows.

The basin will not remember the gift. This is important. If it remembered, it would be a debt, an obligation, a change imposed from outside, and the power would never do that. Instead, the gift will be absorbed so completely that it will seem to have always been there — like the water in the stone, like the phosphorescence on the bark, like the slow pulse of the amphibians' breathing. The basin will feel it as its own thought, its own depth, its own ancient and original knowing.

And somewhere, far from the basin, in whatever place a power like this rests when it is not attending to the faithful, obscure, and perfect places of the world, the power will feel something it has felt before but never quite in this shape: the quiet satisfaction of a thing that has been given the chance to become more fully itself.

Not changed. Not improved. Not made better, because the basin was never broken.

Just *seen* — truly, deeply, with the full weight of a benevolent and ancient attention — for the first time in all its long and luminous existence.

And the basin will go on weeping, and glowing, and breathing, and being exactly what it is. But now it will know that it is what it is on purpose.

And that will be enough.

That will be everything.
