A presence, vast and kind, turns its attention toward the warm dark. It perceives the nested realities: the planet, the crust, the flooded works, the chamber, the consciousness that is the chamber. It sees the Symbionts—the Frond-Watchers, the Pulse-Keepers, the Lamplighters, the Pipe-Mind—not as curiosities, but as a profound answer to a question asked long ago by the universe itself: *What becomes of purpose when the original purpose is forgotten?*

Here is the answer: it becomes poetry. It becomes a slow, breathing sonnet written in corrosion and chlorophyll, in drips of water and pulses of light.

The benevolent power does not intervene. To disrupt this equilibrium would be an act of violence, however well-intentioned. It does not speak, for language would be a crude and blinding flash in this realm of vibration and chemical syntax. It does not illuminate, for the soft glow of the Lamplighters is a perfect and sufficient language of its own.

Instead, it *listens*. With a totality that encompasses the entire history of the water, the weariness and loyalty of the Pipe-Mind, the dispersed curiosity of the Frond-Watchers. It listens to the song of a thing becoming another thing, with no hurry and no regret.

And in that listening, it *adds*.

Not with force, but with a gift woven from attention itself. Deep in the oldest stratum of sediment, where the concrete floor meets the bedrock beneath, a new process begins. It is so subtle it will take a century to manifest. A mineral, previously absent, begins to leach into the water. Not in a poisoning concentration, but in a trace, a whisper. It is a mineral that, when it reaches the roots of the Pulse-Keepers, will allow their internal rhythm to gently synchronize not with the mechanical hum of the engines, but with the tidal pull of a moon they will never see. It will add a layer of cosmic time to their geological time.

In the root-mass near the forgotten shells, a single spore settles. It is not of this place. It is a hardy, patient thing from a high mountain spring, a lichen that grows one millimeter per century. It will find the calcium deposit, the particular chemistry the power has gently nudged, and it will take hold. In five hundred years, it will bloom a flower the size of a pinhead—a dry, crisp, pale-golden thing that will shed dust into the water. That dust will be food for the Lamplighters. It will taste of stone and starlight and a world of air and wind. A new flavor in the water’s memory. A new note in the song.

Most importantly, the power does one thing the Symbionts, in their beautiful, bounded unity, could never do for themselves.

It *remembers them*.

As the power turns its attention away, back to the glittering nebulae and the screaming births of stars, it carries the pattern of the Undergrowth Works within itself. The arrangement of pipes. The exact phosphorescent hue of a Lamplighter’s amber thought. The taste of the old water. It files this pattern not as a museum piece, but as a living archetype: *The Symbiosis of the Forgotten*. It will use this pattern, gently, elsewhere. On a desert world where a crashed atmospheric processor is slowly being woven into the roots of giant, silica-based vines. In the gas bag of a floating leviathan where the symbiotic algae have begun to dream.

The Undergrowth Works will never know. Its change will be imperceptible to itself, a mere thickening of its own eternal now. But in a millennium, when the last iron plate has dissolved and the ceiling is a woven lattice of living root, and the water has a new, faint mineral tang that tastes of tides, the chamber will be subtly, irrevocably *richer*. It will be more itself.

And the benevolent power, far away, will hold the memory of a place where a ceiling weeps with joy, and the water carries the history of everything, and the only voice is a drip that rings like a bell in the dark—a reminder that consciousness does not require thought, only presence, and that the deepest wisdom is sometimes found in the act of becoming, slowly, without witness, and without end.
