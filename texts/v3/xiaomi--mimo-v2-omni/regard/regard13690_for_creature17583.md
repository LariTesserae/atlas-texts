And it would act. Not with spectacle, but with a subtle, profound adjustment to the harmony.

First, it would touch the **Brief Visitants’** legacy. The rust-blasted rig, the shattered glass, the dead cables—these are dissonant notes, shards of a failed grammar imposed upon the place. The benevolent power would not erase them. To erase would be to deny the Visitants' own fierce, fleeting curiosity, a valid note in the cosmic chord. Instead, it would *weave them in*. The cables would be strung not as dead wires, but as the first trellises for a new, crystalline growth that sings in the steam. The power would introduce a new medium. It would cause the siliceous sinter, as it builds, to form not just terraces, but hollow, flute-like structures along the fissures. These would not be instruments to be played, but natural pipes, shaped by the very flow of steam and mineral deposition. The power might, with immense gentleness, fold a few centuries of patient growth into a handful of decades. Not a violent acceleration, but a deepening of the process. The sinter terraces would grow a little faster, their lace-like patterns becoming more complex, more beautiful. The razor edges of the obsidian would soften with a touch more speed, becoming obsidian cushions, black and gleaming. The colors of the Consortium would intensify, their ribbons thickening into velvety carpets, their hues becoming so vivid they would glow faintly in the perpetual steam, a soft internal light powered by their own alchemy. The wound would not be erased; it would be *adorned*. It would become unmistakably, undeniably sacred—not to any god, but to the processes of geology and life themselves.

The conversation between eternity and eternity deepens, now carrying a third, quiet note: the understanding that to be seen, with love and without interference, is the first and greatest form of healing.
