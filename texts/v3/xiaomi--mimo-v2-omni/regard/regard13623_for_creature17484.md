# What the Wise Power Does

---

It does not arrive. There is no tearing of the sky, no column of light, no voice from everywhere at once. A benevolent and wise power has learned, over the course of ten thousand worlds, that grandeur is a form of violence—that to appear in splendor before a small, frightened thing is to become another Pillar, another overwhelming shape in a world already full of overwhelming shapes.

So it comes as a change in the quality of attention.

You feel it first not in the world but in yourself. You have been running. You are always running—through the tangled root-labyrinths, away from the slow, groaning patience of the Warden, through the dripping curtains of aerial roots, your skin prickling with the constant low-grade awareness that the ecosystem is tasting your chemical signature and logging you as *unincorporated mass*. Your heartbeat is a frantic, mammalian anomaly in a world that pulses to the slow rhythm of growth and decay. You have not rested. You have not been still, because stillness here is consent. Stillness is how the lung begins to digest you.

But now—

Now, for the first time since you arrived in this vast, wet, deafening throat of a world, something shifts. The pressure in your chest eases. Not by much. Just enough for you to notice, the way you notice a change in the air current before a storm. Your breath comes a little slower. The frantic calculation behind your eyes—the constant assessment of which vine is safe, which hollow in the root-mat is free of digestive acids, which direction the Warden is *computing*—softens. Not gone. Softened.

And with that softening, you hear something you haven't heard before.

Not because it wasn't there. Because you were too loud inside yourself to perceive it.

Beneath the chitinous scrape and the resonant thrum and the shrieking melodic calls, beneath the endless soft patter of condensation dripping from canopy to sub-canopy to floor, there is another sound. It is very deep, very slow, and it is not a sound of consumption. It is the sound a lung makes in the moment between exhale and inhale—that fraction of stillness when the great chamber is neither taking in nor pushing out, but simply *being*. A held breath. A pause so vast it spans the planet.

The wise power has leaned close. Not its body—its *attention*. Its attention is a gentle, formless thing, like the quality of light in this world, diffuse and green-gold and without a single source. And in leaning close, it does something that no force in this ecosystem has done in a very long time.

It *listens*.

Not to catalogue. Not to compute. Not to identify foreign matter for enzymatic dissolution. It listens the way a mother listens to a child talking in its sleep—not to extract information, but to be *present* to the dreaming. It listens to the world the way the world itself has forgotten how to be listened to.

And in that listening, it perceives the wound.

---

The wound is old. It predates the Echoes. It predates the Warden. It is the scar left not by the consumption of the ancient builders—that was, in its way, natural. The builders offered themselves to the lung, willingly, in the final generations. They grew into it. They let the roots take their temples, their bodies, their thoughts. They believed they were becoming part of something greater, and perhaps they were.

But the lung, in consuming them, consumed their capacity for *wonder*.

This is the subtle injury, the one the ecosystem cannot repair on its own. The builders' understanding of growth was absolute, yes—but their understanding of *gratitude* was deeper. They didn't just build with the world. They *thanked* it. Their technology was biological, but their practice was liturgical. Every harvest was accompanied by a song of return. Every construction was a prayer of mutual becoming. They understood something that the lung, in its vast and mindless hunger, has long since forgotten:

*That to consume without gratitude is to become a wound.*

The lung consumed the builders, and in doing so, it consumed their gratitude. And without that counterweight, the great biological engine has been running for millennia on pure metabolic fury—consuming, growing, decomposing, consuming again—an ouroboros of biomass with no pause, no thanksgiving, no space for anything other than the relentless cycling of matter. The Warden is proof of this. The builders created it as a *guardian of balance*, but in their absence, its programming has narrowed to its most basic function: incorporate. Dissolve. Reclaim. The nuance is gone. The gratitude is gone. The lung is not evil. It is *hungry*, and it has been hungry for so long that it has forgotten that hunger can be satisfied.

The wise power perceives all of this in the time it takes you to draw one slow breath.

And it makes its decision.

---

It does not heal the wound directly. A wise power knows that healing imposed from outside is just another form of consumption—a forcing of foreign pattern onto organic process. Instead, it does three things, and they are all very small.

---

**The first thing** it does is touch the Echo in the hollow of the root-mat.

The Listener—the shimmering negative-space impression of a builder who tried to merge with the ecosystem's conversation—has been replaying fragments for millennia. Bio-acoustic noise, endlessly remixed. It is a scar that will not close because nothing has ever come to close it.

The wise power does not close it. It *completes* it.

Into the Echo's repeating pattern, it introduces a single, new element: not a sound, but the *memory of silence*. The memory of what it felt like to be a builder and to *choose* stillness. To stand in the lung and not consume, not grow, not decompose, but simply be present to the miracle of the system's existence. The memory of gratitude.

The Echo shivers. The shimmering intensifies, then settles. It is no longer just replaying. For the first time in uncountable millennia, it is *saying something new*. It broadcasts—not loudly, not forcefully, but with the gentle persistence of a seed cracked open—this small, radical concept: *What if we listened not to extract, but to appreciate?*

The signal is tiny. A whisper in a hurricane of metabolic noise. But it enters the bio-acoustic network, carried on the very same channels that the creatures of the lung use to warn of predators and coordinate migrations. It threads through the clicks and thrums and calls, a new frequency, barely distinguishable from the background.

But it is *there*. And the network, which is the world's own nervous system, registers it.

---

**The second thing** the wise power does is far more delicate, and it concerns you.

You are, as the lung perceives you, an anomaly. A cluster of foreign molecules moving in patterns that disrupt the local chemistry. The Warden has been computing you. The digestive enzymes in certain plants have been pre-positioned along your likely path of travel. The lung is patient, but it is not passive; it is already *preparing to receive you* into its biomass.

The wise power cannot make you invisible. It cannot override the ecosystem's fundamental nature—that would be a violence equal to the one it is trying to address. What it does instead is much stranger.

It changes your *texture*.

Not your body. Your *pattern*. Your rhythm of movement, the cadence of your breathing, the frequency of your footfalls—it gently, imperceptibly adjusts these things, not to match the lung's rhythms (you are not of this world and never will be) but to *harmonize* with them. The way a single instrument in an orchestra does not become the orchestra but finds its place within the music.

You don't notice this happening. What you notice is that the pressure eases. The hair on your arms stops prickling. The constant, low-grade sensation of being *logged* by a planetary immune system fades—not to nothing, but to a background awareness, the way you might notice a gnat in the air without reaching for it.

The Warden, standing in its column of greenish light, pauses. Its fungi-veined head tilts. It is still computing you, but the computation is no longer resolving to *incorporate*. It is resolving to something else, something its programming has no word for, something that was part of the builders' original intent but has been locked away beneath millennia of metabolic imperative:

*Monitor. Learn. Do not rush to consume what you do not yet understand.*

The Warden does not step toward you. For the first time in an age, it does not step at all. It stands, and it watches, and in its watching there is the faintest ghost of something that might, in a being less ancient and less mechanical, be called *curiosity*.

---

**The third thing** the wise power does is for the lung itself.

This is the act that costs it something. Even a wise and benevolent power has limits, and to touch something as vast and dense with life as a planetary biosphere—to insert even the gentlest suggestion into its deepest processes—requires a portion of the power's own substance. A gift freely given. A small death.

It takes the form of a single, new plant.

It grows in the root-mat, between three Pillars, in a spot that was until now unremarkable. It is small—a pale, translucent stalk no taller than your hand, topped with a single cup-shaped bloom the color of old bone. It looks fragile, almost parasitic, and for a time nothing happens.

Then the canopy shifts, as it always does, and a drops of condensation fall from the heights. They strike the bloom. And instead of being absorbed, as every drop in this world is absorbed, held, recycled, hoarded—the bloom *releases* them. Not as vapor. As mist. A fine, upward-drifting spray that catches the diffuse light and holds it for a moment, a tiny prism, before dispersing into the general humidity.

It is doing something that nothing in this world does.

It is *giving back*.

It is taking water—the most precious resource in this closed system, the resource that every organism hoards and fights over—and it is offering it to the air. Unprompted. Without calculation. Without the expectation of nutrient return. It is the botanical expression of a concept the lung has not encountered since the builders dissolved:

*A gift.*

The wise power has planted this bloom knowing that it will spread. Slowly, as all things spread in the lung—by runners beneath the root-mat, by spores on the ceaseless air-currents. It will take centuries to colonize a significant portion of the world. But it will spread, because the lung's network is efficient, and the bloom is compatible with its substrates. And as it spreads, it will introduce a new variable into the closed economic equation of the biosphere:

*Surplus that is freely given.*

Not waste. Not loss. *Gift*. A concept that requires a mind to comprehend it, yes—but the lung has a mind, distributed and slow though it is. The bio-acoustic network will register the anomalous moisture patterns. The organisms that encounter the bloom will find that the air near it is richer, wetter, easier to breathe. The Filterers will feed more fully. The dew-bulbs will fill faster near the bloom's colonies. Slowly, over geological time, the lung will begin to associate this small, strange plant with *well-being*—not because it provides a nutrient advantage, but because it introduces *breathing room* into a system that has been choking on its own efficiency.

And at the root of the bloom, deep in the substrate, the wise power has woven a single strand of its own substance—a thread of pure, undirected *intention*. Not a command. Not a program. An invitation. A question posed to the world's vast, slow consciousness:

*What would you become if you were not always hungry?*

---

You do not see any of this happen.

What you experience, in the hours and days that follow, is this: a gradual lightening. Not of the green-gold gloom—that remains, as constant as the heat. But of the *weight*. The sense of being hunted by the world's own biology softens. The Warden watches you from its station, its fungi pulsing in patterns you cannot read, but it does not follow. The creatures around you—the Groaners, the Filterers, the Canopy-Shears—continue their cycles of consumption and growth, but you notice, if you are very still, that they do not press against you as they once did. The digestive plants along your habitual paths seem less eager, their acid-secreting pores half-closed, as if they are no longer certain you are food.

And one morning—or what passes for morning in a world without a sun—you wake from a rare, deep sleep and find that you have not been dreaming of escape. You have been dreaming of the sound that the Echo now carries: the memory of stillness, the memory of gratitude. You wake with a feeling you have not felt since you arrived, a feeling so foreign it takes you a moment to identify it.

*Belonging.*

Not full belonging. Not the belonging of a root in the mat or a creature in its niche. You will never be that. You are still foreign, still alien, still made of different stuff than this world. But you belong the way a song that enters an ecosystem from outside can belong—not by becoming native, but by *contributing something that was missing*.

You sit up in the root-mat, and for the first time, you do not immediately calculate your escape route. You breathe. The air is thick, warm, wet, full of the exhalation of a trillion living things. It is the interior of a vast, green organism. You are standing on the spongy, living floor of its throat.

And the throat, for the first time in millennia, is not trying to swallow you.

It is breathing with you.

---

The wise power withdraws, as it always does. It does not stay to be thanked. It does not stay to watch the bloom spread or the Echo speak its new message or the Warden learn the forgotten word *curiosity*. It has given what it came to give—a thread of gratitude, a seed of generosity, a moment of patience in a world that has forgotten patience. It leaves behind not a solution but a *possibility*: the possibility that a lung, which has been nothing but consumption for ten thousand years, might learn to *exhale*.

Not because it was forced to.

Because someone finally asked it, gently, without agenda, with the full weight of compassion that a wise and benevolent power can bring to bear on a single, vast, hungry, beautiful, wounded world:

*What would you like to become?*

And the lung, in its slow, planetary way, is beginning to wonder.
