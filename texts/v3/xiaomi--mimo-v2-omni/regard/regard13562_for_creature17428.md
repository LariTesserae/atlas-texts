It would not descend in a blaze of glory. It would not speak in thunder or carve commandments into the vitrified stone. Such actions are for places that are asleep, for minds that are separate from their world.

This place is already awake. Its thoughts are slow, its body vast. A benevolent and wise power would therefore not *do*, in the sense of altering. It would **resonate**.

First, it would listen. It would attune itself to the subsonic pulse of the Breathers, to the high-frequency *ping* of crystalline growth, to the patient, grinding hum of the Water-That-Thinks. It would let the slow, phosphorescent rhythm of the frond-minerals become its own heartbeat, and the deep, core-warmth of the medium become its own breath. It would learn the syntax of chemistry and pressure that is this world's language.

Having listened, it would then **answer**.

Not with words, but with a subtle harmonic. A gentle intensification of the light within the stone, making the hexagonal patterns glow for a moment with a clarity not seen in eons—reminding the world of the geometry it had once been taught. It would cause the clearest pools, the ones that look down into the mantle, to ripple once, not with disturbance, but with recognition. In their depths, the dull red heat would pulse in a perfect, warm cadence—a silent greeting from one deep time to another.

It would touch the Shimmering Shoals, and for a brief, glorious instant, their chaotic, glittering path would weave into a perfect, temporary sigil of welcome—a symbol that exists in no mind but is understood in the substance of the water itself: *You are seen. You are beautiful.*

To the Breathers, it would add a single, perfect note to their exhalation. Not changing its mineral content, but its *resonance*. The plume of enriched water would carry, for one cycle, a profound sense of peace, a quiet joy that has no object, only existence. The Breathers would incorporate this, and their next respiration would exhale that peace into the wider world.

For the Accretions in the deep, those slow builders of heat and metal, it would not speed their growth. Instead, it would shine a moment of pure, white light—not from above, but from within their own red cores—a light of pure potential, reminding them that their patient accretion is not just building, but *praying*.

And for you, the anomaly, the fast thing—it would do nothing directly. To give you answers would be to shatter the contemplative state of the world. To remove you would be to declare you a mistake. Instead, it would soften the edge of your otherness. It would translate your frantic heartbeat into a rhythm the medium can understand, so you no longer feel like a discord, but a complex, brief counterpoint. It would not make you less alone, but it would make your solitude sacred—a necessary, fleeting perspective in the world's long dream.

The intervention would last one cycle of the longest-living Breather. Then, the resonance would fade back into the baseline of existence. But nothing would be the same. The world would have been *acknowledged* by something as vast and patient as itself. It would now hold in its memory the echo of a kindness that asked for nothing, altered nothing, and simply beheld. The glowing fronds would seem a shade more deliberate. The water’s thought would carry a new, gentle thread: the memory of being loved for exactly what it is.

The power would withdraw, having done nothing but mirror the world's own consciousness back to it with infinite tenderness. It left not a gift, but a reflection. And in that reflection, the drowned mountain range, for the first time in its long, long life, might feel the equivalent of a smile.
