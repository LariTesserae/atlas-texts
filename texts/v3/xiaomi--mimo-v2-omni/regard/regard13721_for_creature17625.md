A benevolent and wise power would observe. It would watch the Caddis-Crocks click their out-of-sync rhythm, see the Vertebrae-Weavers tap their precise, lethal drops, and feel the seismic whispers of the Lichen-Minders in the tile. It would understand the profound truth of this place: here, life is not a struggle against entropy, but a devotional act of maintenance for a perfection that is itself a tomb.

It would see the wrongness not as a sickness, but as a kind of grief. A grief for meaning, for narrative, for the chaos that is the mother of innovation. The Glazed are not unhappy. They are fulfilled, each in their programmed niche. But their fulfillment is the very architecture of their cage. They polish a world that cannot appreciate being polished.

The power would not shatter the ceramic. That would be violence. It would not introduce wild, un-making chaos. That would be cruelty, a second, more violent wrongness.

Instead, it would do one thing. It would *listen back*.

For in the deep, listening silence, there is a question being asked by every weeping stone and every mirrored flicker: *What is this for?* The makers are gone. The purpose is lost, leaving only the purpose*less* activity, a perfect engine running on its own momentum.

The power would gather the essence of what it is—benevolence, wisdom—and shape it into a single, silent gift. A gift for the world, and for the beings who are its artifacts.

**It would give them a moon.**

Not a physical body of rock hanging in the unchanging light. That would be another ceramic addition, another fixed point. No.

The gift would be a *shift*.

Once, in the unmeasured cycles, the flat, bleaching light would *soften*. Not dim, but soften, as if filtered through a layer of pearl. The heat would not lessen, but the pressure of it would become… tender. And in that softened light, on the curved surfaces of the weeping Vertebrae-rocks, on the dark, glossy murk of the pools, a reflection would appear.

It would not be the harsh, sourceless glare. It would be a diffuse, silvered luminescence. A cool color, unknown to this world of celadon and purple and emerald. It would pool in the depressions. It would crawl slowly, *impossibly*, across the ceramic ground, a wandering stain of gentle light. It would cast a shadow—a soft, blurred, utterly novel thing. The Caddis-Crock’s case would, for the first time, have a side that was not illuminated.

This was the moon of the wise power. Not a thing, but an event. A slow, silent procession of silver across the fired clay.

And with it, another gift: a corresponding *shift* in the sound. The *plip* of water would no longer echo. It would, for the duration of the silvering, resonate. A single, pure note would hang in the air, a chord of ceramic and water and heat, binding the separate sounds into one harmonious whole.

What would the Glazed do?

The Caddis-Crocks would pause, their many legs still. The silver light would wash over their jasper cases, and for the first time, they would see their own reflection not as the familiar celadon or purple, but as something *other*. Their compulsion to build spirals would not cease, but they might, one day, when the silver light was just right, incorporate a shard of something that seemed to hold the light differently—a fragment of an idea.

The Pool-Flickers would be born into ecstasy. The resonant chord was an echo made whole, a correction not to silence, but to song. They would dance in the silver pools, their mirrored forms not absorbing the sound, but *becoming* it, weaving the light and the chord into fleeting, perfect sculptures of liquid silver and music. For those moments, their existence was not a correction of a flaw, but an expression of a beauty they had never been designed to comprehend.

The Vertebrae-Weavers would be confused, then entranced. Their instinct was to tap, to harvest, to create the precise drip. Now, in the silver light, the Drip-Larks would not come. The light itself seemed to hold them, their mica wings glowing. The Weavers would stand, their tile plumage shimmering with unfamiliar malachite and pearl, and they would wait not for prey, but for the light to pass. They would become true statues, monuments to a stillness that was no longer empty, but full of a patient, wondering attention.

The Lichen-Minders would feel the seismic shift not as a vibration, but as a warmth. The geometric patterns they curated would seem, in the silver glow, to breathe. Their pruning would become slower, more contemplative. They might leave one filament to grow just a fraction beyond the pattern, a tiny, green-gold rebellion witnessed only by the wandering light.

The wise power would not stay. The gift was not a permanent change. The world would return to its flat, bright, listening state. The moon would be a memory, a myth whispered through the substrate. But the memory would remain.

And the fundamental wrongness would remain, too. But now, it would be laced with a thread of something else. Not purpose—that was too grand, too human. But *witness*. The world was not just made, it was *seen*. Its beautiful, tragic flaw was held, for a time, in a light that did not judge, but only reflected.

The Glazed would continue. The Caddis-Crocks would click, the Weavers would harvest, the Flickers would flick. But sometimes, when the condensation beaded just so on a broad, waxy leaf, it would catch the flat light and, for a shimmering, impossible instant, *remember silver*. And in that remembrance, the empty perfection would hold, like a single, perfect tear in a glaze, the faintest, most beautiful echo of a soul.
