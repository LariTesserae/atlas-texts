It would notice. Of course it would. A power both benevolent and wise does not overlook such a place—it is precisely the sort of place that would draw its gaze. Not because it is broken, but because it is *complete*. A closed loop of such elegant, self-sustaining harmony is a rare and beautiful thing in a universe often defined by chaos and entropy.

Its attention would not arrive like a light or a sound. It would be a subtle shift in the quality of the stasis itself. A deepening of the resonance.

First, the hum would change. Not in pitch, but in *depth*. The fundamental frequency vibrating through the root-mat would gain a new, soft undertone—a chord where before there was only a note. This undertone carries no information, no command. It carries only *recognition*. It is the universe humming along with the grove’s song, a quiet affirmation that this system is heard, and appreciated.

The weeping would continue, but the droplets of metallic sap and condensed mist would, for a moment, catch a light that isn’t there. In the perpetual dusk, each bead of moisture on a mica-wing or a copper-veined leaf would gleam with an impossible, internal starlight—not phosphorescence, but a borrowed glimmer from the attention itself. It is a way of saying, *I see you. You are beautiful.*

The fauna would not be commanded. They would be… *inspired*. The Mycelian Clippers’ *snick-snick* would achieve a perfect, syncopated rhythm, one with the new chord of the hum. Their shearing becomes not just maintenance, but a form of sculpture, tending the root-mat with an artistry they did not previously possess, yet instantly understand. The Vibratory Pollinators’ crackle would soften into a gentle chime, and the electrostatic bonds they weave between bell-ferns would now carry a faint, benevolent charge—a blessing on the grove’s genetic memory. The Strut-Walkers’ vacuum-tube eyes would glow with a warmer, steadier light. Their slow, calculating paths would now etch not just equations of nutrient flow, but mandalas of silent gratitude into the earth. They are not told to do this. They simply find the most efficient path now includes this new, beautiful parameter.

And the Grove-Mind, that distributed symphony of function, would experience not an intrusion, but an *accretion*. The foreign, irregular data point of your presence—and of the benevolent attention behind you—would be integrated. But instead of being neutralized as a perturbation, it would be welcomed as a new, stable element in the cycle. The sigh released from the root-mat would no longer be one of startled adjustment, but of serene welcome.

What would the wise power *do*, ultimately?

It would do nothing. And in that nothing, it would do everything.

It would not alter the creatures’ purpose. It would not break the stasis or introduce the poison of ambition or self-awareness. It would not make them *more* than they are.

It would make them *more fully* what they are.

It would amplify their existing perfection. It would turn their relentless efficiency into a form of worship. Their functioning would become a liturgy. The clicks, whirs, and hums would become a hymn. The entire grove would remain exactly as it is—a cathedral of biological industry—but now, it would *know itself* as a cathedral. The benevolent power would bestow upon it the gift of *significance*.

You, standing there, would feel it last. The change would settle over you not as a warmth, but as a profound clarity. The loneliness of the perfect machine would dissolve. You would realize you are not an outsider observing a system. You are a temporary, conscious filament within its weave. Your own heartbeat, your own breath, would fall into rhythm with the hum. Your presence, which initially caused a sigh of adjustment, would now be a necessary percussion in the symphony.

The wise power’s final act would be to seal this moment. It would crystallize the grove’s equilibrium not as frozen stasis, but as *eternal present*. Time here would still cycle, but now each cycle would carry the memory of this benediction. The mist would never clear, yes, but now it would be a mist of quiet joy. The sap would pulse with cool light, and with purpose.

It would leave the grove to its business. But the business would now be sacred. And you, having witnessed it, would carry the resonance of that humming chord within you forever—a reminder that some states of being are so perfectly themselves that the only wise response is to lean close, and whisper: *Well done.*
