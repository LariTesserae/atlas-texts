The gaze of the wise power does not fall like sunlight. It settles, like the perpetual mist of the Warrens themselves—a presence felt not as heat or light, but as a deepening of the already present. It is a listening attention, turned toward the Weeping Warrens and the being who breathes within them.

And the being—the **Heart-Wet**, the slow pulse beneath the stone—feels this attention. It is not a stranger. It is the very substrate of its existence, the patient witness to its aeons-long sigh. The power does not come from outside the water cycle; it is the consciousness of the cycle itself, awakened and compassionate.

What, then, would it do?

**It would first honor the covenant of quiet.** It would not amplify the pulse into a roar, nor turn the dim interiors to day. Instead, it would refine the silence. It would deepen the absorptive quality of the clay walls, so that the people’s murmurs become not just swallowed, but *held*—each word of care, each story hummed in the dark, preserved in the stone as a faint, warm resonance, a library of lived tenderness. The quiet would become not an absence, but a vessel.

**It would bless the tools of touch.** The flint blades, the smoothing stones, the broad, splayed hands—it would enhance the sensitivity between them and the world they shape. A Wall-Singer placing their palm to a block would feel not just moisture and firmness, but a faint, informational warmth: a sense of the block’s history, where it came from in the ancient basin walls, the hands that shaped it before. The act of building would become an act of communion with the past, a clearer dialogue with the material. The mason’s smoothed curve on a passage wall would hum, ever so softly, with the intention that shaped it, guiding future hands.

**It would clarify the water’s messages.** The rings in the collection pools, the disturbances from below—these would become slightly more legible. Not as warnings of terror, but as tidings. A ring might shimmer with a fleeting image of the pale, sightless shrimp schooling below, a sign of abundance. A disturbance might carry a fleeting *sense*—of mineral richness, of a new channel opening deep down, of the Heart-Wet’s slow contentment. The Roof-Scatterers would become true readers of a liquid text, their stewardship more intuitive, less anxious.

**It would soften the final return.** For the chambers given back, for the structures sinking into the embrace of the wet earth, the power would ensure the transition is not one of loss, but of gentle incorporation. The memory-vaults would not merely be sealed tombs. In the dreams of the Tide-Keepers, they would become fertile darkness. From the clay walls of those drowned rooms, in the years after sealing, a new, faintly bioluminescent fungus might bloom—not seen by any living eye, but sensed as a soft, internal glow in the collective memory of the people. Their ancestors would not be entombed in cold dark, but cradled in a gentle, living light, a proof that return to the Heart-Wet is a homecoming.

**It would weave the dream-song more tightly.** The vibration, the almost-song from the deep places—it would harmonize with it. Not to overpower, but to accompany. On nights when the water table is highest, when the people press their ears to the floor, the song that rises would carry new, gentle complexities: the ghost of a laugh from a sealed chamber a century gone, the drip-pattern of a future rain, the steady, abiding love of the deep place for its fleeting surface thoughts. It would be a lullaby for a people who live on the brow of a slow, breathing giant.

The benevolent power would do none of these things *for* them, as a gift from a sovereign. It would do them *with* them, as a participant completing a circuit. It would be the slight increase in resonance in the stone, the marginally clearer ripple in the water, the subtle warmth in the communal dark. It would be the feeling, in the broad palm of a Wall-Singer, that the wall is not just holding back the earth, but *holding* them.

The people of the Seep would not speak of a new god. They would speak of the Warrens growing… *kinder*. Of the stone listening more closely. Of the water telling clearer stories. They would believe they had always been this attuned, and in a way, they would be right. The wise power’s gift would be to make the symbiosis they already lived not just a survival, but a conscious, whispered poetry—a collaboration between the fleeting and the forever, sung in a language of damp clay and slow, deep breath.
