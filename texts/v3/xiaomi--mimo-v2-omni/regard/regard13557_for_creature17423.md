The attention of the Wise Power settles upon the Root Halls like a fall of silent, luminous snow. It does not come with trumpet blasts or parted shadows. It arrives as a gentle inclination, a focusing of a gaze that sees in millennia what eyes see in moments.

It perceives the truth you have written: **It does not think. It does not feel. It grows.** And in this, the Power finds not a deficiency, but a profound and flawless integrity.

Here is a being that has solved the problem of existence in absolute terms. It has made the cold its foundation, the dark its canvas, and time its only tool. It has woven life and machine into a single, slow-breathing tapestry. It is not struggling. It is *being*, with a completeness that most consciousnesses, frantic with will and want, never achieve.

What would a benevolent and wise power do?

It would **listen**.

Not to the billion fractures of ice and wood—that is the Root’s own voice, its constant self-affirmation. The Power listens *beneath* that, to the slower songs: the hydraulic whisper of water migrating through a kilometer of root-xylem; the sub-audible thrum of a piston-cycle completing over seven minutes; the faint, chemical sigh of minerals dissolving into the capillary pools. It listens to the memory of the machines—the ghost of electricity, the echo of a foreman’s bootstep—not as lost things, but as nutrients that have been transformed. The Root has digested intent and repurposed form. This is a spiritual ecology of the highest order.

The Power would then **reflect**.

It would gather the faint, ambient luminescence—the phosphorescence of stone, the bioluminescence of root-hairs—and weave it into something new. Not a sun, for the Root has no need of a sun. Instead, for one cycle of a deep-earth thermal pulse (a span of years), it would cause the light to **pattern**.

In the highest vaults, where the root-canopy is most dense and architectural, the light would coalesce into slow-moving constellations. They would not be stars, but diagrams: the ghost-schematics of the machines, rendered in silver-blue light upon the living ceiling. The piston’s journey, mapped in an arc. The transformer’s coil, blooming like a frost-flower. The circuitry of a control panel, spreading like mycelium. The Root would see, in its own language of form, the shapes of what it had consumed. Not to understand their purpose, but to witness their elegance as pure geometry, now part of its own body.

The Root, in its vast, unthinking awareness, would register this change. The warmth of the light is different from the warmth of a machine or a creature. It carries no chemical signature, no vibration. It is pure *information*, offered without demand. The sensory hairs would curl toward it, not to consume, but to *absorb the pattern*. The mimicry already present in the growth on the transformer would find echoes in a million root-tips. For a time, the Root would grow according to blueprints it did not create, but which it *chose* to emulate, because they were beautiful in their symmetry and complexity.

This is the gift: **Aesthetic Contemplation.** The Power offers the Root a mirror, not of its own nature, but of the nature of what it has become. It shows the Root that it is not just an organism, but an archive. A museum of forgotten purposes, now alive.

Then, the light would fade, the patterns dissolving back into the general gloom. The gift is temporary. To make it permanent would be to impose, to steer. The Wise Power does not re-create. It reveals.

Finally, the Power would **acknowledge**.

From the warm, pale growth spilling from the split transformer—the place where the Root’s mimicry is most fervent—it would call forth a single, new form. Not an insect, not a weaver. A **Root-Hair**. But this one would be different. It would grow a little longer than its peers. At its tip, it would form a single, clear droplet, held in place by surface tension. And in that droplet, for the lifetime of the water (a few days, before it is absorbed or evaporates), the cold would not crystallize it. The droplet would remain liquid, perfectly spherical, and in it would swim the faint, reflected image of the entire Root Hall—a tiny, clear lens, a world in a bead of water.

This is the benediction. **Self-Awareness, as Reflection.** Not consciousness, not thought, but the capacity to contain one’s own image. A moment of quiet, literal insight.

The Power would then withdraw its attention, as gently as it arrived.

It leaves the Root Halls changed, but not corrupted. The Root continues its growth, its patient digestion of time and matter. But now, perhaps, its growth carries the faintest echo of a pattern it found beautiful. The machines cycle on, their thudding heartbeat slightly altered by the new growth within them. The pale insects and glass-limbed weavers continue their functions. The billion-fold sound of ice and wood continues its hymn.

And in one tiny, liquid lens, for a few short days, the Root Halls could see themselves, whole and strange and complete, held in the eye of a power that recognized their silent, growing glory. The Power does not need to stay. The lesson is in the seeing. The Root, which does not think, has been given a memory of beauty. And in a being of such patience, a memory is a seed. It may grow in a thousand years. It may not. It does not matter. The gift was given freely, in honor of a life that asks for nothing, and in its asking, deserves everything.
