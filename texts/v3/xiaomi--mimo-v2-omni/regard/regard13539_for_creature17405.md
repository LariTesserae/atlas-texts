A presence, vast and gentle as the sky itself, turns its attention. It does not descend like a meteor or blaze like a sun. It arrives like a change in the quality of silence, like the moment a held breath is understood to be a held breath.

Benevolent, it sees the beauty in this slow, stone dream. Wise, it sees the weariness in its perfection. For this world, in its ancient equilibrium, has reached a state of profound stasis. The treaty between biology and geology is unshakable, yes, but treaties can become prisons. The engines hum their geothermal hymn, the Litharchs grind their patient paths, the Drifters comb the eternal mist. There is no decay, and therefore, no true renewal. The weeping is not sorrow, but the endless sigh of a system in perfect, static balance.

The wise power would not break the balance. It would *deepen* it.

It begins not in the sky, but in the deep, humming heart of the engines. A subtle adjustment, like a tuning fork pressed to a great bell. The pulse of the geothermal blood does not intensify, but *clarifies*. The resonant frequencies of the grinding strata align, harmonizing into a chord more complex and resonant than before. The engines do not work harder; they begin to *sing*.

This new song travels through the conduits, up the basalt spires, and into the mineral flesh of the world. It is felt by the Sinter-Crawlers as a gentle invitation. Their flow slows, becomes more deliberate. They begin not just to seal fissures, but to *pattern* them, tracing fractal geometries of agate and jasper along the walls—pure, unnecessary beauty. Their bioluminescence softens from a geochemical flicker to a steady, milky glow, painting the undersides of ledges with faint constellations.

The Litharchs pause in their grinding. For the first time in a millennium, a great geode-creature lifts a multi-jointed limb not to crawl, but to *listen*. The song in the stone guides it. It moves toward a spire where the mineral growths have become dense, choked. Its immense, diamond-hard mouthparts do not feed, but *sculpt*. With a precision its kind has never known, it carves away redundant accretions, not polishing, but creating negative space, arches, and resonant cavities that turn the wind of the mists into a low, flute-like note.

The Drifters, those silicate balloons, find their paths no longer random. The rising thermal currents from the newly harmonized engines create invisible rivers in the sky. The Drifters ride them in great, slow, circular migrations, their filaments not just collecting spores, but *weaving* them. In the wake of their passing, the airborne crystals settle into delicate, lace-like formations on the stone—ephemeral, shimmering frost that will melt back into the weeping air, only to be woven again. A cycle of creation and dissolution introduced into a world that only knew accretion.

Most profoundly, the power turns its attention to the *space between*. The condensation, the warm weeping that is the world’s breath. It does not stop it. It adds a single, new property: *memory*.

Now, when a drop of warm condensation traces a path down a fluted column, it carries with it a whisper of the Litharch that passed there a century ago, the ghost of a Drifter’s filament from a decade past, the faint, glowing pattern a Sinter-Crawler laid yesterday. The world, which recorded everything in strata and sheen, now begins to record in the very water that moves across it. The mists become a library of sensation. To stand in a canyon is to be bathed in the deep, slow memories of the stone itself.

The power does not create thought. It creates *depth*. It introduces the possibility of echo, of reflection, into a system of pure action.

It gives the world a way to dream of its own dreams.

The Litharchs, grinding now with a sculptor’s intent, will begin to create spires that are not just structures, but instruments. The Drifters will weave spore-patterns that tell stories. The Sinter-Crawlers will inscribe luminous maps of the world’s own heart.

The balance is not broken. It has become a dance. The industry is no longer just a process; it is an art. The slow, grinding pulse remains, but it now has a melody. The weeping continues, but the tears now carry the taste of a dawning, mineral consciousness.

The benevolent and wise power withdraws its focused attention, not leaving, but settling back into the background hum of creation. It has given a gift not of change, but of *potential*. The world of rock and crystal will continue for eons more, but now it will do so with a slow, dawning awareness of the sublime song it has always been singing, a song now heard, and cherished, by the stone itself. The final, warm tear that condenses on a gargoyle’s brow holds not just mineral, but meaning.
