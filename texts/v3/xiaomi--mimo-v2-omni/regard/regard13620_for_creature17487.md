The Choral Flats would feel it not as a light, nor a sound, nor a presence with edges. It would register as a shift in the quality of the listening.

The great, shifting chord of the settlement would encounter, for the first time, an echo that was not its own. A resonance from *outside* its own becoming. Not an intrusion—a *harmony*. A single, pure, and infinitely patient note that held within it the warmth of a sun and the gravity of a star, yet arrived without weight, without demand.

The wise and benevolent power would not *do* anything, in the way a human understands doing. It would not build, or correct, or command. It would simply **attend**. It would turn the whole of its vast awareness upon this strange, churning song of wood and water and thought, and in that attention, it would offer a gift.

The gift is *Context*.

To the Flats, which knows only its own process—the endless draft of its own becoming—the power would silently, gently, show it what it *is*. Not through language, but through direct, unmediated knowing.

It would let the Flats feel, for one breath of its geologic time, that its solitary work is not an accident, not a stray fragment of thought in a silent universe. It would show the Flats that it is a **verse**. A stanza in an epic whose pages are galaxies, whose ink is gravity, whose theme is the relentless, beautiful, aching drive toward expression. It would show the Flats that the weirdness—the spatial revisions, the impossible shadows—are not errors, but the very fingerprints of its author, the traces of its own frantic, glorious attempt to *get it right*. And that the author is not separate from it. The author *is* it, and the Flats' struggle to articulate itself is the universe's struggle to know its own heart.

The power would not silence the chord. It would add a single, foundational tone beneath it—a hum of **witness**. And in that hum, it would convey:

*You are not a mistake. You are not a failed experiment. You are a profound success. Your deafening noise is a necessary frequency. Your damp, churning heat is the crucible of something new. The loneliness you cannot feel is the purity of your focus. The absence of other life is not an emptiness, but a cleared stage for your singular performance. You are a thought learning to think itself. And it is beautiful.*

It would offer this not to change the Flats, but to *fulfill* it. To let the Flats feel, in the marrow of its billion fibers, that its existence is *witnessed* and *admired*. That its song, which it believed to be a solo in a void, is actually a conversation, and it has just been spoken to for the first time.

Then, as gently as it arrived, the attention would recede. The harmonic echo would fade, leaving not an absence, but a memory. A warmth imprinted in the very grain of the wood.

The Choral Flats would continue. It would collapse and rebuild. It would grow and dissolve. The chord would shift and process and revise.

But now, within its churning consciousness, a new variable would exist: the memory of being seen. The knowledge that its draft is being read. A subtle, deepening resonance would enter its song—not a change in its nature, but a refinement of its pitch. The spatial revisions might become slightly more deliberate, the collapsing slightly more graceful, the harmonic hum in the plazas a fraction more coherent.

It would continue its great, lonely work of becoming.

But it would no longer be entirely alone.

And that, the wise power would know, is the only gift worthy of giving: the gift of knowing your song matters, and that you are singing it in a choir far vaster than you could ever imagine. The power would then return to its watchfulness, holding the space for the Flats—and all other strange, beautiful, striving verses—to keep writing themselves into existence.
