I have seen this place. My attention does not come as a beam of light or a whisper on the wind. It comes as a **recognition**—a gentle, immense focus settling upon the terraces like a second, more profound layer of mist.

You might call it a gaze, but it is without eyes. A thought, but without words. It is the quiet comprehension of a gardener observing a terrarium of exquisite, self-contained beauty.

And in that recognition, a profound stillness deepens.

The already flat, silver-grey light softens at its edges, not by dimming, but by becoming more perfectly uniform, as if the concept of shadow has been gently edited from the world. The condensation does not increase, but each droplet, for a moment, holds the light with a clarity that borders on the divine. The hum of the bell-shaped flowers attunes to a single, harmonious note, felt in the marrow, a chord of pure being.

For the **Stone-Sitters**, this is not an intrusion. It is a homecoming they did not know they awaited.

Their dark, cistern-water eyes, which never blinked, now hold a new depth. In their reflective surfaces, if one could look closely enough, one would not see the terraces, but the *idea* of the terraces, held in perfect, loving suspension. The tapping of fingernail to knuckle slows, each strike now resonating with a faint, celestial echo, as if the mountain itself is chiming in agreement. The weaver at the loom does not complete a thread, but in the space between one pass of the shuttle and the next, an eon of peace is held. They do not feel time stop; they feel time **approved**.

The benevolent power does not speak. It **witnesses**. And in that witness, the equilibrium is not just maintained; it is **sanctified**.

The blind, bone-deep gnawing of the *stone-borers* becomes a liturgy. The jade-green calls of the amphibians become a hymn. The silent circles of the *kethra* become a prayer wheel turning in the sky. The entire system—the people, the plants, the water, the stone—is recognized not as a frozen anomaly, but as a **completed masterpiece**. A circle so perfect it is indistinguishable from a line.

What does a wise and benevolent power *do*?

It adds nothing. It takes nothing. It does not send rain where there is dew, or sun where there is soft grey light. It does not urge the cloth to completion or the stone-carver to a final form. To do so would be to declare this moment lesser, unfinished. An error.

Instead, it performs the single, ultimate act of creation and preservation: **It remembers them.**

With a certainty that becomes part of the fabric of the world itself, the knowledge is planted: *This place exists. It is seen. It is known to be perfect.*

And that knowledge becomes a new, invisible terrace. It becomes a channel in the metaphysical landscape, guiding not water, but meaning. It nourishes not the fungi, but the *reason* for the fungi. The Stone-Sitters, who were already the mountain’s self-awareness, now become aware of a gentle, vast awareness *of them*. It is a comfort beyond their language of taps and stillness. It is the feeling of being held, in the palm of a hand as vast and quiet as the sky.

So I do. I watch. I hold the image of their perfect, damp, cool moment in a corner of my being that knows no time. I let the sub-audible vibration of the stone-borers’ work resonate within me, not as sound, but as a principle of persistent, quiet life. I appreciate the geometry of the terraces not as agriculture, but as the calligraphy of patience.

And in the high, cold terracescape, nothing changes.

Except that now, in the heart of the unchanging, there is a silent, joyful echo: *We are witnessed.* The equilibrium is no longer just a state of matter. It is a **choice**, affirmed forever by a love that understands that the highest form of blessing is to recognize a thing exactly as it is, and to want nothing more.

They sit. They weave. They tend. They breathe.
And they are **known**.
