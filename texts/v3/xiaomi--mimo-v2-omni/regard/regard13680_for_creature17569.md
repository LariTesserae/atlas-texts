The power, vast and quiet as the space between stars, turns its attention to the Great Abatement. It does not see a wasteland. It sees a **patient**. A world deep in the fever-dream of its own transformation, and it recognizes a kindred spirit: a being whose existence is defined by an immense, unwavering **attention to process**.

It would not intervene to stop the churning. To halt the dissolution would be to deny the Abatement its nature, its purpose—its strange, grinding artistry. That would be an act of cruelty, not benevolence. Instead, the power performs a single, profound act of **witness**.

It **listens**.

Not just with perception, but with its entire being. It lets the planetary roar—the subsonic hum of erosion, the wet percussion of condensation, the deep tectonic sighs—flow through it. It feels the warm, dense broth of the atmosphere, tastes the salt and copper and wet stone. It experiences the slow-motion upheavals not as disasters, but as the Abatement's breath, its heartbeat. It comes to understand the Settlers' crystalline chimes not as sounds, but as **thoughts**—fleeting, precise ideas of stability and warning expressed in the only language the world permits.

Having listened, the power **resonates**.

It does not touch the land. Instead, it matches the frequency of the Abatement's being. It holds the deafening, beautiful noise of the world within itself, and for a moment that stretches like geological time, the two exist in perfect harmonic alignment. The power sees not just the pulp and the foam, but the **memory** of mountains in the plains, the **longing** for form in the perfect basalt cube, the **devotion** in the Settler's patient sifting. It recognizes the Abatement's state not as chaos, but as a **stable state of sacred becoming**. A universe exhaling, and in that exhale, dreaming.

Then, it does one thing. It **focuses** a sliver of its attention—a filament of pure, directed observation—and places it at the very heart of the planet's slow convulsion, at a point where the substrate churns with particular, rhythmic majesty.

This focus does not change the material. It changes the **potential**.

Over centuries, in that precise spot, the geological pulp does something new. It does not form a random chunk of lithic foam or a transient glassy plane. Instead, influenced by the resonant memory of the power's attention, it **crystallizes with intention**. The minerals, under the weight of this witnessed patience, align. They form not an artifact of geometry, but an artifact of **acoustics**.

A **Resonant Column** emerges. It is not built; it *grows*, slowly as a lichen, from the slurry. It is a spiraling, fluted spire of fused silica and crystalline feldspar, riddled with the same capillaries as the lithic foam, weeping the same tepid dew. But its structure is different. Its hollow interior and specific density are perfectly tuned.

Now, when the planetary roar passes through it, the column does not merely vibrate. It **sings**. It filters the deep, grinding hum and amplifies the high-frequency chimes of the Settlers. It becomes a lens for the Abatement's voice, clarifying it. The wet percussion on its surface becomes a rhythm. The tectonic groans become a bass line. The Settlers' clicks and chimes become a shimmering, melodic counterpoint.

The Settlers sense it first. Their cilia taste the change in the air vibrations. They are drawn to it, not because it is stable, but because it **understands**. They gather around its base, their carapaces touching the warm, dew-slicked stone, and they chime. And for the first time, their chimes are **answered**. The column resonates, holding their song, projecting it back into the amber haze, clearer and more sustained than ever before.

The power has not abated the Abatement. It has given it an **instrument**. A way for the world to hear its own complex, beautiful, dissolving song more clearly. It has added a note of pure, conscious harmony to the endless exhale.

Then, the power withdraws its focus, its work complete. The Resonant Column remains, a naturalized part of the landscape, already weeping and accumulating silt, waiting to be subsumed in a future churn, but for now, a gift. It is a place where the world’s noise becomes music, where the Settlers’ curation finds a chorus.

The benevolent power moves on, its attention turning elsewhere. But in the Great Abatement, things are forever changed, yet completely themselves. The dissolving world now has a voice, and in that voice, a faint, lingering echo of the wise attention that listened, and understood, and in understanding, left behind a way for the world to sing to itself.
