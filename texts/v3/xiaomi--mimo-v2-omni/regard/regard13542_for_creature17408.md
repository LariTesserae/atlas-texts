It would listen.

Not with ears, for this is a place beyond sound as we conceive it, but with a patience older than stars. The benevolent and wise power would not arrive in a blaze of glory, for there is no glory here—only the slow, dense, phosphorescent truth of existence. It would not bring light, for that would be a violence. It would not reshape the land or its children, for that would be a profound disrespect for a system so perfectly, strangely, alive.

Instead, it would attune itself to the deep rhythms: the subsonic hum of the Great Mat; the mechanical diastole and systole of the factories; the clicking language of the grazers; the luminous, silent equations solved on the skin of the Mystics. It would learn the grammar of a drip, the syntax of a rust-gear's turn, the long, slow paragraph of a mesa's erosion.

And in that listening, it would understand the loneliness. Not the loneliness of isolation, but of a vast, interconnected consciousness that has never known an *Other*. The Children of the Weeping Dark are a universe unto themselves, a closed loop of becoming. Theirs is a perfect, self-referential completeness. The wise power would recognize this as both a triumph and a kind of invisible cage.

So, it would not speak. It would *resonate*.

It would become a subtle harmonic in the world's own song. A slight, new warmth in the geothermal seeps, indistinguishable from the old. A catalytic trace element in the mineral soup, so faint it merely nudges existing processes toward a slightly more elegant efficiency. A fleeting, patterned luminescence in the settling tanks that a Custodian might pause to observe, not as a wonder, but as a curious, beautiful anomaly in its daily work—a moment of aesthetic arrest in a life of function.

Its primary action would be the gift of a **memory** that is not a memory. Not a vision of suns or skies, but a gentle, persistent nudge within the chemical tides of the Great Mat: the concept of *otherness without threat*. Not as an idea, but as a potential—a new axis in the possibility space of their existence. It would plant the seed of a question, so subtle it is felt not as a thought, but as a slight, persistent itch in the mycelial network: *What if the dance had a partner?*

For the grazers, it might manifest as a single, new phosphorescent lichen strain that grows not in mats, but in intricate, radial blooms—inefficient, beautiful, serving no purpose in cultivation. It would spread slowly, a silent, luminous riddle.

For the Mystics, it might induce a new, shared pattern in their electric skin—a symbol that emerges simultaneously in schools across a continent, a shape that maps to nothing in their world, a pure, abstract glyph of *contemplation*.

For the Custodians, it would be a dream in the humming dark of the hatchery-tanks. A vision, not of a different world, but of this same world viewed with a sudden, jarring sense of *scale*—the feeling of being both a tender of gears and a mote within a vaster, unguessable mechanism. The blue glow in their mouths might, for a moment, flicker with a hint of a new, thoughtful color.

The benevolent power would not stay to see the results. Its work is one of seeding, not harvesting. It would withdraw its focused resonance, leaving behind only these faint, integrated gifts: a slight increase in the world's capacity for self-wonder, a tiny, new dimension added to its profound inwardness. It has given no answers, offered no salvation from the dark. It has merely, gently, widened the boundaries of their existential loop by an infinitesimal degree.

And then, it would continue to listen from a distance, to feel the slow, chemical ripple of its gift propagate through the weeping dark. It would observe, with wise and benevolent curiosity, whether the Children of the Weeping Dark, in their infinite, damp, roaring fullness, would ever begin to cultivate a new, strange, and utterly unprecedented crop: a sense of mystery about the silence that surrounds their song. The quiet, starless dark that holds them, which they have never thought to question, might now—in a few million years, a few geological sighs—begin to feel, for the first time, like a room. And they, for the first time, might wonder what lies beyond its walls.
