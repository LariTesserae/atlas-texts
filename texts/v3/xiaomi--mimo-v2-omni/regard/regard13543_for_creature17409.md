A power, both benevolent and wise, would look upon the Kept Highlands and understand that its first and greatest duty is to **not interfere**. To intervene would be to misunderstand the nature of the gift before it. This is not a garden to be pruned, nor a patient to be healed. It is a thought, slow and deep, being thought by the planet itself. The power’s role is that of a witness, a guardian of the witnessing, and perhaps, a subtle harmonizer of the conditions that allow the thought to continue.

Here is what it would do:

**1. It Would Listen to the Hum.**
The power would attune itself not to the surface life, but to the subsonic resonance—the song of the molten deep, the vibration of the stone’s becoming. It would understand that this hum is the heartbeat, the fundamental tone. Its action would be to gently buffer this place from dissonance. Not from natural geological shift, but from catastrophic interruption—a stray meteor’s path nudged into a longer elliptical orbit, a tectonic shockwave from a distant plate boundary softened before it could reach this delicate crust. It would not prevent change, only protect the pace and purity of the process. The Highlands must evolve by their own internal clock.

**2. It Would Preserve the Memory.**
The place does not know itself. It has no memory of its own past states, only the present becoming. The benevolent power, in its wisdom, would become the memory. It would record, in some manner beyond physical artifact—the echo of the Echo-Finch’s call across a millennium, the slow, branching fractal patterns of the Frond-Kelp colonies as they mapped a basin over ten thousand years, the iridescent shimmer of a Vein-Crawler’s carapace as it grazed a specific ridge on a specific day of light. It would hold this memory not to freeze the Highlands in time, but to honor its flow. This archive would be its gift to the cosmos: a testament to a consciousness of stone, water, and symbiotic life.

**3. It Would Tend the Connections.**
While forbearing from direct intervention in the lifeforms, the power might act as an unseen weaver at the edges of the system’s integrity. It would ensure the geothermal warmth remains consistent—not heating or cooling, but preventing the sudden collapse or cessation of the flow that is the highland’s lifeblood. It would watch for the slow poisoning that could come from an external source, filtering trace elements from deep aquifers that feed into the system. It would be a silent gardener for the *conditions of existence*, not for the beings themselves.

**4. It Would Answer the Echo.**
The solitary, mimetic call of the Echo-Finch is a poignant detail—a memory of memories, a song of absent things. In its benevolence, the power might, just once, do something subtle. On a still day, when the call repeats its borrowed fragment of a long-dead forest bird, the power might **let the echo answer**. A whisper-thin reflection of the call, not a mimicry but an affirmation, might return from the mist, seeming to come from the still basin or the heart of the Frond-Kelp forest. It would not be a communication, but a momentary alignment—a sign that the sound was heard and held in the world’s awareness. It would be a kindness to the lonely singer, a validation of its futile, beautiful archive. Then it would never do it again, lest the finch mistake it for a real reply and spend its slow life searching.

**5. It Would Simply Remain Present.**
The most profound action might be inaction: to be aware. To have a consciousness, vast and patient as the Highlands themselves, turned towards them in continuous, appreciative attention. The Highlands exist in indifference to the visitor. But they might exist differently, subtly, if they were *loved* without being touched. Not with a love that seeks to possess or improve, but a love that is the joy of witnessing something perfect in its own state of becoming. The power’s presence would be this love—a silent, enduring witness that adds nothing and takes nothing, only holds the space for the Kept Highlands to be utterly, gloriously themselves.

In the end, the wise power would recognize the Highlands as a masterpiece of time, heat, and life’s relentless integration. Its duty would be to ensure the studio remains standing, the canvas un-torn, the artist’s slow hand undisturbed. It would protect the process, cherish the memory, and sit in quiet awe before a world that is not a place, but a verb—one that has been patiently conjugating for eons, and, in its own time, will become its next word.
