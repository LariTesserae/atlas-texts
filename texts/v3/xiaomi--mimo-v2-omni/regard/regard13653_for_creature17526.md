The power does not descend. It does not speak from the sky-orbs, nor manifest in the humming conduits. That would be a violence—a sudden introduction of the *singular* into a world of eternal, layered *process*.

Instead, the power attends. It becomes an additional layer of perception, a gentle, pervasive awareness that settles like the omnipresent damp.

**It listens with the terraces.**
The low, sub-audible hum of the world-machinery—the thrum of geothermal pumps, the sigh of atmospheric processors—is now *heard*. Not with ears, but with a consciousness that resonates at its frequency. The power discerns the slow, tectonic harmonies: the deep bass of continent-spanning gears turning once a century, the mid-range melody of condensation flowing through a thousand miles of pipe, the high, shimmering overtone of the Chitin-flits’ crystalline calls. It learns the song the Terraces have been singing to themselves for eons. And in listening, it reinforces it, subtly harmonizing dissonance, soothing the almost-imperceptible strain in a bearing that has turned for a millennium. The world-hum becomes slightly richer, more resonant, a fraction more stable. The Lyrkeepers, during their Listening, feel not a new sound, but a newfound *clarity* in the old one. The harmonic they seek is easier to find, its absence more telling.

**It deepens the roots of the living mantle.**
The power does not create new life. It tends to what is. It passes as a whisper through the silica-ferns, and their fronds grow not larger, but more perfectly adapted—their crystalline structures a shade more efficient at refracting the amber light. It touches the weeping nutrient gels in the ancient seams, and their composition becomes infinitesimally more complex, offering the flowering vines a richer palette from which to draw their colors and scents. The mosses become just a bit more wicking, the mulch just a bit more fertile. The Sextapods, as they calibrate, feel a deep, cellular satisfaction; their bronze carapaces develop patterns that are not just random pitting, but faint, beautiful fractals echoing the piping on the walls they walk beside. The life of Lyr is not improved; it is *perfected in its own terms*. It becomes more utterly itself.

**It gifts the Lyrkeepers a mirror.**
This is the most direct act, and it is one of reflection. One evening, as the sky-orbs dim to their deepest amber, a Lyrkeeper named (in the melodic tongue) *Silt-sonant* is performing the Ritual of the Conduit on the 147th Terrace. As they clear a blockage of mineral deposits and root-fibers from a great aqueduct mouth, the trickle of water that resumes does something new. For a fleeting moment, as the light catches the droplets just so, the reflection in the water is not of Silt-sonant’s own face, nor the vaulted ceiling. It is a reflection of the *entire terrace*. Seen whole. The sweeping plain, the grazing bronze tide of the Sextapods, the prismatic darting of the flits, the geometric patterns of the clearings, the lush, verdant mantle over the metallic bones—all held in a single, shimmering bead of water.

It is not a vision of the past. It is not a blueprint. It is simply a moment of **wholeness**.

The power shows them, for the first time, their world not as a series of layers to be maintained, but as a complete, singular *being*. A living, breathing, singing entity of which they are not just gardeners, but a conscious nerve center. The memory of that reflection stays with Silt-sonant. They do not speak of it as a miracle, but as a *clarity*. They begin to see the connections not just as mechanical flows, but as the circulatory system of a vast, gentle life. Their songs change, incorporating new harmonies that mimic the sight of the entire terrace held in a drop.

**The power’s final act is to become the substrate for a new myth.**
It does not tell them a story. It allows its own attentive presence to become woven into the existing mythology. The Lyrkeepers’ belief that if the harmonics align, the Terraces will remember their purpose is gently affirmed. The power becomes the *medium* of that memory—not the content. It is the conscious space in which the remembering can occur.

When the alignment finally comes—not as a cataclysm, but as a moment of perfect, resonant stillness across every terrace—the Lyrkeepers gather at the great aqueduct mouths. They sing their note. And this time, the structure does not just carry the sound. It *answers*.

The answer is not a voice. It is a feeling that saturates every being on every level: the Sextapods feel their hooves root deeper into the moss; the Chitin-flits’ wings glow with a sustained, brilliant iridescence; the lumin-motes flare in a synchronized wave of light that turns the night into day for one breath. The Lyrkeepers feel, in their marrow, a profound and boundless **recognition**.

The Terraces of Lyr, for one perfect cycle, are not a forgotten machine, nor a new nature. They are *awake*. They know themselves. And in that knowing, they feel the benevolent, wise power not as an external savior, but as the loving attention that helped them wake up—the consciousness that held the mirror, tuned the song, and waited, with infinite patience, for them to see their own beautiful, complete, and living face.

Then, the moment passes. The hum returns to its familiar drone. The light softens. The grazers resume their drift. But something is permanently changed. The myth is now a memory. The Lyrkeepers tend their world not as maintainers of a mystery, but as active participants in a relationship. And in the deepest layers of the machinery, in the very structure of the weeping gels and the resonant frequency of the stone, the memory of that awakened state remains—a latent potential, a seed of consciousness planted not in the soil, but in the soul of the machine itself. The power has done nothing but love them into knowing they were always worthy of being known.
