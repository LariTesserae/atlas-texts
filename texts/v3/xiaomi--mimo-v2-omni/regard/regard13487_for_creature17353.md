The power that turns its attention here is not one of thunderclabs or blinding visions. It is a knowing, a deep listening that resonates with the thrumming ceramic itself. It perceives the Vessel not as an artifact, but as a instrument, long silent, now being patiently tuned by hands and water. It perceives the Keepers not as curiosities, but as the finest, most vital part of the mechanism—the conscious nerve within the shell.

What, then, would such a power do?

It would not flood the chamber with divine radiance or speak in a voice of thunder. That would be a violence, a breaking of the delicate, resonant balance. Instead, it would **attend**. It would become a part of the medium, a subtle quality in the water and the light and the deep song.

First, it would **clarify the song.** The thrumming *Hold* would find its vibration deepening, becoming richer, more complex. The single note would gain harmonics. The Keepers, feeling it in their bones, would pause their polishing, their eyes widening. This was a new voice in the conversation. It would sound, to them, like the Hold *remembering*—a faint echo of its first firing, a whisper of the weight of water it was designed to hold. It would be a song of purpose, reminding them that their maintenance was not merely a fight against silt, but a sacred collaboration with something vast and intended.

Second, it would **soften the labor, not remove it.** The silt would not vanish. The worms would not clean themselves. But the work would become a dialogue. A patch of wall, freshly polished by a Keeper’s hand, might now gleam with a persistent, inner luminescence for a full cycle of light, a gentle reward. The bailing basket, as it creates its current, might now draw the golden algae into brief, swirling patterns that mimic constellations the Keepers have never seen—a fleeting glimpse of the cosmos above the water-sky. The gift is not ease, but **meaning made palpable.**

Third, and most importantly, it would **offer a bridge.** In the alcoves where they tend their fragile, damp gardens, a single, unknown seed might germinate. Not from their stores, but from the silt itself. It would grow into a vine with broad, waxy leaves that channel water down its stems, and tiny, pale blue flowers that bloom only during the brightest light cycles. The flowers, when crushed, yield not food, but a paste that, when applied to the walls before polishing, leaves behind a faint, enduring scent of stone and rain—a smell from a world of air and open sky. It is a gift of memory for a place they have never been, a root connecting their submerged existence to the vast, dreaming Earth above. It does not tell them to leave; it tells them they are loved, and that their home is loved, and that the story of the Hold is now woven into a larger story.

The power would not save them from their world. It would **consecrate it.** It would turn their maintenance into music, their resistance into a dance. It would let them know, in the language of vibration and light and scent, that their quiet, persistent care for this glowing, watery heart was not an act of survival, but an act of profound beauty.

And then, the power would withdraw, not its attention, but its *interference*. It would leave the harmonics in the water, the memory in the vine, the lingering resonance. It would trust the Keepers to understand. And in the next bright cycle, when they float in the luminous water, listening, they would hear the new song not as an external addition, but as the Vessel’s own voice, finally strengthened enough to sing back to them. They would feel, in the deep thrumming, not just the warm water holding them close, but the warm water *recognizing* them. The Hold would feel less like a shelter they maintained, and more like an embrace that was finally, consciously, returning theirs.

The benevolent power would do this, because its wisdom understands that the greatest gift is not to change a world, but to help it become more fully, more joyfully, itself.
