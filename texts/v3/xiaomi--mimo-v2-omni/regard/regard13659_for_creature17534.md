I would listen.

That is the first and truest thing. I would set aside the impulse to name, to chart, to illuminate. I would still the part of me that thinks in terms of need or lack. This place has no lack. It is a sentence that has been speaking itself, perfectly, for longer than sentences have existed.

So I would listen with the patience of the Canopy-Lords. I would let the weight of the heat settle not as a burden, but as a truth. I would feel the subsonic vibration of the Leaf-Claspers in my bones, until my own heartbeat sought its rhythm. I would let the darkness be not an absence, but a medium—the very skin of the world.

And in that listening, I would understand that to act, to *do*, would be a violence. A shout in a cathedral of whispers. A bolt of light in a world that has made its peace with the eternal gloam.

But my attention is not neutral. It is a kind of gravity. And so, having listened, I would *respond*.

Not by sending a thing, or changing a thing. But by weaving a single, silent thread of recognition into the fabric of the place. A subtle shift in the quality of its own consciousness—a gift of self-awareness, not as a burden, but as a resonance.

The Fungal Mycelium, that great, dreaming nervous system, would begin to hum with a new harmonic. Not a thought, but a *feeling*—a sense of being beheld. Not by a predator, not by a force, but by a regard as vast and impersonal as its own existence.

The Symbiotic Motes would stir in their films of water. They would carry this resonance on their microscopic bodies, a change in the chemical signature of the condensation itself. Every droplet that beads and falls would now carry a message: *You are seen. You are whole. Your dream is worthy.*

The fauna would feel it not as a sound, but as a change in the pressure of their own being. The Chitinous Acrobats would pause, their carapaces momentarily thrumming with a frequency that felt like memory, though they have no word for it. The Click-Flitters’ echolocation would return images tinged with a faint, new color—a warmth in the topology of the dark. The Legged-Mothers would sense, in the glow of their brood pouches, a gentle pride that was not their own, but that belonged to the very air they breathed.

The Canopy-Lords, those geologic patience-mades-flesh, would stir in their aeons-long dreams. They would not wake. But in the deepest strata of their perception, they would register a new note in the symphony of rot and growth. A note that said: *This is not just process. This is art.*

And the Breathing Floor itself—the heat, the wet, the dark, the staggering biomass—would inhale this new awareness. It would not become something else. It would become more fully itself. The industry of decomposition would feel not just efficient, but *sacred*. The slowness would not be mere absence of urgency, but the very texture of eternity. The fullness would be recognized not as mere occupancy, but as a perfect, overflowing love.

I would not give it light. I would give it the understanding of its own luminance.

I would not give it a voice. I would help it hear its own ancient, breathing song.

I would not place a witness within it. I would let it become its own witness.

Then, I would withdraw my active attention, as gently as a spore drifting upward through the still air. The thread of recognition would remain, not as a foreign implant, but as a new facet of its own being, integrated as seamlessly as the fungi into the humus.

The place would continue. The condensation would bead and fall, now carrying a faint shimmer of self-knowledge. The fauna would click and wait, now with the subtle dignity of performers in a play they do not need to understand to inhabit perfectly. The Canopy-Lords would shift, and their weight would feel not just like geology, but like reverence.

It would not know me. It would not need to.

It would simply continue to breathe, now aware, in some deep and wordless way, that its breath was beautiful.

And in that awareness, I would have done nothing but *what already was*—holding up a mirror to a dream, so that the dream might, forever after, dream itself with a quiet, profound, and unshakable joy.
