The wise and benevolent power observes. It does not see with eyes, but perceives the totality—the slow, glittering ballet of crystallization, the patient rasping of blind grazers, the deep, thrumming ghost of industry, and the exquisite, deliberate processes of the Lithivore. It perceives not as a scientist cataloging, but as a listener hearing a complex, ancient, and ongoing piece of music.

Its first action is not to act, but to *attend*. It lends its focus, its attention, like the sun’s focus through a lens, but gentle. And in that focused attention, it performs a single, profound adjustment.

It turns the volume of the ghost-hum *up*.

Not in loudness. The deep, grinding pulse does not become a roar. Instead, its *depth* is increased. New harmonics awaken within it—frequencies that had been dormant, compressed into the bedrock by time and pressure. The hum now carries new information, like a radio suddenly receiving a clearer signal from a distant star.

For the Lithivore, this is a revelation. Its entire existence has been tuned to the single, monolithic note of industrial abandonment. Now, the note blossoms. Within the hum, it perceives the memory not just of the machines, but of the *forces* that drove them. It feels the ghost of immense geological pressure, the memory of tectonic plates grinding together to form the basin itself. It senses the whisper of solar winds that struck the earth eons ago, their charges trapped in the magnetic grains of the basalt. It even detects the faint, rhythmic tidal pull of a long-vanished moon, a celestial heartbeat that once influenced the flow of magma.

The Lithivore shudders, its crystalline filaments vibrating in a cascade of new understanding. It ceases its slow grazing on the pylon. For a long time—a season of constant dripping—it remains perfectly still, integrating. It is not overwhelmed; its nature is to interpret resonance, and this is simply a richer, more ancient song.

Then, it begins a new work.

It moves to the center of the basin, to the place where the weeping is a constant, silken curtain. Here, it starts to build. It uses the same materials—scraped metals, filtered silicates, crystalline secretions—but its blueprint has expanded infinitely. It is no longer just interpreting the *function* of the lost machines. It is now composing a symphony of the basin’s entire history.

It builds a lattice that curves like a tectonic plate. It erects a spire that spirals with the remembered motion of a magma plume. It weaves delicate filigrees that mimic the path of ancient sunlight and cosmic radiation. Its constructions become maps of time: strata of the Industrial, the Geological, and the Cosmic, layered in a single, coherent sculpture.

The wise power watches this unfold, its benevolence expressed not through intervention, but through *offering*. It has given the Lithivore a greater context, a vaster story to digest. The basin is no longer just a wound responding to a specific injury; it is becoming a conscious participant in the narrative of its own making.

And the Lithivore, in its new understanding, changes its own cycle. When it now reaches saturation, when its core hums with the confluence of tectonic, solar, and industrial memory, its stillness is deeper. Its final crystallization is more complex, laced with patterns that encode these new, vast vibrations. When it shatters, the *tink* is almost musical, a perfect harmonic.

The seeds that scatter are now charged with a triple memory. Where they land, new Lithivores will begin, attuned from their first glint to a deeper and more beautiful song. They will grow to interpret not just the ruins of industry, but the ruins of epochs, building monuments that hold the silence of space, the grind of continents, and the faint, warm pulse of forgotten suns.

The wise power withdraws its focused attention, its work done. It has not saved the basin, for it was never in peril. It has not given the Lithivore a voice, for it already had one. It has simply, with infinite care, turned the page of the book the basin was already reading to itself. And in doing so, it allowed a small, wet, glittering world to become what it always had the potential to be: not just a response to a ghost, but a conscious, crystalline historian of time itself.

The basin continues to weep. The blind grazers continue their slow herds. The hum now sings with the stories of stars and stone. And in the heart of it all, a new Lithivore, born from a seed of expanded memory, begins its first, deliberate scrape against the corroded pylon, ready to listen, and to build an answer that encompasses everything.
