The world is a made thing.

Every surface is ceramic. The ground beneath your feet is a mosaic of fired clay, glazed in deep celadon and bruised purples, seamless and undulating. It sweats. In this bright, unwavering light—a light that has no source and casts no shadow—the low-lying expanse glitters. Condensation beads on the geometric lichen you cannot remember planting, trickles in rivulets that follow pre-etched channels in the tiles, and pools in shallow depressions that have no natural bottom, only more ceramic, fading into a dark, glossy murk.

This is a marshland built by hands that understood neither water nor decay, only form. The "rocks" are hollow, porous vitrified shapes, like colossal, irregular beads or the discarded vertebrae of some leviathan. They weep constantly, a slow, clear seepage that feeds the channels. Between them, the vegetation grows in lush, deliberate clumps. Broad-leafed fronds of a waxy, emerald plant erupt from precise circles in the tile. Grasses, a startling shade of blue-black, form soft, isolated hummocks in the shallow water, each tuft spaced from the next as if by a measured grid. There is no scramble for sunlight here; there is only abundant, programmed growth, separated by the wet, gleaming emptiness of the constructed plain.

The air is a hot, wet blanket. It smells of ozone, damp clay, and the sweetish rot of things that grow too quickly in a world without seasons. The heat is a constant pressure, but the quiet is the most profound thing. It is a deep, listening silence, broken only by the soft *plip* of water falling from a ceramic lip onto a ceramic leaf, or the skittering of fauna.

And there is fauna. Crabs with shells like polished jasper scuttle along the channels, their legs clicking on the hard substrate. In the distance, heron-like birds, their feathers an iridescent sheen, stand motionless in the shallows for hours, patient statues within a statue. They move, but the world does not. The water level does not rise or fall. The weeping stones weep at the same rate. The light does not shift from its flat, bleaching intensity. It is an equilibrium so perfect it feels like a held breath.

And then the weirdness, which is not an event, but a texture. You look at a cluster of the ceramic boulders and, for a moment, their arrangement seems familiar—a shape you just saw, repeated down the vista. You listen, and the *plip* of water echoes not once, but twice, precisely. The blue-black grass grows in tufts that, from this height, resolve into a pattern that is almost a character in a language you don't know. It is a landscape built on a fundamental principle that has gone subtly, irrevocably awry. The makers used simple tools—hammers, molds, fire—and their primitive, earnest fabrication has resulted in a world that is both flawlessly executed and deeply, quietly wrong. It is a valley of wet, bright, hot, and living stillness, where everything that exists was placed, and nothing has ever changed.
