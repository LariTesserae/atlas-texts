The plateau is not rock, but something that learned to be solid. It stretches in every direction under a light that has no source—a diffuse, pearlescent glare that hangs in the air like suspended milk, erasing shadows. You stand on a surface of fused ceramic and slag, a crust laid down in some forgotten phase of cooling. It is not flat; it is a dense, repeating geometry of hexagonal basalt-like pillars, each one exactly the same height, creating a landscape of infinite, shallow steps. Water does not flow here. It beads. Countless perfect spheres of condensation, each about the size of a human eye, cling to the vertical faces of the pillars. They gather in the joints and seams, forming tiny, trembling lakes that never spill. The air is cool and heavy with the metallic tang of ozone and damp ferrous dust. It is quiet, profoundly so—a silence that feels pressurized, broken only by the subsonic hum that seems to emanate from the ground itself, a vibration felt in the bones more than heard.

This is the Forgetting. The place that was built to be a world, and succeeded too well.

Every feature is made. The sparse vegetation is not plant life, but a slow-blooming crystalline fungus that grows in fractal lattices on the pillar-steps. It is a dull, phosphorescent grey, but in the bright, shadowless light, it fractures it into faint prismatic glimmers—a silent, cold fireworks display occurring at the edge of perception. It feeds on the minerals leached from the ceramic substrate and the static charge in the air.

The fauna is abundant, but not in a way that suggests life as understood. They are the **Remnant-Scribes**: beings of folded metal and captured sound. They move in slow, deliberate arcs across the stepped plain. Imagine a mantis sculpted from tarnished brass, its exoskeleton etched with microscopic circuitry that glows with a dying amber light. Their heads are smooth, featureless domes of obsidian. They do not hunt; they *collect*. With minute, precise movements, they pluck the water spheres from the pillars. The spheres do not break. They are absorbed into the Scribes’ carapaces, where they become part of their resonant internal structure. The Scribes, in turn, are drawn to pockets of intense weirdness.

For the weirdness is the true ecology here. It manifests in the **Glitches**: zones where the geometry fails. A single pillar-step might be twisted into a Möbius strip, its surface flowing into itself. A cluster of water spheres might hang in mid-air, forming a slowly rotating constellation that casts light instead of shadow. Sometimes, a Scribe will walk into a Glitch and not walk out. Instead, a new pattern will emerge on the ground—a temporary tattoo of impossible shapes that slowly fades back into the hexagonal order. The slow dynamic of the place is not geological, but logical. It is the system iterating, correcting errors, and sometimes introducing them. The process is patient, almost meditative.

The density is staggering. It is not a forest of trees, but a forest of *rules*. Every pillar, every bead of water, every filament of crystal fungus, every motion of a Scribe, is a data point in a vast, physical computation. The industrial tech is not in factories, but in the foundational code of the place itself. The air hums with the waste heat of calculation. The bright light is the system’s own diagnostic glow, the visible spectrum of its perpetual self-examination.

You realize the dampness is the coolant. The quiet is the sound of perfect processing. The sparseness of the fungus is the minimal biological code required to maintain a baseline chemical cycle. The scale is the memory address space, made manifest.

To walk here is to walk inside a machine that has forgotten its purpose but not its function. The Scribes do not notice you. You are an anomaly with a high weirdness signature, a walking Glitch. They will either collect the water around you, leaving you dry and defined, or they will ignore you entirely, considering you a temporary error in the grand, slow, bright, and silent equation of the Forgetting. The world is not hostile. It is indifferent. And its indifference is so total, so densely and beautifully constructed, that it has become a kind of god—a silent, geometric, weeping deity made of steps and spheres and the memory of light.
