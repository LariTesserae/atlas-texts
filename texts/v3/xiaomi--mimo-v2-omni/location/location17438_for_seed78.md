This is the World-Root. Or so the scattered tribes who cling to its southern fringes call it. It is not a forest. A forest is a community of individuals. This is a single, planetary-scale entity—a continental mycelial network that has erupted into a three-dimensional, photosynthetic architecture of staggering scale. It is a plant that has become a world.

**Terrain & Scale:** The landscape is not *of* rock or soil, but of living and dead vascular tissue. Imagine a mountain range, but the peaks are not stone; they are the hollow, wind-carved spires of colossal fungal-plant hybrid structures, their "rock" a porous, lignin-chitinous composite. Between them lie valleys not of earth, but of sagging, hammock-like plains of interwoven root-mat, some many kilometers across. The scale is continental; you can watch weather systems—moisture pulled from the deep earth and transpired by the living mass—form and dissolve over the canopies of what would, in an older world, be called mountain-sized root-boles. The horizon is never a line of distant peaks, but the gentle, upward curve of the world-structure itself, vanishing into the perpetually misty dark.

**Atmosphere & Light:** It is dark, but not pitch black. The light is the faintest, ghostly glow of bioluminescence, a slow-cycling pulse from deep within the plant's metabolic layers, and the diffuse, blue-tinged light of the cold, distant sun filtered through a thousand kilometers of translucent, chlorophyll-rich canopy-ice. The air is cold—a permanent, dry chill that stills the atmosphere at ground level, though high above, in the "sky-spans" between root-spires, hurricane-force winds shriek. The air is thin, not from altitude alone, but because the World-Root has consumed most of the soil, and the geological processes that replenish atmospheric gases are smothered under its mass.

**Materials & Erosion:** Everything is **young**. This world erupted recently, in geological terms. The features are sharp, the hollows in the root-spires still angular, the fractures clean. There is no soft loam. The substrate is a crisp, brittle litter of shed vascular fibers and crystallized sap, overlying the hard, living "wood" of the root-mat. It crunches underfoot like frozen leaves and glass. Erosion is not by water or wind alone, but by the plant's own growth—it hollows itself out, sheds its skin, and consumes its dead to fuel new, explosive growth in other directions. It is a landscape of active self-cannibalization.

**Density & Emptiness:** This is the profound paradox. The vegetation is total, yet the density is near-zero. The World-Root is mostly hollow. Vast cathedrals of space exist within the root-boles, their walls meters thick but their interiors cavernous, silent, and empty save for dripping condensation and the faint, echoing groans of structural stress. The "soil" you walk on is a thin crust over a void. You look down into abyssal darkness where the root-mat has delaminated. The emptiness is palpable—a silence so deep it amplifies every sound, making the world feel like a vast, hollow drum.

**Sound:** And it is **loud**. The silence is an illusion. The low, planet-wide hum is the sound of millions of tons of water being pumped through xylem. It is the tectonic groan of growth—new, sharp-edged spires pushing up through the old. The sharp, percussive *crack* of a massive root-limb shedding in the cold. The wind, funnelled through the hollow spires, plays them like colossal, off-key flutes, a constant, dissonant chorus. Fauna adds to it: the skittering of chitin on woody surfaces, the distant, trumpeting calls of the large, six-legged grazers that browse on high-canopy lichen.

**Ecology & Fauna:** Life here is primitive but abundant, adapted to the cold, the dark, and the living ground. The grazers are slow-moving, their metabolism tuned to the low energy of the bioluminescent plants. In the hollows, blind, pale arthropods filter fungal spores from the damp air. Everything is sparse in any one place, but spread across the continental scale, the numbers are immense. The tribes hunt the grazers with stone-tipped spears and woven vine-nets, following the seasonal migration along the "spine-ridges" where the World-Root's growth is most active and the vegetation most nutritious.

**Built & Tech:** The traces are faint. There are no cities. On the highest, most stable spires—places where the growth has stalled, creating a hard, dead "capping"—you find the remains of previous inhabitants. Not ruins, but **marks**: pecked and abraded symbols in the woody flesh, simple stone tools left in niches, the occasional fire-blackened hollow where a tribe once sheltered. These are the artifacts of peoples who saw the World-Weirdness for what it was and either adapted or were consumed by it. The current tribes use tools of sharpened root-thorn, woven fiber, and fire-hardened wood. They build temporary platforms in the growing zones, never settling where the ground is alive and moving.

**Rules of the World:** The World-Weirdness is the rule. Growth is the primary force. It is not peaceful. It is a silent, relentless war of expansion and self-consumption. The cold slows it, the darkness is its cloak, and the emptiness is its strategy—it invests energy in vast, hollow structures that claim territory with minimal mass. The dampness is its blood, drawn from deep aquifers and released in transpiration-fogs that cling to the hollows. To live here is to understand that the ground is not a stage for life, but the life itself, and you are a temporary guest in its anatomy. You do not conquer this land. You navigate its digestive system.
