The place is a hollow heartwood, though the heartwood is the size of a low, sprawling mountain range and made not of dead cellulose but of living, breathing plant matter. This is the *Basin of the Lignified Sky*, and it is the only feature in the world.

You stand in a chamber. The walls, the floor, the vaulted ceiling hundreds of feet above—all are the same substance: a pale, fibrous, living plant-tissue, cool and faintly damp to the touch. It is the interior of a single, continent-sized organism, a species of **Tracheophyta Ultima** that chose, in an epoch now forgotten, to grow not up towards the sun, but *outwards* and *inwards*, sealing itself into a state of perpetual, self-contained equilibrium.

**Terrain & Materials:** The ground is not flat, but gently undulating, like the interior of a vast ribcage. The plant-material is dense, hard as seasoned oak, yet it yields slightly underfoot with a soft, mossy compression. There are no rocks, no soil, only layers of compacted, decomposed plant-matter that have, over millennia, become a springy, humus-like floor. The walls are not sheer; they are laced with immense, vascular bundles—thick as ancient redwoods—that run in sweeping, parabolic arches from floor to ceiling. These are the conduits, the **Industrial Heartwood**. Within them, you hear a constant, deep, rhythmic thrum: the slow, immense pulse of sap being pumped under colossal pressure, the hydraulic machinery of a lifeform operating on a geological scale. It is the source of the **loud, pervasive sound**—a basso profundo vibration felt in the bones, a sub-audible groan punctuated by the sharper clicks and hisses of pressure-release valves and chitinous pistons that emerge, organ-like, from the walls to regulate the interior climate.

**Atmosphere & Light:** The air is **cool** and heavy with humidity, tasting of ozone, wet wood, and a faint, sweet scent of fermentation. Water does not fall as rain here. Instead, it **condenses** on every surface. The "damp" parameter manifests as a perpetual, gentle weeping. Droplets form on the fibrous ceiling, coalesce, and run down the vascular bundles in rivulets, collecting in shallow, dark pools in the ground’s depressions before being reabsorbed through spongy pores in the floor. The **light** is diffuse and uniform, a soft, greyish-green luminescence. It has no single source. It emanates from the plant-tissue itself, a photosynthetic afterglow, or perhaps from vast, bioluminescent glands embedded in the canopy far above. It is enough to see by, but casts no sharp shadows, enhancing the feeling of being inside a living, breathing organ.

**Ecology & Fauna:** This is a **pure-plant ecosystem**, but it is not barren. **Abundant** life thrives in symbiosis with the Lignified Sky. Mosses and lichens, in myriad shades of blue-green and silver, form velvet carpets in the damp hollows. Fungi, some the size of small cabins, sprout from the walls, their gills pulsing with soft, phosphorescent light. The **fauna** is entirely adapted to this biome. Blind, pale arthropods with sensitive antennae navigate the textures of the floor. Bat-like mammals with translucent wings roost in the high crevices, feeding on fruiting bodies of fungus. Schools of eyeless, eel-like creatures swim in the larger condensation pools, their movements silent. Everything here moves slowly, conserving energy in the cool, stable environment. There are no predators in the conventional sense, only filters, grazers, and detritivores in a slow, closed cycle.

**The Rules of this World:** It is a place of **ancient, settled stability**. The erosion is not from wind or rain, but from the internal, grinding metabolism of the plant itself—aeons of slow expansion and retraction, polishing every surface to a smooth, ergonomic finish. There is **no change**. The temperature, the light, the pulse of the heartwood, the condensation cycle—all have held in perfect, **time-invariant equilibrium** for longer than human memory. It is a **small** world in scope; beyond the curving walls of this primary chamber lie tunnels and adjacent caverns, but the entire system is bounded, a hamlet-sized province within the larger organism. It feels eternal, a **frozen equilibrium**. The concept of "built" structures is meaningless; there are no artifacts, only the inherent architecture of the plant. The **industrial** element is not of metal and coal, but of biology: the sap-press is a muscle, the vascular bundles are pipes, the bioluminescence is lighting. It is industry as a metabolic function.

To be here is to be inside a machine that is also an organism, a cathedral that is also a lung. It is profoundly quiet in its purpose, yet overwhelmingly loud in its operation. It is empty of human clutter, yet full of a singular, immense, and ancient life. It is not growing, not dying. It simply *is*, in its final, perfect, and slightly damp form.
