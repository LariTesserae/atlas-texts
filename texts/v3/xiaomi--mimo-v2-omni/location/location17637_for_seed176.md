The place is a high-altitude gorge, a deep and narrow wound in the earth that seems to have been torn open rather than carved. The air is thick with warmth and humidity, a perpetual steam-bath clinging to surfaces. Every rock face weeps. Water doesn't just flow here; it *exudes*, slicking the stone with a constant, glossy film and gathering in trembling, fat droplets on every overhang. The source isn't rain—it’s condensation, drawn from the warm, saturated air that moves through the gorge with a slow, tidal breath. This is a landscape perpetually in the aftermath of a storm that never truly ends.

The walls of the gorge are young, their edges sharp and aggressive. Strata of quartz-veined granite and dark basalt jut out at brutal angles, not yet softened by time or erosion. On these wet, sun-warmed shelves, life erupts with a fierce, verdant urgency. Mosses of impossible emerald and lime form thick, spongy carpets. Ferns with fronds like delicate, green fingers unfurl from every crack, their undersides pearled with condensation. Orchids with waxy, pale blooms cling to the vertical faces, their roots a tangled mesh barely holding them to the stone. The vegetation is dense, layered, and tangled—a vertical jungle compressed into a single, breathtaking column of green.

But threaded through this primal wetness is the ghost of industry. From the cliff faces, rust-streaked iron gantries and corroded catwalks cantilever out over the void, their purposes long forgotten. Massive, silent turbines, their blades furred with moss, sit half-submerged in the plunge pools at the gorge’s base. They are fed by a network of riveted steel pipes that snake across the rock, some weeping the same water as the stone, others humming with a deep, vibrational stillness. The air carries a low, constant thrum—the sound of hidden dynamos or the resonance of the gorge itself. Occasionally, a louder, metallic *groan* echoes through the space as a cooling pipe contracts or a rusted hinge settles. The machinery doesn’t run; it *persists*, a slow, metabolic process that has been happening for centuries, maybe millennia.

The scale is intimate yet overwhelming. You could walk the gorge floor in twenty minutes, yet the verticality pins you. The sky is a brilliant, narrow ribbon far above, its normal light diffused by the rising vapour into a soft, directionless glow that makes every wet surface shine. The warmth is constant, heavy, and close.

Fauna is rare, but present. You might see the silver flash of a blind fish in the deeper pools, or a fat, jewel-toned beetle navigating the moss. More often, you sense movement just at the edge of perception—a rustle in the ferns, a dripping from a higher ledge that isn’t quite water. The weirdness here is subtle, a low-frequency hum in the bones of the place. The water sometimes seems to flow upwards along certain pipes, deferring to some hidden logic. The moss on the northern face of a particular turbine grows in a perfect, geometric spiral. The thrumming in the metal can, if you listen too long, begin to sound like a distorted lullaby.

This is a place of patient processes: the slow ooze of water, the imperceptible growth of rust, the gradual victory of green things over iron, and the deep, cyclic pulse of the dormant machines. It is a cathedral of dampness and decay, where the industrial and the natural have fused into a single, breathing, and enigmatically loud organism.
