The first thing you notice is not the dark, but the sound. It hits you like a physical pressure, a wall of noise so dense it feels like a solid thing. It is not one sound, but a continent of them: the wet, seismic thrum of a million throats singing in a frequency that vibrates in your marrow; the constant, shattering crash of unseen things collapsing or colliding in the black; a pervasive, sibilant hiss that might be steam, or the exhalation of vast lungs, or rain striking a billion broad leaves. The air is warm, thick as broth, and wet with a condensation that beads on your skin and drips from everything.

Your eyes, straining, begin to perceive a truth: this is not total darkness. It is a world lit from within. Every surface, every creature, every droplet of moisture is alive with a cold, chemical light. Pale blue-green fungi network across the trunks of colossal, unidentifiable trees, casting ghostly halos. Veins of phosphorescent sap bleed through fissures in the bark. The ground is not earth, but a spongy, yielding mat of bioluminescent moss and decaying matter, pulsing with faint, rhythmic glows like a slow, planetary heartbeat. In the distance, where the land opens into a vast, low basin, sheets of pale fire—maybe ignited gases, maybe spore-clouds—intermittently illuminate the chaos in stark, theatrical flashes.

This is a place of violent fecundity. The vegetation is not just lush; it is *insatiable*. Trees, if you can call them that, are more like fluid architectures of cellulose and light. Their trunks are not wood but a tough, fibrous latticework, often hollow, serving as conduits for rushing water and nutrients. Great, fleshy leaves, some as wide as tables, are slick with moisture and covered in fine, luminous hairs. Vines, thick as arms and studded with thorns that glow like embers, coil and uncoil with slow, muscular purpose. Everything grows, and grows over, and grows through. There is no empty space, only layers upon layers of life, each competing for a share of the faint, internal light and the rich, warm, wet air.

The fauna is the source of the overwhelming sound, and it is everywhere. The dark is not an absence, but a medium teeming with presence. You hear the skittering of chitinous legs on bark the size of cliffs. You feel the deep, subsonic pulse of something massive moving through the substrate beneath your feet. Winged things, their bodies outlined in stripes of yellow and violet light, swarm in dense, dizzying clouds, their collision with each other and the flora creating a constant, tinkling percussion. In the flashes of distant sheet-light, you glimpse silhouettes: long, sinuous necks weaving through the canopy; broad, flat shapes gliding through the air on membranes of glowing skin; clusters of multi-limbed things hanging in vast, swaying colonies. It is an ecology of perpetual motion, of chase and escape, of display and consumption, all carried out in a symphony of screams, chirps, rumbles, and the constant, wet sound of growth and decay happening in real time.

The land itself is young, and it shows. The low elevation is a maze of recently carved ravines and soft, crumbling ridges. Streams, hot and slightly acidic, cut through the luminous soil, their banks steaming. Pools of dark water, fed by condensation and seeps, are scummed with brilliant, photosynthetic mats that glow a sickly green. There are no smooth, weathered stones here, only sharp, fresh exposures of mineral-crusted earth that glitter in the gloom. The turbulence is in the very ground: you can hear the groan of settling strata, the sudden hiss of a new fissure opening, the plop and slump of mudslides in the distance. It is a landscape in a state of perpetual, energetic infancy, being born and unbirthing itself in the dark.

There is no sign of hands but your own. No tool-marks, no clearings, no paths. The primitive technology hinted at is not here; it is instead a world where biology itself is the technology. The creatures use bioluminescence for communication, hunting, and mimicry. They use sound to map the darkness, to stun prey, to bond. The flora uses chemistry and light to lure, to repel, to cooperate. The rules of this world are written in vibration and cold fire, in heat and moisture, in a relentless, turbulent competition for space and energy within the warm, black womb of the earth.

It is a deafening, dark, and dazzling cradle of life, unaware of the concept of night because it has never known a sun. Its day is a steady state of glowing, roaring, wet, and violent existence. To stand here is to be inside a living, breathing, and screaming organism.
