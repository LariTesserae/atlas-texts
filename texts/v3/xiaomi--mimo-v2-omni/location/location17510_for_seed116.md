The place is a vault.

It exists in the crushing, warm dark of a world that has become entirely ocean. There is no sky, only a ceiling of black water pressing down with the weight of eras. You are inside the vault, which is both the entire world and a single, embedded chamber. The scale is impossible—intimate and monumental at once.

The vault’s walls are not stone, but a fused, vitreous material, born of unimaginable pressure and time. Its surface is ancient, smoothed into undulating curves by millennia of current, like a pebble in the hand of a god. It is the oldest stable thing here. Everything else is a living skin upon it.

That skin is lush. Velvety, photosynthetic mats in shades of emerald and violet cling to every surface, thriving in the normal light that filters through the vault’s own substance, a soft, diffused glow with no source. The vegetation is not just on the walls; it forms thick, sagging hammocks between structural ribs, and curtains of fibrous tendrils that sway in the slow currents. This greenery is the vault’s lung, recycling the manufactured atmosphere.

The air—or the water, for the distinction has blurred—is warm and humid. It carries the constant, active thrum of the vault’s heart: a distant, industrial churn. This is a fully made environment, and its machinery never sleeps. The sound is loud, a low-frequency pulse felt in the bones, layered with the hiss of fluid moving through capillaries in the walls, the *plink* of condensation dripping from fungal clusters onto the ferrous floor, and the occasional, echoing *groan* as the vault’s frame settles another millimeter into its eternal bed.

Abundant fauna inhabits this sparse space. There are no great schools of fish, only scattered, specialized things. Blind, translucent crustaceans with delicate, metallic exoskeletons pick at the moss. They are the decomposers, the recycler caste. Higher up, on the ceilings, hang clusters of leathery sacs—pupae of some winged creature, dormant, waiting for a signal that may never come. Movement is deliberate, energy-efficient. The predator-prey dynamic here is one of slow siege: a patient, grasping vine slowly enveloping a sleeping crustacean; a tiny, electric discharge from a lurking organism stunning a grazing mat-fly.

The weirdness is in the seams. The vault is industrial, total, but nature has hacked its blueprint. The vegetation is too perfectly adapted; it maintains the air, reinforces the walls with root-like filaments. The fauna fills every engineered niche. The active processes—the churning, the dripping, the slow growth—have reached a strange equilibrium with the ancient, eroded structure. It is not a ruin, but a settled-into-final-form symbiosis. The factory has become the ecosystem. The product has become the planet.

You are standing on a grated platform, warm underfoot. Below, the floor drops away into a black, flooded sump where the largest machinery turns, its vibrations making the water’s surface shimmer. Above, a catwalk disappears into the gloom, bordered by that lush, glowing vegetation. The air smells of salt, warm metal, and damp earth. The light is steady, normal, and gives no hint of time. The vault’s processes—its hydrology, its respiration, its quiet, persistent life—continue their active, circular dance. They have done so for longer than memory. They will do so until the water itself is gone.
