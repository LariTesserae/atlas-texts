# The Stackeddark

You cannot see. Not properly. What you can feel is weight — the weight of rock pressing in from every direction, close enough to touch though you dare not reach out, because the rock is *hot*. Not warm. Not feverish. The kind of hot that makes the air itself shimmer and warp, that turns every breath into a scalding negotiation with your lungs.

You are deep inside something. A mountain, maybe — or the remnant of one, hollowed and packed full again with what grew there in the absence of light. Crystals. Enormous ones, the size of your torso or larger, jostling against each other in every direction: amethyst gone copper-brown with iron inclusions, quartz gone opaque and milky, feldspar splitting into paper-thin sheets that catch the faintest glow of their own heat and throw it back as dull orange light. There is no gap between them. They have grown into each other, through each other, competing for space that doesn't exist. To move forward you press between two faces of mineral that radiate heat like the doors of a furnace, and you feel your skin tighten, and you move on.

The sound is the first thing that becomes unbearable. Not a single sound — a *mass* of sound, the way the crystals are a mass of mineral, saturated and without room. There is the groan of thermal expansion: rock shifting against rock on timescales you can't perceive but can *hear*, a subsonic thrum that lives in your chest cavity and makes your teeth ache. There is the hiss of gas — superheated, mineral-laden — forcing its way through fissures so narrow they shouldn't pass anything, yet the pressure beneath is immense, and the gas screams through in threads of white and sulfur-yellow, audible as a thousand tiny, sustained shrieks. And underneath it all, something you feel more than hear: a rhythm. Slow. Patient. The breathing of something geological. A pulse measured in decades, exhaling heat, inhaling darkness.

The creatures are everywhere. You step on them before you see them — or rather, before you *sense* them, because sight is nearly useless here. They are the size of your hand or smaller, pale and translucent, with bodies like soft quartz and limbs like the branching of crystal dendrites. They cling to every surface. They pack the interstitial spaces between the great crystals like mortar, thousands of them, millions, their bodies filling the last remaining gaps in this saturated world. They make the noise worse — a chittering, a clicking, the sound of mineral touching mineral through living tissue. They are warm to the touch. They are warm because everything here is warm, and they have never known otherwise. Their life cycle is the slow dissolution of one crystal face and the growth of another; they feed on the boundary, on the change, on the patient becoming.

You find — eventually, because everything is slow here — that the space opens, briefly, into a chamber perhaps large enough to lie down in. The walls glow dull red. Not from light. From *heat*. The thermal radiation is almost visible here, the crystals so saturated with energy that the boundary between infrared and red begins to blur. In this small clearance, the creatures are thicker. They stack atop each other. Their branching limbs interlock and form secondary structures — lattices, almost architectural, that grow and collapse over hours. New crystals nucleate on their bodies. They carry the mineral world inside them and seed it elsewhere, and the elsewhere is always more of the same.

There is no water. There has never been water. The chemistry here is one of dry reaction — of gas meeting solid, of heat meeting lattice, of pressure meeting time. If you cracked one of these crystals open you would find the interior veined with darker mineral, arranged in patterns that look deliberate but aren't. They are the record of growth. Slow growth. The rings of a tree that is not a tree, made of stone that is not quite stone, in a darkness that has been unbroken for longer than your species has existed.

Beneath it all — beneath the packed crystal, beneath the thrumming fauna, beneath the screaming gas — there is something industrial. Not built. Not made. But *functional*. The geothermal gradient here is so steep, so energetic, that the deep rock behaves like machinery: convection cells turn with the regularity of turbines, heat flows through channels that the crystals have grown to exploit, and the entire system operates with a kind of thermodynamic purpose. Engines of stone. Factories of mineral. Scaled not to human production but to planetary patience, turning heat into structure, structure into heat, in cycles so slow they look like stillness.

You are inside something that is, in every meaningful sense, *full*. Full of crystal, full of life, full of heat, full of sound. There is no room for anything new. The erosion has barely begun — the slowest possible breakdown, the patient rounding of edges that will take ten thousand years to become noticeable. For now, everything is sharp. Everything is close. Everything is hot and loud and alive and dark, and it has been this way for an age, and it will be this way for an age more, and the creatures will click and the rock will groan and the gas will hiss through the narrowing passages, and nothing will change quickly, because nothing here knows how to be quick.

Only dense. Only hot. Only full.
