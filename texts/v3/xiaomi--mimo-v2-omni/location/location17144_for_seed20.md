The place exists at the threshold of drowning.

It is a high plateau, or perhaps it was once a plateau, now besieged. The water is not below but *around*. It hangs in the air as a thick, warm, saline mist that has weight and texture, condensing in sheets that are less rain and more collapsing curtains of brine. To look up is to see a roiling, pewter sky that is also the surface of an ocean, the light filtering down in bruised, lavender and copper shafts, dim as a deep-sea trench. The distinction between sea and sky dissolved long ago. This is an atmospheric ocean, a world caught in the permanent, churning aftermath of a global deluge that never found a floor.

The ground—if it can be called that—is a sharp-featured, newly emerged lattice of black basalt and crystalline salt. It steams where the warm brine meets the slightly cooler stone. There are no soft soils. Erosion is young, not from wind or gentle rain, but from the constant, abrasive *kinesis* of the medium. Features are sharp, fractured, and transient. A spire of fused salt and obsidian that stands today might be ground into a shimmering, abrasive paste by tomorrow, only to reconstitute elsewhere in the churn. The scale is intimate, a series of broken mesas and canyons you could walk across in an hour, if "walking" could be defined in this clinging, buoyant medium. The boundaries are the mist-walls; to go further is to be utterly subsumed.

Life is sparse, a desperate punctuation. Groves of what might be trees cling to the highest pinnacles. They are not wood, but flexible, rubbery columns of a deep burgundy hue, their surfaces slick with a mucus that repels the brine. They have no leaves, only broad, membranous sacs that pulse slowly, filtering nutrients directly from the saturated air. Their roots are not in the stone, but are thick, grappling tendrils that constantly re-anchor them against the subtle, shifting currents. Fauna is rarer still: translucent, jellyfish-like drifters that pulse with a faint bioluminescence as they tumble through the mist, or small, armored things that scuttle in the crevices, their shells composed of salvaged metal and stone, biological and geological fused into a survivalist mosaic.

Sound is a physical constant, a deafening, multi-layered roar. It is the hiss of a trillion condensing droplets, the deep, groaning thrum of the ocean-sky shifting above, the sharp, glassy crack of salt-crystals fracturing as the ground subtly tilts. It is a sound that obliterates silence and thought, a white-noise so total it becomes a kind of pressure. Beneath it, if you can focus past the deluge, there are other frequencies: a sub-sonic hum from the basalt itself, and a high, chittering chorus from the unseen, shelled things.

This is where the reality breaks down. The air *remembers*. In the turbulent flow, you see after-images—ghosts of landscapes that never were. A flash of a green valley, the silhouette of a non-existent sun, the echo of a voice that speaks in the shape of a wave. The artifacts are not built but *imprinted*. Here, a perfect, impossible cube of flawless glass sits on a basalt shelf, untouched by the chaos, its surface reflecting a star-filled sky that does not exist in this world. There, a series of concentric circles are etched into a salt-flat, the lines so fine they might be a trick of the eye, yet they vibrate in sync with the sub-sonic hum. They are not ruins; they are glitches, physical manifestations of the world's failing memory of order.

The dynamics are relentless. This is not a static drowned world, but a creative and destructive engine. New islands of crystal and slag erupt from the mist in minutes, only to be shredded and dissolved. The burgidian groves release their spores not as seeds, but as shimmering, metallic clouds that ride the currents, seeking purchase in the next momentary stability. The turbulence is not chaos in the sense of randomness, but hyper-active *process*—a constant, violent forging.

To be here is to be immersed in the raw, screaming substrate of a world. It is warm, wet, loud, and perpetually in the act of becoming and unbecoming. The sparse elements—the grove, the drifters, the artifacts—are not inhabitants. They are survivors of the process, temporary patterns that have achieved enough persistence to witness the roar.
