The place is a cavern, but not of dark and dripping stone. It is a vast, sunless city carved within the hollow of a world. The air is dry as bone dust and warm as a living thing’s breath. Light does not come from above, but from within. It radiates in broad, glowing veins from the walls themselves—a soft, relentless phosphorescence in hues of chartreuse and deep violet. This is the source of all illumination: the stone is alive with ancient, mineralized light.

The terrain is a complex of plains and mesas, all sculpted from the same pale,ite-like material. It is not rock, but something akin to petrified coral or compressed ash, smooth and chalky underfoot, worn into gentle, rolling forms by eons of a wind that no longer blows. The erosion is absolute, settled, and perfect. Every curve is intentional, as if the city (for it is undeniably a city) was not carved, but grown and then carefully sanded down by time into its final, serene state.

And it is lush. This is the profound contradiction. In this sealed, waterless void, life thrives in staggering profusion. Great, feather-like fronds of a deep-emerald organism spill from fissures in the stone, pulsing with a slow, internal rhythm. They are not plants as understood above—they have no roots, no leaves in the traditional sense. They are more like fungal colonies or crystalline lichens that have evolved to photosynthesize the ambient glow, metabolizing the stone itself. They carpet the low areas in thick, velvety mats and climb the arching structures in delicate, fractal webs. Their texture is cool and slightly granular, like damp sand that never dries because it was never wet.

The structures of the city are simple, primitive in their grandeur. There are no sharp angles. Great, domed dwellings swell from the ground like blisters, their doorways sealed with woven mats of the same fibrous material that forms the fronds. Towers, more like termite mounds or melting candles, rise in clusters, pocked with small, dark windows. Everything is constructed from the local stone, plastered with a hardened resin exuded by the glowing flora, and inlaid with veins of a dull, coppery metal that shows the simplest of hammered patterns. The technology is that of hands, wedges, abrasives, and patient, generational effort.

And it is empty. The silence is a physical pressure, but it is a silence filled with deafening sound. For the city *hums*. The immense cavern resonates with a subsonic drone, the collective vibration of a trillion living filaments drawing sustenance from the mineral world. It is a sound felt in the teeth and the sternum, a constant, low thunder that becomes the baseline of existence. Occasionally, a shift in the great organism’s growth causes a stone to flake free and tumble, the sound of its fall not echoing but being swallowed and amplified into a short, sharp roar that pulses through the hollow for minutes. There are no birds, no insects, no mammals. The fauna is the flora, and the city is its skeleton.

Weirdness is in the details. The glowing fronds retract slowly from the touch of a passing shadow. The coppery inlays in the walls seem to shift pattern when viewed from the corner of the eye, telling stories in a script that is not visual but thermal—you must warm the metal with your palm to see the tale. Pools of a fine, flour-like dust gather in depressions; this is the excretory waste of the ecosystem, and it is softly warm to the touch. The air smells of ozone, hot stone, and a faint, sweet spice, like dried moss.

This is an active, dynamic place, but on a timescale that humbles thought. The great fungal city grows. It breathes. It slowly digests the walls of its own cradle. The processes are geological in pace but biological in nature: the imperceptible creep of a frond across a dome, the century-long settling of a new chamber, the faint brightening of a wall-vein as the collective organism turns its attention there. It is an urban ecosystem in perfect, self-consuming balance, a metropolis of light and stone and life that has never known rain, and never will. It is a secret, pocket world, glowing in the dark, humming its deafening, beautiful hymn to itself.
