# The Littoral Engine

You are inside the sea. Not on it, not beside it, but within its volume, inside a structure that is both ocean and artifact. The water is not natural ocean. It is the **Working Brine**—a conductive, nutrient-rich solution that fills every space, serving as coolant, medium, and bloodstream for the construct.

## The Structure
The **Littoral Engine** is a submerged world-engine. Its scale is continental, a single integrated mechanism built upon the abyssal plain and rising in tiers toward the distant, shimmering surface far above. There is no natural substrate; every surface is fabricated—pitted and scarred alloy plates, vast honeycombed struts, and conduits thick as ancient tunnels. The architecture is industrial, utilitarian, and of a scale that creates its own weather systems within the liquid medium.

**Erosion** is minimal, its features still sharp-edged and raw. The Engine is young in geological terms, perhaps only a few thousand years old. Its surfaces are not worn smooth; they bear the clear, hard lines of its construction, with weld-seams and bolt-heads the size of boulders still distinct. Algae and synthetic patina have begun their colonization, but the bones of the place are unapologetically new.

## The Void and the Vastness
Despite being filled with water, the sensation is of profound **emptiness**. The design philosophy is one of maximal space for minimal mass. Enormous chambers, kilometers across, exist as near-voids—empty except for the circulating brine and the occasional structural member crossing the expanse like a frozen lightning bolt. The distance between solid presences is vast, making navigation a matter of instruments and deep, echoing sonar. It is a cathedral of negative space, where the water itself is the primary occupant.

## Atmosphere and Light
**Light** is normal in spectrum but filtered. It descends from the true ocean surface far above, a diffuse, emerald-gold glow that weakens with depth into a perpetual twilight, then true dark. The Engine’s own bioluminescence and industrial lighting—a cold, actinic blue from plasma arcs, a warm sodium glow from maintenance panels—create pools and rivers of artificial illumination, painting the metal landscapes in stark, cinematic contrasts. The **temperature** is cool, a stable 12°C, maintained by the ceaseless flow of the brine.

The **sound** is immense, layered, and constant. It is the defining sensory experience. There is the deep, infrasonic thrum of the Engine’s heart—a planetary-scale piston or impeller miles away, its vibrations felt in the bones. There is the rushing white noise of water being drawn through intake manifolds, the shriek-whine of turbine farms processing the flow. Closer, there is the groan of metal under tidal stress, the pinging of cooling contracts, and the constant, chattering clicks and whistles of the fauna. It is a loud, busy, and overwhelming soundscape.

## Ecology: The Cultivated Sea
Life here is abundant, but **sparse** in its distribution—concentrated in specific zones designed for it.

*   **Fauna:** The fauna is industrialized. Shoals of **cablefish**—eel-like creatures with bioluminescent lures and metallic scales—stream along power conduits, feeding on electrical parasites. **Gasket crabs**, with bodies shaped like hex nuts and legs like flexible piping, crawl in slow herds across filtration membranes, scraping away biofouling. In the great empty voids, **void-whales** (filter-feeding leviathans of thin, translucent flesh and reinforced cartilage) drift on engineered currents, straining planktonic drones from the water. Predation is managed; it is a balanced, if brutal, ecosystem.
*   **Vegetation:** True plants are rare. What exists is largely **synthetic or symbiotic**. **Conduit-kelp** grows in strict vertical lines up the sides of mega-structures, its fronds embedded with piezoelectric crystals that generate small currents. **Litho-lichen**, a crustose organism, is deliberately cultivated on raw alloy surfaces to fix nitrogen and prevent certain types of corrosive microbe blooms. It appears as a faint, rust-colored dusting on the young metal.

## Processes and Dynamics
The Engine is **active**. Its primary function is not known to its inhabitants (if there are any). It may be terraforming the planet from below, mining the mantle, computing on a geologic scale, or simply existing as an automated monument.

*   **Cycles:** There are tides, but they are artificial—caused by the rhythmic pumping of the heart. There are "seasons" of flow, where certain sectors are flooded with high-nutrient brine, triggering plankton blooms and feeding frenzies, followed by "lean" periods of clear, filtered water.
*   **Weather:** Within the largest voids, **internal weather** forms. Temperature differentials from machinery create convection currents—downwelling columns of cold, dense brine and warm upwellings from reactor piles. These can form stable, slow-moving vortexes miles wide, visible as shimmering, spinning lenses in the water. Sediment and micro-fauna trace their flows like clouds in a sky.
*   **Maintenance:** There are no visible crews. Maintenance is performed by **autonomous systems**. **Shoals of service-mites**, tiny spider-like robots, skitter along surfaces, welding cracks with sparkless cold-bonds. Larger, whale-like **repair drones** drift between sectors, scanning for stress fractures and deploying gel-patches.

## The Rules of the World
1.  **Pressure is Life:** The constant, immense pressure is the normal state. All organisms and structures are adapted to it. A breach is not an explosion, but a catastrophic, inward rush of water that seals itself almost instantly with the formation of hyper-compressed ice-VI.
2.  **Flow is Currency:** The movement of the brine carries everything: energy, nutrients, information (via encoded molecular traces), and larvae. To be cut off from the flow is to die.
3.  **The Engine Comes First:** The primary directive of all systems—biological and mechanical—is the continued function of the Littoral Engine. The ecosystem exists to serve it; the fauna are janitors, filters, and regulators. It is a world built for a purpose, and every element, from the void-whale to the plasma light, is a part of that purpose.

You find yourself on a mid-tier maintenance platform, a grated shelf of metal overlooking a kilometer-deep chasm. The roar of water falling from an upper tier is a constant thunder. The blue glow of a nearby turbine housing paints one side of your face in cold light, while the warm amber of your own helmet lamp cuts a short, defiant beam into the dark. A school of cablefish, their lures flashing in a nervous pattern, darts past you, heading for the deep. The Engine breathes around you—a cool, metallic, and terrifyingly alive breath.
