The world is a pressure vessel. You are inside it.

There is no "ground" in any traditional sense. There is only depth, and within that depth, structures. Vast, hollow architectures built not for inhabitants but for function—cathedral-sized chambers of pitted iron and blackite, their surfaces fused by ancient, unimaginable pressure. They do not rise from a seafloor; they are suspended, anchored by nothing but the sheer mass of their own design, hanging in a column of fluid that is not water.

It is warm. Not the warmth of sun or hearth, but the deep, metabolic warmth of a planet's cooling core. The liquid medium is dense, viscous, and carries a constant taste of minerals and metallic salts. It presses against every surface, a silent, omnipresent weight.

And the sound. The sound is a physical entity. It is a roar that has no source you can identify, because it comes from everywhere. It is the grind of colossal, unseen gears miles away, transmitted perfectly through the fluid. It is the hiss of superheated vapors jetting from ruptured conduits. It is the low, tectonic thrum of the entire structure—this entire, sunken city-engine—vibrating with purpose. The noise does not echo; the medium is too thick for echoes. It simply *is*, a constant, deafening pressure on your bones and teeth.

There is almost no life here. What exists is not biology as you know it. Occasionally, across the pitted surface of a strut the size of a mountain, a slow, phosphorescent bloom of crystal will spread over decades, a cold light in the dimness. Or you might see a drift of spore-like motes, glowing with their own internal friction, spiraling in the currents created by a turning turbine the size of a moon. They do not eat or reproduce in any recognizable way; they simply *are*, temporary patterns in the flow.

The built environment is everywhere, and it is urban in its density, but not for people. It is a city of machines, by machines, for a purpose lost to time. Tubes the width of highways bundle together, their surfaces sweating condensation that forms great, wobbling droplets and slides away. Platforms and gantries extend into the murk, supporting nothing now but the sediment of ages. Lights—some still functioning—cut cones of stark, industrial yellow through the gloom, illuminating whirling flywheels or the slow, majestic rise and fall of a piston the size of a skyscraper. Their movement is the source of the turbulence. The entire medium is in a state of churn, a slow, powerful chaos of currents and vortices created by the world's ceaseless operation.

You are in a pocket. A single, vast chamber within an infinite, submerged machine. The scale shifts. The wall you touch is a rivet head. The ceiling you cannot see is the curve of a single pipe. You are a mote in its circulatory system. The structure is in balance, but it is the balance of a perpetual storm. Erosion is not from wind or rain, but from the constant, scouring flow of the mineral-rich medium and the vibration that shakes loose any weakness.

This is a post-life, post-surface, post-reason world. It exists in its own state, a deep-sea factory forging nothing, powered by geothermal heartbeats, its only fauna the ghosts of its own functions. The weirdness is not in sudden aberrations, but in the fundamental reality: a warm, deafening, turbulent, empty, urban abyss, humming its iron hymn in the dark.
