The world is a drowned engine.

You are inside something. Or it is inside you. There is no dry land here, only the suggestion of it in the great, sloping plains of metal that descend into the crushing dark. The water is not an ocean; it is a medium, a cold, saline blood filling the veins and chambers of a construct so vast its upper reaches are lost in a gloom that is not sky. The temperature is a constant, biting chill that seeps through layers, a cold not of winter but of deep, eternal shade.

Everything is built. The seafloor—or what passes for it—is a tessellated plain of hexagonal plates, each the size of a town square, their edges fused with a silicate weld that has, over eons, been softened by the patient chemistry of the water. From this plain rise the structures: not towers, but baffles, conduits, and immense, hollow pillars whose surfaces are etched with fractal grooves. They are not ruins; they are the state of being. There is no rock, no soil, no organic substrate. Only material that was poured, forged, and set into a purpose so ancient its function is now indistinguishable from geology.

The erosion is absolute, yet settled. This is the oldest state. Edges are smoothed into organic curves. Delicate filaments that might have been circuitry or sensory arrays have frayed into clouds of metallic hair that drift in the slow current. The dim light filters down from a surface hundreds of meters above, a faint, pearlescent glow that makes the particulate matter in the water shine like a nebula. It is a twilight that never ends.

And in this twilight, life—or what the parameters insist is fauna—abounds. But it is not life of flesh. It is life of mechanism and pattern. Swarms of crystalline entities, no larger than your fist, move in shimmering schools. Their bodies are polyhedra of clear mineral, inside which sparks of cold, blue light arc between filigree contacts. They are not swimming; they are executing a drift, a slow-motion computation that produces no sound but a sub-audible vibration felt in the bones. Larger presences glide through the middistance: smooth, torpedo-shaped silhouettes of a black, non-reflective material, moving with a deliberate, tidal grace. They do not hunt. They patrol. They adjust. They are the custodians of a process whose termination condition was never programmed.

The turbulence is not in storms, but in the perpetual, low-grade reconfiguration of the world itself. A distant pillar groans—a sound like whale song filtered through metal—and you see a section of its flank slowly, silently, peel away like shed skin. The flakes do not fall; they disintegrate into a cascade of glittering dust that is immediately seized by the current and drawn into a new eddy, where it begins, impossibly, to coalesce into a different, simpler shape. A hexagonal plate beneath you shudders, slides two feet to the left, and locks into a new position with a deep, resonant *thock*. The water churns with the released energy, a brief, violent silt-up of metallic powder before it settles again. The world is in a state of constant, minor birth and death. It is a machine dreaming of geology, its metabolism a slow, tectonic grind.

Sound is rare, and therefore profound. The dominant noise is the water itself—a deep, pressing hum, the sound of pressure. Over this, the occasional metallic sigh, the crystalline *chime* of the smaller fauna brushing against one another, the distant, thunderous shift of a monolithic component. There is no birdsong, no rustle of leaves, no insectile chatter. The silence between these events is heavy and liquid, a silence of scale and antiquity.

This is a place beyond ecology, in a state of advanced, turbulent senescence. The built environment is the only environment. The fauna are its antibodies, its nervous system, or perhaps simply its idle processes, still ticking in the cold dark after the operator has vanished. The rules are simple: erosion is the only true creative force; stability is an illusion maintained between one catastrophic shift and the next; and all things, from the smallest light-spark to the greatest baffle, are both structure and sediment, waiting to be remade by the endless, patient, turbulent water.
