This is **the Sump**, though no one alive remembers why it was built, or what it was meant to sump.

It is a vast, submerged cathedral of industry, a single bounded area of immense pressure and engineered silence—except for the silence is not silent at all. The scale is paradoxical: a hamlet-sized pocket of absolute construction sunk beneath the seafloor, or perhaps the seafloor was sunk around it. Every surface is metal, ceramic, and treated polymer, worn to a smooth, cold patina by millennia of saltwater and the patient grinding of silt. There is no natural substrate. The floor, walls, and vaulted ceiling are all one seamless, jointed artifact.

Erosion here is not of rock, but of purpose. The structure is ancient, settled into its final, stable form. Edges are rounded. Markings have been leached away. It exists in a state of deep-age equilibrium, the ultimate endpoint of a construction project that consumed a world.

Water is not an element here; it is the medium, the atmosphere, and the weight. It is a thick, cool, perpetual presence, filling every void. Pressure is a constant, sub-aquatic crush, felt in the bones of the place itself. The light is dim, a diffuse, blue-green gloom that filters down through kilometers of ocean above, or is it generated faintly by the structure itself? Bioluminescence from the fauna provides shifting, localised constellations of light.

The air—if one could call it that—is thick with the sounds of the Sump's ongoing function. **It is loud.** A deep, industrial thrum vibrates through the metal, the fundamental note of engines or pumps that have turned for eons. Superimposed upon this are the shrieks and clicks of the fauna, the rush of water through massive conduits, and the periodic, groaning shift of immense mechanical parts deep in the walls. It is an active place, but its activity is cyclic, metabolic, like the beating of a vast, metallic heart.

Fauna is abundant, but life has adapted entirely to the made-world. Clouds of small, eyeless fish with sensitive lateral lines dart along conduits, grazing on films of chemosynthetic bacteria that bloom around minute leaks. Larger, slow-moving things with translucent flesh and lures of cold light drift through the central chamber, their forms echoing the shapes of forgotten machines. Insects the size of a hand, with carapaces of black chitin, scrape at mineral deposits on the walls. There are no plants here, only these animals and their bacterial mats, a food web built from chemistry, pressure, and the remnants of the machine's exhalations.

The built environment is total, but it has been co-opted. Conduits the size of tunnels serve as migration paths. Vast, empty reservoirs are nesting grounds.
