The place is a hollow.

You understand this not by seeing it—you see nothing—but by the sound of it. The sound is immense, a dense, low-frequency hum that you feel in the bones of your chest and the roots of your teeth. It is the sound of a billion, billion filaments growing, flexing, and settling against one another in the absolute dark. It is the sound of a city breathing, if a city were made of living tissue and its purpose was to grow and to listen.

There is no water here. The air is cool and utterly dry, carrying only the faintest scent of spores and stone dust and the sharp, green tang of vegetative chemistry. This is a world of cellulose and lignin, of complex sugars and alkaloids, but not of H₂O in any free state. The moisture exists *within*, bound into the vast, spongy architecture that fills the void.

You are standing on—or rather, in—a spongy mat of interwoven rootlets and decayed, peaty material that gives slightly underfoot. It is the floor, but it is also alive, a dark, russet-black carpet that hums with the same subsonic frequency as everything else. Above, around, through the space, is the **plant**.

It is not a forest. A forest implies separate trees with air between them. Here, the plant is the medium and the message. Great, ribbed columns of a fibrous, wood-like substance rise from the mat, but they are not trunks. They are conduits, swelling and narrowing as they climb, branching into lattices that fuse with other lattices until the air—if you can call it air—is partitioned into vast, interconnected chambers and corridors. It is architecture, but not built. It was *grown*, directly into its final form over an age so long the distinction between intention and erosion has vanished.

The walls are translucent in patches, not with light, but with a faint, bioluminescent **pulse**. It is the only visual reference in the dark: a slow, rhythmic wave of emerald and violet that travels along the veins of the plant-structures, illuminating the intricate, vein-shot membranes that stretch between the lattice-works. These membranes are the "windows" and "rooms," thin sheets of cellular tissue that vibrate, contributing to the resonant hum.

This is where the **loudness** lives. The deep hum is the foundation, but layered atop it are the sounds of the plant's life: the slow, grinding creep of growth against growth, a sound like continents shifting. The sudden, wet *pop* of a spore sac reaching maturity and rupturing. The dry, papery *shush* of a million spores taking flight on an air current you cannot feel, to settle elsewhere and begin the patient process of integration. Occasionally, a deeper, more resonant *thrum* echoes through the chambers as a major structural conduit adjusts, settling a millimeter over a century. These are the events here. They are as dramatic as a thunderstorm.

The **scale** is deceptive. You perceive the chambers as immense, cathedral-like spaces, yet the parameters suggest a "hidden" or "pocket" scale. Perhaps this entire city exists within a crevice of a planet's dead crust, or inside a single, colossal geode of vegetative matter. The emptiness (0.1 density) is not of volume, but of *otherness*. There is the plant, and the spaces the plant has left for itself, and nothing else. No animals. No insects. No birds. The silence that should exist between the plant-sounds is instead filled with the ever-present hum and the memory of sound. It is a profoundly **empty** fullness.

Here, in the dark, the **primitive** tools of its making are evident in the results. No metal, no fired clay. The "buildings" are shaped by differential growth rates, by thigmotropism—response to touch. Walls were thickened where stress was felt. Lattices were reinforced over generations. The "urban" plan is a topological map of resource flow and structural necessity, a city whose blueprint was written in hormones and genetics, not on papyrus. It is the ultimate organic settlement.

Time moves with a **slow**, geologic patience. The cool temperature is stable, unchanging. The erosion here is not of wind or water, but of senescence and replacement. Old conduits are slowly walled off with new growth, becoming solid, inert pillars of hardened lignin. New, delicate filaments explore the margins of the chambers, seeking... what? Nutrients from the air? Resonance from the hum? Their purpose is inscrutable. The cycles are those of growth, resonance, spore-release, and integration, measured in decades and centuries.

The **weirdness** is in the consciousness of the place. It feels deliberate. The pulsing lights follow rhythms that are almost musical, the architecture is optimized for sound conduction, for amplifying that foundational hum. It is as if the entire ecosystem is a single, vast instrument, or a sensory organ tuned to frequencies you cannot comprehend. It is a city built to listen to itself, and to grow in response to what it hears.

You are a brief, warm anomaly in its cool, dry, resonant dark. Your breath is the most foreign thing here. The plant has already begun to integrate you—not with malice, but with the slow, inexorable curiosity of a system that knows only one imperative: to become more itself.
