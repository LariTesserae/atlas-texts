The world here is a high, baking expanse of fused mineral plains and skeletal formations, stretching toward a horizon that wavers not with heat haze alone, but with a subtler, more fundamental distortion. This is a land without a drop of liquid water; the very concept seems alien. The ground is a mosaic of dark, vitreous slag and pale, calcium-like crusts, hot enough to shimmer through the soles of any footwear that might dare tread it. The air is thin, desiccated, and carries a charge that makes the hairs on your arms stand on end.

There is no green. No root, no leaf, no blade. The biology of this place, if it can be called that, is mineral, fungal, and mechanical in ways that defy easy categorization. The "fauna" is overwhelming, but not in the manner of swarming beasts. It is a slow, geological procession of entities that blur the line between organism, mineral deposit, and automated process.

They move with the grinding patience of tectonic plates. Colossal, multi-limbed structures of porous bone-white ceramic and iron-oxide red stalk across the plains on hydraulic legs that sink into the crust with a sigh of powdered stone. They are the **Lithivores**. They do not eat plants; they consume the bedrock itself, grinding it through internal maelstroms of heat and pressure, excreting refined metal beads and inert silicate pebbles behind them in long, orderly trails. Their passage is the primary source of sound: a deep, sub-bass drone of grinding, a percussive *clank-hiss* of piston and exhaust, a sound that feels less heard and more felt in the marrow of your bones.

Other entities are stationary, or nearly so. Vast, fractal colonies of crystalline filaments grow in fractal bursts from the slopes of eroded mesas. They are photosensitive, and in the normal light of the high sun, they glow with a dull, internal luminescence, converting solar radiation directly into complex chemical energy through a process that has nothing to do with chlorophyll. Occasionally, one will shatter with a sound like breaking glass, releasing a plume of metallic vapor that catches the light in a rainbow of toxic colors before being whisked away by the dry wind.

And then there is the **Weirdness**. It is not an anomaly here; it is the substrate. The rules of perspective and distance are suggestions that often go unheeded. A Lithivore seen from the south may appear as a towering, spindly walker; view it from the north, and it seems to compress into a low, beetle-like shape, its limbs folding in impossible, non-Euclidean ways. The horizon line sometimes bubbles upward, creating transient, mirage-like mesas that persist for hours before subsiding. The loud, industrial sounds of the Lithivores occasionally arrive *before* the action that creates them, as if time here is a thick fluid that can be compressed and displaced.

There are no rivers, but there are **Sonic Flows**. In the canyons carved by aeons of wind and the passing of the great machines, standing waves of sound become almost solid. You can see the air ripple in harmonic nodes, and fine dust levitates in shimmering, stratified sheets, moving against the wind. To touch one is to feel a numbing vibration that resonates with the iron in your blood.

The ecology, if it can be called that, is a closed, industrial cycle of consumption and excretion, powered by geothermal heat from deep below and solar energy from above. The Lithivores consume the world, the crystal colonies capture energy, and byproducts feed simpler, fungal-like matrices that creep in the shadows of the great walkers, breaking down waste into raw elements. It is a world of immense, slow industry with no factory, of overwhelming life with no life as we recognize it. It is settled into its own state of being—a state of hot, loud, slow-motion reality breakdown, where the ground beneath your feet is both ancient and constantly, agonizingly, remaking itself.
