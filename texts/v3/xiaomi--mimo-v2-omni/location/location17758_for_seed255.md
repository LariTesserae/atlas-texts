# The Vatscape

It is a world made, and only recently made.

You stand on a floor of fused grit—dark, vitreous stuff that sweats condensation and never fully dries. It is not stone. It is not metal. It is the slag of something else, something pressed and heated into being, and it extends in every direction, a plain of dark, damp glassiness pocked with shallow depressions where water collects in still, black mirrors. The air is cool, not cold, but a deep, clinging coolness that seems to rise from the ground itself. There is no sky. There is only a vaulted darkness above, from which the intermittent moisture falls—not rain, but a steady, irregular weeping of droplets that ping and spatter on the hard ground, each one making a tiny, resonant *tink* before merging with the puddles.

The land is not flat. It is *constructed*. Low, sinuous ridges, like the buried spines of vast serpents, run across the plain. They are not hills; they are laid courses of the same vitreous material, stacked and fused, their sides etched with long, vertical grooves—as if something massive was dragged over them while they were still soft. In places, these ridges coalesce into low, bulbous arches and honeycombed walls, all of the same dark, glassy substance. The density of it is overwhelming. There is no empty space. Every surface is textured, grooved, or layered with the residue of its own making. It feels like the interior of a kiln that never cooled, a place shaped by immense, primitive tools whose marks are the landscape's only features.

There is no plant life. Not a spore, not a lichen. The only ecology here is one of seepage and accretion. In the deepest puddles, where the weeping water pools, thin, crystalline films grow at the edges—translucent, colorless, fractal patterns that crunch to dust if you touch them. They are not alive as we know it. They are a mineral bloom, a slow crystallization from the mineral-charged water. In the hollows of the ridges, something else gathers: a fine, metallic grit, iron-red and copper-green, that settles in drifts. It is not sand. It is the erosion of the world itself, just beginning—the very first wearing away of these new and raw geologies. It is the sound of it you hear, sometimes: a faint, collective hiss, like sand slipping through a sieve, as the walls give up their first grains.

And there are the sounds. Gods, the sounds.

The darkness is alive with noise. It is not a quiet place. There is the constant, irregular percussion of the weeping water, a billion tiny impacts that merge into a soft, pervasive roar, like a distant waterfall heard through a wall. Beneath that, there is the *groaning*. The entire structure is under tension. The vast, fused ridges and arches settle and shift with a slow, grating complaint—the sound of stone under unimaginable weight, but sharper, more metallic. It echoes. The vault above catches every sound and smears it, so that a single groan becomes a chorus, a drip becomes a rainstorm. And there are other sounds: the wet *shluck* of something heavy moving through a shallow pool just beyond sight; a rhythmic, metallic tapping from within the walls, like a slow, colossal heart; the high, whistling sigh of air being pulled through unseen cracks in the fabricated vault.

The fauna, when you find it, is rare and strange. In the glow of the water-pools, you might see them: things like crabs, but made of the same vitreous material as the ground. They are translucent, their interiors a maze of capillary tubes carrying the copper-green and iron-red fluids. They move with a slow, deliberate scrape of glass-on-glass, picking at the mineral blooms or the metallic grit, their movements echoing in the chambers. They are not predators. They are custodians, or perhaps just another form of the erosion, slowly dismantling and reconstituting the world in their own image. A larger shape sometimes moves on the periphery—a low, multi-legged silhouette that drags itself along the grooves of the ridges, leaving a faint, phosphorescent trail that fades within minutes. It makes no sound but a deep, resonant *hum* that you feel in your bones.

This is the Vatscape. A place of total manufacture, freshly begun. The primitive tech that made it is not seen, but its handiwork is everything. The tools were brute-force: immense presses, vitrifying heat, channels for pouring the molten glass-substrate. The marks are everywhere—the grooves, the seams, the unnatural regularities in the arches. It is a world built for a purpose that is no longer stated, if it ever was. The active processes are the processes of settling: the water finding its level, the materials beginning their first, slow decay, the glass-crabs performing their inscrutable maintenance, the entire structure singing its song of tension and release into the damp, echoing dark.

It is dense, active, loud, and profoundly strange. A beginning, raw and wet and groaning, waiting for its first true history to be written in its slowly eroding skin.
