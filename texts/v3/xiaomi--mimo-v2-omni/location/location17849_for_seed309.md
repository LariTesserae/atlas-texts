The place is a wound in the earth that never scabbed over. You descend, not into a cave, but into a *gullet*. The air is close and damp, clinging to the skin like a second membrane, and it smells of deep, wet chlorophyll, of ozone from arcing machinery, and the rich, fecal perfume of things digesting things. The warmth is biological, a body-heat rising from the very ground.

You are inside the **Sinter-Garth**, the Old Churn. It is a cathedral, but a cathedral built by no one, or by everything at once.

**Terrain & Materials:** The floor is not stone, but a dense, springy mat of interwoven roots and fungal hyphae, bleached white in some places, slick and black in others. It gives slightly underfoot, like walking on the skin of a living drum. There is no bedrock visible. Everything is substrate—a living, layered architecture. Walls rise in buttressed swells of **trunk-mass**, not individual trees but a fused, cellular expanse of plant tissue, grooved and scarred. The ceiling, glimpsed in flickers of dim bioluminescence, is a tangled lattice of aerial roots and crystallized mineral deposits, from which hang great, pulsating bladders of **stored water** and nutrient gel. The "rock" you might see is theiteiteite calcium deposits secreted by the plants to reinforce their structure, pitted and etched by centuries of acidic exudates. This is an **established** form; the Garth has found its equilibrium, a slow, silent war of growth and secretion frozen into a architecture of balance.

**Atmosphere & Light:** The air is thick, almost chewable, saturated with **intermittent moisture**. Condensation beads on every surface, gathering in fat drops that fall with a soft *pat* into hidden pools or onto the trembling caps of giant fungi. The light is **dim**, a permanent dusk. It comes from three sources: the faint, sickly green glow of phosphorescent moss in the deeper cracks; the strobing, electric-blue flashes from **industrial** capacitor banks half-swallowed by root-mass; and, most pervasive, the diffuse, bloody amber radiance that filters through the ceiling-lattice from the **plant-substrate** itself—a photosynthetic afterglow, as if the entire space is a lung that glows with its own circulation.

**Soundscape:** It is **loud** with a layered, unending churn. The lowest frequency is the deep, rhythmic pulse of the **overwhelming fauna**—great, soft-bodied creatures, the *Siphons*, moving through unseen channels behind the root-walls, their movement a seismic grind. Layered over this is the constant trickling, gurgling, and sudden *spurt* of water moving through the hydraulic systems of the plant-architecture, a network of capillaries and siphons. Then the **active** biological noise: the high, chittering swarms of *Lens-Wing* insects whose crystalline wings refract the dim light; the wet, tearing sound of predatory *Strangler Vines* constricting around their prey; the resonant, mournful call of a *Root-Warden*, a six-limbed herbivore that scrapes mineral deposits from the trunks with bony plates on its snout. Piercing through it all are the sounds of the **industrial** heart: the metronomic *clank-and-hiss* of a vast, half-alive piston driven by the pressure of rising water; the electric whine of a generator whose cooling fins are furred with moss; the eerie, sub-audible hum of a geothermal tap, its purpose long forgotten, now just another element of the ecosystem.

**Ecology & The Weirdness:** This is **plant-as-substrate**. There is no distinction between the ground, the walls, and the inhabitants. The fauna, which is **overwhelming** in its variety and density, is not just *on* the plants, but *of* them. The *Lens-Wings* have photosynthetic patches on their carapaces. The *Siphons* are filter-feeders, drawing nutrients directly from the plant's vascular flow via symbiotic proboscises. The **strange** rules are evident in the cycles: every few hours, a great shudder runs through the Garth. The capacitor banks discharge in a cascade of blue light, and with a groan of organic machinery, the plant-walls contract, wringing themselves. Stored water is pumped upwards, and a fine, warm rain falls from the ceiling—**intermittent rain**, born not of clouds, but of the structure's own excretory cycle. This "rain" is slightly viscous, faintly sweet, and carries the genetic spores of the Garth, which cling to every surface, ready to grow.

**The Urban & Built:** This is a **settlement-scale** environment, but the settlement is the ecosystem itself. The "infrastructure" is the root-network, the water conduits, the capacitor banks. Long ago, perhaps, this was a mine or a sealed laboratory, an **industrial** complex. Now, the **urban** layer is the sheer density of interconnected life forms, each occupying a niche in the architecture. The **built** elements are not ruins; they are *incorporated*. A rusted catwalk is now a hardened spine for a climbing predator. A shattered control panel is a nesting site for bioluminescent larvae. The entire place is a single, humming, **active** system where the concepts of "natural" and "artificial" have been composted into a new, terrible, and vibrant truth. It is small—**grove-or-room scale**—but within its bounded darkness, it contains a universe of wet, warm, noisy, and relentlessly growing life.
