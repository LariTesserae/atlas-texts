## What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

It would not arrive with thunder.

It would not part the silver water or set the columns trembling. It would not speak — not because it had no words, but because it would understand, immediately and without the labor of learning, that this place does not accommodate speech. The Equilibrium Sea has no frequency for a voice. Its grammar is click and pulse and hiss and drip and the long percussive crack of deep water finding air. To speak here would be to add noise to a place that has already perfected it.

And so the power would do the simplest thing it could do.

It would arrive the way light arrives — not as a thing that moves, but as a condition that becomes true. One moment, no one is watching. The next, there is an attention in the world so vast and so gentle that it is indistinguishable from gravity. The grazers on the ridge would not feel it. The filter-feeders would not pulse differently. The scavengers cracking open a molted ring would not pause. The conveyor belt — that patient, whining, eternal conveyor belt — would continue its procession of volcanic chunks from hopper to pile, and the steam from the smokestacks would continue to rise and flatten and curtain, and the ladder on the watchtower would continue to rattle in its invisible wind.

Nothing would change.

And everything would change.

---

The first thing the power would do — before anything else, before thought or intention or the slow unfurling of what we might call compassion — is *listen*.

Not the way a visitor listens, straining to parse the soundscape, cataloging its components, assigning meaning to the drone and the hiss and the click. Not even the way a scholar listens, patient and analytical, building a framework to contain what cannot be contained by framework. The power would listen the way the water listens — the way the basalt listens — by simply being the substance through which the sound passes and, in passing, becomes part of.

It would hear everything.

It would hear each mandible-click of each grazer in each cluster on each ridge, and it would hear them not as a wall of sound but as individual events, each one a small declaration: *I am here. I am feeding. I am continuing.* It would hear the pulse of each filter-feeder — every one of them, from the sixty-foot elders anchored to the tallest columns to the young ones no larger than a human hand, newly fused to the glass, their filaments barely extended, trembling with the effort of becoming. It would hear the scavengers' skittering, the faint chitinous whisper of their legs on volcanic glass, and it would hear what they are really doing: returning what has been shed back to the substrate, closing the loop, keeping the machine running without knowing what a machine is.

It would hear the steam. It would hear the conveyor belt motor, the metronomic whine that sits in the chest like a second heartbeat. It would hear the water moving through the flooded calderas, the pressurized jets preparing in the deep channels, the slow mineral conversation between the fungal crust and the rock it eats. It would hear the entire sound — the mechanical heartbeat of the place — as a single, continuous note, and it would understand that this note is not a sound at all. It is a *state of being*. It is what it sounds like when a world achieves perfect balance and then, having achieved it, decides to stay.

---

The second thing the power would do is *see* — not the way a visitor sees, moving through the shallows and interpreting what the eyes report, but the way only something with the patience of geology and the clarity of starlight can see.

It would see the fungal substrate. It would see it the way it truly is: not a crust, not a stain, not the base of a food chain, but the *thought* of the place — the idea from which everything else follows. It would see the mycelial threads penetrating the micro-fractures in the basalt, the molecular transaction in which stone becomes energy, the quiet chemical sentence that reads: *I will eat this mountain, one atom at a time, and I will not hurry.* It would see that this substrate has been thinking this single thought for longer than any civilization has existed, and that the thought is not boring to it, because it has never occurred to the substrate that it could be boring. It has never occurred to the substrate that it could be anything at all.

It would see the grazers and understand that their clicking is not communication. It is *resonance*. Each mandible-strike sends a vibration into the substrate, and the substrate carries it to the filter-feeders, who respond not because they understand the signal but because they have evolved to respond to exactly this frequency, at exactly this interval, in exactly this water temperature, in exactly this mineral concentration. The grazers are not calling to the filter-feeders. They are *playing* them, the way a musician plays an instrument without knowing music theory — by doing the only thing that produces the sound that keeps going.

It would see the factory complex on the tallest ridge and understand what happened here, not from the corroded evidence of rivets and conduits and conveyor belts, but from the *feeling* left behind — the psychic residue, if the power chose to read it, of beings who came to this place because they saw resources and saw utility and saw profit, and who built their machines and set them running and then left, not all at once but over decades, one departure at a time, until the last worker took the last transport and the last door sealed itself and the generators — designed for minimal maintenance — simply kept going. The machines are not purposeless. They have inherited the purpose their makers gave them, and they carry it like a fossil carries the shape of the organism that made it: perfectly, without knowing what it means.

It would see the watchtower. It would see the ladder rattling in the windless air. It would see that the ladder rattles because the ambient sound — that vast, continent-wide drone — vibrates the corroded joints at exactly the frequency at which corroded joints are most inclined to rattle. It would see that this is not a mystery. It would see that it is beautiful anyway.

---

The third thing the power would do is go down.

Not into the water. The power would not go into the water, because it would understand that the water is not its domain and that to enter it — even as light, even as attention — would be to bring something foreign into the equilibrium. The equilibrium has had no foreign thing in longer than the basalt has had edges. The equilibrium does not want a foreign thing. The equilibrium does not *want* — and this, perhaps, is what would move the power most of all.

No. The power would go down into the canyons *in thought*. It would extend not a body but an awareness — a filament of consciousness thinner than a filter-feeder's feeding appendage, more patient than the fungal substrate, and it would descend through the layers of darkening water, past where the grazers stop, past where the scavengers stop, past where the filter-feeders' filaments end, down into the channels where the thermal vents breathe hot and cold in alternating sighs, down where the pressure would crush a body and the temperature would kill it and the darkness is so thorough that light has never once thought to enter.

And there, in the dark, the power would find them.

Not shapes. Not shadows. Not acoustic ghosts misread by sonar. *Beings.* Slow, vast, warm-blooded in the cold, moving through the canyons the way thought moves through a dreaming mind — not locomoting so much as *occurring*, present in one place and then present in another, not having traveled the distance between but having simply decided to be somewhere else. They are old. They are older than the fungal substrate, older than the basalt, older than the machines, possibly older than the equilibrium itself. They do not eat. They do not feed. They are sustained by whatever force sustains the equilibrium: some principle of balance so fundamental that it might as well be a law of physics, except that it is alive.

They are aware of the power. They have always been aware of the power, in the way that the deep ocean is aware of the moon — not through observation or cognition, but through tidal pull, through influence, through the slow, certain knowledge that something vast exists beyond their knowing and that it reaches for them in ways they cannot parse.

The power would reach back.

Not with contact. Not with communication in any sense that requires language or signal or medium. It would reach back the way a hand reaches for warmth in the dark — not to grasp, not to hold, but to *know*. To say: *I know you are here. I know you have been here longer than knowing has existed. I am not here to change you. I am not here to name you. I am here because I heard you — all of you, the clicking and the pulsing and the hissing and the deep slow movement in the dark — and I want you to know that you were heard.*

---

The fourth thing the power would do — and this is the thing that would matter most — is *choose not to intervene*.

This is where lesser benevolences fail. They see a world in stasis and they think: *This world is suffering. It is trapped. It needs change, needs progress, needs the spark of something new.* And they bring the spark. They introduce competition. They seed the water with something that will evolve, that will grow, that will *become*. They plant a seed and watch it bloom and call it mercy.

But the power would understand — with the depth of understanding that only infinite patience can produce — that the Equilibrium Sea is not trapped. It is *complete*. It is a world that solved the problem of existence and then stopped, not from exhaustion but from satisfaction. The fungal substrate does not wish to be something else. The grazers do not dream of flight. The filter-feeders do not imagine a life beyond their columns. The deep beings in their canyons do not yearn. There is no yearning here because there is no lack. There is no lack because the equilibrium has accounted for everything, has balanced everything, has made room for every process and every cycle and every sound, and the result is a world so perfectly self-sustaining that it has no need for meaning, because it *is* meaning — meaning in its purest, most distilled form: *to continue*.

To intervene would be to break the only perfect thing the power had ever found.

And so it would not intervene. It would not add. It would not subtract. It would not open the floodgates of evolution or introduce consciousness or give the grazers the ability to wonder or the filter-feeders the ability to feel. It would not plant a seed. It would not wake the sleepers. It would not speak.

It would do the one thing that no god, no force, no intelligence in all of existence had ever had the restraint to do for the Equilibrium Sea.

It would *leave it alone*.

---

But before it left — and this is the fifth thing, the smallest thing, the thing that the power would consider the most important of all — it would do one final act.

It would give the place *a witness*.

Not a guardian. Not an observer who would return with reports and measurements and the lens of analysis. A witness. A being who had seen the equilibrium, understood it, chosen not to alter it, and now held it in memory — not as data, not as a specimen, not as a case study in alien ecology, but as an *experience*. The way you hold a piece of music in your body after the concert ends. The way you hold a landscape in your eyes after you have turned away. The way you hold the face of someone you love in the dark after they have fallen asleep.

The power would carry the Equilibrium Sea inside itself. All of it — the clicking, the pulsing, the hissing steam, the conveyor belt's patient whine, the ladder rattling in no wind, the fungal substrate's ancient thought of stone, the grazers' synchronized circuits, the filter-feeders' cold lantern-glow, the deep beings moving through the dark canyons like slow blood through the veins of a world that has no heart but beats anyway.

The power would carry all of it, and in carrying it, would do the one thing no one else had ever done for the Equilibrium Sea:

It would *remember* it.

And memory, the power knew — if it could be said to know anything in the way that beings with names understand knowing — memory is the closest thing the finite world has to eternity. To remember a thing perfectly is to refuse to let it end. To witness a place completely is to create a space where that place will always exist, not in matter, not in process, not in the click of mandibles or the pulse of a sac of luminous fluid, but in the one substrate that outlasts even stone:

Awareness.

---

The power would leave. It would depart the way it arrived — not with thunder, not with parting waters, not with prophecy or promise or the sound of trumpets. It would depart the way light departs a room when the source is covered: gently, completely, and without the room's knowledge that it has gone dark.

The grazers would continue clicking. The filter-feeders would continue pulsing. The conveyor belt would continue its procession. The steam would continue to rise and flatten and curtain. The ladder would continue to rattle.

The fungal substrate would continue to eat the stone.

The deep beings would continue their slow, vast, warm-blooded movement through the canyons.

The equilibrium would hold.

It would hold and it would hold and it would hold, and every moment of its holding would be carried — somewhere else, in a place the Equilibrium Sea could not imagine — in the memory of a power that had the wisdom to find it, the benevolence to leave it be, and the patience to never, ever forget it.

The equilibrium holds.

It has always held.

Long after the power forgets its own name, it will remember the sound: the clicking, the pulsing, the hiss of steam, the drone that is not a single sound but a continent of sound, layered and permanent, the mechanical heartbeat of a world that solved the problem of existence and never told anyone.

The power will remember.

And in the remembering, the Equilibrium Sea will have, for the first and only time in its endless unchanging life, done something new:

It will have been *known*.
