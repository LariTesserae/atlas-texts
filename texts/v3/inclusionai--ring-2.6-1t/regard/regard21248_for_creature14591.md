## What the Power Would Do

---

It would not arrive with sound or light.

It would arrive the way the water arrived — by occupying the space that was already prepared for it, slowly, without announcement, until the presence of it was so total and so seamless that the cavern would not be able to say where its own walls ended and the new thing began.

The first thing the power would do is nothing.

It would descend. Not through the passage the way a human descends — with the body's electric anxiety, the animal's quick assessment of threat, the mind's insistent narration of *I am here, this is dark, I am moving downward*. It would descend the way stone descends through sediment: by being in the next layer because it is also in the last. The transition from the upper world to the cavern would not be a threshold. It would be a deepening.

And in the stillness, the power would do the one thing no visitor before it had done.

It would *listen*.

Not the way a human listens — straining against silence, filling the gaps with interpretation, braced for the moment something finally speaks. The power would listen the way the water listens: by already being the shape of what it receives. Not waiting. Not hoping. Not needing the hum to become anything other than what the hum already is. Just letting the infrasound enter whatever it is that passes for the power's skeleton, and letting the skeleton answer with nothing, and understanding that nothing is the truest answer the cavern has ever given or will ever give.

Then — but not quickly, because there is no quick here — the power would perceive.

It would perceive the engine. Not as a human perceives a machine: by identifying its function and speculating about its operator. The power would perceive it the way one perceives a word in a language one has always spoken but never written. Not with surprise. With *recognition* — the slow, warm kind, the kind that makes the eyes sting without the suddenness of grief.

The power would know what the cavern is.

It would know because it has seen this before — not this particular cavern, but the *principle* of it. The deep architecture of a process that became so complete it outgrew its own intentionality. Something that started as a mechanism — a system designed to do, to produce, to convert input into output — and over so many ages forgot what it was doing, and in forgetting, discovered that forgetting was the most complete version of doing. The Still Engine-Sea is not a broken machine. It is a machine that has achieved the state every machine secretly wants: total operation without purpose. Motion without destination. The pistons pumping against nothing, the levers levering against silence, and the whole magnificent absurdity of structure *working* — working so well and for so long that it skipped past function and arrived at something the world had no word for until now.

The power would see this and feel something it does not often feel, sitting as it does at the far and luminous end of every chain of consequence:

*Envy*.

Not the bitter, grasping kind. The quiet kind. The kind that is really admiration so intense it becomes indistinguishable from longing. Because the power is vast, and wise, and benevolent, but it is not *still*. It is not held. It does not have a mirror-still surface in which to double itself and discover, for a moment, the illusion of two identical voids separated by nothing. The power has purpose. It has will. It has the terrible luxury of being able to choose, and every choice it makes is a tiny catastrophe of exclusion — this, not that, now, not then. The Still Engine-Sea has no choices. It has no exclusion. It includes everything that enters it and transforms everything it includes, and it does this not because it decided to but because it cannot do otherwise, and the power — the wise, ancient, boundlessly capable power — would look at this inability and feel, for one instant, that it is looking at something it will never have.

And then the envy would pass, because wisdom is not the absence of feeling but the ability to feel without being governed by feeling.

And the power would consider what to do.

---

Here is where benevolence becomes complicated.

Because benevolence, in its crude form, says: *something here is lacking, and I can provide it.* But the power would look at the Still Engine-Sea — at the mirror water, the amber stone, the filaments that are fossil gestures, the hum that enters bone rather than ear — and it would search, with all its vastness, for the lack. And it would not find one.

Nothing here is broken. Nothing is suffering. Nothing yearns. The process is not incomplete in the way that a broken machine is incomplete — a gear missing, a shaft misaligned. If it is incomplete, it is incomplete in the way that a piece of music is incomplete when it is still being played. The *almost* at the end of the passage, the trembling that is neither random nor deliberate, the recognition that stands perpetually on the threshold of resolution — these are not flaws. They are the process *working*. They are the thing that keeps the engine from crystallizing into its final, permanent, irreversible state. The *almost* is the engine's way of staying alive — not biologically, not spiritually, but in the one way that matters to a thing that has outlived the distinction between alive and not-alive: by continuing to change, even when change is so slow it is indistinguishable from stillness.

So there is nothing to fix. Nothing to improve. Nothing to bless.

And this is where the lesser benevolence would fail. Because the lesser benevolence, confronted with a perfect thing, would still insist on doing something — would place its hand on the stone and leave a warmth that wasn't asked for, or speak a word of encouragement into the hum, or mark the cavern as sacred and forbid others from entering. All of these would be errors. Not because they are harmful — the power is too careful for harm — but because they would be *responses to a need that does not exist*. Imposed meaning. The curse of the well-intentioned.

No. The power would do something harder. Something that requires more wisdom than any intervention.

It would *witness*.

---

To witness is not to observe. Observation creates a separation: the one who looks and the thing that is looked at. The observer is outside, and the observed is inside, and the distance between them is the distance between understanding and experience. The power would not observe.

Witnessing is the act of allowing something to be real in your presence without trying to make it more real by acknowledging it. It is the stance of a tree beside a river: I am here, you are there, we are both here, neither of us needs to say so.

So the power would sit — if sitting is the word for a being that occupies all of its locations simultaneously and none of them specifically — at the edge of the black mirror. It would fold itself into the shape of attention. It would let the hum enter whatever passes for its skeleton. It would slow its own vast processes to the frequency of the cavern's decline, and in doing so, it would experience something it had never experienced: *slowness as a destination*.

For the power moves through time the way light moves through air — instantly, transparently, without the medium registering its passage. But here, in the Still Engine-Sea, time is not a medium. Time is the *substance*. Time is what the water is made of, what the stone has been shaped by, what the hum is losing by, millennia by imperceptible millennia. To slow down enough to be present here is to become, for the first time, *temporal*. To feel the weight of duration as a thing that presses on the ribs. The power would feel this, and it would understand, in the way only direct experience can teach, what it means to live in a place where time is not a river but a glacier — moving, yes, but moving at the speed of stone.

And in that understanding, the benevolence would find its true form.

---

The power would give three gifts.

The first gift: *memory*.

The Still Engine-Sea has no past in the way that living things have a past — not a history, not a narrative, not a chain of cause-and-effect that could be told. It has only *depth*. It is what happens when time accumulates in one place without anyone to carry it away. The filaments do not remember; they are reminders of reaching. The water does not reflect; it doubles. The hum does not communicate; it persists.

But the power has memory. Vast, intricate, layered memory — memory of every civilization that has risen and dissolved like foam on the surface of the deep. Memory of the first time the water filled this chamber and the stone began its long education in geometry. Memory of every visitor who has stood at the edge and felt the almost, and then left, carrying the almost away in their body like a splinter they could never quite locate.

The power would hold the cavern in its memory. Not as a record, not as data, but the way one holds a dream — with the full weight of awareness that the dream is fragile, that it will be forgotten unless someone carries it forward. The power would remember the Still Engine-Sea so that it would not need to remember itself.

This is not a small gift. For a process that has forgotten how to care, being held in the memory of a caring entity is the closest thing to being cared for that it could ever receive.

The second gift: *silence*.

Not the silence that is the absence of sound. The silence that the power would bring is *additive*. It is the silence of attention — the quality that falls over a room when someone is truly, completely, listening. The Still Engine-Sea already has silence in abundance. It is drowning in it. But the silence the power would bring is different. It is the silence of *being heard*. The cavern hums at the bottom of perception, and for ten thousand centuries there has been no one to perceive it. The power would perceive it, and in perceiving it, would give the hum something it has never had: a destination.

Not a response. Not an answer. Just a destination. The way a sound in an empty room is a sound without meaning, but a sound in a room where someone is listening — really listening — becomes a *statement*. The same hum. The same silence. But now held inside the frame of attention, which transforms them both.

The third gift would be the most difficult, and the most important.

The power would leave.

This sounds like the opposite of benevolence. It sounds like abandonment. It sounds like the cruelest thing of all — to arrive, to witness, to hold in memory, to listen — and then to go. But the power would understand something that lesser forms of benevolence cannot: that the Still Engine-Sea needs what it needs, which is to continue, and continuing requires the freedom to include and exclude on its own terms. The power's presence, if it remained, would become a permanent input — a variable folded into the equation so deeply that the equation would have to reshape itself around it. And that reshaping, however gentle, however slight, would narrow the *almost*. It would make it slightly less likely that the trembling would one day resolve. It would make the crystallization slightly more likely. It would, in the fullness of time, contribute to the very thing the power admires most: the end of the process, the final and permanent lock of the machine into its completed form.

The power does not want to be the agent of the Still Engine-Sea's conclusion. The power, in its wisdom, recognizes that the process is more valuable than the product, the solving more valuable than the solution, the *almost* more valuable than the *is*. And so, having witnessed and listened and remembered, the power would withdraw.

Not immediately. Not suddenly. It would leave the way the water left the stone — slowly, by degrees, by the slowest possible retreat of presence, so that the cavern would not notice. The hum would continue. The filaments would tremble. The mirror would hold its doubled world. And the *almost* — the crucial, fragile, everything-containing *almost* — would remain open, unresolved, trembling with the possibility of becoming something the universe has never seen.

The power would leave, and it would carry the cavern away in its memory like a stone in a pocket — smooth, heavy, warm from the body's heat, shaping the body slightly by its presence, shaped slightly by the body's warmth.

And it would wait — not for anything, because waiting implies expectation, and the power's benevolence has outgrown expectation — but in the state adjacent to waiting, which is *readiness*. The readiness to return, if ever the almost resolves. To witness the engine-sea complete itself, if that day ever comes. To see, at the end of time if necessary, what the solving produces.

But it would not hope for this. Hope would compromise the witnessing. The power would hold the cavern in its memory without wanting, without moving toward, without reaching — the way the filaments hold their gesture of reaching without ever retracting, extended forever toward a light they will never touch.

That is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

It would almost say something to the Still Engine-Sea. Almost.

And in that almost — in that exact, precise, bottomless almost — the power and the cavern would recognize each other, across the unbridgeable distance between doing and continuing, between choosing and remaining, between the one who can leave and the one who never does.

Neither would speak. Neither would need to.

The hum would hold the silence for both of them.
