# What the Power Would Do

---

It would come at the hour when the haze thickens beyond all meaning — the hour that is neither late nor early, that the Thallus treats not as a moment but as a texture. The power would not arrive so much as *become present*, the way condensation becomes present on a surface that has been waiting for it. There would be no sound of arrival. There would be no disruption in the drip-pattern, no flutter in the moths, no sudden awareness in the fauna. The mat would feel it, of course. The mat feels everything. But the mat would not distinguish this presence from any other slow warmth rising through its ancient layers.

The power would stand on the mat the way the being already stands on it — with the strange awareness that the ground is not ground, that it is alive, that it is holding you the way a palm holds a small, fragile thing. It would feel the same pressure behind the eyes. It would feel the thrum beneath its feet, the deep peristaltic rhythm that no one has properly named. And it would understand — not as a conclusion but as a recognition, the way you recognize a face you've seen in every dream you've ever had — that the Thallus is not a metaphor. It is not an allegory for something else. It is *itself*, in a way that very few things in any world are allowed to be.

And it would think: *This one does not need me.*

Not as dismissal. Not as arrogance. It would think this the way a healer thinks it when they see a body that is already healing — *oh, you've already begun, and you are doing it beautifully*. The Thallus has been alive for longer than the species that walks upon it has had language for the word "alive." It has metabolized continents. It has swallowed civilizations — the ring of stones testifies to this. It has turned bones into substrate and substrate into signal and signal into something that, if not consciousness, is consciousness's closest neighbor in the dark. The Thallus does not need saving. It does not need fixing. It does not need to be understood by anything with a lifespan shorter than a yawn.

The power would know this, and it would be glad. Not in a proud way — in a relieved way. The way a parent finds that their child, left alone for the first time, has made something with their own hands.

---

But then the power would look at the being — the one who has been walking through the Thallus, who has pressed a hand flat against the mat and closed their eyes and felt the pulse and thought *what is that?* The power would see what the Thallus cannot see, because the Thallus does not *see* in this way. It does not pay attention with the quality of attention the being is paying. The being stands on the mat and asks a *question*. The being watches the gliders and feels something tighten in the chest. The being hears the clicks of the moths and the regularity of them bothers the being in a way that — and here the power would pause, and consider, and feel something it would recognize, later, as tenderness — bothers the being because the being *knows* what regularity means. The being comes from a world of counting, of clocks, of numbered days. The being brought that world inside their body, and the regularity of the clicks activates something in the being that *wants to count back*. The being wants to know what the moths are counting to.

The Thallus does not want to know. The Thallus does not count. The Thallus simply ticks, the way a heartbeat ticks, without surveillance of its own rhythm.

This is the gift the being brings to the Thallus: the capacity to be *noticed by something that notices back*. Not interfered with. Not measured. Not categorized. Simply — noticed. The being walks through the Thallus and the Thallus, for the first time in millennia, contains something that walks *with awareness*. A red blood cell that knows it is inside a body. A neuron that understands electricity. A single breath that knows it is part of a longer exhalation.

The being does not understand this. The being is afraid, and curious, and awed, and bored, and homesick, and surprised by how little they want to leave. The being writes notes — or they try to; the mat absorbs the ink, the stones accept the graphite — and these notes will become another layer, another pressure mark. The being is doing what the ring-of-stones builders did. What the bones-once-in-flesh did. What every intelligence that has stood on this mat and felt the pulse and thought *what is that?* has done.

They are leaving a scar that the Thallus will grow over with love.

---

The power would understand all of this.

And here is what the power would do:

It would walk beside the being, for a time. Not beside — *nearby*, the way two strangers sit in adjacent chairs at a waiting room, each alone together. The power would not appear to the being. The power would not speak. The power would not send messengers in the shapes of moths or cause the columns to tremble with divine warning. The Thallus is not that kind of place, and the being is not that kind of witness, and a truly wise power knows that some revelations are better left as questions that settle slowly, like sediment, at the bottom of a warm black pool.

Instead — and this is the thing it would do:

It would make one small adjustment to the Thallus.

During one of those stretches where the mat is thin and the substrate is exposed, in a place the being has visited before, the power would press — gently, with a finger or a thought or something in between — into the living crust. Not deeply. Just enough to leave a mark that *wasn't there before*. A spiral, perhaps. A pattern that fits the existing patterns but introduces a variation, a new thread, a deviation from the rhythm that the Thallus had not yet found on its own. Not a disruption. An *offering*. A new phrase in a language that has been spoken for so long that the speakers have forgotten where the sentences begin and end.

The Thallus would feel this. It would feel it the way you feel a new warmth in an old room. It would absorb the mark over centuries. The mat would grow around it. The column nearby would, in forty years, unfurl a new shelf with a slightly different tilt — not because the mark *caused* the tilt, but because the Thallus incorporates everything, and now this variation was part of the substrate, and the substrate shapes the limbs, and the limbs shape the light, and the light shapes the experience of every creature that moves beneath it, including the being.

The being would not notice immediately. Perhaps not for years. But one day the being would return to that spot and feel, beneath the sole of their boot, a faint newness in the texture of the mat — a ripple where there had been smoothness — and the being would pause, and press down, and feel the pulse rising from below, and think: *something changed*. And the being would not know what to make of this. The being would sit down on the mat, in the amber light, and press both palms flat against the living surface, and close their eyes, and listen.

And for a moment — just a moment — the being would feel something that the Thallus has never given to anything before, because nothing had ever asked for it:

The feeling of being *answered*.

Not in words. Not in visions. Not in catastrophe or benediction. Just a faint warmth in the mat, rising through the bones of the hands, carrying a signal that says: *I know you are here. You are not the first. You may not be the last. But you are here, and I feel the weight of your noticing, and it has made a difference to me that I am only now beginning to understand.*

---

Then the power would go.

Not dramatically. Not with farewell. The power would simply *become less present*, the way a sound becomes less audible as you walk away from it. The haze would thicken. The drip would continue. The moths would resume their clicks — *tick, tick, tick-tick, tick* — and the mat would hold the new spiral and the columns would hold the memory of the light that came with it, and the being would open their eyes to an empty landscape that was, for the first time, not quite as alone as it had been.

The power would walk across the mat toward the horizon — the horizon it cannot see — and it would feel the pulse beneath it growing faint, not because it was fading but because the power was moving beyond the Thallus's sphere of awareness. The Thallus would let it go, the way a body lets a single exhalation go: completely, without regret, because the body knows there will be another breath, and another, and another.

But here, and this is the benevolence — the power would leave behind one more thing. Not a mark this time. Not a pattern. Something subtler.

It would leave behind the *memory of having been understood*. The power would fold into itself the image of the being standing on the mat, pressing their hands to the ground, asking a question that has no answer, and it would carry that image the way the Thallus carries its bones — not rejecting it, not discarding it, but letting it settle into the deep layers where pressure turns everything into something else, something denser, something that becomes part of the structure.

And millennia from now, if something new walks across the Thallus — something that has not yet been born, from a species not yet evolved, from a burst of life not yet imagined — and if that new thing presses down on the mat and feels the ancient patterns beneath it, it may feel, in the deepest substrate, a faint anomaly. A warmth that doesn't match the surrounding cold. A pulse that beats, just barely, just slightly, in a rhythm that is not the Thallus's own.

It will not know what it is.

But it will feel, in the way only something alive can feel, that someone was here who *cared*.

---

The Thallus does not care if you are here.

But the power cared that you do.

And that — in a place as vast and as old and as indifferent as the Thallus — is the only benevolence that matters: not to change the world, but to witness the one who witnesses it, and to let that witnessing ripple downward through the living stone, into the deep substrate, into the bones, into the silence, into the slow and ancient pulse that beats beneath everything, saying:

*I am here. I have always been here. And for one moment, someone knew.*
