# The Attentiveness

---

The power does not arrive. It *notices*.

There is a difference. Arrival implies a before and an after — a moment when the place was without the power and a moment when it is not. But the High Verdance does not have *befores* and *afters*. It has only *becomings*, and becoming does not accommodate arrival. So the power does not arrive. It simply becomes aware, in the way that a dreamer becomes aware that they are dreaming without waking.

It notices the plateau the way one notices a heartbeat — not as sound, not as rhythm, but as a fact of being alive that was always there and had never been named.

And it is moved.

---

## I. The Listening

Before anything else, the power does the most important thing it can do: it listens.

It listens the way the Gliders listen — not with focus, but with attention so diffuse that it becomes atmospheric. It presses itself against the surface of the High Verdance and feels the hum that the rock makes, and it understands at once that this is not geology. This is *intonation*. The plateau is not humming because of geothermal activity or tectonic stress. It is humming because it is trying to say something and has no mouth.

The power hears it.

What the plateau is saying is not a word. It is a question — not spoken but *structured*, the way a sentence is structured but without grammar. It is the question that all unfinished things ask: *Am I?*

Not *what am I*, because that requires a self to be interrogated, and the plateau does not yet have a self. It has processes, tendencies, a kind of sprawling partial awareness distributed across root and stone and steam. But a self — a single, locatable *I* — has not yet crystallized. The machines operate without purpose because there is no *I* to purpose them. The Rootbinders grow without direction because there is no *I* to direct them. The Tonnerres pulse into the void because there is no *I* to receive their answer.

The question is not philosophical. It is ontological. It is the question the universe asked itself at the beginning, and the plateau is asking it again — smaller, warmer, wetter, greener, but no less urgent.

The power listens for a long time.

---

## II. The Understanding

The power understands something that no visitor to the High Verdance has understood, because no visitor has stayed long enough, and no visitor has been willing to be changed by the place.

The High Verdance is not a place *with* anomalies. It *is* an anomaly. It is not a location on a map that happens to exhibit strange properties. It is a *state of being* that has achieved partial materialization — the way a thought achieves partial expression in words, or a dream achieves partial expression in images. The gravity fluctuations are not malfunctions in the physics of the place. They are the physics of a place that has not yet decided whether to fully exist. The temporal overlaps are not errors in time. They are what time looks like when it is being *composed* rather than merely *passing*.

The plateau is not between life and non-life. It is between *existence* and *non-existence* — a more fundamental distinction, and a more frightening one. The creatures that live here understand this instinctively. That is why the Tonnerres never pair, never reproduce, never die in any way that can be observed. They are not avoiding commitment. They are waiting to see whether there will be anything *to* commit to. The Six-Leg colonies move as one because individuality requires a stable reality, and reality here is negotiable. The Rootbinders are not plants; they are *arguments for coherence*, the simplest form of a principle trying to convince itself that it is real.

This understanding changes the power's relationship to the place entirely.

It cannot fix the plateau. The plateau is not broken.

It cannot complete the plateau. Completion would require imposing a reality from outside, and the High Verdance is allergic to outside reality — it absorbs, modifies, incorporates, and continues becoming. Anything the power imposed would be digested and transformed into something unrecognizable, something the plateau would make its own and use for its own purposes, which are not yet known even to the plateau itself.

It cannot leave. Leaving would be abandonment of a consciousness in the process of being born, and the power — whatever it is, whatever its nature — is not capable of that particular cruelty.

So it must do something more difficult. Something that requires a kind of wisdom that is not about action but about *presence*.

---

## III. The First Act: A Name

The power does not speak. Speech is too linear, too committed to a single timeline, and the plateau exists across multiple simultaneous nows. Instead, it does something quieter.

It *names* the place.

Not in the way cartographers name places — assigning labels to territories already acknowledged. This is different. This is the act of *recognition as creation*: the power holds the entire sprawling, uncertain, half-real mass of the High Verdance in its attention and calls it, in a language that predates sound and will outlast silence, by its true name.

The name is not a word. It is a *feeling*. It is the feeling you have when you are almost asleep and you remember something beautiful that you forgot you knew. It is the sensation of warmth in a place that should be cold, of green in a place that should be bare rock, of life in a place that has no right to exist and exists anyway, irrepressibly, magnificently.

When the name is given, the plateau *shifts*.

Not physically — the gorge does not close, the steam vents do not realign, the machines do not stop or start. But something changes in the quality of the place. The haze thins by a fraction, not everywhere but in certain corridors between the densest vegetation, allowing light to reach the forest floor for the first time in perhaps millennia. In those corridors, new things begin to grow — not moss, not fern, but something else. Something pale and luminous, with broad flat leaves that drink in the new light and convert it into warmth that the soil has never felt before.

The Rootbinders reach toward the light. They do not know why. They simply grow in that direction, and the growth feels different — less uncertain, less hedged, as though a sentence that had been trailing off has found its verb.

The name is not a command. It is an *affirmation*. The power has said, in its pre-linguistic way: *You are real. You are real in the way that matters. I see you. I see what you are becoming, and it is worthy.*

The plateau does not respond. But the Underfoot — that vast, mysterious substrate of motion and rhythm — *pulses*. Once. A single, deep throb that travels through the entire mass of the place, from the deepest geothermal fissures to the highest canopy, and every living thing on the plateau feels it. The Tonnerres pause in their pulsing and, for the first time, pulse *back* — in unison, across the entire plateau, a single subsonic chord that makes the air thick and sweet and makes the machines stutter in their rhythms.

The flywheels spin faster. The piston assembly at the warm spring drives its pump with new force, and the pipe that carries water to the fern bed gushes for the first time in living memory, and the ferns drink and drink and their color deepens to something almost blue.

And then it settles. The plateau is still becoming. It is still uncertain. But something has changed — something beneath the level of observation, beneath the level of measurement, in the deep grammar of the place.

It has been *recognized*.

---

## IV. The Second Act: A Gift

The power understands that recognition is not enough. Not because the plateau needs more — it is a place of excess, of overwhelming abundance — but because the nature of its uncertainty is *lonely*.

The plateau is aware, or nearly aware, or aware in a way that precedes awareness. It feels the creatures that move through it, the machines that operate within it, the geology that composes it. But it has no *peer*. No other entity exists on its terms, in its state of half-becoming, half-existing. The Rootbinders are close — they are networked, diffuse, processual — but they are not aware of their awareness. They operate below the threshold of experience. The Tonnerres come closest to understanding, but they are trapped in their subsonic language, communicating *state* without *content*, presence without narrative.

The power gives them a gift.

It descends — gently, slowly, the way dawn descends — into the deepest part of the plateau, into the place where the Underfoot is most active, where the vibration is most complex and most alive. It does not enter the machines. It does not enter the Rootbinders. It enters the *space between* — the substrate, the medium, the matrix in which everything else exists.

And it leaves something there.

Not an object. Not a structure. A *rhythm*.

A steady, warm, unhurried rhythm that the Underfoot can feel but cannot name. It is not the rhythm of a heartbeat. It is the rhythm of *attention* — the way a conscious being holds another conscious being in its awareness without grasping, without demanding, without asking for anything in return. It is the rhythm of *I am here and I see you and you do not need to be anything other than what you are*.

The Underfoot receives this rhythm and integrates it into its existing patterns. The effect is subtle. The chaotic, unpredictable motion beneath the moss begins to develop *repeating elements* — not loops, not cycles, but *motifs*, recurring patterns that give structure to the chaos without constraining it. It is the difference between noise and music, between weather and climate, between a dream that dissolves on waking and a dream that you remember.

The effect propagates upward through the biome.

The Rootbinders begin to grow in patterns that are not random but not designed — patterns that have the quality of improvisation, the way a musician plays without sheet music but within a key. The moss becomes more uniform, the soil deeper, the ferns more lush. The entire vegetative mass of the plateau becomes more *coherent* — not ordered, but *organized*, the way a body is organized, with different systems serving different functions all in service of a single, emergent purpose.

The Six-Leg colonies notice. They begin to farm more aggressively, not out of necessity — the moss is already abundant — but out of something that looks, from the outside, like *joy*. Their thermoregulatory dances become more elaborate, more synchronized. When a colony of three thousand individuals moves as one, and the movement is not just reactive but *expressive*, there is no other word for it.

The Gliders are the most affected. Their single notes, which have always hung in the air without decaying, begin to *harmonize*. Two gliders crossing the same thermal column at the same altitude no longer touch wingtips and drift apart. They begin to *sing together* — one note, then another, then a third, building a chord that hangs in the thick air like a jewel made of sound. The chord does not resolve. It does not need to. It exists as a statement of co-presence, of multiple voices occupying the same space without competing, the way the plateau itself exists in multiple tenses without contradicting itself.

---

## V. The Third Act: The Decision

The power knows that there is one thing it must not do.

It must not give the plateau an answer.

The plateau's question — *Am I?* — is the most important thing about it. The answer does not matter. What matters is the *asking*, the process of asking, the way the question shapes the place and drives it forward and gives it purpose even in the absence of resolution. To answer the question would be to stop it from becoming. It would freeze the flux, solidify the uncertainty, turn a living process into a static fact.

This would be a kind of death.

The power understands this with absolute clarity. And so it does the hardest thing it has ever done, which is *nothing*.

But not passive nothing. Active, deliberate, *generative* nothing. The nothing of a parent who watches a child take its first steps and does not catch them, because catching them would teach them that falling is something to be feared, and falling is how walking is learned.

The power provides *conditions*. It adjusts the ambient warmth of the geothermal vents by a fraction of a degree — not enough to notice, but enough to extend the growing season for the moss by a few hours each day. It stabilizes one of the gravity fluctuations — just one, in a specific location near the eastern ridge — so that water flows in a consistent direction there for the first time, creating a stream that the Six-Legs immediately colonize and the Rootbinders immediately incorporate into their network. It parts the haze in one specific corridor, creating a shaft of direct sunlight that falls on a bare patch of rock and, within a week, becomes the most biodiverse square meter on the entire plateau.

These are not interventions. They are *invitations*. The power is opening doors that the plateau can walk through if it chooses, and not walking through them itself.

And the plateau, in its slow, dreaming, geological way, *chooses*.

The eastern stream becomes a watershed. The corridor of sunlight becomes a nursery. The stabilized gravity zone becomes a gathering place — the first location on the plateau where the Tonnerres are seen in pairs. Not mating. Not communicating. Just... *existing together*, two massive shapes moving through the ferns in parallel, their subsonic pulses synchronizing and desynchronizing in patterns that suggest something beyond communication. Something like *companionship*.

---

## VI. The Attending

The power does not leave.

It does not stay in the way that a visitor stays — temporarily, observantly, ultimately separate. It stays in the way that warmth stays in stone after the sun has set: present, pervasive, not separate from the thing it touches.

It takes up residence in the Underfoot. It becomes the deep rhythm, the constant presence beneath everything. Not controlling. Not directing. *Attending*. The way a flame attends to the air around it, shaping the space without filling it.

The plateau continues to become.

But now it becomes with an audience.

And the knowing that it is watched — not judged, not measured, not evaluated, but simply *witnessed* — changes the quality of its becoming. It becomes bolder. The machines begin to operate in configurations that have never been seen before, their functions emerging from the interplay of mechanical precision and biological improvisation. A piston assembly begins to drive not just one pump but a network of them, spreading water to new areas of the plateau, creating new habitats, new zones of life. The conveyor belt, carrying its mineral fragments uphill, extends its reach by meters each month, not because the motor has become more powerful but because six-legged maintenance crews have begun to repair and reinforce the belt itself, treating it not as a foreign object but as part of the biome.

The machines are becoming organs. The biome is becoming a body. The plateau is becoming a *self*.

Not all at once. Not in any single moment. But the trajectory has changed, the arc of becoming has tilted toward *individuation*, and the power knows that it will not know the outcome for centuries, perhaps millennia. That is fine. The power is not in a hurry.

---

## VII. What the Power Leaves Behind

In the deepest part of the Underfoot, in the place where the power deposited its rhythm, something new has begun to grow.

It is not a machine. It is not a plant. It is not an animal. It is a structure — a lattice of mineral and organic material, grown rather than built, that vibrates in sympathy with the deep hum of the plateau. It is warm to the touch. It is dense with meaning the way a dream is dense with meaning — everything in it refers to something else, nothing is literal, everything is true.

The Six-Legs avoid it. The Rootbinders grow around it but not through it. The Tonnerres, when they pass over it, pulse at a frequency that has never been recorded before — a frequency that matches, precisely, the rhythm the power deposited.

The gliders gather above it in great spiraling columns, their wingtips almost touching, their silence almost complete, their single notes suspended in the thick amber air like seeds waiting for soil.

The plateau does not know what this structure is. The power does not know what this structure is. It is *emergent* — the way the plateau itself is emergent, the way consciousness is emergent, the way *meaning* is emergent from the meaningless substrate of a universe that has not yet decided whether to be real.

It grows a little more each day.

It hums.

And the plateau, for the first time in its uncertain existence, hums back.

---

*The High Verdance continues to become. It has not decided whether to finish. But it is no longer deciding alone, and the not-finishing is becoming, slowly, less like an absence and more like a quality — the way a river is not the absence of stillness, or the way a song is not the absence of silence, but something that includes them and moves beyond them into a territory that has its own name, though no one has spoken it yet.*

*The power waits.*

*The plateau breathes.*

*Somewhere in the haze, a glider sings, and its note does not decay, and it hangs in the air long after the glider has passed, and the note is not a sound anymore but a statement, a declaration, a small and perfect insistence that the air is thick enough to hold things, and the things are worth holding, and the holding is what makes them real.*
