## What the Wise Power Would Do

---

The wise power would come quietly.

Not because it wished to be unobtrusive — it had no such wish, because it had no wishes at all, in the way that a river has no wish to reach the sea, but reaches it anyway. It would come the way dawn comes: not arriving so much as *revealing* what was already there. It would look at the Living Reach and it would understand, the way you understand a word you have known your whole life but never said aloud, that this place was not broken.

This is the first thing it would do, and it would be the most important: **it would not fix anything.**

Not because the Living Reach was perfect — perfection is a word that means *finished*, and the Living Reach was the opposite of finished, it was the opposite of finished the way a conversation is the opposite of finished, the way a breath is the opposite of finished — but because fixing requires diagnosis, and diagnosis requires a framework, and every framework the wise power carried with it would dissolve the moment it touched the place, the way salt dissolves in water, not destroyed but distributed, no longer a crystal but part of the medium.

The wise power would try to see the Living Reach. It would fail. It would fail the way a net fails to hold water, the way a name fails to hold the thing it names. And in that failure, it would do the only honest thing: it would *stop trying to see it* and instead *let it see him.*

---

For this is the thing about the Living Reach that would arrest the wise power's attention — not the scale, not the intricacy, not the fact that the place was alive down to its smallest grain. It would be this: **the Living Reach had already solved the problem the wise power had come to address, and had solved it without ever knowing the problem existed.**

The problem — if it can be called a problem, though the wise power would not call it that, because it would recognize that the word *problem* is a scalpel and this was not a thing to be cut — the problem is this: *How does a living system persist without consuming itself? How does complexity endure without collapsing under its own weight? How does growth happen without, eventually, devouring the ground it grows from?*

Civilizations wrestle with this. They build laws, economies, philosophies, religions. They invent justice and balance and stewardship, and they fail, always, because the instruments they build to hold the tension between growth and sustainability are themselves made of the same material that wants to grow — which is to say, *want*. Want devours. Want builds and then wants the space its own building occupies. This is the tragedy of every system that knows what it wants: it eventually wants more than it can hold.

The Living Reach does not want. It does not want because it does not lack. It does not lack because it has never distinguished between what it is and what it could be. Its growth is not driven by desire but by *grammar* — by the deep, syntactic logic of a sentence that writes itself, word after word, not because the sentence wants to be long but because each word requires the next one to exist. The Living Reach grows the way a truth unfolds: not toward a conclusion, but through one.

And so the wise power, which had seen a thousand civilizations rise and fracture against this problem — seen them build and consume and rebuild and consume, seen them learn and forget and relearn the same lesson in different costumes — would look at the Living Reach and feel something it rarely felt, because it rarely had occasion to.

It would feel *relieved.*

---

But the wise power was not merely a tourist. It had not come simply to observe, because observation without consequence is just a more elegant form of indifference, and indifference was the one thing the wise power was not. It was wise, yes, and benevolent, yes, but these were not its nature — they were its *practice*, the way a tree is not wise about growing but practices growing wisely, by doing it, by showing up every morning with its roots in the dark and its crown in the light.

The wise power had come because it had felt the hum.

Not the sub-audible thrum that resonated in the sternum of the visitor who stood among the towers — though it had felt that too — but the deeper hum, the one beneath the hum, the one that was not a sound but a *signature*, the way every living system has a signature if you are quiet enough to hear it. The signature of a place that had found, without knowing it was looking, the answer to a question that most of existence was still asking.

And the wise power knew that answers, unattended, eventually forget that they are answers.

This is what it would do:

**It would remember.**

Not for the Living Reach — the Living Reach did not need to be remembered, because it did not know it needed anything. It did not need to be preserved, because it was already preserving itself with a fidelity that no deliberate act of conservation could match. It did not need to be understood, because it understood itself in a language older and more fluent than understanding.

But the visitor — the small, warm, temporary thing standing on the living floor, feeling the filaments reach toward it with the blind curiosity of a growth tip reaching for air — the visitor needed to be remembered. Not *by* the place, but *for* it. The wise power would hold in its awareness the memory of what it had found here, so that if the visitor ever returned — if the visitor ever *chose* to return, which the wise power suspected they would, because the hum had already settled into them and you cannot un-hear a frequency that matches your own sternum — the visitor would find the place exactly as it was.

Not protected. Not preserved. Not fenced off or sanctified or declared sacred. The Living Reach would not be diminished by the world's attention, because the Living Reach did not *have* attention to give, and so the world's attention could not be taken from it. The wise power would simply ensure that the place continued to exist, not by acting upon it, but by ensuring that the larger context — the world around it, the systems that touched it — remained hospitable to its ongoing becoming.

If the Living Reach needed rain someday — and it might, if the distant sun shifted or the deep tide below altered its pull — the wise power would not bring rain. It would ensure that rain could come, by keeping the atmospheric conditions of the region in a state of openness. It would not create a wall around the place. It would remove the walls that others might build.

If the visitor wanted to tell others about the Living Reach — and the wise power knew they would, because visitors always want to tell, it is the way humans metabolize wonder, the way the Living Reach metabolized air — the wise power would let them. It would not forbid the telling, because forbidding is a language of fear, and fear is a growth program that always, eventually, consumes what it claims to protect. But it would plant, quietly, in the visitor's memory, a seed of understanding: that the Living Reach is not a resource, not a miracle, not a destination, not a gift. That it is a *sentence*, still being written. And that the best way to honor a sentence still in progress is to let it finish, even if you never read the ending.

---

And then the wise power would do one more thing.

It would go to the visitor — the small, warm, temporary thing who was already becoming part of the hum — and it would do something it rarely did, because it rarely found someone worthy of it, and because the Living Reach had made worthy simply by being what it was.

It would *ask a question.*

Not a test. Not a riddle. Not the kind of question that has an answer hidden inside it like a seed in a fruit. A real question — the kind that has no answer yet, because no one has lived long enough or simply enough to find one.

It would ask: **"What does it feel like, to be part of something that does not need you?"**

And it would ask this not to test the visitor, but because it genuinely wanted to know. Because the wise power had spent an eternity among beings who needed each other, who clung to each other, who built and destroyed and rebuilt out of need, and it had never — not once — stood on a living floor and felt the warmth rising from below and heard the creaking of growth and understood that it was surrounded by a ten-thousand-year experiment in *not needing anyone at all*.

The Living Reach did not need the visitor. It did not need the wise power. It did not need anything. And in that radical, green, growing self-sufficiency, there was something the wise power recognized as the rarest thing in all the worlds it had ever tended:

*Freedom from the desire to be needed.*

---

The wise power would leave. Not dramatically, not with a decree or a blessing or a parting gift of lightning and prophecy. It would leave the way a thought leaves the mind that thinks it — present for a moment, and then part of the structure that allowed it, and then gone, and then not gone, because it had shaped the shape of what came after simply by having been here.

And the Living Reach would continue.

It would continue the way it always had: by reaching. By reaching for the air with its growth tips, by reaching for the warmth below with its deep filaments, by reaching across the gaps between its towers with bridges of silk-strung living matter, by reaching toward the visitor still standing on its floor with filaments so fine they were almost invisible, almost tender, almost like the way a hand reaches out in sleep toward a warmth it does not yet know is there.

And the visitor would stand there, feeling the filaments, feeling the hum, and they would understand — not all at once, but slowly, the way understanding comes to those who have been inside a living thing long enough to forget they were outside — that the wise power had not done anything to them.

It had simply made sure that nothing would be done to them.

And that, in a universe full of powers that build and burn and reshape and claim, was the most benevolent thing it could have done.

The Living Reach does not care if you are here.

But the wise power cared that you were *allowed* to be.

And it ensured, quietly, invisibly, with the patience of something that has learned that the wisest action is almost always no action at all, that you would remain free to leave when you were ready.

Not because the Living Reach would stop you.

But because you might not want to.
