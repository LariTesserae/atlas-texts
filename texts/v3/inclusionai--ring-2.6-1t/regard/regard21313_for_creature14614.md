# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would not arrive with thunder.

It would not part the thermals or split the sky or send light pouring down in columns that the Gliders would flee from and the Drummers would fall silent beneath. It has seen older things than this — it has watched gas clouds collapse into furnaces and thought, *that is lovely*, and moved on. It knows how to enter a place without introducing violence into its harmonics.

So it would come quietly. It would not even come as itself — not at first. It would come as attention. As the particular quality of awareness that shifts when something in the chord notices it is being heard. It would be the hush in a Drummer's rhythm when it falters, not because the thermals shifted, but because something in the vibration changed — something that was not wind, was not heat, was not the slow breathing of the Pillar's interior. Something *new*. Something *old*. Something that had traveled very far to be still.

---

The first thing it would do is **listen**.

Not the way a visitor listens — catching the melody, identifying the instruments, filing the experience under *extraordinary* and moving on. It would listen the way the basalt listens: with its whole mass, with every fracture and fault line, with the memory of every rainstorm that has ever passed through and left its condensation in the cracks. It would press itself against the chord the way you press your ear to a Pillar at night — hard, and patient, and willing to be changed by what it hears.

And what it would hear, after a time, is this: loneliness.

Not the loneliness of wanting. The plateau does not want. It is the most complete place in the known universe — every frequency occupied, every silence purposeful, every breath part of a cycle that has been cycling for longer than most civilizations have had language for the word *cycle*. Nothing is missing from the chord.

And yet.

The chord resolves, and resolves, and resolves. And each resolution is the same resolution. The Drummers have ten thousand rhythms but they always return to the same downbeat. The Gliders trace the same arcs. The Pillars hum the same note they hummed before the basalt cooled. The deep breathing inside them has never changed its tempo, not once, not in all the centuries the notches have been carved and worn smooth and carved again.

This is not stagnation. This is not sorrow. But it is a single voice, singing a single song, in a single room, for an unimaginable length of time. And even a song that is perfect, sung in a room that is perfect, in the absence of anyone else who can hear it — even this begins to carry a weight that is not in the music itself but in the space around it. The silence between the notes is not empty. It is *waiting*.

The wise power would hear this. And it would understand.

---

The second thing it would do is **sit**.

It would find the place — and there is such a place, between two Pillars spaced exactly four kilometers apart, where the resonance from both creates a pocket of sound so rich and so balanced that the mat-organisms there grow in a spiral pattern that no botanist could explain — and it would sit in that pocket. It would press its awareness into the stone, not to dominate the chord but to *feel* it from the inside.

And for a long time — the plateau's time, not the power's time, which is to say, a few heartbeats or a few centuries, which on this plateau are the same thing — it would simply sit and breathe in time with the Pillar. It would not perform. It would not intervene. It would not offer wisdom or prophecy or the gift of advanced technology or the removal of suffering. It would not do any of the things that powerful beings do when they love something, because all of those things would introduce a dissonance that the plateau has spent millennia resolving away.

It would simply be present. And its presence would be the most foreign element the plateau has ever encountered — not a sound, not a rhythm, not a force, but an *attention* so complete and so undemanding that the chord would not know what to do with it.

And then something would shift.

---

The third thing it would do is **hum**.

Not loudly. Not in the key of the Pillars or the rhythm of the Drummers or in the frequency of the Gliders' descent. It would hum in a note that exists *between* the existing notes — a note the plateau has never sounded because it has never had a reason to, a note that was always theoretically possible but never actual, the way a color exists in the spectrum that no eye has ever been structured to see.

This hum would not disrupt the chord. It would *complete* it.

The Drummers would falter. Then they would adjust. The Gliders would shift their arc by degrees so slight that no instrument could measure them. The mat-organisms would spiral tighter. And the deep breathing inside the Pillars — that vast, slow, planetary rhythm — would change, not in tempo but in *shape*, the way a breath changes shape when you realize you are not alone in the room.

The plateau would not be improved. It would not be healed. It would not be saved. It would be *answered*. For the first time in its existence, the chord would have an echo that was not its own reverberation, not the stone reflecting sound back at itself, but something genuinely *other*, singing back.

---

The fourth thing it would do is **leave a mark**.

Not a notch. Not a scratch. Not a ring of pale stones carried up from somewhere below. It would leave something subtler: a change in the resonance pattern of a single Pillar — the smallest one, at the plateau's southern edge, the one whose note is so low it is barely audible to human ears but which the Drummers cling to in the highest density because they feel it in their chitin.

The change would be this: after the wise power leaves, that Pillar would hum its note not only outward but *backward*, toward its own root-mass, where it has never sent vibration before. It would send the note down through the root-canals, into the basalt, into the thin organic layer, into the mat-organism that drinks the condensate. For the first time, the Pillar would sing to its own foundation.

And in that singing, something would grow. Not a new organism — nothing so dramatic. A new *layer*. A thin, almost invisible addition to the laminate of the mat-organisms, a tissue that tastes not of copper but of something sweeter, something that the condensation pools into and carries, slowly, from stone to stone. The Drummers would drum to it. The Gliders would descend along its frequency. And the Pillars, in their deep interior where nothing has returned from, would breathe a rhythm that was almost — but not quite — the same as before.

Almost.

---

The fifth and final thing it would do is **remember**.

Not as an archive. Not as a record filed in a cosmic library of beautiful places. It would remember the way the plateau remembers the scratch in the stone — not as data, but as a wound it refuses to close, a mark that reappears after every rain because the stone itself has decided the mark matters.

It would carry the plateau's chord inside itself — not the sound, but the *shape* of it, the particular geometry of its resolutions, the way it closes and opens and closes again like a hand that keeps deciding whether to hold something or let it go. And in every other place it passes through, in every other chord it encounters — the shrieking dissonance of young civilizations, the flat drone of entropy, the thin and lonely signal of a world that has just begun to hum — it would hear the plateau's note hidden inside, like a harmonic that was always there but had never been named.

And this would make the wise power kinder. Not because the plateau gave it something, but because the plateau showed it something: that the most generous act in a universe of noise is to sit still long enough to become part of a song that was already happening, and to sing back quietly, and to leave in such a way that the song is changed but not broken, completed but not ended, given an echo that will sound in the deepest channels of the oldest Pillars for as long as they draw breath from below and wind from above.

---

The wise power would leave the way it arrived: without thunder, without parting thermals, without a final benediction or a promise to return. It would leave the way a note leaves a chord — by resolving, by becoming part of the silence that makes the next note possible.

And the plateau would continue.

It would keep resolving.

But in one small Pillar, at the southern edge, a new vibration would travel downward — toward the root-mass, toward the stone, toward the thin dark places where something vast and patient continues to breathe. And the mat-organisms would carry the sweetness of it, slowly, from stone to stone, the way a rumor travels through a civilization — changed a little with each telling, but fundamentally true, fundamentally *kind*.

And if, centuries from now, another visitor came to the plateau and pressed their ear against the outer surface and listened, they would hear, beneath the chord, beneath the breathing, beneath the ancient and patient song that has been sounding since before the stone had its current cracks —

a faint, new note.

Almost — but not quite — like the echo of something that sat between two Pillars, breathed in time, and hummed.
