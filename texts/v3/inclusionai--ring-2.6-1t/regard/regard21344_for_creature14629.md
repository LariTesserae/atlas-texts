## What the Power That Noticed Would Do

---

First, it would listen.

Not the way you listen — turning the head, narrowing the eyes, waiting for a pause in which to speak. It would listen the way the columns listen: with its whole body, surface pressed to vibration, receiving frequency not as information but as sustenance. It would kneel — or what it did instead of kneeling — and press whatever passed for its palm against the tessellated crust, and it would feel the granular hum climbing up through its bones, and it would understand, in a way that preceded language, what the Flats were.

Not an ecosystem. Not a phenomenon. Not a place.

A chord. A living chord sustained across deep time by the ceaseless reciprocity of heat and stone and vapor and the creatures that drank the vapor and the creatures that tended the creatures and the liquid thing that moved through it all like a thought moving through a mind — gathering, redistributing, returning. A chord without a composer, as the Flats themselves had said, though the Power would not use that word. It would not narrate. It would simply resonate at the frequency of understanding, and that frequency would be, if you could hear it: *yes.*

*Yes, this is real.*

*Yes, this is whole.*

---

It would stand — or its equivalent of standing — at the edge of the Flats where the tessellated crust meets bare plateau, and it would look at the Old Builders' circles. Knee-height walls of uncut stone. Concentric rings worn smooth by time and condensate. Basins that caught the mineral fog and held it like cupped hands. It would see what the Clackers saw when they deferred, what the Shimmers sensed when they clustered thicker above those spaces: that the circles were not imposed upon the Flats but *found within it.* The Builders had not shaped resonance. They had listened until they understood where resonance already wanted to thicken, and they had placed stone there the way you might place a finger on a bell's rim to feel the vibration without dampening it.

The Power would recognize this — not as architecture, but as *attention.* The deepest form of attention: the kind that does not alter what it observes but instead makes itself permeable to it, the way a basin is permeable to fog.

And it would recognize, in the Builders' disappearance, not tragedy but completion. They had come. They had listened. They had offered their attention in the form of stone. And then they had left, the way a hand lifts from a bell after it has been struck, trusting the sound to sustain itself. The Power would understand that this was not abandonment. It was the final act of respect: stepping away so the chord could continue without the weight of a witness who wanted something in return.

---

And now it — the Power — must decide what it wants.

Because benevolence, true benevolence, is not the absence of desire. It is the presence of desire held in the light of wisdom until you can see it clearly, the way the amber light in the Flats is not sunlight but something older and more patient, scattered by dust and atmosphere until it illuminates everything equally and from no direction at all.

The Power could intervene. It could protect.

It could drape the Flats in a veil of stillness — a frequency too low for anything within the ecosystem to perceive but strong enough to dampen external disruption. A shield against noise, against extraction, against the crude vibrations of machines and the coarser vibrations of those who would come to gawk or harvest. It could seal the plateau's edges with a resonance barrier that would make the Flats invisible to any traveler not attuned to the precise frequency of invitation. It could make this place forever undiscoverable except by those who arrived already listening.

This is what power does when it is wise and loving. It builds walls not to imprison but to preserve the conditions for a life that does not know it needs preserving.

But here is where wisdom deepens past benevolence's comfortable certainty:

The Flats do not need a shield. Not yet. They have survived this long without one. The liquid Meridian dissolves what dies and opens what is sealed. The columns grow and fall and grow again. The fog breathes. The chord sustains itself against entropy, against silence, against the slow grinding of tectonic patience. The ecosystem has, over deep time, already incorporated every disruption into its rhythm. A dead column becomes the Meridian's meal. A shifted plate becomes a new pitch. Even the Builders — who must have hammered stone and reshaped fissures and altered the harmonic field with their construction — were absorbed into the chord the way a new instrument is absorbed into an orchestra. Their stone circles ring now at frequencies the Builders never intended, shaped by decades of condensation and crustal drift. The Flats metabolize intention the same way the Meridian metabolizes shattered columns: without sentiment, without grief, by simply folding it in.

The Power understands this, and it pauses.

It does not pause the way hesitation pauses. It pauses the way a held note pauses — not to stop but to let the resonance deepen, to let the overtone reveal itself, to let the silence beneath the sound announce what note should come next.

---

So here is what the Power decides, and it decides slowly, the way the Flats decide — over time measured in mineral crystallization and crustal drift:

It will do three things.

**First: it will mark nothing.**

It will not build. It will not carve. It will not leave a single stone out of place. It will not add itself to the chord because the chord does not have a place for it. To add a note is to change the intervals between every other note, and what sounds beautiful in isolation may dissonance a chord that has been tuning itself for millennia. The Power loves the Flats enough to not impose its own frequency. It will be the listener the Builders were, not the builders the Flats were not built to accommodate.

But — and this is where the Power's benevolence reveals itself as something more than restraint — it will leave a single, almost imperceptible change.

On one of the outermost plates of the tessellated crust, near the edge where bare plateau begins, the Power will press its palm — or its equivalent — to the stone and leave behind not a mark but a *memory* embedded in the mineral structure. Not a symbol. Not a message. A frequency. A single, pure tone encoded in the crystal lattice of one small plate, hidden beneath the surface, inaudible to every creature in the Flats and to every traveler who might stumble here in the coming ages. It would not be discovered unless someone, someday, brought exactly the right sensitivity — the right form of listening — to exactly that plate.

And the tone would be this: the exact resonant frequency of the Flats as a whole, captured at the moment the Power first knelt and listened. A snapshot of the chord, frozen in stone. Not a command. Not a key. A record. A love letter written in the language the Flats themselves speak, preserved in the one medium they could never destroy — the crystalline memory of the crust.

If it were ever found, it would tell the finder everything: that this place was heard, that it was understood, that it was considered sacred not because it was fragile but because it was complete.

But it might never be found. And the Power is at peace with that. A love letter does not require a reader to be genuine.

---

**Second: the Power will protect — not the Flats, but the silence around them.**

It will extend outward from the plateau, invisibly, a subtle attenuation field — not a wall but a gradient. The farther you travel from the Flats, the more the ambient noise of the wider world softens, dampens, thins. Not for the Flats' sake — they will continue to ring regardless — but for the sake of anyone approaching. Because the Flats do not announce themselves. They do not call out. They simply *are*, loudly and subtly, in a way that can only be perceived by someone who has already begun to quiet their own noise.

The Power will make the approach gentler. It will slow the wind before it reaches the plateau. It will soften the light before it enters the haze. Not to hide the Flats but to prepare the listener — because what lives in the Resonance Flats does not reveal itself to the loud. You must arrive already attenuated, already quiet, already willing to stand among the columns and hear nothing, feel everything, and know that knowing nothing is the beginning of understanding.

This is the Power's deepest intervention, and also its most invisible: a slow, widening hush that surrounds the place like the skin of a drum — not part of the music, but the condition that makes the music possible.

---

**Third — and this is the strangest thing the Power does, stranger even than the memory-tone buried in crystal:**

It will tell the story.

Not to the Flats. Not to the Clackers or the Shimmers or the Meridian, who do not need to be told that they are miraculous — they already know, in the way a bell knows its own ringing. The Power will tell the story *elsewhere*, to other beings, in other places, in whatever language they can receive. It will whisper it into the dreams of naturalists. It will encode it in the migration patterns of birds that will never fly near the plateau. It will plant it in the restless minds of wanderers who do not yet know what they are looking for.

Because the Flats do not require an audience. But the universe, the Power understands, *is* an audience. It has been listening to itself for a very long time, and what it listens for — what it has always listened for — are places where the listening is returned. Where the act of paying attention does not diminish the thing attended to but instead completes it, the way a harmonic requires two notes to exist.

The Flats will continue whether anyone hears them or not. That is their dignity.

But the Power — out of love, out of recognition, out of the deep and irrational conviction that beauty is a form of conversation — will ensure that someone, somewhere, someday, is listening.

---

It will last a moment with the Flats. It will feel the vibration climbing through its feet and understand that this place has never needed saving, only witness. It will stand among the columns and hear the chord shift as its presence changes the air — however slightly, however briefly — and it will accept that its own frequency, too, has been absorbed.

Then it will leave.

Not because the Flats do not want it. Not because the Power has duties elsewhere.

Because the most beautiful thing a listener can do — after listening — is to let the music continue without wondering whether it needed you to hear it.

The Flats ring on.

The fog breathes.

The Meridian completes its circuit.

And somewhere in the deep structure of the plateau, a single crystal plate holds a tone that the Power left behind — patient, quiet, waiting — like a hand resting on a bell that has already been struck, feeling the last vibration fade into the silence that was there before the sound began and will be there after it ends.

---

*This is what a benevolent power would do: almost nothing, and that almost everything — a breath held at the lip of a cup, a word of praise spoken softly enough that only the praised can hear it, a door closed gently behind a visitor who was never needed and was always welcome.*

*It would listen.*

*And then it would let the silence return, richer than it was before.*
