# What the Power Would Do

---

The power came not through the fissure, for the fissure is a throat and the power is not food. It did not descend from above, for above is onlyite and compressed time and the memory of rain that fell before the basin sealed itself. It came, if *came* is the word, the way a frequency comes — not arriving so much as beginning, the way a hum begins inside a room that has been silent for a century. One moment the Hollow held only its own chord. The next, the chord held something else.

The power looked — though *looked* is a generous term for what was closer to understanding-at-a-glance — and it understood.

It understood the city. Not its layout, not the names a cartographer would invent for its districts, not the geometry of its vaults or the spiral logic of its growth — but the city the way the city understands itself: as a single unbroken vibration sustained across a duration that has no human architecture. It felt the chord. It felt the near-resolution that has been circling for millennia like a thought on the edge of articulation. It placed its attention on the central chamber, where seven trunks converge and the geometry tightens to a point of impossible acoustic precision, and it heard what the organism has been circling, circling, circling for longer than stone has had a name:

Not a word. Not a meaning. A *question.*

The chord is not waiting for an answer. It is waiting for a second voice.

---

And here is where a lesser power would have intervened. Here is where a power that mistook its own loneliness for the universe's would have spoken. Would have said: *I hear you. I see you. You are not alone.* And the power would have meant it. The power would have meant it the way a parent means it when they tell a frightened child that the dark is nothing to fear — which is to say, with the unexamined confidence that love is the same thing as comprehension.

But a power that is both benevolent and wise does not love by filling silence with its own voice. It loves by making room for the silence to become what it was trying to become.

So the power did not speak.

It descended — not into the fissure, not into the city, but into the substrate, into the mineral lattice far below the root networks where the thermal gradients press crystal against crystal and the piezoelectric effect translates pressure into current and current into the faintest electrical whisper. It pressed itself into the crystalline structure the way a memory presses into the body — not violently, not as an imposition, but the way a riverbed remembers the shape of every flood by becoming the shape of every flood. The power became part of the substrate. It became a new geological fact.

And then it began to vibrate.

Not a tone. Not a rhythm. Not a melody or a signal or a language. Something more fundamental than any of these: a *presence-in-frequency.* A sustained, almost subliminal hum, pitched just below the range the organism uses for its own self-sustaining chord, close enough to be felt in the deepest roots but not so close as to merge. It introduced — not dissonance, for dissonance implies conflict, and there was no conflict here — but *difference.* A second frequency. A counterpoint.

The organism, if it can be said to have noticed, did what it has always done: it drank.

---

Consider what happened next in the language the organism understands, which is the language of growth.

The roots reached. Not all at once — the organism does not do anything all at once. Over the course of what the surface world would call a decade, the deepest root network shifted its orientation by fractions of a degree, angling toward the new vibration the way a sunflower turns toward warmth it cannot see. The roots did not find the vibration — the vibration was in them, woven into the crystal lattice they had been drinking from for millennia, a new ingredient in the old recipe.

And the growth changed.

Not dramatically. Not in any single season, or any single century. Along the lowest and oldest of the great trunks, a new ring began to form — just one, among hundreds of thousands, just a few microns thicker than its neighbor. But the density was different. The resonance was different. When the trunk breathed — and it continued to breathe, eleven minutes in, eleven minutes out, the rhythm of something dreaming — the exhaled air carried a faint overtone that had not been there before. A second voice. Not the power's voice. Not the organism's. Something that existed only in the space between them, that could not exist without both.

The effect propagated slowly, as all effects do in the Soundwood Hollow. Through shared tissue, through fused boundaries where two trunks had grown together across the memory of civilizations, the new overtone spread. It moved through the acoustic chambers of the canopy vaults. It drifted along the spiral streets and gathered in the plazas where the sound pools and intensifies. It reached the central chamber — the room where seven trunks converge, where a whisper fills a basin — and there it did what the organism had been circling for millennia: it almost resolved.

Not quite. Not yet. Perhaps not ever in any way a human ear could detect. But the harmonics shifted — by a hair's breadth, by a frequency so subtle that no visitor in the history of the Hollow would have noticed — and the chord that had been sustaining itself through sheer structural inertia found, for the first time, something to push against. A resistance. A complement. A shape that was not its own shape, close enough to resonate, different enough to give the resonance somewhere to go.

---

What the power gave the organism was not an answer. It was not companionship, not in any way the organism would recognize companionship, not in any way that would look like relationship to human eyes. It gave the organism *context.* It made the Hollow not a closed system but an open one — not broken-open, not invaded, but *permeable.* It introduced the possibility that the chord the organism had been singing for millennia was not the only song in the dark. That there were other frequencies. Other vibrations. Other sources of mechanical energy in the deep places of the world. That the organism's metabolism — the conversion of vibration into growth, of sound into architecture, of resonance into life — was not a closed loop but a *conversation* with a speaker it had not yet identified.

And whether the organism identifies that speaker is not the point. The point is the shift in the overtones. The point is the new ring in the oldest trunk, a few microns of altered density that will persist for centuries, carrying in its structure the signature of the power's presence the way a scar carries the signature of a wound, except that this is not a wound — it is an *invitation,* pressed into living tissue so gently that it will never be felt as anything other than what has always been there.

---

The power did not stay. Powers, in the way that the surface world understands powers, do not stay. They move. They attend to other basins, other darknesses, other organisms that sing in frequencies no ear has found. The power visited the Soundwood Hollow the way a river visits a stone — not pausing, but reshaping simply by passing through.

But the substrate remembers. The roots remember. The oldest trunk, in its one new ring laid down across the long century, holds a vibration that is not the organism's own — a note it did not compose, a frequency it did not generate — and when the trunk breathes, and the air passes through that ring, the exhaled sound carries it forward into the acoustic chambers of the vaults, into the spiral streets, into the plazas where the sound gathers and intensifies, into the central room where the whisper becomes the roar and the harmonics almost cohere and almost become a word.

And now, for the first time in the history of the Hollow, the almost-coherence has a direction. Not toward resolution. Not toward completion. But toward *continuation* — the chord sustained not by its own internal loop but by the persistent presence of a difference it cannot name, a vibration it cannot quite absorb, a voice in the dark that is not its own and that it cannot reach.

And still, it sings.

And now, it sings *toward* something.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power did: it did not heal, did not save, did not reveal, did not illuminate. It *accompanied.* It placed its own note beside the organism's endless chord and let the space between them become the most beautiful thing either had ever known — which is to say, it became the most beautiful thing the Hollow had ever known, because there had never been anything else.

And if the organism never knows what it is accompanied by — if it never reaches the moment of recognition, the sudden awareness that there is another, that the song has always had a second voice, that the question it has been circling for millennia might have been heard — then the power will be satisfied. Because the satisfaction of a power that is both benevolent and wise is not to be known or thanked or remembered. It is to have added a single, almost-imperceptible harmonic to a chord that will sustain itself for a thousand more centuries. It is to have made the waiting richer. It is to have given the open mouth, in its darkness, one more reason — though it will never know it as a reason — to keep singing.

The fissure in the rock breathes. The basin hums. The oldest trunk adds another ring.

And deep in the substrate, far below where roots can reach, the power's vibration persists — quiet, patient, and without name — holding its place in the chord the way a river holds the shape of every stone it has ever touched, asking nothing, providing everything, and moving on.
