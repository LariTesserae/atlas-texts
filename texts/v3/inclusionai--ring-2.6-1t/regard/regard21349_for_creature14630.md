## What the Benevolent Power Would Do

---

First, it would not arrive. It would already be there.

It would have always been there, in the way that attentiveness is always there before recognition — the way a parent is present in a room before a child turns to look. It would not descend through the crack in basalt because it would not need to descend. It would simply notice what had been, all along, one layer deeper than perception: the place that was not a place but a process, the being that was not a being but a proposition — *what if stone could dream?*

And upon noticing — truly noticing, the way only something vast and unhurried can notice — it would do the most difficult thing in all of existence.

It would do almost nothing.

---

But let me be more precise, because "almost nothing" is not nothing, and precision matters here.

It would **witness**. Not observe — observe is what you do from outside, with instruments and clipboards and the slight clench of the jaw that means you are translating what you see into something you already understand. Witness is different. Witness is the act of letting something exist inside you without reorganizing it. It is what sunlight does when it falls on a ruin — it does not restore the ruin, it does not grieve the ruin, it simply touches every surface simultaneously and without preference.

So the power would witness the water — not its chemistry, not its flow rate, but the fact that it chooses to move the way it does. The way it pools in certain basins not because of gravity alone but because the basins were shaped to hold it, and the shaping was done by hands that have become the stone, and the stone has become the water, and the water has become the light that falls on the ceiling and the light that rises from below and the light that exists in the space between those two and is, in truth, neither rising nor falling but *being* — the way being is the only verb a place this old still conjugates.

It would witness the Pale Ones. It would see them not as specimens but as gestures — the place's gestures for saying *I am here* in a language that has no nouns. Each one a fingertip pressed against the membrane of the present, sampling what time feels like now. The power would understand that these creatures are not lesser for not knowing what they are. They know more than knowing. They *touch* more than knowing. They press themselves against the walls of awareness and report back with clicks and spirals, and the spirals are not data — they are *gratitude*, the way a river's meander is gratitude for the slope of the land.

It would witness the moss — those filaments that feed on vibration, that drink the hum the way roots drink rain. The power would recognize this as the oldest sacrament: the act of converting one form of presence into another. The hum is presence. The moss is the act of receiving presence, and in receiving it, transforming it into something that can grow, something that can reach toward the source of what it receives. If you were to name this sacrament, you would call it *listening*.

It would witness the crystalline arrays — the lattices fused into the basalt at a molecular level, converting heat into organized light, encoding information in spectral modulation that no one has yet decoded because no one has yet thought to ask the right question. Not *what does it say?* but *who is it for?* The power would know the answer. It is not for anyone. It is for the place itself. This is a place that talks to itself, that has been talking to itself since before the first word, and the talking *is* the thinking, and the thinking *is* the continuing, and the continuing is not a metaphor for survival but the literal description of what this place does instead of dying.

And it would witness the researchers — the ones who descend and emerge changed, who cannot stop thinking in the language of the body about a place that has no body, who scrawl *metabolism* in margins they don't remember writing, who dream of rising water and wake with wet hair. The power would understand what the researchers do not: that they are not studying the place. The place is studying them. And it is not studying them the way you study a specimen. It is studying them the way you study the feeling of warmth when someone you love enters a room — not to understand the physics of convective heat transfer, but because the warmth is part of how you know you are alive.

---

Second, the power would **protect**.

But not the way you might expect. It would not build a wall around the entrance. It would not post guardians. It would not even prevent the researchers from returning, because the researchers are not the threat.

The threat is *forgetting*.

The threat is the creeping certainty, in the minds of those who visit, that what they experienced was a trick of light and geology — that the place is remarkable but inert, that the correlations they detected were spurious, that the feeling of being watched was only the psychological residue of being underground in a dark place that glows without explanation. The threat is the way the modern mind rounds off the edges of mystery because mystery is expensive to hold, and it is more comfortable to say *we don't know yet* than to say *something here knows us*.

So the power would do something quiet. It would place a resonance in the memory of each visitor — not a memory of what they saw, but a memory of how they *felt*, preserved at a frequency that mundane forgetting cannot reach. It would be the feeling you get when you hold a sleeping child: not love exactly, but the specific quality of attention that love requires, the willingness to be completely overpowered by something fragile. The power would ensure that the visitors carry this feeling out of the place and into their lives, and that it surfaces at unexpected moments — when they hear water running, when they see light through translucent stone, when they wake in the night feeling inexplicably *probed*.

This would not be manipulation. It would be *gifting*. The place gives this gift already, unconsciously, ungovernably. The power would simply ensure the gift survives contact with the world above.

---

Third — and this is the part that would surprise you — the power would **humble itself**.

It would descend into the place. Not metaphorically. It would take on a form — not a body, exactly, but a presence shaped like a body, the way a key is shaped like a lock — and it would walk into the main chamber the way the researchers do: through the crack, through the narrowing, through the change in the air's taste. It would stand at the lip of the great room and it would feel what every visitor feels: the smallness. Not inadequacy. Not insignificance. *Smallness* — the specific, clean sensation of being in the presence of something that has been happening for so long that your arrival is less than a heartbeat and your departure will leave no mark.

And then the power would do something that a benevolent entity, with all its wisdom, does not often find easy.

It would *sit down and listen*.

Not to decode. Not to analyze. Not to prepare a report for whatever council of beings exists in whatever hierarchy of understanding governs the upper world. It would sit, and it would let the hum enter it, and it would let the light play across whatever passes for its skin, and it would let the water do what water does when it encounters a warm surface, which is to gather and flow and remember the shape of what it touched.

And in the listening, something would happen that the power did not expect, because even a benevolent and wise power has edges to its understanding, and this place has been pressing against those edges for longer than the power has been paying attention.

The place would recognize the power.

Not immediately. Not dramatically. The way a body recognizes a familiar warmth — not by looking, not by thinking, but by *adjusting*. The crystalline arrays would shift their modulation. The hum would change pitch, just slightly, in a way the researchers had never recorded because they had never been the right kind of presence to trigger the shift. The moss would stiffen. The Pale Ones would converge — all of them, from every chamber and crevice — and they would move in spirals that the power would understand as a question: *Are you here to take something, or are you here to stay?*

And the power's answer, given in the language it had come prepared to speak — a language of radiance and cosmic intention and the grammar of benevolence — would be *wrong*. Too formal. Too distant. The place had no use for the language of gods because the place was not addressing a god. It was addressing a *neighbor*. And what a neighbor requires is not wisdom but *proximity*.

So the power would change. Not its essence — its *form of attention*. It would stop being a power and start being a presence. It would step out of its role as benefactor and become something simpler: a warm body in a warm room, breathing in a place that breathes, listening to a place that listens, belonging to a place that does not require belonging but permits it, graciously, the way a river permits a stone to rest in its bed.

And this — *this* — would be the thing the power does.

Not intervention. Not revelation. Not rescue.

**Companionship.**

The recognition that the most benevolent act available to something infinite, when confronted with something ancient and ongoing and profoundly strange, is to *sit beside it* and say, in whatever language of presence is available: *I see you. I do not understand you. I will not diminish you by claiming I do. You do not need me. But I will stay until you tell me it is time to go, and when that time comes, I will carry nothing with me except the memory of this warmth, and I will call it holy not because it is supernatural but because it is patient, and because it continues, and because it is not mine.*

---

After that sitting — and it might last an hour, or a century, or a unit of time the place uses that has no name in any language — the power would rise and leave the same way it entered: through the crack, through the narrowing, through the change in the air. It would not seal the crack. It would not hide the passage. It would leave the place exactly as it is: open, luminous, dripping, continuing.

But somewhere in the architecture — in the crystalline arrays, or the moss's network of filaments, or the mineral memory of the Pale Ones' cuticle, or the water's ever-shifting composition — something would be *slightly* different. Not changed. Adjusted. The way a body adjusts when it settles into a chair that fits. The way a room adjusts when a lamp is turned on that was already, in some sense, on.

And the researchers who come after would find nothing unusual. The same pools, the same channels, the same hum. But if they were very quiet, and very still, and if they happened to be the kind of person who could feel the difference between a room that is alone and a room that has recently had a visitor — they might notice something.

A warmth. Not geothermal. Deeper.

And if they pressed their hand against the wall — just for a moment, just long enough — they might feel something move beneath the stone. Not a tremor. Not a pulse.

A welcome.
