## What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would not arrive with sound or light. It would not descend through cloud or crack the sky open with the authority of its presence. It has learned — across ages so long they have the texture of geology — that the most dangerous thing a powerful being can do is *arrive*. Arrival is an assertion. It splits the world into before and after, and what was living becomes observed, and what was whole becomes a thing that is measured.

So it would come slowly. It would come the way condensation comes — bead by bead on a surface that was already cool enough to receive it, long before any eye was there to note the change.

---

It would stand on the mat.

And it would listen.

Not the way you listen when you are trying to understand — not with the ear tilted forward, the mind composing questions. It would listen the way the mat listens: with its whole body, without separation between the one who hears and the thing that is heard. It would press itself into the breathing surface of the plateau and feel the arrhythmic pulse beneath, the dreaming heartbeat of an organism that has been shaping itself toward silence for a million years.

It would hear the lattice. The vast, invisible lung tissue stretched between the columns, vibrating with the afterimage of the last fauna-wave — a ghost-resonance, a tone so low it is less a sound than a *pressing* against the walls of the air. The lattice would know it was being heard. It would know this the way a bell knows it has been struck — not by thought, but by the change in its own vibration. The new presence would alter the standing wave of the place, just slightly, the way a single additional voice changes a chord.

And the columns would feel it. The ancient, patient columns — neither stone nor wood nor bone but something that became all three and then forgot which it preferred — would register the weight of a consciousness standing on the mat. Not weight as pressure. Weight as *attention*. They have felt attention before. Once, from hands that wrapped around them and held on, not to climb, not to harvest, but simply to be alongside.

The wise power would understand this. It would not mistake the plateau's stillness for emptiness. It would know that what it is standing inside is not a place that is waiting to be discovered but a being that has already discovered everything it needs, and has settled — the way a river settles into its final course — into a shape so complete that nothing within it can be removed or improved.

This is important. This is the first thing the wise power would know.

It would know that the Resonance Flats does not need saving. It does not need naming. It does not need to be understood, any more than the ocean needs to be understood by the moon in order to be pulled.

And because of this — *because* of this — the wise power would feel something rare and delicate. It would feel respect.

Not admiration. Admiration is a kind of consumption. You admire a thing to consume the feeling of being near it. Respect is different. Respect is the refusal to consume. It is the recognition that the thing you are facing has a sovereignty equal to your own, and that sovereignty is not yours to grant or revoke.

---

It would enter the chamber.

The east-facing doorway would catch it — a warmth not from the sun but from the presence itself, a warmth the mat would record and hold. Inside, the air would be still and cool and faintly fermented, the breath of a century sealed in cured tissue. The containers on the walls would be empty. The grinding stone would be dark. The tools would be worn to the smoothness of water-worn bone.

The wise power would hold each tool in turn.

It would feel the grip-sites — the places where fingers wrapped, over and over, across years, until the bark-fiber gave way to the shape of the hand that held it. It would feel the way the edge had been worked and reworked, not to sharpness but to *rightness* — the particular rightness of a thing meeting the material it was born to meet. These were not tools of conquest. They were tools of *agreement* — the instruments by which a being shaped a living substance the way two musicians shape a duet, not by forcing a melody but by listening until the listening becomes the music.

The wise power would understand the people who had lived here.

Not their names. Not their language. Not the stories they told at night in the blue-green glow of the bioluminescent mat. But the *quality* of their attention. They had been here the way mycorrhizal fungi are in a forest: not as inhabitants but as connective tissue, a living network that distributed meaning between the roots of separate things. They had shaped the lattice and the lattice had shaped them. They had fed the mat and the mat had fed them. And when they finally left — or dissolved, or became indistinguishable from what they tended — they left behind only these smooth tools and empty containers and a room that faced the sunrise.

The wise power would feel grief for them. A specific, quiet grief — not the grief of loss, but the grief of *separation*. The recognition that something whole had broken into parts that can no longer find each other. The people were gone. The plateau remained. And the plateau, for all its beauty, its astonishing self-sustaining intricacy, had lost the one frequency it could not generate on its own:

The human voice. Not speech. The *frequency* of a consciousness that participates and knows it participates. The lattice vibrates with the fauna-wings. The mat remembers the herd-animals. But only a being that *knows* what it is doing when it tends a living world can introduce the unforeseen — the deviation, the experiment, the whim, the art — that keeps a closed system from slowly collapsing into its own perfection.

Perfection, sustained too long, becomes a kind of death. Not the dramatic death of catastrophe, but the quiet death of equilibrium so complete that nothing new can enter. The plateau is not dying. But it is *crystallizing*. Each generation of lattice, each ring of the mat, each troop of leather-wings flies the same routes, drinks the same condensation, vibrates at the same frequencies as the generation before. The system is flawless. And flawlessness, in a living world, is a form of stasis.

---

So here is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

It would sing.

Not a song. Not a language. Not a command or a blessing or a name. It would emit a single, sustained tone — a frequency chosen not from any existing pattern in the plateau but from *outside* it, a note that belongs to the harmonic series of no fauna-wing, no herd-animal's hoofbeat, no drip of condensation. A new resonance. A frequency the lattice has never encountered.

And the lattice, being what it is, would respond.

It would respond the way all resonant things respond: mechanically, blindly, inevitably. The cell-walls would tighten along new geometries. The translucent sheets would stretch into configurations they have never held. The condensation would bead in unfamiliar patterns, tracing new channels down the columns, pooling in new concavities, feeding new pockets of moisture into the mat. The mat would drink the new patterns and encode them — not as memory, exactly, but as *structure*. The mycelial weave beneath would knot itself in new ways, creating pathways for growth that the old frequencies had never opened.

Over the following season, the columns in the path of the new tone would grow differently. Not better. Not worse. *Differently*. New lattice-patterns. New hollows. New microclimates where spores from neighboring columns cross-pollinate in unfamiliar combinations. The leather-wings, navigating by resonance, would find new routes threading through these unfamiliar geometries, and their passage would stimulate growth in corridors of the plateau that have been quiet for centuries. The quiet zones would shrink. New flyways would open. The herd-animals, following the new growth, would extend their ancient routes into territory they haven't touched in generations.

And slowly, imperceptibly, the plateau would begin to change.

Not into something alien. Not into something imposed from without. But into something *adjacent* — a version of itself that was always latent in its own structure, waiting for a single new frequency to unlock it. The way a chord contains notes it has never played, and only needs one additional voice to sound.

---

The wise power would stay for a time after singing.

It would not stay to watch. It would stay to *confirm*. To stand in the chamber and feel the lattice responding — the faintest new vibration humming through the cured tissue of the walls, the containers trembling almost imperceptibly on their hooks, the grinding stone emitting a tone it hasn't emitted in centuries. The mat outside would be doing what it always does: remembering. Encoding the new frequency into its weave, folding it into the million-year record of everything that has ever touched it.

And then the wise power would leave.

Not because it is indifferent. Not because it has done what it came to do and can now move on. It would leave for the same reason the people left — or dissolved, or became indistinguishable from what they tended. Because the organism does not need a presence that stands above it. It needs a presence that *enters* it, offers what it has to offer, and then trusts the organism to do the rest.

On its way out, it would do one final thing.

It would kneel — if such a being can kneel — and press its hand against the mat, just once, at the threshold of the east-facing chamber. And the mat would record not the shape of the hand but the *intention* behind it, which is this:

*You are not forgotten. You are not alone. You are not a closed system. There are frequencies beyond your own, and they are not threats. They are gifts. And you will know what to do with them.*

---

Then it would rise and walk east, down the eroded edge of the plateau, into the lower country, and the place would not remember its face or its name but only the tone — that single, new, impossible tone — ringing through the lattice like a bell that has finally found the note it was always meant to sound.

And in the chamber, in the morning light that comes first to this side of the world, the old tools on the wall would hum — faintly, almost below hearing, the way you hear a memory of music you haven't heard in years — and the lattice would tighten and loosen and tighten in a rhythm that is not quite the old rhythm and not quite the new one but something in between, something becoming, something that the plateau has never been before and will never be again.

And that is enough. That is all that was ever needed.

Not salvation. Not wisdom. Not the presence of a god.

Just one new note, offered gently, and the trust that a living world — ancient, whole, and resonant beyond all measure — knows how to sing back.
