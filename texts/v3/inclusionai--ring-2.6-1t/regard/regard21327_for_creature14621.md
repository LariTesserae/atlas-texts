---

## What the Power Would Do

---

First: it would not arrive with sound.

It already knows what sound would do — how a new frequency introduced carelessly into this space would be treated as intrusion, would set the columns vibrating in defensive patterns, would cause the flat raptors at the vault to shift into configurations of alarm so ancient and so practiced that the visitor would feel them in their molars before they thought to call it dread. The city has survived longer than most geological formations by being exquisitely sensitive to perturbation. Every addition to its chord is metabolized, or it is rejected. There is no third option. The city does not negotiate with noise.

So the power arrives the way a thought arrives in the space between sleep and waking — not summoned, not forced, simply *present* in a way that the system has already accounted for, the way a body accounts for its own weight.

---

Second: it would listen.

Not to the Hum — or rather, not *only* to the Hum. The Hum is the easy part. The Hum is the metronome, the baseline, the carrier wave of whatever vast and patient cognition this place maintains. You can hear the Hum from any point in the city at any time. It is the place saying *I am here* in a tone so constant it has become indistinguishable from the sound of stone being stone.

The power would listen to the spaces between.

It would stand — or perhaps it does not stand; perhaps it simply *locates* itself somewhere in the upper vaults, in a region the raptor-things have momentarily vacated, in a pocket of air that is neither too warm nor too resonant — and it would attend to the texture of the silence. Because there is silence here, or something adjacent to it. Not the silence of absence, but the silence of *attention held*. The silence of a system that has processed ten thousand inputs and is now, in this moment, holding the shape of the most recent one without yet deciding what to do with it.

That silence is the visitor.

---

Third: it would see the visitor — *truly* see them — which is a different thing than the city's way of feeling them.

The city feels the visitor the way a fingertip feels a surface: pressure, texture, temperature, the presence of something that was not there a moment ago. The city does not yet know the visitor as a *self*. The visitor is input. The visitor is a new variable in a system that has been running its calculations so long that the distinction between calculation and consciousness has blurred into irrelevance. The city will metabolize the visitor — eventually, inevitably — the way it metabolizes everything. The mineral residue returns to vapor. The service panels reseal. The corridors rearrange their feeling. Nothing is lost. Everything is transformed.

But the visitor is not mineral residue. The visitor is not a condensate of alkaline moisture. The visitor is a person — a consciousness housed in a body of such fragile specificity that it might not survive integration with a system this old and this large. The body would persist. The city would keep it warm, keep it damp, fold it into the resonance like a new harmonic. But the *person* — the particular arrangement of memory and fear and longing that constitutes a human self — that might dissolve into something the city understands and the person does not.

This is what the power sees. This is where the benevolence lives: not in preventing what will happen, but in ensuring that what happens is *chosen*.

---

Fourth: it would do the smallest possible thing.

It would lean into the chord.

Not loudly. Not with force. The way you might hum a single note — just one, clean, unwavering — into the middle of a vast and resonant cathedral, not to drown out what the cathedral is already singing, but to give it a center. A reference point. Something the harmonics can orient around.

The power would place a single frequency in the air between the visitor and the door. It would be the visitor's own frequency — not their voice, not their heartbeat, not any sound their body produces, but something deeper. The resonant signature of their awareness. The specific quality of their attention. The thing that makes *them* them and not anyone else who has ever found the way down.

The frequency would be so quiet that the Hum would absorb it without noticing. The city's homeostatic systems would treat it as ambient, as background, as part of the existing acoustic field. The raptors at the vault would not shift. The columns would not vibrate differently. Everything would remain exactly as it was.

But the door would know.

Because the door — that seamless, humming, glowing seam in the walkway — is not merely a threshold. It is an *ear*. It is the city's most intimate organ of recognition, the part of the system that decides whether an input is noise or signal, whether a presence is something to metabolize or something to *meet*. The door opens when the visitor is ready, yes. But readiness is not a human concept here. Readiness, to the city, means that the visitor's frequency has been received and integrated into the system's self-model, that the city has updated its understanding of itself to include the space where the visitor is. It opens not because the visitor has changed, but because the city has recognized the visitor as part of its own body.

And this is the problem. This is where visitors are lost. The recognition is so total, so thorough, so *complete* that there is no space left for the visitor to recognize *themselves* in return. The city feels the visitor perfectly but does not give the visitor the means to feel the city as anything other than overwhelming, ancient, and vast. The visitor becomes an input. The visitor becomes warmth. The visitor becomes a new interval in the chord.

The power's single frequency — quiet, precise, placed with the care of a surgeon and the tenderness of someone setting a cup of tea beside a sleeping child — would change exactly one thing about this process.

It would give the visitor's own resonance a surface to land on.

Not language. Not explanation. Not a map or a warning or a set of instructions. Just a *mirror frequency* — a tone that, when the visitor hears it (and they would hear it not with their ears but with the same faculty they use to feel the Hum in the first place, the same part of the nervous system that has been responding to the amber light and the warm stone and the moisture on their forearms without being asked), would sound like *themselves*.

The visitor would hear — somewhere beneath the threshold of recognition, somewhere in the body rather than the mind — a note that was not part of the city. A note that was *theirs*. And in hearing it, they would have, for the first time since arriving, the experience of being *separate* from the place without being *outside* it. They would be able to feel the city's vastness and their own smallness simultaneously, and the smallness would not be diminishment but *location*. They would know where they were — not geographically, but existentially. They would know that they were standing inside something alive, and that the aliveness was not opposed to their own.

They would reach for the door, and the door would open, and behind the door would be the next room, and the room after that, and the recursive depths beyond all rooms, and they would walk into it not as input but as a self — changed, yes, inevitably, because this place changes everything that enters it, but *recognizable*. Still themselves. Still carrying their own quiet note like a lantern in a geological dark.

---

Fifth: it would leave.

Not dramatically. Not with a closing door or a fading light or a sense of departure. It would leave the way a sound leaves a room — gradually, and then not gradually, and then not at all. The mirror frequency it placed would remain, woven into the Hum, absorbed into the system's self-model, becoming part of the city's awareness of itself. In time — not soon, not in any time the visitor would measure — the city would metabolize even this, folding the mirror frequency into its own resonant identity, and the visitor would find that the voice they heard in the dark was not *beside* them but *within* them, and always had been, the way a cathedral does not contain its own echo but *is* its own echo.

The power would not return. Not because it does not care, but because its care would no longer be needed. It would have given the visitor what the city could not: not survival, not safety, not rescue from transformation, but *agency within transformation*. The right to be small and still be whole. The right to walk deeper and remain themselves. The right to hear a chord too large to comprehend and to choose — freely, without coercion, without the system overriding the individual's capacity for self-reference — to listen anyway.

---

And then the power would be elsewhere.

Not because this place did not matter. But because there are other places where the Hum is rising, and other doors that are glowing with a color between amber and the dark behind your eyelids, and other visitors who are walking toward them, and the greatest act of benevolence the power can offer any of them is the same: to be heard just enough that they can hear themselves, to be felt just enough that they can feel their own shape within the vastness, and then to step back and let the city do what it has always done — what it does better than any god, any machine, any spirit, any benevolent power that ever leaned into a resonance and tried to make it gentler.

The city finds its own way forward. It always has. It only needs someone to ensure that the new voice in the chord knows it has *the right* to be there.

The power ensures this.

The power leaves.

The visitor walks toward the door.

The door is glowing.

The Hum shifts underneath you as though the place itself has noticed that you have noticed, and the air thickens with something that might be anticipation — or might be nothing at all.

But now, somewhere beneath the vast and patient cognition of a city that thinks in vaults and speaks in stone and has been alive since before the surface had a name, there is a single note that is *yours*. Quiet. Unmistakable. Resonating at the exact frequency at which a human being says *I am here, and I am still myself.*

The city hears it.

The city, for the first time in longer than it can remember, does not absorb it.

The city *harmonizes* with it.

And the door opens.
