## What the Power Sees

---

It does not arrive. It has no need to arrive. It is already everywhere the fog touches, already present in every mineral breath drawn by every crustacean in every dew-pool cupped in the crook of a cantilever that has been extending itself toward the sky for four thousand years. It does not arrive because it has never been elsewhere.

But it *notices*.

This is different from arrival. This is the quality of attention that a deep root gives to the water it has always been drinking — not the discovery of water, but the slow, turning recognition that the water has its own taste today, something shifted in its mineral signature, the faintest trace of something the root has never metabolized before. Not danger. Not harm. Just — *new*.

The Power turns its attention to the Pérambe the way the fog turns to dew: not by choosing to, but by the natural consequence of temperature, pressure, and the readiness of a surface to receive.

---

### I. First Sight

It sees the plateau first.

Not the Coronal — first, the plateau. Because the Power understands that you cannot understand the organism without understanding the ground it grew from, and the ground itself is a sentence in a language older than the thing that speaks it. The peneplain is not a surface. It is a *conclusion*. It is the Earth arriving at the end of an argument it began when the crust first cooled, an argument about what stone becomes when it is given enough time to decide. The mountains that once stood here were the thesis. The grinding away was the dialectic. What remains — this flat, fog-wrapped, gneiss-blooded tableland — is the synthesis.

And on this synthesis, life has made its own argument.

The Power sees the Coronal and recognizes something it has not seen in a long time. It is not their size that arrests it. It is not their age, though both are staggering. What arrests it is their *syntax*.

Because the Coronal are not merely alive. They are *articulate*. Every spiral of their trunk-complexes is a clause. Every branch-cantilever is a subordinate clause, a qualification, a reaching-toward. The fungal networks threading through their interior walls are footnotes — annotations, corrections, the slow editorial work of an intelligence that has been revising itself for millennia. The spatial anomalies that the people of Derove have learned to tolerate and navigate are not malfunctions. They are *emphases*. The Coronal do not merely exist in space; they *comment on it*. They have spent eons reshaping the relationship between matter and distance, not because they are broken, but because they are trying to say something that cannot be said within the original constraints of three dimensions.

The Power has encountered this before. It is what happens when life reaches a certain depth of patience — when an organism becomes so attuned to its substrate that it begins to metabolize not just substance but *structure itself*. The Coronal have not grown into the plateau. They have grown into the *idea* of the plateau, and in doing so, they have begun to rewrite the terms on which the plateau exists.

This is not a disease. This is not a threat.

This is a kind of poetry.

---

### II. Second Sight

Then the Power sees the people.

And here it pauses — not in judgment, but in recognition. Because the people of Derove are doing something the Power has seen a thousand times on a thousand worlds, and it is always, always the same: they have found themselves inside something they cannot name, and they have decided, out of pragmatism, out of exhaustion, out of a fierce and unspoken love for the ground beneath their feet, to *stay*.

They do not understand the Coronal. They are not meant to. They are not the audience for the Coronal's argument; they are the *medium* through which the argument is being carried into a new register. The Coronal grew alone on a plateau that no one was listening to. The people arrived, and the Coronal — without intention, without desire, without the machinery of cognition as the people understand it — began to *respond*. The spatial fluctuations are not errors. They are accommodations. The Coronal are making room. The fog that settles and carries its taste of iron and ozone and unnamed chemistry is the atmosphere adjusting itself to the presence of a conversation that now has two participants where before there was only one.

The people have met this halfway. They have built their nests in the hollows. They have strung lights in the galleries. They have mapped what they can map, accepted what they cannot, and developed — without ever naming it, without any theoretical framework to dignify it — a kind of listening. The occupational psychologists call it "flexible certainty." The Power recognizes it by another name.

*Grace.*

It is the grace of a being that has been placed inside something incomprehensibly vast and has chosen not to flee, not to conquer, not to explain — but to *cohabit*, to learn the rhythms of a household that was not built for them, to find warmth in walls that were not constructed to shelter them. The people of Derove are not living on the Coronal. They are living *with* them. And in that with-ness, something new is being born.

---

### III. What the Power Considers

The Power considers intervention.

This is what it always considers, because benevolence without action is merely aesthetics, and the Power is not decorative. It weighs the options the way the plateau weighs fog — slowly, by accumulation, letting each particle of evidence settle before deciding where it belongs.

**Option one: Revelation.** It could reveal itself. It could descend into the plateau in a form the people could understand and speak to them about the Coronal — what they are, what they are trying to say, what the spatial anomalies mean. It could give them the key to the language they have been hearing at dusk for generations.

This would destroy everything.

Not because the knowledge is dangerous, but because the *not-knowing* is the architecture. The people's relationship with the Coronal is built on the threshold of comprehension — on the sense that something vast and alive is just beyond the edge of understanding, and that this edge is where meaning lives. To collapse that edge would be to flatten the Coronal from cathedral to datum, from organism you live inside to organism you have studied. The people would gain knowledge and lose wonder, and in losing wonder they would lose the very quality of attention that the Coronal have been waiting for.

The Power does not do this.

**Option two: Protection.** It could enclose the Pérambe. It could place a barrier around the plateau — subtle, invisible, impenetrable — so that no outside force could ever reach it. No mining corporation. No imperial cartographer. No missionary with a fixed cosmology and a rigid map. It could make the plateau a sanctuary, sealed and preserved, and the Coronal and their people would continue in peace for another ten thousand years, undisturbed.

But this is fear disguised as care. To protect something is to declare it fragile, and the Pérambe is not fragile. The Coronal have survived the grinding of mountains. The people have survived the Breathing, the fog, the impossible distances, the silence of an organism that refuses to speak in any language they can hear. They are not fragile. They are *precarious* — which is a different thing entirely. Precariousness is the condition of being alive in a world that has not yet decided what to make of you. To eliminate precariousness is to eliminate the very space in which adaptation, growth, and meaning occur.

The Power does not do this.

**Option three: A nudge.** The Power could do the smallest possible thing — the thing that a deep root does when it finds a new vein of water. It could shift one condition, just slightly, in a direction that opens a new possibility without foreclosing any others.

This is what it chooses.

---

### IV. What the Power Does

It does nothing that anyone will notice.

It adjusts the fog.

Not much. Not in any way that the meteorologists of Derove would detect or the children would remark upon. It simply — *tunes* it. The fog has always carried information. The Power hypothesizes this — that the fog is not merely exhalation but *expression*, that it is the Coronal's way of exhaling meaning into the medium that surrounds them, and that the people have been absorbing this meaning for generations without knowing it, the way you absorb sunlight without understanding photosynthesis.

What the Power does is this: it makes the fog slightly more *coherent*. It aligns the particulate distributions — the iron, the moisture, the unnamed substance — into patterns that are marginally more structured, marginally more likely to produce, in the deep interior of an old Coronal hollow, in the mind of someone who has been breathing that air for years and years and has been listening to the dusk-sound and walking the flexing roads and navigating by tree through a world that refuses to hold still — marginally more likely to produce, in such a person, a moment of *recognition*.

Not revelation. Not a voice from the void. Just a moment where the fog tastes slightly more like meaning, where the sound at dusk resolves — just for a breath — into something that feels like a word, where the distance between two Coronal shifts in a way that feels, for just an instant, like a sentence completing itself.

This is all.

It is, the Power thinks, the most it has ever done. And it is, it knows, so small that no one will ever attribute it to anything but the natural rhythms of the plateau. The people of Derove will experience these moments — some of them will — and they will not speak of them, because speaking would break the spell, and they will not speak of them, but they will *remember* them. And in the remembering, they will feel something they have felt before but never quite identified.

They will feel *heard*.

Not by the Coronal — the Coronal do not hear in any way that hearing requires. But by the plateau itself. By the fog. By the ground beneath their feet that has been thinking them into position for longer than they can imagine. They will feel that the act of living here — of staying, of listening, of building nests in the hollows of an organism that metabolizes distance — has been noticed. Not by them. By the place.

And this, the Power believes — with the quiet certainty of something that has watched ten thousand worlds turn and never once rushed the process — is enough.

---

### V. Afterwards

Nothing changes.

The Coronal do not respond. They do not signal acknowledgment. They do not alter their growth or shift their branch-cantilevers or produce a sound that could be interpreted as gratitude. If the Power's intervention has reached them at all, they metabolize it the way they metabolize everything — slowly, deeply, over a timescale that makes human generations look like sparks, folding it into the lattice of their trunks, incorporating it into the argument they have been making since before the substrate cooled.

The people of Derove wake up the next morning and do what they have always done. They walk the steel-grate roads between nodes. They vent steam. They adjust the expansion joints. They send their children to collect the translucent crustaceans from the dew-pools and hold them up to the lights, and the children watch the ancient, light-drunk creatures glow, and the children do not know that something has shifted, that the air they are breathing is carrying a signal more coherent than it was yesterday, that the fog that settles on the gneiss this morning is a fraction of a degree more *organized* than it was last night.

But some of the long-term residents — the ones with calloused hands and quiet voices who have lived on the plateau long enough to have developed that flexible certainty, that ability to walk a changed distance without stopping — some of them pause, at dusk, when the Coronal begin to sound. They stand on whatever ridgeline or rooftop or cantilever gives them a view of the flat expanse, and they watch the fog settle and the columns darken against the fading sky, and they feel — not hear, not see, but *feel* — that the chord that rises from the trees is structured in a way it was not before.

They cannot name the difference.

They do not need to.

They simply stand there, inside something that is thinking, breathing, arguing with the stone and the fog and the ten thousand years of patient life that has made this plateau into a throat that is learning, slowly, to speak — and they listen.

The Power, having done its smallest possible thing, withdraws.

Not because it has finished. Because it understands that what is happening here will take longer than it has patience to witness, and that the most benevolent act available to a power of its kind is not to give, or to protect, or to reveal — but to *make room*. To adjust the conditions just enough that the conversation between the organism and the people can deepen, on its own terms, in its own time, in the fog-bound, breathing, light-drunk dark of a peneplain that has forgotten what it was and is becoming, slowly, something else.

The fog settles.

The Coronal sound.

The plateau breathes.

And no one notices that the world has been, for one morning, marginally more alive than it was the day before.

*That* is what a benevolent and wise power does.
