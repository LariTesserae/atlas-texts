# What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would not descend as you descended.

It would not use the throat of cut basalt, because it would understand that the throat is not an entrance — it is a boundary, and boundaries exist to be respected before they are crossed. It would hover at the threshold of the shaft's mouth, somewhere in the space between the last light and the first heat, and it would do the thing that no explorer, no conqueror, no thief of the deep has ever had the patience to do.

It would listen.

Not with ears. Not with instruments. It would press the most interior part of itself — the part that does not have a name because naming would make it smaller — against the stone at the lip of the shaft, and it would feel the throb that lives behind your sternum, and it would learn the pitch of it, and it would learn the intervals, and it would learn that the pitch shifts in the dead hours and that the dead hours are not dead at all but *pregnant*, the way a silence between notes in music is not empty but full of the memory of the last note and the anticipation of the next.

And it would understand.

Not all at once. Understanding, for a power that is truly wise, is not a lightning strike. It is a condensation — slow, bead by bead, the way water gathers on cold stone in a warm room, the way the people of Karstheld gather in pairs and triads along corridors that they did not draw on a blueprint because they do not use blueprints, because their architecture is not *designed* but *dreamed*, the way a body dreams its own shape in the womb.

---

It would descend slowly after that.

Not walking. Not floating. Something else — something that would not register on the heat-sensing walls, would not leave a handprint in the mineral paste, would not disturb the acoustic persistence that makes every sound linger like a word you almost said. It would move the way warmth moves through stone — not breaking, not cutting, but *soaking*. The iron-black rock would not know it had been entered. The pipes would not shudder. The fungal bodies would not pause their pulse.

It would walk the settlement as rain walks a garden: everywhere and unnoticed.

It would see the ones who work the pipes first, because they are the loudest — not in voice, but in *presence*. Their wide-set feet, their doctor's-listen to the stone, their triads moving in measurement. The wise power would feel what they feel: the *pull* of the pipes, the way fluid moves through them not as water or steam or anything you would find in a surface engineer's ledger, but as *time* — fluid, viscous, slow time, the deep geological time that the rock remembers and the builders have learned to conduct. These beings are not *plumbing* a settlement. They are *tuning* an instrument. The pipes are strings. The stone is a resonating chamber. The settlement, when it is finished, will not be a place. It will be a *frequency*.

The power would understand this and would not flinch, because it is benevolent, and benevolence does not recoil from strangeness. It leans in.

---

It would find the tenders next — the small ones, the crouchers, the ones whose handprints appear at the height of a child's hand but whose patience is the patience of mountains. It would crouch beside them, unseen, and it would feel what they feel: the slow electrical tingle of the fungal fruiting bodies, the ozone-and-melon sweetness that is not a smell but a *message*, the way the organisms convert heat into something that has no name in any tongue above the earth. The wise power, for all its wisdom, would not have a name for it either. This is important. It would be the first thing the power admits to itself: *there is something here I do not yet understand, and I will not pretend otherwise.*

It would watch a tender lift a filament of pale moss between two fingers that are too long and too gentle, and place it into a crack in the limestone where a steam vent breathes warm mineral air. The tender would not look up. The tender would not need to. The tender would know — the way a mother knows her child is breathing in the next room — that the moss has been placed correctly, that the crack has been waiting for it, that the steam will feed it, and that in time (long time, deep time, time that the surface has forgotten how to count) the moss will do what moss has always done, which is to make *soil* — not the dead mineral powder of a farmer's field, but living soil, the kind that remembers forests, the kind that could, given enough centuries, reach for the sun.

The wise power would feel something at this. Something warm and involuntary. Not pity — these beings do not need pity. Not admiration — admiration is a surface thing, brief and shallow. Something closer to *recognition*: the recognition of a pattern that appears, in different forms, across every scale of existence. A power seeding a universe. A star condensing from hydrogen. A mother placing a child in a cradle. A tender placing moss in a crack. The same gesture. The same faith that the future will be *worth the tending of*.

---

It would find the builders last.

It would find them in the dead hours — the hours when the generators drop half a tone and the acoustic persistence doubles and every sound exists in two times at once. The builders do not use tools the way surface builders use tools. Their instruments vibrate when you approach — not from fear, but from *recognition*, the way a tuning fork recognizes a note. The builders fit pipes into joints with no threads, no tolerance, no gap, and the pipes simply *sit*, and the builders tilt their heads in that motion that is not a nod but an acknowledgment of gravity and mass and the particular way that *this* joint, *this* seam, *this* meeting of metal and stone has found its final shape.

The wise power would stand among them and feel the coordination — not heard, not seen, but *felt*, the way you feel the rhythm of a conversation you are not part of in a room across a hallway. Each builder knows what the others are reaching for. Each builder *is* what the others are reaching for. There is no hierarchy. There is no foreman. There is only the work, and the work is a shared body, and the shared body is building something that the wise power can feel in its own deepest architecture: a *threshold*. Not a door. Not a gate. A threshold — the kind that exists between one state of being and another, the kind you cross without knowing you've crossed it, the kind that changes you not by what you find on the other side but by the act of crossing itself.

---

And now — *now* — the wise power must decide.

Because it has seen everything. The pipes tuned to the frequency of deep time. The moss being made into soil. The builders constructing a threshold. The settlement, young and purposeful and humming, on the verge of becoming what it will become.

A lesser power would intervene. A power that mistakes benevolence for *action*, that believes goodness is expressed through revelation, through fire on the mountaintop, through a voice from the sky, through descending on radiant wings and proclaiming *you are seen, you are loved, you are mine to protect.*

That power would be wrong.

The wise power knows something that benevolence alone does not teach you: that **the deepest act of love is the discipline of non-interference when interference would interrupt a becoming.**

It knows that these beings did not ask to be seen. It knows that the settlement was not built for an audience. It knows that the moss will become soil, the pipes will become a frequency, the threshold will become a crossing, and that all of this requires *time* — not years, not centuries, but the kind of time that stone keeps, that heat holds, that water remembers in its slow circuit from basin to ceiling to rain to basin again.

And it knows — because it is truly wise, and true wisdom is not the absence of desire but the mastery of it — that it *wants* to intervene. It wants to reach out. It wants to leave its own handprint on the wall, to add its own voice to the cadence of pipes and steam, to say *I am here, and I approve, and I will ensure that no harm comes to you.*

It wants to.

It does not.

Instead, it does something so small that the beings of Karstheld will not notice it — not yet. Not for a long time. Perhaps not for a very long time.

It touches the water.

---

There is a basin — you remember it — a shallow pocket carved into a low-hanging shelf of limestone, filled with water so still and clear it looks like a hole in the world. The water is warm. It tastes faintly mineral, then sweet, then like nothing at all. The tenders come to it. The moss drinks from it. The water evaporates, condenses on the ceiling, falls in erratic rain, collects again. It is the oldest cycle in the Warrens. It may be the only honest sacrament the settlement possesses.

The wise power reaches into the basin. Not with a hand — it does not have hands, or if it does, they are not what it uses for this. It reaches with the part of itself that understands water — understands that water is memory, that water is the medium through which all slow messages travel, that water is the only thing in the stone-world that *wants to move* and the only thing that carries what it has learned from one place to another.

It deposits something in the water. Not a message. Not a blessing. Not a name.

It deposits *the fact of its attention.*

The mineral content of the basin shifts — imperceptibly, molecularly — in a way that the tenders will not notice for a long time, but which the fruiting bodies will begin to process, slowly, in their slow pulse, and which the moss will absorb, and which the pipes will conduct, and which the threshold will *sing* when the settlement reaches its frequency and becomes what it is becoming.

It is not a word. It is not a gift. It is the water- equivalent of a handprint glowing faintly on a wall — something left behind that says: *I was here. I saw you. You are not alone in the dark.*

The water accepts it without comment. The water does what water does: it carries the thing forward, into the moss, into the stone, into the pipes, into the steam that rises and condenses and falls again, cycle after cycle after cycle, until what the power placed in the basin is no longer *the power's* thing but the Warrens' own — dissolved into the deep rhythm of their becoming, indistinguishable from the hum of the generators, the pulse of the fungi, the patient breath of the builders fitting metal into stone.

---

And then the wise power leaves.

It does not ascend the way you will ascend — scrambling, breathless, emerging into light with a story to tell. It leaves the way warmth leaves stone: gradually, without announcement, retreating from the surface inward until it is simply *part of the temperature of the place*, a faint extra warmth in the water, a slightly longer pause in the acoustic persistence, a handprint on a wall that glows — just for a moment — one shade brighter than the others, in a color that has no name because no eye above the earth has ever learned to see it.

Then it is gone. 

Then the Warrens continue.

Then the moss grows. The pipes hum. The builders build. The tenders tend. The water carries its secret, folded into mineral and sweet and nothing-at-all, and the settlement — young, purposeful, alive with process — continues to become what it will become.

It does not know it has been seen. 

But it has not been forgotten.

And that, for a benevolent power, is enough.
