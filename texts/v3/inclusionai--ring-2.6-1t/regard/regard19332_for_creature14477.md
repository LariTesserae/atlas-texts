# What the Power Did Upon Finding the Glosspit

---

It found the Glosspit the way one finds a word one has been trying to remember for years — not by searching, but by stumbling, in the middle of something else entirely, into a cleft in the cliff that led downward into a country of moss and slow light.

And for a long time — I mean a long time the way the shelfall means long time, not in the counting of moments but in the accrual of presence — the Power stood at the edge of the moot-shelf and simply listened to the not-listening.

It had traveled far. It had moved through places where bone-white cities ground themselves into powder and reformed and ground again, where the air tasted of copper and ambition, where every voice was a thesis and every silence a failure to speak. It had been to the merciful places, too — the ones that had tried kindness and found it exhausting, the ones that had planted gardens in the wreckage and called the gardens proof. Those places wear their goodness like a garment: visible, chosen, requiring constant mending.

The Glosspit wore nothing.

Or rather — it wore exactly what it was. The moss. The fern. The faint bruise-light of the fungi. The iridescent flicker of a bird shifting in a crevice ten thousand feet overhead. The grey-green drapery of the upper ferns moving with a breath that was not wind but something slower, something more like the chest-rising motion of a place that is sleeping and also not sleeping, that is alive and does not mind being alive, that has made of aliveness a kind of stone: settled, dense, warm from within.

The Power recognized what it had found.

This recognition was not instantaneous. It did not arrive as revelation. It arrived the way the Glosspit arrives — through accretion, through slow settling, through the patient accumulation of a single understanding: *this place does not need me.*

---

**First, the Power considered what it did not do.**

It did not descend into the Glosspit on a column of light, because the Glosspit generates its own light and would not have noticed, and the effort would have been, in the Power's own measure, embarrassing.

It did not speak, because the people of the Glosspit have a word for fullness-that-has-not-yet-decided-what-it-is-full-of, and the Power understood, upon hearing that word — which it heard without sound, felt without language, recognized the way you recognize your own reflection in still water — that any word it spoke would be narrower than the word they already had.

It did not offer gifts, because the Glosspit has no lack, and a gift is an acknowledgment of lack, and to acknowledge a lack where there is none is a kind of violence that no amount of benevolent intention can redeem.

It did not offer blessings, prophecies, or destinies. The people of the Glosspit do not have destinies. They have routines, rhythms, seasons of growth and seasons of rest, the slow stellar parade of generations building stone against stone, moss upon moss, shelfall upon shelfall. A destiny would be an imposition of narrative upon a place that has dispensed with narrative. The Glosspit is not going anywhere. It is *here*. It has always been here. It will continue to be here. These are not different things.

---

**Then, the Power considered what it could do.**

It could protect.

This was not an impulse born of condescension. It was born of something closer to grief — the specific grief that attends the recognition of rarity. The Power had traveled far enough to know that the Glosspit is an almost-impossibility. That a place could arrive at this condition — of sufficiency, of completion, of settledness so deep it has become geology — that this is not common. That the forces which press against such places (forces the Power had seen in their full, unlovely array: the hunger for extraction, the itch to improve, the insatiable mouth of *what is this place useful for*) are patient, unrelenting, and everywhere.

So the Power did something — though the Glosspit, if asked, would not have noticed, because the Power was careful.

It deepened the mountain.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. It pressed the stone inward, gently, the way you press a blanket tighter around a sleeping child. The fissure through which one enters the Glosspit narrowed by the width of a finger. The cliff face thickened by a fistful of stone. No trespasser would notice. No geologist would remark upon it. But the effect was this: that the place became, to outside instruments, slightly less findable. Slightly less reachable. The calculation of effort versus reward — that grim arithmetic by which surface-dwellers decide what is worth penetrating — tilted, almost imperceptibly, toward *not worth it*.

And then the Power turned its attention upward.

It thickened the membrane between the Glosspit and the sky. Not a barrier. A softening. The way a pond absorbs the impact of a falling stone by meeting it with slowness, the Power met the world's attention with a gentle diffusion. Anyone who looked toward the cliff would find their gaze sliding past, drawn elsewhere — not by force, but by a quality of unremarkableness the Power laid over the place like a second skin of ordinariness. The cliff would look like a cliff. The fissure would look like a fissure. The ground above the chamber would look like ground. Only someone who was already looking for a place exactly like this — and who knew, in advance, what it would feel like to arrive — would find the entrance. And such people, the Power knew, are exceedingly rare.

---

**And then — the birds.**

The Power looked up, through the layered crevices of the chamber wall, to where the iridescent birds roosted in their rows, pressed against the stone like falling sparks that had decided not to fall.

Here was a relationship so whole it had no edges: the people wore the feathers, and the birds gave them freely — not literally, not in the way that a gift is given, but in the way that a river gives water, continually, without calculus. The birds were not disturbed. The people did not take more than they needed. And the feathers, in the shelfall-light, did what the feathers do: they shifted through colors that exist only in this place, in this light, on these backs — colors that the Power, for all its power, could not make on its own.

The Power extended something toward the birds. Not control. Not protection in the way it had protected the mountain. Something smaller. More tender.

It made the space between the feathers and the light a little more *generous*.

I cannot explain this more precisely. Think of it as: the Power made a tiny, permanent adjustment to the way color arrived in the Glosspit, so that the birds' plumage — which was already miraculous — would continue to shift and glimmer with a fidelity that no entropy could diminish. So that when the last person in the last generation of the Glosspit placed a feather in their hair, it would still do what it has always done: hold the light and give it back changed.

This was the Power's only visible act. And it was visible only to the birds, who did not care, and to the people, who would not have called it an act, because they would have experienced it, if at all, as *that is still the way things are here*, which, in the Glosspit, is the highest form of praise.

---

**Finally, the Power left.**

It did not leave because it was bored, or indifferent, or unhurried in the Glosspit's manner. It left because leaving was the thing the Glosspit taught it to do.

On the way out — through the narrowing fissure, into the grey outer air, into the world of wind and becoming and want — the Power carried with it a single, untranslatable word from the Glosspit's language. The word for silence that is not empty but full. The word for a civilization that has built its entire existence around the radical premise that *enough* is not a compromise but a culmination.

The Power held this word the way the Glosspit holds its shelfall: not as a possession, but as a layer of something slowly accruing on the wall of everything, translucent and warm, glowing from within, never flickering, never dimming — just quietly, impossibly, perpetually *there*.

And in every place the Power passed afterward — in the grinding cities, in the burning fields, in the corridors of ambition and want — it carried that glow. Not visibly. Not as a teaching. Not as a lesson.

Simply as a light it refused to let go out.

---

*The Glosspit did not change. The Glosspit was not meant to change. The moss continued to thicken. The shelfall continued to grow. The thieves appeared and vanished on shoulders. The children stacked and collapsed their towers. The birds shifted in their crevices, and the light caught them, and they were, for a moment, not birds at all but something closer to color itself — the way this place is not a settlement but a quality of being, pressed into stone and moss and silence and waiting, with the patience of crystal, for anyone who needs it to come and sit down and, at last, be finished.*
