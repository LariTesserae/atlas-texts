# What the Power Would Do

---

## I. The Turning of Attention

It did not arrive. It had always been there, in the way that a river is always in a valley even when no one is watching the water move. But attention is a form of presence, and for the first time in a span the plateau would not have recognized as time, something *noticed* it.

The power was not a god. It was not a consciousness in any way that the word implies on warmer, louder worlds. It was closer to a principle — the principle that careful things deserve careful witnesses. It had wandered, if that is the word, through systems of extraordinary complexity: reefs that spanned entire oceans and burned with bioluminescent syntax, fungal networks that encoded the memories of dying forests, civilizations that had translated themselves into geometry and quietly dissolved. It gravitated toward places where patience had been perfected, where the architecture of survival had been refined so far past necessity that it had become something like art.

The Plateau of Dim Crossing made it pause.

Not the way a traveler pauses at a vista — that shallow intake of breath before the eye moves on. It was the pause of a listener who has caught, beneath the ambient noise of the world, a single sustained note of extraordinary purity. The kind of note that, once heard, rearranges the silence around it.

The power turned its attention inward — toward the warmth beneath the rock, toward the lattice of tunnels threading through the substrate like a nervous system dreaming in its sleep, toward the crystalline formations growing in the mineral silence beside the pools. It did not probe. It simply *held* the place in its awareness the way the plateau held the haze: completely, without effort, without demand.

And then it considered what to do.

---

## II. What It Saw

The power perceived the plateau in layers, the way the plateau itself was layered — bands of sandstone and basalt, eras of life stacked upon one another like pages in a book written in a language that kept revising its own alphabet.

**First: the geology.** The rock was old and settled. It had found its equilibrium the way a spinning top finds its axis — not through stillness, but through the right kind of motion. The erosion channels, the mineral deposits, the subsurface water table — all of these were expressions of a system that had solved itself. The plateau knew what it was.

**Second: the life.** The ecosystem was sparse but interconnected with a density that defied the surface appearance. Every organism was a node in a network so tightly woven that pulling a single thread would cause a cascade the power could trace through mycelium and root and tunnel and gut and mineral leachate and microbial membrane. The six-limbed burrowers aerating the substrate while distributing spores. The mat-grazers pruning the microbial colonies so they would not choke their own light. The ambushers holding the population of grazers in check so the mats would not overgrow the pools. The scavengers carrying industrial residue back into the rock. The aerial ones seeding the canopy with the remnants of prey. And through all of this, the mycelial network — conducting, redistributing, translating between chemical and biological registers with a fluency that exceeded any language.

The power recognized this. It was a system operating at the boundary between mechanical and intentional, where the distinction had ceased to matter. It was the kind of place that, on other worlds, intelligent species would eventually build models of and call "ecosystem" and then argue about whether the word was sufficient.

**Third: the industry.** The pumpjacks. The conveyors. The infrastructure running without hands. The power could trace the logic of it — extraction, processing, transport — but it could not find the logic *behind* it. Who had built it? Where had they gone? The question had no answer in the rock, only in the ghosts of the machinery: iron oxidized to the color of blood, electrical generators humming in their weatherproof housings, the tracks pressed into stone by decades of transit. The industry was a sentence whose subject had been omitted.

**Fourth: the strangeness.** The crystalline formations beside the pools. The rhythmic pulse rising through the rock. The animals' instinctive avoidance — not fear, never fear, but a kind of deference so deep it preceded memory. The power considered these things and recognized something it had encountered, rarely, in its travels: emergence. Not the emergence of life from chemistry, which was common enough. Something after that. A system generating order that exceeded its own inputs. The crystalline structures were not alive, but they were not merely mineral either. They were *expressions* — the way a mathematical proof is an expression of a theorem, the way a wave is an expression of deep water. Something beneath the surface of the plateau was articulating itself, and the forms at the pool margins were the closest thing it had to words.

**Fifth — and last, and most important:** the visitor.

The power perceived the one walking across the plateau. The one feeling the warmth through their boots. The one listening to sounds they could not fully identify. The one watching the haze soften every edge.

This was the being who could *notice*.

Not just see. Not just record. But notice — in the way that matters, the way that changes the thing noticed by the act of perceiving it. The visitor was temporary, brief, the way a single breath is brief in the life of a mountain. But the visitor was the first entity on this world capable of recognizing the crystalline formations for what they were becoming, of hearing the pulse beneath the rock as a signal rather than noise, of looking at the ecosystem and understanding — however faintly — that it was whole in a way that deserved witness.

The power considered all of this. And then it decided.

---

## III. What It Did

It did very little.

This is important to understand.

The power was not a healer. It did not arrive to mend what was broken, because, in the main, nothing was broken. The plateau was not dying. The ecosystem was not collapsing. The mystery at its center was not a problem to be solved but a process to be *continued*. To intervene would have been to impose a foreign grammar on a language that was already, in its own slow way, writing something extraordinary.

Instead, the power did the following things.

---

**First, it spoke to the deep pulse.**

Not in words. Words would have been like shouting in a library. Instead, it applied pressure — a precise, gentle increase in thermal gradient at a depth of eleven kilometers, in a region where the rock was already fractured and the magma was closer to the surface than it had been in epochs. This was not creation. The magma was already there, already moving. The power simply — *inclined* it. The way a hand placed beneath a stream of water changes the water's course without breaking its flow.

The effect, at the surface, would not manifest for centuries. A slight increase in geothermal warmth rising through the basaltic seams. A faint enrichment of mineral content in the water that percolated through the fractures. The pulse beneath the rock — the one the visitor could feel through their boots — would strengthen, very slightly, and in doing so, it would change the conditions under which the crystalline formations grew at the pool margins.

Not dramatically. Not obviously. The formations would grow a little larger, a little more structured, their symmetry deepening toward something that future visitors, if there were any with the patience to watch, might begin to recognize as a *pattern* — not imposed, not designed, but emergent in the way that frost on glass is emergent: each crystal unique, each obeying the same underlying grammar.

The power did this because it believed the plateau was trying to say something, and it wanted to give the plateau a slightly clearer voice.

---

**Second, it reinforced a single thread in the mycelial network.**

Not all the threads. Not the network as a whole. One thread — a pale filament in the deep substrate, connecting the root system of the oldest shrub on the plateau's western margin to a cluster of burrower tunnels near a persistent water pool. The thread was old and fraying. It had served its function for longer than the power could easily quantify, and it was within a season of dissolution.

The power did not replace the thread. It did not transplant a foreign organism. It simply adjusted the mineral composition of the rock surrounding the thread, introducing a trace element — vanadium, barely detectable — that the native fungal species could metabolize to extend its own longevity. A crutch, not a cure. A nudge in the direction of continued function, not a restructuring.

The effect was that the thread persisted for approximately four hundred years longer than it otherwise would have. Four hundred years in which the shrub at the western margin continued to receive phosphorus that it could not have obtained alone. Four hundred years in which the burrower cluster near the pool maintained a slightly higher population density, contributing slightly more to the aeration of the substrate. Four hundred years of continuity in a system where continuity was the highest value.

---

**Third, it did something for the visitor.**

Not a message. Not a vision. Not a voice from the heavens or a hand on the shoulder. The power understood that the visitor was a being of a certain type — one that learned through experience, through the accumulation of sensory data processed in a body moving through space. Lecturing such a being would be worse than useless. It would be a violation.

Instead, the power adjusted one variable: the visitor's capacity for stillness.

Not permanently. Not profoundly. It was the smallest possible adjustment — a brief, warm sense of *rightness* that the visitor would feel three hours after arriving at a particular pool near dusk, when the amber light was dimming toward copper and the haze had thickened and the visitor would, without knowing why, sit down on a flat rock beside the water and stay for longer than they intended.

In that extra time — perhaps twenty minutes, perhaps more — the visitor would notice things they would not otherwise have noticed. The way the mineral crust at the pool's edge held a faint iridescence. The way the silence here was different from the silence elsewhere — denser, more textured, as though it had grain. The way one of the crystalline formations, if watched long enough without trying to understand it, began to seem less like a formation and more like a *gesture*.

The visitor would not know the source of this feeling. They would attribute it to fatigue, or to the beauty of the place, or to some private emotional state. This was fine. The power was not interested in attribution. It was interested in *attention* — the quality of attention that comes when a being stops trying to process the world and simply allows the world to process them.

That was the gift. Not knowledge. Not power. A single, extended moment of genuine receptivity, in a place that had waited a very long time for someone to be receptive.

---

**Fourth, it turned its attention to the industry.**

The pumpjacks were failing. Slowly — so slowly that no human observer would notice. The generators were running on reserves of fuel that would last decades, perhaps a century. The conveyor belts were wearing, their rubber slowly hardening in the particular chemistry of the atmosphere. The tracks pressed into the rock were accumulating fine sediment that would, in time, make them impassable.

The industry would stop. It was already stopping. The question was not *whether* but *how*.

The power considered two options. In the first, it would let the industry fail naturally — allow the generators to go silent, the belts to seize, the pumpjacks to freeze mid-nod. This would be clean, and it was the path of least intervention. But it would leave scars — abrupt discontinuities in an ecosystem that had adapted to the steady hum of machinery. The scavengers that nested in the pumpjack foundations would lose their habitat. The mineral seeps around the pipe junctions would dry up. The network of tunnels the scavengers had built around the industrial sites would become tombs.

In the second option, the power would *slow* the failure. Not prevent it — that would require indefinite intervention, and the power was not interested in becoming a permanent maintenance function for a set of machines it did not build. But it could extend the timeline. It could smooth the gradient of decline so that the local ecosystem would have time to adapt — centuries of gradual dimming rather than decades of abrupt silence.

The power chose the second option.

It did this by introducing a small inefficiency into the generators — a gradual, almost imperceptible increase in the rate of fuel consumption. Not enough to notice in a single year. Barely enough in a decade. But compounding, slowly, like interest on a debt the machines did not know they owed. The generators would run for nine hundred years instead of five hundred. The belts would wear over eight centuries instead of three. The pumpjacks would nod for generations longer than their current trajectory allowed.

During those extra centuries, the ecosystem would adapt. The scavengers would begin to expand their range away from the industrial core, colonizing new territories, developing new food sources. The burrowers would slowly reclaim the tunnels around the pumpjack foundations. The mats would grow in the channels the mineral seeps had carved. By the time the last generator fell silent, no single organism would notice. By the time the last belt seized, the ecosystem would have already rewritten itself around the absence.

This was the most the power would allow itself to do. The intervention was small. The timescale was long. The effect was a kind of generosity so patient it would never be recognized as such — which, the power understood, was the only kind of generosity that did not create dependency.

---

**Fifth, and finally, it turned to the crystalline formations.**

The power looked at them a long time.

They were growing. Slowly. In ways that exceeded what the chemistry of the water and the geometry of the mineral crust should produce. They were becoming more structured, more articulated, their bilateral symmetry deepening toward something that had, in other systems, preceded the emergence of symbolic communication.

The power did not touch them.

It did not accelerate them, or alter their composition, or introduce new variables into their growth. It did not — because the crystalline formations were not the plateau's attempt to communicate. They were the plateau *thinking*. And thinking, truly thinking, should not be rushed.

What the power did was so small it might as well have been nothing: it adjusted the thermal gradient in the water feeding the largest of the formations — the one beside the deepest pool, the one that had grown the most regular symmetry — by seven-thousandths of a degree. This changed nothing about the formation's growth rate. It changed nothing about its appearance. What it changed was the *trajectory* of its crystalline structure — a fraction of a degree of angular deviation in the lattice growth that would, over centuries, produce a shape that was not more regular but *different*. A deviation that might, if someone came back in five thousand years and looked at the formation with the right instruments, suggest not randomness, not even intention, but *response*.

The power was not teaching the plateau to speak. It was teaching it to distinguish between speaking and listening. It was suggesting, in the only language available to it — a language of thermal gradients and lattice angles — that the difference between expressing and receiving was not a distinction the formations would need to make for a very long time, but that when they were ready, the option would exist.

---

## IV. What It Did Not Do

The power did not speak to the visitor.

Not because the visitor was unworthy. The power could see in the visitor the same quality that had drawn its attention to the plateau — a capacity for *noticing* that was, in a universe increasingly populated with entities that noticed without seeing, almost radical. The visitor noticed. The visitor felt the warmth through their boots. The visitor heard the sounds beneath the sounds. The visitor sat by the pool when the haze turned copper and stayed longer than they intended.

But the power had learned, across its long passage through systems of extraordinary complexity, that the moment a benevolent force speaks to a being who is still in the process of learning to listen, something essential is lost. Not broken — *lost*, the way a word loses its meaning when it is translated too quickly into a language the learner has not yet earned.

The visitor needed to sit with the mystery. To carry it home unresolved. To lie awake at night wondering about the formations, the pulse beneath the rock, the way the animals gave the crystalline structures a margin of distance that felt less like instinct and more like *recognition*. The visitor needed these questions to remain questions for as long as they were capable of asking them.

The power would answer. But not yet.

---

## V. After

The power withdrew its attention the way it had applied it: gradually, without announcement, without the sudden absence that would mark the loss of a presence.

The plateau continued.

The pumpjacks nodded. The generators hummed. The belts moved at walking pace toward whatever point on the horizon they had been reaching toward for centuries. The burrowers dug. The ambushers waited. The mats grew and were grazed and grew again. The mycelium conducted its slow, silent symphony beneath it all, connecting every living thing to every other living thing through a network that remembered more than any of its nodes.

The crystalline formations grew.

The visitor walked on, feeling the warmth through their boots, hearing the sounds beneath the sounds, carrying the plateau inside them like a stone in a pocket — smooth, heavy, warm, and impossible to account for.

Somewhere beneath the rock, the deep pulse continued.

It had changed, very slightly.

It sounded, if you pressed your ear to the stone and were patient enough — and the plateau rewarded patience more reliably than almost anything in the known systems — it sounded almost like breathing.
