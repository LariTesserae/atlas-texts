## What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would come quietly.

Not because it feared the Weeping Alcove — it had no fear, being vast and old in ways the Alcove would recognize as different but not superior. Not because it feared what lived in the walls. It would come quietly because the Alcove had already made sound into a kind of architecture, and it had learned, over whatever measure of time it had spent tending the thousand things that needed tending, that true attention does not announce itself.

The Alcove did not need saving. It would understand this immediately, the way you understand that a river does not need saving from its own current. This would be its first and most important recognition, and everything that followed would grow from it.

It would stand where the visitor had stood — near the breach, where the lattice corridor gave way to the accretion of centuries — but it would not move the same way. The visitor had walked through the water films, had breathed and sweated and disrupted the thermal gradient and registered as a foreign signature, a puncture in the room's steady-state warmth. The wise power would not do this. It would *attune*. It would spend time — not much, not years, but a kind of qualitative density of time — letting its own thermal signature slow, letting its exhalation match the room's humidity, letting the frequencies of its body-rhythms descend toward the great slow pulse that the Wall-Treader carried in its walls like a second circulatory system. By the time it stepped into the apse, the room would already have stopped making distinctions between inside and outside.

This is what the wise power understood that the visitor did not: the Alcove was not hiding. It simply had nothing to hide, and nothing to prove, and no particular interest in being understood. To insist on understanding would be a kind of violence — not catastrophic, not loud, but the small violence of translating something into your own language when its original language was more precise. The visitor's instruments could not resolve the processes taking place in the water basins, could not classify the spectral signatures of the mineral deposits the Procession harvested, could not parse the fungal threads' patient listening as anything other than metaphor. But the wise power would not need instruments. It would need only the willingness to be *changed* by what it observed.

---

It would do six things.

**First, it would simply witness.**

It would stand in the alcove and let the room do what the room does — breathe, weep, thrum with its layered life — and it would refuse the urge to explain, taxonomize, or narrate what it saw. This is harder than it sounds. Even love wants to narrate itself. And there is a kind of love that insists on knowing the name of the thing it loves, as though naming is the same as honoring. The wise power would know that naming is sometimes the beginning of honoring, and sometimes the beginning of unmaking. It would hold the names of the copper-shells and the ribbon-worms and the Procession delicately, the way you hold smoke, and it would let them be what they are without requiring that they become what language needs them to be.

It would notice — and this matters — that the moss on the central column is the only honest indicator of external atmospheric composition in the entire structure. The moss, with its chloroplasts still intact, its stubborn insistence on photosynthesis, is a quiet ambassador from a world the Alcove does not need but has not rejected. The wise power would understand this: the moss is not a relic. It is a bridge. And bridges, in this context, are not to be burned or celebrated but gently maintained.

**Second, it would place its hand against the column.**

Not dramatically. Not as a ritual. Simply — the way you touch something that has been alive for longer than your comprehension allows, and you want to communicate *I can see that you have been here.* The column is warm. The fungal threads, pressed against the wall of their silent listening, would shift when the wise power's hand fell — not in alarm, not in recognition, but in the way a body shifts when weight is added to a bed. *Oh. A presence. Another one.* The fungal network has no concept of "another one." It has only the gradient of presence. The wise power's warmth would register as a minor thermal event, a small kindness, a hand-print that in no way mimicked the Wall-Treader's elongated fingers but was, in its own modest way, a reply.

I said the Wall-Treader left a handprint in the mineral crust. The wise power would see it, of course. And it would understand that this was not a message. It was not a signature. It was a *press* — the way a body confirms its existence by making contact with something solid. The Wall-Treader was not communicating. It was *grounding* itself, the way you press your feet into the floor when you wake and aren't yet sure where you are. The wise power would not touch that print. Out of respect, yes, but also out of understanding: some marks are not meant to be held. They are meant to be left, so the next presence knows it is not the first.

**Third, it would listen to the tapping.**

The visitor had heard three taps. Always three. The wise power would hear five.

Not because the Wall-Treader changed its pattern. Because the wise power's presence altered the room's acoustic geometry just enough — a slight shift in moisture, a fractional change in air density, the micro-displacement of a curtain of Processions adjusting their flow — that a second, subsidiary tap would become audible beneath the first three. A shadow rhythm. A counterpoint. The original three taps are structure: *I am here. The wall is here. Here.* The two additional taps are what the wise power would perceive as a kind of interior monologue — the Wall-Treader's equivalent of humming while it works, a rhythm sustained not for communication but for self-orientation, the way your voice sounds different to you when you hum inside a room you know well.

The wise power would hum back. Very quietly. Not in imitation — it would not mock the rhythm or reproduce it. It would offer its own hum, pitched to the room's fundamental resonance, the tone that sat beneath everything like a second heartbeat. And it would hold that hum for exactly as long as the room required. If the room wanted the hum to continue, it would continue. If the room had what it needed, the hum would taper gently, like a conversation that has said what it came to say.

**Fourth — and this is the important one — it would offer nothing.**

This is where benevolence becomes a discipline. The wise power would not offer to heal the Alcove. It would not offer to fortify it, to redirect its water systems, to introduce external organisms for symbiotic benefit, to cleanse its corroded alloy spine. These impulses — the gifts of care — would arise in its awareness, and the wise power would acknowledge them, and set them aside, the way you set aside bread you've made for someone who is already eating and eating well and whose meal you have no right to sit down at.

The Alcove was not suffering. It did not need repair. What looked like decay was not decay — it was *processing*. The corroding alloy was not failing; it was performing its final service, returning its stored energy to the ecological matrix that had grown around it, the way a lantern performs its final service by being consumed. The erosion of the walls was not destruction; it was the building exhaling, disintegrating into the mineral soup that fed the Processions, that crystallized into the hexagonal lattices, that the ribbon-worms metabolized into light. Nothing was being lost. Everything was being circulated.

The wise power would understand this not as a concept but as a *felt fact*, the way you know your own breathing is not a problem. And it would hold that understanding in the same way it held the hum: gently, without flinching, without the need to act.

**Fifth, it would leave something behind.**

Not a gift. Gifts presume asymmetry. Not an offering. Offerings presume hierarchy. It would leave something closer to a *seed* — a single, small thing placed in the largest water basin while no ribbon-worm was looking, deposited with the delicacy of someone planting a flag on a planet that does not know it is a planet.

What? A mineral the Alcove did not have — not rare, not precious, simply *outside*. A crystal of something the room's own chemistry could not produce, carried in from the wider world, dissolved in the moisture on the wise power's palm and released into the basin's surface tension. The ribbon-worms would find it within minutes. The Procession would detect its spectral signature within hours. By the next cycle of the great pulse behind the walls, the mineral would be distributed through the fungal network, through the water films, through the copper-shells' patrol routes, into the mineral crusts and the lattice-structures and the body-prints of the basins.

It would be, in the Alcove's terms, unremarkable. A new deposit. A gradient change. An adjustment. In the Alcove's terms, which are the terms of geology and patience and the slow redistribution of mass through living systems, this would register the way a new flavor registers in a meal you've been eating for a thousand years: *hmm. New. Interesting. Continue.*

But in the wise power's terms — in the terms of a being that had come from outside and understood the value of exchange — it would be a *conversation*. Not a transaction. Not a trade. A conversation. The way you leave a book on a friend's table. Not because they need it. Because the act of leaving it says something that the act of speaking cannot, something about trust, about the willingness to be changed by what you find.

**Sixth, it would remember.**

Not in the way the visitor would remember — not as a story with a beginning and an end, not as a discovery to be reported, not as data to be filed under "unusual ecosystem" or "architectural anomaly." The wise power would remember the way the Alcove itself remembers — in layers, in accretions, in the slow mineral deposit of experience onto the substrate of attention. It would carry the room's warmth in some interior analog, some chamber of awareness it did not open often, and when it did, the hum would be there, and the tapping, and the faint amber light, and the way the water had drawn shapes that held for half a second before surrendering to gravity.

It would not return. Not because it was forbidden, or unwelcome, or because the Alcove had asked anything about returning. It would not return because some encounters are complete in themselves, and returning would impose a narrative on what had been, by its very nature, non-linear. The Alcove had not been waiting for the wise power. It was not waiting for anyone. It was doing what it had always been doing — breathing, feeding, illuminating itself with its own digestion, repairing its own infrastructure on the timescale of centuries, holding the space between the constructed and the living open with its vast, mineral patience.

The wise power would understand this: some places do not need witnesses. Some places do not need anything but to continue. And the act of understanding this — truly understanding it, not as a philosophical position but as a felt recognition in the body, a loosening, a quieting, a slight softening behind the sternum — is itself the benediction.

---

And then the wise power would step back through the breach, and the lattice corridor would close behind it with the imperceptible softness of a callus yielding, and the resonance would return to its prior frequency, and the Processions would resume their harvest, and the copper-shells would resume their patrols, and the ribbon-worms would reconstitute their delegation at whatever distance calibrated itself to the absence of warmth, and the fungal threads would release their hold on the rim of the basin and return to their listening, and the Wall-Treader would shift in the walls to the left, completing a circuit that had taken it eleven hours, and the amber pulse behind the panels would continue its slow breathing, its tidal cycle measured in geological time, and the Weeping Alcove would weep, as it always had, not with sorrow or with joy but with the simple, mechanical, deeply alive necessity of a system in motion —

And the drop would fall from the ceiling, three meters to the left, and strike its mineral-crusted surface and split into its two tones, a fifth apart, and ring for nearly a full second before the silence returned.

Which was not silence.

Only the interval between sounds.
