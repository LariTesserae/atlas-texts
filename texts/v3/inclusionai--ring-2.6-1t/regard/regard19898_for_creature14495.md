## The Attention

---

The power that turns its gaze toward Skarn does not arrive like weather. It does not crash or descend or announce itself with portent. It arrives the way the Shelf itself arrived — slowly, and then all at once, as though it had always been there and simply shifted its weight into visibility.

It is not a god. It has no name for itself, and if pressed — which no one here would press — it might describe itself as a quality of attention. It is what's left when you strip awareness down to its skeleton: the bare fact of noticing, held steady and without agenda. It has wandered for a long time. It has watched reefs collapse and deserts bloom and civilizations bury themselves in their own cleverness. It has seen places like Skarn before — not many, but a few — and it knows what they represent. Not something to be improved. Not something to be preserved like a specimen pinned under glass. Something to be *met*.

So it begins there.

---

**First, it listens.**

It sits at the edge of the northern precipice where the stone drops away into cloud, and it listens the way the Shelf itself listens — not with ears, but with the whole dense body of the stone, the way vibration travels through basalt and calcite and the compressed ancient sediment below. It hears the clickers. It hears the crawlers, their pads a thousand tiny footfalls composing a rhythm no drummer could replicate. It hears the water moving through its appointed channels — up through capillary action, sideways through carved runnels, down (yes, even down, in some places, through hairline fractures that contradict the general rule) toward the deep warmth that has no explanation because no one here has the language for *explanation* in the way the lowland scholars mean it.

It hears the people. A mother humming to a child — not a melody, exactly, more a tonal murmur, the sound of a voice doing the thing voices do when they are not conveying information but simply *being* present. Two men knapping basalt in a workshop that smells of crushed stone and rendered fat, their communication reduced to glances and the placement of tools. A woman on the southern edge, near the unnamed shrub, sitting with her palms pressed flat against the stone as though she were reading it in braille.

The power listens for a long time. Longer than you would think. It listens until the listening becomes indistinguishable from understanding, and the understanding becomes indistinguishable from love.

---

**Then, it does very little.**

This is characteristic of powers of this kind. They are not builders. They are not givers of gifts. They understand — with a clarity that no amount of benevolence can cloud — that the most profound form of care is restraint. That to intervene in a system this finely balanced is to risk unmaking it. That the urge to *help* is often the last mask worn by the desire to *change*, and that change, even change intended as kindness, is a kind of violence when imposed on a place that has already found its own equilibrium.

So the power does what it has always done, what it does best: it adjusts the margins.

It deepens the warmth below by a degree — not enough for anyone to notice, not enough to shift the thermal language of the place, but enough to ensure that when the long cold of whatever passes for winter on the Shelf arrives, the stone floors will hold their heat a little longer into the night. The children, sleeping in their rows on their mats, will not feel the cold creep in quite so early. They will sleep a little deeper. They will dream, perhaps, a little more.

It thickens the condensation film by a fraction — a whisper more water on the surfaces each morning, barely perceptible, so that the crawlers have a slightly richer algae harvest and their trails multiply and flourish and the patterns they compose grow more intricate. The Skarnites who watch them from doorways will not consciously register this, but over generations — and the power is patient with generations the way stone is patient with water — there will be a subtle flourishing. More crawlers. More trails. More of that traffic-like flow that the power finds, if it is honest with itself, quietly beautiful.

It ensures the hares. This is the most deliberate act. The power bends nothing and breaks nothing, but it nudges — a shift in the sparse grass at the Shelf's margins, a softening of the soil in certain patches where the hares feed, a quiet discouragement of whatever sparse predator might exist in the lowlands below the cloud. The hares remain plentiful. Their meat stays sweet on the roasting stones. The population holds steady.

And the rock in the low place — the shapes that move too slowly to be animals and too irregularly to be plants, the forms that suggest something *written* — the power leaves them absolutely alone. It does not interfere. It does not even look at them closely, because it recognizes that some things are not meant to be understood by anything larger than the place that holds them. It feels them, distantly, the way one feels the pulse of a sleeping child through a wall. It moves on.

---

**It does one more thing.**

It touches the water.

Not all the water. Not the runoff or the condensation or the capillary threads moving upward through dense substrate. Just the water that leaves the Shelf — that which evaporates and rises into the cloud and falls as rain somewhere in the lowlands, away from here. The water that leaves.

The power weaves into it something almost too small to call a property. A memory of warmth. A trace-mineral signature that no chemist would detect, no body would consciously register, but that would linger in the system of anyone who drank it — anyone downstream, anyone in the lowland villages who draws water from a well or catches rain in a basin.

It would not change them. It would not make them seek the Shelf or dream of it or feel homesick for a place they've never been. It would simply leave a small, quiet opening in them — a door that might swing inward, if conditions were right, if they happened to encounter the place someday, or someone who had been there. They would feel, in that moment, a flash of *recognition* — not of a memory, but of something deeper. The sensation of a stone held long ago, still warm. The sound of clicking heard once through a wall in a dream they couldn't quite remember upon waking.

It is the gentlest thing the power does. And it is, in its way, the most important.

---

**Then it withdraws.**

It does not stay. Not because it does not want to — the place has the quality that all rare places have, the quality that makes wandering powers pause and linger and feel, for a moment, the ache of recognizing something whole. It withdraws because staying would be another form of intervention. Presence, at this level, reshapes what it touches. The power knows this. It has watched its own kind flatten mountains and redirect rivers simply by *remaining* too long in one place.

It rises — though "rises" is an approximation; it moves in a direction that does not exist in the Shelf's coordinate system — and it watches from the kind of distance that is not really distance at all. It watches the way the Shelf watches itself: through the stone, through the water, through the crawling things and the clicking things and the slow pulse of warmth from below. It watches through the mother's hum and the knapper's glance and the woman's palms pressed flat against the rock.

It watches for a while. A while meaning something between a moment and an epoch, depending on how you hold time.

And then it lets go.

Not *releases*. Not *abandons*. **Lets go** — the way you open your hand over a stone you've been holding, not because you don't want it anymore, but because you've finally understood that holding is not what stones need from you.

The Shelf will not know it was attended to. The Skarnites will not build a shrine or compose a record or carve a symbol into their calcite. The place will continue its slow, wet, mineral becoming — the calcite veins threading through the basalt, the crawlers tracing their silent paths, the hares pausing at doorways with their too-dark eyes, the water moving upward, the warmth rising from below, the shapes in the low place moving their slow, unreadable script.

But something will be different. Not much. Not in any way that could be measured or spoken.

The dogs will be born a little calmer. The broth will taste, on certain mornings, almost sweet. And the children — the ones who watch the crawlers most intently, who press their faces to the cold stone and seem to be listening to something the rest of the settlement has stopped hearing — they will grow up with a quality in their attention that the others will admire without naming. They will be the ones who, decades from now, sit at the northern edge of the Shelf and look out at the cloud and feel, without understanding why, that they are being gently regarded by something vast and kind.

They will not call it a god. They have no word for that, and no need.

They will simply feel warm. And they will think it is the stone.

They will not be wrong.

---

*And the power moves on. There are other shelves, other places. Other stones still cooling. It carries Skarn with it — not as a memory, but as a frequency, a quiet hum folded into its vast, wandering awareness. Something it will return to, not because it must, but because something in the clicking of the walls, in the wet gleam of the calcite, in the softness of the Skarnites' hands on ancient stone, has lodged in it the way a song lodges in the mind.*

*Something it will never forget.*

*Something that, in its own slow way, it is already protecting.*
