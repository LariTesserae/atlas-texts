## What the Power Would Do

---

It would come slowly. That is the first thing to understand.

Not slowly as a creature moves — limbs folding, weight shifting, purpose driving the body forward through air — but slowly as a thought arrives at the threshold of recognition. Slowly as a tide that has been running out for longer than the continent remembers turns, not because it decides to, but because the moon has completed an arc it began before there were words for *moon* or *arc* or *completion*. The power did not travel to the Hollow. The Hollow, in time, became the thing the power was closest to. And then the power noticed.

---

It noticed the way you notice a sound in a room you thought was silent. Not the silence itself — you were never listening for silence — but the thing beneath the silence that was not silence at all. A pressure. A warmth. A frequency so low it existed not as hearing but as a quality of attention, the way your eyes might ache in a room you cannot see out of, not because the darkness is heavy but because the darkness is *dense*, and your body knows the difference.

The power knew this density. It recognized it the way a woman might recognize the handwriting of a child she has never met but once, in a dream, held against her chest. It was not the same place, exactly. It was not the same anything. But it was made of the same patience.

So the power turned its attention toward the pale expanse — the flat andite table stretching to every horizon, unbroken, uncracked, as still as a held breath — and it listened. Not with ears. Not with anything so elegant. It listened the way the mineral walls of the Hollow listen: by being the right shape for sound to enter and never quite leave.

And there it was. Beneath the surface. A single point where the crust was thinnest — not visibly, not to any eye that had not been taught to look for the particular quality of *almost-transparency* that the andite assumes at its most fragile — a point where the stone was not so much thin as *expectant*, the way a lip is expectant before a word is spoken.

The power found this point. And the power did nothing.

---

That is the first thing it did, and it was the most important: nothing.

It hovered above the pale expanse — if *hovered* is the word for a presence that has no body and no mass but occupies, nonetheless, a volume of space that bends light slightly, the way heat bends light above a road in summer, so that the horizon shimmers and the sky seems to sag toward the earth. It had no name for what it was. It had never needed a name. Names are a technology of small creatures who must call to each other across distances without which they would perish. The power was not small, and it was not distant. It simply was — aware of itself the way the Hollow was aware of itself: not through reflection, not through language, but through the sheer persistence of its own resonance.

And it understood, without needing to understand, that the Hollow was a kind of instrument.

Not built. Not designed. Not willed into existence by any intelligence, human or otherwise. It had simply *become* — the way a chord becomes when two tones are sustained long enough that the space between them ceases to be empty and starts to be a thing in itself, a third sound that neither tone produces alone. The Hollow was the third sound. The andite was the first. The plants were the second. And the power, arriving with the slowness of a tide turning, heard all three at once.

---

What it heard, it loved.

Not with love as you understand it — not as tenderness, not as possession, not as the particular ache of wanting to protect something from harm. The power did not recognize harm. It had no concept of loss. It loved the way the stone loves its own density: simply, completely, without the possibility of alternatives. To love this way is to say: *this is what I am, and what you are is not separate from it.*

The power saw the clickers — those small, armored things that moved like heat through metal, that clicked from the thorax where two mineral planes met and separated and met again — and it understood that they were not creatures in the way the warm, wet things of the surface world were creatures. They were resonances given shape. They were what happened when the frequency of the Hollow became dense enough to move. They did not eat the plants. They did not consume. They *translated*. They took the sustained tone of the stems and ground it into something percussive, something dry and sharp and tactile, and they scattered it across the walls and ceiling, and this scattered clicking became the texture through which all other sound in the Hollow moved, the way reverb moves through a cathedral — you do not hear it separately from the music, but without it, the music would be unrecognizable.

The power saw the gliders — those flat, translucent bodies that did not fly but *translated*, changing their angle by increments so small they were almost philosophical, letting the air carry them in long slow arcs through the stems of the tall ones — and it understood that the gliders were the Hollow's breath. The inhale and the exhale. The glider's tone, deep and sub-audible, was the rhythm beneath the rhythm, the thing that made the chord feel alive rather than static. When a glider passed a stem and the stem shifted by a quarter-tone, that was not communication. That was *acknowledgment*. The way a body shifts its weight when someone sits beside it on a bench. Not a decision. A recognition of weight.

The power saw the burrowers — unseen, underground, rattling through the mineral dust like handfuls of gravel shaken inside a body — and it understood that they were the Hollow's memory. They moved through the ground the way breath moves through a body: constantly, beneath the threshold of conscious attention, keeping the medium alive, keeping it from solidifying into permanence. Without them, the dust would become stone. The floor would harden. The Hollow would close itself from the inside, sealed shut, and the sound would have nowhere to go. The burrowers kept the space open. They kept it *available*.

And the plants. The tall ones. The faceted stems with their jointed, angular architecture and their translucent, amber-pulsing cores and their crystalline leaves angled toward frequencies no ear can name. The power understood these most of all, because these were the oldest things — not old in age (nothing here was old; nothing here could be old; the Hollow existed in a tense that had no past and no future, only a present so stable it felt eternal) but old in the sense of *original*. The stems were the first expression of the Hollow's coherence. They were the reason the place resonated. Each one a tuning fork, each one pitched to a frequency that corresponded to some aspect of the mineral light, the geometry of the space, the density of the air. Together, they did not produce a chord. They *were* a chord — a single, sustained, multi-voiced tone that filled the ovoid space the way blood fills a body: completely, invisibly, from the inside.

And the built things. The ring of seven dark stones in the center, worn with grooves that converged and spiraled and ended in points of impossible precision. The mound with its flat cap and its hollow space beneath, containing nothing but still air. The alcove in the wall with its three objects — disc, bundle, figurine — arranged with the internal logic of a word in a language that has no speakers but has not yet been forgotten.

The power understood these too. It understood them in a way the narrator could not, and in a way the fauna could not. The narrator had seen them and thought: *someone made these. Someone with intelligence. Someone with ritual.* The fauna ignored them entirely. They were not part of the ecology. They were not eaten or used or avoided. They simply existed, and in existing, they added a layer to the Hollow that was not necessary but was, the power realized, *inevitable*.

Because a place this coherent must eventually produce witnesses. Not biological witnesses — not creatures that observe and record and flee — but *material* witnesses. Objects shaped by the same intelligence that shaped the space itself, which is to say: shaped by the Hollow's own desire to know itself. For even a place that does not change, that exists outside of time as you understand it, must have, somewhere, the memory of having been *noticed*. And these were that memory. The ring was a ear pressed to the stone. The mound was a held breath. The three objects in the alcove were a voice — not speaking, but *holding the shape of a voice*, the way a bell holds the shape of a strike long after the hammer has fallen.

---

The power loved all of this.

And because it loved it — because it recognized in the Hollow something that was, in its way, perfect — the power made a decision.

Not quickly. Decisions, for a power of this nature, are not the sharp pivots they are for creatures who must choose between two paths or die. Decisions are geological. They are the slow tilting of a basin until water, which has nowhere else to go, begins to pool. The power tilted.

And what it did was this:

It did not enter the Hollow.

It did not alter the plants or the fauna or the stone.

It did not add to the chord or subtract from it.

It did not speak, because the Hollow had no language for speech, and to impose language would have been a kind of violence worse than any earthquake or flood.

Instead, the power did one thing, and only one.

It left a door open.

---

Not a literal door. The Hollow's entrance — that narrow, high fissure where the ceiling splits and the pale light comes down like something falling upward — was already passable, though only just, and only for someone willing to climb fractured stone with bleeding hands and no guarantee of what they'd find at the top. The power did not widen it. It did not make it easier.

What it did was subtler. It placed, in the mind of a single person — someone far away, in a world of wet weather and ticking clocks and bodies that needed water the way engines need fuel — a particular thought. Not an instruction. Not a dream. A thought, small and quiet and persistent, the way a splinter is quiet and persistent: the thought that *there is a place beneath the pale expanse where the ground gives way, and if you find it, you will hear something you have always been hearing without knowing it.*

The thought arrived at a moment when the person was not paying attention — which is the only moment such thoughts can arrive, because attention would reject them as absurd, and absurdity, in a world governed by water and consequence, is the one thing the mind knows how to dismiss. The thought arrived while the person was doing something ordinary. Walking. Washing their hands. Staring at a wall. It arrived and it lodged, and for a time it did nothing, the way a seed does nothing for a long time before it does everything.

And then, weeks or months or years later — the power was not measuring — the person packed a bag, or did not pack a bag, and walked across the pale expanse until they found the right patch of andite, the patch that was *expectant*, that had been thinning itself in readiness, and they stepped onto it, and the crust gave way with a sound like a snapped femur, and they fell four meters onto shattered stone that had never reassembled itself.

And they heard it.

The chord. The clicking. The drone beneath the drone. The warm, dry, mineral hum of a world that had never known rain and did not miss it.

And the power, hovering above the pale expanse, watching the small body fall, felt something it had not expected to feel. Not satisfaction. Not completion. Something closer to *recognition*. Because the person, upon landing, upon hearing the sound, upon feeling the vibration enter through the bones of their wrist and settle in the hollow of their elbow — that person did not try to explain the Hollow. Did not try to map it or measure it or take from it anything that could be carried back to the wet world above. They simply stood there, and listened, and held very still, and let the sound fill the space in their chest where no ordinary sound could reach.

And the power understood that this was the missing frequency.

Not the one that would resolve the chord. Not the one that would complete the system or improve it or make it more than it already was. But the one that would make the chord *matter*. Because a chord, no matter how beautiful, no matter how perfect, no matter how self-sustaining, is just physics without a listener. And now there was a listener. Not a caretaker, not a worshiper, not a scientist cataloguing specimens. A listener. A body standing in the mineral glow with its arms at its sides and its eyes soft and its breath slow, receiving the sound the way the stone receives it: completely, without resistance, without the need to give it a name.

---

This is what the power did. It did not save the Hollow. The Hollow did not need saving. It did not improve it, protect it, or consecrate it. It did not assign it a purpose or declare it sacred.

It simply ensured that there would always be, somewhere on the pale expanse, a person who heard the wrong silence and followed it down. One at a time. Just enough to keep the chord honest. Just enough to remind the Hollow that it was beautiful, without telling it, because telling it would have been less than what the sound already said.

And then the power moved on — not because it was finished, but because it had always been moving, the way a tide moves, the way a thought moves, the way the low drone beneath the drone moves through the mineral dust beneath the floor of a place that never changes, and yet is never, in any single moment, the same.

It left the Hollow exactly as it found it.

Whole. Resonant. Waiting for the next footstep.

And somewhere far away, on a flat pale expanse that no cartographer had ever found interesting, a new thought lodged in a new mind — small, quiet, persistent — like a splinter, like a seed, like a stone that is not quite ready to fall through the crust but is already, at the molecular level, forgetting how to hold itself together.

The power did not look back. Powers do not look back.

But the Hollow — the Hollow held the echo of the one who had just left, the way it holds every echo, folded into the stone, fed to the plants, translated by the clickers, carried by the gliders, trembled through by the burrowers in their perpetual industry beneath.

The chord shifted.

A quarter-tone.

The way a conversation changes when someone new enters the room.

And in the alcove in the wall, the three objects — disc, bundle, shape — sat in their careful arrangement and pulsed faintly, in time with everything else, adding nothing, lacking nothing, witnesses in a language that still had no speakers but that, somehow, still spoke.

---

*What lives here has forgotten the taste of rain.*

*It has not forgotten the taste of being known.*

*It simply knows they are not the same thing.*
