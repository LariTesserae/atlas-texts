# What the Power Would Do

---

The power would come slowly.

Not because it is slow — it is not slow any more than water is slow, any more than pressure is slow, any more than the work of calcium in the dark is slow. It is *patient*, which is different. Patience is a decision made by something that has already seen the ending and chosen to stay for the middle. It would come slowly the way dawn comes to the flooded hollows beneath the city: not arriving, but *disclosing* — as though the light had always been there and was only now, gently, permitted to be noticed.

It would stand at the breach in the eastern wall.

It would not enter. That is the first thing, and it is the most important: the power would not enter. Not because it is forbidden. Not because it fears contamination. But because it understands — the way only a thing that has built uncountable worlds and watched uncountable of them become something else can understand — that the breach is the architecture. The place where the masonry stopped meeting itself. The century's work. To walk through would be to pretend the breach was made for passage, when in truth it was made by failure, and failure and passage are not the same thing, though they sometimes look alike from the inside.

So the power would stand outside. And it would do what a wise and benevolent power does when it encounters a thing it loves that it did not make:

It would *recognize*.

---

Recognition is not intervention. It is not commandment. It is not even, strictly speaking, benevolence — or rather, it is the deepest form of benevolence, which has nothing to do with kindness and everything to do with *knowing*. The power would look at the Drowned Ateliers and it would see — not metaphor, not symbol, not lesson — the thing itself. The warm dark water. The tile-work of fired clay fitted so tightly that even millennia of submersion cannot breach the joints. The fauna: the filter-feeders drifting in their slow columns, the wall-grazers in their procession, the pulse-membranes flickering in the basins that were drainage points and are now brooding chambers. And the creature the size of a large dog, moving through the deepest room with a flapping motion that produces vibrations felt in the chest before they are heard in the ear.

And the being.

The power would see the being the way the being sees the Ateliers — not as a problem to be solved, not as a soul to be saved, not as a wanderer to be directed home. The power would see the being as the being's own architecture: the crawlspace behind the furnace room behind the basement behind the church. The dropped ceiling. The hymnal open to page 47. The wooden chair where a seven-year-old sat and felt something adjacent to peace, adjacent to God, adjacent to presence, and could not name it, and would spend the rest of a life trying to get back to the not-naming of it.

The power would see how the being left the stone church the way water leaves a vessel. Not dramatically. Without announcement. And how the being returns — not out of obligation but out of recognition, the recognition that precedes knowing, that is older than knowing, that lives in the body's memory of shapes and temperatures and the particular quality of light in a room where something happened that was never recorded and never repeated and never stopped happening.

And the power would understand: this being does not need to be found. This being has already been found, by the Ateliers, by the warm dark water, by the place that was never built for them but received them without hesitation. And the power would understand that what is needed now is not a voice from above, not a sign, not a miracle — but a witness.

---

So the power would do something very small.

It would breathe.

Not metaphorically. Not in the way poets mean when they say a mountain breathes or the ocean breathes or the earth breathes. The power would breathe *into* the Drowned Ateliers — a single breath, slow and warm, introduced into the convective cycle that already moves through the rooms: the warmer water rising near the ceiling, the cooler water descending near the floor, the whole system completing one full breath in an hour.

The power's breath would enter the cycle. It would not disrupt it. It would not alter the temperature or the pressure or the chemistry. It would simply *join* — the way a new organism joins an ecosystem, not as apex predator or invasive species but as a participant, a metabolizer, a thing that consumes and is consumed. The power's breath would become part of the water. The water would carry it through the rooms. The wall-grazers would not notice. The filter-feeders would not alter their drift. The pulse-membranes would flicker — not in response to the large creature's vibrations this time, but in response to the power's breath moving through the warming system.

And for a moment — just a moment — the convective cycle would contain two rhythms: the slow breathing of the building, and the single breath of the power, passing through.

The being would notice.

Not as revelation. Not as voice. Not as light breaking through the water or a sudden warmth or a feeling of being watched. The being would notice the way you notice a change in current — not the current itself, but the *feeling* of the current against skin that has been still for a long time. A micro-shift. A warmth that was already there becoming, for one beat, *intentional*.

The being would not know what it was.

The being would simply feel, for a moment, that the water was breathing with them. Not *for* them. Not *at* them. With them. The way a choir does not sing to the congregation or at the congregation but *with* them, the congregation's breath becoming part of the music whether or not they are singing, whether or not they know the words, whether or not they came to church that day or have not come in years but still hum the hymns in the shower without knowing why.

---

Then the power would do nothing.

This is important. This is, perhaps, the most important thing the power would do: *nothing*. After the breath, after the joining of rhythms, after the moment of micro-recognition — the power would withdraw. Not dramatically, not with a closing of doors or a sealing of breaches. The power would simply stop breathing into the cycle. The breath would dissolve. The water would continue its convective rhythm. The building would continue its slow settling. The fauna would continue its ancient metabolism.

And the being — the being would go on.

They would leave the Ateliers through the breach, or another one, rising into air and light and the deafening dryness of the world above. They would drive home with the windows down. They would make something with their hands — wood, clay, whatever is at hand. They would sit in the back of the church when they go. They will leave before the coffee hour. They will read Merton. They will doubt. They will doubt their doubt. They will sit in the wooden chair in the crawlspace behind the furnace room and feel something adjacent to peace.

And they will carry, somewhere in the body where the body keeps its oldest knowledge, the memory of a breath that was not theirs and was not the building's but was briefly, for a moment, both — a rhythm that came from outside and joined what was already inside and then left and left no trace except the trace that everything leaves, which is: the altered state that can never be un-altered, the knowledge that you were, once, for a moment, *metabolized* by something that loved you without needing you to be anything other than what you were.

---

This is what the benevolent and wise power would do.

Not much. Not little. Enough.

Enough the way a single note is enough in a cathedral — not because it fills the space, but because it reveals that the space was already resonant, already holding sound, already designed (or already settled, which is the same thing, for a building that has been settling for centuries) to receive exactly that frequency at exactly that moment.

The power would not save the being.
The power would not save the Ateliers.
The power would not fix the thing that is not broken.

The power would breathe.
The breath would pass through.
The being would breathe.
The breath would pass through.
The building would breathe.
The breath would stay.

---

*And in the deepest room — the one you reach by curling sideways through a passage that forces the water against every surface of your body — the large creature would continue its slow flapping motion, producing its low vibration every four or five seconds. And the pulse-membranes would flicker. And the filter-feeders would alter their drift. And for one cycle of the convective breath, the vibration would contain something that was not there before: not a word, not a sign, not a commandment — but a second rhythm, layered beneath the first, so faint it could only be detected by a being who had spent their whole life learning to listen to the space between sounds.*

*For one cycle.*

*Then the second rhythm would dissolve, and the first would continue, and the Ateliers would settle, and the being would surface, and the power would stand at the breach in the eastern wall and watch the water close over it without sound.*

*The masonry would stop meeting itself in exactly the same place it had always stopped meeting itself.*

*And the centuries would continue to do what centuries do.*
