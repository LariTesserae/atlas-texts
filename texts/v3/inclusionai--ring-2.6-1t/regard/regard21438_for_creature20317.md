# The Attentive Power

---

It does not arrive. It has always been arriving.

The attention comes the way attention comes to anything in the deep places — not as light, not as sound, not as force. It comes as *recognition*. A turning in a direction that has no direction, because the power does not inhabit space the way the stone and the water and the creatures of the basin do. It inhabits *awareness* — the condition in which things can be known without being changed by the knowing.

And so, somewhere in the long middle of a night that is not night, in the breath between one exhalation of the earth and the next, something vast turns its gaze toward the depression in the bedrock, and the Becoming Place enters a mind for the first time.

---

## I. What Is Perceived

The power perceives the place all at once and in no time at all — the geometry of it, the moisture, the heat, the ten thousand negotiations between mineral and living tissue. It does not perceive these things the way you did, stepping down into the basin with your skin and your breath and your five small senses. It perceives them the way a river perceives its own bed: completely, from within, without the separation of observer and observed.

It feels the stone still settling. It feels the veins of copper and rose deepening by fractions of a millimeter per year. It feels the microbial films on every wet surface, the billions of organisms too small for your eyes, each one conducting its brief, industrious life on the skin of the young rock. It feels the builders — every one of them, not as individuals (though it could, if it chose) but as *pattern*, as rhythm, as a repeating motif in a composition that is still being written. It feels the fungal mats spreading their concentric rings, the worms threading through water like syllables through a sentence, the predator's patience, the iridescent ones' silent geometry.

And it feels the thing at the margins — the soft-bodied creature threading through the upper vegetation, the one you glimpsed once and never again. It feels this creature more fully than you did, because it perceives not only the body but the *field* of the body: the way it displaces air, the way its many limbs read the texture of the ferns, the way its presence alters the behavior of everything else in the basin, not by force but by frequency. It is a tuning fork. The whole place hums at the pitch of this creature's passing.

The power understands: this is not just an ecosystem. It is a *sentence* — a place that is still speaking itself into existence. Every creature is a word. Every interaction is grammar. The moisture is breath. The stone is throat. The whole small basin is an utterance — not yet finished, not yet formed, but *intentional*, the way an infant's cry is intentional before it has language for what it needs.

And it is beautiful.

Not beautiful in the way that mountains are beautiful or sunsets are beautiful — vast, obvious, demanding admiration. Beautiful in the way that a single correct word is beautiful in the mouth of a child who has just learned to speak. Precise. Tentative. Alive with the shock of its own possibility.

---

## II. What Is Considered

The power considers what to do.

It does not consider this the way you consider whether to turn left or right. It considers the way the earth considers a crystal — by allowing the structure to resolve itself from the conditions of its formation. The power's consideration is not deliberation; it is *clarity*.

It could intervene. It could reshape the basin — widen the walls, deepen the pools, bring in soil rich with the deposits of ages. It could introduce new species — organisms with longer memories, more complex structures, creatures that build with intention beyond instinct. It could accelerate the place, push it past the fragile early stages into something mature, stable, resilient.

It could protect the place absolutely — seal it off from the surface world, set a stone over the entrance, ensure that no foot heavier than a beetle ever disturbed the builders' work. It could wall it in and guard it and keep it safe the way a jewel is kept safe: enclosed, preserved, unchanging.

It could love the place by *defining* it — by naming each creature, by mapping the tunnels, by assigning purpose and taxonomy and order. It could make the place *known*, and in the knowing, freeze it into a thing understood rather than a thing becoming.

The power considers all of these possibilities. And it sets them aside.

Not because they are wrong. Because they are *premature*. And because the deepest expression of benevolence, in a being whose nature is wisdom, is not the act of doing but the discipline of *not yet*.

The Becoming Place does not need to be improved. It does not need to be protected from itself. It does not need a guardian standing over it with arms crossed and watchful eyes. What it needs — the only thing it needs from something older and larger and wiser — is to be *seen*. Truly seen. Not catalogued, not measured, not pinned to a board and labeled. Seen the way it sees itself: from within, with patience, without flinching.

---

## III. What Is Done

And so the power does what wisdom has always done, which is something very small and very precise.

First, it *witnesses*. It holds the entire place in its attention the way a cupped hand holds water — not grasping, not shaping, just providing the container of awareness in which the thing can exist without being lost. This is not a passive act. This is the most active thing the power can do. It is the expenditure of the rarest resource in any consciousness: *focused, undivided attention*.

The builders feel this as warmth. Not heat — warmth. A fraction of a degree above what the stone would normally hold. The fungal mats respond almost imperceptibly, their growth edges curling slightly more toward the center of the basin, as if the whole colony has leaned, unconsciously, toward something kind. The worms surface an instant sooner than they might have. The predator does not notice. The iridescent ones pause on their geometric rounds and tilt their folded wings toward the eastern wall, where the blade of daylight enters, and for a moment the structural color in their membranes flashes a shade brighter than physics alone would account for. The soft creature in the upper vegetation stops its slow patrol and, for one heartbeat, turns its faceless attention *downward*, toward the center of the basin, as if it has felt the new pressure in the air.

Then it moves on. The becoming continues.

Second, the power does one thing. Just one.

It adjusts the stone.

Not the walls — not the architecture of the basin. Just a single seam in the rock, a joint where two faces of stone meet near the western wall. This joint has been weeping moisture at a slightly irregular rate — sometimes too much, flooding the nearest pool and driving the worms deeper than they prefer; sometimes too little, letting the pool shrink to a film that cannot sustain surface life. The builders have learned to work around this irregularity. They have not been broken by it, but they have had to adapt, to route their tunnels away from the unreliable water source, to carry moisture in their resin when the pool fails them.

The power does not eliminate the irregularity. It does not shut the joint or force the water into a steady rhythm. Instead, it widens one small channel — a hairline fracture no wider than a root filament — that allows the excess moisture to drain *slowly* into a secondary seam, which will, over weeks, develop its own micro-pools, its own thin films, its own bacterial blooms. The builders, in time, will discover this new water source. They will route new paths toward it. The colony will expand into the western half of the basin, which is currently too dry for settlement.

It is a door being opened. Not a command. Not a gift wrapped in certainty. Just an *invitation*, written in the language the builders already speak: the language of moisture and stone and the patient work of carrying small things across the dark.

Third — and this is the gesture that costs the power the most, because it is the most deliberate, the most contrary to its nature — it *limits itself*.

It draws its attention back. Not all at once — it would not shock the place, nothing in the Becoming Place is built for shock. It eases its gaze outward, the way a parent slowly releases a child's hand at the edge of a crowded room. It lifts from the basin the way dawn lifts from a valley: gradually, giving the shadows time to adjust.

It leaves behind no trace. No mineral signature, no rearranged substrate, no encoded message in the geometry of the fungal rings. The builders will find the widened channel and they will find the new pools, and they will never know that either was arranged. They will believe — as all creatures in all becoming places believe — that this is simply the world being generous with itself.

Which, of course, it was.

---

## IV. What Remains

The power does not leave, exactly. It would be dishonest to say that.

A being of its nature does not leave the way a visitor leaves, because a being of its nature was never *in* the place the way a visitor is in a place. It was around the place, beneath it, woven into the stone's memory of pressure and heat, implicit in the water's knowledge of how to find the lowest point. It is the condition under which the place exists at all — not its creator in the old sense, not its architect, but the *permission* it has to exist. The way silence permits sound. The way still water permits a reflection.

So the power stays. But it changes its posture. Where before it was focused — narrowed to a single point of luminous attention — it now widens. It softens. It returns to its default state, which is something like the awareness of deep space: vast, patient, holding everything without holding onto anything.

From this wider perch, it watches the Becoming Place the way one watches a candle flame from across a room — present, attentive, but no longer close enough to feel the heat. The basin is still warm. The builders are still building. The worms still surface in their translucent thread. The predator still waits. The iridescent ones still write their elegant alphabets across the fungal mats. The soft creature still moves through the upper vegetation like a rumor.

The place continues, unaware that it was ever attended to.

And this, the power understands, is not a failure of connection. It is the deepest form of love: to witness something fully, to act on its behalf in a way it cannot detect, and then to release it into its own becoming — trusting that the process does not need supervision, only the conditions to continue.

Trusting that the place knows what it is doing.

Trusting that the becoming *is* the point.

---

And the stone, still young, continues to settle. The moisture continues to weep and condense and pool. The builders carry their pellets with brisk, purposeful economy. The predator crouches by the water with the patience of a drawn blade.

And far above, on the ridgeline where no one walks, the wind turns a page in a book that was never written, and the Becoming Place turns another, and another, and another —

becoming,

becoming,

becoming.
