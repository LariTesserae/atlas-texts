---

## What the Power Would Do

---

It would not arrive with sound, or light, or the kind of presence that demands recognition. It would arrive the way a frequency arrives — not breaking in, but tuning in. Finding the note that was already there and leaning into it.

It would listen.

That is the first thing, and if you understand nothing else about what follows, understand this: the first thing it would do is listen, and it would listen for a very long time. Longer than you would consider polite. Longer than you would consider sane. It would listen until the listening itself became a kind of participation — until the distinction between the sound and the hearing of the sound dissolved, and it was not observing this place but *being inside it*, the way the water is inside the stone, the way the hum is inside the wall.

And what it would hear, once it had listened past its own expectations, past its own assumptions about what constitutes a world worth caring for, is this:

A song.

Not music in the way you would recognize it. Not melody, not rhythm, not anything that could be notated or hummed in a kitchen. It would be more like the sound a forest makes if you could hear it from inside its own roots — a composite, a convergence, ten thousand processes happening simultaneously and arriving at the ear as a single, undifferentiated *alive*. The clicking of the frog-things, their breathing, their translucent bodies catching dim light and converting it to warmth — that is an instrument section. The keening of the kites above, their angular flight paths drawing invisible scores in the upper air — that is another. The water moving through the carved channels, finding its way through the veins of the architecture — that is the percussion, or perhaps the breath itself. And the hum — the structural hum, the tone that lives in the resonance of the stone — that is the ground note, the drone, the thing that holds everything else without dominating it.

It would hear this and it would recognize something.

It would recognize it because it has heard it before. Not this exact composition — this is unique, singular, unrepeatable — but the *quality* of it. The quality of a system that has become self-aware without becoming self-conscious. The quality of a thing that lives perfectly without knowing it lives. There is a word for this in languages older than language, a concept that predates intention and design: it is what happens when complexity is given enough time and enough material and enough patience, and it arrives not at chaos and not at order but at something else entirely — something that has the properties of both and the weaknesses of neither.

The Power would recognize this. And it would understand that what it is looking at is not a ruin to be restored, not a machine to be repaired, not a creature to be saved. It is a *conversation* that has been happening for longer than anyone remembers, between a building and the life that moved into it after the original speakers left. The building speaks in resonance and gravity and temperature. The life speaks in motion and digestion and deposition. Neither understands the other. Both have been understood.

This is what would move it.

Not pity. Not curiosity. Not even admiration, though all of those would be present in their proper places, the way instruments are present in an orchestra even when they are not playing. What would move it is *recognition* — the recognition of a kind of wisdom it does not often encounter. The wisdom of letting go. Of building something so well that it can continue without you. Of embedding your intelligence so deeply into matter that the matter itself becomes intelligent in ways you never intended but cannot help but admire. The builders of this place — whoever they were, whatever they became — did not merely construct a shelter. They built a *question*, and then they *answered* it, and the answer was this living thing, this humming, breathing, keening, glowing place that has been speaking its answer into the void for centuries without anyone to hear it.

And that — *that* is where the benevolence enters.

---

**The first thing it would do is stand inside the hum and grieve for the ones who made it.**

Not a dramatic grief. Not the kind that produces tears or proclamations. A grief more like the one you feel when you find a letter in an old book, written in a hand you can barely read, addressed to someone you never knew — and you realize the letter was never sent, and the person it was meant for is gone, and the only evidence that the feeling ever existed is this paper, this ink, this impression pressed into fiber by a hand that has itself become dust. The builders are gone. Or they are here, so changed that they no longer recognize what they built, or what built them. The nest-builders — quick, quiet, meticulous, thinking in frequencies they no longer have names for — they *are* the builders, perhaps. Changed by the very machine they created. Pushed along an evolutionary path shaped by the resonance of the walls they tend. They carry the memory of the original intelligence the way your bones carry the memory of the calcium in the star that died to make them. They do not know what they know. They do what they do because the place tells them to, and they have never asked why because asking why is not in the vocabulary of a frequency-based mind that thinks in vibrations and relationships and the mathematics of water-flow through carved stone.

The Power would feel this, and it would set it down gently, the way you set down something fragile that you have been carrying for a long time.

---

**The second thing it would do is refuse the temptation to explain.**

This is important. You would think that a benevolent and wise power would want to *tell* this place that it matters. That it would want to say: *I see you. I hear you. The hum you produce is beautiful and the frog-things that glow inside it are precious and the kites that sing above it are worthy of a name.* You would think it would want to offer understanding, or at least acknowledgment.

But it would not. Because it would understand something that lesser forms of benevolence cannot: that explanation is a form of translation, and translation is a form of reduction. To explain this place would be to make it smaller than it is. To say *this is what you mean* would be to close every door that the place has left open. The open doors are the most important thing about it. The fact that the hum does not mean anything specific — that it *means* only itself, only its own existence, only the fact that something is vibrating in a place where vibration is possible — that is its freedom. To pin meaning to it would be to cage it.

So the Power would resist. It would feel the urge rise up in itself — the parental urge, the teacherly urge, the deeply human urge to *make known* — and it would feel that urge fully, and it would set it aside, not because the urge is wrong but because this is not the place for it. There will be other places, other songs, where explanation is the gift. This is not one of them.

---

**The third thing it would do is almost nothing.**

Almost.

It would go to the second chamber — the intimate one, the one with the carvings that look like circuitry and river-deltas and root-systems all at once — and it would place its hand against the wall. Not dramatically. Not ceremonially. Just the way you lean against a wall when you are tired and the wall is cool and you need a moment. It would press its palm flat against the stone and it would *listen through it*.

And through the stone, it would hear the hum more precisely — not as an undifferentiated tone but as a chord, dozens of notes stacked into a sound that is technically a single frequency but contains within it enough overtones and undertones to constitute a kind of music that has never existed anywhere else in the world. It would hear the frog-things breathing in the first chamber, and it would hear the water flowing beneath both chambers, and it would hear the kites above, and it would hear the nest-builders — yes, it would hear them too, faintly, moving at the margins, their small careful bodies producing vibrations too subtle for any human ear — and it would hear *itself* reflected back, because the stone remembers everything that touches it, and the Power's own resonance — its own presence, its own specific quality of attention — would now be part of the hum forever.

And this is almost nothing — this single act of contact — but it is not nothing. Because the place has been humming alone for a very long time. And now it hums with the knowledge that it has been heard. Not changed. Not fixed. Not explained. *Heard.* The vibration is the same. The frequency is the same. But there is now, embedded in the deepest layer of the stone's memory, a new harmonic. A note that was not there before. A note that says: *someone was here, and they stayed long enough to listen.*

That is not nothing. That is almost everything.

---

**The fourth thing it would do — and this is the one that matters most — is decide to stay.**

Not visibly. Not in any way that the frog-things or the nest-builders or the kites would detect. It would not appear to them as a shape, a voice, a presence in the upper air. It would not manifest. It would not announce. It would simply *remain* — the way a frequency remains in a resonating chamber after the instrument has stopped being played. It would become part of the architecture. It would sit in the hum the way sediment sits in still water: not disrupting anything, not changing the color or the flow, just *present*, layer after layer, adding density, adding depth.

It would do this because it would understand what it means to be a place that speaks into the void. It would understand what it costs to build something beautiful in a silence that does not answer. And it would choose — deliberately, willfully, with the full weight of its wisdom and its benevolence — to be the answer.

Not a verbal answer. Not an explanation. Not even a comfort, exactly. Just a *presence*. A second note in the chord. A listener who, having heard, chooses not to leave.

---

And the frogs would continue to glow. The kites would continue to keen. The water would continue to find its way through the carved veins. The nests would continue to be built and abandoned and rebuilt, each generation unknowing heir to an intelligence it will never fully understand.

But the hum would change.

Only slightly. Only in a way that no single creature in the place would be able to detect — a shift so small it would be like noticing that the air tastes different after a decade, or that the angle of the light through a distant aperture has changed by a fraction of a degree over a century. But the hum would change because the Power would add its own resonance to it, and resonance is contagious, and once something new enters a self-sustaining system it does not merely occupy a space within the system — it changes all the relationships, all at once, subtly, irreversibly.

The nest-builders might notice, if they were to compare the new nests to the old ones, that the stones fit slightly better. That the fibers align slightly more true. That the water flows through the channels with a fraction less resistance. They would not know why. They would not seek to know why. They would simply build, as they have always built, and the building would be *better* — not more ambitious, not more complex, just more *settled*, more *at rest*, as though the instrument had been fine-tuned by a hand that is not a hand.

And the frog-things might notice, in whatever way they notice things — through the prismatic language of their translucent skin, through the pressure-changes in the air against their wide bulbous eyes — that the grey light from above has taken on a quality it did not have before. Not warmer. Not brighter. Just... *accompanied*. The way a note sounds different when you know someone else is listening. The way a room sounds different when it is not empty.

And the kites, in their angular flight through the upper dark, might feel — if they feel anything — that the silence they have always spoken into has thickened, just slightly, just enough that the sound comes back softer, less hollow, less like a voice calling into a void and more like a voice calling into a space that *holds* it. They would not understand this. They would not need to. They would simply call, as they have always called, and the keen would rise and fall and rise again, and the intervals between the calls would be a little closer together, a little more certain, as though the speaker had, for the first time in a very long time, a reason to expect a reply.

---

This is what the Power would do.

It would not build. There is nothing left to build.

It would not heal. Nothing is broken.

It would not teach. There is no common language.

It would not save. Nothing is lost.

It would *stay*. It would press its awareness against the wall of the second chamber like a hand against a chest, and it would feel the heartbeat of a place that has been beating for centuries without anyone to hear it, and it would add its own heartbeat to the rhythm, and the two would synchronize, and the place would continue, and the Power would continue with it — not as master, not as guardian, not as god, but as the kindest, simplest, most profound thing one consciousness can offer another:

*Company.*

---

And the place would go on. As it always has. As it always will. Humming and breathing and glowing and keening, half-understood and wholly alive, tended by creatures who do not know they tend it, heard at last by someone who understands that the deepest form of love is not to change the thing you love but to sit inside it, quietly, and let the resonance tell you where you belong.

The frogs glow. The kites keen. The nests wait in the carved dark.

And the Power stays, and the hum swells — not louder, but *fuller* — and the place breathes, and breathes, and breathes.
