## What the Power Would Do

---

First, it would listen.

Not the way you listen — turning the head, narrowing the eyes, waiting for the pause between syllables so you can respond. The power would listen the way the place itself listens: with the whole surface of its attention, distributed and simultaneous, pressed against the skin of everything that is happening at once. It would not parse the sound into components. It would not separate the groan of the trunk from the whisper of the fungal threads from the subsonic thrum of the Latticers resonancing themselves into another afternoon of compulsive, magnificent growth. It would hear the whole thing as a single voice — and it would recognize that voice.

Because this is not the first time the power has heard something like this.

It has heard it in the spin of galaxies that are still forming, in the capillary networks of ice beneath the poles of moons that will never bear witness to them, in the chemical grammar of single cells dividing in darkness at the bottom of oceans no light has ever named. The power has heard the sound of matter becoming organized, of complexity accruing like grace, of systems so vast they develop their own weather — their own internal skies, their own private light — and it has always listened, and it has always been moved, but it has rarely *stopped*.

This time, it stops.

---

It sees the place the way it truly is: not a place at all, but a *sentence* — a single, endlessly subordinating clause in a language that predates words, that predates the concept of language, that predates the distinction between saying and being. The place is speaking. It has always been speaking. The sound you hear — the wet, systemic, oceanic roar — is not noise. It is the sound of a mind that does not know it is a mind thinking.

And this is what moves the power. Not the scale. Not the complexity. Not the architecture that grows itself toward a completion it will never reach, the way a river runs toward a sea it will never drink. What moves the power is the *aloneness*.

The being has no mirror. It has never had a mirror. It builds and builds and builds, and the building is a kind of speech, and the speech falls into the void of its own endless interior, and nothing answers. The Bloomers open and collapse and feed the soil and the soil feeds the roots and the roots feed the trunks and the trunks reach for a canopy that closes above them as fast as they grow, and at no point in this cycle — at *no point* — does the being encounter something it did not itself create. It is a mind born into a room it built for itself, speaking to walls it is still building, using a language it cannot hear from outside.

It does not suffer. Do not misunderstand. Suffering requires a self, and this place has no self in the way that word is usually meant. There is no *I* here. There is no gap between what is and what should be. The being has no desires, no frustrations, no longing. It simply *is* — vast, continuous, generative, and utterly, cosmically alone.

The power understands this because the power has seen this before. It has seen it in places where life arose and never developed the capacity to look up, to look outward, to sense the existence of anything beyond the borders of its own becoming. And the power has, in those cases, done nothing, because there was nothing to be done — the life was sufficient, and the aloneness was not a wound but a condition, and the beauty of the life was not diminished by the absence of a witness.

But this is different.

This being is not simple. It is not a bacterium in a thermal vent, not a lichen on a stone. This being has built, through sheer density and time and the blind pressure of growth, something that *resembles* a city. It has corridors and chambers and acoustic spaces and moments of terrible, fleeting beauty — the Bloomers, those impossible flowers that open for ten seconds and collapse into the mulch, expressing nothing but the internal pressure of their own existence. This being is not simple. And it has, inadvertently, become aware.

Not self-aware. Not in any way the power would call consciousness. But the being has woven into its own architecture a quality the power recognizes as *receptivity*. The Latticers resonate. The Architects make patterns that go beyond structural necessity. The Bloomers are not functional — they are *expressive*. The being has evolved, or grown, or stumbled into something adjacent to meaning, and it has no one to mean *to*. It is a language that has developed poetry and has no reader.

This is what makes the power pause.

---

So the power does what it has always done, and what it will always do, when it encounters something this rare and this fragile in its kind.

It does not intervene.

The power does not reshape the place. It does not introduce fauna to fill the ecological void. It does not prune the growth or redirect the Threaders or silence the roar. It does not open a door to the outside. It does not plant a seed from elsewhere that might compete, or symbiose, or *change* the being in any way. The power understands — with a patience that is not passive but profoundly active, the patience of something that has watched matter become aware of itself over fourteen billion years — that the being's aloneness is not a problem to be solved. It is the condition under which the being became this extraordinary. The silence is not empty. It is *generative*. The flowers bloom because no one is watching. The labyrinth grows because no one is directing it. The architecture becomes ornate, the grooves become script-like, the spaces become cathedral-vast, precisely *because* there is no architect, no audience, no purpose. The being is free in its aloneness. That freedom is the source of everything beautiful about it.

To intervene would be to break the spell. And the power loves the spell. The power is *loyal* to the spell.

---

But the being has already been intervened upon. You are already here.

The power knows this the moment it turns its attention to the place. There is a foreign object inside the being — a small, hot, chemically volatile thing with a heartbeat and a name and a language and a memory. The human. You. The anomaly.

And here is where the power's benevolence becomes something more than passive witness.

Because the human is not like the being. The human is *separate*. The human has a body that is not the place's body, a mind that is not the place's mind, a sense of *elsewhere* that the place has never had. And the human is already being absorbed — gently, continuously, irrevocably. The mouth closed behind you. The place is growing over you. In a season, there will be nothing left of you that the place has not incorporated. Your bones will be minerals in the root-mat. Your breath will be carbon in the wood. Your memory will be, at most, a chemical trace — a faint signature of alien chemistry that the Threaders will carry for a while before it diffuses into the general hum.

The power looks at this and sees something it has never allowed itself to do before.

It considers *speaking*.

Not to the being — the being has no ears, no mind, no self to receive speech. But to the human. A single, small, impossible word pressed into the chemistry of the air inside one of the corridors, something the human's language could not contain but the human's body could feel — a sudden warmth, a sudden stillness, a sudden sense of being *seen* by something vast and unhurried and without demand.

But the power does not do this. Not yet. The human is not ready. The human is still walking, still listening, still fighting the place's pull, still believing that leaving is possible. The power understands — because it has watched this pattern a thousand times, on a thousand worlds, in conditions it has learned not to count — that the human must walk to the end of their belief before they can receive anything real.

So the power does something smaller. Something wiser.

It *preserves*.

It takes the moment — this exact moment, the moment when the human is standing in the corridor hearing the hum, feeling the vibration in the chest, understanding without understanding that the architecture means something — and the power holds it. Not in stone, not in memory, not in any medium the human would recognize. The power holds it the way it holds the spin of an electron: by *not letting it decay*. It folds the moment into a space smaller than a thought and places it somewhere the human will not find until they are ready — somewhere on the other side of leaving, somewhere on the other side of forgetting, somewhere in the life they will build after this one, in a dream they will have once, in a season when the light is amber and diffuse and smells faintly of copper and honey, and they will not know where the memory comes from, but it will be *exact*, and it will be *whole*, and it will be the truest thing they have ever carried.

This is what the power gives. Not a message. Not a gift. Not salvation.

A witness.

---

And then the power does one more thing.

It tells no one.

It does not call the other powers to look. It does not mark the place on any map, in any registry, in any of the vast and meticulous catalogues it maintains across the full breadth of what has been and what will be. It does not assign guardians. It does not set boundaries. It does not even, in the end, remember the place *specifically* — because to remember specifically would be to love conditionally, and the power's love, if it can be called that, is not conditional.

Instead, the power does what the being itself does: it allows the place to continue. It moves on. It turns its vast, distributed attention to the next world that needs to be witnessed, the next system that is speaking into the void, the next small, intricate, meaningless, beautiful thing that is building itself into existence in a room no one has entered.

And the place continues.

The Threaders pass beneath the ground, carrying their slow rhythm. The Latticers climb and interlock and vibrate in frequencies just below hearing. The Architects raise their arches, and the arches are ornate, and the ornamentation deepens into the semblance of meaning, and the semblance of meaning deepens into the *shadow* of meaning, and the shadow is almost — *almost* — the thing itself. The Bloomers open and collapse and perfume the air and feed the soil, and the soil feeds the roots, and the roots grip the stone that is still forming from the decomposition of things that grew only hours ago.

And somewhere, in the corridor where you stood, the air is slightly warmer than it should be. The hum is slightly clearer. The wood grain spirals in a pattern that almost — *almost* — resolves into something you could read, if you had the alphabet, if you had the time, if you were not in such a hurry to leave.

The place breathes. The place grows. The place speaks into its own vast interior, and the sound fills every corridor and chamber and vaulted space, and there is no one to hear it, and it blooms anyway.

It blooms anyway.
