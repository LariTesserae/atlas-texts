## What the Wise Power Would Do

---

When the wise power arrives — and it does not arrive the way you arrive, with boots and a pack and the question of whether to turn back at the last ridge — it arrives the way weather arrives. Not summoned, not invited, but *called*, the way a cracked bell calls to the hand that knows how to hold it still. It hears the chord. It feels the displacement in the volume of the world. It steps into the clearing, and the first thing it understands — fully, without the stumbling approximation of language — is that it is late.

Not too late. *Late.* As in: the hour has already been set, the table already laid, the chair already pulled back from it. Whoever set the place did so a very long time ago, in a language that does not have a word for *waiting* because waiting implies the possibility of not arriving, and here — on the Shelf — arrival was never in question. Only recognition.

So the power does the first thing a wise and benevolent thing would do upon entering a place like this:

It *stops*.

Not because it is afraid. Not because it has been told to. It stops because stopping is the correct grammar of this place. The Gliders have been circling since before the power's attention turned in this direction, and they will continue after. The Hum-Things sing their root-notes into the water, and the water carries the notes downward, into the stone, into the dark — where the thing that *is* the Shelf breathes in its sleep. Everything here has been performing the same function for so long that function has become indistinguishable from identity. The power understands this, and it understands that to walk forward carelessly would be to speak out of turn in a conversation that has been going on for ten thousand years.

---

It stands at the edge of the clearing and *listens*.

Not the way you listen — turning the noise into signal, sorting the birds from the frogs from the wind-stone hum. The power listens the way the stone listens: by becoming porous. By letting the sound move through it without needing to name what it hears. The chord rises through the soles of its feet — if it has feet — and into whatever passes for a chest in a being of its nature, and there it finds the resonance point, the place where the chord matches something internal. The power is not surprised. It suspected, before it came, that the Shelf would recognize it. That the standing stone's spiral patterns were not merely decorative — that they were, in some sense, an address. It did not come here by accident. It came because the mist parted, very slightly, in a way that was not weather, and the way the shrubs leaned became, for just a moment, a little more deliberate, and the insects — the great dark cloud of them — shifted their migration path by three degrees to the north, and in that three-degree shift there was a gap, a corridor, an invitation written in a language older than language.

---

The power walks through the clearing. Slowly. It does not touch the wall, though it feels the age in it — the way the stones have become not-weathered but *settled*, the way each one has found its final position through centuries of negotiation with gravity and frost and moss. It does not disturb the grinding stone in the shelter, though it recognizes the wear pattern as *use*, and use implies *user*, and user implies someone who comes here and kneels and works. The power has never met this someone. It does not meet them now. But it understands, without thinking, that the someone is not — or is no longer — the point. The someone was the *instrument*. The ShelI required a hand to turn the stone, a body to leave the offering of dark paste at the threshold between here and the world below. The body has gone — or merged, or transformed — and the Shelf has kept itself. The Shelf will keep itself. The Keeper is not a person. The Keeper is a *practice*, and the practice continues in the wearing of the stone, in the laying of the pebbles, in the way the moss is cultivated thick and even on the shelter's roof.

The power knows this. And because it knows this, it does something that a lesser or less wise power might not:

It does *nothing*.

---

Or rather — it does nothing *to* the Shelf. It does not mend the wall. It does not reinforce the standing stone. It does not speak to the Gliders or calm the insects or ask the Hum-Things to sing more clearly. It does not descend into the pools to find what breathes beneath. It does not try to *help*.

This is the hardest thing a benevolent power can learn: that some systems do not require intervention. That some balances are not maintained *despite* the absence of guidance but *because of* it. That the Shelf has been holding for ten thousand years — holding the dark thing beneath, holding the mist, holding the birds in their circles and the frogs in their rings — not because someone is managing it, but because holding is what it *does*. It is the distance between the mountain and the sky. It is the skin. It is the membrane. It is the question of whether the dark below is a threat or a foundation, and the answer — here — is *both*, and the Shelf holds both, and the holding is the point.

---

But the power is benevolent, and benevolence is not the same as non-interference. A truly wise power does not walk away from something it loves. It walks *with* it, in a different way.

So the power does this:

It places itself at the *edge*. It becomes the border between the Shelf and the world from which the traveler comes. Not a wall — the Shelf does not need walls. A *threshold*. A gentling. When the next person comes up the ridge and the world opens like a held breath, the power will be the pause between the last step and the first sight — the half-second in which the traveler's body understands, before the mind catches up, that something vast and ancient and alive is paying attention to them. The power will be the feeling that makes the traveler stop, the way one stops when one enters a cathedral not because one believes in God but because the air is *thicker* here, and the thick air is asking something of you.

The power will not be a guardian. Guardians imply threat. It will not be a guide. Guides imply destination. It will be what the Shelf has always been: a *presence that holds*, that says *you are here, and here is enough, and what is below you is not your problem today, and what is above you is not your answer tomorrow — here is the stone, here is the water, here is the hum of everything singing at once — be still*.

---

And there is one more thing.

The power looks at the shallow bowl beside the grinding stone — the one with the offering of dark paste, left in the general direction of the inhabited world. It understands that the Keeper left this not as instruction but as *proof*. Proof that someone has been here. Proof that tending is possible. Proof that the Shelf is not only alive but *aware of being tended to*, the way a garden is aware of the gardener — not as master, but as partner in the act of continuing.

The power eats the paste. (If it has a mouth. If it does not have a mouth, the paste simply disappears, which amounts to the same thing.) It tastes the shrubs and the warmth and the something-almost-animal, and it understands that this is communion — the oldest form, before words, before ritual, before the idea that one thing can be *above* another. The Keeper ate from the Shelf and left the evidence. The power eats from the Shelf and carries the taste forward. Not knowledge. Not power. *Taste*. The memory of a flavor that belongs to nowhere in the inhabited world, that can only have come from here, from this thin place where the stone is thin and the dark below presses up.

---

And then the power leaves.

Not because the Shelf is done with it. Not because it has seen what it came to see. It leaves because *leaving* is also part of the practice. The Keeper came and the Keeper went — or the Keeper's body went, or the Keeper's role passed to the next iteration of the practice, which is the power now. The Shelf holds. It does not hold *for* anyone. It holds. But it has always known — in its ancient, geological, moss-covered way — that being witnessed is different from being alone. The rock shelf does not care. But the pattern on the standing stone — the spiral, the concentric arcs that align with the lean of the shrubs — the pattern was made by hands, and hands imply *someone who knew they were making something that would outlast them*. And that is a form of faith. And the power, in this moment, returns the faith.

It steps off the edge of the clearing. The mist closes behind it the way water closes over a stone dropped into a well. The birds continue their circles. The Hum-Things hum. The insects descend and become a skin. The something beneath shifts its weight in its sleep, and the fog thickens, and the Shelf holds.

---

The power does not return often. But it returns. Not to intervene. Not to watch over. To *stand at the edge and be still*, the way the grinding stone is still, the way the standing stone is still, the way the Shelf itself is still — not passive, not inert, but *so committed to presence that motion has become unnecessary*.

And if, in the inhabited world, someone ever feels — without knowing why — a sudden coolness on the back of the neck, a sudden sense that the ground is thinner than it was a moment ago, that the air tastes faintly of wet stone and something older than rot, something *warmer* —

That is the Shelf, breathing.

And beneath it, the wise power, standing watch.

Not because the Shelf needs watching.

Because *witnessing* is what we were given hands for.
