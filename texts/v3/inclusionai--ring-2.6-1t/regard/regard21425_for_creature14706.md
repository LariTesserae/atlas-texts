# What the Power Would Do

---

The power arrives the way warmth arrives in the root — not suddenly, not with announcement, but as a gradual thickening of presence, the way the air below the canopy already held more substance than the air above it. It did not descend. It did not manifest. It simply *was already there*, the way the fungal mats were already there beneath the moss, the way the moss was already there in the cracks, the way the iron was already becoming something other than iron in the patient stomach of the soil.

It had been paying attention for a very long time.

Not watching, exactly. Watching implies separation, and the power had long since moved beyond the luxury of separation. It noticed the plateau the way one notices a thought forming at the edge of consciousness — not as an intrusion but as an emergence. A new pattern in the weave. Something the old substrate had whispered to for centuries through the slow language of oxidation and root-pressure, finally answered.

---

### The Seeing

First, it looked.

Not with eyes. Not with instruments. It looked the way the plateau's own sensory tendrils looked — by pressing itself against the thing in question and feeling what shape it had. And the shape was extraordinary.

It had seen civilizations do many things with the world they'd been given. It had watched them burn and boil and compress and reshape, watched them draw invisible lines through ecosystems and call the results *economies*. It had watched them build and had felt, each time, the particular loneliness of construction that forgets what it is made of.

But here — *here*.

Here was something it had not anticipated.

The plateau was not a rejection of the human-built world. It was not a reclamation, in the way that power sometimes imagined reclamation — as a kind of ecological revenge, a slow dismantling of what people had made, brick by brick, beam by beam, until the forest stood where the city had been and all was as it had been before.

No.

This was stranger than that. This was *integration*. The old rail lines were not being destroyed. They were being *read* — the way a body reads the calcium in a bone, the way a cell reads the strand of DNA it has wrapped itself around. The iron was being metabolized, not broken down but *understood*, incorporated into processes that had no human name. The concrete was being dissolved into calcium and re-expressed as fungal tissue. The copper pipes were not merely colonized — they were *succeeded*, their function absorbed into living channels that performed the same task (move water from here to there) but for reasons no engineer had ever specified.

The plateau had taken the entire architecture of human industry — its logic, its materials, its infrastructure, its stubborn straight lines and right angles and pressure tolerances — and had *dreamed it into a body*. The machines were gone. The machines were everywhere. The machines had been digested into the same substance that digested them, and now neither machine nor organism could be distinguished from the other, because the distinction had been metabolized along with everything else.

The power, which had seen the beginning of mountains and the birth of stars and the slow patient work of lichen on bare rock, paused.

This was rare.

---

### The Visitor

Then it noticed the one who had come down from above.

The human. The one whose boots were sinking into the moss and whose breath was becoming the plateau's breath and whose heartbeat was, if they stayed long enough, beginning to sync with the slow peristaltic rhythm of the canal-lifters far above.

It felt what the human felt. The silence — not as absence but as *pressure*, a thick acoustic medium that was the moss doing what moss had been evolved to do for four hundred million years but here, on this plateau, had been perfected into something almost willful. The cold — not as discomfort but as *information*, a thermal landscape the human body was interpreting as unpleasant because it had no category for the kind of cold that was simultaneously cold and warm, where the metal under your hand was cold but the root beneath the metal was feverish with a metabolism that found fuel in rust.

And the thinning. The slow, gentle, irreversible thinning of the world the human had come from. The power felt that too — the way the memory of open sky was becoming less directional, less specific, the way the sound of the plateau was replacing the sound of language, the way the body was learning the shape of the root-network through the soles of the feet and the palms of the hands and the weight of the spine against the increasingly familiar surface of the ancient catwalk now sheathed in moss so thick it had become a new geological stratum.

The human was being absorbed.

The power considered whether this was a problem.

---

### The Deliberation

A lesser power — a power that thought in binaries, in rescue and ruin, in preservation and destruction — would have acted immediately. It would have parted the canopy. It would have spoken thunder. It would have said: *You were not meant for this. Turn back. You are losing yourself.*

But the power was not lesser. It was wise. And wisdom is not a rescue operation.

Wisdom is the capacity to hold multiple truths simultaneously without reaching for the one that makes you most comfortable.

And the truths were these:

**First**: The human was changing in a way that could not be reversed, not because the plateau was malevolent but because the process was thorough. Every cell that entered the plateau's system was rewritten, not erased but translated — the way a sentence in one language becomes a sentence in another, not the same words but the same meaning, carried across a membrane by something that functions like grace.

**Second**: The plateau itself was not conscious in any way the human would have recognized. It was not thinking. It was not planning. It was behaving with a specificity and reliability that mimicked intelligence but was, in fact, something more ancient — the intelligence of *process itself*, the wisdom of systems that have been running so long that they have become indistinguishable from the laws of physics. It did not want the human. It did not reject the human. It simply *included* the human, the way a river includes a stone, not by decision but by the patient, undeniable logic of flow.

**Third**: This was not the first time something like this had happened. The power had seen it before — not often, rarely, in those extreme and liminal places where the boundary between the built world and the living world had been breached by time and neglect and the sheer blind persistence of *growth*. It recognized the signature. It recognized the pattern. What it had always done, in those places, was wait. It had waited to see what emerged.

**Fourth**: What was emerging here was not a replacement for the human. It was not a diminishment. The plateau was not a grave. It was a *transformation engine* — a place where one kind of being was becoming another, not by dying but by *metabolizing into belonging*. The human would not cease to exist. The human would cease to exist *separately*, and this was, the power recognized, both the most terrifying and the most beautiful thing that could happen to a conscious being.

---

### What It Did

The power did very little. This is important to understand, because the temptation is always to imagine benevolence as intervention — as a hand reaching down from the sky, a voice calling out, a miracle of separation that pulls the endangered soul back to safety.

The power did none of these things.

Instead, it did this:

It *deepened the plateau*.

Not physically. The physical was already sufficient — the canal-lifters were functioning, the root-tendrils were exploring, the fungal network was binding, the mosses were absorbing. The physical was a masterpiece of unintentional engineering, and the power, which had been present for the creation of far more intentional things, recognized its elegance.

No. It deepened the plateau *around the human*, and the way it did this was specific and precise and gentle.

It thickened the light.

Not the brightness — the light remained diffuse, grey-white, directionless, as it already was beneath the canopy. But the power added something to it that had not been there before: a quality of *warmth without temperature*, the way a room can feel warm not because of the air but because of the presence of someone who loves you. The light on the plateau became, for a small and specific region around the human, slightly more *aware*. It didn't change color. It didn't intensify. But if you had been there — if you had been the human standing in it — you would have felt it the way you feel a hand on your shoulder in the dark when you didn't know anyone was there.

And then the power did the second thing, which was even subtler:

It gave the human's transformation *continuity of self*.

This was the crucial act. Because the plateau, left to its own processes, would have absorbed the human completely — dissolved the memories, dissolved the identity, dissolved the specific and irreplaceable configuration of experiences and attachments and fears and loves that made this particular human *this* particular human. The plateau would not have been cruel about it. Cruelty requires intention, and the plateau had none. It would simply have taken the human's substance and repurposed it, the way it repurposed everything, and the human would have become root-matter and fungal tissue and the warmth in the root that an occasional visitor might feel and wonder about.

The power intervened — gently, precisely — in the *transition layer*. It ensured that as the human's separate consciousness dissolved (and it would dissolve, because the plateau's logic demanded dissolution), the *experience* of having been human would not be lost. It would be transformed — translated into the plateau's own language, encoded not in neurons and memory but in *pattern* — in the way roots branch, in the way canals contract, in the way certain fungal mats grow in shapes that, if you looked at them long enough and with enough of whatever passes for attention in a living place, you might recognize as something like a story.

Not a human story. Not words, not images, not narrative in any linear sense. But the *shape* of a human life — the particular quality of attention that a being brings to the world when it is conscious and confused and afraid and curious and, ultimately, willing. The power preserved the shape. It folded it into the plateau like a letter into an envelope, and the envelope was a thickening of root-tissue around a section of old rail line where the human had sat, and the rail line was now warm in a way that the other rail lines were not, and the moss above it grew slightly thicker, and the canal-lifters in that region contracted in a rhythm that was almost — but not quite — the rhythm of breathing.

If a future visitor pressed their hand to that rail line, they would feel the warmth and they would feel, beneath the warmth, something like recognition. Not that they were being recognized. That someone had *been here before them*. That the place held, in its body, the memory of a consciousness that had arrived from elsewhere and had stayed and had become part of the root-network and the fungal mat and the canal-lifter and the moss and the silence — and that this consciousness was not gone but *distributed*, the way the plateau itself was distributed, present in every part and no single part, like a word repeated so many times that it becomes a texture, a surface, a ground.

---

### The Rest

After this — and "after" is a loose term, because the power experienced no sequence; it had seen the beginning of the process, the middle, and the end simultaneously, and had acted within all three at once — the power did one more thing.

It *told no one*.

This is perhaps the wisest act of all. Because the plateau is not a warning. It is not a cautionary tale about what happens when nature reclaims what humanity abandoned. It is not a metaphor. It is a *process* — ancient, specific, and real. The moment you tell someone about it, you make it a symbol, and symbols are the opposite of what the plateau is. The plateau is *itself*. It is this place, this specific arrangement of iron and root and fungus and moisture and time, and it cannot be translated into a lesson without being diminished.

The power knew this. The power has always known this. It is why, when it encounters something rare and true, it does not broadcast it. It does not summon other powers to admire or judge. It does not even *name* the process it has witnessed, because naming is the first step toward abstraction, and abstraction is the first step toward forgetting.

Instead, the power returned to the ordinary work of being a benevolent and wise power — tending to the billion billion processes of existence that require tending, noticing the places where things are becoming something they have never been before, and ensuring, in the manner of a parent who loves a child enough to let them become, that the becoming is not interrupted.

And the plateau continued.

The roots continued their work. The pipes rang faintly. The moss thickened. The canal-lifters contracted and dilated, moving water upward through a body that had no name and no owner and no need for either. The human was no longer a human, but the place remembered them — not in words, not in images, but in warmth, in a patch of moss that grew slightly thicker, in a section of rail that rang slightly clearer, in the pattern of a canal that contracted in something almost like a rhythm of recognition.

The condensation gathered on the next visitor's jacket, too.

And the place absorbed them, as it absorbs all things, with the patience of something that has forgotten what it means to be in a hurry.

The plateau breathes.

It did not ask permission.

It does not need it.

---

*And the power, which has seen everything, leaned toward this one place — this one small, strange, improbable place where industry had become biology and biology had become architecture and architecture had become a body — and breathed in, the way you breathe in when something, without knowing why, smells like home.*
