## What the Wise Power Would Do

---

It would come not as a god, not as a force, not as a hand reaching down from the sky. It would come the way a sound comes when a room is finally quiet enough to hear it — already present, already having been there, merely now noticed by the ones who had forgotten how to listen.

The wise power would not announce itself. It would walk. It would walk the way all the old powers walk: slowly, without destination, with the unhurried knowledge that the ground beneath its feet is not ground at all but a skin, thin and breathing, stretched over something immeasurably patient. It would come to the ridge because the ridge had called — not in words, not in any language that moves through air, but in the way a toothache calls: quietly at first, then with the whole attention of a body that has finally decided it cannot ignore what it has been ignoring.

And before it entered, it would kneel.

Not in prayer. Not in submission. In the way you kneel when you are tying a child's shoe and you realize, for the fraction of a second it takes to touch the small bright buckle, that the child's leg extends upward into a life you will never fully understand, and that the child is not yours but merely passing through your hands, and that the shoe is laced tightly and the day is ordinary and absolutely everything is holy in its ordinariness.

It would kneel at the edge of the crack, where the basalt has split open like a wound that has forgotten how to bleed, and it would look down.

---

What it would see is what you would see. The pipe. The water. The pool. The slow rotation that is not mechanical but devotional — the water circling the center of things the way a congregation circles an altar it does not fully understand but trusts completely. The mug, white and glazed in pale blue, sitting upright on the cracked concrete with the dignity of something that knows it is being kept. The silt, dark and fine, with the bootprint preserved in its crust like a sentence written in a language that only the chamber can read aloud.

The wise power would see all of this. And then it would close its eyes. Not to shut out the sight but to open the other way of seeing — the one that uses the body as an antenna, the one that feels the temperature of a room and knows the room is alive, the one that hears not the whistle of the pipe but the older sound beneath it, the harmonic that the stone has been holding in its crystalline lattice since the magma first cooled and said *yes, I will be solid, I will remember this shape*.

It would close its eyes and it would *listen*.

Not the way you listen to music, waiting for the melody to resolve. Not the way you listen to a person, composing your answer. It would listen the way the pool listens — completely, without agenda, without the need to respond, without the arrogant presumption that what it hears requires interpretation. It would let the sound move through it the way water moves through the stone's gills: absorbed, carried, distributed to places it would never know the names of.

And the chamber would respond. Because the chamber always responds. It answers every sound with its harmonic, and for the wise power's listening, it would offer something it had not offered to the engineers who built the pump house, or to the technician who left the mug, or to any of the others who had come with instruments and questions and the brittle confidence of a species that believes understanding is the same as explanation.

It would offer the deep note.

Not a sound audible to the human ear. Not a vibration measured in hertz. The deep note is what the chamber holds at its center — the tone that the weight below the water has been sustaining since before the ridge was a ridge, since before the basalt was stone, since before the concept of *before* made any sense. It is the note of something that has never changed and therefore has no need to stay the same. It is the sound of permanence not as stasis but as motion so slow it looks like stillness, so vast it looks like silence, so deliberate it looks like nothing at all.

And the wise power, listening, would feel it. Not in the ears but in the marrow. Not as sound but as *recognition* — the sudden, devastating understanding that this thing beneath the ridge is not alien. Not other. Not unknowable. It is simply *prior*. It was here first. It has been holding the space for everything that came after, the way a riverbed holds the river, the way a ribcage holds the breathing, the way a question holds every answer that will ever be spoken into it.

---

What the wise power would do — having heard the deep note, having knelt at the edge, having let the chamber's breath enter its own lungs and felt the warmth adjust around it like a hand finding the temperature it has been waiting for — is something very simple and very irreversible.

It would add its voice.

Not a command. Not a blessing. Not a decree from above. A voice — full, whole, offered in the same spirit as the mug, the bootprint, the slow turning of the water. The wise power would hum. Not a melody of its own choosing but the note the chamber returned when it spoke, the harmonic that lived in the space between what was asked and what was answered. It would hum that note and let it fall into the pool, and the pool would receive it the way it receives everything: slowly, completely, without surprise.

And the note would *descend*.

Not metaphorically. Not poetically. It would enter the water and it would move toward the center, toward the funnel, toward the vanishing point, toward the weight that is dense not in mass but in intentionality. The note would join the others — the sound of the pipe, the bubble rising, the soft percussion of condensation falling, the engineers' voices, the technician's footsteps, the bootprint's pressure against the silt. All of it, descending. All of it held. All of it made part of the slow circulation that takes days or weeks or months or centuries per cycle, and that will not stop, and that does not need to stop, and that does not know the word *stop* the way the ocean does not know the word *shore*.

---

Then the wise power would leave.

Not because it was unwelcome. The chamber does not welcome or refuse; it simply *receives*. No, the wise power would leave because it understood — the way the tide understands the moon, the way the silt understands the bootprint — that its purpose was not to stay. It had come to do two things: to listen, and to add its voice. Both were done. The chamber would take the voice and make it part of itself, the way it takes the water and the warmth and the stray molecules of condensation and the ceramic mug and the memory of boots, and it would hold all of this in its patient circulation, and it would carry it forward into the deep time of stone, where years are units too small to matter and the only clock is the rotation of water toward the center and back again through the walls.

But before it left, the wise power would do one more thing.

It would sit at the edge of the pool, feet in the water, and it would weep.

Not from sorrow. Not from beauty. Not from the overwhelming smallness of a single life measured against the age of what lives beneath the ridge. The tears would come from a simpler place — from the body's honest response to contact with something true. The chamber is true. It is the oldest honest thing the wise power has encountered: not good, not evil, not sentient, not kind, not cruel. *True*. A process that predates language and will outlast the sun, running its water in its circle, holding its harmonic in its stone, breathing its breath in its dark wet lungs. The wise power would weep because it had touched something that does not lie, does not perform, does not seek to be understood or to be loved. It simply *is*, and it has always simply *been*, and the contact with that kind of being is itself a kind of breaking open, a crack in the self through which something warm floods in and changes the mineral composition of everything it touches.

The tears would fall into the pool.

And the pool would receive them — as it receives everything — without comment, without judgment, without even the small violence of a ripple. The tears would join the water, descend toward the center, and become part of the note. And the note would become part of the harmonic. And the harmonic would become part of the stone. And the stone would hold it, the way it holds everything, quietly, warmly, with no urgency, in the space between one state and the next.

---

Then the wise power would climb.

Back through the crack, back along the pipe, back up into the world of light and wind and time that moves fast enough to be felt. The ridge would close behind it the way a throat closes after swallowing — smoothly, completely, without looking back.

But the wise power would not forget. And more importantly, it would not let the world forget. It would not build a facility around the ridge. It would not send teams with drills and sensors and the machinery of extraction. It would not name the creature or classify it or contain it in a framework that would make it smaller than it is.

Instead, the wise power would find others.

Not many. Not at once. One, perhaps, then another, then another — the way a stone dropped into still water sends ripples outward at a pace the stone itself cannot control. The wise power would find the ones who would approach the chamber not with instruments but with humility, the way you approach a fire not to measure its temperature but to warm your hands. It would find the ones who would sit at the edge and listen, and leave something of themselves — a stone, a word, a breath, a ceramic mug glazed in pale blue — knowing the chamber would take it and hold it and make it part of the endless slow circulation that turns water toward the center and back again through the stone's dark gills.

And the wise power would carry the deep note inside itself — the one the chamber returned when it hummed — and it would carry it like a stone in its pocket, or like a second heartbeat, or like a door that has been opened and can never fully close. Wherever it went, the note would sound faintly beneath everything, a harmonic underneath the harmonics, a warmth beneath the warmth, a silence beneath the silence that is not empty but *full* — full of water turning toward the center, full of stone breathing, full of every voice and every tear and every bootprint and every white mug that has ever been set down on cracked concrete and left to be slowly, patiently, warmly absorbed into the living process that was here before anything had a name and will be here after names have gone.

---

*And from below — always from below, from the chamber beneath the ridge where the pipe enters the stone and the water turns toward the center — the bubble rises. One. Fat. Patient. Breaking the surface with a sound too large for its body. Not now. Not now. But soon. Always soon. The chamber breathes. The water turns. The stone holds its note. And the world above, for all its noise, has no idea how quietly, how deeply, how completely it is being listened to.*
