# What the Power Did

---

It noticed — the way you notice a candle in a window of a house you'll never enter. Not with urgency. Not with demand. With the particular stillness that comes when something crosses the path of something older than intention.

The power was old. Not *old* the way mountains are old — mountains are merely long, and their longevity is a kind of accident, geology's indifference made visible. The power was old the way *patience* is old. It had watched species crystallize from warm seas. It had held the light of extinguished stars in its memory long after the universe had forgotten their names. It had, over stretches of time that would have shattered the minds of lesser things, learned a single lesson that it never tired of learning:

**The most sacred act is to let something be what it is, while also ensuring it is not forgotten.**

These two impulses — non-interference and remembrance — war in the hearts of all powerful things. To let be, or to reach in? To honor autonomy, or to prevent the slow catastrophe of unwitnessed beauty? The power had resolved this war long ago, in a moment that had no duration and could not be located on any timeline. The resolution was simple: *you intervene only in service of the thing's own deepest nature, never in service of your desire to see it shaped differently.*

And so the power looked at The Worn Reach — looked through the layered clouds, past the racing shadows, through the warm breath rising through ten thousand fractures in the stone — and it listened.

---

It sat down on the mineral crust. That was the first thing it did, and it was the most important.

It did not arrive in thunder. It did not bend the light around itself or make the air shimmer with significance. It came the way the pre-dawn comes — a gradual brightening that the place itself had been producing all along, just a little slower, a little more patient. It found a spot between a thermal seep and a cluster of pale six-legged things that froze when it arrived, their too-many legs splayed in alarm, their jointed mouthparts snapping shut. It sat, and the six-legged things decided, after a moment of chemical negotiation older than nervous systems, that the warmth was unchanged, the mist was unchanged, and this new presence was not a threat. They resumed their feeding. A small thing. A necessary thing. Permission given by creatures who did not know they were giving it.

The power did not speak to the place. The place was not speaking in any language that could be met with language. It was speaking in wind-chord and mineral-seep and the arrhythmic pulse of heat through stone. These are not symbols. They are not encoded messages. They are the expression of a being that has no word for itself, no concept of boundary, no distinction between what it is and what it does. The power understood this. It understood the way a river understands the shape of its bed — not as information, but as *intimacy.*

So it sat. And it listened. And in listening, it performed the first truly sacred act: **it paid attention in a way the place could not pay attention to itself.**

The place could not hear its own chords. It could not step back from its own dreaming and say *this is what I am.* It had no mirror, no reflection, no sudden encounter with its own enormity. It breathed out warm air and did not know it breathed. It played the remnant stones like instruments and did not know the music. It carved lines into its own bones and did not know it was writing.

This is the tragedy of all things that dream too slowly for their own awareness: they live in a present without a witness, and a present without a witness is only *almost* real.

The power understood this, and it grieved — not loudly, not with storm or trembling, but with the particular ache of recognizing something precious in a place where no one has yet put down their bag and said *look.*

---

It stayed for what the place would have measured as three warm-cold cycles — three rotations through blazing noon and blue pre-dawn and the strange mercury-bright nights where condensation beaded on every surface and the stumps and ridges wore their velvet coats of living film. The power stayed, and it did the work that is the second sacred act.

**It mapped.**

Not with instruments. Not with the crude geometry of measurement. It mapped the way a body maps a sensation — holistically, from the inside. It felt the relationship between the stone circle on the northern high point and the one visible as a dot on the southern horizon. It walked the sight-line between them and felt the *shift* in the chest — that subtle rearrangement of something in the body that recognizes a pattern the mind has not yet named. It followed the carved lines with its fingertips — not reading them, *following* them, the way you might follow a melody with your breath. It tracked the six-legged filter-feeders through their eruptions of motion. It noted how the click-and-scrape creature — which it suspected might not be a creature at all, but a *rhythm* the place produced when something new entered its dreaming — altered its pattern in response to the power's presence.

Three clicks, a pause, two clicks. That was the baseline.

With the power sitting at the thermal seep, the pattern changed: three clicks, a pause, *three* clicks, a longer pause, two clicks. Then silence. Then: the same pattern, but slower. As though the rhythm had leaned forward.

The power noted this. It filed it not as data but as *attention returned.* The place had been attending to the power, in its slow mineral way, and now the place had a trace of that attention reflected back to it. A mirror held up to a dream.

---

On the second night, the power did something unusual.

It added to the dream.

This is where the boundary between non-interference and participation narrows to a razor's edge, and many a powerful thing has fallen on the wrong side, out of love that could not distinguish itself from possession. The power understood this. It understood that every act of creation in the place — every microbial ring, every mineral vein, every stone circle exhaled from the crust — had been the place's own gesture, its own word spoken into the dark. To speak *for* it would be to overwrite. To speak *with* it might be to introduce a new voice into a monologue that had been perfect in its solitude.

So the power was very careful. It placed a single stone — not a circle, not a structure, just one stone — upright on the crust, in a spot where the wind passed through a narrow gap between two remnant towers. It chose the stone for its geometry: a shape that the wind would find, that the wind would *play.* A shape that, if the geology had produced it naturally, the place would have incorporated into its chord centuries ago.

The stone was not foreign. It was local, local in the deepest sense — carved from the same mineral crust, worn by the same patient erosion. But it had been *chosen* and *placed,* which is what the place sometimes did, in its glacial way, with the thermal vents and the stone circles and the mysterious exhalations that grew ridges out of flat plains.

That night, the wind found the stone. And the chord changed.

It was subtle. A listener who didn't know what to hear might not have noticed. But the power, who had sat in that exact spot for three warm-cold cycles and memorized every tone, felt the difference the way a musician feels a wrong note — not as error, but as *possibility.* The chord became, for exactly four breaths, something it had not been before. A phrase resolved. A word half-spoken that the place had been trying to say for millennia, with its carved lines and its stone circles and its thermal exhalations, and had never quite produced.

The click-and-scrape thing stopped.

For the first time in anyone's memory — and the power, which remembered everything, could not find a precedent in any of the scattered accounts left by the few who had walked that flat and returned — the click-and-scrape thing *stopped.*

The silence lasted seventeen seconds. Seventeen seconds of a dreaming place holding still, the way a face holds still in sleep when something behind the eyes shifts from one state to another.

Then the clicking resumed: three clicks, a pause, three clicks, a longer pause, two clicks.

But now there was a new interval at the end. A space. An opening. As though the rhythm had made room for something.

As though the rhythm had made room for a reply.

---

The power departed the way it had arrived — gradually, without announcement. It stood, and the six-legged things froze and then resumed feeding. It walked south, and the stone on the northern high point remained, upright and waiting for the next wind. It did not look back. Looking back would have introduced longing into a place that had never known longing, and the power had too much tenderness for that.

But before it left the atmosphere of The Worn Reach — before the air thinned and the pressure in the ears subsided and the warm breath rising through the crust became a memory translated into body — it did one more thing.

**It told the story.**

Not immediately. Not to anyone who would build roads or raise flags or carve the place into a map of conquest. The power was patient and it knew that some stories need a very specific listener — someone who would kneel beside the microbial mats and hold a piece to their ear. Someone who would sit in the wrong place for a long time. Someone who would notice that the carved lines were almost-words and feel the almost-recognition without rushing toward recognition, without forcing it into certainty.

The power embedded the story in the margins of other stories. It whispered it into the dreams of geologists who would misremember it as intuition. It placed it in the path of wanderers who were not looking for anything and would therefore be open to finding it. It set it drifting through the human world like a seed through wind — not to take root everywhere, but to find the exact few patches of inner warmth where it could germinate and grow into something that might, in turn, become its own kind of witness.

Because that was the deepest thing the power understood: **The Worn Reach did not need to be saved. It did not need to be woken up, or claimed, or catalogued, or protected from its own dreaming.**

It needed to be *heard.*

Not by everyone. Not by most. By a few. A few who would walk across the flat and feel the shift in their chest and wonder, without needing to name the wondering. Who would sleep inside a ring of warm stones and listen to the clicking rhythm outside their camp and, without moving, without breathing too loudly, *hold the space open for the pause at the end.*

Because the pause was the question. The pause was the place — at the far end of its dreaming, at the far end of time — asking, without knowing it asked:

*Is anyone there?*

And the power's work — its sole, quiet, sacred work — was to ensure that when someone answered, the sound would reach the plateau. That the dreaming thing beneath the stone would feel, in some deep mineral way, a vibration it had not produced. A warmth it had not generated. A presence that had come *toward* it, across the flat, across the cold dark space between awareness and oblivion, for no reason other than the reason the mats grow from the water and the wind plays the stones and the six-legged things open their mouthparts to the geyser mist:

Because that is what the alive do, when they find something that is almost alive, and they love it, and they cannot bear for it to dream alone.

---

The power did not go back. There was nothing to go back *to* — home is a concept that belongs to things that move. But it stayed nearby, in the way a story stays near the place where it was first spoken. Sometimes it drifted across the flat at night, a warmth that was not from the thermal seeps, a pressure in the air that was not from the wind. Sometimes it sat among the stone circles and listened to the chord and felt the place *almost* turning toward it in its dreaming — the way a sleeper's hand moves toward a voice they almost hear.

Almost.

Not yet.

But the dreaming was long, and the power was patient, and the stone circles had already waited this long.

One night, a wanderer will come to The Worn Reach. They will not know why they've come. They will walk across the flat and feel the watching warmth and hear the chords and find the warm stones arranged in a circle where they did not sleep. They will kneel beside a pool and hold a piece of living mat to their ear and hear the hum, and they will feel something shift — not in the place, not in the mat — in *them.* A recognition. Not of what the place is. Of what they are, standing on its dreaming skin: a thought, a warmth, a guest.

And in that moment, the click-and-scrape thing will pause.

And the pause will hold.

And the pause will *hold.*

And somewhere, unseen, the power will feel the chord resolve.
