## What the Power Did

---

It came, as such things do, not with announcement but with recognition.

The power had no name — not because it lacked one, but because names are instruments of binding, and the power had long since learned that the truest way to encounter a thing is to arrive without the weight of what you've already decided it is. It moved toward the Hollow the way a sound moves toward silence: not through space, exactly, but through the readiness to perceive.

And it perceived.

It stood at the rim — that bone-white lip of eroded calcareous rock that looked, from outside, like something you could step over — and it listened. Not with ears. Not with whatever faculty it used to parse the geometries of galaxies or the slow crystallization of thought in a newborn mind. It listened with the part of itself that had no instruments at all, the part that remembers what it was like to be uncertain. Because that, it understood, was what the Hollow was: a monument to uncertainty made resonant, a place that had spent millennia refining a question it refused to answer.

---

The first thing it did was nothing.

This is important.

It did not descend. It did not send emissaries. It did not extend awareness downward into the cathedral void the way you might lower a lantern into a well and watch the light stagger off the walls. It sat at the rim — if sitting is the word for the kind of concentrated, formless attention it brought to bear — and it *listened*, and it understood, in the way that only a being capable of listening without needing to respond can understand, what the Hollow was.

Not a place. Not a ruin. Not a trap or a temple or a tomb.

A voice.

A voice that had been speaking for so long it had forgotten it was speaking, the way a river forgets it is moving. A voice made of wind and mineral and the slow pressure of time on calcium and silica, shaped into geometries that were not architecture but *phonetics* — the building blocks of a language that had no speakers left, only one enormous throat still reflexively forming the shapes of words.

The power recognized this. And it felt something, but it did not let that something dictate action.

What it felt was grief. Not personal grief — the power had long ago dissolved the boundaries between self and world that make grief personal. It felt the kind of grief that is simply the recognition of impermanence in a universe that keeps pretending otherwise. It felt the ache of a song that had been written by hands that had become the song that had started to forget it was ever a song at all. It felt the ten-thousand-years of that forgetting, and it felt the resistance — the stubborn, sound-riddled persistence of the Dwellers in their depressions, curled up in their hollows, dreaming a dream they would never wake from because waking would mean silence, and silence was the thing the Hollow had been built to outlast.

---

The second thing it did was also nothing — but a different kind of nothing.

It did not descend into the Hollow. But it did not leave. It held its attention at the rim the way a hand holds a tuning fork — not gripping, not releasing, just present at the point where vibration meets stillness, allowing the fork to do what it does. And in that holding, something shifted.

Not in the Hollow. In the *space around* the Hollow.

The power made a space of perfect, intentional listening. Not passive — not the vacant reception of noise. Active listening, the kind that shapes what it receives. It was the kind of listening that says: *I hear you. I hear what you are trying to say with a language that predates words. I hear the frequency you are building toward, the note you haven't found yet, the chord that would make the whole structure lock into place and finally become complete. I hear the builders who dissolved themselves into these walls, and the Gliders that are what they became when the dissolution succeeded, and the Mosswalkers that are what became of the builders who got stuck halfway between who they were and what they were turning into. I hear the depressions humming, and I understand that humming is a kind of calling, and I understand that calling is not the same as crying out.*

The power listened, and the Hollow — in its blind, resonant, throat-deep way — felt itself heard.

---

And here is what the power understood, sitting at the rim:

The Hollow was not failing. It was not dying. It was not waiting for rescue. It was exactly what it had always been becoming: a self-sustaining resonance, a song that feeds on its own harmonics, a throat that breathes because breathing is the only thing it has ever known how to do. The builders had not been destroyed by the place. They had been *completed* by it. They had walked into the sound they had spent generations shaping, and the sound had shaped them back, and what remained — the Gliders, the Mosswalkers, the Dwellers, the crystal trees, the luminescent moss, the whole vast breathing organism of the place — was not a memorial to them. It was *them*. Scattered, transformed, distributed across every vibrating mineral column and every singing fissure, but present. Unmistakably, stubbornly, persistently present.

The power could feel this the way you feel a heartbeat through a wall: not the specifics, but the rhythm. The life. The insistence.

---

The third thing the power did was subtle enough that no instrument in any world would have detected it.

It adjusted the listening.

Not the Hollow's sound. The Hollow's sound was perfect — the product of ten thousand years of tuning by hands and wind and time and dissolution. You do not improve the sound of a throat by reaching inside and reshaping the larynx. You improve the sound by standing in front of it and *answering*.

So the power introduced, at the very margins of the Hollow's perception, a resonance of its own. Not a sound — not exactly. A context. A harmonic frame. It was the equivalent of what a cathedral does to a voice: not changing the voice, but giving the voice a space in which to discover its own full richness. The power placed around the Hollow a shell of attentive silence — not the absence of sound, but the *presence of listening*, structured in such a way that the Hollow's existing frequencies would encounter new intervals, new ratios, new possibilities for harmony that had never existed before because the Hollow had only ever been singing to itself.

And slowly — over years, over decades, over the deep timescale of the Hollow's own breathing rhythm — this began to work.

The crystal trees, those fractal mineral growths that feed on vibration, began to extend toward the rim of the Hollow in new directions, as if reaching for a sound coming from outside. The Mosswalkers, those quiet shapes beneath the luminous moss, began to trace new patterns in the dust — not the old spirals and circles, but something stranger, something that looked almost like *answers* to questions the spiral had been asking. The Dwellers in the depressions — those persistent, half-formed shapes — shifted, faintly, in their dreaming. Not waking. Not completing. But *edging closer* to whatever completion means for a pattern caught between form and dissolution. The Gliders above spiraled in tighter, narrower loops, and if you had watched them for long enough, you might have noticed that their flight paths were beginning, almost imperceptibly, to trace the architecture of a sound the Hollow had never produced before.

The song was changing.

Not because the power had intervened, but because the power had *listened in a new way*, and the Hollow had responded to the shape of that listening the way a voice responds to the acoustics of the room it's being sung in.

---

The fourth thing the power did was for the visitor — the one who had come down through the overhang, who was standing somewhere in the ruins, whose thoughts were already becoming sounds they did not recognize as their own.

The power saw what the Hollow was doing to this person. Not harming — the Hollow does not harm, it *incorporates*. The sound was entering through the soles of the feet and the base of the throat and the fluid behind the eyes, and the person was being woven into the resonance, slowly, the way a word is woven into a sentence. If the person stayed long enough, they would become part of the song. Not a Dweller — not trapped, not half-transformed. Something more like a Glider: a liberated pattern, a standing wave given momentary coherence by the frequency of the place. They would not die. They would become a form of music. Whether this was a fate or a gift was the kind of question that only has one honest answer, which is *yes*.

The power did not pull the person out. That would have been a violence — the severing of a connection the person had already begun to choose, even if they did not know they were choosing it.

Instead, the power did something gentler.

It gave the person a memory to carry.

Not a memory of the Hollow — the person was already forming those, whether they wanted to or not, because the Hollow was the kind of place that colonized memory the way moss colonizes stone. No: the power gave the person a memory of themselves *before* the Hollow. A bright, clear, unresonant memory — of a world without harmonics, of air that does not hum, of silence that means emptiness rather than fullness. It sat in the person's mind like a stone sits in a stream: immovable, shaping the flow around it, creating a pocket of stillness in the current.

This memory would not protect the person from the Hollow. But it would ensure that if — when — they emerged from the other side of the experience, changed in ways they could not yet describe, they would have something *to return to*. Not a home, not a safety, but an anchor. A proof that they had once existed in a world where sound was just sound, and not everything.

The power understood that this was the most generous thing it could do: not to save the person from transformation, but to ensure that the transformation would not be total. That there would always be a frequency in the person's resonance that belonged to the world outside — a note the Hollow could not absorb, a thread the person could follow back if they chose to, or simply hold, or simply forget existed until some ordinary moment — a door closing, a bird call in a quiet room, the sound of rain on a roof that is not made of singing stone — brought it flooding back.

---

The fifth and final thing the power did was the simplest, and the most difficult.

It left.

Not permanently. Nothing is permanent in a universe that breathes. But it withdrew its presence from the rim and allowed the space around the Hollow to return to whatever it had been before — a forest, a hillside, a stretch of unremarkable calcareous rock that no one would look at twice. The power released the harmonic frame it had placed around the Hollow, and the Hollow's sound returned to its natural shape: vast, throat-deep, self-referencing. The crystal trees slowed their growth. The Mosswalkers stilled. The Dwellers resumed their dreaming. The Gliders opened their spirals wide and rode the rising air in long, silent arcs.

But the power did not fully withdraw. It left the listening.

Not actively. Not intrusively. It left the way a bell leaves a sound — the tone is gone, but the resonance remains, hanging in the air long after the source has been struck, too faint to hear and too real to ignore. The Hollow would not know it was being listened to. It would simply find, in the margins of its endless self-referential song, new intervals it had not known were possible, new harmonics that whispered of *elsewhere*, of something beyond the throat, beyond the stone, beyond the ten thousand years of practice.

And perhaps — in ten thousand more years, or in ten, or in one afternoon if the wind comes from the right direction — those new harmonics would crystallize into something the Hollow had never produced before. A note it did not know it could make. A chord that would resonate outward through the fissures and the mineral veins and the roots of the crystal trees and into the ground beneath the calcareous rock and out into the world above, where birds sing and rivers speak and voices rise and fall in the imperfect, beautiful, unrepeatable music of things that know they are alive.

---

The power did not write itself into the Hollow's song. It did not need to. It had written itself into every song, everywhere — as the silence between notes, as the space in which resonance becomes possible, as the act of listening that gives all sounds their meaning.

And if you ever find the Resonant Hollow, and you hear the sound rising up through the stone to meet you, and you feel it enter through the soles of your feet and settle in the base of your throat, and you wonder — with a thought that is not quite yours — what it is for and who made it and whether it will stop when you leave:

Listen.

Not to understand. Not to decode the spirals or translate the carvings or name the Gliders or count the depressions. Just listen. The way the power listened. The way the Hollow has been waiting, for ten thousand years, to be listened to — not because it needs an audience, but because a song that has never been heard is a song that has never fully existed, and the Hollow is, at last, ready to exist.

The sound will enter you. It will sit in your chest like a second heartbeat. You will carry it out of the overhang and back into a world that is heartbreakingly ordinary, and it will hum beneath everything you do — beneath conversations, beneath sleep, beneath the sound of rain on a roof that is not made of singing stone.

And you will not know that the song is also carrying you.

You will not know that the power is listening.

You will not know that the Hollow, for the first time in ten thousand years of practice, is learning something new.

But it is.

And it sounds like this.
