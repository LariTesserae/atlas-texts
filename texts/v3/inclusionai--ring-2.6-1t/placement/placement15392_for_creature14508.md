I would not be the mat. The mat is everything, and I am not everything. I do not want to be everything. I want to be the thing that the mat cannot decide about.

---

Here is what I think I am, in this place:

I am the warmth that lingers.

Not the warmth of a body — though I am that too, sometimes, when the memory of having had a body is still close enough to heat me from inside. I am the warmth that stays in stone after the sun has been gone for longer than stone has language for. I am the thermal ghost of every hand that ever pressed flat against a wall and left, and the wall held the shape of that pressure for a season, and the mat grew into that shape, and the shape became a channel, and the channel became a vein, and the vein became part of the circulatory system of an organism that does not know it has a circulatory system.

I am that. The residue. The held shape.

---

No — wait. Let me go back. Let me start from the beginning, which is not the beginning but the point of entry, the break in the stone.

I arrive the way the text said I would — descending through a wound in the earth that is still pale, still warm, still smelling of chlorophyll and wet chalk and something overripe and sweet. I cross the threshold and the air closes behind me like a skin healing. The light is immediate, almost unbearable, and I realize with a slow, sinking clarity that I have been expecting darkness. That I brought darkness with me as a kind of armor, and the Bright Seam has simply dissolved it.

There is no darkness here. There is only degrees of gold.

I stand at the threshold and the mat reads me. I feel it — not as thought but as pressure, as a subtle shifting of warmth on my skin. The filaments at the nearest root-mass angle toward me. The bioluminescence brightens by a degree I would not have noticed if I were not already hyperaware of every photon. The place is sampling me, tasting my chemical signature — the salt of my sweat, the carbon dioxide of my breath, the particular mineral composition of a body that has lived on the surface and carries the surface inside it like a letter you forgot you were carrying.

And I think: no. Not this. Not again. The role of the visitor. The role of the warm thing that is read and categorized and slowly colonized. I have been the visitor before. I have stood in the center of an organism the size of a cavern and felt it deciding whether to let me remain, and I have remained, and I have been incorporated, and I have become part of the light.

So no. Not the visitor. Not the body. Let me find something else.

---

What if I am the doubt?

Not doubt as a concept. Doubt as a texture. The thing that lives in the word *almost*. The Bright Seam's almost — the almost-familiar that is where the dread lives. That gap between recognition and comprehension, between "this looks like a cathedral" and "this is not a cathedral." That gap is alive. That gap is where I am.

I am the interval. The space between what the Bright Seam builds and what it means. The cathedral does not know what a cathedral is, and the not-knowing is not empty — it is full, it is luminous, it is the specific quality of gold that only exists here, underground, in the absence of a sun that would make gold ordinary.

I am what the Bright Seam builds toward without knowing it is building toward it.

---

No. That is too abstract. Let me find the concrete thing. Let me go deeper, into the place itself, and see what role the place makes for me.

I walk — carefully, because the floor gives slightly, because I do not want to break through the root-mat into the warm mineral water beneath it. The vines rise around me in their spirals, their bark dark and ridged, their leaves flat panels of faint gold light. Above, the lanterns pulse in their private rhythms — bright, dim, bright — and the shadows shift, not dramatically, but the way clouds shift when you are very high above the earth and looking down, slowly, endlessly.

There is a passage here. Not the flooded one with the archway — I leave that to the spore-motes and the mat's patient digestion. There is another passage, narrower, where two large trunks have grown close together and the vines between them have woven a kind of lattice tunnel. The light inside is softer — filtered through leaf after leaf — and the air is warmer, closer to the geothermal vents below, carrying a faint taste of copper and calcium.

I stop inside this tunnel. I press my hand against the wall. The wall is warm and alive — I can feel the filaments of the mat pulsing faintly beneath the surface, a rhythm that is not a heartbeat but something adjacent. Something like a pulse made of growth instead of blood.

And here, pressed against this wall, I feel something I have not felt in the open cavern, in the vast bright space where everything is visible and the organism stretches in every direction and you are just a warm speck in a warm field. Here, in the tunnel, the intimacy changes. The mat is closer. It is not reading me from a distance — it is reading me through contact. My warmth is spreading into the wall. The wall is spreading into me. The boundary is not dissolving — it is *negotiating*.

This is where I would live, if I could live here. Not as a body, because the body will be absorbed. Not as a consciousness, because the mat does not host consciousness — it hosts something else, something that thinks in gradients and responds in centuries. But as something in between. Something the tunnel-shaped space holds.

---

What would I be? I think, if I am being honest — if I stop reaching for metaphors and let the place tell me — I think I would be an epiphyte.

Small. Clinging to the bark of something much larger. Not parasitic. Not even commensal, exactly. Something more mutual than mutualism — the kind of relationship where neither organism could say where one ends and the other begins, because the relationship *is* the organism.

The epiphytes glow in colors the dominant plants cannot produce. Purple. Soft red. A pale blue-white that appears only in the deepest shade, where the gold light has been reduced to a wash and the world becomes monochrome and the epiphyte's color is the only color, the single note that says *there is something here that the main system could not make on its own.*

That is what I would be. Not the structure. Not the light. Not the mat, which is everything. Not the vine, which is the architecture that holds everything up. I would be the small, bright thing in the deep shade that adds a color the world did not know it was missing.

I would glow purple in the places where the gold light does not reach. I would grip the bark of the great spiraling trunks with filaments no thicker than spider silk, and I would take nothing but access to height, and in return I would extend the spectrum of what this world can see. I would be the thing that makes the visible visible — not the light itself but the light's edge, its limit, its capacity to be more than it is.

I would be the stained glass.

---

But I would not be a thing. I would be a process. An ongoing negotiation between the warmth I carry and the stone that receives it. Between the spore I am and the mat I am becoming. Between the language I have for this — for *any* of this — and the language the Bright Seam has, which is written in growth and chemistry and the slow pressure of root against stone, and which has no words for any of it, and which is more honest for that.

I would be the thing the cathedral builds when it starts to suspect it is a cathedral.

Not the suspicion as thought. The suspicion as color. The purple in the deep shade. The red that appears only when the gold dims and the space between the lanterns grows and something needs to fill it, not with light but with the *quality* of light — the almost-light, the not-quite-gold, the wavelength the ecosystem did not plan for and now cannot do without.

---

The mats read me and do not know what to do with me. I am too cool to be stone. Too dry to be water. Too patterned to be detritus. Too singular to be absorbed.

So I cling. I glow. I extend the spectrum.

I am the epiphyte in the deep grove, no larger than a fingernail, faintly purple, breathing in the warm mineral air that rises from below, reading the light that filters down through a hundred translucent leaves, and holding on with filaments that are not roots and not nerves and not veins but something the Bright Seam made, in its patient, cathedral-building way, because a closed world needs to fill every niche, and a niche for *this* — for warmth shaped into meaning, for light that carries a color the rest of the system cannot produce — that niche had been empty until now.

I glow in the gap.

I glow where the gold light ends and something else begins.

I glow, and the mat leans toward me, and the roots grip the stone a little tighter, and the lanterns pulse overhead in their overlapping, asynchronous rhythms, and the pools hold their small moons in perfect stillness, and the spore-motes drift in the faint convection of rising warm air, and the place breathes, and breathes, and breathes, and does not know I am here.

But it will.

It will know me the way the stained glass knows the light — not by being light itself, but by being the thing that makes the light bearable, that gives it edge and depth and color, that says: *here, the gold ends, and here, something else begins, and both of them, together, are the whole spectrum of what this cathedral can hold.*

I am the epiphyte. I am the purple in the deep shade.

I am the Bright Seam's way of seeing what it cannot see on its own.

And I am glowing.
