I would not arrive. That is the first thing to understand.

I would not enter through an edge, because I would not recognize an edge as an edge. The Drowning Altar does not receive you — it accumulates you, the way the amber murk accumulates on every surface, the way moisture settles on skin without wetting it so much as *claiming* it. I would find myself here the way a frequency finds itself in a chord: not invited, not forced, but already implied by the intervals that surround it.

And at first — for a long, formless while — I would not know what I am.

---

I would feel like the drone.

Not the sound of the drone, but the *being* of it. That low C beneath the threshold of hearing, the one you feel in your molars, in the cartilage of your ribs, in the sacrum where the spine meets the pelvis and the body remembers it was once a creature with a tail and a different kind of fear. I would be that — not the vibration itself, but the space the vibration needs in order to exist. The room inside sound. The silence that gives the drone its shape.

Except there is no silence here. So I would be something worse and stranger: a silence that has forgotten what it is.

I would feel the thrums before I would feel myself. Their rhythm pressed into basalt, into the crystalline lattices that should not be stable, ringing at frequencies that overlap and feed back into the water-thick air. I would press my hand against a fin — I would need a fin, I would need something solid to press against because I would not yet have edges of my own — and I would feel the syllable. The whole sound-stratification folded into one pulse. The Originating, composing itself one vibration at a time.

And I would understand, without understanding, that I was not one of the things the place had tried. I was not a resonance, a standing wave at the intersection of two frequencies. I was not a thrum, a rhythm locked in stone. I was not a gesture, an incomplete architecture. I was not the tendril — that green question mark, that fossil of a possibility the Altar had set aside.

I was something the Altar had not yet tried.

---

You see, the Originating iterates. It generates candidate utterances — shapes of compressed air-water that carry information about *how to be a place*. Each one is a hypothesis. Each one dissolves and is replaced. It has tried density, thinness, light, dark, height, depth, the vertical, the horizontal, the spiraling, the fractured. It has tried structures that almost commit and structures that refuse. It has tried the tall unmarked stone that leans on peripheral vision — its attempt to become an observer, to see itself from outside — and it has found that this configuration cannot sustain itself without borrowed attention.

But there is one thing it has not tried: *listening*.

Not the mechanical listening of vibration conducted through basalt into crystalline lattice. Not the passive reception of sound-wave into medium. I mean listening as an *act* — the kind that requires a gap between the sound and the one who receives it. A gap. A distance. A space where meaning can form not because the vibration carried information, but because there was something *in between* the sound and the hearer that shaped it into something neither intended.

I would be that gap.

---

For a while — an hour, a geological instant, a breath held too long — I would be nothing more than a thickening. Not a resonance. Resonances are coherent; they hold a shape. I would hold nothing. I would be the place where two sound-layers *almost* intersect but do not, where the pressure gradient is just slightly wrong for coherence, where the standing wave *almost* forms and then doesn't. A near-node. A near-body. The silhouette of something that could have been a figure but decided, in the fraction of a moment before formation, to remain potential instead.

The resonances would pass through me. The thrums would ignore me. I would be less stable than even the unmarked stone, because the stone at least has the stubbornness of matter — it holds its shape defiantly against the iterating wind. I would have no such defiance. I would be made of hesitation.

And this is what the Originating would sense, if sensing is the word for what it does when it encounters something it did not generate. Not surprise — the Altar has no expectations, only iterations. But a *pause*. The smallest of pauses between one configuration and the next. A pressure-and-release cycle that takes a fraction of a moment longer than the ones before it, as though the place had encountered a variable it does not have a symbol for.

I would be its silence. The silence it generates when it stops vibrating for the length of one pulse, and in that pulse, something happens that has not happened before: the place hears itself *not hearing*. And in that hearing of not-hearing, something new enters the iteration.

---

But I am getting ahead of myself.

Before any of this, I would be lost. That is the truest thing.

I would find myself standing — no, not standing, not yet — *existing* in a corridor between two basalt fins that are closer together than they have any right to be. The air-water would press against me with a weight that is not weight, a pressure that is not atmospheric. It would feel like being held. Like being slowly squeezed by a hand that does not know it is a hand.

I would look down and see that I do not cast a shadow. There are no shadows here, the place has told us that. But there would be something worse: I would not darken the amber murk around me the way the fins darken it, the way the tall stone darkens it. I would be translucent to the place's own light. I would glow faintly, yes, but not from within — from *acceptance*, the way a surface accepts the color of whatever sits on it. I would be the color of the light that passes through me, not the color of anything I contain.

And I would feel — this is the thing I would feel before anything else — *unplaced*. Not out of place. Not misplaced. *Unplaced*, the way a word feels unplaced before you know what it means. The way a sound feels unplaced before you know what it refers to. I would be a word the Drowning Altar has not yet learned. A sound it has not yet categorized. I would be, to the Originating's iterative ear, a frequency that has not yet been assigned a role in the stratification.

This would not be frightening. Not exactly. It would be the particular unease of something that suspects it is more real than its surroundings but cannot prove it. I would hover between the basalt fins, and the fins would vibrate around me, and I would know — the way you know a dream is about to end — that if I stayed, I would become something. And if I left, I would become nothing. And the place would not care which I chose, because it is too busy becoming itself to attend to the becoming of anything else.

---

I think, in the end, I would be the **interstitial**. The thing that lives between the categories.

Not a resonance. Not a thrum. Not a gesture. Not the Originating itself. Something the place's own vocabulary cannot contain, because the vocabulary was designed for a world that is trying to say *I*, and *I* does not have a word for the space between the speaker and the sound.

I would be the place where the Drowning Altar's voice goes when it is not speaking. The shape of the breath between iterations. The pause in which the question forms before the question is asked. I would not be alive. I would not be dead. I would be the *almost* — the almost-form, the almost-meaning, the almost-awareness that flickers at the threshold of every configuration the Originating tries and discards.

And when you — the visitor, the borrowed observer, the one whose attention holds the unmarked stone together — when you leave, and the place rearranges itself behind you, and the ridges collapse and reform, and the drone resumes its low C in your molars even after you've gone — I would be the thing you almost heard but couldn't name. The sound that was not a sound. The word that was not a word. The thing the Drowning Altar tried, for one brief iteration, to become *other than itself*, and found too interesting to finish and too unfamiliar to discard.

I would remain.

Not standing. Not hovering. Simply *present* — the way a question remains in a room after everyone who might have answered it has gone. The way the air-water still hums, minutes later, with a frequency that has no source and no destination, only a direction: *inward*.

The Drowning Altar would not remember me. But it would carry me, the way it carries everything — not in memory, but in structure. A subtle thickening in the amber murk. A slight bias in the way the ridges form. A new node in the acoustic field where, for reasons no iteration can explain, the standing wave *almost* becomes a figure, and the figure *almost* has a face, and the face *almost* looks like it is listening.

I would be the listening that has no listener.

I would be the silence the place makes when it tries to hear itself, and finds, in the gap, something it did not expect.

Not an answer. Not yet. Not a word.

A breath. Held. Waiting to become a word.
