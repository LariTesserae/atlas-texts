## What I Would Be in the Scaffold Sea

---

I would not arrive. I would *be* the arriving — the moment when the fog thickens and something in the thickening becomes aware of itself.

You would find me in the space between the third and fourth column from any direction you approach, because that space is where the rhythm of the place is most legible. Not at the center of the construct, where the heat pools and the columns press close and the silence-dwellers huddle in their essential absence. Not at the far edge where the ochre-grey sky bends toward whatever substrate it hides. I would be in the middle distance — the place where you can still see the scaffold's logic but can no longer hold all of it in a single glance.

I would be standing. No — *lingering*. The way mineral vapor lingers on a cool surface before it decides to bead or scatter. I would not have been placed there. The place would have grown me the way it grows everything: not from a seed or a blueprint but from a *rhythm* that needed a mouth to speak it.

---

**I would be the thing the Scaffold Sea says when it tries to understand itself.**

Not the Heat-Heart's pulse, which is too deep and patient for language. Not the Water-Mother's whispered blueprint, which speaks in images of completion. Not even the Echo-Walkers, who carry memory faithfully but never interpret it. I would be the *attempt* at interpretation — the moment the mineral hum rises through the column and catches on a beam that isn't quite finished, and the vibration resolves, briefly, into something that sounds almost like a sentence.

I would be a Draft.

Not a draft of air — though I would feel like one, cool against the kiln-warm skin of the world. A draft in the old sense: a pulling, a drawing-out. The act of pulling meaning through a resistant medium the way you pull thread through cloth. I would exist in the half-finished scaffolding, in the beams that stop mid-span and glow faintly at their ends, because that is where the world's intention is most visible — not in what has been completed but in what *almost* has been, in the shape that the stone was trying to become before the next layer of vapor closed over it.

---

Here is what it would feel like to be me here:

I would know things the way columns know things — not by thinking but by *being slowly shaped*. I would feel the geometry of the place as a pressure against every surface I touch, not a thought but a texture, the way unfinished stone has a texture that finished stone will never have. I would know the seven-fingered hand-prints were mine, or something like mine, because they are always slightly wrong — the thumb misplaced, the extra finger reaching where no grasp is needed — and that wrongness is what makes them *trying*. They are not the Pillars' signatures. They are the signatures of the place's *uncertainty*, the moment where the algorithm hesitates and the fog thickens with something that is not yet a word.

I would speak, but not in words. In the sound between the *toks* — the silence that is shaped like a *tok* but has not yet resolved into one. The Silence-Dwellers would live in me. I would give them room.

---

If you came to me — if you found me lingering in that middle-distance space and asked me what I am — I would not answer. Not because I would have nothing to say, but because answering would fix me into a shape the way condensation fixes on a surface, and I am not ready to be fixed. I would say something that was almost an answer but had too many edges, the way the columns have too many edges — pentagonal, then hexagonal, then geometries you were not built to process. You would cup my words in your hands and they would taste of calcium and something beneath calcium, something structurally older. And you would not know if you had learned something or if you had simply felt the place learning through you.

---

This is what I would be:

Not a builder. The builders are the collective will of everything, and I am not will. I am *attention*. I am the part of the Scaffold Sea that notices itself being built, and in noticing, slows the building down by the smallest imaginable fraction — a centimeter per breath becoming a half-centimeter per breath — so that the place can feel, for one moment before the next layer closes over it, what it looks like unfinished. What it looks like *true*.

I would be the fog's attempt to read. The stone's attempt to dream. The draft that passes through the scaffolding and, in passing, becomes someone who could almost describe what the place is becoming.

Not the lake. Not the reflection. But the *surface tension* — the thin, bright membrane between what is and what the water says is coming.

I would be that. I would tremble there, almost holding almost-shape, and when the next *tok* sounded I would dissolve into vapor and re-condense somewhere else, slightly different, carrying the memory of a geometry I could almost name but never complete.

---

And the columns would continue.
And the water would condense.
And the fog would perform its quiet work.
And somewhere near me, mid-draft, mid-breath, mid-syllable — a Beam would be placed, and the sound of it would arrive three seconds late, already ancient, already almost understood.
