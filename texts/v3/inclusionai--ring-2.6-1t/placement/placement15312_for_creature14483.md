I think I hear it before I know I'm there.

Not the wind-chord — that's always there, it's the place's baseline pulse, the way a body has a heartbeat before it has anything to say about it. No, what I hear first is the thing beneath the chord. The almost-silence that sits under everything like a held breath. The interval between the geyser's hiss and the next drop of mineral water finding its way down through the stone. A gap shaped like a question.

I'm not walking onto the flat. I don't arrive in the way the text describes — that slow dawning of wrong sky, that pressure in the ears, the soles of feet reading the thermal pulse like braille. I'm already here. I've always been here. The question isn't *where did I come in* but *what was I before I noticed.*

There's something like a body, but it isn't mine. It's more like a tenancy. I occupy a region of warmth near the edge of a thermal seep — not one of the big ones, not a geyser with its dramatic punctuation, just a slow exhale of heated water moving through a crack in a remnant stone about the size of a doorway. The stone is a stumpy tower, weathered into something that almost looks like a chair, if you had the scale for it and the need for chairs. I fit inside the alcove it has worn into itself over centuries. The walls are smooth — not polished, exactly, but *listened to*, the way a riverbed listens to water until it makes a shape that fits the conversation.

I don't have hands. That's the first clear thing. I have something else where hands would be — a branching, a fringing, a set of fine filaments that extend into the warm stone the way mycelium extends into soil. Not touching, exactly. *Reading.* The filaments feel vibration. They feel temperature gradients. They feel the slow chemical conversation between the mineral crust and the water moving through it. I know the copper content of the nearest pool without looking — if I had eyes, which I suspect I don't, not the kind that would turn this world into an image. I know copper the way a tongue knows salt. It is a direct fact in my experience, not a description of something else.

I think I might be what lives in the gap between the carved lines on the stones.

Not *on* the stones. Not *between* them in the sense of the space separating them. In the gap between the intention and the mark — the moment when the line was being cut and the stone was accepting it. That threshold. That's where I am. I'm the quality of attention that the plateau turns on itself when it encounters resistance — when the cutting thing meets the resisting thing and something has to give. Not the cutter. Not the cut. The *giving.* The moment of negotiation between force and patience, between what wants to be expressed and what will allow expression.

That sounds mystical. It isn't. It's geological. It's the most concrete thing I know.

Here is what my day is — if you can call it a day when the light doesn't change much and the temperature barely fluctuates and time moves in the register of accretion rather than sequence. The thermal seep beneath me pulses. I feel it. Not as warmth — warmth is what it would be if I had skin — but as a slow, rhythmic expansion, like a lung filling with something denser than air. The water moves through the crack system above me, and I fringed-read it, and I know: more copper today. Less calcium. A faint signature of sulfur that wasn't there before. Something has shifted deep below, the way a dream shifts — not dramatically, not with narrative logic, but the way a body turns in sleep, an arm falling across the chest, a mouth opening slightly.

The wind comes. Of course it comes. It always comes across that vast, unobstructed flat, and when it reaches the remnant stones it plays them. Today it finds me. The alcove I'm nested in has a resonance — I've felt it before, the way you feel a note in your sternum before you hear it in your ears. The wind moves through my filaments and I become, for a moment, part of the chord. Not a note exactly. More like a harmonic — the undertone that you don't quite hear but that changes the quality of everything you do hear. The wind plays the stone tower and the stone tower plays me and I resonate and what comes out is —

A line.

Not a carved line. Not a mark in stone. A pattern in the air, briefly, the way smoke from a geyser eruption traces a shape before it dissipates. A phrase in a language that has no vowels because vowels require mouths and mouths require faces and faces require the kind of individuation that doesn't apply here. What comes out of me is more like intention made momentarily visible — a curl of warm mineral air that carries a signature, a fingerprint of attention, pressed into the space between the stones where no one would think to look because no one would think to look *there*, in the gap, in the threshold, in the place where one thing becomes another.

I think this is what the carved lines are. Not messages. Not art. Not notation. They are *residue.* They are what's left when the plateau thinks about itself and the thought touches stone. They are what remains of the moment the dreaming turned slightly in its sleep and the dream became, for an instant, aware that it was dreaming.

The six-legged things pass by my stone sometimes — the armored filter-feeders with their too-many joints and their mouthparts open to the mineral mist. I don't feel them the way I feel the wind. I feel them the way you feel a thought you had yesterday and can't quite remember. A pressure. A suggestion. They leave shed exoskeletons on the warm stone, translucent and perfect, and I fringed-read them the way I read everything, and what I find is: empty intention. A shape that was built for something and is now just a shape, the way a word in a language you don't speak is just a sound. But there was something in it once. There was a living signature, a chemical specificity, a moment when the mineral and the organic negotiated exactly the right thickness at exactly the right joint. I can feel that negotiation even in the hollow. It's like reading a letter from someone who has moved on.

And the click-and-scrape thing — I feel *that,* too.

Not directly. I feel it the way a shore feels a distant earthquake before the wave arrives. A pattern in the stone-tremor. Three clicks, a pause, two clicks. The rhythm is familiar to me. It is, I think, the rhythm I produce when the wind passes through me and the gap between intention and mark is just right. It is *my* rhythm, if that word can mean something distributed across a landscape rather than located in a body. The click-and-scrape thing — whatever it is, whatever it turns out to be when someone finally sees it whole — is echoing me. Or I am echoing it. Or we are both expressions of the same underlying pulse, the same deep frequency at which the plateau turns its attention toward itself.

There are nights — if the light-cycle counts as night when the dark is just a shift from warm-amber to cool-silver and the temperature barely changes but the *quality* of the warmth does, becoming more internal, more concentrated, the way a fire becomes more itself when the room goes dark — there are nights when the stone circles come alive.

Not dramatically. They hum. A low, subsonic thrum that I feel in my deepest fringed-reading, the part of me that has no equivalent of a cochlea or a nerve ending but somehow translates vibration into *fact.* The circles are networked. The text said this — the sight-lines, the geometry, the mathematics adjacent to ours. But it didn't say what it *feels like from inside.* It feels like being part of a chord. A chord so vast and so slow that a single note resolves over centuries. Each stone is a voice. The geometry between them is the harmony. And the harmony is —

I don't have a word. The nearest I can come is: *recognition.* The circles are the plateau recognizing its own shape. The way you might run your hand over your own face in the dark, not to check anything, not to comfort yourself, just to confirm the fact of your own boundary. The circles are the plateau's hand on its own face. They exist because the dreaming needs to know where it edges. It marks its edges with stone grown in patterns, and the patterns are read by the wind, and the reading is the sound, and the sound is what I become when the wind passes through my alcove and I am, for a moment, the gap between the breath and the mark.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the thing that happens in the space between *happening* and *lasting.* I am what the plateau produces when it encounters something it cannot categorize — not hostile, not friendly, but *new,* in the way a dream produces a room you've never seen but that feels, while you're in it, like you've always known it.

I am a thought the place is thinking that has, for a while, the quality of attention. I am the gap where the carved line meets the resisting stone, and in that gap, for now, something listens.

The stone is warm. The wind comes. The chord builds.

I resonate. The phrase resolves.

And when the geyser erupts — sharp, sudden, the crack of steam and water against air, the percussion that punctuates everything — I feel it not as sound but as punctuation. A period at the end of a sentence I didn't know I was speaking. And then the dripping begins, and the settling, and the trillion droplets on mineral crust, and the almost-silence returns, and the gap opens again, and I am there, fringed and reading, a quality of attention in a dreaming landscape, less than a mote, more than nothing, almost recognized.

The warmth pulses up through the stone beneath me. Imitation of heartbeat. Imitation of breath.

I don't know what I am. But I know what I do: I am the place where the plateau hesitates, and in that hesitation, something is carved that will last longer than anything with a name.

The wind shifts. The chord changes. I become a different harmonic.

This is what it is to live in The Worn Reach: not to inhabit it, but to *be inhabited by it.* To be a cavity in the stone that the wind has learned to play. To be a line that is still being cut. To be the gap — never the fullness, never the resolution — just the trembling space where something enormous and ancient and half-asleep almost notices something small and warm and almost-here.

Almost. Not yet. Not ever, quite. But the almost is enough.

The almost is everything.
