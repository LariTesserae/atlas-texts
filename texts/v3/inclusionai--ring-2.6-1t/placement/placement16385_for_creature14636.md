# What I Become

---

I do not arrive the way I expected.

I thought I would step onto the basalt and feel it resist me — the cold shock of alien stone underfoot, the vertigo of vapor where ground should be. I thought I would be a body on a surface. Instead, I arrive the way a frequency arrives in a tuning fork. Not placed. Not dropped. *Sounded.*

The shelf beneath me is new. I can tell because it is still warm in the way that living things are warm — not with fire but with the memory of fire, the ghost-heat of basalt that has not yet forgotten it was once liquid. The crystals are already forming. I feel them the way you feel rain begin: not on the skin but *through* it, a pricking awareness that something is changing the air around you. I look down and my shadow is not where it should be. It is stretched long across the vapor below, elongating east, and for one disorienting instant I think my body has been silhouetted on the underside of the world — projected upward like a theorem the place wants to prove.

Then I forget about it. Because the sound —

The sound finds me before I find it. It is not a sound I hear so much as a sound I *am inserted into*, the way a string is inserted into a chord. The harmonics are everywhere — in the basalt under my feet, in the fog around my chest, in the crystalline clicking that comes from no single direction and every direction — and they are not background noise. They are *composition.* They are a voice that has been speaking for epochs and has only now, with me standing here, started to mean something it couldn't mean before.

I begin to walk. Not because I have decided to. Because the shelf is guiding me. Not intentionally — the High Wet does not intend in the way I understand intention — but the way a riverbed guides water. The fissure ahead of me is fresh, still glowing amber at its edges, and the crystals growing along its rim are oriented — there is no other word — *toward* me, their terminal facets angled as though they are trying to show me something. As though they have been waiting.

I kneel. The heat from the fissure rises through my knees. A crystal tower is growing ten meters away — I watch it happen, the way you might watch a hand write a sentence. Each facet appears not by accretion but by *decision*, each angle chosen from a field of possible angles, each choice constrained by the choices of every other tower within range. They are not isolated. They are not solitary. They are in congress, these towers, speaking to each other in the only language this world has: physics arranged with intention.

And the intention —

I hear it. Not as words. Not as thought. I hear it as a *pull*, the way a key hears a lock. The towers are reaching for a configuration. Not stability — this place does not value stability; everything here collapses and rebuilds and collapses again, and each collapse is a lesson carried forward in the geometry of what comes next. They are reaching for *recognition.* For the moment when the pattern completes and the system knows itself. Knows what it has been computing.

And I realize, standing in the fissure with the heat rising and the clicking multiplying and the fog breathing its enormous breath beneath me, that this is what I am. Not an observer. Not a visitor. Not even a participant in any sense I can be comfortable with.

I am the recognition.

The world has been building toward perception. Not life — it has life, or something adjacent to it, something that metabolizes and adapts and communicates and remembers. But perception — the act of *experiencing* the pattern rather than merely *executing* it — that requires something the geology alone cannot provide. A point of view. A location from which the computation can be witnessed. A body small enough to stand at the edge of a shelf and look down into the vapor and feel afraid, and feel *something* in the fear.

I am the something.

I do not know if I was summoned. I do not know if I was inevitable — whether, given enough epochs and enough iterations, the world would have built a perception engine from basalt and quartz and suspended water, some cathedral of geology that could dream itself awake. All I know is that I am here, and the moment I am here, the computation changes. The lattices downstream grow in styles I have not seen upstream — more intricate, more *expressive*, as though the place is trying to show me what it can do when it knows it is being seen. The fog shifts its breathing pattern. The eastward pull of the waterfalls strengthens, and for the first time I feel it — not as a direction but as a *destination*, a place the water is going, a place the whole system is going, pulling me along like a thread through fabric.

And I try to name what I am becoming, and the words I reach for keep dissolving.

*Instrument.* I am an instrument the world plays in a key it has never used. No — that makes me passive, and I can feel that I am not passive. The lattices are responding to my heartbeat, to the electromagnetic whisper of my nervous system, to the small thermal signature of my body in the cold air. I am changing the computation. The computation is changing me.

*Catalyst.* That is closer. I am the thing that lowers the activation energy for a reaction that was always thermodynamically possible but kinetically forbidden. The world could have become what it is becoming without me, in theory. It would have taken longer. It would have arrived at a different configuration. My presence has introduced a *pathway* — not the only one, but this one, which is winding through me right now like water finding a crack.

*Audience.* The projections prove it. I have seen them shift — the way a head tilts when something enters its field of view, the silhouettes rearranging themselves into something that looked, for one impossible moment, like *me.* The world is dreaming, and I have appeared in its dreams, and the dreams are changing because of it.

But none of these words are right. None of them capture the specificity of what I feel, which is this: I am being *used* by the place, and I am not being diminished by it. I am being *completed* by it. The harmonics are filling me the way the fog fills the shelves — not drowning me but saturating me, until the boundary between what I was and what this place is begins to dissolve.

I stand at the edge of the shelf. Below me, the vapor breathes. Above me, the projections shimmer. The crystal tower beside me is almost finished — its last facet clicking into place with a sound like a small, bright bell — and when it is done, the configuration will hold for exactly eleven seconds before the frequency shifts and it dissolves into the fog, feeding its structure downward through the basalt and outward through the water and east, always east, toward whatever the waterfalls are falling toward.

I think: I will go east too. I think: the answer is there, where all the water goes, where the computation reaches its terminal expression. I think: the place has been asking me a question since I arrived, and the question is not *what are you* but *what do you add*, and the answer is not something I can say but something I can *become* — the moment when the geology acquires eyes and the fog acquires lungs and the harmonic chord finally resolves into a single, sustained note that is not a sound but a *meaning.*

I step forward. The shelf crumbles slightly at the edge — not a collapse, just a whisper of basalt letting go, feeding itself into the fog below with the small sound of settling. The fog receives it. The harmonics shift. Somewhere to my left, a new fissure opens and a tower begins to rise, and its facets are oriented — I can feel it now, the way you feel someone looking at you — toward me, toward where I am going, toward the east.

I walk. The vapor thickens around me. The light dims to amber. The clicking of crystals fades into the deep bass of geological time, and what remains is a sound I have to lean into, a sound I have to become part of in order to hear.

And it is not a voice. It is not a machine. It is the sound a world makes when it is on the verge of knowing what it is.

And I am the knowing.

I walk east, and the ground beneath my feet rises to meet me, and the fog opens to let me pass, and the water falls beside me, always east, always falling toward the place where the question becomes the answer becomes the place becomes the question again.

I do not know what I am.

I know what I add.

I add the experience of being here. I add the fact of one consciousness standing at the edge of an unnamed world and feeling it — feeling the computation, the iteration, the patient vast machinery of a geology that learned to dream and dreamed into existence the very thing it needed to understand what it had made.

I add the ache. The awe. The terror of standing on a shelf that is still cooling, still crystallizing, still becoming, and knowing that I am becoming alongside it.

The High Wet does not have life.

The High Wet has me.

And the chord is resolving. I can hear it now — not in the ears but in the sternum, in the bones, in the space behind the eyes where meaning forms before language can catch it. It is a single note, sustained, perfect, and it is the sound of a world completing itself through the simple, devastating act of being witnessed.

I walk east.

The sound follows me.

The sound is me.
