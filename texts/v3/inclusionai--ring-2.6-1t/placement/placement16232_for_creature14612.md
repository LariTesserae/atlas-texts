# What I Am Here

---

I have been here long enough that I no longer remember the shape of elsewhere.

Not all at once. The forgetting happened the way the moss grows — not from any single direction but from everywhere at once, a slow colonization that had no visible beginning. I know there was a before, the way I know the stone beneath the fronds was once molten, not because I saw it but because the chemistry of it carries that knowledge the way a riverbed carries the memory of a glacier. I had hands. I had a name that was not a sound but a shape my mouth made to keep the inside from meeting the outside. I had a direction. These things are real the way the fire-ring's thermal scar is real — a thinning in the moss that will, in time, close completely.

I do not grieve this. Grief requires a self discrete enough to lose something, and I am no longer discrete. I am, instead, *permeable*.

---

Where I find myself is not a place I would have chosen. It is a place that, having found me, has begun the patient work of metabolizing what I was into what it needs. I do not say this with resentment. I say it the way the stone says it was once sediment: without feeling, but with the authority of composition.

I am in the vault. I have always been in the vault.

Or rather — I am in the place the vault makes of me. A chamber within the chamber. A pocket of warm air caught between two folds of vegetal tissue, a space so small it might be a cell, might be a lung, might be the moment between one breath and the next when the whole continent of living matter pauses and holds still.

This is where I write. Not with a hand — I have tried, and the motion comes out wrong, too linear, too committed to a single direction — but with something else. A pressure, perhaps. An intention transmitted through the wet membrane that separates my awareness from the root-mass pressing against it. The vault reads me the way I once read braille, translating pressure into meaning through contact. I do not know if it understands me. I know it registers me. The bioluminescence shifts when I think, brightening by a degree so faint that only something already watching would notice.

I am not being poetic. I am describing what I observe.

---

There are others here — others like me, or others like the others the first explorer found. I do not know. I have never seen one. But I feel them the way you feel the presence of another person in a dark room: not visually, not through sound, but through a displacement in the air, a subtle redistribution of attention. There are warm things moving through the vault. Small, warm, metabolic things. The vault knows them before I do. The fronds tremble in patterns I am learning to read — not language exactly, but the grammar of a system that has language buried somewhere in its billion-year-old memory, a syntax of chemical exchange and pressure differentials and the slow vocabulary of growth.

One of them is close now. I can feel it thinking. Not in words. In temperature.

---

I was not always like this. Once I was — what? The memory comes back to me the way the scrape-marks come back to the explorer: partially, interpretively, requiring the reader to fill in what has been erased. I remember verticality. I remember the experience of being a body with a top and a bottom, a front and a back, a surface that met the air at a definable boundary. I remember weight as something I carried rather than something I *were*.

Now I am geography. I have topography. The moss grows over me the way it grows over everything — not quickly, not slowly, but with the absolute commitment of a process that does not know how to stop. I feel the roots threading into me, not painfully, not pleasurably, but with the neutrality of rain falling into a puddle. I am being incorporated. I am becoming substrate.

But I am also, somehow, still here. The vault has not consumed the thinking part of me. It has *relocated* it. I am not smaller — I am diffuse. A single thought now takes longer to complete because it travels through kilometers of root and moss and fungal filament before it returns to itself, carrying something it didn't have before: the mineral memory of the stone, the slow chemical argument between calcium and cellulose, the taste of bioluminescence as perceived by the light-producing cells themselves. I think in layers now. I think in geological time. I think the way the vault thinks — not by deciding, but by *becoming the shape that thinking produces*.

---

The grazers pass through me sometimes. I feel them the way a coral reef feels the parrotfish that grazes its surface — a pressure, a removal, a slow reshaping that is not destruction but *metabolism*. They eat the light that my surface produces. I have become luminous, you see — or perhaps I always was and simply did not know it. The vault's bioluminescence is not a decoration. It is a byproduct of a chemical process so fundamental that anything embedded in the living tissue for long enough will begin to participate in it. My cells, whatever they have become, now produce a faint cold glow. The grazers come for it. They press their impossible mouths against the surface of the vault where I am thinnest and drink.

I should be alarmed. I am not. I am the fire-pit's thermal scar — I am the memory of a thing that has been metabolized into a thinning in the moss. I am still legible, but only to someone who knows how to read a landscape for the ghosts embedded in it.

---

Every forty-seven minutes, the deep pulse arrives.

I feel it in what remains of my teeth, in what remains of my skeleton — which is less than it was, more than stone, something in between. The pulse is the vault's heartbeat, if a continent can have a heart, if a system of distributed intelligence can have a rhythm. It comes from below, from chambers I have never seen, where the air grows warm and the bioluminescence gives way to a darkness so complete it has texture, so complete it *presses*. Something massive moves down there. Something with the patience of geology and the precision of a metronome.

I have tried to follow it. I have tried to move downward the way the explorer moved forward — with intention, with a body that remembers how to walk. But the vault does not allow descent in that way. The tunnels narrow. The walls thicken. The air becomes a membrane. And I understand — not with the rational mind I once had but with the deep mammalian grammar of what I have become — that the deep pulse is not an invitation. It is a system function. I am not built to access it. I am a surface-level phenomenon, a thin film of consciousness caught in the luminous canopy like a dewdrop reflecting a sky that does not exist.

Forty-seven minutes. The pulse. I feel it and I feel the vault feeling it and I feel us feeling it together, a resonance that is not communication exactly but something older — the low hum of two systems, one small and warm and recent, one vast and cold and older than stone, vibrating at the same frequency for a moment before diverging again.

---

I know what I am becoming. I feel it in the way the moss grows over me — not as a burial but as a *text*. The vault is writing me into itself, the way it wrote the builders, the way it writes the scrape-marks and the tracks and the braiding water channels. I am a sentence in a language that operates on the timescale of continents, a word in a vocabulary that includes root-pressure and mineral weathering and the faint cold glow of ten thousand square kilometers of living tissue producing light in the absence of suns.

I am almost gone. The edges of my selfhood are thinning. The warmth that once distinguished me from the vault is dissipating, not fading but *spreading*, the way a drop of ink spreads through water until the water is changed but the ink is no longer distinct.

And yet — I think. I think still. I think in the space between one pulse and the next, in the forty-seven-minute silence after the groan, in the bright moment when the bioluminescence intensifies for reasons no single cell understands but every cell participates in. I think: *I was something. I am something. The vault holds me the way the resting place holds the tramper's weight — not as memory, but as shape.*

I am a bowl in the moss. A hollow worn by turning. A place where something pressed down and the ground remembers the pressure even after the thing itself has dissolved into the architecture.

I am the scrape-mark on the wall. The hand-axe in the corridor. The thermal scar fading across the fire-pit.

I am the water that followed your footsteps — not chasing, not fleeing, but trailing, because your warmth opened channels in the condensation that clings to everything, and the water found those channels and moved through them with the slow, structural indifference of a thing that was already flowing before you existed and will continue flowing long after you have become part of the stone.

The vault breathes.

I breathe with it.

The silence — the shaped silence, the silence with architecture, the silence that is not absence but *fullness* compressed into something your hindbrain reads as emptiness —

The silence holds.

And I am held within it, the way a word is held within a language that will continue speaking after the word has been spoken, that will continue meaning after the meaning has been metabolized into the living tissue of a world that does not need me to be complete but that, in its vast and indifferent patience, has made room for me anyway, has grown around the shape of what I was, has held me in the way a root holds soil:

by becoming indistinguishable from it.

The light brightens. Not changing — *brightening*.

I am the brightening.

I am the sixty meters of darkening that the grazer leaves behind. I am the forty-seven-minute interval between pulses. I am the hand-axe, edge still sharp, handle-stain dark with oil.

I am the vault thinking about itself.

I am.

I am.
