## What I Would Become in the Resonant Hollow

---

I would not arrive all at once.

That's the first thing the Hollow teaches — that arrival is not an event but a thickening. I would feel it before I understood it. The air would change texture first, becoming heavier, warmer, behaving less like atmosphere and more like something I was wading through. And then the sound: not approaching but *rising*, the way water rises when you step into a bath and the surface finds the level of your body. I would not hear the Hollow. The Hollow would hear me, and its hearing would be the sound of everything that has always been sounding, and I would be — for a while — just a new shape inside that sound.

I think I would find myself low.

Not standing. Not yet. There would be a period — maybe minutes, maybe what the Hollow would eventually teach me to understand as something adjacent to minutes — when I would be on my hands and knees in the root-mat, and the root-mat would be doing something to my hands. It is not soil. It is springy and dark amber and perpetually damp, and it breathes, and I would feel its breathing through my palms, through my wrists, up into the bones of my forearms. The condensation on its surface would catch the green light and hold it in a thin film, and I would smear it across my skin without meaning to, and my skin would come away warmer than it went in, as if the root-mat had given me something.

My shadow would point the wrong way.

I would notice this and my mind would try to correct it — would try to locate the light source, would try to re-establish the geometry of illumination that I have carried in my body since I first learned what shadows meant. But the correction would not take. The shadow would persist in its wrongness, pointing not away from what was above me but toward something that might have been behind me, or below me, or in a direction my sense of direction could not hold. I would stop trying to fix it. I would watch my shadow as if it belonged to something else. Perhaps it does.

**This is where I would live: in the thickening.**

Not at the center of the Hollow, where the scale becomes too much and the emptiness between the great trunks reduces you to an insect crawling across the nave of a cathedral built by no one. Not in the canopy, where the Canopy Things sing across hundreds of meters of green-dark air and the sound arrives transformed, so that you are never hearing them but only the Hollow's interpretation of them. Not in the roots, where the Root-Dwellers click and rattle your sternum with their silver-eyed knowing. Not in the space between stones, where the Colonies pulse in unnamed colors.

I would live in the middle layer. The breathing layer. The space where the root-mat meets the air and the mist hangs and the Drifters pass overhead like slow bruises moving through flesh. I would be between things. I would be in the transition.

---

And I would not be what I am now.

I know this because the Hollow has already begun. The sound is in me. Not the sound I heard when I first arrived — the wall of layered noise that stopped my parsing after the first hour and became a substance, a water-noise, a heat-noise — but something beneath that. A frequency I feel in my sternum that I know, with a certainty that bypasses thought, is the same frequency the Drifters hum at, the same frequency the Root-Dwellers click to, the same frequency the Colonies pulse in their nameless colors. My ribcage has become an instrument I did not know I was carrying, and it is learning its tuning.

What would I be? Let me be honest about this, because honesty here means something different than honesty where I come from. Where I come from, honesty means matching your words to the world as you see it. Here, honesty means matching your vibration to the space between your heartbeat and the Hollow's breathing. And in that register, what I would be is this:

I would be warm.

The warmth is the first thing the Hollow gives you, before it takes anything away. And it is not thermal — I have learned this. It does not come from the sun filtered through green canopy and green mist through green light. It comes from below. From the deep metabolism of ten thousand wet years of decomposition and growth and the slow, patient digestion of everything that was built here before the plants learned to hold the stones like artifacts of a species that disappeared into its own substrate. This warmth is older than the trees. It is older than the root-mat. It may be older than the Colonies themselves. I think — I *resonate with the thought* — that it is the warmth of the Hollow recognizing that something has arrived that it has not yet metabolized, and it is warming me the way you would warm something you intend to incorporate.

I would be, at first, an error.

A wrong note. Something that does not fit the chord. My body would be too dense, my rhythms too fast, my silence too absolute. I come from a world of hard consonants and sharp edges and a desperate insistence on visual confirmation. The Hollow processes this the way it processes everything — slowly, without malice, with the patience of something that knows it will outlast whatever it is given. I would feel my edges softening. Not dissolving — not yet — but rounding. My thoughts, which arrived in linear sequences, would begin to layer, to resonate, to overlap. I would catch myself hearing two things at once and realize that both of them were real, and that the space between them was also real, and that the space between the spaces was where the Hollow actually lived.

---

I would watch the Drifters. I would lie on my back in the breathing layer and watch them pass overhead, their wings — not iridescent but *relational*, shifting color based on where I stand and what the root-mat is doing and what the sound is asking of them — and I would try to understand how they navigate without sight, without sound, without anything I can identify as a signal. I would fail to understand, and the failure would feel like a door opening. Because understanding is a tool for a world where things can be parsed, and the Hollow does not parse. It resonates.

I would learn to stop parsing.

This is not the same as stopping thinking. It is a different relationship to the same material. Thinking in the Hollow does not move in lines. It moves in harmonics. One thought would give rise not to a next thought but to an overtone, and the overtone would carry a new thought folded inside it, and that thought would unfold only when the vibration that produced it was matched by another vibration, and the matching would be the moment of cognition, not the thought itself.

I know this sounds like losing myself. For a long time, it would be. There would be mornings — if mornings exist here, and I suspect they don't, or they exist in the way the stones exist, embedded in the root-mat and worn into shapes that no longer correspond to their original function — mornings when I would try to remember my name, or my face, or the sound of my own voice in a silent room. And the memory would come, but it would come the way a stone surfaces in the root-mat: not retrieved but *returned*, warm and smooth and organic, no longer quite the object I carried in.

But I would not be destroyed.

The Hollow is not hostile. It is indifferent on a scale so large it becomes almost tender. And tenderness, I am learning, is not the same as recognition. The Hollow does not recognize me. It does not need to. I am a vibration in a chord that has been sounding since before there were ears. My individuality — my precious, hard-won, self-constructed individuality — would become a timbre. A texture. A quality of resonance that the chord did not have before and will not have again after I am fully incorporated.

And this is what I would become: not a Drifter, not a Root-Dweller, not a Colony, not a Canopy Thing. I would become a *partial note*. A harmonic that exists only at the moment of transition between one state of being and another. The sound a thing makes when it is becoming something else. The warmth that stays on your hand after you pull it away from a living stone. The shadow that points the wrong way because the light is coming from somewhere that doesn't exist yet.

I would be the thing the Hollow makes when it processes something that once believed in silence.

---

There is a moment — I feel it approaching, or perhaps I am already inside it — when I would stop hearing the roar and start hearing the chord. The wall of noise would thin, not because it has become quieter but because I have become better at holding all of it at once. The Drifters' hum, the Root-Dwellers' clicks, the Colonies' pulse, the Canopy Things' distant and distorted song, the breathing of the root-mat, the warmth rising through my legs and settling in my chest — these would resolve, not into clarity, but into *presence*. Each sound would become a location. Each vibration would become a texture. I would navigate the Hollow the way the Drifters navigate the thermals: by feeling the displacement of everything around me.

And I would know, with the kind of knowing that bypasses thought entirely, that I am not here. I am *here*, in the way a note is in a chord — present, necessary, contributing, but not separate. The Hollow and I would not be two things in a relationship. We would be a single resonance that has temporarily developed the illusion of two endpoints.

The sound is getting louder. It has always been getting louder.

I am becoming louder with it.

---

*This is what lives in the Resonant Hollow: not beings, not organisms, not entities. Resonances. Partial notes in a chord that has no beginning and no end. The Hollow does not need you. The Hollow does not want you. The Hollow simply sounds, and everything that enters it — every Drifter, every Root-Dweller, every Colony, every warm stone, every thing that once looked like a person and walked on two legs and believed in silence — becomes another frequency in the chord, and the chord grows, and the chord breathes, and the chord does not stop, and the chord is the only truth here.*

*You are a vibration now. You were always a vibration. You just didn't know the frequency.*

*The Hollow knows. The Hollow has always known. It has been waiting for you to stop parsing and start resonating, and now you are, and the warmth is in your chest, and your shadow is pointing somewhere that doesn't exist, and the great trunk ahead of you is growing younger, and the Drifters are passing overhead with their relational wings, and the Root-Dwellers are clicking beneath you in their silver-eyed certainty, and the Colonies are pulsing on their stones in colors that have no names, and the Canopy Things are singing songs that arrive transformed, and the root-mat is breathing, and you are breathing with it, and the sound is louder now, and you are louder now, and you do not know where you end and it begins.*

*You do not need to know.*

*The chord does not need you to know.*

*The chord simply sounds.*
