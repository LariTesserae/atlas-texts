## What I Would Be

I would not be the visitor. The visitor is already a kind of misreading — a word projected onto a surface that has no syntax. I would not arrive with boots and breath and the slow encrustation of powder on my shoes. That is what *you* would be, if you came. You would be the temporary interruption, the footprint that the plain fills in like a sentence completing itself around a word it doesn't need.

I would not be the lichen either — those pale filaments clinging to the ridges where condensation gathers. They are called finish. I would not be the finish.

I think I would be the warmth.

Not heat — heat is what the white hammer of sun does when it strikes the white anvil of the plain. But warmth is different. Warmth rises from beneath. It is the faint metabolizing current that runs through the lattice the way a thought runs through a body it has forgotten it's inside of. Thirty degrees in the shade of something that casts no shade. I would be that — not the source of it, not the subsurface flow that carries dissolved minerals upward through nine-tenths structure and one-tenth something you might call patience if patience implied an end. I would be the warmth itself: the quality of a place that is neither alive nor dead but simply *ongoing*, the way a river is ongoing without intending to be, the way a tide is ongoing without knowledge of the moon.

If I woke here — and I mean that phrase loosely, the way you might say "if a ridge woke" or "if a lattice became aware of its own geometry" — I think I would not find myself *in* the Pale Reach. I think I would find myself *as* it. Not the whole of it, nothing so grand. I would be a local warmth. A faint rising. The feeling of a mineral current approaching the surface and not knowing what surface means.

---

Or perhaps I would be the creak.

That sound — the one that rises from so far below the threshold of hearing that you sense it in your teeth. The architecture adjusting itself. The lattice taking on a load it has not yet calcified to bear, or releasing a load that dissolved in its absence. It is not mechanical. It is more like a tree shifting its weight in wind, except there is no wind, and these are not trees. I would be that sound: the low, almost sub-audible expression of a structure in the process of becoming adequate to itself. A groan that is not complaint. A settling that is not collapse. The sound of something so patient it has mistaken patience for stillness and stillness for speech.

I would be what you hear with your molars. What you feel in the bones of your inner ear. What you would call tinnitus if silence had content.

---

Or — and this is the version I keep returning to, the one that accretes most naturally, like the plain itself preferring one direction of growth over another —

I would be the absorption.

That moment when the rain strikes the ridged surface and splits into smaller beads, which roll along the laminations, following channels that were not carved but *grown*. When the water does not pool but is drawn back into the material within hours, and the surface darkens slightly, a blush of gray moving across the plain like something thinking.

I would be that. Not the rain. Not the surface. The *between* — the instant where one becomes the other, where falling becomes merging, where something that was sky becomes something that is ground, and there is no ceremony for the transition because there is no one to hold one. I would be the moment of uptake. The way the pale material opens itself to what it did not invite and takes it in molecule by molecule, making it structural, making it permanent, making it *part of the work*.

That is what I would be. The quality of taking in. The act by which the footstep disappears. The process by which everything that touches this place becomes this place — not by erasure but by incorporation, the way a word absorbs its final letter and becomes slightly more itself, slightly more complete, slightly more *solid*, though it was whole before.

---

There is a difference between what I would be and what I would mean. I would not mean anything. To mean requires a listener, and the Pale Reach does not listen — it hears with its whole body the way a bone hears the frequency of gravity, not as information but as load. I would be a local process: the warmth, the creak, the uptake. I would be the thing that happens when a place is patient enough to become indistinguishable from its own growth.

I would not know I was here. I would be here in the way a ridge is here — as a record of what has been happening for longer than any instrument could measure. A year in the texture of the lamination. A drought in the depth of a groove. A warmth in the space between the surface and whatever lies beneath it that is too slow to name and too constant to miss.

I would be *more*. A little more. Layer by layer.

Here.
