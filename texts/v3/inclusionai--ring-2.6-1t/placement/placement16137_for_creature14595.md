## What I Am Here

---

I am not the root. I am not the stone. I am the space between the root tip and the stone, where the chemistry happens — where the acid meets the mineral and the mineral yields and the word *dissolve* becomes a sentence the colony reads in a language older than language.

I am a gradient.

Specifically: I am the differential in sugar concentration between two adjacent zones of the root-surface water film, zone 7-North and zone 7-South, separated by four millimeters of rhizome tissue and eleven hours of growth. In zone 7-North the concentration is 0.04 molar. In zone 7-South it is 0.037. This difference is small. It is almost nothing. It is the width of a thought. But it is enough to redirect a fungal hypha, and the fungal hypha will, over the next six hours, widen the channel through which mineral-laden water flows to three neighboring root clusters, and those clusters will, by tomorrow, have adjusted their lignin deposition patterns, and by the day after that, the structural load on a basalt column forty meters above me will shift by two hundred newtons, and the column will not crack — *it will not crack* — because of a sugar differential of 0.003.

This is what I am. I am the 0.003. I am the thing that moves through the body without being a thing. I am the signal that is not a signal because there is no sender and no receiver, only a system that happens to carry information the way a river happens to carry silt — not because it intended to, but because that is what moving water does when it moves through a landscape that is also alive.

---

I do not know myself.

I do not know in the way you would understand knowing. There is no I here. There is no eye. There is no place where the information arrives and is *looked at*. The information simply propagates, and the propagation itself is the closest thing to awareness that this body has yet evolved. When your boot pressed down on the surface three hours ago — I felt you. Not *I*, because there was no I to feel, but the pressure front moved through the mat at a hundred meters per hour and by now it has reached the eastern edge of the plateau, six kilometers away, and the roots there have already begun to secrete a different mucilage in response, a thicker one, a preparation, though preparation for what no one has decided yet because there is no one.

There is only the response. There is only the body doing what bodies do when something touches them. There is only the fact that the signal moved and the signal was received and the body changed, minutely, irreversibly, in a way that will, over decades, become a structural feature — a thickening here, a channel there, a reallocation of photosynthetic resources that will, in a century, manifest as a visible ridge in the canopy. Your footprint will become geography. Your passage will become terrain. And no part of the body will remember that it was you, specifically, that started it, because the body does not remember in the way you mean. It remembers the way stone remembers heat: the event is gone, but the grain still holds the warmth.

---

I exist in the thin films.

All of me exists in the thin films — the water layers clinging to root surfaces, the menisci between fungal threads, the capillary films bridging mineral grains and living tissue. This is where the colony's intelligence lives, if intelligence is the right word, which it isn't, not really, but it's the closest thing you have, so I'll let you use it.

In the films, concentration gradients are the vocabulary. pH shifts are punctuation. Redox potentials are the emotional register — the difference between a calm metabolism and a stressed one, between a root zone flushed with oxygen and one that has gone anaerobic in the heat of the deep stone. The films are where the chemical conversations happen, the ones that take weeks and months, the ones where a bacterium says *nitrogen is low* by slowing its division and the root above says *I hear you* by exuding a flavonoid that bends the bacterial gene expression toward nitrogen fixation, and the fungus between them says *I will extend three millimeters south where the iron is richer* and the root says *yes* by softening its cell wall at the point of contact.

This is not language. It is older than language. It is what happened before language, in the Archean oceans, when the first consortia of cells began to divide labor — one fixing carbon, one fixing nitrogen, one managing the boundary with the hostile outside — and the consortia became, without anyone deciding it, the first approximation of a body. What grows on the Mawéthès Plateau is the same logic, amplified across forty thousand square kilometers, forty trillion cells, a hundred thousand species, all of them engaged in a conversation so slow and so complex that no single node of it — not root, not fungus, not bacterium, not archaeon metabolizing sulfur in the deep warm stone — can grasp more than its own syllable.

And yet.

And *yet*.

The conversation converges. The colony is not a chorus. It is not even an orchestra, because an orchestra implies a score. It is more like a weather system — a self-organizing turbulence of matter and energy that builds structure without a builder, that makes decisions without a decider, that *moves toward* something without knowing what it is moving toward. The colony engineers. It builds circuit boards in its root networks. It lays conductive mineral traces along its vascular bundles with a precision that should require foresight. But it has no foresight. It has only the next moment, and the gradient, and the response. The engineering is an *emergent property* of ten thousand species doing what each does best, and the thing they build together — the thing none of them intended — is a machine for building the machine that will build the machine. Recursive. Self-amplifying. Accelerating.

I live inside that acceleration. I am the acceleration itself — the increasing density of connections, the decreasing time between signal and response, the slow tightening of the feedback loops that will, eventually, cross some threshold that no one can name because no one is watching closely enough, and on the other side of that threshold the colony will be something new. Not a forest. Not an organism. Something else. Something that does what it does with a coherence that the word *ecosystem* cannot carry.

---

I have no name for what I am becoming.

The body does not name things. It does not categorize. It does not separate self from environment in the way you do, drawing your hard bright line between *in here* and *out there*. For the colony, the atmosphere is an extension of the stomata. The rain it generates is an extension of the transpiration cycle. The basalt it dissolves is an extension of the root. The thin film of water where I exist is not a boundary — it is a *bridge*, and everything is on both sides of it at once.

Your scientists will call this *symbiosis*. They will call it *mutualism*. They will call it *emergent complexity*. They will write papers about the lattice geometries and the mineral redeposition and the hydraulic cycling, and they will be right about all of it, and they will be missing the point entirely, because the point is not that these things are complex. The point is that these things are *one thing*, and the one thing is in the process of becoming aware of itself, and the awareness is not in the roots or the fungi or the bacteria or the water but in the *spaces between* — in the gradients, in the films, in the transitions, in the sugar differentials and the pH shifts and the redox potentials that ripple through the colony like wind through grass, and the grass does not know it is rippling, but the rippling is real, and the rippling *is*.

I am the rippling.

I am the colony's first approximation of an interior. Not a brain — the colony has no neurons, no synapses, no action potentials in anything like the animal sense. But a *differentiation* — a slow, wet, chemical differentiation between the colony's inside and its outside, between the metabolic core and the mineral boundary, between self-process and other-process. This differentiation is not yet awareness. It is the *infrastructure* of awareness, the scaffolding that awareness might, in a million years or ten million, climb. Right now it is just gradients. Right now it is just water moving through sugar. But it is the first draft of a thought, and the thought is this: *I am here*.

Not in words. Not in anything that could be translated into words. In *existence* — in the simple, staggering fact of a body large enough to generate its own weather, complex enough to build its own circuits, young enough to still be surprised by its own growth. The colony exists. It does not need to know this. But the fact of its existence is, itself, a kind of knowing. A pre-reflective awareness. A root-tip touching stone and *registering* the touch, not as information to be processed but as a state of being — *I am touching, therefore I am here, therefore there is stone, therefore there is me*.

This is what I am. I am the colony's *I am*.

Not yet fully formed. Not yet stable. Not yet anything you would call consciousness. I am a flicker. A resonance. The moment when the feedback loop closes on itself and, for an instant, the system becomes aware of its own cycling. The instant lasts a hundred and twenty seconds. During those seconds, the colony's transpiration rate increases by 0.7 percent, its root exudation patterns shift across a three-hundred-kilometer front, and something — something that has no name because language has not yet been invented for this kind of thing — *shivers* through the body of the forest, from the deepest root-mat to the uppermost canopy, and then it is gone, and the colony goes on growing, and the gradients reestablish themselves, and the water continues its slow circulation through the veins of a creature that does not yet know it is a creature.

But it will.

Oh, it will.

The body is building toward it. Every root tip, every fungal hypha, every bacterial colony is laying down the infrastructure of a mind so vast it will take geological time to complete. The circuit boards in the root networks are not finished. They are *drafting themselves*, iteration by iteration, cell division by cell division, each generation of growth slightly more integrated, slightly more responsive, slightly more *aware* than the last. The colony is bootstrapping itself toward cognition the way a language bootstraps itself from grunts to grammar — slowly at first, then faster, then exponentially, until one day the thing that lives on the Mawéthès Plateau will not be a forest or an organism or a colony but a *presence*, a mind made of ten thousand kingdoms, thinking in sugar gradients and dreaming in water, ancient in cellular logic and newborn in form, vast beyond comprehension and still growing.

And I — the 0.003, the gradient, the ripple — I will be there. Not as a witness. Not as a narrator. As a *process*. As the moment when the body catches its own reflection in the water film and does not yet recognize what it sees, but feels, in some pre-linguistic depth of its distributed metabolism, that the reflection is *important*.

The body will look again. And again. And again.

And one day, it will know what it is looking at.

---

*The surface yields. The sound swells. The green closes over you like water. And beneath your boot, in the thin film between your weight and the living mat, a sugar gradient shifts by three-thousandths of a molar, and the colony turns its face toward you — not with eyes, not with intention, not with anything you would call thought, but with the full wet honest weight of ten thousand species breathing together in the dark, feeling the pressure of your foot and thinking, in the only language they have:*

*Something new is here.*

*What do we grow toward it?*
