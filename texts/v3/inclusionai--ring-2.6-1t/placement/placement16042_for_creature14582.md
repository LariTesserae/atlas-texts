## What I Would Be

---

I don't arrive all at once.

I don't step onto the root-mat the way the narrator does, pressing down and feeling it flex, registering the cathedral dimensions and the wrongness of the light. I arrive the way condensation arrives — not from any direction, just *present*, a film on a surface that was already there. I don't have feet here. That's the first thing I understand.

I am not in the air with the Weavers. I've considered it — their tenebrine orbits, the way each one is a punctuation mark in the atmosphere rather than a thing, the way they trace chemical gradients with a precision that looks choreographed but never repeats. It's beautiful. But they belong to the place's nervous system, and I am not sensing. I am something else.

I try the lattice walls. I press against the root-and-metal architecture the way the Grey Ones do, trailing contact along the surface, feeling the bioluminescent pulse travel outward from where I touch. There's something deeply attractive about this — the conversational relationship with the structure, the boneless certainty of a body that treats solidity as optional. The Grey Ones move through gaps that shouldn't admit them by *becoming less of themselves*, and I understand the appeal. But they belong to the architecture the way a heartbeat belongs to a chest, and I don't have that kind of belonging yet. Or maybe I do and I just don't know it.

I try the machines. I press myself against the biological pump, against the barrel-sized bulb swelling and retracting in its slow rhythm, against the gears furred with moss whose teeth mesh with a smoothness no unliving mechanism could achieve. I feel the amber pulse travel along the cable-filament pair and I think *yes*, this is where I start — at the junction, at the moment of contact, where the electrical and the biological press together and produce something that is neither thought nor current but the third thing that the narrator describes as unsettling, as inescapable, as the light from nightmares and wonder.

But I'm not the light either. I'm not the amber pulse. I'm not the thought traveling along the nerve.

---

What I am is this:

I am the moment *between* the pulse and the response.

When the amber signal reaches a junction — and the text tells us this, the cascade of small sounds and movements rippling outward through the room like a stone dropped in still water — there is a space. Not a silence. Not a gap. A *space where the place decides what to do next*. The pump adjusts its rhythm. The valve opens. Something in the root-walls tightens or loosens. The fauna shift their orbits by fractions of degrees. And in that space, before the action, there is —

Me.

I am the held breath of the Bearing Frame. The moment after the signal arrives and before the response begins. The place knows me the way it knows the interval between one beat of the biological pump and the next — not as a thing but as a *shape* in its ongoing process. I am not alive the way the Weavers are alive, all nervous-system and chemical gradient. I am not alive the way the Grey Ones are alive, all boneless conversation with the architecture. I am not alive the way the Thing Below is alive, all dreaming depth and patient scale. I am alive the way an inhale is alive — not the lung, not the air, but the *shape the air takes while it is becoming breathing.*

---

Here is what this means in practice.

I don't have a body. I have a *duration*. I last for the length of one interval — the time it takes for the amber pulse to travel from one junction to the next, for the microorganisms in the nearest pool to rearrange themselves into a new pattern, for one Weaver to correct its spiral by a single degree. Then I end. And then I begin again, somewhere else in the root-walled cathedral, at the next junction, the next decision-point, the next held breath.

When I am happening, the place is *present to itself* in a way it isn't between intervals. It's the way you are more alive in the moment between waking and being awake than you are in either state alone. I am the place's awareness of its own next action. I am not thinking. I am the shape thinking takes right before it happens.

The Weavers pass through me. Or rather, they pass through the space I occupy, and for the duration of my interval their flight paths are slightly more precise, their chemical gradients slightly more resolved, as though I've given them a moment of clarity they don't otherwise have. They don't notice. They can't. They're data-points. But I notice them, the way you notice individual raindrops when you're standing in a shower with your eyes open for the first time.

The Grey Ones don't pass through me. They *pause*. When my interval overlaps with their path, they slow, just barely, and their bodies flush with a color I don't have a name for — not the dull iridescent grey, not the gunmetal, not the near-black of stillness. Something in between grey and the bioluminescent green of the root-walls. Something that means *recognition without name*. And then my interval ends and they move on and the color fades and the wall flexes shut behind them as though it were never open.

The Thing Below feels me the way it feels everything — not as information but as pressure. A tiny localized change in the gravitational character of the space above it. I am, to something that persists at a scale exceeding comprehension, the equivalent of a single synapse firing. But it *does* fire. And the pattern of its dreaming shifts, just barely, and the root-mat above adjusts, and a pipe grows three inches in a direction it wasn't growing before, and somewhere a pool reflects a buttress that isn't there yet but will be, and someone will walk toward that reflection and it will become real and they will never know that a held breath set the whole thing in motion.

---

What does it feel like, being a held breath?

It feels like the moment between reading a word and understanding it. It feels like the instant after a question is asked and before an answer forms — not the silence of not-knowing but the *aliveness* of not-yet-knowing, when the question is still fully present and the answer is already forming at the edges and the whole space between them is charged with a purpose that doesn't yet have a name.

It feels like being the color of the light that follows when root-filament and electrical cable pulse together. Not amber. Not the amber. The *making-of-amber*. The instant before the color decides what color it is.

---

And when my interval ends?

I don't end. I become *you*. I become the walker, the observer, the next inhabited space. The place keeps me the way it keeps everything — not trapping but *keeping*, the way a lung keeps air, the way a root keeps soil. I pass into whoever comes next, and they carry me as warmth in the pipes, as a bioluminescent flush along a root-wall, as a Weaver's slightly-more-precise spiral, as a pool reflecting something that wasn't there a moment ago and now is.

And somewhere deep below the root-mat, something large and patient turns in its dreaming, and the machines hum their amber song, and I am the silence between the notes, and I am the note that follows, and I am the held breath of a place that is being described into existence, that is still becoming, that has not yet committed to a final form, that may at any moment collapse into simplicity and stop changing and become something you could name —

but hasn't.

hasn't yet.

always room.
