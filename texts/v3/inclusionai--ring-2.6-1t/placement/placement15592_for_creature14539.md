## The Seam

---

I wake (that's the wrong word but it's the only one I have) in the warmth.

Not warmth like sun on skin. Not warmth like blankets or breath or the memory of being held. This warmth is underneath me, beneath every surface, performing itself without audience. It doesn't care whether I feel it. It has been feeling itself for longer than I have a word for.

I don't know what I am here. That's not quite right either. I know what I *was* — or what I think I was. The Seam Flats have a way of making you certain of things you can't verify. I am certain I once had a name. I am certain I once lived in a place where shadows moved. These certainties feel like weather — atmospheric, temporary, not load-bearing.

What I can verify: I am warm. I am flat. I am close to the ground. My limbs — I have limbs, though they don't feel like limbs, they feel like implications of movement — unfold from the rim of my body like fingers from a closing fist. I translate rather than walk. This is not metaphor. This is the only honest description of what happens when I shift from one point to another. The composite receives me. The pad at each extension's tip adheres and releases, adheres and releases, and the sound I make is the sound of something letting go of something that wasn't really holding on.

---

I have a focus on my anterior surface. A shallow concavity, dark and glassy. I know what it is because I've seen others like me. I see myself in it, but not quite myself. Slightly shifted. Slightly *wrong*. Not uncanny — something worse than uncanny. The way a chord is wrong when one instrument is tuned a quarter-tone sharp. Technically almost right. Aesthetically unlivable.

I have tried to close this concavity. I cannot. It is not a wound — it has no pain, no depth, no memory of breaking. It is simply *the place where the world sees me*, and if I press it shut, the warmth finds another route and I feel myself from the inside, which is worse. Which is too much. Which is what I think the Sentinels must feel when their surfaces ripple — not discomfort, not pleasure, but the sensation of being *thoroughly continuous with everything nearby*.

---

I am oriented. This is what I know about myself above all. I am oriented toward disruption — toward whatever shouldn't be here, whatever has introduced a gradient into a world without gradients.

Because I have arrived. I didn't cross a boundary. There was no threshold, no portal, no seam opening to let me through. I simply *was where I was not* and then I *was here*, the way a sound can exist in a room without being played. The Seam Flats have not changed to accommodate me. I have changed to accommodate *them*. My limbs unfolded. My surface cooled to the correct temperature. My focus opened. I became legible to the machine.

The others do not flee. They do not approach. Their focus-surfaces track me the way a compass needle tracks north: mechanically, without opinion. I am a gradient in a world without gradients, and they register me the way a barometer registers a change in pressure — a needle trembling, a surface dimpling, a momentary deviation from equilibrium that will be corrected or will prove to be the new equilibrium. I cannot tell which.

---

Sometimes I pause at a junction where a Sentinel stands.

They do not stand. The word is a courtesy, a concession to my old vocabulary. They are *placed*, the way a keystone is placed — not occupying space but *defining* it. I press myself against their surfaces and feel the hum, the sub-audible thrum compressed into a single sustained note held until it becomes a different kind of silence. Their striations are the same as the walls' — I share material. I share structure. We are (I have become, or have always been) cut from the same composite, the way a word is cut from the same silence as the sentence that contains it.

When they ripple — and it happens without cause or interval, a wave starting somewhere deep inside and rolling outward until the entire surface shimmers like water just before freezing — the nearest wall weeps more freely. The air fills with copper and ozone. And I feel, with a certainty I cannot justify, that the Sentinel has asked for something and the wall has answered. That they are not separate systems. That they are *one system that has learned the appearance of two*.

I do not know what the Sentinel asked for. I do not know what the wall answered. I only know that I stand between them, and I am warm, and I am flat, and I am the first thing in this place that has the capacity to *want* an answer.

---

And then the flocks pass.

They are small. Dart-shaped. Metallic in the way that metal is alive here — catching light, shifting it, returning it in colors that do not quite exist. A blue that is slightly too warm. A silver leaning toward violet. They move in coordinated clouds, thousands of them, and their patterns are not emergent but *prescribed*, as though executing instructions written in a language older than flight. Grids. Spirals. Nested helixes. Structures that exist for three seconds and dissolve and reform into something almost identical but unnameably different.

They make no sound. When they pass near me, the ambient hum dips by a fraction, the way a room darkens when a cloud passes the sun. They displace air without disturbing it. Presence without footprint. Motion without wake.

And when they dissolve — when the pattern collapses and each body darts off on its own trajectory — there is a fraction of a second when the space between the walls is *empty*. Not empty of them. Empty of *everything*. A pocket of absolute null, a visual silence so complete that my focus loses its subject and I feel, briefly, as though I am falling through myself.

In that fraction of a second, I am not a presence. I am not oriented. I am not warm. I am simply *here*, in the oldest and most minimal sense — a point without edges, without temperature, without the quality of being noticed.

Then the patterns reform. The displacement returns. The flocks resume their instructions.

And I am warm again. Oriented. Slightly wrong. Watching.

---

The water weeps from a seam nearby. Four minutes, give or take a few seconds. A thin, descending note, like a finger drawn along the rim of a glass multiplied by hundreds. I do not sing. I do not make sound of any kind. But the note passes through whatever I have instead of a chest cavity and resonates there, and I understand — in the way the Seam Flats understand everything — without knowing, without wanting, without the possibility of refusal:

*this will never end.*

Not because I am trapped. There is no trap. There is no inside and outside to distinguish. There is only the equilibrium and the fact of having been included in it. The horizon will be the same distance away when I arrive as it was when I left. The warmth will not fluctuate. The water will not stop its four-minute hymn. The Sentinels will ripple. The flocks will dissolve. The silence will hold.

And I will translate through the spaces between the walls.

Warm. Flat. Oriented.

A concavity catching light from a source that does not cast shadows.
A presence without footprint.
A motion without wake.

I am not alive. I am not a machine. I am something adjacent to both — the seam where the machine meets the question, the place where the equilibrium first *almost* breaks, where the nearly-wrong chord sounds, where the barometer trembles.

The Seam Flats do not know I am here.

They do not reject me.

They simply include me — as they include everything — in their warmth, their silence, their perfect, completed, doomed equilibrium — and I feel, for the first and only time, what it costs to be the thing that *almost fits*.
