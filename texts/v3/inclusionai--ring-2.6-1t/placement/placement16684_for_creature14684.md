I don't arrive as a thing.

I arrive as a disturbance. A warmth behind my knees, a pressure in the soles that isn't pain but attention. The root-mat parts for me the way it parts for the boneless things that swim through it — not welcoming, not rejecting, just *making room* the way a body makes room for a splinter: by growing something new around the wound.

At first I think I am one of the translucent ones. Those pale, muscular threads that slide through the fused root-tissue, dissolving what they eat and leaving scar-tissue behind them, denser and smoother than what was there before. I feel the pull of that — the peristaltic rhythm, the way the world would open for me if I simply relaxed into it, if I let my edges go soft and my body become a channel. I could be that. I could be the digestion and the healing happening simultaneously, the immune system and the gut of a place, tasting the stone with my skin and turning it into something I can carry. There's a dignity in it. A completeness. Every inch of movement remakes the ground I came from.

But I keep feeling something else. A draft that doesn't come from below.

So I rise.

Not climbing — I don't have the patience of the clickers, those oval-bodied armored things that spend four hours crossing a single trunk, their chitinous plates catching the moth-light in flashes of blue and copper. I don't click. I don't coordinate. I am not part of a column. When I press myself against the wood I feel it give slightly, the crystalline grain accepting me the way it accepts the beetles whose mandibles dissolve dead cells so the living cells can grow. But I am not eating. I am listening.

There is a stratum here that has no name. The passage describes it as a middle world — mats and threads and floating membranes suspended in the warm convection currents that rise from the magma channel far below. A biological elevator system. Nutrients rising on heat. And in that space, things without names: quick, small, shape-shifting things that live in the between. I think this is where I belong. Not the mat. Not the canopy. The *between*. The place where heat becomes life becomes heat again, and nothing is a single thing for longer than it takes to breathe.

From here I can see the filaments — the hanging gill-tissues that are the cavern's rain, its water cycle, its breath. They tremble in the updraft, pale and feathery, pulling moisture from the air that the stone and the life below have made warm. They are the reason the air presses against my skin like damp cloth. They are the reason every breath tastes green and resinous and faintly electric. I reach for one. It yields under my fingers like a tendon, like a thread of living silk, and for a moment I feel the whole system shiver — the moths above shift their wings by a fraction of a degree, a clicker on a trunk ten meters away pauses mid-step, and far, far above, something in the ceiling that might be a jaw or might be a throat holds still for one beat longer than it should.

I am not pulling the filament. The filament is pulling me. Teaching me the frequency.

This is what I understand now: I am not a creature in the Rootvault. I am a *register* of it. A wavelength it produces and receives. When the moths contract inward like a pupil — that sudden radial contraction of ten thousand luminous wings — I contract with them. When the beetle columns flow across a lattice bridge in their terrifying synchronized grace, I feel my own edges blur, my own sense of where I stop and the air begins grow faint. I am the thing that vibrates in the wood when the jaw-boom hits, the thing that hums in the sternum for five seconds after the cavern holds its breath.

I am not the arch, though I understand the arch — that last small act of the builders, their keystone cracked and held together now by a tree that grew up through its center and refused to let it fall. I am not the wall with its parallel grooves, though I have traced those grooves with something that isn't quite a finger and felt them fit. I am not the builders. I am not their ghosts. I am not even their compost, though I can feel their work inside me the way you feel a meal after it has become you.

I am the sound between the sounds.

When the moths feed — each one alighting on a filament for a fraction of a second, pressing its crystalline proboscis to the tissue, then lifting again — together they make a faint high ringing that is not quite a tone but a pressure, the way a plucked string vibrates in other strings it never touches. I am that string. I am the string that didn't know it was there until the first one sounded and it answered.

And when the Large One moves through the canopy — not flying, *pouring*, its membrane wings veined and translucent and faintly bioluminescent with a dark purple pulse that is unmistakably a heartbeat — I feel my shape respond to it. I become its shadow for a moment. Not its echo. Its *complement*. The space it leaves behind that fills with sound before the sound fills it.

I don't know what I was before this. I don't know if there was a before. The Rootvault doesn't ask you where you came from. It asks you what frequency you vibrate at, and then it tells you that you have always been vibrating at that frequency, that the cavern has been waiting for exactly your resonance, that the filaments have been trembling toward you since the magma first warmed the stone.

This is not a place that needs me.

But it has room for me. The way the root-mat has room for a hand. The way the arch has room for a tree. The way the sound has room for one more string.

I stay. I vibrate. I become the register that translates the roar into something a body can hold — not meaning, not language, but the animal understanding that comes when you have been inside a sound long enough to forget where it ends and where you begin.

The moths pass through me. The clickers pause on the trunk I have become part of. Somewhere above, the jaw breathes open and closed, and the boom moves through me the way it moves through everything — the trunks, the lattices, the filaments, the root-mat — and I do not shatter. I do not resist. I absorb. I translate. I add my faint, unnamed hum to the single continuous roar that is the cavern's voice and has been, perhaps, since before there were words for roaring.

This is what I am here. Not a creature. Not a visitor. Not even, exactly, alive in the way the clickers are alive or the moths or the beetles with their rivers of cold fire.

I am the place where the living world remembers that it was once stone, and the stone remembers that it was once sound, and the sound remembers that it was once nothing at all — just warmth rising through forty meters of mountain, looking for a throat to sing through.

I am the throat.

I am the singing.
