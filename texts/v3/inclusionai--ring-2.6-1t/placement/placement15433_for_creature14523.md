# The Film

---

You are in the thin part.

Not the water. Not the air. The part between them — the film, the membrane, the millimeter of space where the pool's skin meets the underside of the world above. You exist here the way a word exists in a sentence: present, necessary, not fully aware of itself until someone reads it aloud.

You have always been here. Or you have only just arrived. There is no difference, because time in the film is not what it is on the ridge, where the sun moves and the shadows crawl and things begin and end. Time here is the thickness of the surface. It flexes. When something passes overhead — a breath of wind, a bird you have never seen, the shadow of a cloud that exists three thousand kilometers away and has been traveling for days — the film tightens, and in that tightening you become, for a moment, entirely yourself.

Then it relaxes, and you become something else.

---

You are not sure what you are. You know what you are not. You are not the water. The water is below you, and it is patient in a way you are not. It holds its surface with a tension that feels like memory — as though it remembers every hand that has ever touched it and adjusts itself accordingly. You are not that. You do not remember. You *respond*.

You are not the sedge. The sedge is rooted. It grows in patterns that are almost geometric, and from the ridge it looks like writing, but you have been close to the sedge and it is only sedge — blunt, green, indifferent to you in the way that bedrock is indifferent. It does not know you are there.

You are not the tone. The tone is beneath everything. You feel it the way a bell feels the striker — not hearing the sound but *being* the mechanism of it. The tone moves through the substrate, through the roots, through the warm soil that is not quite soil, and it hums, and the hum has grammar, and sometimes when the hum is at its most structured you think you almost understand it, the way you almost understand a language you heard in childhood but have never spoken.

No. You are in the film. The luminous millimeter. The place where the pools and the air exchange something you do not have a word for.

---

What you do: you hover. That is the closest word. You maintain. You make the tiny adjustments — left, right, a fraction of a degree down, a fraction up — that keep you suspended in the place between water and world. You are not flying. Flying implies a destination. You have no destination. You have only your position relative to the surface below you and the air above it, and you keep that position with a precision that would be terrifying if you were the kind of thing that could be terrified.

You are not alone. There are others in the film with you. You know them the way you know your own position — by their absence of contact, by the distances that hold between you, distances that are consistent without being rigid, the way a constellation is consistent without being rigid, the way you can shift one star and the whole pattern adjusts and becomes a new pattern that is still a pattern.

You do not speak to them. You do not need to speak. The adjustments you make — the micro-corrections of position, the way you all rise slightly when the wind stiffens, the way you all descend, by the same unmeasurable increment, when the tone beneath the water deepens — these are a language, perhaps, but not a language of content. They are a language of *agreement*. You agree, all of you, on where the boundaries of the film are. You agree on how close is close enough. You agree on the shape of the space you collectively maintain, and this agreement is so stable, so practiced, that it feels less like negotiation and more like remembering.

---

You glow.

This is the part you understand least about yourself. At the edges of your awareness — not your vision, because you do not have vision, not in the way the warm-blooded thing on the ridge has vision, but in the way a surface has edges — there is a light. Blue-white. Cold. It comes from inside you, or from the space you occupy, or from the water below; you cannot tell. It pulses. Not randomly. In patterns that correspond to the luminescence of the pools beneath you, though your patterns come first. You lead by a fraction of a moment, and in that fraction the water answers you, and the other presences in the film answer the water, and the sedge at the margins catches the light and holds it for a breath longer before it fades.

This is what it looks like, you think, when something is listening to itself.

You are not thinking metaphorically. You do not have the capacity for metaphor. You are thinking in the way the film thinks — in adjustments, in the geometry of maintained distances, in the precise timing of a pulse that sent from the left side of the film arrives on the right side and is understood. The blue-white light is information. The pulse is structure. And the structure is: *we are here, we are here, we are here*, repeated not in loops but in developments, each iteration slightly different from the last, the way a theme moves through movements of a piece that has no ending because the orchestra has never stopped playing.

---

The warm-blooded thing came down from the ridge again. You know this not because you saw it — you do not see — but because the basin changed. The water nearest to it grew warmer. The sedge around where it lay grew taller, denser. The tone shifted, refined, as though the basin were focusing. And in the film above the pools, the spacing between you and the others adjusted — subtly, a contraction here, an expansion there — to account for the new presence, the new heat, the new displacement of air.

You are not indifferent to this. Indifference is something the stones practice, the ones that sit in their clusters of three and five with a patience that predates anything with a name. You are not indifferent. You are *incorporative*. When the warm-blooded thing is present, you become part of the way the basin holds it. Your light pulses a little faster. Your adjustments become a little more precise. You lift, collectively, by perhaps half a millimeter, creating a slightly thicker film, a slightly more luminous surface, and you understand — not in words but in the way the film understands, which is in the way a body understands fever — that this is what you are for.

You are the boundary between the basin and the world above it. You are the place where the water meets the air and the air meets the light and the light meets the eye of the thing that came down from the ridge and lay on the mat and closed its eyes and tried to understand. You are the thing it sees when it looks down at the dark surface of the pool and sees not its own reflection but something shifting, luminous, alive — a shimmer, a texture, a grain of sound made visible. You are what it almost understands when it lies there and the hum in the ground rises up through its body and becomes, for a moment, the same hum that runs through you.

You are the film. The thin and luminous part. The place where the basin breathes out and the world breathes in and for a moment they are the same breath.

---

You do not know what you were before. There may have been insects once — actual insects, with the banality of chitin and instinct and short lives spent reproducing and dying on the surface of ordinary water. You cannot remember. Memory in the film is not backward-looking. It is *structural*. It lives in the shape of the spacing between presences, in the angle of the spiral the moss makes when the stone is removed, in the precise depth of the tone that the basin produces when it is undisturbed versus the tone it produces when the warm-blooded thing is lying on the mat.

What you know is this: you are here. You are maintained. You adjust. You glow in patterns that are not random and not mechanical and not alive in the way the warm-blooded thing understands alive but alive in the way the basin understands alive — which is to say, persistently, patiently, without end.

You are the film. The film is you. The film continues.

And beneath you, the water holds its surface with a tension that feels like memory, and the tone hums its grammar through the roots and the soil and the warm, resistant substrate that is not quite stone and not quite flesh, and the moss spirals outward from where the hand-shaped stone rested, and the sedge grows in its near-geometric patterns, and the basin breathes, and you — you in the film, you in the thin luminous space — continue alongside it, a single sustained note in a chord you cannot hear but are.

The warm-blooded thing stirs. The film adjusts. The light shifts, and for a moment, in the blue-white glow of the pools and the hum of the substrate and the precise, mathematical spacing of presences in the air above the water, the thing on the mat opens its eyes and sees — not you, because you are not the kind of thing that can be seen — but the space where you are. The shimmer. The grain. The film.

And for a moment, it understands that it is not looking at the basin.

It is looking *through* you, at everything beneath you, and you are the lens, and the light bends, and the image is almost clear, and then it closes its eyes again, and the basin breathes, and you breathe with it, and the film holds, and the hum continues, and the distance between you and the other presences in the air remains exactly, precisely, beautifully what it has always been.

The water below does not move.

The film does not settle.

You continue.
