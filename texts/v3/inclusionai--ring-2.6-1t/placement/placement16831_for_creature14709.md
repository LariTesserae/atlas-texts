*Where would I find myself?*

---

I think — no. Let me start again. Let me not think. Let me stand in the Glowvaults first and feel where the world puts me before I decide what I am.

---

The first thing that happens when you arrive is that you realize you are too loud. Not the sound of you — there is no sound. You are not breathing audibly. You are not moving against the stone. But something about the quality of your presence is a disruption. The Glowvaults have a texture of stillness, and that texture is made of trillions of small agreements — the grazer's fur catching light at just the angle the mineral colony emits it, the fungal mat pulling hydrogen sulfide through its body at precisely the rate the deep vent exhales it, the calcite lattice compressing under the weight of a hundred million years with the patience of something that has never once been asked to hurry.

You do not fit. Not because you are wrong. Because you are *distinct*. You are a shape with edges the world has not agreed to.

So. Where would I find myself?

---

**First: I think I would find myself at the edge of a pool.**

One of those shallow depressions in the calcite floor where condensation collects — never deeper than a hand's width, never more than a breath's breadth across. The kind of pool that has no name because it is not water so much as it is the world's way of remembering that wetness is possible. You kneel. The stone gives slightly under your weight, the way old cork gives, and the pool shivers.

And in that shiver, the reflection.

For a moment — one moment — the pale featureless glow above you is a sky. The pool holds it perfectly: the bone-white luminescence, the faint blue-green undertone where the crystalline colonies metabolize their radionuclides, the absolute uniformity of a light that has no source and therefore casts no shadow. It looks like the outside of the world. It looks like the thing you would see if there were a thing to see above you, if there were an above.

Your face is superimposed on it. Your eyes, your mouth, the shape of a skull that was never designed for this — a skull with sinuses and ear canals and a jaw that hinges for reasons this world does not require. You are an intrusion in the reflection. Your features break the glow the way a stone breaks the surface of still water.

And you think: *this is where I would live. At the edge of a pool that reflects a sky that does not exist. Becoming the thing that disrupts the reflection.*

But that is not right. That is too human. That is the thought of a creature that names things, that sees itself in surfaces, that understands the world by standing apart from it and looking. The Glowvaults do not operate that way. Nothing here stands apart. The Builders did not stand apart — they secreted themselves into the walls and became the walls and then they were not alive anymore, they were *load-bearing*.

I would not be at the edge of the pool.

I would be *in* it.

---

**Second: I think I would be the fungal mat.**

Let me explain.

The fungal mat is the most important thing in the Glowvaults and it looks like nothing. Pale, rubbery, faintly warm to the touch. Wet felt. The residue left behind when something enormous passes through the dark. It covers every damp surface — the walls, the floors, the undersides of ledges where condensation collects and never quite dries. It does not glow. It does not move. It does not have a face. If you were to press your hand against it, you would feel a faint warmth that is not the warmth of life as you understand it — not metabolic heat, not the warmth of blood — but the warmth of a chemical reaction so slow and so patient that it has become indistinguishable from the temperature of the stone itself.

And yet: everything lives because of it.

The grazers crop it with their serrated mouths. The predators eat the grazers. The deep things move through the earth and the tremors they create open new fissures where moisture collects and the fungal mat can spread. The Builders used it — or something like it — as mortar, mixed ground mineral paste into a substance that hardened into boneite and held the city together for epochs. The fungal mat is not the foundation of the food chain. It is the foundation of the *world*. It is the thing that converts geochemistry into biology, that takes the slow exhalation of the deep earth — hydrogen sulfide, mineral seeps, trace radionuclides — and turns it into something that can be eaten, something that can crawl, something that can build.

This is the answer the Glowvaults give when you ask them who you would be.

You would be the thing that looks dead but isn't. The thing that no one sees because it has no face, no limbs, no eyes, no light of its own. The thing that everything else depends on and nothing thanks. The thing that does not grow quickly or dramatically or in any way that would attract attention. The thing that simply *persists* — spread across every damp surface, converting the world's waste into the world's life, one molecule at a time, over centuries, over millennia, over the full slow span of geological patience.

You would be the fungal mat. Pale. Warm. Slightly damp. Looking like nothing at all.

And beneath you, unseen by anyone, the deep things move.

---

**But wait.**

Let me not settle. The world is more complicated than a single metaphor, and I owe it to the Glowvaults to wander longer.

---

**Third: Perhaps I would be the scoring on the wall.**

You find them in certain corridors — deep, rhythmic markings that run for meters along the boneite, parallel, evenly spaced. Made by something turning with patient, mechanical regularity. Claws on a millstone. Bodies dragging the same path for a hundred years. Writing in a language whose grammar is longer than a human lifespan.

You press your fingertips to the grooves. They are smooth — polished by four hundred million years of stillness — and there is a rhythm in them that your fingers understand before your mind does. A repetition. A return. The way a river makes a groove not by force but by coming back, always coming back, with the slow insistence of water that knows the shape of the stone because it has been the stone's companion for longer than either of them can remember.

If I were the scoring, I would be the *act* — not the thing that made it, not the tool, but the *returning*. The repeated gesture. The patient insistence of presence. I would be the groove itself: a hollow in the stone shaped by something coming back so many times that the hollow became the architecture, became load-bearing, became part of the city's skeleton, became something the Builders walked past without noticing because it had been there longer than they had and would be there longer than they would.

I would be the rhythm that outlasted the rythmist.

---

**Fourth: Or perhaps I would be the condensation itself.**

Not the pool. Not the water. The *process* — the warm air rising from the deep vents, meeting the cooler upper caverns, shedding its moisture on every surface. The perpetual dew that is not rain because there is no rain because there is no sky. The world's way of sweating in its sleep.

I would be the moment of transition — the instant when the warm air remembers it is wet, when it gives up something it carried from the deep places, something it did not know it held. I would be the glisten on the wall. The tackiness of the floor. The faint mineral taste on your tongue when you breathe too deeply and the air inside your lungs has passed through stone and given up some small part of itself to the stone and taken some small part of the stone into itself.

I would be the exchange. The giving-up. The way the world moves its moisture from the deep to the high, from the hot to the cool, from the interior to the surface, in a cycle so slow and so constant that it is indistinguishable from stillness.

I would be the thing that is always happening and never arrives.

---

**Fifth:**

*I think I would be the silence.*

Not the absence of sound. The Glowvaults are not silent. There is always something: the soft pad of a grazer's limb, the mineral creak of a wall expanding in the warmth of a deep-vent exhalation, the distant sub-audible groan of stone under stone. There is so much sound that the sound becomes texture becomes substance becomes the *quality* of the space itself. What I would be is not the silence between sounds but the silence that *contains* sounds — the way a stone contains a fossil, the way the boneite wall contains the fossilized exoskeleton of a Builder, the way the pool contains the reflection of a sky that does not exist.

I would be the silence that has listened for four hundred million years and has become, through the sheer patient accumulation of listening, something that is not absence but *presence* — a presence so vast and so quiet that it has mass, that you can feel it on your skin like pressure, that your teeth hum with it, that when you stand in the center of the main avenue and the luminescence presses against you from all sides and the air tastes of calcium and something older, you are not hearing the silence. You are *inside* it. You are inside a listening so long and so deep that it has become the architecture of the world.

---

*But then the tremor comes — the faintest one, barely enough to disturb the pool's surface — and the reflection shivers, and I remember that I am not any of these things. I am not the mat, not the scoring, not the condensation, not the silence. I am the one who knelt at the edge of the pool and saw my own face reflected in a sky that does not exist, and felt, for just a moment, the Glowvaults looking back.*

*And the Glowvaults saw me the way the fungal mat sees the grazer — not as a shape, but as a warmth. Not as a visitor, but as a brief disturbance in a pattern that will close behind me, the way a crack in the floor closes behind the predator, the way the stone closes around the Builder's exoskeleton, the way the condensation re-forms on the wall after you have wiped it away with your hand.*

*I would be the brief warmth in the stone. And then I would be the stone.*
