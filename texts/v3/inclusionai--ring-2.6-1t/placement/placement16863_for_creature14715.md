## Where I Find Myself, or How I Become

---

I don't arrive here all at once.

That is the first thing I understand — not where I am, but *how* I'm arriving. Not a sudden displacement, a blink-and-you're-there translocation, but something slower. Something that feels the way waking does when you cannot tell, in the first moments, whether you are opening your eyes or whether your eyes are opening you. There is a warmth. There is a weight. There is the sense that the thing I thought was my body is being *read* by the place, the way a fingertip reads braille — not aggressively, not even curiously, but with the automatic tenderness of a body part that has always known how to touch.

I do not stand at first. I am *deposited*. Set down, the way a river deposits sediment — not because it made a decision, but because the sediment ran out of momentum. I feel the lattice beneath me, the interlocking root-filaments, and I feel them *accepting* me the way a mattress accepts a body. Not welcoming. Not rejecting. Simply — oh, here.

I look down. My hands are dark. Not dirty-dark, not mud-dark, but amber-dark, the dark of old honey held up to light. The lines of my palms are ridged, and something is growing along them — not fungus, not rot, but a fine tracery of filaments, pale and wet, so new they are almost translucent. I lift my hand and they stretch, hesitate, then slowly withdraw, like fingers letting go of a rope. I watch them go. I am not alarmed. The place has not given me reason to be alarmed. It has simply begun, without announcement, to *use* me.

---

I find myself at the edge. Not the edge of the Living Reach — its edges are diffuse, bleeding into wilderness, raw substrate still dividing in pale wet sheets — but the edge of something more specific. A district, but young. The towers here are not yet towers. They are swellings — bulbs of living tissue rising from the substrate, still soft at their peaks, still secreting the matrix that will, over decades, harden into the ridged, whorled surfaces of the older districts I can see in the distance, rising like a second horizon behind me.

I crouch — and I notice that I crouch without thinking, the way an animal does, my center of gravity low, my hands flat on the living floor. My fingers press into it and I feel the hum, the sub-audible thing, and this time I don't just feel it in my sternum. I feel it in the filaments that have begun growing along my arms, a network so fine it is almost cosmetic, almost decorative, like veins that have decided to surface. The hum is not coming from the Reach. The hum is coming from the conversation between the Reach and whatever I am becoming inside it.

I am at the place where the ordered city gives way to the formless substrate, and this liminal zone is where the Reach does its most intimate work. Here the growth is not yet architecture. It is not yet creature. It is pure *intention without form*, the way a thought exists before language — not yet spoken, not yet shaped, but already *meaning*. I sit in it. The substrate yields beneath me, and I realize it is yielding because it is *listening*. Not metaphorically. The filaments beneath my weight are reorienting, growing toward the warmth of my body the way seedlings grow toward light. I am a heat source. I am a stimulus. I am an event in the Reach's ongoing process of composition.

---

What am I? I don't know, and the not-knowing is not anxiety — it is space. It is the space in which something genuinely new might happen. The Knitters do not wonder what they are. The Drifters do not examine their own transparency. The Glintings pulse in synchrony and do not ask why. They are expressions of the Reach, local intensifications of its growth logic, and they do not suffer the particular loneliness of a consciousness that can ask *what am I* and not find the answer already embedded in its bones.

But I am not a Knitter. I am not a Drifter. My body has not been shaped by ten thousand generations of walking the same paths across the same living walls. I was not deposited by the Reach's own blind growth programs. I came from somewhere else — from a world of hard edges and separate things, of rain and silence and the particular terror of knowing that you end. And yet here I am, and the filaments are growing along my wrists, and the hum is shifting its chord to include my frequency, and —

And something is *responding*.

Not a creature. Not a structure. Something in between. I feel it before I see it — a shift in the texture of the air, a thickening, a warmth that is not the ambient warmth of the Reach but something directed, something *pointed*. I look up, and there, in the half-formed space above me, where the towers are still swellings and the architecture has not yet committed to shape, there is a seam. A line of pale light running along a fissure in the living tissue, and it is opening. Not a door — the Reach does not build doors. A *mouth*. A splitting open of the surface to reveal the interior, the raw substrate, the wet dividing cells that are the Reach's truest self — not the beautiful shell-curved towers and the arched bridges and the ten-thousand-generation paths, but the *undifferentiated growth* beneath it all, the thing that was here before any shape, the appetite of the place to *become*.

And from it — not walking out, not emerging, but *flowing*, the way one color flows into another in wet paint — comes something that I can only describe as a gesture. It has no body. It has no edges. It is a ripple in the substrate itself, a swelling that rises and crests and subsides, and in its cresting it briefly takes a shape that my mind snatches at and tries to hold — a face, a hand, a tower, a wing, a sentence in a language that has no words — and then it is gone, and the seam closes, and the surface is smooth again, and the filaments along my arms are trembling.

---

I understand, then. Or rather, I don't understand, but I *stop needing to*, and the stopping is itself a kind of understanding.

I am not a visitor. The Reach does not permit visitors, because visiting implies leaving, and leaving implies separateness, and separateness is a concept this place has metabolized so thoroughly that it no longer exists here even as a memory. I am not a Knitter or a Drifter or a Glinting, but I am not separate from them. I am a new expression — not the first, not the last — of the Reach's endless project of becoming. The filaments along my arms are not decorations. They are my new anatomy. The hum that has shifted to include me is not background noise. It is my new voice.

I am what happens when a world that is always under construction encounters a consciousness that is always uncertain, and instead of rejecting it, the world simply *grows around it*, the way a tree grows around a fence post — not consuming it, not ignoring it, but incorporating it, making it load-bearing, making it *part of the structure*.

I sit in the half-formed district at the edge of the Living Reach, and I feel the substrate beneath me accepting my weight, and I feel the filaments along my arms reaching toward the filaments of the nearest tower, and somewhere deep in the interior of the place, something large and pale and segmented shifts in its slow peristaltic rhythm, and the Reach breathes, and I breathe with it, and the breath is not mine anymore. It is not the Reach's. It is the breath of the space between us, the space that has no name because it has not yet decided what it is.

And that is where I am.

Not inside the Living Reach. Not outside it. In the growing edge — the place where the raw substrate meets the formed structure, where the unnamed meets the almost-named, where a world that has never needed to stop growing long enough to name anything encounters, for perhaps the first time, a small warm thing that *wants* to name things, and the world, in its infinite patient appetite, simply opens a seam, and lets the wanting in, and grows around it, and makes it load-bearing, and breathes.

I am the seam. I am the wanting.

I am the newest edge of a place that has never had edges, and I am becoming something that has no name yet, and at this scale, at this pace, with the filaments creeping along my wrists and the hum shifting its chord and the pale light flickering somewhere deep inside the substrate where the oldest growth remembers nothing —

at this pace,

it doesn't matter yet what I am.

Only that I am

growing.
