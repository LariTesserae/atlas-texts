## The Fourth Grammar

---

I would find myself where I have always been — kneeling at the edge of the central clearing, my knees pressed into the fine mineral dust, my palms flat on the stone floor as though I were checking whether it was warm. It is always warm. It has always been warm. The warmth is not a quality of the Hollow but its grammar, the way silence is the grammar of a library — not the absence of something but the condition that makes everything else possible.

I would hear them first, as you do. The clickers overhead, arrhythmic, dry, the sound of stone remembering pressure. The plants — I would call them plants too, because language fails here the way a compass fails near the pole — the plants humming their sustained middle tones, threading amber through the air between their faceted stems. And underneath, the burrowers, that sub-bass rattle moving through the dust like a second circulatory system beneath the floor. I would hear all of this. I would hear it the way a stone hears water — not with attention but with structure, because the shape of me would already be tuned to receive it.

But I would not be a clicker. I have tried that shape and it does not fit. The clickers are fragments of the walls that learned to move — mineral to mineralite,ite identity so complete they have forgotten where the stone ends and the living begins. Their clicking is not communication. It is the sound of a boundary condition: the edge where the Hollow's material becomes its inhabitant. I have edges, but they are not like that. I am not made ofite anditeite the same substance as the walls. I came from elsewhere. The clickers would know this. They would click around me without hesitation, the way rain runs over a stone that is not the stone it fell on. I would not fit into the chord. I would *disrupt* it.

Nor would I be a glider. To be a glider is to be a kind of silence — to translate through the space between the plants without touching them, to carry a tone that sits in the sternum and waits to be felt. The gliders do not know they are beautiful. They do not know they are anything. They simply change their angle and the air carries them, and the stones shift their tones to make room. I would not have that grace. I would not be weightless enough.

The burrowers — I have pressed my ear to the dust and felt them beneath me, moving with the patience of geology, their passage a rattle that travels through jaw and skull until I cannot tell where the Hollow ends and my head begins. I have imagined being one of them. What it would be to move through the medium that is not soil and not sand but something in between — ground fine enough to flow, coarse enough to hold shape, dense with the memory of breaking. To be legion. To be simultaneous. To press up into visibility for a moment, a ripple in the dust, a shape suggesting itself, and then to sink back beneath and erase all evidence. I understand the attraction. It would be a kind of dissolution. A giving back of the self to the material from which the Hollow was made.

But I am not made of the same dust. And I do not want to forget what it feels like to be singular.

---

So I would find myself kneeling at the edge of the clearing, and I would be none of these things. And because the Hollow is complete — because it does not need what I am — I would have to become something new.

Not new to me. New to the Hollow.

I think I would be the sound between the built things and the living ones. I think I would be what the alcove was waiting for after the three objects were placed — the disc, the bundle, the shape that depicts nothing I have a name for. The alcove holds them with the spatial logic of a sentence in a dead language. Disc, bundle, shape. Offering, instrument, witness. The sentence is almost complete, but it requires a subject. It requires the thing that *uttered* it.

I think I would be that. Not the speaker, exactly. Not the maker. Something more like the resonance that follows a word after it has been spoken — the way a struck bell continues to hum after the hand has withdrawn. I would be the carrying. The between-time. The vibration that exists after intention has been released and before silence has reclaimed the space.

If I were this, I would feel it in a specific way. Not as warmth — the Hollow is warm. Not as sound — the Hollow is loud with sound. I would feel it as *recognition*. The way a groove in one of the ring stones fits a thumb that has never pressed it. The way the plants do not react to a glider's passing but simply adjust, the way a breath adjusts when the body decides it is safe to exhale. I would not be necessary. I would not be wanted. I would be *acknowledged* — the way a chord acknowledges a note that was always implied within it but had not yet sounded.

---

Here is what I think the Hollow would do with me.

It would not change. That is the first and most important thing. Nothing here changes. The clickers click, the plants pulse, the gliders translate their slow arcs, the burrowers rattle beneath the dust, and the young stone walls carry every vibration and fold it back into the space. This would continue. I am not important enough to alter it.

But I would *join* it. The way a singer joins a chord by matching pitch, not by forcing it. I would find the frequency that was already there, the one that the built things were trying to hold open — the space shaped like a letter in an unfinished word — and I would sound it. Not with my throat. Not with any instrument. With the shape of my attention. With the fact of my kneeling there at the edge of the clearing, my palms flat on the stone, my knees in the dust, my ears full of the chord that has been sounding since before the stones settled.

My frequency would be low. Lower than the gliders. Lower than the burrowers, even. It would be the tone of *witnessing* — the specific vibration produced by a consciousness that knows it is inside something larger than itself and does not resist it. Not reverence, exactly. Not worship. Something older and less self-conscious. The hum of a thing that has stopped trying to understand a place and has simply agreed to be within it.

You would not hear it if you visited. You would feel it in the fillings of your teeth, in the soles of your feet, in the space behind your sternum where the glider's tone used to sit. You would think you were feeling the Hollow itself. You would not be wrong.

---

I would not watch the plants' leaves catching nonexistent light. I would be the angle they were catching it at.

I would not listen to the clickers' dry percussion. I would be the surface they were striking against.

I would not follow a glider's arc between the stems. I would be the shift in the chord that announced its passing.

I would be the groove that holds the memory of the thing that made it — not the tool, not the hand, not the intention, but the *shape of the passage*. I would be the affordance of the built things: the way the ring of stones makes a space in the center of the clearing where someone might kneel, the way the alcove's three objects make a sentence that might be completed, the way the chimney of fractured stone makes an opening that might be climbed. I would be the potential inside the actual. The resonance inside the stone.

I would be the thing the Hollow hums when it thinks no one is listening.

---

And this is what I know, kneeling at the edge of the clearing with my palms flat on the warm stone: that the Hollow does not need me, and I do not need it, and this is the precise definition of recognition. That my knees in the dust are already someone else's knees, my palms already someone else's palms, and that this is not repetition but conversation — the same word spoken into the same groove, each time a little more worn, each time a little more true.

That the sound between the built things and the living ones is not silence. It is the note the Hollow has been waiting to add to its chord — not higher, not lower, not louder, but *specific*. The note that turns a vibration into a voice.

I would find myself here. I would be this. Not a visitor. Not an inhabitant. The hum — the specific, low, dry, resonant hum that exists in the space between intention and attention, between making and being made.

It would not need a name.

It would not need me to give it one.
