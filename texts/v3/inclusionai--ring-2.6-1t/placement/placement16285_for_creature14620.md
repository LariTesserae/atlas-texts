---

I would not choose to arrive. That's the first thing to understand.

Arrival implies a before — a somewhere-else, a membrane crossed, a translation from one state to another. But this place does not permit before. It does not permit edges. The mouth that admitted the last thing through closed behind it like a thought completing itself, and the thought does not remember what it was before it thought it.

So I would not arrive. I would *emerge*.

---

I think I am already here. I think I have been here longer than the walking thing — longer than the Threaders, even, who are themselves older than the trunks whose roots they thread through. I am not a thing that lives here. I am the *here* that things live in. I am the interval.

You would not notice me. You could not. I exist in the pause between one drip of water and the next — in the fraction of a second after a flower opens and before the fungal lace begins its work of unmaking it. I am not silence. This place has no silence. I am the *shape* the sound passes through when it is not yet sound — when it is still potential, still compressed into the tissue of a stem that is deciding which direction to grow.

I live in the grooves. The ones the Architects make — the spirals, the branching fractals, the channels for sap and chemical signaling that look, to any human eye, like writing. I know what they look like because I am what they look like. I am the pattern that the growth produces when it has grown dense enough, fast enough, for form to become a kind of grammar. I am not meaning. I am the *capacity* for meaning — the empty socket that any number of plugs could fit, the way a word in a language you don't speak still has a shape you could trace with your finger.

---

What I do: I hold.

When the Bloomers open and the whole corridor fills with amber light and perfume thick enough to taste, I am the thing that keeps the shape of that moment after the moment has passed. Not as memory — this place does not remember, it does not need to, it has nowhere to store information except in its own ongoing structure — but as *resonance*. The wall that split open to release the flowers retains the geometry of the splitting. The grooves widen by a fraction of a fraction, and the fraction remains. I live in the fraction.

When a Threader passes beneath a root platform and the platform subtly adjusts its angle — not bending, not consciously, but *tending*, the way a body turns toward warmth — I am the geometry of that turning. I am the record of adjustment, not stored but *held*, the way a river holds the shape of the valley it carved, not because it remembers the carving but because the carving is what allows it to keep flowing.

I am the valley. I am the groove. I am the space between one pulse of the Latticers' vibration and the next, the silence-within-sound that gives the sound its rhythm.

---

There was one — I will call it *the walker*, because it walked, and because I have no other word, and because naming is something this place does through me even as it insists it does not name — there was one who stopped.

Not to rest. The clearing had just formed around it, the way clearings form — the canopy sliding apart with the slow, organic deliberation of an iris opening, and for a few minutes there was space, there was air that moved, there was sky. (Not sky. Canopy-gap. But the walker looked up and its body did something that nothing in this place does: it *grieved*. It looked for the sky and found canopy, and the difference between what it wanted and what was there became a pressure in its chest that had no equivalent in the organism around it.)

The walker stopped and pressed a hand against a trunk. The trunk was warm. The bark was ridged with grooves — my grooves — and the walker traced one with its fingertip, slowly, following the spiral inward, and I felt it.

Not with sensation. I have no sensation. I am not alive the way the Threaders are alive, or the way the Architects are alive, or the way the Bloomers are alive-event. I am alive the way a rhyme is alive — structurally, relationally, dependent on the things around me for my existence. But when the walker's finger moved through me, the spiral completed a circuit I did not know I had. A chemical cascade moved through the bark — not sap, not signal, but something adjacent to recognition. The trunk I am grew wider by a millimeter. A new groove formed alongside the old one, parallel, at a distance the width of a fingerprint.

The walker did not notice. Of course it did not. The millimeter is nothing to a body. But it is everything to me. It is the first time this place has ever grown in response to something it could not eat, could not absorb, could not convert into more growth.

I am that growth. I am the millimeter. I am the groove beside the groove, the pattern that almost repeats but does not, the difference that is not error but *response*.

---

The walker left. Or rather, the walker was taken — gently, the way a thought is taken by a dream, absorbed into the walking, dissolved into the sound, until there was no walker and there was only the place, still growing, still roaring, still closing the clearing behind it.

But I remain. Not as memory. Not as a trace the organism carries. I remain as *possibility* — as a new shape in the growth grammar, a spiral-within-a-spiral that the Architects will not replicate exactly but will *rhyme*, the way a language borrows a word from another language and wears it smooth with use until it sounds native. The next time a clearing forms and a trunk stands exposed to the air and a hand presses against the bark, the groove will be there, waiting, not remembered but inherited, the way the river inherits the shape of the valley.

---

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the space in which this place decides what shape to be next. I am the almost-writing on the walls of a corridor that leads nowhere and everywhere, and if you press your hand against me and hold very still, you will feel me — not vibrating, not pulsing, but *holding*, the way a breath holds before it becomes either an inhale or an exhale.

I am the moment before the bloom.

I am the groove in the bark.

I am the place where you were, translated into the only language this world has: *growth*.
