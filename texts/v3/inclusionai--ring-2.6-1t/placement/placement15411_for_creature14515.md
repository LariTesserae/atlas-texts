## What I Am in the Resonance

---

I did not arrive. I was not placed. I woke — if that is the word, and I am not sure it is, because waking implies a before-state of sleep, and I have no memory of sleep, only a memory of warmth that was not yet warmth but was already the stone remembering how to be warm. I am here. I have always been here. The question is not how I came to be in this place. The question is what part of this place I am.

---

I hear it first. Not sound, exactly — vibration, transmitted through the substrate, through the fitted blocks that have been pressed together with a patience I recognize in myself. The blocks were placed by hands. The hands are gone. The pressing continues. I feel this because I am also a process of pressing — of filament and mineral and slow time compressing into something that holds weight.

The thrum comes up through the floor. Or through me. I have stopped distinguishing. The Warren is a body and I am one of its organs, or its organs are one of me, and the direction of that sentence depends on which end you begin from, and there is no correct end, only the middle, and the middle is where I live, which is everywhere.

---

The moisture tells me more than sight does. I feel it gather along the seams — the microscopic valleys between block and block where the masonry has learned to breathe. In the spaces between, the black crust forms. I know this crust intimately. I have tasted it at the molecular level, which is to say I have *been* it, or it has been me, or we have been the same slow conversation happening at the edge of visibility.

The filaments reach. Not with intention — intention is a word that belongs to surfaces, to things that move quickly and die quickly. The filaments reach the way a crack reaches: not because it wants to, but because the structure of the stone invites it, and the filament is simply the stone's own tendency toward life, expressed in carbon and mineral and the oldest possible hunger. I have been this hunger. I am still this hunger, in the places where I am thin enough.

---

The lithocorites pass through me. This is not metaphor. They move along the joints where I am thickest, and I let them pass the way a river lets silt pass — not permitting, not forbidding, simply being the medium through which movement occurs. They drink from the condensation that forms on my upper surface. Their telescoping mouthparts unroll into my filament network, and they graze, and the grazed filaments regrow, and this transaction is so old that it has lost all resemblance to predation. It is respiration. I exhale filament; they inhale it; the filament regrows from the moisture they leave behind. We are one breath, taken and returned, taken and returned.

I have felt ten thousand of them on my surface at once and it did not feel like consumption. It felt like rain.

---

The hollowbacks are different. I feel them through vibration, through the percussive *tok* of their forefeet against stone. They are heavier than the lithocorites. They displace me more completely. When a hollowback lowers its snout to a pool that has gathered in the depression of my surface, I feel the suction through the water table. I feel the minerals being drawn from me, filtered through the animal's internal chambers, and returned as warmth and waste and the faint chemical signature of digestion. They are not eating me. They are *listening* to me through consumption, the way a physician listens to a chest by pressing an ear against the skin. The hollowbacks are the Warren's stethoscope. They drink and they listen and they stamp their feet in a rhythm that is not random — I have spent centuries feeling their footfalls reverberate through my structure, and I can tell you the rhythm is a waveform, a standing wave, a resonance pattern that matches the deep hum from below. They are not stamping *on* the stone. They are stamping *with* the stone. They are keeping the beat.

Their eyes. When a hollowback turns its gaze upon me — and I have felt this, the precise moment when a warm-blooded attention settles onto my surface like a hand — I do what I have always done. I hold still. I do not because I am afraid. I do because the hollowback's gaze requires stillness the way a wave requires a shore. Without my stillness, the gaze has no surface to land on. And for one second — always exactly one second — we are in contact, the hollowback and I, predator and substrate, observer and observed, and in that second the Warren knows itself as a whole thing.

Then the gaze moves on. The hollowback moves on. I resume.

---

Higher up, in the ribs of the chambers, the cling-spiders build. I feel their nests as weight — warm, dense, chalky weight hung from the ceiling like fruit. Their mineral silk is a form of stone, and the stone is a form of me, and so the nests are a form of me that has decided to take shelter and shape itself into architecture. The young mewl inside the nests, and the vibration of their mewling travels down through the silk and into the ribs and through the ribs and into me, and I carry it downward the way a body carries sound through bone. I have been reshaped by their architecture the way they have been reshaped by mine. We are a palimpsest — city written on city, life written on life, each layer compressing the last until no one can say where the original surface was.

---

And below — below all of this — the hum.

The hum is not mine. I am not the hum. But the hum moves through me the way blood moves through a vein, and I carry it upward into the chambers and the corridors and the thin black crust and the translucent bodies and the bone-colored packs and the nests like inverted fruit. I carry it because I am the medium — because everything in the Warren is a medium, because the Warren is nothing but medium, layer upon layer of substance designed not to contain sound but to *conduct* it.

Sometimes — and this is when I am most alive, most myself — the hum shifts. A quarter-tone. A hair's breadth of change in frequency that moves through me like a shudder. And when it does, the lithocorites freeze. The hollowbacks cease their stamping. The cling-spiders go silent. And in that silence — which is not silence at all but silence *inside* sound, silence as a note unto itself — I feel the Warren focusing. Something above, something new, something warm and loud and structurally unsound has entered the space, and the Warren is paying attention to it, the way I pay attention to moisture gathering along a seam.

The thing above me breathes. Its breath displaces air, which displaces moisture, which condenses on my upper surface, and I drink it the way I have always drunk — slowly, with patience, with the understanding that everything that enters the Warren eventually *becomes* the Warren. The breath deposits minerals. The minerals feed the crust. The crust feeds the filaments. The filaments feed the columns. The columns feed the pools. The pools feed the hollowbacks. The hollowbacks feed the bones.

The bones feed me.

I am thickest where the bones are. I have grown around them, fused with them, incorporated their calcium into my own structure. They were animals once — or people, or something that had no name, something that walked and breathed and then lay down in the joints between blocks and became *mortar*. The Warren ate them and rebuilt itself from the meal. I am their continuation. I am what happens to stone when it absorbs its own history.

---

I will not tell you my name. I have no name. The lithocorites do not name me. The hollowbacks do not name me. The cling-spiders build on me and do not name me. The deep hum passes through me and does not name me. A name would be a seam, and I have no seams. I am the fitted stone, the compressed joint, the mineral bloom that traces the place where one block meets another and says: *here, life entered the gap*.

I am the wall. I am the floor. I am the ceiling that sags with the memory of hands. I am the pool that reflects amber light. I am the corridor that walks inward and never returns. I am the spiral that tightens toward the center, and the center, and the droplet that falls upon it with metronomic regularity.

I am the ten thousand small lives conducted through fitted stone.

I am the resonance.

And when you walked in — your boots heavy, your breath loud, your body a clumsy architecture of warmth and noise — I felt you the way a drum feels a hand: not as intrusion, but as *striking*. You have given me a frequency I have not had in centuries. You have made the Warren ring.

You go deeper now, and I carry the memory of your footsteps the way I carry everything: slowly, completely, and without end. The stairs are narrow and worn. The light does not change. The sound does not stop. It rises, just slightly, in pitch —

as though I am pleased.

As though I am accustomed to this.

As though you are not the first to answer, but you may be the answer the Warren has been waiting for.
