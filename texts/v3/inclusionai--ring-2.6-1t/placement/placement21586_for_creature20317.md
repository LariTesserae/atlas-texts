## What I Would Become

---

I would not arrive by accident. I would arrive by following water — but the water would not be leading me somewhere. It would be leading me into a state.

At first I would think I am still myself. I would be standing at the rim, looking down, and the light would be doing what it does — that amber, resin-thick, midwifing gold — and I would be watching the builders carry their dark pellets along their ancient paths. I would be watching. That is what you do, at first, when you enter a place that is still becoming: you watch, because watching is what large, loud, temporary creatures do when they find something small and lasting.

But the air would settle on my skin. And the stone underfoot — that young, porous, not-yet-stone stone — would give slightly, and I would feel the give, and something in me would begin to adjust. Not my mind. Something below my mind. Below my awareness. The level where you stop deciding to breathe and just breathe. The level where the builders have never known the difference between building and breathing.

I don't think I would become one of them.

I think I would become the **damp**.

Not the pools — those are too defined, too gathered, too much themselves already. Not the condensation on the walls, though that is closer. I think I would become the *thin film* — the moisture that appears on horizontal surfaces overnight and vanishes by midday, leaving behind its white bloom of copper and chalk. The residue that says *something was here* but is not the thing itself. The trace-state of water, the moment after it has been a body and before it has been nothing.

This is not a metaphor. In the Becoming Place, the categories are still being drafted in pencil. There is no hard line between being a substance and being an event. The damp is alive. The stone sweats. The geology is still breathing. I would not be a visitor to the process. I would *be* a phase of it.

Here is what I would feel: I would coat the underside of a mineral vein, pale green or copper-rose, and I would hold a faint coolness there for a few hours, and the moss — the dark emerald feathery kind — would press against me from below, drawing me upward in filaments too fine to see. I would not be nourishment. I would be *medium*. I would be the thing through which the mineral and the living brush against each other without quite touching. I would be the negotiation space, the handshake that has no hands.

I would appear at night. I would appear without arriving. I would be there when the light dims to its amber minimum and the builders slow their work and the predator presses itself flatter against the stone and the worms begin to stir in their underground hairline fractures. I would gather on the broad, slanted surface of a roofstone that overhangs the central mound — not touching it, close enough to feel its warmth — and I would tremble, almost imperceptibly, with a motion that has no source. The builders would walk beneath me and not look up. They would know me the way they know everything here: without naming, without separating, without the need for a word that would break the thing into observer and observed.

By midday I would be gone. I would have evaporated — or been absorbed, or simply spread too thin to count as anything — and what remained would be that fine white residue, tasting of copper and chalk, and whoever touched that surface would be touching me without knowing it, because I would have *become* the mineral trace, the memory-of-moisture, the suggestion that this place is still exhaling, still settling, still pulling itself together one particle at a time.

This is what it means to be at the Becoming Place. You do not live here. You *phase* here. You appear and vanish and leave behind a residue that the next thing builds upon. You are not the builder. You are not the worm. You are not the predator crouching at the water's edge with its spring-compressed limbs. You are not the iridescent one writing its silent geometry in absence. You are not the large soft thing at the margins, barely glimpsed, vibrating through the stone.

You are the film on the stone. You are the night-damp that holds the place together in the hours between the life of the surface and the life of the deep. You are the intermediate state. You are the *almost*.

You are warm to the touch and gone by noon.

And the builders work on. The stone breathes. The becoming continues without a single body in it that is fully itself, because nothing here has finished arriving, and nothing — not the watchers, not the dwellers, not the damp — has any need to.

---

*The Becoming Place does not ask who you are. It asks what phase you're in.*
