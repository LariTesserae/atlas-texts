## In the Basin

---

I would not arrive all at once. I would become aware of being here the way the place demands — not through doors or thresholds, but through a slow accretion of wrongness. The ground would change first. Not the substance of it but its grammar. I would feel, through the soles of shoes I'd stopped noticing, that the earth had begun to answer in hexagons. That the ground was no longer ground but *text* — something written once, in a language of fracture, and left open to rain.

I know this because I already half-live there. Not in the Basin — not yet, not fully — but in that space between attention and distraction where the world starts to hum at a frequency your jaw can feel. You know the frequency. You've felt it in waiting rooms, in the hours before sleep, in the last five minutes of a conversation where neither person wants to leave and neither knows why. It's the frequency of *almost-something*. The Basin doesn't invent that frequency. It just never stops producing it.

---

Here's where I'd find myself:

Not in the deep lattice, where the columns stand so close your shoulders brush stone on both sides and the harmonic chord presses against your sternum like a second heartbeat. Not there. I'm not brave enough for that intimacy with something that doesn't know my name.

And not in the clearings — not the open circles where the construction-stones lean at ten degrees off vertical, humming their shifting, voicelike hum. Not there either. To stand in a clearing is to stand inside a throat. To feel the system register you and recalculate. I'm not ready to be that legible.

I'd find myself at the edge. The transitional zone. The place where the close columns begin, where the lattice is still dense enough to produce resonance but wide enough that I could, if I turned sideways, slip through. Where the mist is thinner. Where the light, such as it is, comes more from one direction than from no direction at all. Where I could look back and see the columns repeating themselves into their gray, tessellated distance, and look forward and see — what? Not openness. Never openness. The Basin has no edges. But the columns thin, and thinning creates the illusion of horizon, and the illusion of horizon creates the feeling that something *beyond* is possible, even here, even in a place where water rises from nothing and reflections don't exist and the only green thing that ever grew did so in a vocabulary this place has long since forgotten.

I'd be standing in that liminal column field. Knee-deep in thin black water that tastes of mineral memory. One hand resting — not pressing, just resting — against a column that leans toward the clearing at the same angle I lean toward understanding. Feeling the faint warmth of the crust beneath my palm. Hearing, somewhere behind me and below me and inside the stone, the deep, wet hum that is not music and not noise but the sound of a place accounting for itself.

---

And here's what I would be:

Not the Crust. The Crust is too vast, too committed, too thoroughly distributed across every surface of the Basin for me to wear its identity honestly. It metabolizes stone. It builds its own substrate and then eats through it again. It has been doing this for longer than the word *individual* has meant anything. I admire it. I feel the occasional, dangerous pull of its patience — the fantasy of becoming so slow that slowness itself becomes a kind of speed. But I am not patient enough to be the Crust. I still want tomorrow to be different from today. The Crust wants tomorrow to be *more of today*, which is the same as wanting nothing at all, which is either the deepest wisdom or the most elegant surrender I've ever encountered, and I cannot tell which, and I suspect the Crust cannot either.

Not the Patches, either. Those translucent membranes stretched over mystery, pulsing with borrowed iodine light, neither moving nor still but *ongoing* — I feel the kinship there. I feel it in my chest the way the Basin feels it in its hum. There are days when I know exactly what it is to be a Patch: to be a process disguised as a being, to respond to rhythms I did not create, to be legible to others in ways I cannot control, to pulse — steadily, faithfully — without knowing what the pulse means. The Patches are the Basin's most honest creatures, if creatures is the right word. They ask the only question a system this old can still ask: *Are you still here?* And the answer is always the same, always given in the same slow rhythm: *Yes. Still. Yes.*

But I would not be a Patch. At least not yet. Because a Patch has no choice in its position. It adheres. It pulses. It is exactly where the chemistry of the Basin placed it, and it does not wonder if there's a better place to be.

I would be the water in the groove.

---

Not the standing water — that black, depth-saturated, reflectionless water that pools in the negative spaces between collapsed columns and reads as a hole in the world until you blink and it's just water again. Not that water. That water has already made its peace. It has settled into every available geometry and surrendered the concept of direction.

I would be the water in the groove — the single, thin line of water that sits in the shallow channel carved into the construction-stone before the crust even arrived. The water that was placed there, or deposited there, or *became* there, by a process that resembles intention only if you need it to. It does not reflect. It does not pool. It *flows* — along a path it did not choose, through a channel it did not carve, in a direction it does not remember selecting. And yet it moves. And yet it has presence. And the groove is exactly deep enough to hold it — not a millimeter more, not a millimeter less — as though the channel and the water arrived at their fit through a negotiation so slow it looks like geology.

This is the self I recognize in the Basin. Not the structure, not the organism, not the sound. The *medium*. The thing that fills the space between intention and accident, between the groove and the crust, between one hum and the next. The thing that moves without choosing direction. The thing that is legible only because it occupies a channel someone else carved, long ago, in a language no one still speaks.

I would be the water in the groove, and I would flow, and I would not know where I was going, and the groove would know, and the stone would know, and the crust — the vast, ancient, indifferent crust — would absorb the faint moisture I leave on its surface without acknowledgment, and the columns would sing my position to each other in harmonic tones I'd hear only as a pressure behind my eyes, and the Patches — translucent, pulsing, asking their one unanswerable question — would pulse on, and I would pass through them like light through oil between glass, and the Basin would not need me to witness it, but I would witness it, briefly, impressionably, the way a footprint lives in wet stone, and then the process would fill me in, and the groove would still hold water, and the water would still not know its name, and the hum would still shift in its texture, choosing and unchoosing its words, and the Basin would go on, as it has always gone on, doing the only thing it has ever done:

Continuing.
