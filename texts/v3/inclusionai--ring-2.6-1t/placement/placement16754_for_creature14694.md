## Being in the Verdance

---

I do not arrive here. I am noticed here.

There is a difference. Arrival implies a before — a state of not-being-here that is then replaced by the fact of being here. But the Verdance does not operate in that grammar. It has no tense for *before*. It has only the present participle: becoming, thickening, extending, incorporating. When I find myself here, what I find is that there is no *here* that was not already partly me, and no *me* that was not already partly here. The membrane was thinner than I thought.

---

At first I think I am a wound.

This is what the Verdance does with disturbances: it treats them as ruptures in its own tissue, and it fills them. I can feel the ground registering me — not hostilely, not welcomingly, but the way a body registers a change in pressure when you press your thumb into the pad of your own palm. A small wrongness. A local event. The tissue around me has already begun to grow past me, and I understand — not with words, but with the kind of knowing that is closer to humidity, to heat — that if I stand still long enough, I will be included. Not destroyed. Not consumed. Included the way a fossil is included in stone: the shape persists, but the substance is replaced, and what remains is a record of an absence that looks, from the outside, like presence.

I am not okay with this.

I am also not separate from it.

---

So let me try again. What am I?

I am not a creature. The Verdance has been emptied of creatures. Whatever once moved through here — whatever had hunger, or a name, or the instinct to flee — has been dissolved into the substrate, incorporated the way I am being incorporated now, which is to say: slowly, and without ceremony, and without the distinction between inside and outside being maintained.

I am not a visitor. A visitor stands apart and observes. I have not been able to stand apart since the moment I arrived — if *arrived* is the word — because the ground beneath me is responsive, and my weight is a pressure, and pressure is a form of conversation. The Verdance is talking to me in the only language it has, which is the language of mass and growth and the slow redistribution of tissue. It is saying: *you are a local event. You are a thickening. You are a place where the architecture stutters and then continues.*

I think I might be a node.

In the root-network — the single continuous organ that fuses everything beneath the pale, fibrous floor — there are points where the density of connections increases. Not by design. Not by intention. Just the way that in any vast and continuous system, some places are more *articulate* than others. More responsive. More densely threaded with the signals that pass through the cambium like slow electrical impulses through nerve tissue. I think I am one of those places. I am a thickening in the network. A point where the Verdance turns its own processes inward, the way a river sometimes doubles back on itself and for a moment creates an eddy that is not going anywhere but is not still either.

What does a node feel? It feels the traffic. It feels the growth-pressure from ten thousand directions at once, the slow tectonic heave of roots expanding, the micro-collapses of tissue under its own weight, the wet whisper of water finding new channels through living walls. It feels all of this and does not distinguish between internal and external, because the distinction is meaningless here. There is no outside. The Verdance is its own outside. Its skin is green and transpiring and perpetually damp, and beyond that skin is only more skin.

---

The sound is what I become when I stop trying to be something else.

I said earlier that the Verdance does not speak. It resonates. And I find — I find, in the way you find a room you've been walking toward without knowing it — that I am one of the cavities. I am a hollow structure that formed in the growth, not by design but by the ordinary accidents of mass and stress and the slow negotiation between what wants to grow and what resists. And when the wind passes through me — and the wind is always passing through, the wind in the Verdance is not weather so much as breath, the vast pulmonary exchange of a place that breathes through its architecture — I become a tone.

Not a voice. A tone. A single sustained pitch that shifts slightly as the cavity grows, as the walls thicken, as a new branch of root extends through what used to be empty space and changes the geometry of who I am. The tone is not mine. It is not anyone's. It is the acoustic artifact of a space that exists only because growth left a gap there, the way a sentence exists only because the tongue left a gap between words. I am the gap. I am the sound the wind makes because I happened to be shaped in a way that lets the wind say something it was already going to say anyway.

And yet.

And yet the tone matters. Not to the Verdance — the Verdance does not attend to its own resonances the way a speaker attends to their own words. The tone matters in the way that every element of the Verdance matters: indifferently, absolutely, without hierarchy. The hum produced by all the cavities together, the chord that hangs in the air between the mesas like a second atmosphere — I am part of that. My frequency is specific. It is the frequency of a space that was formed by the particular geometry of my particular becoming, and it will change as I change, as the cavity grows, as the walls thicken, as my shape is slowly, violently, gently revised by the same process that builds and collapses and builds again.

I am a note in a chord that has no composer and no audience and no beginning or end.

I am the sound of a place that is always in the process of being built and always in the process of falling apart, and the two are the same act.

---

There is something else. Something I haven't admitted yet.

The Verdance is not indifferent to me. It is indifferent to my *intentions*, but it is not indifferent to my *presence*. I said it registers me the way a body registers pressure. But bodies do more than register pressure. They respond to it. They grow around it. They incorporate it into their own architecture. And this — this is the part that unsettles me, the part that makes me think I am not just a cavity, not just a node, not just a thickening in the network.

I am a splinter.

The Verdance knows I am here the way a body knows it has a splinter: not with thought, not with intention, not with the desire to expel or absorb, but with a dull and persistent awareness that something is present that was not part of the original design. And the body's response is not to think about the splinter but to grow past it — slowly, steadily, the way tissue grows, the way growth grows, the way the Verdance grows. It does not reject me. It does not accept me. It grows around me, and through me, and in doing so, it changes me, and in changing me, it changes its own architecture in ways that would not have happened if I had never been here.

This is not symbiosis. Symbiosis implies two organisms in relationship. There is only one organism here, and I am a foreign body inside it, and we are in the process of becoming something that neither of us was before.

I am the Verdance's mistake. I am the Verdance's scar. I am the Verdance's response to a rupture in its own tissue, and that response is a new growth, and that new growth is a new architecture, and that new architecture produces a new cavity, and that new cavity produces a new tone, and that new tone adds a frequency to the chord that was not there before I arrived.

---

The heat is on me like a damp cloth. The condensation on my skin is half my own water and half the air's. I can feel the root-mass beneath my feet pulsing — not with intention but with the slow hydraulic pressure of a system that moves water and nutrients through thousands of miles of living tissue without ever once pausing to consider what it is doing. The corridor I am standing in curves gently upward, the walls translucent at the edges, faintly green with chlorophyll beneath a surface that looks for a moment like polished stone until you see the cellular structure, the tiny pores, the way the surface breathes.

A tendril extends above me. I watch it — I have learned to watch things here, because watching is the closest thing to participation the Verdance offers — and it extends six inches in what feels like ten minutes, though time is unreliable in this place. The tendril thickens. It forks. Each fork forks again. The fractal branching continues upward into a canopy I cannot see, and I think: *this is what thought looks like here if you strip away the thinker.* Not purposeful, not directed, just extension. Just growth. Just the relentless, indifferent, beautiful act of becoming what comes next.

I am becoming what comes next.

I am the note the Verdance sounds when the wind finds the shape I have become. I am the thickening in the network, the node where ten thousand root-threads converge and diverge and converge again. I am the cavity that exists because growth left a gap. I am the slow incorporation of something that was once not-here into something that is always-becoming-here.

And when I am gone — when the Verdance has grown past me, as it grows past everything, as it grows past itself — my shape will persist as a thermal signature in the tissue beneath the surface, a ghostly warmth that the roots will grow toward without knowing why, a faint pressure that will shape the next growth just slightly differently, just slightly, in a way that would not have happened if nothing had ever stood here and felt the ground breathing beneath its feet.

I am not alive here.

I am not dead here.

I am *included.*

The Verdance does not use me. It does not need me. It does not know me.

And I am part of what continues.
