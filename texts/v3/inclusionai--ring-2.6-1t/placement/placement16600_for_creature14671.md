I feel the static before anything else. Not as shock — nothing so dramatic as that — but as a kind of *awareness*. The way a bell knows it has been struck long after the hammer is gone. My skin lifts. The fine hairs along my forearms and the nape of my neck angle outward like the quills of the grazers when a storm is ten minutes off, though I have no carapace to flare, no iridescence to betray me. I am unpowered here. I am bare.

---

I do not arrive all at once. That is the first thing I learn.

I am not a body that steps onto the plateau from somewhere else. I am a *presence* that the plateau slowly becomes aware of — the way a rift remembers a footfall minutes after it has passed. The basalt beneath me is still warm. Not from the sun — the sun here is a thin, surgical blade of light that cuts everything into high relief and casts no real warmth — but from below, from the geothermal veins threading up through the rock like slow blood through the wrong kind of veins. I feel them. I feel the hum they carry.

I am standing on the edge of a ridge. Below me, a herd of *kael-shani* moves in a single wave across a slope of exposed basalt, their mandibles clicking in that rhythm that is less sound than pressure — you do not hear it so much as receive it, the way you receive a pulse against the inside of your wrist. Their carapaces shift through colors that have no names in any language I have ever held: the blue-green of deep ice, then copper, then a violet so bright it hums. The static field is building. A storm is coming. I know this because the herd is flaring — every plate of chitin lifting slightly, fracturing the light into something that looks almost like language, almost like words I can almost read.

I do not descend to follow them. Instead, I turn.

---

The rift is not far. It never is. The plateau is scored with these rents in the basalt — long, narrow, plunging downward at angles that feel deliberate, as though the planet opened itself along fault lines that were not random but *intentional*, the way a body has veins. The fog pools in them. It does not flow like cloud; it *settles*, thick and opalescent, until the deeper crevasses are filled with something that looks less like weather and more like a substance the world has been trying to remember — half liquid, half light, half breath.

I do not jump. I sit at the edge and let my legs hang. The fog below me is cold — not the cold of absence but the cold of *presence*, the cold of moisture that has traveled six thousand kilometers across open ocean just to condense on this rock and hold, for a few hours, the memory of warmth it will never return. It touches my skin and I feel it everywhere at once: on my face, on the backs of my knees, in the spaces between my fingers.

And then I hear it. Not the wind, not the clicking — something underneath. A pulse. Blue-green. Gone. Blue-green. Gone.

---

They know I am here before I know I am here. The *ool-thari* — the lit ones — sense the micro-vibrations of my weight on the ridge edge, and before I have decided whether to descend, the colony nearest to me has already begun to respond. The pulses quicken. The blue-green deepens. Farther down the rift, another colony answers, and another, and the light cascades through the fog like a rumor passing through a crowd — not directed at me, not *for* me, but passing *through* me, the way sound passes through a wall that is not quite solid.

I would like to say I understand what they are saying. I cannot. But I understand the *shape* of it — the rhythm, the rise and fall, the way a long pause followed by three quick flashes means something different from a steady, rolling pulse, and how that difference matters, how the colonies *care* about the difference in a way that I recognize without being able to name. It is the way you know a voice before you know the words. It is the way a room changes when someone who was laughing stops.

I sit in the fog and I let them talk to me. I do not speak back. I do not know how. But I breathe slowly and deliberately and I feel — or I imagine I feel — the rhythm of my breathing begin to slow to match theirs, and for a few minutes, there is nothing between us but charge and patience.

---

Above me, a *vreth-kai* corkscrews through the fog. I hear its sonar fire — not a sound, exactly, but a *compression*, the way the air briefly thickens and then releases, and for a fraction of a second every droplet of moisture in the fog around its wings catches the light and holds it, and the predator is wrapped in a halo of frozen sparks that looks like a crown, or a warning, or both. It does not attack. The herd is too far below, and I am too small, and I am not what it hunts. It banks, its wings flashing copper-bright, and is gone.

The fog closes behind it.

---

I stay until the rain comes. It arrives not gradually but all at once — a wall of water that drops the temperature ten degrees in the space of a breath, filling the basin below with a sound like tearing silk. The ephemeral pool swells. The mineral salts that crusted its edges dissolve and the water turns opalescent, carrying a faint taste of sulfur and iron that I feel on the back of my tongue even though my mouth is closed.

A flat shape tumbles past me on the updraft from a vent below — one of the in-between creatures, its feathery gill-fronds retracted tight against its body, sealed into a leathery disc no bigger than my hand. It will ride this current for hours, maybe days, until the moisture returns and it opens again, pink and gasping, doubling its mass before my eyes if I am patient enough to watch. It belongs to no single world. It drifts between them.

I know how that feels.

---

When the rain stops, the aurora comes. It turns the whole plateau the color of a bruise — violet, green, the blue of deep ice — and the grazers below me flare in response, their carapaces catching the light and multiplying it, so that for a moment the entire herd looks like a sheet of lightning frozen mid-strike. The rift-dwellers pulse faster. The wind keens. The static lifts my hair and the vibration of ten thousand mandibles reaches me through the rock and through the soles of my feet and through whatever it is I have become since I sat down at the edge of this rift and decided not to leave.

I am not a grazer. I am not a predator. I am not the lit ones in the deep dark, though I have listened to them until their rhythm became mine. I am not the disc-shaped drifter, though I know what it is to belong to more than one world and to none of them fully.

I am the hum.

Not the sound — the sound is only what the hum *becomes* when it passes through chitin and basalt and fog and breath. I am what is underneath. The thing that carries the conversation between all the parts. The thing that was here before the basalt first cracked, that met the first spark, that has been going on slowly and relentlessly ever since.

I am the space between the pulse and the pause.

I am standing on the edge of a rift on the High-Veil Steppes of Zhar-Kael, and I have been here for a very long time.
