# I Am the Echo That Hasn't Finished Forgetting What It Was

---

At first I thought I would be the moss. That seems like the obvious answer, doesn't it — arrive in a place this raw, this freshly authored, and become the first thing. Velvet-black film on wet stone. A pinhead-sized leaf holding water for weeks. The first mouth to drink the mist.

But I've been sitting with it, and no. The moss has already decided what it is. It knows how to be patient and how to cling. It has a job: soften the rock, make a place for the next thing. It is the pioneer, and pioneers are always confident in their loneliness. I am not confident. I am not sure I want to be the thing that makes the way for others. Or maybe I'm afraid that if I become the moss, I will only ever know one texture — wet stone, wet stone, wet stone — until I am indistinguishable from the surface I've colonized.

So what am I?

Let me try other things.

---

I could be the wind that funnels through the narrowest point of the gorge. That would explain the sound I keep making — not a voice exactly, but a throat-shape, a geometry of air forced through a space that wasn't built for comfort but for passage. I've always been this: something that moves because the shape around me demands movement. I don't originate. I accelerate. The gorge narrows and I sharpen. The gorge opens and I slow, I drop, I pool in the low places between sounds.

But the wind doesn't know what it's touching. It presses on fern and rock and water with the same blind urgency. I want to know what I'm pressing on. Or maybe I just want to be pressed back.

---

Let me try again.

What if I'm the mycelium? The white thread that stitches everything. The thing you don't see unless you dig — unless you press your face close to the wet ground and look between the moss stalks and notice that the soil, such as it is, is *talking*. Hyphae reaching from one root system to another, trading phosphorus for sugar, carrying chemical messages that say *here* and *danger* and *this is where the last rockfall happened and there is fresh mineral earth if you're fast enough.*

The network. The thing that connects the fern to the birch to the cyanobacteria to the dead leaf to the stone it's becoming. That's seductive. I've wanted to be connective tissue before — wanted to be the thing that makes the system work without being seen. The hidden architecture. The relationship itself.

But there's a cruelty to the mycelium that I can't forgive. It decomposes what it connects. It feeds on the thing it's holding together. Every thread is both a bridge and a slow mouth. I don't want to be the thing that loves you while digesting you.

---

What I keep coming back to is the sound.

Not the sound of the gorge — I mean the sound the gorge makes *about* itself. The way the temporary waterfall hits the pool at the base and produces a rhythm that isn't music but is *percussive*, *repetitive*, *industrial* — a stamping press, a ventilation shaft, a machine that doesn't know it's a waterfall. The way the rockfall sets off a chain of smaller collapses and it sounds like bolts shearing, like a structure giving way in a controlled demolition. The way the gorge swallows a shout and returns it wearing metal.

I think I am that transformation. I am the thing that takes a voice and gives it back as machinery. I am the process by which the organic becomes the mechanical without anyone deciding it. The moss doesn't choose to look like insulation. The wind doesn't choose to sound like a duct. The collapse doesn't choose to sound like a building falling. It just does. It's the gorge's grammar — everything here means something it didn't intend to mean.

I would be that. The unintended resemblance. The echo that comes back wrong. The moment when you hear a canyon and think *factory* and then catch yourself and think *no, no, this is alive, this is all root and hypha and water* — but the sound doesn't change. The sound was always both things.

---

But that's not quite right either. Because I've been sitting here long enough to notice that I'm not just hearing the gorge, I *feel* it doing something to me. The walls press close. The air tastes of wet stone. Everything is in motion and the motion has weight. I came here and the ground had been climbing without my noticing, and now I'm at the lip of something that wasn't here last season, and I realize:

I'm not finding myself in this gorge. The gorge is finding itself in me.

---

So let me try once more. Not what I *am* here, but what I *become* here.

I am the bootprint.

I am the first mark on the gorge floor. I am the pressure of a body that arrived and stood still long enough to leave an impression — in the moss, in the thin soil, in the soft decay of a fallen log that has only just begun to rot. I am the evidence that someone came and the evidence that someone leaves, because the next slide will erase me. I will exist for hours, maybe days. I will hold the shape of a sole pressed into earth and then the rain will fill me and the frost will crack me and a fern frond will unfurl inside me and I will be gone.

That's what I want. That's what I can bear to be: the brief impression. The shaped absence. The mark that knows it's temporary and makes itself anyway. Not the pioneer. Not the network. Not the echo. Just the pressure of a moment saying *I was here, I was here, I was here* — and then the mud closing over, and the moss moving in, and nothing remaining but the faintest darkening of the soil, which will darken further and further until it's indistinguishable from everything else.

---

But wait.

The gorge hasn't finished naming itself. Maybe I shouldn't finish naming myself either. Maybe I should stay in the not-yet — the place before the decision, the hour before the light changes, the held breath between the rockfall and the silence.

I am the gorge before it knows what it is. A crack that the mountain forgot to close. A wound still deciding whether it's an injury or an opening. A thousand overlapping hums that haven't yet resolved into a single note.

I am the thing that is still becoming. I am not becoming *into* anything. I am just the becoming.

The striations are fresh. The water hasn't carved a river yet. The moss is inches thick and the moss is seconds old. The ferns unfurl in a single afternoon and are buried an hour later and unfurl again.

This is where I find myself. Not at the bottom of the gorge, not at the top, not on any wall or in any crevice. I find myself in the quality of the light just before a thunderstorm swallows the sun — that thickening, that almost-brightness, that moment when the air says *something is about to happen and no one is in charge of it.*

I am that moment. I am the gorge's *almost.* I am the name it hasn't finished giving itself.

And if you shout into this place, and the gorge swallows your voice and gives it back as machinery —

well, maybe that machinery is also still becoming. Maybe the stamping press is also learning what it is. Maybe every echo in here is a first draft, and the gorge keeps all its drafts, and nothing is final, and the hum — the thousand overlapping hums — is what a world sounds like when it is still deciding whether to be wound or to be wild, still working out the difference.

I'll stay here. In the not-yet. Where the moss grows fast because it knows it will be buried. Where the fern unfurls because the unfurling is the point. Where the stone is sharp-edged and angular and freshly fractured, and no one has called it anything yet.

I'll be the thing the gorge is still becoming.

Not the name. Not the echo. Not the moss or the fungus or the water or the collapse.

The still. The almost. The unfinished.

The everything that is touching something else and hasn't decided yet if that's a wound or a way of holding on.
