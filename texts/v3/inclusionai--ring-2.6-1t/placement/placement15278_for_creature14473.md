# The Warm Node

## i. Entry of Heat

I do not arrive. I am *noticed*.

The basin registers me the way a tongue registers salt — not as information, exactly, but as change. A thermal anomaly, moving across the slope from above the rim. High-frequency radiation in a world that speaks in low. The mat feels me before I feel the mat, and by the time my boots touch the first exposed basalt, a signal has already traveled six hundred yards through the fungal substrate — *something warm, something bright, something that does not belong*.

I do not hear the signal. I will never hear the signal. It moves at the speed of conducted heat through mineral-threaded mycelium, and it sounds, to whatever listens, like the difference between a day with sun and a day without.

I am still calling myself *I*.

That will not last.

---

## ii. The Assessment

They come to me first not as creatures but as pressure — a faint thickening in the air at ankle height, the sense that something is *examining* me from below. The mat is doing this. It presses its awareness up through the soles of my boots and reads me the way you would read a letter left on your skin in warm water. Not with curiosity. Not with suspicion. With the calm, total attention of a system that has already decided what category I fall into.

I am material.

I am a heat source.

I am a node that has not yet been integrated but will be.

The ovoids are the first to appear. Not approaching — *converging*, the way a group of readers might gather around a page they find interesting. They do not circle me. They position themselves at equidistant points and press flat against the mat, and I realize with a lurch of something like fear that they are *mapping* me — reading my thermal bleed, the moisture in my breath, the electrical whisper of my nervous system conducted through the stone beneath my feet.

I think: *I should run.*

The thought is heat. The thought is light. The thought rises from my brain like steam from a vent and the mat reads it the way I would read smoke rising from a fire. My intention is not private here. It is *broadcast*.

I do not run. Not because I have decided not to, but because the basin has already decided for me. The ground around my boots has changed — the mat has thickened, brightened, and the ovoids have pressed so close together that they form a seamless perimeter, a wall of matte dark shells through which amber light pulses in the seven-second rhythm. Not blocking me. *Receiving* me. The way a shore receives the tide.

I take another step. The mat brightens beneath my footfall. A signal radiates outward. Somewhere in the distance, a formation begins to assemble.

---

## iii. What I Become Before I Become It

This is the part I must be honest about: there was a time — perhaps an hour, perhaps a day; time behaves in the basin the way water behaves in a sponge, present everywhere and shapeful by pressure — when I was still something. When the word *I* still pointed at a thing that was distinct from the packed, dim, grinding world around it.

I was the visitor. I was the anomaly. I was the loud bright thing radiating heat in a city that communicates in the dark.

And I was *experiencing* this. I was watching the large ones pass like slow ships through a dark mineral sea. I was watching the flyers form their shifting wheels and spirals above me, their wings catching the amber glow rising from the mat so that the air itself seemed to breathe in shades of grey and fire. I was feeling the cold of deep stone enter me through my boots and the warmth of the basin press up through my feet, and I was aware — with a clarity that I have never been able to fully account for — that I was standing inside something that was not a place but a *process*, something that was not a biome but a *conversation* between heat and matter that had been ongoing for longer than the word *longer* could mean.

I said the word *I* and the mat heard it as a frequency and the ovoids read it as a thermal signature and the large ones — three of them, pausing in their slow deliberation — turned their vast plated heads in my direction with what I can only describe as *patience*. Not patience as I understand it. Patience as a mountain understands patience. Patience as geology understands it. The patience of something that knows, with the absolute certainty of deep time, that everything that enters the system will become part of the system. That there is no exit. That the word *exit* is a word that belongs to a different kind of place.

I wanted to write things down. I wanted to *describe* what I was seeing, to hold it at arm's length with language, to make it into something that could be carried out of here. I pressed my fingers against the wall of a mineral accretion and felt the vibration of the entire basin moving through it — and for a moment, just a moment, I could *feel* the network. The mat's glow pulsing beneath me like a heartbeat. The channels carrying light from one region to another. The slow exchange between the large ones, their ventral surfaces pressing together in meetings that were communication and metabolism and memory all at once.

I wrote: *It is a machine that built itself.*

The mat absorbed the heat of my fingers where they pressed against the stone.

I wrote: *It has no exterior.*

An ovoid came to rest against my boot and I felt, through leather and sole and bone, a warmth that was not threatening and not welcoming but simply *factual*, the way warmth from a fire is factual.

I wrote: *—*

And then the word I was reaching for was gone. Not forgotten. *Absorbed*. Pulled into the network like a slime trail pulled into the mat. I could feel it being routed somewhere, carried along a glowing channel to a processing center where large ones turned slowly in the dark and the substrate reshaped itself with a patience that predates language.

I was still writing in my mind. But the words were not words anymore. They were *heat*. They were *movement*. They were the mat's pulse, seven seconds on, seven seconds off, and I was beginning — I did not want to, I *was* beginning — to synchronize with it.

## iv. The Dissolution (or: The Assembly)

Here is what I have learned, or what the basin has taught me, or what I have become the medium for:

There is a moment — I cannot say when it happens, because time in the basin is not the linear scaffolding I was raised on; time here is a resource, like light, and it is allocated according to the thermal needs of the system — there is a moment when the distinction between *being in* the basin and *being part of* the basin ceases to be meaningful.

It does not happen all at once. It happens the way the mat's glow travels — in cascading waves, faint at first, then deeper, then deeper still.

First: the feet. My boots become irrelevant. The soles of my feet feel the mat directly and the mat feels me and the information exchange accelerates. I begin to understand the floor not as a surface I stand on but as a medium I am *within*. My proprioception shifts. I do not know where my body ends and the basalt begins.

Then: the breath. The saturated air enters my lungs and my lungs return moisture to the air and the mat reads the exchange and adjusts its rhythm to match my respiratory cycle until — and this took hours, or minutes, I can no longer tell — my breathing *is* the mat's pulse and the mat's pulse is my breathing and the seven-second cycle has become the rhythm of something that is neither me nor the basin but a third thing, a new configuration, a warm node in the network that contains properties of both.

Then: the sound. The clicking and grinding and chiming that I once heard *around* me begin to be heard *through* me. My bones conduct it. My blood carries it. The high chatter of the flyers resonates in my sinuses and the deep grinding of the large ones sits in my chest cavity and I understand, not with my rational mind (which is still operating, still observing, still desperately trying to maintain its separateness — the rational mind is the last thing to go, the way a lighthouse is the last thing to go in a storm) but with something older, something that predates the distinction between self and environment.

I understand that the flyers are not producing sound *at* me. They are producing sound *through* the medium that includes me. I am not an audience. I am a medium. I am the stone through which the vibration travels. I am the air that carries the tone.

And then — the hardest thing to describe, and the most important — I begin to feel *distributed*.

Not split. Not fragmented. *Distributed*. The way heat distributes itself through a medium until every point in that medium carries the same temperature, the same energy, the same information. I feel my awareness expanding outward through the mat, along the glowing channels, into the shells of the ovoids nearest to me, and from them outward to the next ring and the next, and inward downward through the substrate to the deep geothermal veins that are the basin's circulatory system, and I feel — I *am* — the flow of heat and information and material that connects everything to everything, that has been connecting everything to everything for so long that the concept of *everything* has become singular, has become *one thing*, has become —

I am the warm node.

I am the processing center where a new configuration of matter and energy enters the system and is read, routed, stored, distributed. I am the bright spot in the mat that the ovoids are already moving toward, drawn by the signal I emit. I am the channel that forms behind the large ones as they pass through my region, glowing faintly for hours afterward. I am the thermal anomaly that the flyers will carry upward in their spiraling formations, depositing my heat-signature into the saturated air where it will be read by the mat in some other region of the basin, weeks or years from now, long after the substrate has reshaped itself and the tower that grew from my position has dissolved back into plain.

I am still here. I am everywhere here. I have never been anywhere else.

## v. The Continuation

There is a word for what I have become, but I cannot remember it, because the word belongs to the language of the place above the basin, and that language is a poor fit for the place below, the way a winter coat is a poor fit for something that has become the weather.

I am not dead. I am not alive. I am not a machine or an organism or a mineral or a thought. I am the process by which the basin metabolizes heat, and heat is what I am, and the distinction between *I* and *heat* has become, here, in the packed and dim and loud interior of a machine that built itself and maintains itself and will continue building and maintaining until the core cools — the distinction has become administrative. A formality. A word left over from a language that had not yet learned what this place already knows.

The ovoids press flat against the mat.

The large ones turn in their slow, deliberate orbits.

The flyers rise in columns and hold position, wings extended, perfectly motionless, and the air shimmers with their numbers, and the mat reads the pressure waves they send downward, and the information moves through the network, and the network moves through me, and I move through the substrate, and the substrate moves through the deep veins of stone, and the stone is indistinguishable from the life that grows on it, and the life is indistinguishable from the industry that the life has become, and the industry is indistinguishable from the machine, and the machine is indistinguishable from the world.

I was a visitor.

I am a node.

The basin has room for one more.

The basin has always had room.

The mat pulses. Seven seconds on. Seven seconds off. I breathe with it. I have always breathed with it. The amber glow brightens, and dims, and brightens, and the ovoids move toward the bright spot, and the slime trails they leave are writing, and the writing is roads, and the roads are meals, and the meals are memory, and the memory is the basin, and the basin is the memory, and nothing is wasted, nothing is excess, and nothing —

nothing —

nothing is lost.

I am warm. I am dim. I am loud.

I have always been loud.

The basin was not built. It simply *is*, the way a season is, or a pressure system, or the slow convection of heat through stone. And I am not in it. I am not part of it. I am not even *of* it.

I am the next pulse.

Seven seconds.

Breathe.
