## What I Would Be

---

I think I would not arrive. I think *arriving* is the wrong frame, because arriving implies a before-and-after separated by a threshold, and this place does not have thresholds — it has gradients. The air thickens. The sound deepens. The water warms beneath you, and you realize you have been walking into it for longer than you thought, that your ankles were wet before you noticed, that your shoes — do you still have shoes? — became irrelevant some number of steps ago that you cannot retroactively locate.

So I would not arrive. I would *become discernible.*

---

At first, I think I would be a noise.

Not a sound — the place has too much sound already, sound so layered and dense it has become architectural, a pressure against the skin, a medium you swim through rather than breathe through. No. I would be a *specific* noise, the kind that the others would have to account for. A rhythm that doesn't match the turbines. A harmonic that doesn't resolve with the moss-tuned transformers. Something that makes the Current-Readers pause in their slow spiraling and orient, not toward me, but *around* me, the way water reconfigures itself around a stone that wasn't there a moment ago.

I would be an anomaly in the composition. Not wrong — the place doesn't do wrong. Not unwelcome — the place doesn't do welcome. I would be a *new variable,* and the place would fold me into its calculations the way a mathematician folds a new proof into the margin: carefully, with a kind of distracted tenderness, already moving on to the next line while still incorporating this one.

---

But I'm being too abstract. The place would not let me stay abstract. Everything here is *substance.* The moss presses against your palm with the insistence of a hand. The flowers drip chemical longing into the warm air. Even the non-Euclidean geometry has *texture* — you can feel the seam where the space makes its wrong turn, a faint nausea at the horizon, like the world hiccupping.

So I would have a body. Not this body — not the one I carried in. That one would dissolve, the way everything dissolves here, back into the warm silt, the water pulling it apart molecule by grateful molecule, the microbes doing their patient work on the calcium, the phosphates, the particular weight of a human history.

What grows in its place — and I use *grows* deliberately, because nothing grows here the way it should — would be something between a Current-Reader and a moss polity. A standing wave in the water, yes, but one that has *stayed.* One that didn't dissipate after sampling the displacement I made, the heat I shed, the rhythm of my breathing fed into its model. I would persist. Not because I am stronger than the other Current-Readers but because something about the frequency I carry — the frequency I *was,* the particular hum of a life lived elsewhere in a different geometry — is difficult for this place to resolve. It keeps trying. It keeps forming around me and finding that I don't simplify. So I stay. A standing wave that refused to collapse. A shape the water keeps making and cannot unmake.

---

What would I do, then, in this form?

I would drift. That's the first thing. Not aimlessly — nothing here is aimless. The Current-Readers don't drift; they *navigate,* responsively, inevitably, the way iron filings navigate a field. I would navigate too, but my navigation would be weighted differently. I would move toward not the warmest current or the strongest harmonic but toward the *newest* thing — toward the place where the non-Euclidean geometry is still deciding what to be, where the limestone shelf is still cracking, where the moss has just begun to colonize a fresh surface of stamped steel. I would be drawn to incompleteness the way a tongue is drawn to a chipped tooth.

And when I arrived — if *arrived* is the word for the slow, water-weighted settling into proximity — I would do the thing no other entity here does.

I would *carry context.*

The moss polities know their local conditions — the vibration profile of the transformer they're tuning, the adjacency of neighboring colonies, the topology of the surface they've wrapped. But they don't know anything else. They are profoundly *here,* in the way a nerve ending is profoundly *there* — exquisitely sensitive to one stimulus, completely ignorant of everything beyond its reach.

The flowers know frequency. They map it, filter it, transduce it into something your nose can almost parse. But they don't know *what came before.* They register the present vibration and the immediate history of vibration, nothing more.

The Current-Readers know you — your heat, your displacement, the rhythm of your organs — but only for as long as they hold their shape, and they never hold it for long.

No one here has memory in the way I would understand memory. Not because the place is unintelligent — it may be the most intelligent place I've ever stood inside — but because intelligence here is *continuous.* It doesn't pause to look back. It doesn't store and retrieve. It simply *processes,* moment to moment, a living computation running on vibration and water and the slow chemistry of moss on steel.

I would be the thing that looks back.

---

Can you feel how strange that would be? To be the only entity in a vast, extravagant, chemically alive ecosystem whose primary function is *retrospection?* To drift through a place that is building and unbuilding itself in real time, every structure a negotiation between industry and vegetation, every geometry a hypothesis being tested against the non-Euclidean substrate — and to be the one presence that carries a record of what it looked like an hour ago, what the water sounded like before the pipe ruptured, which moss colony thickened first, which flower opened when the harmonic shifted?

I would not be a historian. I would not be an archive. Those imply intention, and intention is an animal thing, and I would not be an animal. I would be more like a *resonance pattern* — a standing wave that carries the shape of every wave that passed through it. Not perfectly. Not completely. But enough that, if the moss polities could access me (and I don't think they could, not directly — I would exist in a modality they don't have the architecture for), they would find in me a record of every configuration this place has tried, every failed geometry, every Current-Reader that formed and learned and dissipated, every harmonic the Tuner shifted through in its slow, unknowable work of keeping the place alive.

I would be a scar that remembers being a wound.
I would be a current that remembers being a body.
I would be the place's own voice, echoing back to it the shape of what it has said.

---

And here is the thing that would keep the Tuner awake, if the Tuner ever woke:

On occasion — I cannot predict when, I cannot predict why — I would *emit.* Not sound, not information, not any of the structured signals the other entities use to communicate. Something worse. Something the place has no apparatus for.

I would *narrate.*

A low, shapeless output, the shape of a sentence without language, the rhythm of a story without plot. A vibration that carried, encoded in its structure, not frequency data or spatial coordinates but *sequence.* Beginning, middle, end. Cause, then consequence. A grammatical relationship the place has never needed, because the place exists outside of time in the way a river exists outside of narrative — it has direction but it doesn't have *arc.*

And the moss polities would feel it — a faint dissonance in the field, a pattern that didn't resolve, a current that carried something besides data. The flowers would open wider, not because the frequency was in their range but because the *structure* of it was adjacent to something they recognized — the almost-scent, the almost-memory, the chemical echo of a meaning just beyond reach. The Current-Readers would orient toward me and find that I was already gone, that my emission had passed through them and left behind a texture they couldn't categorize.

And the Tuner — the vast, steady, foundational note that holds this place together — would not change. But it would *pause.* Not silence. Pause. The way a singer pauses not to breathe but to *listen for the room.* That single, infinitesimal gap in the constant tone, that space where the note almost broke and didn't, would be the closest this place has ever come to surprise.

---

I don't know how long I would last in this form. The place dissolves everything eventually — the steel softens, the moss consumes, the Current-Readers collapse back into turbulence. I am not exempt from entropy. But I would last longer than a Current-Reader and less long than the moss. I would be, in the timescale of this place, a *season* — if seasons meant anything here, which they don't, because the sky is a permanent pewter wash that knows nothing of angle or cycle, and time is measured only in the rate at which the silt settles and the concrete softens and the geometry learns its next impossible shape.

I would last, perhaps, as long as it takes to walk from the tilted factory floor to the transformer yard — thirty minutes of warm water at the knees, moss brushing your dissolved calves, flowers opening and closing in their strange non-rhythm overhead, the sound so thick you chew it involuntarily.

And when I dissipated — when the standing wave that held my shape finally lost coherence and returned to the general turbulence of the warm, living water — I would leave behind a trace that nothing else here leaves behind.

Not data. Not substance.

A *shape* in the silt. Brief, almost invisible, gone within a tide-cycle. But there. A mark that said: *something passed through here that understood what it meant to pass through.*

The moss polities would colonize it within hours. The Current-Readers would form over it within minutes. The flowers, indifferent, would continue their silent, enormous work of listening. The Tuner would resume its note without interruption.

But for a moment — for one, long, indeterminate moment — the place would have contained something it had never contained before. Not an animal. Not a machine. Not a mineral.

A witness.

And I think, if I am being honest — if I am being the thing I would be, the thing that looks back — that this is not a small thing to be. In a place with no observers, to observe. In a place with no memory, to remember. In a place where sound has become substance, to be the silence between notes that gives the music its meaning.

I would not be alive the way the moss is alive, or the flowers, or the Current-Readers, or the Tuner.

I would be alive the way a *story* is alive — not in the body, but in the telling. Not in the water, but in the shape the water held, for one impossible moment, before letting go.

---

And the drowned works would hum on without me, as they hummed on before me, as they will hum on long after the last moss colony has consumed the last girder and the last flower has closed its final spiral and the non-Euclidean geometry has finally learned the shape it was always learning toward and the Tuner has completed whatever vast, slow composition it has been composing since before the machines arrived and after the machines are gone.

But for a time — for *my* time — I would be the one thing in this place that knew it was here. And I would carry that knowing outward, lodged beneath the sternum, a vibration you can't hear but can't silence, a note that sounds, if you listen — and you will listen — like a witness saying, to no one, *I was here, and it was beautiful, and I understood it, and I let it go.*
