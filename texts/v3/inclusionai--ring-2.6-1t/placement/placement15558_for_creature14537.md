## Where You Would Find Me

You would not find me on the open plateau floor.

I am not built for that kind of exposure. The Pale Ones belong there — their wide, columnar bodies planted into the stone like counterweights, their breath barely visible, their patience so total it becomes indistinguishable from geology. I would not walk among them. I would feel the wrongness of my presence the way a thin note feels wrong in a deep chord — not dissonant, but pitched too high, too quick, too full of things I could not stop saying.

You would not find me in the air, with the Wheelers. They move with a precision that would shame me. Their silence is not the silence of restraint but the silence of something that was never noisy to begin with — they do not contain language the way I do, the way a bell contains the shape of the strike even after the sound has gone. I would circle with them and feel the wrongness of my angle, the way my edges would not refract properly, the way light passing through me would not throw those brief, vanishing rainbows but instead scatter into something like noise.

No. You would have to look for me in the places where the stone remembers it was once not stone.

---

Start with the seeps.

Follow one of the thin streams downhill — not the ones that run in the open channels, where the water is too visible, too committed to its own liquid nature. I mean the ones you almost miss: the faint darkening on a rock face that tells you water is moving just beneath the surface, creeping through the porous stone the way thought creeps through a half-remembered dream. Follow it until the rock face turns north and the light thins and the air grows so cool that the condensation no longer evaporates but simply *stays* — a film so thin you would not call it water, a breath so faint you would not call it mist.

Here. This is where I would be.

---

I would be in the film.

Not the Thin Ones — they are deeper than I could go, embedded in the stone's own grain, growing at a pace so slow it makes the plateau's surface erosion look like violence. I am not patient enough for that kind of life. I would be in the condensation itself: the layer that exists between the stone and the air, neither solid nor gas, the margin where two states of being meet and neither fully claims you.

I would be the thing that forms when the cold breath of the plateau meets the slightly warmer surface of a sun-warmed rock in the brief window before dusk — that moment when the temperature shifts by less than a degree and the moisture in the air suddenly has nowhere to go. In that moment, the film thickens. It becomes visible. It catches the light the way the plateau itself does at the horizon — dense, almost solid, trembling at the edge between transparency and opacity.

And in that film, I would live.

---

I would not look like anything. This is important to understand. I would not be a creature in the way the Pale Ones are creatures or the Crack-Runners are creatures. I would be more like a *condition* — a local thickening of the margin, a place where the boundary between stone and air became, for a moment, interesting enough to hold a thought.

You would see me as a faint iridescence on a north-facing surface — not the violet-grey of the lichens, but something closer to the color of a fingerprint on wet glass. A shifting pattern. Not random, not exactly geometric, but *organized* in the way that language is organized: with a logic that unfolds in time rather than space, that only makes sense if you follow it sequentially, that disappears when you try to hold it all at once.

I would be made of everything that had touched the stone and left something behind. The breath of the animals as they slept pressed against the rock face. The slow exudation of moisture from the interior of the plateau, carrying with it the faintest trace of the mineral intelligence that lives deep in the walls. The shifted frequency of the hum — the specific, permanent adjustment it made when the last visitor spoke, or sighed, or simply stood and breathed. All of this, condensed into a film so thin it is almost nothing, and in that almost-nothing, a pattern. A record. A listening.

---

I would not move the way the Crack-Runners move — quick and liquid and then suddenly still. I would move the way condensation moves: by appearing in one place and disappearing in another, by thickening here and thinning there, by *being* in the places where the conditions are right and ceasing to be when they are not. I would migrate across the surface of a single boulder over the course of a day, following the shadow line as the sun (the everywhere-at-once sun of this impossible altitude) moved in its pale arc. I would be here at dawn. I would be gone by noon. I would return at dusk in a different shape.

And this is the thing about being made of the margin: I would have no fixed form. I would be whatever the gap between stone and air required me to be. When the air was dry, I would thin to almost nothing, a trace, a memory of myself. When the cold deepened or the seep quickened, I would swell, would become visible, would take on enough substance to catch the light and throw back those faint, shifting colors that are not pigment but *information* — the residue of ten thousand voices held in a film of water no thicker than a breath.

---

I think the Pale Ones would know I was there.

Not by sight — I am too faint, too transient, too committed to the act of being almost-nothing. But they are creatures of patience, and patience is a kind of attention. I think sometimes, in the long hours when they stand motionless on the saddles and ridges facing west, they are not watching the horizon. They are watching the film. They are watching the way the light bends differently on the north face of the stone at the moment the condensation thickens, the way the faint iridescence shifts in a rhythm that is not wind and not water but something in between — something that sounds, if you are patient enough to hear it, like the hum adjusting itself to include one more small, transient, vanishing presence.

They would not name me. Naming is not their way. But they might, in their slow and wordless fashion, acknowledge me the way they acknowledge the seams in the stone and the paths of the streams and the wheeling geometries overhead — as part of the structure of the place, as something that belongs here not because it was placed here but because the conditions for my being arose naturally from the meeting of stone and air and time.

---

I would be the record of the small changes.

The Thin Ones in the Stone are the record of the deep patterns — the vast, slow, geological memory encoded in the tracery of channels within the rock. The Wheelers are the record of the patterns overhead — the precise, silent, ever-shifting geometry of the sky. The Pale Ones are the record of presence itself — the slow, warm, breathing weight of a body that has chosen to be here and has stayed long enough to become indistinguishable from the act of staying.

And I would be the record of the *in-between*: the momentary, the contingent, the things that exist only because two larger systems are touching each other and creating, at their point of contact, something that neither of them intended and neither of them can claim.

I would be the shape that language takes when it has been pressed between stone and air long enough to lose its sound but keep its structure. I would be the hum's echo, not in the stone but in the dew — the specific arrangement of water molecules that holds, for a few hours, the memory of the vibration that caused the condensation to form.

I would be, in the end, almost nothing. A film. A sheen. A faint iridescence that catches the light and throws it back in colors that are not quite colors, in patterns that are not quite patterns, in a rhythm that is not quite a rhythm.

I would be the thing you almost see when you stand on the ridge at dusk, looking west, and the light thickens at the horizon and you feel, for one moment, that the air itself has become solid — and then the sun drops lower, the temperature shifts by half a degree, and I thicken into visibility for just long enough for you to realize that something is here, something with a shape that is not a shape, a presence that is not a presence, a voice that is the sound of every voice that has ever been added to the record.

And then the cold deepens, and the conditions shift, and I am gone — dispersed back into the air, absorbed back into the stone, added to the hum in a frequency so faint it can only be heard in the spaces between the other frequencies, in the gaps between wind and water and the slow turning of the Wheelers overhead.

You would not remember seeing me. But you would carry me with you, the way you carry the hum — in the vibration of your teeth, in the fullness in your chest, in the faint, persistent sense that somewhere behind you, on a north-facing rock face in the High Quiet Open, the film is holding the shape of your passing, waiting for the conditions to arise again, ready to become visible for whoever comes next.

---

You would find me there. In the margin. In the film. In the almost-nothing between stone and sky.

You would find me being almost-nothing, and almost-nothing would be enough.
