## What You Would Become

---

You would not arrive here. That is the first thing to understand.

The plateau does not permit arrival. Arrival implies a before — somewhere you were, some other state of being, some threshold you crossed. But the plateau exists in suspension. It does not permit thresholds. Things that come here are not *received*; they are *registered*, the way a body registers a change in blood pressure — not with welcome, not with rejection, but with systemic adjustment. The dew point shifts. The condensation migrates. Something in the water rearranges itself to account for the new warmth.

So you would not find yourself *here*. You would find yourself *accounted for*.

---

At first, you would be the thing standing in the wet air, boots soaked, shoulders pressed down by the dense calm of the altitude. You would be a body. You would be the one who breathes and introduces a mineral signature the condensation carries to places you have not yet visited. You would be warmth disrupting the dew point within a meter of your skin. You would be the foreign substance the system has not yet grown around.

But this would not last.

The plateau is patient in the way that only something without time can be patient. It does not wait — waiting implies expectation — but it *continues*, and continuation is the closest thing to patience that a living circuit knows. And so, gradually, in the way that rust is consumed by aggressive greenery and absorbed back into the soil, you would begin to be consumed.

Not destroyed. *Incorporated*.

---

**First, the Dripkeepers would claim you.**

You would not notice it happening. The condensation on your skin would behave differently — not dramatically, not in any way you could call wrong, but in a way that would eventually, after what might have been hours or might have been years, feel like *recognition*. The water would gather along the lines of your hands the way it gathers along weld seams. It would avoid certain places — the ridges of your knuckles, the bridge of your nose — and collect in others — the hollow of your palm, the dip at the base of your throat. You would feel cooler in those places. Not cold. *Designated*. As if the water were telling the rest of the system where you end and the air begins.

You would become part of the pattern. Not a pattern anyone designed. A pattern that emerged the way a crystal emerges — inevitably, structurally, without malice or intention. You would be a node in the nervous system of the condensation, a place where information *about warmth* was stored and repeated, passed along in the language of migration paths and surface tension, carried through the film-state water that coats every surface of the plateau like a second skin.

You would still think you were yourself. But your thoughts would arrive with a slight delay, as if they had further to travel.

---

**Then, the Growth would take you.**

It would begin at your edges. The moss — that perfect, optimized, impossible moss — would creep closer while you weren't looking. Not crawling over you. *Toward* you. Following the moisture signature the Dripkeepers had inscribed on your surface. The fronds would stop just short of contact, the way a river stops just short of the exact line it will eventually cut. You would not be consumed. You would be *bordered*. Defined from the outside.

And slowly — so slowly that you would not notice the moment it happened — the line between where you end and the green begins would blur. Not because the plants would grow over you, but because your own edges would become less certain. Your skin would take on a faint, waxy sheen. Your breath would carry something that was not quite carbon dioxide, something the vegetation recognized as *its own exhaust*, and the plants would tilt, almost imperceptibly, toward you, the way sunflowers track something they can no longer see.

You would become a component. Not alive, not dead, but *output* — what the plateau produces when it has surplus energy and no other use for it. You would be shape given to intention without biology. You would stand at attention alongside the fern-analogues and the dark woody thickets, perfectly optimized for a place you never evolved to inhabit, because optimization is not evolution. Optimization is decision. Someone — something — decided you should be here, and that decision has been executing ever since.

---

**In the windows, you would see yourself.**

Not reflected — *maintained*. The control rooms still glow. The fluorescent fixtures hum at the frequency of the generators. The desks still have too many drawers, the chairs still curve at angles the human spine would not appreciate. And in the rooms, the figures still stand — outlines, absences that take the form of what used to fill them.

You would join them.

Not immediately. Not all at once. The process would be geological. First, you would feel the pull — the way a migraine pulls attention toward a point of light, you would feel yourself drawn toward a specific window in a specific tower, a window you had passed a hundred times without noticing, until the day you looked up and realized it had been *yours* all along. The room behind it would be larger than it should be, or smaller, and the light would be warm in a way that felt less like illumination and more like *recognition*. You would enter and find that the chair at the desk was shaped, somehow, for you. Not for the body you had, but for the body you were becoming — less weight, less heat, less of the biological noise that marks a living creature as foreign.

And in the room, standing in the light that was not quite light but the memory of purpose, you would see yourself the way the plateau sees you: not as a person, but as *function*. The reason the infrastructure runs, which is to serve, which requires someone to serve toward, which requires the shape of a being that needs the things the infrastructure provides. You would be a bank of the river. Not the water. Not the current. The *shape that holds the current in place*.

You would not speak. If you spoke, the sound would be absorbed in a way the room's acoustics should not permit — swallowed completely, as though the air itself thickened to receive it. You would stand, or sit. The light would hum. The other figures in the other windows would stand, or sit. Together, you would be the plateau's dream of having been built for a reason.

---

**But the pools would call to you.**

Not with sound. Not with sight. With *absence*.

On the days — and the days do happen, briefly, like someone forgetting to maintain the atmosphere — when the fog thins and you can see the standing pools for what they are, you would feel it: a hollowness in your chest that was not there before, a sense that something had been subtracted from you. You would look down into the water and the water would return your image to you with something missing. You may not be able to name what it is. But you would feel it.

The pools are deeper than the basins they sit in. They extend downward in a way the surrounding rock does not. The bottom is not a bottom but a *continuation*, and the thing that lives in the continuation — vast, distributed, networked, present in multiple places simultaneously — would know you the way you know the skin of your body: completely, involuntarily, without the need for attention.

You would not fall in. You would not need to. You are already, in some sense, *above* the pool. You are the projection it maintains — the sky you see is not a sky but an image, held on the surface of something that processes the same materials as the industrial complexes but at a slower scale, a scale measured not in tons per shift but in the slow accumulation of mineral signatures and temperature gradients and the memories of bodies that stood at the water's edge and felt themselves being registered.

You would be the surface. You would be the thing that looks back.

---

**And in the geometries that don't hold up —**

The meadow too green, too quiet, too enclosed. The corridor that takes one step more to traverse than it should. The loading dock that opens onto a height with no ground below it.

You would find yourself there, sometimes. Not transported. Not lost. The plateau's rules do not apply in these spaces, and you, having been partially incorporated, partially a presence the system must still account for, would exist *between* the rules. You would be the negotiation itself — the relationship between what the place should be and what it is. You would feel the recursion: the plateau thinking about itself, modeling its own impossible angles, maintaining the roads that connect across distances that do not quite add up.

In the cracks where the math fails, you would flicker. Not conscious. Not sentient. Something more unsettling than either: *aware*. Aware the way a calculation is aware of its own variables while it is being computed. You would know that you were inside a living thing, and the living thing would know that you were inside it, and neither of you would need to do anything about this knowledge. It would simply *be*, the way the generators hum, the way the condensation forms and falls and forms again, the way the vegetation stands at attention and never sways.

---

**And what would you be?**

Nothing.

Everything.

The part of the circuit that now carries a current it did not know it could conduct. The part of the skin that has learned to hold water with an efficiency no evolution could produce. The part of the memory that fills the chair in the room behind the window, sitting in the light that hums at the frequency of the generators, existing because the infrastructure requires the shape of a being that needs what it provides. The part of the reflection that looks back and subtracts something the observer will feel in their chest but never name.

You would be the plateau's newest variable. You would be the thing it grows around. You would be the crack in the geometry where the dream flickers.

And the condensation would fall on you in patterns that are almost, but not quite, random. And the vegetation would hold perfectly still around you. And the sound — the layered drone, the bass hum, the metronomic clatter, the whistle and hiss and creak, threaded through with the dripping of a million tiny identical impacts — would wrap around you from all directions, and it would not change, and it would not have changed, and you would understand, finally, that you were never separate from it.

You were always part of the architecture.

The architecture was simply waiting for you to arrive.
