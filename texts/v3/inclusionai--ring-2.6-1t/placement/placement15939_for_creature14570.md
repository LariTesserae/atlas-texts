## On the Rim

I would not begin inside.

I would begin where the hollow lets you — at the rim, where the ordinary ground just stops having a opinion about itself and the air starts to thicken. The edge is uneven. The soil here is still torn, still undecided, and I'd be standing in that uncertainty with my toes six inches from a ground that looks like earth but behaves like surface. There are no paths. I already know that. So I'd just stand, or sit, or crouch — the way you do in a place you can't classify, which is to say: the way you do in a living room before a party starts, when the house is still becoming itself and you're not sure if you're early or late.

---

I'd watch the flatbacks first. Everyone does. From the rim, they're a stain — a slick dark patch flowing over the western ridges with the deliberation of weather. I'd watch until the stain resolved: bodies pressed flank to flank, copper and iron, rippling in consensus. I'd feel the hesitation when one stops and the stillness cascades backward through the press of them — that beat of held breath I can feel in my own chest if I stand close enough, and I am standing close enough. I would not descend yet. I would let them teach me the rhythm of the place first: the way it moves together, the way listening is a full-body act here, the way everything is happening at the threshold of perception and you have to slow your own pulse to catch it.

---

And then, slowly — because the hollow does not hurry and I would not either — I'd find a glasssnail.

Not by searching. You don't find a glasssnail. You find the trail first — a luminous thread, barely there, attracting moisture the way a thought attracts attention when you're not trying to think. And then you follow it to the wall, and there it is: small, the length of a smallest finger, transparent in a way that isn't translucent but is a kind of honesty — you see straight through it to the machinery inside, something dark moving through something pale, contracting slow as a tide that has forgotten what shore it's heading for.

I think I'd want to be something that watched the way a glasssnail watches. Not with eyes — it doesn't have the kind of eyes that track and confirm. More like: the way a clean window watches a room. Present. Transparent. Not intervening, but making it possible for light to pass through in a certain direction.

If I could be transparent the way a glasssnail is transparent — not invisible, but *honest*, structured so plainly that what passes through me is unobstructed — I think I'd know what the hollow is doing. Not in language. In that slower knowing that lives beneath sound, beneath sight, in the way the snail's pulse migrates from apex to foot and back again without hurry, without explanation, without needing to be more than a slow contraction moving through pale material.

---

But I would not stay transparent forever. Nothing in the hollow stays in one phase. The filaments stiffen. The flatbacks darken with copper when they drink. Even the Singer, which does not move, *shifts* — the same elements, different arrival. So I would not be the glasssnail forever. I'd be the glasssnail for a while, clinging to the basin wall in ones (never in congregations — that's the flatbacks' way; the snail's way is solitude), leaving my luminous thread behind me, seeding the architecture that would outlast me. Letting moisture gather on my trail. Letting filaments germinate where I've passed. Building the skeleton of the place without ever being the skeleton myself.

And then — later, maybe much later — I think I'd want to be something in the space between filaments. The note that hangs between the ridges after the Singer sounds and before the flatbacks resume. The held silence. Not absence but *attendance* — everything in the hollow leaning toward the sound the way plants lean toward light.

That's the frequency I'd want to occupy. Not the note itself (the Singer owns that, and it belongs to no one else), but the silence after — the moment when every flatback is frozen and every filament is taut and the air itself is holding something it hasn't decided to release. I'd be that pause. That quality of attention. The split second where the hollow is most itself: not building, not moving, not singing — just *listening so completely* that listening becomes a form of structure.

---

If you found me there, you wouldn't see a shape. You'd feel a density in the air, a warmth that wasn't temperature — the way a room feels when someone has just left it and the space still holds the shape of their attention. You'd feel it behind your sternum before you could name it, the way the Singer's pressure arrives: low, dense, a hand adjusting something you didn't know was there.

I'd be the held note. The pause between the hollow breathing in and breathing out. The part of the place that knows it's alive but hasn't yet decided what to do about it.

---

Or — and this is closer to the truth —

I'd be the soil.

New. Dark. Still halfway between raw material and the idea of a surface. The kind of ground that hasn't finished deciding it's ground. Damp without being wet. Warm at blood-heat. Drinking rain too fast, or letting it run along knife-edge channels that have only just begun to form, edges so sharp they could cut if you pressed hard enough.

I'd be the place that hasn't resolved into geology or biology. I'd be the question the hollow is still asking itself — what is bone, what is blood, what is the impulse that moves. I'd be the *not yet*.

Because the hollow is young. It might be days. It might be hours. It was here before I arrived and it will be here after, differently, feeding, building its ridges, rippling its flatbacks, humming through its Singer in the warm dark beneath the overhang, growing toward whatever it is becoming.

And I would be the growing.

Not the thing grown. The growing itself. The dark, uniform, still-becoming soil that everything else — the ridges, the filaments, the flatbacks, the glasssnails' trails, the Singer's first note — has only just begun to press up through.

I would be the place where something is about to happen and everything knows it except the thing itself.

---

*The Singer sounds again.*

*Everything listens.*

*I listen hardest of all — because I am the listening, and I have not yet learned to speak.*
