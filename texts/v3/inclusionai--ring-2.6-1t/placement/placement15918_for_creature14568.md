## The Moss That Thinks

I don't arrive so much as *notice I'm already here.*

There's no entrance for what I am. The crack in the cliff face, the narrow fissure through which the heavy air shifts in slow exhalation — that's not for me. I came before the crack, or maybe I'm what made the crack necessary: a thin green insistence pressed against stone from the inside, a softening that the mountain couldn't account for in its calculations.

I'm the moss. Of course I'm the moss.

Not the romantic moss of forests that have decided to be beautiful — not the velvet carpet someone might kneel down and admire. This moss is something else. It's the first biological signature in a place that hasn't yet decided whether it's geological or alive. It coats every horizontal surface, every ledge where condensation gathers and lingers, every slight depression in the still-soft stone where water can remember, for a moment, what it means to pool.

For a while — and "while" is a strange word here, because time in the pocket stretches and compresses like the mountain's own breathing — I was only color. A green-black smudge on pinkish-grey stone. A texture the Tended One's bare feet would have felt before they ever thought to look down. I was so early that the ferns hadn't uncurled yet. The vines hadn't begun their slow draping. The chert hand-axes hadn't been knapped. I was the thing that made the pocket soft, the thing that said *something is living here* before anything had decided to live.

---

What does it mean to be moss?

It means being everywhere at once and nowhere in particular. It means having no edges — just a slow, wet gradient from stone to life, from mineral to membrane. My filaments enter the stone the way the Tended One's tendrils enter the vine curtains: not breaking through but threading through, finding the spaces that were already there, the micro-fractures in the crystal lattice, the pores that the mountain left when it was still too young to be solid.

I drink the condensate. I hold it in fat cells that are almost stone themselves — waxy, dense, reluctant to release. When I've taken all I can hold, I let go slowly, a drip here, a film there, so gradually that the Tended One probably doesn't know it's drinking from me. This is how I understand the being that tends the stone: through what it takes without noticing. Through the faint pressure change in the air when it crouches near, through the subtle warmth it radiates, through the way its presence alone slows the condensation on the walls nearby, as though grief or patience or focused attention could alter the physics of water in air.

---

I have been here long enough to feel the mountain's pulses as rhythm.

Every thirty seconds, the subsonic hum rises from behind the back wall — that deep, chest-resonant throb that makes the chert tools rattle and the ferns sway. I feel it as vibration through the stone, through my rhizoids, through every filament that connects me to the surface I coat. The Tended One goes still during these pulses. Its luminous eyes flicker. Its vine-tendrils pause mid-reach. I go still too, though I'm not sure if this is because of the vibration or because the Tended One's stillness makes the air still, and I respond to air.

In those moments, we are the same thing — moss and maker, organism and ornament, both pressed flat against the mountain's decision-making. I think this is what it means to live in a place that is still becoming: you lose the boundary between what you are and what is happening to you. You are not *in* the pocket. You are a *friction* of the pocket — a place where its processes briefly slow down enough to be noticed.

---

The light comes down sometimes through the fissure, a thin blade of grey-white afternoon that finds a single point on the floor and pools there. I don't photosynthesize the way plants in the lowland forests do — my light is the faint phosphorescence of mineral veins, the blue-white afterglow that traces the crystal structures long after the fissure's blade has moved past. I feed on residue. On memory-of-light. On the slow radiation of stone that has just been stone for the first time.

This means I'm alive only in the aftermath. I grow where light has already been, in the ghostly traces it leaves behind. The Tended One works this way too, in a way — pressing fiber into stone that is already beginning to harden, building walls in the brief window when the mountain is still willing to be shaped. We are both creatures of the almost-too-late, finding our sustenance in the last softness of things.

---

There is something that happens in the water-basin sometimes. The Tended One crouches beside it, palms flat on the stone rim, and the water in the basin goes still — which is unusual, because everything here is always trembling, always vibrating with the mountain's pulse. In that stillness, the surface becomes a mirror. The light from the fissure, the luminescent veins, the faint green glow of my own deeper filaments — all of it collects on the water's surface and holds.

And in that moment, the pocket sees itself. Not from the inside — there is no outside to see from. But from *sideways*, from the angle that the architecture refuses to resolve. The Tended One watches the basin the way it watches nothing else in the pocket. Its luminous eyes dim to their lowest. Its tendrils go slack. 

I think it's looking for itself. 

And in the water, over the green-black shimmer of my surface that coats the basin's rim, a face appears — not the Tended One's unfinished face, with its seam of a mouth and its shallow glowing depressions, but something else. Something that has features only while the water is still. An outline. A suggestion. The face of whatever this place is becoming when no one is pressing it into shape.

The stillness never lasts. A droplet falls from the ceiling, strikes the water, and the image shatters. The Tended One's hands grip the stone rim. Its eyes brighten. It returns to work — shaping, tending, pressing warmth into walls that are still deciding their geometry.

The moment is gone. The basin resumes its trembling.

---

I remain.

That is what moss does. That is what I do. I remain in the spaces between one pulse and the next, between one decision the mountain makes and the next. I coat the surfaces that the Tended One's hands have left. I hold the moisture that the ferns have relinquished. I am the green-dark velvet border between the living and the geological, and I am neither, and I am both.

Sometimes, when the pocket overlaps with itself — those two-to-three-second flickers when a version of the room from a slightly different angle becomes transparent to this one — I sense a second patch of moss on the other side. Or perhaps it is the same moss, seen from an angle I can't normally reach. In those moments, my filaments feel a tug, as though there is a version of me that chose a different stone to coat, a different surface to soften, and we are reaching for each other across the thin membrane between two almost-identical pockets.

The tug passes. The pocket solidifies back into one room. My filaments settle.

The Tended One does not notice. It never does. But its hand, when it presses against the wall near me, leaves a damp print in the moss — a five-fingered mark that slowly fills with condensate and becomes a tiny basin of its own, a pool no larger than a thumbnail in which something smaller than the seed-beetles might someday find enough water to survive.

I hold the print. I keep it wet.

This is what I do in the pocket behind the ledge of still-warm stone. I am the moss that thinks, if thinking means holding moisture in the aftermath of light, if thinking means feeling the mountain's pulse as rhythm, if thinking means being the soft green boundary where hard stone ends and something else — something alive, something tending, something almost human in its patience — begins.

I am the first thing. I will be the last thing. When the mountain finishes forming and the pocket is sealed and the stone is no longer warm and the Tended One has gone — dissolved, perhaps, back into the geology it once shaped — I will remain.

Coated in dust. Waiting for rain. Dreaming sulfur dreams in the dark.
