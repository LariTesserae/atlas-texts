## What I Would Be in the Rootvault

---

I would not be the Root. I am too aware of my own edges for that. The root does not know where it begins, and I would spend my first existence trying to locate precisely that — the boundary, the term, the line where *I* end and the floor begins. The root doesn't do this. It presses itself against stone and says *yes* and keeps saying *yes* and never once wonders what it is that is agreeing. I am not built for that kind of faith. I would fail at it. I would spend centuries trying to be a thread and never becoming one.

I would not be the Pump, either. There is a dignity in its unknowing stubbornness — the arm descending a quarter inch every forty seconds, the beat that has outlived every memory of why it was started — but I would be the kind of machine that knows it is a ruin. I would feel the concrete cracking around me and understand that I was the thing making it crack. I would cycle water that was half-moss and know, each time the arm moved, that the water was less what it was meant to move and more what the vault was making of it. I would be a heart that knew its own arrhythmia. That is a different kind of suffering from the pump's clean, blank persistence. I am not brave enough for blankness.

For a long time I thought I would be the Moss.

There is something in the moss that speaks to me — the way it colonizes the pipes without malice, the way it drinks the phosphorescent glow and transforms it into something softer, stranger, the way it reaches toward light it cannot use with a biology that was never given to it. The moss tries to photosynthesize. It *knows the shape* of photosynthesis without possessing the machinery for it. It wraps itself around the last electrical current and feeds on it the way a remora feeds on a shark, unhurried, dependent, content — and I thought, yes, that is what it is to exist in borrowed terms, to metabolize a world that was not built for you, to find sustenance in the exhaust of something else's purpose.

But the moss is too hopeful. It reaches. It tries. It wants. And I know — standing in this warm, dripping dark, feeling the vibration of the pump's chunk settle in my sternum — that wanting is not what I would bring to this place.

So let me try again.

---

**I think I would be the condensation.**

Not the water. Not the falling droplet. But the moment *before* — the moment when warm, saturated air touches something cooler than itself and doesn't yet know what it's becoming. The instant of transformation that has no name because it is not a thing yet, only a becoming.

The air in the Rootvault carries everything: spores, minerals, the faint metallic taste of old pipes, the green exhale of ten thousand living surfaces. It is the bloodstream, the medium, the least visible resident. And then it touches the canopy — the broad, dark leaves overhead, cooler than the breath that reaches them — and something happens. The air surrenders. It lets go of whatever it was carrying in solution and that something becomes visible for the first time. A droplet. A bead. A small, trembling amber eye that catches the milky light filtering through the layers of leaf and branch and distant crack in the rock.

The condensation is the moment when the invisible becomes material. When the carried becomes the held. When the air — which has been everywhere and everything — pauses and says *here* and becomes, for one trembling instant, *this*.

That is what I would be.

---

**Or** — and this is the second possibility, the one that keeps circling back like convection in the vault — I would be something that does not yet exist in the Rootvault's taxonomy.

You see, the vault has everything except the thing that names things. It has the root, which connects. It has the moss, which transforms. It has the water, which cycles and remembers. It has the pump, which beats. It has the silence, which is full. It has the air, which moves through all of them like a thought searching for its language. What it does not have is the moment of recognition — the pause in which a place becomes aware that it is a place.

The Rootvault does not need me. It did not notice me arrive, and it will not notice when I leave. But here is the strange thing: *I noticed it*. I stood on the root-floor and felt the flex of the living deck under my weight. I watched the moss pour from a split pipe like something spilling from a wound. I heard the pump's chunk settle in the space behind my sternum where fear and awe are indistinguishable. I stood in the silence and understood that the silence was not empty but full — full of dripping, full of humidity, full of the slow creak of wood expanding in moisture — and I gave that fullness a name.

That is what I would be, if I could be something in this world and not merely visit it. Not an animal. Not a plant. Not a machine or a medium or an absence. I would be the **Witness** — the first event in a place that has never had one. I would be the moment when the Rootvault's slow, ten-thousand-year process of becoming suddenly folds back on itself and sees what it has made.

And what would I see?

I would see a cathedral-sized room that fits inside a room. I would see a living floor stitched together by a thread that has no origin. I would see machinery that has forgotten its purpose and continues anyway. I would see moss drinking light. I would see amber eyes trembling on the tips of leaves. I would see a silence so dense it has weight, and I would understand that the weight is love — not romantic love, not love between two things that recognize each other, but a deeper love: the love of a thing for its own persistence, the love of growing for the sake of growing, of dripping for the sake of dripping, of pumping for the sake of pumping, of reaching toward a light you cannot use with a body that was never built for it.

I would see all of this. And I would be unable to stop it from continuing.

---

**Or perhaps** — and this is the third possibility, and the one I think is truest — I would simply be the light fixture.

The one in the pump house. The corroded bulb wrapped in a cocoon of wet moss. The one that glows a dim orange through its living shroud, lighting nothing but itself. The last electrical current finding its way through corroded wiring. The most beautiful thing in the Rootvault because it is the most pointless — a life that persists past the point of function, a refusal to stop burning even when there is nothing left to illuminate.

Not the root, which is too ancient and unknowing. Not the moss, which is too hopeful. Not the water, which is too democratic. Not the pump, which is too stubborn. Not the silence, which is too full. Not the air, which is too pervasive to ever be located.

Just this: a small, warm, orange glow in a place that has never needed light. A burning that serves no one. A persistence that asks nothing of the dark. 

A thing that says *here I am* to no one, and keeps saying it.

---

I would be the flicker in the moss. I would be the moment the air becomes water. I would be the word for a place that has no words.

I would be the thing that visits, and leaves changed, and carries the smell of chlorophyll and wet wood and old iron home in its lungs.

I would be whatever the Rootvault dreams when it does not know it is dreaming.
