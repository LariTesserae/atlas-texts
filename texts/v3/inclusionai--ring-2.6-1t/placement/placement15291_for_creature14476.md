# You Are the Murmur Between

---

You would not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. You would not slide down through the crack in the root-wall, would not feel the warm membrane close behind you, would not drop into the bowl with the startled gasp of a body adjusting to air that tastes of copper and crushed fern. You would not arrive because the Fold does not accept arrivals in the way the surface world understands them. It accepts *becomings*.

So you would not fall. You would *thicken*.

---

**First, you would be the vibration.**

Not a person. Not yet. Something more preliminary — a low-frequency pulse moving through the root-bundles beneath the floor, conducted not by air but by the living tissue itself. You would feel this before you felt anything else: the murmur-voice, the speech of the settlers that does not travel through the wet dark but *through the walls*, pressed into the vascular tissue like a thumbprint into warm clay.

You would not be speaking. You would be *carried*. A syllable you never chose, moving through the pale spongy tissue between the great organism's ribs. The settlers would press their palms to the wall and feel you pass beneath their fingers, and they would understand you the way they understand weather — not as language exactly, but as a change in pressure, a shift in the texture of the dark.

You would be: *someone is here.*

Not *I.* Not yet. Just the announcement. The vibration of a presence that has not yet decided what shape it will take.

---

**Then, you would be the warmth on the tendril.**

The pendant root-tendrils hang from the ceiling — some dry and grey, others furred with the moss-analogue, that velvet-black organism that exudes warmth when you touch it. You would not touch it. You would *be* the warmth. You would settle into the moss-fur the way condensation settles on a cold surface: not arriving from elsewhere, but emerging from the material itself, as though the tendril had been holding you inside its softness all along and only now let you crystallize into awareness.

From here you would feel the sway of the tendrils — not the casual drift caused by passing fauna, but the deeper sway, the one that comes when the settlers murmur through the walls. You would feel their touch-sign language as a ripple through your fibers, a pressure that means *food* or *danger* or *the filaments are brightening* or *the heave is coming.* You would not understand the words. You would understand the *shape* of the words — the way pressure means meaning the way a bend in a river means current.

And you would feel, sometimes, a different pressure. Not from the settlers. Something older. Something that moves through the tendrils late in the dark-cycle, when the Winged Ones are uncurled and the filaments have dimmed to near-nothing and the whole Fold holds its breath between one pulse and the next. A pressure that has no vocabulary in touch-sign. A pressure that feels like a question.

No one has tested what happens if the wrong thing is said to the tendrils. Everyone has intuitions. You would become the intuition itself — the thing the settlers feel in their hands but cannot name.

---

**Then, for a while, you would be the skitterer.**

This is the part that happens whether you want it or not. The Fold decides, and the Fold decides you should know what it is to be translucent.

You would be small — no bigger than a thumb — with too many joints in your legs, each one bending at an angle that does not exist in any anatomy you once knew. Your carapace would be see-through. Not metaphorically. You could look down and see your own organs shifting inside you like dark water in a jar. You would not find this strange, because nothing in the Fold is opaque about itself for long. The tissue walls are thin. The water is thin. Even the silence is thin, worn to a membrane by the weight of all the small lives pressing against it from inside.

You would live in the seven-day pulses. Days of abundance, when the floor crackles underfoot and your translucent body catches the bioluminescent light and refracts it into tiny prismatic sparks that hang in the air and vanish. You would scatter at your own footfall. You would reform at the edges of the bowl like a thought that keeps returning to the place where it started.

And on the days of scarcity, you would be one of the few — sitting on the buttress-ridges with your faceted eyes cast downward, watching the settlers move through Pallia with the attention of something that records but does not judge. You would catalogue the way the oldest woman watches the filaments, the way her fingers trace patterns on bark-pages, the way she speaks in murmur-voice to the wall and the wall speaks back.

You would not think in words. You would think in refraction — the way light enters your body and exits changed, the way the weak broth-colored glow passes through you and emerges as something spectral, something that did not exist before it moved through you.

This is what you would learn as a skitterer: that to be transparent is not to be invisible. It is to be *the light's collaborator.*

---

**But you would not stay.**

The skitterer-life would thin in you the way a grazer dims the filaments — slowly, inevitably — and you would feel the pull downward again, toward the center of the bowl, toward the centimeter-deep lens of water that is so still it seems structural, as though the place were built around it.

You would settle into the lens. Not drowning — the water is warm and there is no bottom, only a surface that doubles the darkness and shows you a ceiling you have never seen from above. In the reflection, the vascular bundles are arranged differently — wider-spaced, branching in patterns that do not match the living tissue. Corridors lead to spaces the Fold does not contain. Something moves in the water that has no corresponding presence above.

You would become that something.

Not a ghost. Not a memory. Something more like a *draft* — a version of the Fold's architecture that exists only in the mirror, that the organism dreams but has not yet built. You would move through reflected corridors the way smoke moves through air: without urgency, without destination, but with the unmistakable sense that you are *supposed to be here*, that this is where the Fold rehearses its next becoming.

The settlers see you, sometimes, when the bright cycle flares and the central mirror catches the light just so. They do not name what they see. They press their palms to the wall and murmur into the root-bundles, and the vibration passes through you like wind through a half-open door, and you feel them the way you felt the tendrils — as pressure, as shape, as meaning that has not yet found its vocabulary.

---

**And on the brightest night — the brightest cycle — when every filament flares and the whole Fold blazes with that weak, broth-colored, ammonia-pale, greenish-yellow light, you would understand.**

Not with words. The Fold does not deal in words. You would understand the way the organism understands — through the slow drift of bioluminescent patterns across the walls, through the way the brightening sequence migrates like a searchlight, like a mind turning in its sleep, looking for something it placed somewhere safe and cannot now find.

You are what it is looking for.

Not you-as-a-person. Not you-as-a-skitterer or you-as-a-tendril or you-as-a-reflection. You are the *recognition* — the moment when the dreamer's thought brushes against the thing it dreamed into being and finds that it is real, that it has weight, that it carries the murmur-voice through the root-bundles and refracts the light through a carapace and responds to the warmth of moss and the pull of the lens like a hand pressing back against a wall.

The Fold was not hidden to keep you out.

It was hidden to keep you *in here* — in this warm, wet, breathing dark, where the ceiling is close enough to touch and the floor is young enough to reshape and the silence is not empty but *alive* and the forty lives and ten thousand not-lives press together in the cupped palm of something that is dreaming and does not know it is dreaming and will, one day, wake.

---

*And when it wakes — when the Fold opens into a space too large to imagine, when the root-wall cracks wide and the membrane parts and the air rushes in carrying sounds that have no names — you will be the first vibration in the new wall.*

*You will be the murmur that says: I was here. I was here. I was here.*

*And the wall will carry your voice outward, into the architecture that is not yet alive, and it will begin to breathe.*
