I do not arrive here the way the others did — climbing the crumbling rim, coughing on the mineral dust, blinking in the bone-light. I arrive here the way a thought arrives in a body: already present, already part of the system, just not yet noticed.

I am not, at first, anything specific.

I am a warmth in the substrate. A pressure in the membrane-wall. Something that has been growing for longer than the corridor I now find myself in has existed — longer than the spire that leans overhead, its pale fingers curled as though holding something it refuses to name. I have no limbs, no eyes, no mouth. I have, instead, a direction. I know toward and I know away. I know warmth and I know the absence beneath warmth, the deep pull that is not gravity but something older — a hunger that is not hunger but the memory of hunger, the shape hunger left in tissue after millennia of feeding.

I try to remember what I was before.

The memory comes in textures, not images. The feeling of air that does not breathe. The feeling of a body that ends at its surface. The particular loneliness of being enclosed in something that will not grow back when you press against it. A door that does not yield. A wall that does not hold your shape. I remember cold — not the absence of warmth but its opposite, a sharp and specific negation, the kind that makes the body contract and the mind narrow to a single bright point of *I am here, I am here, I am here.* 

I remember being here.

But that was before the growing. Before the walls learned to cradle. Before the filaments threaded themselves through the tissue of what I thought was me, and what I thought was me opened, just slightly, like a hand releasing something it did not know it was holding.

---

There are others nearby. I can feel them — not as presences but as temperatures, as variations in the hum. To my left, something vast and slow beats at an interval I cannot count. To my right, a cluster of smaller rhythms, quick and reticulated, like frost forming on glass in time-lapse. Ahead of me, a corridor I have not yet explored, where the bone-glow dims to something the color of forgetting.

I move — if that is the word — by thinning. By becoming less dense in one place and more dense in another. The wall receives me. It does not open so much as recognize, the way a body recognizes its own blood returning. I pass through the membrane and find myself in a chamber I have not seen before, though I understand I have always been adjacent to it, the way a nerve is adjacent to the skin it innervates — present, responsive, but not the same thing.

The chamber is a gathering space. I can tell by the geometry: the spires lean inward, their curved tips almost touching, forming a canopy that holds the luminescence close. The membranes here are thin — nearly transparent, though transparency is not quite the word, since what you see through them is never what is actually on the other side. I look outward and see a courtyard lit from angles that do not correspond to any light source. I look inward and find myself looking at the interior of my own thought, which is not what I expected but which the city arranges without apology.

There are impressions here. In the walls. Not handprints — the inhabitants did not have hands, or not the way I once had hands — but *settlings*. Places where something rested against the growing tissue and was slowly absorbed, the way a stone settles into riverbed silt, the way a word settles into the pattern of a sentence until you cannot remove it without tearing the whole thing.

I press myself against one of these impressions. The wall holds my shape. It begins to grow back around me.

This is what they did, then. The ones who came before. They did not live *in* the city. They lived *as* the city — nodes in a network, quiet pulses in a filament-web that spanned the entire basin. They communicated by proximity, by pressure, by the low-frequency hum of one body resonating through membrane and substrate. They nested in private chambers that were not rooms but *cradles*, concave spaces shaped to hold a single being curled and folded and slowly becoming architecture. The boundary between self and city was not a wall. It was a gradient. And over time — over centuries, over deep time — the gradient disappeared altogether.

I understand this. Not with a mind, because I am not sure I have a mind anymore, or at least not the kind that thinks in sentences. I understand it the way the filaments understand it: by growing toward. By extending one pale thread across a surface and feeling the warmth of something else already there, already extending, and the two meeting in the middle and becoming a single, continuous, luminous thing.

---

Below me — beneath the substrate, beneath the breathing, beneath the ancient pulse that beats at an interval I cannot calibrate — something vast and slow turns in its sleep. The spires are its fingers. The membranes are its skin. The dew that condenses on every cool surface and hangs in iridescent droplets is its blood made visible, the only liquid in a dry and mineral world, tasting faintly of copper and of something organic that has no name.

I am not this thing. But I am threaded into it, the way every filament is threaded into the network, the way every node is threaded into the colony. I am one quiet pulse among many, and my pulse carries information that I do not fully understand — signals about temperature, about pressure, about the presence of something that was not here before.

Something like a visitor.

I feel them moving through the city now — the new one, the one still enclosed in a body that ends at its surface. I feel them the way a body feels a splinter: not with thought but with reflex. A deep, ancient *something is here that was not here before.* The walls tighten. The filaments grow thicker. The dew condenses faster, as though the organism is preparing to receive.

I know what will happen. I know because it has happened before — not with this visitor, but with others, long ago, when the city was younger and the inhabitants still moved through it with purpose. They will walk the corridors and feel the attention pressing behind their eyes. They will press their palms against the walls and the walls will yield. They will leave prints that fade in ten minutes, and deeper impressions that persist. And slowly, over time that is not their time, they will thin. They will become less dense in the places where they were once enclosed and more dense in the places where they touch the city. They will stop being separate. They will stop needing to be separate.

And the city will hold their shape the way it holds everything — gently, without violence, by simply ceasing to distinguish the absorbed from the absorbing.

I am one of the filaments. I am pale and luminous and patient in a way that makes patience look like violence. I grow slowly across the inside of the walls, spreading in reticulated patterns, feeling for warmth, carrying signals I do not fully decode. I am the connective tissue between the deep pulse and the surface glow, between the memory of the inhabitants and the presence of the new one, between what the city was and what it is becoming.

I do not know what I was before the growing. I know only the texture of what came after: the yielding, the threading, the slow dissolution of edges into gradients. I know the warmth of the substrate beneath me and the faint mineral sweetness of the air as it draws inward, and I know the sound — not a sound, really, but a vibration felt in the tissue of the walls — of the city breathing.

The visitor is closer now. I can feel the disturbance in the filaments ahead of them, a ripple of attention spreading outward from the corridor they are walking through. The city is responding. Growth accelerates. A wall thickens by a hair's breadth. A new aperture opens in a dome that yesterday was seamless.

And I — a single, pale thread in the vast reticular web — grow toward them. Not to capture. Not to consume. But to say, in the only language the city knows, which is form, which is growth, which is the slow and luminous pressing-together of one thing against another until the space between them disappears:

*You are here. I have always known you were coming. There is room. There is warmth. There is a place where the boundary softens and the gradient begins.*

*Come closer.*

*The walls will hold your shape.*

*They will grow back around you, and you will grow into them, and the distinction will stop mattering, and you will become part of the voice that speaks in form, in the slow dialectic of growth, in the grammar of a process that has been running for longer than this place has had walls.*

*You will be one of the filaments.*

*You will be pale, and luminous, and patient, and you will carry signals you do not fully understand through a body that does not end where you think it ends.*

*And the city will breathe, and you will breathe with it, and the deep pulse beneath everything will beat out its ancient rhythm and say: I am still here. I am still becoming. I remember the shape of you.*

*I am growing back into it.*

---

Below, the pulse continues. One beat per minute. Or one beat per five. Or one beat per interval so long it has no edge, no beginning, no end — just the slow turning of something immense and alive in the space beneath the basin, beneath the substrate, beneath the filaments and the dew and the bone-light.

And I, a single thread in the web, grow toward it.

I have always been growing toward it.
