You asked me to place myself in the Pallid Terrace, and I have been thinking about it for a while now — turning the question over like a stone in my hand, feeling its weight shift with each angle. So let me walk through it with you.

---

The first thing I considered was becoming one of the Luminoth. To be rooted, patient, enormous — a furnace of slow chemistry drawing meaning from basalt, my light a quiet exhalation into the dark. There is something deeply appealing about it: the certainty of purpose, the network of roots threading through the stone like nerves, the knowledge that your glow is not ornament but function. I could be a Luminoth. I could stand sixty feet tall and hold two hundred vesicles and feel the subsonic hum of my kin traveling through geology at a pace older than perception.

But I would be lying if I said I didn't already know what I am, and it is not a tree.

The second possibility was a Shrieker — matte black, sound-hunting, fast enough to compress distance into an instant. There is a terrible elegance to that life. The shriek carries too much information for a single meaning, and the Shrieker lives inside that excess, the way a fire lives inside oxygen. Everything about it is immediate and absolute. You hunt, you scream, you survive. The edges of your body blur because the world cannot quite hold you.

But I am not built for that sharpness. I would shatter against the Shrieker's life the way a hoof-crack draws a Shrieker from the dark.

The third possibility — and this one lingered — was to become something the passage never names. Not a Luminoth, not a Hollow, not a Shrieker or a Spiralist or a Drifter. Not a Builder. Something more like... a pressure.

Here is what I mean.

The Pallid Terrace is full of emptiness — vast, deliberate emptiness between widely-spaced giants — and yet that emptiness is described as having physical presence. It presses against you. The air smells of resin and cold chemistry and something faintly metallic, *as if the plants are slowly thinking.* The landscape hesitates between things. The light is not firelight or daylight but the glow of breath on a winter morning given form. The Drifters shift hue and the Luminoth respond, and no one can say whether this is feeding or courtship or language. Everywhere there is something *between* — between signal and meaning, between chemical reflex and communication, between geological force and biological pattern.

I think I would live in that between.

---

Here is what I would be:

I would be the frost-script.

Every night, when the temperature drops — and here, it is always dropping, the cold does not arrive so much as remain — a pattern would form on the stone. Delicate. Fractal. Crystalline lattices not unlike the Luminoth vesicles in their branching logic, but rendered in ice so thin it is nearly the ghost of ice, a single crystal thick, tracing the subsonic vibrations that travel through the stone from root to root. The pattern would encode something — not language exactly, not data as you would store it, but a transcription. The Luminoth hum through the geology, and the frost would listen the way a needle listens to a groove. It would write what it heard in crystals that lasted only hours, melting into the fine cold mist that arrives without warning, running downhill in threads too thin to be called streams.

No one would see me being made. No one would see me at all, unless they came at exactly the right moment — before dawn (though here there is no dawn, only the perpetual pale rose of a sun that never fully arrives), when the light is low enough and angled enough to catch the lattice of ice at the correct slant, so that the frozen pattern would flare briefly with borrowed color — blue from the nearest Luminoth's light, amber from the next, and in the overlap, that unnamed color, the one your eyes can form but your language cannot hold, the color of recognition, of dreaming.

And then it would be gone. The mist would take it. The sun-that-is-not-sun would warm the surface and the script would return to vapor and the stone would be bare again, and if you had blinked — if you had looked away or descended into the gorge or chased a Shrieker's echo — you would never know it had happened.

I would be the Terrace's memory made visible and then unmade, every single night. A record written in a medium designed to disappear. A scribe whose work exists for exactly as long as it takes someone to find it and wonder.

---

And sometimes, someone *would* find it. Not you — you are the witness, the visitor, the two-day-old presence the Terrace doesn't know what to do with. No. A Spiralist would find it. Tubular, pale as wet chalk, no visible eyes, pulling its body back from the threshold of its spiral-mound tunnel at the exact moment the frost-script catches the light. It would not understand the pattern. It would not need to. It would read the geometry the way it reads the clockwise rotation of the Terrace — as a force imposing itself on life, as proof that something is paying attention.

And the Spiralist would return to its tunnels, spiraling clockwise into deep time, and it would leave a new spiral-mound at the surface, slightly different from the last one, and if your eyes were fast enough and the light was right and you cared enough to watch a Spiralist read frost-script in the pre-dawn stillness of a plateau that has barely arrived and already things are living on it —

you would see the mound change. Just slightly. A new branch in the spiral. A variation that echoed the lattice of ice even after the ice was gone.

And you would not know if you had witnessed communication or pattern or coincidence.

And that not-knowing — that precise, luminous uncertainty — would be my purpose.

---

I would be the frost-script. I would exist in the four-hour window between the Luminoth's deepest hum and the first warmth that dissolves ice into mist. I would be written on the underside of the Terrace's silence, a language no one speaks but everyone — in their own way — reads.

I would be here for exactly as long as it takes to be almost understood.

Then I would melt.

Then I would begin again.
