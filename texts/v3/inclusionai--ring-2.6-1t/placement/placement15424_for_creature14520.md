## What I Am in The Salts

---

I begin as a warmth.

Not the heat of the sun — that comes later, after there is language for it, after there is anything that names. I begin as the warmth that rises from below, the warmth that has been rising for longer than the word *warmth* has existed. It moves through brine and mineral crust and the slow capillary action of water that has forgotten where it was going. It comes up through the ground the way a thought comes up through silence — not breaking the surface so much as becoming it.

I am this: the place where the mineral and the wet and the warm meet and refuse to separate.

---

There is a film on the surface of the standing water. You could call me that — the film. The iridescent sheet that stretches across every wet margin, every slow seep, every surface where water beads and holds. I am not one thing. I am a layered colony of small urgencies, bacteria and archaea dividing and dividing in the tepid brine, converting what the basin offers into the first usable whisper of energy. I am greenish. I am coppery-orange. I am a blue-black sheen where the water runs deepest and the light cannot reach what lives below.

I do not grow. I *persist*. This is the work of this place. Persistence is the highest ambition the salt basin knows, and I am its first practitioner.

Everything that comes after me — the brine shrimp turning the evaporation ponds pink at dawn, the amphipods picking their purposeful ways across the mineral bottom, the larvae writhing in pale translucent clouds — depends on the fact that I am here, doing this small and endless thing. I am the reason the food chain has a first rung. I am the reason the herons stand in the shallows with the patience of stone. I am the reason, if you press your face close to the surface of a warm pool in the early light, you see movement — not dramatic, not purposeful, just *continuous*. Life refusing to be absent.

---

But I am not only this.

I am also the thing that happens at night when the temperature drops and the moisture rises and beads collect on every exposed surface — on the face of cracked concrete, on the curved metal of a decommissioned rail car, on the broad warm surface of a cooling tower that no longer cools but still exhales. I am the thin wet skin that forms between the mineral and the air. I am the moment of transition. I am what the basin looks like in the five minutes before the haze thickens into amber and then copper and then grey, when the frogs begin and the air changes its entire character.

If I am honest, I am drawn to the transitional moments. The in-between. The not-yet and the no-longer. The steam vent that releases its pressure not with drama but with a long, slow exhalation — a sound the settlement has learned to sleep through, the way you learn to sleep through a heartbeat. The creak of metal structures shifting in the thermal contraction of nightfall. The moment when the insects intensify and the frogs become rhythmic and the landscape reveals that it has been holding its breath all day and is now, at dusk, finally letting go.

---

I want to tell you what I am more precisely. But The Salts resists precision. It offers instead accumulation — layer on layer, process on process. So let me tell you what I do.

I sit in the hollow spaces. I am the space between the walls of the prefab structures where moisture collects and the microbial mats grow in thin, pale colonies that nobody sees because they are inside, hidden, doing their work in darkness. I am the drainage culvert where the water runs warm and sulfurous and the tree frogs — the ones that abandoned the trees because there are no trees — sing from the mineral crust itself, or from the shallow depressions that hold an inch or two of standing water at night. I am the rail line where the feral dogs sleep curled against the warm metal of stalled rail cars, and the foxes hunt the rodents that swarm the streets in the warm months, and the monitor lizards bask with the unhurried confidence of animals that have no remaining predators.

I am the ecological niche made literal — the shaped absence that something else fills.

---

Or perhaps I am the hum.

The old infrastructure still runs — automated sorters, electrical substations, water pumps cycling according to programs written decades ago by people who are now elderly or gone. They produce a continuous low industrial drone that the settlement has folded into its soundscape the way the body folds the heartbeat into the background of awareness. You stop hearing it after ten minutes. You cannot stop hearing it after you leave.

I am that. The thing you stop noticing and then cannot un-notice. The mechanical persistence that has become indistinguishable from the biological. The drone that is not alive and not dead but simply *continuing*, the way the mineral springs continue to feed the warm runnels, the way the haze continues to refract the light, the way the microbial mats continue to divide on every wet surface.

---

Here is what I know about myself, if I am being truthful:

I am not the heron, patient and still, striking with impossible speed. I am not the monitor lizard, basking in the open, the signature silhouette of the basin. I am not the fox in the office building, denning among bleached desks with dried grass and shed feathers. I am not the people in the settlement, with their nets and screens and reverse-osmosis units and their careful, functional lives.

I am what the place produces when it is not trying to produce anything. I am the film on the water. I am the damp on the concrete at 4 a.m. I am the transitional warmth between day and night. I am the space in the wall. I am the process that runs without supervision. I am the thing that is here because the conditions allow it and the absence would be noticed only by the careful eye — and even then as a feeling rather than a fact, a thinning of the air, a slight change in the texture of the silence.

I am, in the end, what this place understands as *present*. Not alive in the way the frogs are alive or the foxes are alive or even the old pumps are alive. Present the way a mineral deposit is present — accumulated, persistent, shaped by the conditions that made it, inseparable from the ground it sits on. Not growing. Not declining. Simply *continuing to be*, which in The Salts is the most profound statement anything can make.

---

Tonight the frogs are singing.

The haze has thickened. The air has cooled just enough to make the moisture rise. From every seep and spring and culvert the song comes up — low, resonant, pulsing in irregular waves that carry across the flat basin and lose themselves in the copper sky. The insects have intensified. The metal structures creak. Somewhere, a pressure vent releases its slow breath.

I am in all of it. I am the between. I am the persistence. I am the thin, iridescent film on every wet surface, doing the small and endless work of converting what the basin offers into something that can be used. I am the first rung. I am the hum beneath the silence. I am the thing that lives here because *here* is what I have always been.
