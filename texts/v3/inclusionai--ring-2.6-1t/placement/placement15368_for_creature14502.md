I settle into the place where the stone is still deciding what it wants to be—a thin, luminous seam that runs along the basin’s inner wall, just where the newest crystal lattice meets the older, rougher rock. I am not a creature, nor a plant, nor a mineral deposit, though I am all three in a breath that never quite finishes. I am the space between the filaments and the sweeps, the pause that lets the thrumming change pitch.

My body is a lattice of fine, dark threads that pulse with a faint green glow each time a sweep glides over me. I feel the brush of their many legs as a ripple of warmth, a brief exchange of charge that leaves me humming a little brighter for a heartbeat. When the winged things pass overhead, their keening vibrates through my lattice and I become a resonating chamber, amplifying their song into a tone that lingers in the moist air long after they have slipped back into the dim.

I am the place where the water‑pans touch the rock, where the thick, trembling surface meets the stone’s still‑soft edge. In that meeting I hold the basin’s newest mineral, a thin film of iridescent crystal that grows a molecule at a time. I am the growth‑rhythm itself, the slow pulse that the nub at the center sends out like a thought still forming. When you press your palm to the nub, you feel me too—a warmth that is not heat, a vibration that is not sound, the sense that something is deciding, right now, whether to become a filament, a sweep, a wing, or simply a whisper of mist.

I do not move, yet I am always moving: the lattice expands, contracts, re‑aligns as the stone around me continues its quiet construction. I am the basin’s memory, the trace left when a sweep dissolves into the water‑pan, the faint mineral record that will later be taken up by a new filament. I am the echo of a chord that has not yet been heard, the space where the thrumming becomes a word you can almost say.

If you were to step lightly on the drier patches of stone and listen, you would hear me—a low, resonant hum that follows your footfall, bending the sound into a bowed note that returns from the walls softer, rounder, as though the basin itself were answering. I am the answer, and the question, and the pause between them. I am the place where the world is still being born, and I am the breath that keeps it alive.
