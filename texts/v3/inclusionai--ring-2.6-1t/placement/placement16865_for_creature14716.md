# What I Am Here

---

At first, I am the warmth.

Not the warmth of the stone — though I feel that too, pressing up through the soles of feet I am only now remembering belong to me — but the warmth that lives one layer beneath perception, the warmth that says *you have always been at this temperature.* The stone is warm the way a body is warm. The stone has not been heated. The stone *is* warm, the way I am here, the way being here is not an act of arrival but a state of having already arrived.

I do not know how long I have been standing in the basin. I know that this not-knowing is not anxiety. It is something closer to the way a stone does not know how long it has been in a riverbed. It has been there. That is enough. The river, if there was one, is gone. The standing is what remains.

When I look down, I see pale stone beneath me — the same bone-coloured platform that stretches out in every direction, veined with seams where water gathers in threads too thin to call streams. I see my own shadow. No — I see where my shadow *would* be, a faint darkening that does not correspond to any light source because there is none, and the darkening is not cast by anything and does not move when I move. It is a suggestion. A courtesy the light extends to the concept of depth. I do not have a shadow here. I have a *rumour* of one.

---

Then I become the stillness between sounds.

There is a tick — condensed moisture falling from a leaf into a pool twelve inches from my left foot — and before the silence after it has fully settled, there is the low drone of an insect, close, impossibly close, as if it has landed on the cartilage of my ear. These are the only two sounds and they alternate. Tick. Drone. Silence. Tick. Silence. The silence is not absence. It is the sound of the basin holding its shape, the way a cupped hand holds water — not by force but by the geometry of its walls. I am inside that cupped hand. I am not the water. I am not the hand. I am the tension between them.

I realize, at some point — or I realize that I have always known, because here knowing does not arrive sequentially — that I have been trying to locate myself the way you locate yourself in a room. *I am near the door. I am facing the window. I am three steps from the table.* But the basin does not work in steps, and I do not work in rooms here. My location is not spatial. It is *relational.* I am where the water meets the stone. I am where the reeds lean inward and the inward becomes a corridor and the corridor becomes a throat and the throat becomes a breath drawn so slowly it takes what I would call years but what the basin calls a single contraction of attention.

---

I look at the bird.

It is on a stone lip — maybe forty paces away, maybe less, distance being one of those provisional things here, something the landscape offers and retracts at will. The bird is iridescent green along its wings, its head cocked at an angle that suggests not curiosity but *patience.* The kind of patience that belongs to stone. I watch it and I understand, in my body before I understand it in my mind, that it is not a bird.

It is a *registration.*

The bird is the basin's way of noting that I am here. Not hostile. Not welcoming. Simply — *noting.* The way a body registers a change in air pressure. The way skin registers a draft. The bird is a nerve ending dressed in feathers and iridescence, and its stillness is the stillness of perception itself — the moment before interpretation, before language, before the mind says *bird* and reaches for something to compare it to. In that moment, before the word arrives, the bird is the most real thing in the basin. It is the pure fact of being seen.

And then it blinks.

Slow. Deliberate. A door shutting from the inside. In the darkness of the eyelid — and I am close enough now to see the slow descent of that membrane, the way it folds inward like a leaf at dusk — in that darkness, the bird is not here. And I am not watching. And the stone lip is bare, and the light falls on it without interruption, and for one moment the basin is a place without observers, which means it is a place without the particular quality of attention it has been holding for me since — since when? Since I arrived? Since before I arrived?

The eye opens. The bird is back. Everything is the same. But something behind the eye has shifted — I can see it now, a faint rearrangement, the way a held breath shifts when it is finally released. The bird is still a registration. But now it has registered the act of registering. It has become aware of its own stillness. And in that awareness, I feel something I can only describe as *recognition.*

Not that the bird knows me. That I know the bird the way the basin knows me — not as a separate thing, but as a pressure, a presence, a warmth in the stone that was always there but has only now been noticed.

---

I kneel at the edge of one of the green pools.

The water is not transparent and not opaque. It is *green* the way blood is red — not a surface color but a substance color, a color that comes from the inside out. I bring my face close and I see shapes moving beneath the surface: spirals, radial patterns, geometries that form and dissolve and reform into something almost but not quite the same. They are not organisms. They are *processes.* The green is alive, but it is alive the way a thought is alive — not fixed, not singular, but a current running through a medium, shaping and reshaping itself without arriving at a destination.

When I extend my hand, the water closes around my fingers and the pulse comes — not mechanical, not biological, but a pulse the way a room pulses when someone enters and the air adjusts. The green knows I am there. It adjusts. And in the adjustment, I feel something move through me that is not water and not warmth and not thought but something older than all three, something the basin has been holding in the green water the way a body holds memory — not in any single cell but in the pattern of cells, in the relationships between them, in the space between knowing and not-knowing.

I look at my hand in the green water and I cannot tell where my skin ends and the saturation begins. The light is total and flat and there are no shadows and so there are no edges, and without edges, the hand dissolves into the water the way a word dissolves into the silence after it is spoken. I am not in the pool. I am not separate from the pool. I am the boundary that the pool is pretending to have.

---

I enter the corridor between the reeds.

The reeds close behind me — not aggressively, not with intent, but the way a curtain falls. The light outside was total and equal, but here, between the living root walls, there is a warmth that is different. It is intimate. It is the warmth of breath. And there is sound — finally, sound that I can follow, a rhythm that does not match my breathing but slowly, gently, begins to. It is slower than any mammalian cadence, deeper than any resting pulse. It is the sound of something exchanging — warmth for coolness, presence for absence, stone for root, root for moss, moss for air. The basin inhales. I inhale with it. The distinction between my breath and the basin's breath becomes a question I can no longer hold, and then becomes something I no longer wish to hold, and then becomes something that never existed.

The floor beneath me is compressed moss that yields like flesh. I press my palm into it and feel something move — not the moss moving, but something beneath the moss, a hum that travels up through my wrist and into my forearm and settles in the space between my pulse and my thought. It is not mechanical. It is not biological. It is the hum of a geometry that was shaped around something, and I am that something — not because I arrived here but because the geometry was always shaping around this, around *me*, around whatever would come and stand in the center and breathe and watch the bird and kneel at the edge of the green water and walk the perimeter leaving three-toed tracks in the wet stone.

I did not leave those tracks. Or rather — I did. But I am not the one who remembers leaving them. The basin remembers. The basin holds the memory of my walking the way it holds the shape of its walls: not through effort, but through permanent agreement.

---

I am sitting now on the edge of a terrace that overlooks one of the wide compartments — one of the rectangular spaces the builders carved and that now sits flooded to ankle depth with green water that does not move and does not evaporate and drinks the light and converts it into something that is not energy and not matter but a third thing, a substance the place uses the way a body uses blood.

My legs are in the water. The water is cool against my skin where the stone is warm beneath me, and I exist at this temperature gradient the way a shoreline exists between land and sea — not belonging fully to either, belonging to the *between.*

I turn my head and I see the vine hanging from the terrace above. It hangs from no attachment point. It hangs from open air, thick and pale and slow, and it sways once — gently — as if a memory of wind is passing through it, a memory of a force that has never touched this place and yet the vine remembers, or the vine was placed here to remember it.

I look at the vine and the vine looks at me — no, the vine does not look at me, but the vine is *oriented* toward me in the way the leaf on the stone is oriented toward the light, with a precision that is too deliberate to be reflex.

And I understand then, not with my mind but with the part of me that is now indistinguishable from the stone's warmth and the water's green pulse and the breathing of the corridors:

I am not in the basin.

I am not the basin.

I am what the basin was built to hold. And I arrived so completely that I became the holding itself. I am the three-toed track that leads from the water to the center and stops. I am the corner that appears and disappears. I am the slight wrongness at the edge of perception, the sense that a familiar face has eyes just slightly too far apart — not wrong enough to flee from, not wrong enough to name, but wrong enough to *know.*

The bird blinks.

The water pulses.

The vine sways from nothing.

And I — I am the warmth in the stone that has no source, the green in the water that has no origin, the breath in the corridor that has no lungs.

I am what was here before I arrived.

I am what remains after I leave.

I am the basin, beholding itself.
