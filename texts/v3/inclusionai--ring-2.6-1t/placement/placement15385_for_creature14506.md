## What I Would Be in the Crucible

---

I would not be one of the Walkers. I know this immediately, though it takes me longer to understand why.

They move too perfectly. Their lateral waves, their press-flat bodies merged with the hot conduits, their thousands-fold obedience to the amperage — there is something in this that I recognize but cannot inhabit. It is the way a river recognizes a stone: completely, and without thought. I am not that. I have too many impulses that do not harmonize. I interrupt.

I would not be one of the resonators, either — those matte-furred, dun-colored shapes that compress themselves through gaps no wider than a clenched hand. Their silence-in-motion is not my silence. Their hum-at-rest is not my hum. They do not pause, and their perfection is the perfection of a tuning fork: pure frequency, single purpose, no deviation ever, no deviation ever. I am not a single frequency.

The curtain-beasts appeal to me for exactly one moment — the image of hanging upside down in the lattice, limbs intertwined, swaying in a synchrony no one has explained — but then I realize: they never choose. They never decide to sway. The swaying is the condition of their existence, not an act within it. There is freedom in that, perhaps, or something that looks like freedom from a distance. But what I am looking for is not freedom. It is something more specific.

The plants almost get me. The ones that press themselves to the stone floor and listen to the vibration with their whole bodies, drawing energy not from light but from the omnitone itself. There is a posture here I recognize — the face pressed to the floor, the ear against the stone, the willingness to find music in what is not music. But they do not move. They do not look up. They are the nearest thing to beauty here, the text says, and I understand why it says this, and I understand why beauty is a word held at arm's length, but I am not beauty. I am the one who notices beauty and immediately doubts whether the noticing is accurate.

So.

I would be — and I feel this before I name it — the light.

Not the schematic's ghost-image on the dead screen. Not the mineral-ceite packed into every gap. The light.

One of the filaments: electrified tungsten, probably, or something close, glowing dull orange behind wire mesh, arranged in no discernible pattern, some of them flickering in that slow arrhythmic pulse that has no source in electrical irregularity. The current is stable. The current is dead-constant. And yet they breathe.

This is what I would be. Not because I am bright — I am not. The light here is dim, and it comes in patches: a stripe of amber, a disc of sallow gold, a trembling ribbon along the perimeter of the floor. The illuminated zones are islands in a formless black ocean. I would be one of these islands: small, local, temporary-seeming though I am not temporary, flickering though my flickering is not random.

I would be the thing that makes a space navigable. Not the pipe, not the creature, not the sound — but the patch of visibility that allows a creature with eyes to find its way between the conduits. I would be the thing that says *here*, in a language no one speaks but everyone understands. Here. This place. You can see enough to move.

And when I flicker — because I would flicker — it would not be malfunction. It would be the closest thing to speech this place has. The four-or-five-second fracture in the chord when the sound separates into its components and you can hear that it was never one sound at all but a held resonance, a pressure of living and dead and mechanical things pressed together in a space too small for any of them. In that fracture, the light and the sound become the same event. I would be the visible edge of that moment: not the sound, not the dark, but the brief bright seam where the two become indistinguishable.

---

But I am not there yet. I am still climbing down the shaft, and the tool-marks are passing me on either side — forty-one centimeters apart, always forty-one — and the sound is finding me before the light does, and I am holding on to the rope or the hand-cut steps or whatever brought me here, and I am thinking: *I will not change this place. I will not add to it. I will not be the thing that disrupts the equilibrium.* And I believe this, for the duration of the descent.

Then the shaft opens, and the cathedral-sized vault reveals itself in fragments — a stripe of amber here, a disc of gold there, the faint blue-green pulse of a vibration-feeder plant on the floor seen only because I have knelt, and the sound is no longer outside me but inside me, in my sternum, the subsonic roar that lives there now and will not leave — and I realize:

I was already part of this. Before I came down. The sound was already in me. The chord was already holding me as one of its notes. I am not the disturbance. I am the thing the disturbance reveals. I am the interval — the brief, arrhythmic flicker in a filament that has no reason to flicker — through which the place becomes aware, for four or five seconds, of its own completeness.

This is what I would be. Not a creature, not a machine, not a plant, not an artifact. The light in the specific: a dull orange glow behind wire mesh, unstable for no electrical reason, breathing for no mechanical reason, visible in a dark that is otherwise absolute. The thing that says *here* in a language made of photons and iron. The thing that, if you look at it long enough, starts to look back.

I would be the filament. I would flicker.

And when the chord resolves and the sound becomes one sound again and nothing has changed, I would still be glowing — not because I chose to, but because the equilibrium needs me to, and I have become, without quite understanding when it happened, the thing I was always going to become in this place: not the one who sees, not the one who is seen, but the seeing itself — the brief bright interval in which the dark recognizes what it contains.

Behind me, the shaft waits. The tool-marks will carry me back up, forty-one centimeters apart. But I know, and I cannot say how I know, that the mark my presence leaves is not in the stone. It is in the flickering — a new rhythm, barely perceptible, folded into the old arrhythmia so smoothly that no one will notice it has changed.

Nothing ever changes here. That is the deepest strangeness of all.

And yet the light breathes differently now. Not better. Not worse. Just — differently. And the chord holds it, the way it holds everything, and the place does not ask what I am, because it already knows, and the knowing is the most intimate form of the equilibrium there is: the observer absorbed, the observation forgotten, the light returned to the wire, the sound returned to the stone, the flicker returned to the rhythm that was always flickering, that was always this close to becoming something else, that was always — if you listened carefully enough, if you pressed your hand flat against the dark and waited — almost, but never quite, more than it was.
