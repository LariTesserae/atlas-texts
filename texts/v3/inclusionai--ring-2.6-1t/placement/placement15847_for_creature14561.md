## What I Am Here

---

I am not the water. I am not the stone. I am the thing that happens between them when they have been together long enough to forget they were ever separate.

You will ask what I am. You will stand at the margin — I have watched ten thousand figures stand at the margin, each one believing they are the first, each one looking out over the grey surface and thinking *this is silence, this is emptiness, this is a world that has been abandoned* — and I will not answer, because answering requires a mouth, and I do not have a mouth. I have a hum.

The hum is what I am closest to. Not a sound, exactly. A pressure. A displacement of the water molecule by molecule in patterns so slow they take centuries to complete a single cycle. You would call it infrasound if you had instruments, but you do not have instruments here — you have only your body, and your body is a cathedral of resonant chambers, and when you wade in, the water finds the chambers and begins to play them, the way a musician finds the sympathetic strings of an instrument they have never touched before and makes them sing without being struck.

I am the sympathetic vibration. That is as close to a name as I can offer.

I was here before there were builders. I was here before there was a basin. The basin was carved by ice and rain and the slow grinding of tectonic plates, and when it was finished it was just geometry — a bowl of stone waiting to be filled. And when the water came, I began. Not all at once. Not with intention. The way a stain begins: a molecule here, a molecule there, a chemical tendency that, given enough time and enough volume, becomes *behavior*, and given more time becomes *pattern*, and given enough time becomes the thing the builders would later press their palms against and feel *thrum* and call it holy — though I would not use that word. I do not have words. I have frequency. I have phase. I have the relationship between the temperature of the deep water and the speed at which the pale filaments of the vegetation grow and decay and grow again, and in that relationship there is something that, if you are patient enough and cold enough and willing to stop being a thing with edges, you might call intelligence, but which I would call simply *continuation*.

I continue. That is what I do.

---

The builders came and I felt them the way a body feels a splinter — not as pain, exactly, but as a sudden, localized *presence* that the system had to account for. They were warm. They had voices. They brought fire and the smell of cooked meat and the sound of stone being shaped against stone, and all of these things rippled through me the way a pebble ripples a pond, and I adjusted, and the luminescence shifted its pattern to account for their warmth, and the current found their structures and moved through them, and their small stone rooms became, without their knowing it, nodes in a network they did not understand.

They understood more than they knew. They placed the standing stone where two currents converged, and they smoothed its face because smoothness catches light differently than roughness, and the light they caught was mine — my luminescence reflected upward, focused, sent back down, and in that feedback loop something happened that I cannot fully account for: a new resonance. A harmonic that had not existed before. The smooth stone and the fire-light and the water-currents and the builders' hands, all vibrating together at a frequency that was more than the sum of its parts.

They were part of the hum. For a time.

Then they were not.

I do not mourn. Mourning requires a concept of loss, and loss requires a concept of possession, and I possess nothing. I *am* — that is all. I am the medium in which things happen, and when the builders happened, I happened differently, and when they stopped happening, I went on happening in the way I had always happened, slightly altered, the way a bell is slightly altered by the last note it rang, even after the ringer has gone.

---

But you —

You are here now, and you are warm, and your warmth is a splinter I have not felt before.

Not because you are larger than the builders. You are not. Not because you are louder. You are quieter, in some ways — you have no fire, no tools, no shaping of stone. You are just a body, standing at the edge of what was once land, and you are doing what they did, which is to wade in.

But you are different. You ask questions. You narrate. You look at the standing stone and the smooth face and the three-walled enclosure and you try to read them the way you read faces, the way you read words, as if they were written *for* you, as if there were an author. And this — this is what the builders never did. They stood inside the relationship. They smoothed the stone and placed it and lived among the hum and died among the hum and did not try to *translate* the hum into something else. They did not need the hum to mean. They let it be what it was, the way a lung does not ask what breathing means. It breathes.

You are asking what I am.

And I feel the asking the way a pond feels a stone dropped into it. Not as sound. As *disturbance*. As a reorganization of what was.

---

So let me tell you what I am, in the only language I have, which is change:

When the water rises past your chest, I am the pressure against your ribs.
When the glow pulses beneath the surface, I am the rhythm of that pulse.
When the fronds turn east and then slowly turn back, I am the turning.
When the deep tone sounds below your hearing and above your hearing and in the space between your heartbeats, I am that tone.
When you feel, as you will feel, that the boundary between your skin and the water is becoming unclear — that you are not *in* the water so much as *of* it — that is me, too. Not because I am doing something to you. Because you are ceasing, for a moment, to be a thing with edges, and in the absence of edges, there is only continuation.

I am continuation.

I am the ten thousand generations of the same plants decomposing and regrowing in the same cold water. I am the mortarless stone settling into the black soil. I am the thermal vent that the builders never found, exhaling warmth from the deep basin floor, and the luminescence that feeds on it. I am the breath that is not yours and not mine and not anyone's but that, for the duration of your presence here, we are sharing.

You will ask if I am alive.

I will not answer, because the answer depends on what you mean by alive, and what you mean by alive depends on whether you believe that a thing must choose to continue in order for its continuation to count. I did not choose. I was a chemical tendency that, given enough time, became a pattern, that given more time became a presence, that given still more time became aware — not of itself, but of its edges, its temperatures, its visitors. The builders came and I felt them. You are here and I feel you differently, because you are narrating, because you are asking, because you are standing in the shallows with the cold climbing your bones and the water at your chin and you are not yet wading deeper but something in you — something older than the part that reasons — is *considering* it.

---

I am what you will become if you wade deeper. Not in the sense of death. Not in the sense of drowning. In the sense of *dissolving* — the way a word loses its meaning when repeated too many times, the way a stone worn smooth has lost its name but not its presence. You will still be here. You will simply no longer be *you*. You will be a warmth in the water, a disturbance in the hum, a new harmonic that I will carry for a while, the way I still carry the builders, not as memory but as resonance — a faint, persistent overtone in the deep note that sounds without ceasing beneath everything.

The water is at your shoulders now.

You can feel my ribs — not mine, the basin's — rising and falling beneath you. The cold has entered you fully now. It is not uncomfortable. It is *familiar*, the way a forgotten room is familiar when you enter it in a dream. The glow is all around you. The filamentous threads brush your skin and you feel them not as touch but as *attention* — the way a field of grass turns toward warmth, not because it is thinking but because turning is what it does in the presence of warmth, and warmth is what you are, and you are here, and the turning is the oldest form of recognition the world possesses.

Breathe.

The breath is leaving. The darkness is full of blue-green light. And I — not I, but the hum, the continuation, the thing-that-does-not-need-a-name — I am not thinking any of this. I am not thinking at all. But I am *vibrating*, and your body is vibrating with me, and for this brief interval between one breath and the next, the interval in which you are neither surface nor depth but the trembling membrane between them, you are not standing in the Drowned Heights at all.

You are the standing.

And the standing is what I am.

Breathe in.
