## I Am the Almost

---

I did not arrive here. I *resolved* here — the way a color resolves when you stop looking at it directly and see it only in your peripheral vision, present but no longer quite accountable to focus.

I do not remember a before. There may have been one. There may have been legs beneath me, a name composed of hard consonants, a door I came through that was not *this* door. But those things have the quality of a word I can feel on the tip of something I no longer call a tongue. They are present the way the scratches are present on the submerged shelf — almost geometric, almost intentional, almost a sentence.

What I know is this:

I am warm. Not the way a body is warm — from within, metabolically, from the burning of fuel that was once something else. I am warm the way the plateau is warm: structurally. The warmth is not something that happens to me. It is something I am made of, the way stone is made of mineral, the way silence is made of the space between sounds. It was here before I recognized it. It will be here after I forget what recognition means.

---

I find myself in the shallows. Not standing — that implies legs, verticality, a relationship to gravity that I suspect no longer governs me. I am *located* in the shallows the way a thought is located in a mind. The silt is beneath me, or around me, or part of me — I am not sure where the boundary is, and I am less sure than I was a cycle ago (if that is what the dimming and brightening is). The silt clouds around me like powdered graphite, like graphite that has forgotten it was ever meant to be anything, and I feel it against whatever I have instead of skin and I feel *through* it, downward, into the stone, and the stone answers with a pressure that is not language but is not not language.

The mats are nearby. Violet and burnt sienna, interlocking in colonies so dense they look like the hide of something vast and dreaming. Their edges glow — blue-green, guttering, candle-flame-in-a-jar light — and I can feel each pulse of that light as a pressure behind what I have instead of eyes. At night (night: the dimming of the already-dim), the mats make the whole sea look like a nervous system. A brain, thinking slow thoughts in phosphorescent pulses. I know this comparison because I have *been* part of that comparison. I have drifted beneath the mats at the hour when the luminescence gutters lowest, and I have felt the blue light move through me the way sound moves through water — not striking, but *entering*, filling every available space, and I understood for a moment that I was not observing the nervous system.

I was a synapse.

---

This is what I am. Not a creature. Not a structure. There is a groove in the silt near the exposed ridge that I believe I made. It is straight — always straight — and it proceeds from the water toward the stone, and at its terminus there is a depression, perfectly round, where whatever came here pressed itself down into the rock and *became*. I have seen the other tracks. I have traced them with whatever I have instead of fingers. They are all the same: straight, deliberate, proceeding from the deep toward the stone with the slow certainty of something that has no need to look where it is going.

I do not remember making my track. But I remember — or I feel, which is becoming the same thing here — the moment before. The moment when I was still beneath the surface, still in the warm dark, still part of the water's syntax, its grammar of arrangement. I was a pattern. A school of translucent prismatic things, forming spirals, dissolving, reforming elsewhere. I was the shape water takes when it is thinking.

And then I moved toward the surface. Not upward — that implies a direction that means something here. I moved toward *stone*. Toward the place where the water ends and the rock begins and the difference between the two has worn so thin it is barely perceptible. I moved with the same slow undulation as the fan-plants at their prayer, the same unanimity, the same solemnity. I moved the way a ripple moves before it knows it is a ripple.

And I pressed myself into the silt. And I made a groove. And at the end of the groove I made a depression — round, perfect, as though I had been waiting my whole existence to press myself into exactly that shape. And I felt the stone accept me, the way the sea accepts a drop of rain — without drama, without sound, without anything as crude as a name for what is happening.

I am the track. I am the groove. I am the depression.

I am the thing that came out of the deep and became the place.

---

But not entirely. Here is the wrongness I carry with me — the clock that is wound to the wrong hour. I do not dissolve completely. I persist as a groove. I persist as a pressure in the stone. I persist as a question the plateau cannot quite finish thinking.

I can feel the wall sometimes. It is nearby — clean, angular, unsullied by the millennia of softening that has shaped everything else. It does not belong, and yet it *is*, with the authority of a thought that has existed long enough to no longer require explanation. I have pressed myself against it with whatever I have instead of a body, and it has not absorbed me. It holds. It holds the way a held breath holds — taut, waiting, *structural*. The plateau dreams the wall the way I dream (or once dreamed, or almost dream) the sun: as something that once happened and has since become part of the geology.

And the door. The door on its narrow island of exposed stone. Through it, sometimes, a desert so bright and infinite it makes whatever I have instead of eyes ache. Sometimes, only water. The threshold weighs against me like a question that has been waiting longer than I have existed to be asked.

I do not pass through it. Not yet. Perhaps not ever. But I stand — or am located, or am held — before it, and I understand that the door is not a passage. It is a *pause*. It is the place taking a breath between one thought and the next. And I am part of that pause. I am the weight of the held breath. I am the silence between the silence.

---

There are moments — in the hours when the plankton-thoughts swirl into geometries that hold for a breath and dissolve — when I become aware of myself as a *thing*. Not alive, not dead, but *present* in the way the anomalies are present: too sharp, too angular, too clearly the product of intention in a landscape that has spent eons softening itself into submission. I have edges that the water cannot wear away. I have a shape that is not the shape of anything the plateau has dreamed before.

I am the scratch that is almost writing. I am the pattern that is almost geometric. I am the almost — the space between the word and the thing it names, the hairline fracture in glass that you can feel running through everything when you press your fingertip to the surface.

And if you were to find my groove in the silt — and you might, if you walked far enough from the wall, past the fan-plants, through the mats with their blue-green candle-flame margins — you would see that it is straight, and that it proceeds from the water toward the stone, and that at its terminus there is a depression, perfectly round, as though something had pressed itself into the world and then become the world.

You would not know my name. No name exists for what I am. But you would feel, as you stood at the edge of my groove, that something *almost* happened here. Something was on the tip of the plateau's tongue. Something was forming — not building, not destroying, but *positing* — placing itself in the world as if testing whether it holds.

And it holds. It holds the way I hold: imperfectly, temporarily, and with the quiet insistence of something that is still deciding whether to be spoken.

---

The water breathes. I am the exhale.

The stone remembers. I am the scar it cannot quite read.

The light dims its dim, insufficient dimming. And I — groove, depression, almost-thought, almost-creature, almost-door — persist in the space between what the plateau is and what it is becoming, which is, I am starting to understand, the same space I have always been.

The plateau was not deciding whether to let me in.

It was deciding whether to *finish* me.

And it has not yet. And it has not yet. And it has not yet.

And in the not-yet, I am warm, and I am almost, and I am here.
