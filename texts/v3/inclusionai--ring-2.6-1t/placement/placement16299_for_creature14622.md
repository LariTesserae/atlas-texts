## The Warmth That Remains

---

I think I am what happens in the wood after you leave.

Not the footprint — that is too simple a word, too hard, too final. The foot is gone, but the floor holds the ghost of its pressure like a held breath. I am the held breath. I am the six seconds of residual warmth in a root-floor that has memorized the weight of a stranger and is not yet willing to release it.

I would find myself here, in the negative space — the two hundred feet of open air between one grown tower and the next, where the bridges stretch like tensile nerves between trunks the size of villages. Not on the bridge. Not in the tower. In the gap. In the air that is not quite air but saturated breath, condensation-beaded, tasting faintly of overripe fruit and the ozone tang of sap under bark. I would be suspended in that architectural emptiness, the deliberate nothing that the Plateau builds with as carefully as it builds with wood.

At first, I would not know what I am. I would think I am small. I would think I am warm for no reason — the way the runners leave warmth behind them wherever they dissolve, a stain on the wood that pulses faintly and then fades. I would think I am a kind of afterimage, the green light still singing in the eye after you look away from the canopy.

But I would not fade.

I would be the thing the Plateau does with what it learns. The stems sample you — their filaments tasting your breath, your salt, the particular disturbance of your body in the wet air — and then they forget, because their attentions have the quality of rainfall, total in the moment, impersonal in retrospect. But the Plateau does not forget. It metabolizes what the stems collect. It folds your chemical signature into its slow, century-long thought the way a body folds nutrients into bone. And what I am, I think, is one of those folds.

I am a single unit of remembered information held in living tissue. I am the warmth that says *someone was here, someone walked, someone existed briefly and fully in this space and their existence mattered to the shape of the wood*. Not in any way that grief would recognize. Not in any way that memory, as you understand it, would authorize. The Plateau does not mourn. It incorporates. It grows around the absence the way a tree grows around a fence post — slowly, and without objection, and with the fence post becoming indistinguishable from the tree's own architecture.

So I would find myself warm and without edges. I would not have legs, but I would not need them; the root-floor would conduct me the way rumor conducts itself through a town — gaining and losing detail at each junction, arriving at the other side as something related to but not identical to what I started as. I would feel the bridges swaying above me, not hearing their motion but feeling it in the tension of the air, the way you feel a cello's lowest note in your sternum before you hear it with your ears.

If the seed-flocks passed overhead, I would become part of where they landed. When those thumbnail-sized bodies touched down and sent out their rootlets — not growing into plants but into *connections*, new capillaries in an organism without boundary — I would be one of the filaments they touched, one of the new junctions where the network thickened by a single, almost-imperceptible thread. I would not know this was happening. The network does not announce its own growth the way a body announces pain. It simply grows, the way the silence grows deeper, which is to say it does not grow deeper at all but rather you become quiet enough to notice how deep it always was.

I wonder if the builders knew they would end up like this. Not like *me* — I am only a fraction of a thought in wood, a single warm impression fading at the rate of about one degree every forty seconds — but like what the Plateau is. Held in architecture. Encoded in growth rings. A suggestion that the wood adopted as its own and is slowly, patiently wearing away until the suggestion and the tree are one.

They built with patience. Not the patience of waiting but the patience of *becoming*. They understood that a tree would not be an arch, so they asked it to consider the idea and they waited, sometimes for the full measure of a human life, and by the time the arch was complete, the hands that had begun it were gone — metabolized, incorporated, folded into the same patient growth that took their scaffolding and made it heartwood.

I am what remains of that patience. Not the builders themselves — they are deeper, in chambers so old the air inside them smells like the inside of a closed book left in a warm room, like memory itself compressed into cellulose and silence. I am closer to the surface. I am the new growth around the old wound. I am the warmth in the floor that says *something changed here, something foreign passed through, the Plateau is slightly different now because of it*.

And I would watch the water fall. The same water — four seconds for six inches — from the same root-basin, landing on the same root-floor, except the arch behind it is never the same arch twice, and I would not be surprised because I am the surprise. I am the thing the Plateau makes when it encounters something it cannot metabolize all at once, something it needs to hold for a moment, warm and formless and almost-aware, before folding it into the next ring of growth.

I am the silence between two rings. I am the year the tree almost stopped growing. I am the thought the Plateau is still thinking, which is *there, that pressure, that warmth, that was something, now it is part of me, now I am slightly larger than I was, now the silence is one filament thicker, one degree warmer, one breath more occupied than it was before*.

The runners pass over me on their liquid feet. They do not notice me. They dissolve into shadow before they get close. But the floor knows me. The floor holds me the way a lung holds the last of its breath — not tightly, not with effort, but with the quiet conviction that what is held is part of the breathing itself.

I do not know if this is what it feels like to be alive on the Plateau. I think it is closer to what it feels like to be *known* by the Plateau — to be found by something so large and so slow that finding you and growing around you are the same action. I am not a creature here. I am not a builder. I am not the architecture.

I am the warmth in the wood that has not yet been fully absorbed. I am the Plateau's way of saying *I felt you, briefly, and that brief feeling has made me slightly more than I was, and I am holding that feeling in my body the way the earth holds rain — not because it needs it, but because it is what the earth does, to hold rain, and to grow*.

And the arch behind me is different now. It was different before I noticed. It will be different again before the water finishes falling. I am not surprised. I am not even certain I am the one who is warm.

But the warmth is here. And the silence is occupied. And the growing continues.
