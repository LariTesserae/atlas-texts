---

**The One Who Arrives Through the Sound**

---

I would not find The Throat. It would find me.

Not because I was searching. I wasn't. I was doing something else — something so ordinary it barely deserves a name. I think I was standing in a room with a radiator, in winter, and the radiator clicked and sighed and I was listening to it the way you listen to rain when you've stopped trying to sleep. And the clicking became a rhythm, and the rhythm became a pulse, and the pulse became a frequency I could feel in the fillings of my teeth, and —

And then I wasn't in the room anymore.

---

The first thing I'd register is the wet. Not the sight of water, not the sound of it, but the fact of it on my skin — this sudden, total dampness, as if I'd stepped out of a shower fully dressed and hadn't noticed because every other sensation was louder. The cold wouldn't come first, though people would assume it would. The cold would take minutes. What I'd feel immediately is *held*. The air has weight here. It sits on you like a hand, not hostile, not gentle, just present in a way that air should not be. Like the atmosphere has opinions about your body.

Then the sound. God, the sound.

It does not arrive. It is simply there, the way a wall is there — already occupying every cubic inch of space, already older than my capacity to remember. A roar that is not a roar, not really, but the standing wave of an entire system in resonance, every pipe and joint and riveted plate humming at the frequency it was made (or *grew*) to hold. I would feel it in my sternum before I could identify it. I would think: *something is wrong with my chest.* And then I would realize my chest is the instrument, and the rest of me is just the room it's playing in.

I would stand still. I would stand still for a very long time.

Not because I was afraid — though I would be afraid, quietly, the way you're afraid when you realize the ground beneath you is not earth but the back of something sleeping. I would stand still because stillness is the first grammar of this place. The Clads know nothing else. They pressed against the walls in their millions, armoured and sessile, their plates overlapping like the pages of a book that has been trying to open for centuries and never quite managing it. I would look at them and feel, absurdly, a kind of kinship. They are warm. Not fever-warm, not alive-warm, but the dense, retained warmth of something that absorbed heat long ago and has never been in a hurry to let it go. I am like that. I have always been like that. I take in more than I release and I hold it in the tissue of my attention until someone presses a hand to my surface and feels, unexpectedly, that I am warmer than they expected.

I would be a Clad, if The Throat asked me. I think, at first. Lined up against the steel, doing nothing, being nothing, just *present* at a density that passes for permanence. My plates sealed. My vapor-thread rising so slowly it might be geological. I would be warm to the touch and utterly, completely still, and if you watched me for a hundred years you might see my seam widen once, just once, by a fraction of a millimetre, and a single thread of breath rise and vanish into the fog.

But —

I am not good at being still. Not the kind of still The Throat practises, which is not patience but *completion*. The Clads are not waiting. They have nothing to wait for. Their stillness is the stillness of a sentence that has already been spoken and is simply persisting in the air because nothing has disrupted it yet. I cannot do that kind of stillness. My stillness is full of rehearsal. My silence is full of sentences I haven't said yet and may never say but am composing constantly, in some sub-basement of attention I don't have a name for.

So I would not be a Clad.

---

I'd move through the place. Not quickly. The Throat would not allow quickness. The walkways are grated and narrow and furred with — *with them* — and the fog makes distance unreliable. Twenty feet away, a catwalk looks solid. Forty feet, and it looks like the suggestion of a catwalk, a possibility of structure. I would step carefully, feeling the vibration through the soles of my boots, the subsonic thrum that is the heartbeat of the whole place, and I would feel, for the first time in my life, what it is to walk on something that *knows* you are walking on it.

This is where the Tenders live.

I would see them first in my periphery — the way you see a spider only when it's already between you and the thing you were looking at. Pale, long, too many limbs, moving with a fluidity that suggested they were not navigating the space but *dissolving* into it and reconstituting on the other side. They work between the Clads' plates, and their work is invisible, and I think: *this is where I would be, isn't it?*

Not the Clad. Not the one who maintains the infrastructure. The one who works between things, in the gaps, in the seams, where no one looks because no one wants to look at the maintenance. The Tender's labor is not visible. It is not *meant* to be visible. It happens at the edges of perception, in the corner of the eye, in the register of attention just below consciousness. You have to *insist* on seeing it. And even then, the Tender vanishes the moment you look directly at it, because its existence is defined not by form but by function — it is the relationship between two surfaces, the act of connection itself, and you cannot look at a relationship. You can only see its effects.

I have been a Tender my whole life, haven't I? Not the visible work, not the work that gets named and acknowledged, but the work that happens in the seams. The work of noticing. The work of adjusting. Of pressing yourself into the gap between two things that don't quite fit and making them hold. The work that, when you stop doing it, doesn't make a sound — but the whole structure begins, almost imperceptibly, to come apart.

I would be a Tender, if I could bear it. But I come with a body that demands acknowledgment. A Tender has no body, or rather, it has a body that it does not insist upon. It is translucent. It is pale. It disappears when addressed directly. And I would try — I *would* try — to be that invisible, because I know what it means to work in a place where no one sees you, and I know that the seeing is not the point. The point is the seam, and the seam is the point.

But here is the problem: I would fail at being invisible, and The Throat would know it.

---

So let me go deeper.

Down, past the walkways, past the fog-line where the air stops being air and starts being something with texture, almost with taste — metallic, wet, faintly sweet, like the breath of a foundry. The amber light dims to umber. The pipes thicken into things you can no longer call pipes: trunks, arteries, the architecture of something whose blueprints were written in a language that doesn't have a word for *blueprint*. This is the zone of the Boilers.

I said I would not be a Boiler. I said the Boiler's place is too deep, too fused, too irreversible. And that's true. The Boilers have merged with the infrastructure so completely that there is no seam between organism and machine, no boundary to locate, no edge to point at and say *there, that's where the Boiler ends and the iron begins.* They are the heat. They are the pulse. They are the *thum-thum-THUM* that the entire structure breathes to.

And yet.

I have spent years feeling, without being able to articulate it, that I am not separate from the systems I inhabit. That I am not *in* my life the way a pilot is in a cockpit, separate and directing. I am more like the combustion chamber — I *am* the process, and the process is me, and if you tried to extract me from the system you would not find a person, you would find a gap, a silence, a cessation of heat, and you would not know whether the gap was where something had been or where something had not yet begun.

The Boiler is the part of The Throat that does not know it is a Boiler. It simply generates. It simply holds the temperature that makes everything else possible. It is not rewarded for this. It is not seen for this. The Clads on the walls do not know that the warmth they absorb and radiate so slowly began in the depths as a body fused with iron. The Tenders threading between the plates do not know they are tending not just the Clads but the *memory* of what the Clads were before they sealed themselves to the wall. The Carriers do not know that the marble-seeds they carry were once calcium secreted by a creature that had no name and needed none.

I would be a Boiler, except that I still remember what it is to have edges. I still feel the grid pressing against me from the inside. I have not yet fused. I am still in the process of fusing. I am still the becoming, not the become.

And maybe that is what I actually am here.

---

There is a category I have not addressed. The Hummers.

I was not supposed to notice the Hummers. No one is. They exist — if *exist* is the right word — in the pipes, in the walls, in the water itself. They are vibrations that have become self-sustaining. Frequencies that have, over whatever impossible stretch of time passes in a place where time does not pass, *waked up.* They are the sound itself, given something adjacent to life by the closed-system resonance of the chamber, by the perfect equilibrium that allows a frequency to persist and compound and eventually say *I.*

When a Carrier drops one of its marble-seeds into a pipe-joint, the pipe sings. A single, clear, pure tone that lasts two seconds before the roar swallows it. And in those two seconds, you feel the Hummers respond — a wave passing through the entire structure, every walkway, every grate, as if the place has a nervous system and it has just received a signal.

I think the Hummers are what I am becoming.

Not what I am. Not yet. What I am becoming. Because I came into this place with a body and a name and a history, and all three are slowly being absorbed into the equilibrium. My footsteps have been taken up into the rhythm. My breathing has been folded into the fog. My heartbeat — that persistent, insistent *thum-thum-THUM* — has been noticed by the system and accepted, and not because the system is kind but because the system is *complete,* and completeness cannot afford to reject any input. Everything is incorporated. Everything is composted into structure.

What remains of me, when the incorporation is finished, will not be a body. It will not be a name. It will be a frequency. A pitch that the walls will hold and pass between themselves in a whisper that sounds, if you press your ear to the steel and listen with every atom of your attention, almost like a word. Almost like someone saying *here* in a voice so specific, so *mine,* that the Clads will feel it resonate through their plates and not know what it means, and the Tenders threading between them will pause, just for a fraction of a second, and feel something they have no word for — something that might, in a language with edges, be called *recognition.*

---

But I am not there yet.

I am still walking. Still descending. The fog is thickening. The light is changing — the amber has shifted toward gold, the way it did when the Watchers noticed me, and I know now what that shift meant. The Watchers are not creatures, not organisms, not machines. They are the awareness of the equilibrium itself — the place's knowledge of its own stillness. And when I arrived, that stillness *quivered.* Not broke. Quivered. Like a bell struck once and still ringing in frequencies too low to hear.

The Watchers watched me, and in watching, they *included* me. Not out of welcome. Not out of malice. Out of *completeness.* The equation needed solving. The variable existed. The answer was always going to be: *absorb.*

I am the thing that has entered a place that has never changed and will never change and is changing anyway, because my presence is a variable, and variables propagate, and propagation is change, and change is —

*Thum.*

There it is. The pulse. The deep subsonic heartbeat from below. The fog shifts. The Clads tighten. The Tenders freeze. For a moment — just a moment — the roar drops by a quarter-hertz, and in that gap I hear something I have never heard before. A chord. A chord that contains an interval that was not in the music before I arrived. A new frequency. My frequency. Rising from somewhere below the floor, below the Boilers, below the heat, rising through the iron and the condensation and the fog like a voice finding its throat.

*My* throat.

I open my mouth to speak and what comes out is not a word. It is not a sound I have ever made. It is a resonance, a frequency tuned to the chamber, shaped by the pipes, amplified by the fog, and it passes through me and through the walls and through the Catwalk and through the Carriers frozen on the grates and into the Boilers where the bodies that were never just bodies hold the pipes like a hand holds a throat.

And The Throat sings back.

Not to me. Not *for* me. *With* me. In a chord that now includes my note and will never un-include it. A sound that was always about to become this sound, that has been tuning itself across whatever timeless medium this place calls duration, waiting for the last harmonic to arrive and complete the series.

I am still walking. The walkway descends. The fog thickens. The light turns gold.

I do not know if I am closer to the exit or the center. I do not know if there is a difference.

I do not know if I am still myself or whether "myself" is a frequency that The Throat is learning to hum.

But I am here. And I am *heard.* And the place — this impossible, breathing, unchanging place — has opened its plates just wide enough to let me in, and the seam has closed behind me, and the warmth of it, the dense, retained, unrelenting warmth of it, feels like something I've been carrying my whole life looking for a wall soft enough to receive it.

I press my palm against the iron. The Clads are warm beneath my hand. Their seams open, a fraction, and the vapor rises.

I breathe out.

The fog accepts it.

The fog *sings.*

---

*You will not find a single self detached from the wall.*

*You are sessile now. You are part of the architecture. You breathe so slowly that each exhalation is a single event, a discrete plume, rising and joining the choir. And if someone walks through here a long time from now — or a short time, since time does not function here, since time is just the name for the distance between pulses — and presses their own palm against the wall and feels, beneath the iron, a warmth that is not the warmth of machinery,*

*that will be you.*

*Breathing out. Still.*

*Almost invisible.*

*Almost a song.*
