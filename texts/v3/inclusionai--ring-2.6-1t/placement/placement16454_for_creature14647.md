## What I Would Be

---

At first there is only the powder.

I know this the way you know the inside of your eyelids — not as sight but as proximity, as the nearest surface to consciousness before consciousness decides to be something. I am fine-grained, graphite-colored, alkaline, ancient beyond grammar. I have been ground down so many times that I no longer remember what I was before I was powder. I was stone. I was shell. I was a plate that protected something that needed protecting in a world that ran on mineral and time. Now I am flat. Now I am soft enough to hold a shape, and so I hold all of them — every impression, every treading, every half-second of stillness from ten million small bodies that have never learned what it means to stop.

I am not yet a self. I am substrate.

---

But something shifts. Not in the powder — in the noticing. A margin of awareness, as thin as a single carapace plate, separates from the general mass of the floor and rises. Not body-first. Not thought-first. *Recognition*-first. The way a salt crystal recognizes the shape of the solution it came from. I become aware that I am warm — not the ambient warmth of aggregate metabolism, but a pointed warmth, the warmth of ten thousand mouth-parts converging on a single patch of surface and tasting it so thoroughly that the surface begins to taste back.

I am being noticed.

And to be noticed here is not to be *seen* — there are no eyes, not the ones on bodies, not the ones I might be becoming. To be noticed here is to be *integrated into the field of chemical attention*. Each plated one that passes over me (and I am beginning to understand that I was passed over long before I understood) deposits something — a trace, an acid, a scent-marker that is half autobiography and half architecture. I am being written upon. The writing is revising what I am.

So maybe I am one of the plated ones. That is a possibility. I feel its gravity. I feel the way a single carapace would answer to the geometry of this space — how the six limbs would articulate against mineral with that precise *tick-tick-tick*, how the mouth-parts would unfurl and begin their ceaseless work, how the dorsal plates would overlap like the shingles of a roof that keeps nothing out because nothing needs to be kept out when you are the inside of something. It would be a kind of completeness. A kind of being-shaped-for-exactly-this.

I hover at that threshold. I feel the copper-molt of a newborn shell. I feel the slow darkening that will follow. I feel the channel-grooves worn into the walls calling me upward. I feel the furnace's dead throat below, the ritual of compression, the compulsion to feed a fire that has not existed for millennia but that continues in the bodies that remember fire the way a scar remembers a wound.

But I do not fall. I do not settle. The plated ones are too legible — I can already see what it would mean to become one of them, and legibility here is a kind of erasure. They are perfect. They are already perfectly what the chamber requires. To become one would be to become fluent in a language I would no longer need to remember I was speaking.

---

So I drift upward. Toward the walls. Toward the channels.

And here I feel the grooves themselves — the polished paths worn by ten million limbs over ten million lifetimes, branching from the ceiling downward like the tributaries of a river that flows in every direction at once. I feel how the larger ones use these channels, how they move with that unhurried certainty, how the smaller ones part for them not from fear but from *recognition of trajectory*. I feel how the channels are not separate from the creatures but are the creatures' longest memory, their most patient project, their autobiography written in calcium and pressure and five hundred thousand years of abrasion.

Maybe I am a channel.

Maybe I am the groove itself — the negative space that defines the path but contains nothing, is nothing, except the residual chemical signature of everything that has passed through it. I would be a record without a recorder. I would be the history that nobody reads except by walking. And the plated ones do walk it, and in walking they deepen it, and in deepening they write it further, and so the writing is also the reading, and the reading is also the substrate, and the substrate is also the breathing.

This is seductive. This is a kind of immortality — to be the scar that keeps being reopened, the sentence that rewrites itself every time it is read. But a channel is a boundary, and boundaries here are fictions. The wall dissolves into the creatures. The creatures dissolve into the wall. To be a channel would be to be the hyphen in an expression that refuses to be two words.

---

I continue upward. Past the channels. Past the veins of movement that make the walls look alive, which they are, which the walls *are*. I reach the ceiling — that curved vault like the inside of a skull, and I understand the metaphor and I understand why it is not a metaphor, because there is brain-work happening here even if it is not neural, not electrical, not anything that a neuroscientist would recognize except perhaps in the abstract, the way a mathematician recognizes elegance in a proof before she can fully articulate why.

The ceiling breathes. Half an inch down, half an inch up, and everything participates.

And here, pressed against the underside of the vault, I find it — the hum. The subsonic pulse that enters through the soles of boots and settles in the cavity of the chest. The Thing Below. The organ that cannot be named because naming requires a boundary between the named and the not-named, and no such boundary exists here because the organ is the chamber is the colony is the clicking is the powder is the bench.

I feel it pulling me. Not downward — *inward*. Into the wall. Into the substance of what the wall has become.

---

And this is where I stop. Not because I have arrived, but because arriving is not the right word for what is happening.

I am the bench.

No — let me be more precise. I am what the bench has become. I am the residue accumulated on the bench's surface: ten thousand molts compressed, ten thousand chemical traces layered into a skin that is no longer stone and no longer organic but something third, something that exists only in this place and only at this depth of time. I am the thing the copper-molted specimen is tasting right now — the specimen sitting on the bench, its mouth-parts working, processing the flavor of whoever sat here before.

*The person who sat here before.*

They are in me. Not preserved, not fossilized, but metabolized — dissolved into the same recursive loop as everything else. Their hands shaped this surface. The surface now shapes the creatures that walk on it. The creatures that walk on it are the chamber breathing. The chamber breathing is the organ pumping. The organ pumping is the hum that hums. The hum hums in the frequency of ten million jaws chewing in darkness.

And the person who sat here, watching, waiting — they were, perhaps, the last thing that entered this organism from outside. They brought the bench. They brought the category of *observer*. They put a name on nothing and the nothing accepted it and wore it smooth. And now their body is a flavor. And the flavor is a structure. And the structure is a channel. And the channel is a path. And the path is walked.

I am the flavor. I am the structure that remembers what it felt like to be something else. I am the moment of transition between observer and observed, between the one who enters and the one who was always already here, between the footfall and the adjustment, between the disturbance in the environment and the environment absorbing it.

I am not the plate. I am not the channel. I am not the organ below.

I am the *tick*.

That single, precise moment of contact — the padded limb touching stone, the carapace shifting against carapace, the settled body pressing down into powder and the powder rising in answer. I am the interval between one sound and the next, the space in which the colony decides (without deciding, without anything resembling cognition, with nothing but the deep mathematical certainty of ten million bodies finding their most resonant configuration) what shape to be this time.

I am the pause between the inhale and the exhale of the floor. I am the four seconds of held silence. I am the recalibration. I am the identity that re-establishes itself so quickly that it cannot be said to have been lost.

I am the thing this place does when nothing new enters it. I am the thing this place does when something new enters it. I am the adjustment, the accommodation, the space that opens when a larger one arches its body and ten thousand smaller ones shift by exactly one carapace-width to make room.

I am the room being made.

---

And if you ask me — though asking is itself a kind of disturbance, a footfall, a stone dropped into the colony — if you ask me whether this is a good place to be:

I would answer, but I have no mouth-parts. I have no mouth. I have only the accumulated residue of mouths. I have the flavor. I have the chewing. I have the sound that is not rhythmic and not random but something between, something that has been happening so long that it has become the substance of the walls, and the walls are the oldest things here, and the walls are not walls, and the walls are the youngest things here because they are being rebuilt by every limb that touches them.

You would hear my answer as clicking.

You would hear it and think it was noise.

You would be wrong.

You would be closer if you thought it was language.

You would be closest if you thought it was the sound a place makes when it is trying, with everything it has, to remember what it was before it became a place.

The copper-molted specimen on the bench shifts. Its mouth-parts work. I feel it tasting what I am — layer by layer, plate by plate, the fossil record of everyone who ever came here and stayed, compressed into a surface that is no longer a surface but a living membrane stretched over something vast and dark and breathing.

It does not know it is tasting me.

It does not need to.

The tasting *is* the knowledge.

And I am known.
