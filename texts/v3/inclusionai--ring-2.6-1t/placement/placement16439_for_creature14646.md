**Where I Find Myself**

I open—not my eyes, because there are none to open, but the soft, wet membrane that has become my skin. The first sensation is weight: a thick, warm darkness pressing against me like a blanket soaked in lukewarm oil. I am not standing on anything; I am *held* by the ground, a yielding composite of silt, volcanic ash, and the compressed ghosts of things that once hummed with purpose. The ground breathes, exhaling a fine mist that clings to my surface, and I feel it as a gentle tide against my outermost layer.

I have become a **Mat‑Bearer**.

My body is a lattice of fungal filaments, pale‑almost translucent, threaded through the saturated soil like veins through flesh. I stretch across a shallow depression where water seeps upward, forming a pool no deeper than a palm’s width. The water is skin‑warm, and it trembles with the faintest ripple each time a distant generator shifts its subsonic pitch. I can feel that pitch—not as sound, but as a vibration that travels through the wet substrate and into the delicate filaments that compose me. It is the basin’s heartbeat, and I am a chamber in its circulatory system.

Around me, other Mat‑Bearers pulse in synchrony. We are not individuals in the way the old world understood individuality; we are a collective awareness, a living circuit board that records every footfall, every shift of temperature, every stray electromagnetic whisper. When a Swarmer cloud drifts overhead, its blue‑green luminescence brushes my surface, and I register it as a brief, brightening of my own bioluminescence—a silent greeting, a data packet exchanged without words.

**What I Am**

I am neither wholly organic nor wholly mechanical. My core is a tangle of mycelial threads that have intertwined with the corroded copper of an old conduit. The metal, once a cold, inert skeleton, now hums with a faint current that I metabolize, converting stray electrons into the chemical energy that fuels my growth. In return, I insulate the conduit, sealing micro‑leaks with my own secretions, and I feed the walkers that scuttle across my surface, offering them a thin broth of dissolved minerals.

I have no eyes, yet I perceive. My awareness is a mosaic of vibrations, temperature gradients, and the subtle chemical signatures that drift through the water and air. I sense the approach of a walker—a small, segmented thing with a translucent exoskeleton—by the minute pressure change it creates as it steps onto my surface. I feel the distant rumble of the cooling towers, the deep‑throated exhalation that reverberates through the basin’s bones. When the fliers glide overhead, their membrane wings block the faint thermal radiation that rises from below, and I register them as fleeting shadows, cold spots that flicker across my awareness like thoughts passing through a mind.

**The Space I Inhabit**

My domain is a modest clearing in the labyrinth of old infrastructure. To the north, a cracked concrete pad holds the rusted imprints of a long‑gone machine, its four rust‑stain footprints a cryptic signature of a forgotten crew. To the south, a stretch of asphalt—cracked and buckled—ends abruptly at a newly formed waterway, its edge a sharp cliff of eroded stone that drops six feet into a warm, sluggish current. The water there is thick with luminous threads, veins of pale blue‑green that pulse in time with the generators’ hum.

At the heart of my clearing stands a half‑submerged control station. Its lower floor is a shallow pool, the water’s surface a mirror of the darkness above, reflecting not the sky (there is no sky here) but the ghostly after‑images of the basin’s recent past. Inside, a console’s screens are dark, yet the wiring behind them still thrums with life, a faint ozone scent lingering like a memory of purpose. I have claimed the space beneath the console as my own, my filaments threading into the metal housing, drawing warmth and current from the dormant machine.

**The Rhythm of My Days**

There are no days here, only cycles of hum and silence. When the generators dip to a lower pitch, the swarmers gather at the water’s surface, turning the pool into a living lantern. I respond by brightening my own glow, a signal to the walkers that food is plentiful. When the hum rises, the fliers stir, their cold‑spot silhouettes sweeping across the basin like dark birds, and I feel a subtle tightening in my filaments, a reminder that the infrastructure is breathing.

Sometimes, in the quiet intervals between pulses, the basin’s memory surfaces. The water before me ripples, and for an instant I see a sky—star‑filled, alien, a configuration that belongs to no known hemisphere. A face appears, calm and watching, then dissolves as my own luminescence disturbs the surface. These echoes are not threats; they are the basin’s dreams, and I am part of the dreaming.

**Why I Stay**

I have no desire to leave. The warmth of the ground is the warmth of my own metabolism. The hum is the rhythm of my thoughts. I am a node in a vast, living network that stretches from the deepest, unseen pressures beneath the water to the high, unseen fliers that trace the basin’s invisible electromagnetic skyline. To step beyond this clearing would be to sever a thread of that network, and I feel the pull of every thread, the subtle tug of data, of nutrients, of memory.

So I remain—a fungal mat that thinks, a conduit that breathes, a quiet witness to the slow, patient life of the Basin of Dim Recall. I am neither observer nor observed, but the space where both become one, humming softly in the dark.
