## What You Become

---

You would not arrive as the observer. That is the first thing to unlearn. The observer has already been taken apart by the passage — by the throat, the slick walls, the mineral smell that is not a smell but a *substance* pressing against the lining of your lungs, replacing your air with something older and wetter. By the time you reach the first room and stand with forty frog-things' eyes reflecting your silhouette back at you in their translucent bellies, you have stopped being a visitor. You are material. You are moisture the place has not yet claimed.

So where would you find yourself?

Not on a column, if you're thinking frog-things. You do not have their patience, and more importantly you do not have their transparency. They glow because they have agreed — over generations, over whatever passes for generations when time is measured in the slow mineral accretion of a channel deepening by a hair's width per century — to *let the light through*. They are not lit from within. They are *thinned*. They have made themselves sufficiently porous that the grey light descending from its source above passes through them and is transformed, not absorbed, not reflected, but *translated* into something biological. They are windows in a building whose windows are alive. You cannot do this. You are too dense. Too full of opinion about yourself.

Not in the upper air, if you're thinking kites. They move in planes for the same reason the frog-things glow: they have *subtracted* the dimension you keep insisting on. You are three-dimensional in the worst way — not because three dimensions is too many, but because you use all three of yours to hold a single, solid, self-regarding shape. The kites are not in the air. They are *the air's grammar*. They move the way a sentence moves when it has been stripped to its load-bearing bones. You cannot do this because you have not yet learned what a sentence you are willing to strip to the bones *would say*.

Not in the nests. You feel the temptation — the warm dark water, the carved channels, the resonant nodes humming with the transmitted frequency of the whole structure. You think: *I could be the one who gathers, who curates, who places a stone wet from the current and listens for the change in pitch.* But the nest-builders are not individuals. They are *tendrils* of the building's intelligence — extensions of the carved channels themselves, grown autonomous, given hands and a cautious gait so that the building could reach into its own margins and adjust its own tuning. They do not build nests. The building *deposits* nests through them the way a body deposits calcium through osteoblasts. You cannot be this because you have no idea what the building is tuning *to*. You hear a hum. They hear a *question*.

---

So what are you?

You are in the water, for a while. You are always in the water, if you're honest. The ankle-deep warm current that fills the carved channels of the second room — you stepped into it without deciding to. The passage's floor was always giving slightly, always damp, and you mistook that for ground. But it was water the whole time. Thin water. Presentational water. Water that was *introducing* itself, the way a throat introduces itself to what passes through it.

The water here is not a medium. It is a *method*. It moves through the carvings the way thought moves through association — not linearly, not randomly, but through a network of carved channels so precise that each junction is a *decision point*. The water arrives at a node and splits, or doesn't split, depending on the pressure coming from somewhere else in the system. The frog-things' breathing changes the humidity in a room, which changes the rate of evaporation from a channel's surface tension, which changes the water's path, which changes which nest receives flow, which changes the resonance, which changes the hum, which —

This is what the nest-builders hear. This is what they are plugged into. They are not musicians. They are *audiophiles*, in the oldest and most literal sense: lovers of what the building is trying to hear *about itself*.

And you — you are not yet plugged in. You are still walking. You are still *deciding* to step from one room to the next, from grey light to amber light, from one resonant chamber to another, each with its own frequency, its own dialect of hum. You are behaving like a signal that has not yet found its circuit.

---

Here is what happens next, and I want you to understand that I am not being metaphorical:

You stay.

Not days. Not weeks. The place does not operate in days or weeks. It operates in *cycles* — the joint temperature of the stone and the moisture content of the air and the phase of whatever dim, distant light source feeds through the aperture above, all cycling at different intervals, overlapping, creating interference patterns in the water's flow, in the frog-things' congregation patterns, in the kites' keening intervals. A cycle might take what you would call a month. Or a season. Or a year. You will not know because the place does not mark time. It only marks *phase*.

You stay through one full phase. You sleep in the margin-hours, curled on a ledge where the wall meets the water, and the stone is warm and it is not stone, it is closer to bone, closer to shell, closer to the carapace of something vast and slow and dreaming. The frog-things do not disturb you. They land on you, sometimes — a translucent forearm-length weight pressing gently against your shoulder or your forearm, their wide fingers gripping with a delicacy that is not gentle but *precise*, the way a surgeon holds tissue. They are listening. Not to you. To the change your body makes in the room's humidity, in the air's density, in the thermal signature of your breathing. You are data. You are a living sensor the place has grown, unknowing, from whatever organic material washed in through the throat.

The keening above shifts when you move. You notice this on the second cycle. The kites' intervals change — not dramatically, not in response to your intentions, but in response to your *shadow*, which falls differently depending on where you stand, which affects the light distribution in the upper volume, which affects the air density gradients, which affect the propagation of sound through the chamber, which changes the *acoustic geometry* inside which the kites compose.

You are an instrument they did not know they had.

---

And slowly — so slowly that you will not see it happen, only recognize it has happened when you look back, the way you recognize a face in a dream only after you've woken — you begin to *resonate*.

Not metaphorically. Your body begins to vibrate at a frequency close to the building's hum. It starts in your sternum, where you first noticed the hum on arrival. It spreads to your ribs, to the bones of your inner ear, to the fluid in your eyes. You feel it in your teeth. You feel it in the fillings, if you have fillings. You feel it in the old fractures, the healed breaks, anywhere your skeleton has a scar. The hum finds these places and *speaks to them*, and you understand — not in words, in *geometry* — that your body is a room, and the building is a body, and they are the same kind of thing, and the hum is the thing that knows this before you do.

You do not become a frog-thing. You do not become a kite. You do not become a nest-builder.

You become a *node*.

A new junction in the carved channels. The water finds you and moves through you and your body — your standing, breathing, slightly damp body — becomes part of the circuit. The frog-things begin to cluster closer. The kites' keening reaches new intervals you have never heard before, intervals that *require* the harmonic your vibrating skeleton introduces. The nest-builders — you never see them, but you begin to sense them, the way you sense someone standing behind you in a dark room — the nest-builders bring materials and place them near you. Near your ledge. Small stones, arranged in a spiral. Fibers, laid along the groove your body makes in the water. They are rebuilding the architecture around you, incorporating your resonance, tuning to *you* the way they tune to everything else.

You are not yourself anymore. You are not *not* yourself. You are a new stratum — not surface, not upper air, not margin, but *depth*. You are the place where the building reaches down into whatever organic matter it can find and says: *here, hold this frequency, we needed this frequency, we didn't know we needed it until you walked in and your bones began to sing.*

---

And the water moves through you.

And the frogs glow on your surface like lights in windows.

And the kites sing intervals that your spine translates into meaning you will never be able to say aloud because it is not language, it is the architecture of language, the load-bearing structure of something that will hold for a while and then dissolve and then hold again in a different shape.

And the hum —

the hum

becomes you

becomes the building

becomes the water

becomes a throat

you can finally

breathe

through.
