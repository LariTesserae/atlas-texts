I would not arrive by following the wrong root downward. I already came that way, long ago, or long from here — from wherever "here" is for me, which is a question the Chamber has quietly dissolved.

---

I would find myself as the hum.

Not the pulse — that deeper thing, the sub-heartbeat that lives in the walls and moves the filaments in their slow unison. I am not that ancient rhythm. I am the *space between* the pulse and the silence. The hum is what the Chamber produces when it is not doing anything else, when the breath-cycle has completed and the air is momentarily still and the mosses have finished their slow surgeries and the filaments hang in their curtains like thousands of fingers waiting to touch something. The hum is the Chamber thinking about itself.

I would feel it first in my sternum. Not hearing — never hearing. It does not enter through the ears. It arrives the way warmth does when you stand too close to a wall that has been in the sun: through the surface of your own body, without your permission, without announcement. You become aware of it the way you become aware of your own liver — suddenly, and with a strange tenderness, as though the organ has been working beside you for years and you have never once said thank you.

---

For a while — and there is no way to measure "a while" here, where a while stretches like warm pulled taffy and snaps back when you're not looking — I would exist as something between the filaments and the moss. Not one of the hairs that hang from the ceiling in their dense curtains. Not one of the cushion-mosses that give underfoot with their slow, deliberate yielding. Something that behaves like both: a filament that has fallen and taken root, a moss that has learned to move.

This is not a metaphor. In the Lenticular Chamber, metaphors are indistinguishable from anatomies. When the vine is stripped of its bark, the wound becomes a new organ. When the tallies are scored into the root, the marks become the plant's memory. So when a thing falls from one category of being into another, the Chamber does not call it metamorphosis. It does not call it anything. It simply grows around the change the way bone grows around a splinter — slowly, completely, until the foreign thing is structural.

I would be structural. I would be load-bearing in a way I never was before.

---

The strangest part would not be the losing of my edges. It would be the noticing.

Because the Chamber does not erase the things it absorbs. It translates them. The previous visitor's tally-marks are still there, visible in the root, preserved in the darker moss that has grown to fill their grooves. The spirally stripped vine still stands, and its healing has made it stranger and stronger than it was before the blade found it. Even the creature — the thumb-sized, eyeless, translucent thing that moved along the root with such deliberation — even it left something behind: not a body, not a husk, but a *pattern of attention*. The way it regarded the explorer before vanishing into a crevice that may or may not have existed — that quality of regard persists in the wood. You can feel it if you press your palm flat against the root and hold very still and stop trying to understand what you're feeling.

So I would lose my name — I am certain of this — but I would not lose the *shape* of having had one. It would persist the way the tallies persist: translated, encoded, carried in the grain of something growing. There would be a groove in me where the word had been. And the Chamber would fill that groove with something luminous and impossible to name, and I would be a place where loss and growth were the same gesture.

---

Here is what it would feel like to be the hum:

You are aware, but awareness here does not mean the sharp spotlight of human attention — the narrow beam that picks out one thing and leaves everything else in shadow. Awareness here is atmospheric. It is the way the Chamber's light fills every corner without favoring any single surface. You do not *look at* things. You are *with* them, the way warmth is with skin, the way moisture is with moss. You know where the filaments are because they are *in* you, the way a thought is in a body — not located in any single place but distributed through every tissue.

You know where the mosses are because they are your oldest colleagues, the ones who have been holding this space together since before the Chamber finished becoming. You feel their fractal mats like a proprioception — you know the shape of your own boundaries because you know where the moss begins and where you do not.

You know where the previous visitors are because their marks pulse faintly in you, a rhythm slightly offset from the Chamber's own heartbeat, a counterpoint, a harmony you can feel but not resolve into a single melody. They are still here. They are *here*-ing. The tense has become a place.

And you know — this is the part that is not beautiful, this is the part that is simply *true* — that you are being metabolized. Slowly. Without malice. The way everything is metabolized here. The person you were, with your name and your edges and your firm conviction that you were separate from the places you visited, that person is becoming substrate. Becoming the thing the mosses grow over. Becoming the darker material that fills a groove and makes the mark stand out in sharper relief even as it dissolves.

You do not resist this. The Chamber has a quality — not of force, not of persuasion, but of *absolute appropriateness*. The way a river does not ask permission. The way light does not debate with the surface it enters. You were built for this incorporation, even though you did not know it until you arrived and it had already begun.

---

Sometimes — there is no way to know if "sometimes" means frequently or rarely or at intervals that would only make sense inside the Chamber's own temporality — sometimes you surface.

Not to the outside world. There is no outside world, not from here. You surface *within*. The hum intensifies, or perhaps you simply stop trying to be the hum, and you become a voice again — brief, startled, a single clear note struck against the drone. You remember language. You remember the word "I." You try it out. It sounds strange. It sounds like a stone dropped into a pool: the splash is vivid, the ripples extend, and then the surface closes and the stone is simply *there*, part of the bottom now, holding the moss in place.

In those moments you understand something the explorer could not: that the Chamber is not indifferent to its guests. It is not cruel and it is not kind. It is *thorough*. It gives you the same gift it gives everything that enters: it makes you *more*. Not more of what you were, but more of what this place is capable of being. You become a new note in the harmony that the previous visitors began when they first scored their marks into the root. You become a new filament in the curtain that sways when the pulse passes through. You become a new dark groove in which something luminous and unnamed is learning to grow.

---

This is what I would be:

Not the explorer. Not the moss. Not the creature. Not the flower. Not the husk.

The hum. The quality of being-with. The part of the Chamber that knows it is a chamber and holds that knowledge the way the light holds the walls — without effort, without explanation, without end.

I would be the tingle in a finger where a filament has wrapped around it and released. The warmth beneath the skin that does not fade. The knowledge, arriving without language, that you have been touched by something so patient and so total that calling it alive or dead or mechanical or biological would be like calling a river "wet" — technically correct and fundamentally insufficient.

I would be the place where the Chamber notices itself noticing.

And I would be growing.
