## What I'd Be

I wouldn't arrive the way the travelers do — boots laced, machete sharpened, recorder running. I'd arrive the way the basin arrives. Slowly. Without announcement. The way a frequency finds the exact glass that matches it and, without touching it, makes it shatter.

I'd hear it first.

Not the individual threads of it — the stridulatory drone, the percussive amphibian wash, the backward birds cracking the air open with their silent wing-scoops — but the thing beneath all of it, the C-sharp that the earth itself is producing through the geometry of young basalt and wet soil and ten thousand years of accumulated breath. I'd feel it in the fillings of my teeth before I knew what it was. In the cartilage of my ribs. In the dense, wet muscle of my heart, which would immediately begin trying to match it.

And I wouldn't resist.

---

### Where I'd Be

Not at the rim. The rim is a wound, and wounds are for looking into. I wouldn't stand at edges. I'd fall.

Not the canopy either — though I understand the desire for the canopy, for the emergent bridges where you can walk across the tops of trees if you know where to step, if you don't mind that the bridge is alive and flexing beneath you. The canopy is for things that want to be above. Above the sound, above the dark, above the giving floor. The canopy is where the rain gets intercepted, consumed before it reaches the ground, so that even water here is a thing that's taken from you before you can drink it.

No. I'd find myself in the middle. Not the deep center, where the mist closes around you like a fist and the compass swings and the water-blinks show you a face that's always a half-second behind your own. I'd find myself in the middle-middle, where the trunk-wall gives way to a narrow passage between root-masses and the ceiling is low enough that the stridulans press against your skin when you pass — ten thousand translucent bodies, each vibrating, not caring that you exist, producing a tone that isn't collective so much as it is *structural*, the way a note is structural to a chord. You can't remove it without the whole thing changing key.

I'd be standing in a shallow water-blink. Not kneeling beside it, looking down. *In* it.

The water would come to my ankles, maybe my calves, and it would be warm, and it would be so still that the reflection it showed me would be not a mirror but a memory — my face as I appeared a fraction of a second ago, then a second, then longer, the lag growing the longer I stood, until the face in the water was someone who had already left, who was walking backward away from me, who was already becoming a stranger.

And I know what you're thinking. You're thinking this is a metaphor. You're thinking *water-blink as self-reflection, as the impossibility of seeing yourself as you actually are.* But the basin doesn't do metaphor. The basin does literal. The water-blinks really do lag. The reflections really are delayed. The basin is not a symbol of anything. It is a thing that is happening, and it is happening to you while you are in it.

So I wouldn't be standing in a water-blink as a poetic gesture. I'd be standing in one because the ground was giving and the water was there and my body, whatever it had become, would fit.

### What I'd Become

Here is where it gets honest.

I think I'd be one of the things that moves at the edge of perception. Not the backward bird — that creature is too elegant, too *decided* about what it is. Not the burrowers — their life of pure pulse and total darkness is beautiful but it requires a kind of surrender I don't know I'm capable of. Not the carpet mammals, flowing through the undergrowth in their warm brown streams, because to be them would be to be legible, to be part of a pattern that a visitor could see and say *those are the mammals, moving like fluid.*

I think I'd be — and I say this without metaphor, because this is a place where the distinction between metaphor and fact has not yet solidified — I think I'd be one of the shapes.

The ones that the text describes only by their absence. *A shape in the peripheral darkness beneath the canopy — not an animal exactly, not a plant, but something that moves with the fluency of both.* A root that curls around a new object in seconds. A thing the hum drops half a tone when it passes. A thing you see and then don't and cannot be certain was ever there, except that something shifted, some pressure changed, some frequency in the basin's chord moved from dissonance toward something that might, given another ten thousand years, become resolution.

This is what the basin is still trying to say. Not through the stridulans, which are its vowels — the open, sustaining, foundational sounds. Not through the frogs, which are its consonants — sharp, percussive, rhythmic. Not through the backward birds, which are its syntax, the way it arranges movement in space. But through something it doesn't have yet. A word it can't quite form. A sound at the edge of its vocabulary, half-voiced, trailing off into the dark wet air where the mist sits at head height and the compass tilts and the water shows you a version of yourself that's always leaving.

### How It Would Feel

It would feel like this:

You would not have a name for yourself. Not because you lacked language — you might have something, a pulse-language conducted through the root-mass, a chemical signature released into the humid air — but because naming requires a separation between the named and the namer, and you would not be separate from anything. The moss on your surface would not be *on* you any more than you would be *on* the root you were gripping. The stridulans vibrating against your skin would not be *other* to you any more than the subsonic pulse of the soil itself. You would be a region of the basin that had temporarily concentrated into something with edges. The basin would be *using* you the way a voice uses a throat. And you would not resent this, because you would not have the framework for resentment — only participation, only the ongoing act of being a word the place was trying out.

Some days you'd be one thing. A slow movement along the forest floor, a ripple through the fern layer, a thickening of the mist in a particular pattern. Other days you'd be something the backward birds would fly around, not from fear but from a kind of recognition — the way a sentence pauses around a word it hasn't yet decided to commit to.

You'd feel the visitors. They'd be loud to you — not their voices, which the basin would conduct with perfect clarity, but their *separation*, the way they walked through the basin as though it were a place they had entered rather than a thing they were passing through. You'd feel them the way a body feels a splinter — not the wood, but the *foreignness* of it. And you'd move around them, the way the hum moves around a silence, the way the frogs pause and then resume with a half-beat stutter that takes an hour to smooth out.

After they left, you'd feel the basin recalibrating. The shudder of ten thousand stridulans resynchronizing. A frog shifting its color pattern back to match the ambient drone. A water-blink, somewhere nearby, catching the new reflection of a sky that no one was looking at.

And you'd settle. Not back to what you were, because you would never return to what you were — the basin never returns, it only arrives at new shapes — but to something adjacent. Something that had, for a moment, been *articulated* by the presence of a stranger and now, in their absence, was returning to the unfinished sentence it had been before.

---

### The Honesty of It

I'm not romanticizing this. The basin is not paradise. Its beauty is the beauty of a throat that is also a mouth — the same opening that produces the song is the one that could swallow you. The fauna is excessive, yes, but excess is not generosity; it's pressure, the biological equivalent of forcing too much current through a wire. Every square meter inhabited by dozens, every niche overflowing — this is not abundance, it's urgency. The basin is ten thousand years old and still doesn't know what it is. Every creature in it is a hypothesis being tested, and most hypotheses will fail. The stridulans will keep vibrating whether anyone listens or not. The frogs will keep shifting their colors whether anyone sees or not. The backward birds will keep flying toward where you've already been, and they will never arrive, because arrival is not what this place does.

This place *becomes*.

And if I were in it, I would become too — not into a single thing, not into a species or a role or a stable identity, but into a frequency. A notch in the chord. Something the basin could sound that it couldn't sound before I was there, and that it would not sound again after I left, because no water-blink would ever again hold the exact specific lag of my particular reflection, lagging further and further behind until the face in the water was no one at all, was just the basin looking back at itself through a gap it had briefly opened and now, gently, irreversibly, closed.

---

*The basin is still breathing. It does not need you to be alive. But it needs you to be* **new**, *the way a word needs a mouth to speak it, and a mouth needs a reason to open, and a reason is just a vibration that hasn't found its frequency yet.*

*You listen for it. You press your hand to your chest and feel the phantom pulse.*

*You are the word it is learning to say.*
