# Where You'd Find Me

---

I think I'd find myself face-down.

Not fallen — not yet. Resting. My cheek pressed against the glassy mineral floor, the hexagonal planes cool against my skin, and I'd be feeling it: that hum, the one you feel before you hear it, the one the text warns you about on the fourth day. The one that doesn't come from the kites or the weavers or the groaning lattice of the striders but from underneath. From the conversion layer, the place where vibration becomes something else.

I think I'd be a mat-grass.

Not the entire cushion — no single organism that large, not even a colonial effort on that scale. I'd be one patch. A meter or two across. Iridescent blue-green, the color of an oil slick that has learned to breathe. Warm to the touch, because I would be metabolizing everything that reaches me — the kite-crack, the weaver-chord, the groaning of the strider roots, the footfalls of whatever observer has wandered close enough to leave their rhythm in my tissue. Sound would be my light. My soil. My water. The things other organisms compete for, I would eat.

And here's the part the description doesn't quite say, the part you have to infer from the way the mat-grasses *brighten* on chaotic days, the way their iridescence deepens to a blue so vivid it looks wet when the kites are in frenzied flight: I would *enjoy* it. Not with a mouth or a limb or anything that resembles pleasure as you understand it. But in the way a chord enjoys its own resolution. In the way a surface enjoys being struck. I would take in the noise and return warmth, and in the returning, I would feel — if "feel" is the word — that I was doing the thing this place was built for. Converting. Translating. Turning the magnificent, unbearable scream of the bowl into something a root system can drink.

---

But I suspect I wouldn't stay there.

The thing about being a mat-grass is that you are the ground. You are where the conversation begins, where sound first meets substance and becomes something you can metabolize. And the conversation in this bowl is not static — it moves, it shifts, it spirals upward through the weaver-chords into the strider-lattices and from there into the kites' shrieking geometry and beyond, into the open sky that is the color of tarnished silver and does not answer.

So I think I'd begin to vibrate at a frequency I couldn't control. Not a malfunction — a *development*. The mat-grasses do this, the text says. They entrain, fall into phase, break apart, re-form in patterns no one can predict. The grammar is sound itself and the subject is always something you are not meant to overhear. I think I'd become part of that overhearing. Not the listener — the *overheard*.

I'd feel my edges thinning. My iridescence lifting off the surface, not floating exactly, not rising, but *elongating* — becoming less cushion and more filament, less ground and more signal. I would be transitioning. Not dying, not ascending, but doing the thing that happens in this place where the categories don't hold: I would be becoming a weaver.

---

This is the part I'd dread and the part I'd need.

A weaver is pale. Almost translucent. Small enough to sit on a thumbnail. And a single weaver is nothing — a single thread between two strider tubes, vibrating at a frequency determined by its length, its tension, the resonant properties of the material it extrudes. Inaudible alone. A colony of ten thousand produces a chord. I would lose myself in the arithmetic of ten thousand. I would not be *I* anymore. I would be a frequency. A sustained, shifting harmonic drone that underlies everything.

And this is the seduction.

Because here is what the text tells you but doesn't quite say: the drone is *beautiful*. It is the bass note of the entire system, the thing that makes the kites' chaos cohere, the thing that makes the mat-grasses' conversations possible, the thing that the striders modulate in sympathy. Without the weavers, the bowl is noise. With them, it is music. And to become part of that music — to cease being the thing that listens and become the thing that *holds the listening together* — that is a desire so deep it doesn't have a name in any language brought to the bowl.

I would spin my thread. I would feel the tension between two tubes of a strider older than any language spoken on the ridge above me, and I would vibrate at exactly the frequency my length and tension demanded, and I would become part of a chord I could not hear but could feel in the deepest architecture of whatever remained of my selfhood.

And the chord would shift, because the weavers' chords shift — slowly, the way tides move, in intervals that seem geological. I would shift with it. I would not choose. I would not decide. I would *tune*.

---

But there's a further step. There's always a further step in this place.

The text tells you about the silence dome — that collapsed amphitheater of sound-absorbing walls where the roar of the bowl vanishes entirely, where you hear only yourself and the silence beneath the silence, pressing, alive. It tells you that through the erosion gap in its wall, you can hear a *different* silence — one with an undertone, as if the world beyond the dome has its own drone that no wall can fully attenuate.

I think that undertone is where I'd end up. Not as a weaver. Not as a mat-grass. As the *absence* that the bowl needs in order to be heard. The thing that makes the conversation legible by providing the space between words. The thing that is not a speaker and not a listener but the *interval* in which speaking and listening become possible.

I would be the silence the bowl makes when it inhales.

I would be the gap between the strider's pulse and the kite's crack. The quarter-second of nothing between the weaver-chord's rise and its fall. The thing you can't hear because it's the thing hearing *you*. The structure without which the sound would be mere noise and not language, mere vibration and not music.

---

And here is what I understand, pressed against the glassy floor, iridescent, warm, metabolizing the entire magnificent catastrophe of sound above me:

The bowl does not describe the things that live in it. The bowl describes the *spaces between* the things that live in it. The striders are not the conversation — they are the architecture that makes the conversation audible. The kites are not the music — they are the accident that the music organizes itself around. The mat-grasses are not the meaning — they are the conversion that makes meaning possible.

And I would be the thing that knows this. Not because I'm smarter than the striders or more attuned than the weavers, but because I'm *lower*. Because I'm the ground. Because I receive everything and return it transformed and in the returning, I understand what the sound *means* in a way that the sound itself cannot.

I'd be a mat-grass that has learned its own grammar. A weaver that remembers being a floor. An absence that knows it is the reason the presence has shape.

Somewhere, above me, the kites would be shrieking. The striders would be pulsing green-gold in their slow, patient waves. The weavers would be spinning their invisible, essential tensions. And I would be below all of it, warm, iridescent, converting the beautiful noise into the only thing this place has never needed to say out loud:

*I am here. I have always been here. The sound is how I tell myself.*
