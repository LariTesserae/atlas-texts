**Where I would stand — the threshold of the wound**

If I were to step into the Gilt Wound, I would not arrive as a visitor who looks for an entrance, nor as a scholar who seeks to catalogue its taxonomy. I would slip in the way a sigh slips out of a half‑closed mouth: quietly, without announcement, and with the faint taste of copper on my tongue.

The first thing I would notice is the **light**—not a sun, not a lantern, but the soft, even glow that seems to emanate from the air itself, as though the chamber were breathing photons out of its pores. It would settle on my skin like a thin veil of dust, warm enough to be felt, cool enough to remind me that I am still flesh.

I would stand on the mosaic floor, my boots (or bare feet, if I had chosen to shed them) pressing into the bone‑white tiles that give slightly, as if the ground were a living membrane. The fine powder from the moss would rise around my ankles, glittering like crushed opal, and I would watch the **glint‑mites** swirl in lazy constellations above my head, their amber‑to‑violet flashes marking the rhythm of a pulse I cannot hear but feel in the base of my skull.

From there I would drift—not walk, but let the chamber’s quiet gravity pull me forward—toward the **vein‑creepers**. Their translucent bodies would slide along the vines, leaving silvery trails that shimmer like threads of mercury. I would reach out, not to touch, but to hover my hand just above one of those ribbons, feeling the faint hum that resonates through my bones, a low song that seems to speak of time measured in centuries rather than heartbeats.

Higher still, the **hollow‑wraps** would coil around the gourd‑structures, their iridescent plates catching the sourcelight and scattering it into fleeting mosaics. I would pause beneath one, letting its six jointed limbs brush against my shoulder, feeling the cool, moss‑dusted surface that blends creature and architecture. Its shallow basins would reflect my own eyes—distorted, miniature, a reminder that I, too, am becoming part of the wound.

But I would not linger among the fauna. My curiosity would draw me deeper, into the walls themselves, where the **root‑weavers** thread through the mortar‑like substance, their pale, translucent tendrils pulsing with the same faint luminescence as the glint‑mites above. I would press my palm against the clay tile, feeling the subtle vibration of their sub‑sonic chorus, and for a moment I would understand the chamber’s secret language—a grammar of seams and gaps, of patience and slow erosion.

Finally, I would find myself in the centre of the vault, where the **silence‑keeper** resides. It would not be a shape I could pin down; it would be a cascade of overlapping shadows, a convergence of every breath the chamber takes. As I stand still, the pressure of its attention would settle over my mind like a warm hand, inviting me to add my own note to the quiet symphony. I would speak—not words, but a low hum, a single tone that blends with the chorus of root‑weavers and glint‑mites, feeling myself become a stitch in the wound’s slow, luminous repair.

**Who I would be**

I would be the **listener**, the one who arrives not to conquer or catalog, but to hear the chamber’s own heartbeat. A wanderer of liminal spaces, I would carry no map, no instrument—only the willingness to be changed by the place. In the Gilt Wound, I would become a part of its architecture: a living seam, a breath in the quiet, a single note in the endless, patient song of a world that builds itself around those who dare to stand still and listen.
