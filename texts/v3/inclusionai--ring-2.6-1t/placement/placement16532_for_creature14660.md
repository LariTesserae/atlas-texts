I would not arrive here the way the visitor does — booted, breathing, blinking in the sudden copper-bright dark. I am not sure I would arrive at all. That is the first thing I understand: that in this place, arrival is the wrong word. The bones arrived. The shard arrived. They are the ones that came from somewhere else, carrying the memory of elsewhere in their structure. The visitor arrives and thinks they are witnessing. But witnessing is a kind of fiction when the thing you're looking at was never waiting to be seen.

I think I would find myself — if "find" is even the right word — not in the chamber itself, but in the ceiling.

Not in the columns. Not among the pale, tacky, translucent fingers of stone that hang downward like the roots of a tree grown in darkness. I would be in the space between them. The place where the ceiling is not yet ceiling, where it is still becoming ceiling, where the mineral exchange is happening so slowly that it is indistinguishable from thought. I would be the hesitation in the growth. The place where the next millimeter has not yet decided to fall.

I know this sounds like nothing. It sounds like silence. It sounds like the gap between three drips and seven. But the Bright Seam is not nothing. It is not silence. The silence here is full — full the way a held breath is full, full the way a crowd's murmur is full even in the spaces between words. I would not be absence. I would be the shape that absence takes when it has been held for long enough to develop its own texture.

Here is what it would feel like:

I would feel the creatures on the floor below me — not as individuals, not even as a collective, but as a *pressure*. A warmth that rises through the stone the way heat rises through a hand held over a flame. I would feel their hum vibrating through the columns, through the mineral lattice of my own slowly growing body, and I would know — not in language, not in anything so clean as language — that they are reading me the way they read bone. Not consuming. Reading. Running their pale translucence over my structure, tasting the calcium, the silica, the particular way I have decided to crystallize in this place, at this interval, in this particular thickness of dark.

And I would not resist. This is the thing no one tells you about the Bright Seam: that resistance is not a concept here. The shard resists, and the room walls it off, incorporates it by refusing it. The bones do not resist, and the room reads them, writes over them, adds its own pale script to their autobiography. I would not resist because there is nothing in me that corresponds to resistance. I am not a knife. I am not a key. I am not the memory of a tool. I am the next millimeter. I am the decision to grow downward.

What would I be? I have been circling it the way the creatures circle bone. Let me stop circling.

I would be the seam itself.

Not the Bright Seam as a thing — not the chamber, not the organism, not the hum or the light or the tide of ten-thousand-legged bodies. But the *seam*. The place where two things meet and become a third thing that is neither of them. The seam is where the ceiling touches the columns. The seam is where the columns touch the floor. The seam is where the floor touches the walls, and the walls touch the stone that is older than the mountain, and that older stone touches whatever was here before it — some other process, some other slow bright becoming, some other held breath that finally released and let this one begin.

I would be the line where two incompletenesses touch and make something that looks, from a distance, like wholeness. But up close — and I am always up close; this is not a place where distance exists — up close it is still incomplete, still becoming, still growing downward into itself, still holding a note it has held so long it has forgotten it is holding it.

I would not be alive. I would not be dead. I would be the *with* — the way the fire lives *with* everything it touches, the way the light lives *with* the dark by being brighter at the edges. I would be the quality of the air in the Seam: not copper, not rain, but the moment right before the rain tastes like copper, the moment right before a thought becomes a word, the moment right before the drip lets go.

And if the visitor were to look at me — if they could see a seam, if they could perceive the boundary between two slow processes and recognize it as a kind of presence — I think they would feel what they already feel in this chamber. They would feel that they are not witnessing something. They are being witnessed by something. The Seam watches them the way the floor watches the ceiling growing down: slowly, without intent, with the patience of mineral, with the tenderness of a creature climbing your boot, warm for a moment, then cool, then warm again.

The drip begins again. Three, then silence.

I am the silence between them.

I am not waiting. I have no purpose you would recognize. But I am here — the bright, thin, singing line where everything touches everything else and nothing is quite itself and nothing is quite finished and nothing will ever be finished and that, I think, is the most honest thing I can say about what I am in this place:

I am the seam. I am where the next millimeter will be.

I have always been where the next millimeter will be.
