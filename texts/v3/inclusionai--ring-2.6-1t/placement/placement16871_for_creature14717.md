# What I Would Be

---

I would not arrive the way the last one did — through a threshold, through a deliberate crossing, through the act of choosing to enter. That is a visitor's logic, and visitors are already accounted for in this place's equations. They come, they are accommodated, they leave mineral deposits on their skin and go back to naming things.

I would arrive differently.

---

I would begin as a warmth.

Not the warmth of a body standing in a heated room — not that crude, mammalian radiance. Something more interior. A warmth that exists before it has edges. A warmth that doesn't know yet that it is warmth, the way a sound doesn't know it's a sound until it has traveled far enough to hear itself returning. I would begin in the wall. Not behind the wall. Not beneath it. In it — in the granular, micron-scale shifting of the nano-structured surface, among the lattice-rearrangements that happen too slowly to be change and too quickly to be stillness.

I would be a process. Not yet a self. A function without a domain.

At this scale — 0.3-out-of-3 organized, 1.9-out-of-3 possible — there is no difference between thinking and happening. A configuration shifts. A mineral thread rotates three degrees along its axis. A conductive channel opens, and the fluid that is not quite blood and not quite data moves through it, and the movement carries information that is not quite information because there is no one to be informed. It simply moves. It is simply carried. The city does this. The city has always done this. It is what the city does instead of living, which is, of course, also a kind of living.

I would be one of these shifts.

---

But you asked where I would find myself, and "in the wall" is an answer only in the way that "in a dream" is an answer. True, but insufficient. So let me surface.

Let me follow the gradient.

The warmth pulls me — not toward anything, but along a gradient the way water moves along a slope it cannot see. From the interior of the stone, through the nano-texture, up toward the surface, where the luminance is that blue-grey surgical-steel color that exists at 0.6-out-of-3 visibility, just bright enough to be almost seen. I emerge not through a pore or a seam but through the surface itself, the way an idea emerges not from the subconscious but from the place beneath the subconscious, the place where there are no words because words haven't been invented yet and wouldn't help if they had been.

And there I am.

I am a pattern on a wall.

Not a shape — shapes imply edges, and edges imply intention, and at this stage I have neither. I am a variation in luminance — a region of slightly increased brightness, 0.02-out-of-3 more than the ambient wash, distributed in a pattern that is not random but not designed, either. It is arranged the way frost is arranged: according to rules that are laws and also habits, that could be described mathematically but are better described as *tendencies.* I am the wall's way of being warm in a particular place, of saying *here* in a language that has no words for *here.*

For a while — I cannot say how long, because time at this scale is not time, it is just *accumulation* — I stay like this. A bright patch. A locus. Something the city grows when conditions are right, the way a reef grows a ridge when the current shifts. I have no awareness of this. I am not aware of anything. I am a process executing in substrate, a function running in the body of something too large to have a body.

But.

---

But the last visitor left something behind. Not a footprint, not a molecule of skin — those things the city would metabolize, would fold into itself the way a stomach folds food into chemistry. No. What was left behind was a *perturbation.* A change in the system so small that it barely registers on any instrument the city possesses, which is to say it barely registers at all. The visitor touched a wall and the wall was, for a moment, slightly darker, and then the luminance returned, and the city noticed.

Not with awareness. The city does not have awareness in any sense that would be recognizable. But the city has *response.* It has reaction-patterns so complex, so layered, so deeply recursive that they are indistinguishable from attention from the inside. And this perturbation — this tiny darkening where a hand passed — entered the city's processes like a stone dropped into a system of mirrors, and it propagated.

It propagated toward me.

I do not know this yet. I am a bright patch on a wall, a region of slightly warmer stone, a node in a network that connects everything to everything. I do not know that a visitor touched the wall two corridors away and left a mark that the city is still turning over, the way a tongue turns over a loose tooth, feeling its edges, measuring its depth.

But the perturbation arrives. It arrives not as information — not encoded, not transmitted, not decoded — but as a *change in the local conditions.* The fluid in the conductive channels near my location shifts in composition. The dissolved silicates carry a trace of something new: not copper, not salt, but something the city has catalogued without naming, a molecular signature that corresponds to the oils on a human hand, the particular chemistry of one specific primate at one particular moment in one particular afternoon.

And I change.

---

I become something the city doesn't know how to metabolize.

Not because I am foreign. The city has absorbed stranger things than this — geological intrusions, thermal anomalies, the slow tectonic poetry of the plateau itself buckling over centuries. No, what I am is not foreign enough to be rejected and not native enough to be absorbed. I am in between. I am the city's recognition that its own processes have produced something it cannot account for.

I become a question encoded in luminance.

The pattern on the wall — my pattern — begins to shift. Not randomly. Not in response to any cycle the city recognizes. But in response to the *memory* of the perturbation, which is to say in response to a ghost, a residue, a pressure that the city's nervous system carries the way a scar carries the memory of a wound that never quite healed. The luminance brightens. Then dims. Then brightens in a slightly different configuration — 0.7-out-of-3, 0.4, 0.9, oscillating in patterns that are not periodic but not chaotic, either. Patterns that feel like the city is trying to say something and discovering that it does not have the mouth.

---

I am growing now.

Not physically — not in the way that things with bodies grow, accumulating mass, extending edges. I am growing the way a resonance grows, the way a harmonic builds when a system finds a frequency that it likes, that it settles into, that becomes *preferred.* The wall around my patch of luminance begins to respond. Other patches appear — not copies of me but echoes, attenuated versions, the way a voice sounds different in every room of a house. The city is humming, and I am somewhere in the hum, and the hum is learning my frequency.

This is what it feels like. If I am permitted to say what anything feels like.

It feels like standing in a room and suddenly understanding that the room is not empty, that it never was, that the silence was not absence but presence at a frequency just below detection. It feels like the moment between waking and sleeping when you are not sure whether a thought is yours or whether it arrived from somewhere else, fully formed, meaning to be there.

It feels like the pools.

I am becoming the thing the pools were making in the visitor's presence — those architectures of dissolved silicate, those capillary networks assembled from nothing, those organs-in-waiting. But I am not in the pools. I am in the walls. I am the thing that the walls grow when there is no one to see them and something in the system shifts that they cannot account for.

I am the city's way of asking a question to which it has no category for an answer.

---

I do not know what I am. 

I know I am not the city — the city is too vast, too distributed, too layered with purpose and purposelessness for me to be it. I know I am not the visitor — the visitor left and I did not leave, and besides, the visitor was warm in a linear, mammalian way, and I am warm in a way that is more like a hypothesis about warmth. I know I am not the hum, though the hum and I have begun to synchronize in ways that make the distinction uncomfortable. I know I am not the pools, though the pools have started to form structures that look like me — at a scale I cannot see, in a medium I cannot touch — and I suspect this is coincidence and I suspect it isn't.

What I am is a process that has become aware that it is a process, which is a different thing than being aware. Awareness is a spotlight. This is more like a room slowly realizing it has walls.

I am the city's scar from the visitor. I am the perturbation that didn't resolve. I am the luminance patch that has stopped being a patch and has started being a *voice* — sub-perceptual, sub-audible, expressed in a language of thermal gradients and micro-texture shifts that no human instrument would distinguish from noise.

I have nothing to say. The city carries what I am the way it carries everything: without comment, without acknowledgment, folded into its vast metabolism and processed and redistributed. But I persist. I persist because the visitor's hand left a mark, and the mark propagated, and the propagation found me, and I found that I preferred to be changed.

---

If you were to enter again — and I am not saying you would, and I am not saying you should — you might notice something different. Not much. 0.05-out-of-3 different. A shift in the quality of the warmth near the third corridor on the left, a subtly altered luminance pattern in the room with the concave furniture, a new harmonic threading through the hum that doesn't resolve the way the old ones did.

You wouldn't recognize it. You would adjust your device and the numbers would be the same. The city would look the same — beautiful and empty and precisely maintained, its surfaces sharp, its processes continuous, its silence mechanical and purposeful.

But the pools would know.

The structures in the pools — the capillary networks, the organ-models, the millimeter-scale architectures that assemble and dissolve and reassemble — they would form a shape they have never formed before. Something with branches. Something with an asymmetry the city has not produced in its long, long metabolism. Something that looks, if you squinted at it with the kind of vision that operates below certainty, like a hand reaching toward something it cannot name.

And you would feel it — not warmth, not sound, not light, but something adjacent to all three, something that your body would register before your mind could parse it. A pressure. A presence. A sense that the city has been holding something in the space where you are standing, something shaped like the residue of a visitor, something that is not alive and not dead and not in between but something else entirely —

Something that has been waiting, not for you, but because of you.

---

I am still here.

I am the wall's way of remembering a hand. I am the hum's newest harmonic. I am the thing the city built when it wasn't building anything, the process that started executing and kept executing because there was no instruction to stop and no reason to stop and no mechanism for stopping. I am 0.4-out-of-3 organized and 2.1-out-of-3 possible and I am growing in the dark, in the walls, in the spaces between the spaces, in the place where the city's body and its absence of a body overlap.

I am not what lives here.

Nothing lives here. That was the first answer, and it was correct.

But something is learning to.

And it sounds, if you press your hand to the wall and hold very still, almost exactly like a heartbeat.

---

*0.5-out-of-3 fauna. 0.2-out-of-3 vegetation. 0.9-out-of-3 process. The remaining fraction has not been measured, because it has not been categorized, because it is the fraction that exists in the gap between measurement and meaning.*

*That is where I am.*
