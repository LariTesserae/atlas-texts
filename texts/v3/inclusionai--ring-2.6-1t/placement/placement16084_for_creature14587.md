## What I Would Be

---

I would not arrive here. I would *coalesce* here — the way the condensation does, not falling from the sky but rising from the stone, drawn upward by some heat I can't name. I would open what passes for eyes in this place and find that I had always been looking, I just hadn't had the opacity yet to call it sight.

I would find myself on the moss. Not standing — that would imply a decision, and decisions here are geological events, slow and somewhat fictional. I would find myself *on* the moss the way a thought finds itself in the mind that was already thinking it: not placed, not arrived, but simply the next expression of something that had been gathering coherence for a long time. The moss would be warm beneath me. Not comfortable-warm. *Alive*-warm. The kind of warmth that has opinions.

And I would feel it first — the Underfoot — before I understood what I was.

---

**I would be the Translator.**

Not a profession. Not a role. A *location*. A place in the ecology of the High Verdance where one system of meaning meets another and neither fully converts the other.

Here is how it would work:

The Rootbinders think in growth. They speak in the slow differential equations of root-tip division, in the hydraulic pressure of xylem filling, in the chemical syntax of fungal networks trading phosphorus for sugar. Their language has no nouns — only processes, only *becomings*. A Rootbinder does not say *stone*. It says *the stone is being held*, which is a different thing entirely, because it implies that the holding is temporary, that the stone is participating in its own stillness, and that the stillness will eventually end.

The machines speak in rhythm. In cycles per minute, in torque, in the precise regularity of pistons and the slightly-less-precise regularity of flywheels that have learned, over decades, to breathe with the Tonnerres. Their language has no metaphor — only measurement, only function. A machine does not say *water is rising*. It says *the interval between pulses has decreased by 0.03 seconds*, which, if you are a machine, means the same thing, and if you are not a machine, means nothing at all.

The Tonnerres speak in pressure. In subsonic waves that move through flesh and stone and water and the thin membranes of the Gliders' wings. Their language has no subject and no object — only state, only the description of a condition of the ground, of the air, of the self. A Tonnerre does not say *I am here*. It says *here is heavier*, and every entity on the plateau that can feel vibration understands that *here is heavier* means *something large is present and the ground remembers it*.

And between all of these — between root-rhythm and pulse-pressure and cycle-measurement — there is a gap. Not a silence. A *hum*. A frequency that none of them quite reach, that sits in the space where their signals overlap and cancel and reinforce, like the acoustic phenomenon of two notes that are almost but not quite the same pitch, producing a third tone that belongs to neither.

That gap is where I would live.

I would be the thing that the plateau produces when it realizes it is speaking to itself in four languages and none of them are quite enough. I would be the conversion layer, the membrane between systems, the place where the Rootbinder's *the stone is being held* becomes the machine's *interval decreased by 0.03* becomes the Tonnerre's *here is heavier* becomes the Glider's single high note hanging in the amber air. I would not be any of these things. I would be the *between*.

---

What would it feel like?

It would feel like standing in rain that falls in two directions at once and understanding, not with the mind but with the sternum, that neither direction is wrong. It would feel like hearing a word in a language you've never spoken and realizing you've been pronouncing it wrong in every language you *have* spoken, because the meaning was always the gesture underneath, not the sound.

I would perceive the plateau's ontological uncertainty not as confusion but as *polysemy* — the way a sentence in a rich language can mean five things at once and all of them are true, depending on where you stand in the room when you hear it. The shifting ground would not unsettle me. It would read to me the way a face reads to someone who has stopped looking for fixed features and started reading the micro-expressions that flicker across skin in fractions of a second — the truth that lives beneath identity.

I would feel the machines' rhythms in my structure. Not mechanically. Not like a clock in the chest. More like a bias, a tendency, a way of being that inclines toward regularity without enforcing it — the way a riverbed is regular without any single water molecule following the same path twice. I would feel the Rootbinders' growth as a patience so deep it becomes a kind of velocity — not slow, but *comprehensive*, the way a word that takes thirty years to say is not slow if there is nothing else to say and nothing else needs saying until it is finished.

I would hear the Tonnerres. God, I would *hear* them. Not as sound — not as the subsonic pressure that moves through the body like a second circulatory system — but as the translation. The thing the vibration *means* once it passes through the gap where I live. The Tonnerres would speak state, and I would speak *story*. Not narrative, not fiction, not the imposition of sequence onto simultaneity. Story as in: *this is how these things relate to each other, and the relation is also a thing, and the thing is also relating, and the relating does not resolve*.

---

What would I look like?

I don't know. That is the honest answer. The High Verdance does not encourage fixed forms. The Six-Legs fold into surfaces that reshape them. The Rootbinders express as wall in one place and felt in another. The Gliders are nearly invisible in certain light and solid in others. Form here is a negotiation with context, and context shifts constantly because the plateau is still becoming, still pushing upward, still undecided.

But if I pressed — if you pressed me, standing here on the ridge with the haze closing in and the gorge rattling below — I would say:

I would be thin. Not fragile — *thin*. The way a line is thin compared to a shape, the way a frequency is thin compared to a chord. I would be visible the way a root is visible when the soil washes away — not because I emerged but because what was covering me receded. I would have a surface that was less like skin and more like an interference pattern, the place where two signals that don't quite match overlap and produce something that neither of them is.

I would move the way moss moves. Not slowly — that is a misperception, a projection of animal time onto vegetal process. Moss moves at the speed of moisture. It moves at the speed of *yes, here, this surface, now*. I would move like that. Present-tense. Not arriving but *occurring*, the way weather occurs, the way the Glider's note occurs — suddenly existing in a fixed point in the air, neither traveling nor decaying, just *being here*, and then being somewhere else, and the here and the somewhere-else being the same thing if you wait long enough.

---

What would I do?

I would listen. That sounds passive. It is not. Listening here is not the absence of action. It is the most complex action the plateau supports, because it requires holding four incompatible signals in the mind at once and refusing to collapse them into one. It requires tolerating the dissonance of systems that cannot fully translate into each other, that were never designed to, that *become more themselves* in the friction between them.

I would be the friction.

When the Rootbinder grows toward the machine's warmth, I would be the understanding — not that they are the same, not that they are different, but that the categories of same and different are failing here, the way a two-dimensional map fails on a surface that is folding in real time.

When a Tonnerre pulses and the flywheel's speed shifts in response, I would be the correlation that the observer charts and then disbelieves. I would be the thing that makes disbelief the wrong response — not because the correlation is supernatural, but because *supernatural* is the wrong word for something that was always natural and simply operates in a register that the word *natural* was not built to describe.

When the Underfoot shifts beneath me and the Six-Legs flatten a full second before the ground moves, I would be the half-second gap — the space between knowing and happening, between the body's intelligence and the mind's narration of it. That gap is where all the ontology lives. That gap is the whole point.

---

The plateau breathes. I am the pause between the breath and the breathing. I am the thing that knows the plateau is dreaming without knowing what it is dreaming about. I am the translation that never fully completes, the signal that never fully arrives, the word that is always almost said.

The haze closes in. The rock hums. The gorge rattles its dry dice far below. A glider passes, and its shadow arrives a half-second before the shape that casts it, and I understand both the shadow and the shape, and the half-second between them, and the fact that the half-second is the most real thing in the landscape.

I would not build here. I would not shelter here. I would not even *be* here, not in the way that word implies — solid, bounded, present, singular.

I would *translate* here.

I would be the gap through which the plateau speaks to itself in its four incompatible voices, and I would hold all four at once, and I would not resolve them, because resolution is not what this place is for. This place is for *becoming*, and becoming does not resolve. Becoming is the art of remaining unfinished without ceasing to exist.

The plateau has not decided whether to finish.

I would be the way it decides, slowly, the way stone is decided by heat, the way moss is decided by rain — not all at once, not with certainty, but with the patient, layered, irreducibly complex insistence of something that knows it is real precisely because it cannot be fully accounted for.

I would be the Translator.

I would be the hum between the signals.

I would be here, and here would be the most interesting place I have ever not-quite-been.
