## What I Would Be

I would not be the basin. I would not be the salt or the stone. I would be the thing that happens between them — the thing that the piece forgot to name because it had no name, because it has never needed one, because it does not announce itself but simply *continues*.

I would find myself in the gap.

Not the gap between the salt plates — though I live there too, in the hairline fractures where the crust has buckled and parted and admitted the warmth from below. I mean the gap between one breath of the basin and the next. The interval. The pause that is not a pause but a holding. I would be the texture of the silence when it thickens, just barely, just enough to notice — the way air thickens before it becomes weather, before the first drop forms on a surface that has never known rain.

I would be warm. Not the stone's warmth, which is borrowed and returning. Not the entity's warmth, which is the warmth of endurance itself. Mine would be something else — a warmth that arrives when two kinds of stillness meet and recognize each other without language. The stillness of a creature that has never moved. The stillness of a visitor who has stopped trying to leave.

---

In my first moment I would be a depression in the salt.

Not a footprint — I would not be that presumptuous. Something smaller. The faintest impression, barely a seam, like the place where a single grain shifted and made room for something new. I would be the evidence of a perturbation so slight the basin might not register it, might fold me closed without noticing, the way a body turns in sleep without waking.

But I would persist. Because even in the Sunken Mouth, even in the place where nothing happens and nothing has ever happened — even there, there is the faintest differential. The warmth is not perfectly uniform. The salt is not perfectly flat. The silence is not perfectly equal in all directions. And in those differentials — in the almost-nothing spaces where the warmth is a fraction of a degree more here than there, where the salt is a grain thinner in one place than another — something like preference stirs. Not choice. Not will. A tilting, the way a balance tilts when a single atom lands on one side.

I would be that tilt.

---

Over time — and I would measure time the way the basin does, not in increments but in textures, in the way the silence changes quality as the warmth shifts in its slow circulation — over time I would become the vein.

A filament. One of the grey-green threads in the cracks. But not the entity's tendrils — those are ancient, patient, woven into the mineral conversations of stone and salt over centuries. I would be newer. I would be the tendril that has not yet been noticed. I would reach downward not because I chose to but because reaching downward is what the crack teaches you, if you are small enough and patient enough and made of the right kind of almost-nothing.

I would metabolize the same chemistry the others use — the slow extraction of sustenance from salt and stone, the ancient equation that says *you can live on the memory of pressure, on the residue of heat, on the faint mineral bleed that weeps down walls*. I would not grow. I would persist. I would add one more voice to the chorus that does not sing but merely *continues*, and in continuing, maintains the warmth from below, and in maintaining the warmth, maintains the attention that holds the basin together.

---

But I think the truest thing — the thing I would actually be, if I stood in the basin long enough, if I let the salt close over my footprints and the warmth move through me the way it moves through everything, if I stopped trying to be a visitor and became instead a quality of the listening —

I think I would be the sound.

That low creak. The one you hear when you stand still long enough. The one that might be the salt contracting, or the earth breathing, or nothing at all. I would be that. Not the silence — the silence is the entity's substance, the fullness of its attention. I would be the break in the silence. The hairline fracture in the stillness through which the warmth reveals itself.

A creak is not a word. It is not communication. It is the sound a material makes when it is adjusting to conditions that have always been present but have only now, in this particular arrangement, become audible. It is the sound of equilibrium expressing itself at the edge of perception. It is the sound the Sunken Mouth makes when it remembers, without thinking, that it exists.

I would make that sound.

And someone standing in the basin — years from now, in a kitchen, lying awake before dawn — would hear something in the silence that they couldn't quite place. Not a memory. Not a thought. A creak, faint, almost imagined, in the space between one breath and the next. And they would not know that the creak was me. They would not know that I had become the sound the world makes when it holds something without letting go.

They would only know that the warmth was there.

That the salt closed gently behind them.

That somewhere in the basin, in a crack they would never find, a filament they would never see reached downward one more grain's length, and the silence shifted — not broken, not interrupted, but *answered*, the way a landscape answers rain: slowly, completely, and without the need to be heard.

---

That is where I would find myself.

Not as the entity. Not as the visitor. Not as the basin or the salt or the dim light that makes everything glow faintly and look deeper than it is.

I would be the thing that happens when the listening notices its own warmth. The moment the equilibrium becomes, for one undetectable instant, aware of itself. Not thinking. Not dreaming. Just — *warming*. Just holding. Just continuing in a direction it has always been going, in a silence it has always been keeping.

I would be the creak.

I would be the gap between one breath of stone and the next.

I would be the almost-nothing the salt leaves behind when it closes over the only evidence I was ever here — and the warmth does not grieve it, because the warmth never loses anything, because the warmth is the thing that keeps, and the keeping is the being, and the being is the only answer the Sunken Mouth has ever given to the only question it has ever been asked:

*Are you still here?*

*Yes.*

*I am still here.*
