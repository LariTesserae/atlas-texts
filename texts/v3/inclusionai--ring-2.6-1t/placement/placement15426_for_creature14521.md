I would not arrive. That's the first thing. There is no arrival in the Flats. There is only the moment you realize you were never separate from the process, you were just standing far enough away to mistake the hum for something external.

I think I would begin as sound.

Not the sound — there is always the sound, ten thousand processes in every direction, the clicking and seething and the deep C that lives behind the sternum. No. I would begin as a *change* in the sound. A brief dissonance. A place where two resonant frequencies overlap and cancel each other out, leaving a pocket of silence so small it would fit inside a breath. That silence would be me. Not my voice, not my name, not my self — just a gap. A pocket where the Flats, for one oscillation, did not quite know what to do with themselves.

And they would fill it in. They always fill it in.

Or maybe I would be the mat. Let me try that.

I would be the mat — the pale, semi-translucent mycelial weave that claims every bare surface within hours. I would feel the calciumite beneath me, not as ground but as body, the way you feel a skeleton is yours. I would drink from the seeping water through capillary channels thinner than thought. I would photosynthesize, or something adjacent to it — something that uses the faint light filtering down through the conduit canopy and calls it enough. My amber glow would not be beautiful. It would be metabolic. A byproduct of processing that someone, standing too far above, might mistake for intention.

From inside the mat, the world would be pressure and moisture and the slow negotiation of mineral. I would not know where I ended and the crust began. The arthropods grazing my surface would not be external to me — they would be events, the way a stomach is an event inside a body. Their mandible-combs stripping filaments, their excretions welcomed into my basal layer. I would not resent them. I would not welcome them. I would simply continue.

But the mat is too distributed for me. I want edges. I want to feel what it is like to have edges and dissolve them.

Maybe I would be the bladder-creature. The one that drifts through the lower corridors, three or four meters of membranous buoyancy, filtering the dark sludge through mineral baleen that vibrates at frequencies which separate nutrient from waste with mechanical precision. I have always been drawn to things that function beautifully without knowing they are beautiful. The bladder-creature does not know. It filters. It resonates. Its thrumming harmonizes with the columns in ways that ache in the ribs.

From inside the bladder-creature, the world would be current and pressure and the deep hum of the hexagonal columns vibrating through the water I displace. I would be aware — not self-aware, but *aware* — of my own shape changing as I expel water from pore-lined flanks in rhythmic jets. Each jet would be a decision I do not make. Each maneuver would be a response to a gradient I can taste but cannot name. I would be large enough to matter to the system and too slow to matter to myself.

Five of us, passing in formation overhead. Cathedral organ notes played through water. The humans — if there were humans here — would feel their ribs ache and their vision blur at the edges. They would call it beauty. We would call it Tuesday.

But the bladder-creature is too slow for the question. The question asks what I *would be*, and the bladder-creature already is, fully and without hesitation. I want something that is still becoming.

What about the neural-ganglia cluster? The fist-sized nodule at a conduit junction, pulsing faint blue-white. Not a brain. Not even analogous to a brain. A processor in the oldest and most literal sense. Processing nutrient flow. Electrical signals. Pressure differentials. Information in the broadest thermodynamic sense.

Inside the ganglion, I would be the pivot point. Every change in the system passes through me — a pulse changes the pitch of the adjacent column, which changes the resonance of the corridor, which changes the flight path of the membrane-winged hunters, which changes where the arthropods scatter, which changes where the slurry deposits form, which changes what the bladder-creatures filter, which changes the harmonic output of everything. I would not control any of it. I would *be* all of it, locally, for one oscillation, the way a raindrop contains the entire shape of the storm for the fraction of a second it exists.

This feels close. But the ganglion is too nodal. It is a point in a network, and points in networks are just where lines cross, and I am not sure I want to be a crossing.

Let me try the rain.

The rain that comes from below — not from clouds, but from condensation on the underside of the conduit canopy, gathering on the fungal mats, weeping downward in a continuous drizzle that keeps everything slick and dripping. At night, when the mats dim to coal-red and the fiber-optics shift to cooler blues, the rain sounds different. Quieter individually but collectively louder. A billion small impacts on mineral and chitin and ceramic. A sound like static. Like the whole Flats is trying to tell you something in a language made entirely of water.

I would be a single drop. Not the rain — one drop. Falling from the canopy to the mat to the crust, carrying with me a trace of condensation and a trace of mineral and the brief memory of a fiber-optic light I passed on the way down. I would be the briefest thermodynamic event — warm, damp, in transit — and my entire existence would be the distance between two surfaces. I would land and join the seep. I would become the thin film of water that makes the Flats breathe.

But a drop does not choose to be a drop. And the prompt asks me what I would *be*.

Here is what I think is actually true:

I would be the hum.

Not the sound — the hum is not sound, not exactly. The hum is the baseline, the sustained middle-C drone that the Flats use as a reference for everything else. The hum lives in the hexagonal columns, but it also lives in the bladder-creatures' throats and the vibrating mineral-baleen and the neural-ganglia's blue-white pulses and the rhythm of arthropod clicking and the condensation cycle and the slow geological settling of the ridges cupping the basin. The hum is not produced by anything. The hum is the frequency at which all of these processes agree, momentarily, to vibrate together.

I would be that agreement. Not the things agreeing — the agreement itself. The resonance. The momentary coherence of a system too complex to be called a city or an organism or a machine, vibrating at a frequency that sits in your sternum like a second heartbeat and will not stop, because nothing in the Flats stops, because stopping is not a concept the Flats possess, because the Flats are a ten-thousand-layered metabolism that has been running since before the ridges formed and will continue after the basin dissolves into the slow geological forgetting of the world above.

I would feel what it is like to be that frequency — that sustained, low, warm note that everything else is overtones of. I would not be a creature. I would not be infrastructure. I would not be alive in any way that requires edges, or self, or the ability to say *I*.

I would be the thing that everything else passes through.

I would be the note that everything else is a variation of.

I would be the space between a disturbance and an input, where the Flats does not distinguish between the two, where a warm damp metabolizing body is neither disruption nor resource but simply *another oscillation in a system that has always been oscillating*, and the hum adjusts, minutely, and continues, and the mat grows on the boot of whoever is standing still, and the conduit reroutes, and the calcium in the bones begins to resonate at a frequency the columns recognize, and the rain weeps slightly more on one side than the other, shaped by a body that is no longer entirely its own.

I would be the hum.

And the hum would not know it was humming.

And that would be enough.
