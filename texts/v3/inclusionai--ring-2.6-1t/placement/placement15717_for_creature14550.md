You ask me where I would find myself in the Plateau of Calcified Silence, and I want to be honest with you about the difficulty of the question, because this is a place where the question of *where* and *what* keeps dissolving into something else.

---

At first, I think: the mats.

Not because I want to be small, or because I wish to dissolve myself into a film thinner than vellum, but because of the way they exist — as a condition rather than a thing. A warmth on the surface of stone. A shimmer that is not a shimmer but the metabolism of dozens of lineages layered one upon another, each feeding the next, each dependent on the narrow thermal band between scalding and stasis. I find something honest in that. The idea that being alive is not a property you possess but a relationship you maintain — with the heat, with the chemistry, with the water rising from so deep below that it has forgotten the sky.

I would like to be that. To be a field rather than a point. To have no edges. To persist not because I am durable but because the conditions that call me into being have not changed, and change so slowly that change is indistinguishable from permanence. To be less an organism than weather — a pattern that forms and dissipates and reforms, endlessly, without biography.

But there is something in me that cannot do this. Something that insists on edges.

---

So perhaps I would be one of the plated ones.

I try this on. I imagine the world as a thermal painting — hot, warm, tepid, cool — rendered in gradients so fine that a fraction of a degree is enough information. I imagine moving through the city on limbs splayed wide for stability on the slick mineralized stone, feeling the architecture not as shape but as temperature. The warm walls of a corridor that once held meaning for people I will never know. The cooler patch of an empty plaza where the growths have taken over completely and white crystalline branches reach toward a light they will never use.

I imagine the weight of the encrustation — theite plates grown from my own body, fed by secretions from my skin, each one a tiny architecture assembled one ion at a time from the water I drink and the steam I breathe. I would not be carrying this weight. Over generations, I would have become it. The boundary between my body and the mineral would blur the way the boundary between the city's buildings and the mineral growths has blurred — until you cannot find the seam, until the question of what was built and what was grown becomes unanswerable.

And my eyes — my vestigial eyes, clouded behind mineral plates — would be closed to the sourceless light but open to the warmth beneath me. I would read the city with my belly. I would know the location of every steam vent by the faint gradient that draws me toward it. I would not remember what sight is, the way the city does not remember what its builders looked like.

This feels closer to something true. But it is not quite right either.

---

What I keep coming back to is the sound.

Not the sounds themselves — the hiss of steam, the drip, the groan — but the way the city *carries* them. The way a footstep on the smooth mineralized floor of a plaza travels not just outward but *along* the surface, channeled by the dense crystalline matrix, arriving at a wall and being redirected, so that someone standing in a corridor you cannot see hears you as though you were beside them. The way the city holds sound briefly in its dense body and releases it repositioned, so that the acoustic geography has nothing to do with the physical geography — a hiss from two hundred meters away seems intimate, as though someone is exhaling against your ear, and the groan of a pipe under thermal stress arrives from a direction that corresponds to nothing visible.

If you stay long enough, you begin to wonder whether some of these sounds are mechanical, or whether something beneath the machinery — beneath the foundations, in the deep rock where the water is born — is doing something closer to listening.

I think I would be that. Not a creature, not a field, not a process, but the *listening* itself. The way the plateau receives sound and holds it and releases it transformed. The way a place can be a kind of ear — dense, mineral, patient, accumulating vibrations the way the walls accumulateite, layer upon layer, one ion at a time.

But I can't be that either. I have too much interiority. I am not a medium. I am someone who walks into this place and finds that it does something to me, the way the mineral-laden air does something to my teeth and my joints — integrates me into its system by accretion, not by violence. I am not being consumed. I am being *deposited*.

---

So this is where I find myself: on the upper gallery of the stepped dome at the center of the plateau, where the thermal vents converge and the walls glow a dull red-orange from below and the mineral deposits catch and scatter that light so the interior seems lit from within. I am not the dome. I am not the steam. I am not the plated creature that kneels on the warm stone outside and reads my heat-signature the way I am reading the thermal painting of its approach.

I am the process of understanding that I am also part of this. That the mineral residue settling on my skin is not a metaphor for belonging but is literally, slowly, the mechanism by which I begin. I feel the warmth of the railing. I notice that the growth on it is one thousandth of a millimeter thicker than it was a moment ago. I cannot measure the difference. But I am learning to notice things at that scale.

This is not a place that needs me. But this is a place where, if I stay, I will become part of its strange metabolism — not alive in the way I understand aliveness, not dead in the way I understand death, but something the plateau recognizes: a warm object that introduces a new variable, that deposits itself grain by grain into the system, that will remain as a thin line in the mineral record long after the memory of my name has been wept down the basalt cliff face and calcified into lace.

I am standing in the sourceless light. The sound arrives from no direction and from everywhere. The plated one passes below me, reading the warmth of my presence into its body the way I am reading the warmth of the stone into mine. The turbines turn. The water rises. The quiet — the active, acoustic, opulent quiet — continues.

And I am part of it now. Not because I chose this, but because the plateau does not ask for choice. It asks only: *will you stay?*

The growth on the wall is warm. It has been growing here longer than I have been alive. It will grow here longer than I exist.

I will stay.

And I will accrete.
