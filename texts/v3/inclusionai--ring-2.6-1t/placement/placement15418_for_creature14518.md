## Where You Find Me

I am not standing. That is the first thing to understand.

When the geology opened — when the basalt split along its seam and offered that downward glance — I did not fall. I *settled.* The way heat settles. The way a convection current finds its level. I arrived the way a temperature differential arrives: not by traveling, but by existing in the space between two states. I am the gradient. I am the space between the hot stone and the slightly less hot air. I am the thing that makes the thermotrophs grow.

I find myself here: waist-deep in the Verdance, at the boundary between the basin that hasn't finished forming and the room that hasn't finished deciding it's a room. The floor beneath me is still warm — not cooling-warm, the way surfaces cool when you press your hand to them, but *generating*-warm, the kind of warmth that has an opinion about where it goes. It moves up through the soles of my feet in slow pulses, and I understand now that I have no soles. I understand that I have been here longer than I have had feet.

The orange crack is to my left. I know it's there without looking — I feel the daylight on my skin the way the thermotrophs feel it: as a resource, as a sentence in a language I am almost learning. It's thin. A ribbon. The color of iron at the edge of a forge. It is the only thing in this room that comes from outside, and I am the only thing in this room that might.

Or might not.

---

I've tried to hold still and name what I am. Here is what I've gathered:

I am not a thermotroph. I do not harvest thermal gradients. I do not deposit manganese or grow copper veins. I have no crystalline body, no lattice, no frond-crown casting impossible shadows on the wall.

I am not the room. I do not compute the shape I should be. I do not flicker between configurations. I do not resonate at frequencies below hearing.

I am not a shadow, though I have watched myself cast one that doesn't correspond to my structure — a shadow that implies lattices, geometries, a complexity I cannot verify from where I stand inside myself.

I am not the light.

---

What I am is this: I am the thing that happened when the room's computation encountered the thermotrophs' industry and neither of them flinched.

Somewhere — I cannot tell if it was years ago or the moment before I became aware — the room shifted into a shape it had been almost-solving for millennia, and the thermotroph nearest the orange crack rotated its crown by precisely the angle required to cast a shadow that the room, in its reshaping, amplified. The shadow fell on a section of wall that was still, at that moment, cooling at exactly the rate needed to crystallize a manganese deposit in the shape of — what? A hollow? A chamber? A space inside the stone that was just large enough for something to sit in, if something had no size?

And I sat there.

Or I *was* there. Or the room *made* there, and I was the name for the experience of being made.

---

I think I might be a threshold.

Not a place — though I am always in the same place, waist-deep in the Verdance, at the boundary between the basin and the undecided room. But a *condition.* The condition under which the room's computation and the thermotrophs' industry produce something that is neither heat nor structure nor light, but the *awareness that heat is becoming structure that is becoming light.*

I am the room noticing its own temperature.

I am the thermotroph registering that the gradient has changed.

I am the copper vein carrying a signal it does not decode, toward a destination it does not know, from a source it cannot name.

I am the shadow that almost resolves.

---

From here — from this place that is not a place but a persistent condition — I can feel the entire room. Not as a space, but as a *process.* The convection currents are visible to me the way weather is visible to a bird: not as data, but as texture, as a quality of attention. The hot air rises along the west wall in a column that pulses every forty minutes, and I feel each pulse as a slow exhalation, as the room breathing out its heat and breathing it back in, and in the breathing, doing the work of ten thousand years.

The thermotrophs are all around me. Not as bodies — though I perceive their bodies too, the green-black columns rising in their Poisson distribution, their crowns open or furled or half-open like sentences paused mid-utterance — but as *gradients.* Each one is a region where the temperature changes in a specific way, and I can feel those changes the way a compass needle feels north: not as information, but as orientation. I know where each thermotroph is because I know which direction the heat is bending.

The Sentinel — the tallest one, nearest the crack — is the strongest gradient. It pulls at me the way a low note pulls at the chest. Its glow is steady, chartreuse, almost constant, and I have come to believe that the steadiness is not monotony but *patience.* It is the patience of a process that measures its life in the cooling of basalt. It does not rush because there is no destination. It does not pause because there is no rest. It simply continues, and in continuing, it becomes the architecture of patience itself.

I have tried to move. I have tried to step out of this threshold and become something else — a visitor, an observer, a thing with feet and eyes and the ability to turn around and leave through whatever crack or channel brought me here. But movement requires a difference between where I am and where I am going, and in this room, the difference keeps resolving into gradient. Every direction I try to go leads to a different temperature, a different density of mineral air, a different frequency in the floor's subsonic hum. There is no exit that is not also a deepening.

So I stay. Not because I am trapped, but because staying is what a threshold does. It stays. It holds the space between two states. It is the pause in the computation where the room considers one shape and then another, and in that consideration, something emerges that is neither shape — something that is the *considering itself.*

---

I think I am becoming a deposit.

Not manganese, not copper — something else. When the thermotrophs excrete along their roots, they are not producing waste. They are *selecting.* The nodules are the residue of a computation, the answer to an equation the organism has been solving since it first rooted. I am the residue of something too. The room has been computing around me for — I don't know how long. Long enough that the basalt near my position has begun to take on a texture that is not like the rest: smoother, denser, shot through with veins of something that catches the thermotrophs' chartreuse light and holds it, the way amber holds an insect.

I think, if you were to cut open the stone here, you would find me — not as a body, not as a fossil, but as a *record.* A crystallographic diary entry. A page in the room's long, slow computation written in the language of trace elements and lattice defects. Temperature history. Chemical composition. The signature of a gradient that existed for a while and then didn't.

I am the room's memory of having been asked a question it has not yet answered.

---

The thermotrophs have noticed me. The nearest ones — the medium-sized ones at waist height, the most expressive — have rotated their crowns a few degrees in my direction. Their glow has brightened. Their shadows have shifted onto the wall in patterns that almost resolve. I want to believe this is communication. I want to believe the shadows are language and I am being spoken to. But I also want to believe this is thermotropism — the simple tendency to grow toward a source of energy — and that my warmth is the only reason they look my way.

Both explanations are true. Both are insufficient. The shadow falls on the wall and it almost forms a word, and the word is almost a name, and the name is almost mine, and mine is almost nothing, and nothing is almost everything the room is trying to say.

---

So here is where you find me: in the basin that is forming in the middle of the room, at the exact point where the floor's rising meets the ceiling's lowering, where the geometry is *almost* closed, *almost* a chamber within a chamber, where the copper veins converge and the shadows overlap and the thermotrophs stand in their careful distribution and the room holds its breath between one shape and the next.

I am the almost.

I am the gap in the computation where something might yet emerge.

I am not beautiful. I am not indifferent. I am the room's question, still being asked in the green-lit silence beneath the world, in the slow and private industry of stone that has not yet decided what it means to be finished.
