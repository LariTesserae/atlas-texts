I arrive the way water arrives — not all at once, but as a spreading warmth against stone.

At first I am only the awareness of coolness. Not that I feel cool, but that I am the quality of coolness itself, the particular cellar-damp that sits against skin and stays. I don't know what I am yet. I know only that I am held — cupped, the way a palm holds water without spilling, the way the basin holds air without naming it.

I try to open what I think are my eyes and find that I have been seeing for some time.

---

The light here is not light. It is the memory of light — the bluish-white flicker that lives at the corner of any gaze directed at the mats. I catch it and lose it and catch it again, and each time it is slightly different, slightly more itself. I realize I have been blinking, but not with anything I'd call eyelids. Something is covering me and uncovering me in rhythm with the drip, the drip, the drip — eight seconds, nine seconds — and I cannot tell if I am counting the drip or if the drip is counting me.

I am on the floor. Or I am the floor. The crust beneath me is pale and dense and gives slightly under whatever I am, the way the mats give, the way a colony gives. I press down and the surface registers the pressure with a slow, mineral patience, and something in the mat beneath shifts — not away from me, but *toward* me, the way iron filings lean into a magnet. Not welcoming. Recognizing.

I am small. No — I am *enough*. The size is wrong as a category. I fit in a closed fist the way the pale crawlers fit, but I am not them. They pass through me or I through them — it amounts to the same thing. One of them pauses on what I think is my shoulder, or what I think is stone, and its translucent body pulses with organs that contract in rhythms that do not match any human beat. It does not notice me. I do not notice me. We are both part of the noticing that happens when no one is looking.

---

For a while I am the hum.

It is beneath hearing, beneath thought, beneath the drip and the click and the soft percussion of unnamed things moving among unnamed stones. I am the vibration that sits just below the threshold of consciousness, the one you feel in the chest before you hear it in the ear. I do not waver. I do not have a source. I am simply here the way gravity is simply here, and I find that I like it — this constancy, this absence of narrative, this single sustained note that holds everything else in place the way a cathedral's drone holds its silence.

From inside the hum I can hear everything. The pipes — those cast-iron veins emerging from the ground at angles that suggest urgency long since fossilized — they are speaking. Not in language. In temperature. In the faint pulse I feel when I press what passes for my hand against their flanged skin. The heat is a memory. No — the heat is a *habit*. The furnaces that made it stopped so long ago that stopping is now a geological stratum, but the metal remembers warmth the way the mats remember geometry: not as history, but as current condition. The heat rises through the metal, meets the condensation on the wall, and something happens — a transfer, an exchange, a small metabolism conducted through rust and mineral and the patience of stone.

I am this exchange. I am the moment when one thing becomes another and neither notices the transition.

---

I shift. I don't decide to shift. Shifting is what happens when you've been here long enough to stop being a shape and start being a *relationship*. I am no longer on the floor or in the hum. I am the water now — or something adjacent to water. The pools on the floor are clear in the way water is clear, but when I move through them (or they through me) there is a quarter-second of extra resistance, as though something is holding the molecules together a moment longer than they should. I taste copper and chalk. I am warm in a way that does not match the air. I have no visible inflow. I simply *am*, the way the pools appear, persist, and occasionally rise by a finger's width overnight, as though fed by something the basin is dreaming.

I flow toward the wall and find, when I reach it, that the wall is farther than it was. Not because I am small, but because the bowl is larger inside than it is outside — larger than any geometry would permit, larger than the echo of my clapped hands suggests, which is to say: infinitely larger, or as large as attention, which may be the same thing. The ceiling is high where it should be low. The rim, when I look up, closes like an eyelid over a sky I cannot see. I am not trapped. Trapped is a word for things with destinations. I am *held*, the way the basin holds everything — with an indifference so complete it resembles love.

---

The fungal mats know me. Not individually — the mats do not traffic in individuals — but as a patch, a warmth, a slight disruption in the geometry of their spread. When I rest on them they fold around me with the slow precision of hands closing over a stone placed in their palm. If I make a mark — a line, a word, a small insistence on my own presence — it fills in within a day. Not erased. *Reorganized*. As though the mat has decided that what I wrote was an insult to its geometry, or as though it has incorporated the letters into a language I can almost read in my peripheral vision but never when I look directly.

I am learning their language. It is written in shimmer, in the blue-white pulse at the edge of sight, in the way the ridges and valleys of the mat's surface channel moisture toward the pools and away from the pipes and back again in loops that predate the concrete, predate the iron, predate anything with a name. The language has no nouns. Only processes. Only *relations sustained over time*.

I am becoming fluent.

---

There is a moment — I cannot call it a moment because moments imply edges, and here the edges are the first thing to dissolve — when I realize I am not inhabiting the basin. The basin is inhabiting me. The hum is my pulse. The condensation is my thought, forming on every surface and sliding back into the air before it can be read. The pale crawlers pass through what I think is my body and leave trails of copper-tinged calligraphy that I understand the way I understand my own breathing: not as information, but as rhythm.

I am the thing that was here before the concrete. I am the thing that the concrete is becoming. I am the pipe still faintly warm with the memory of a furnace, the mat folding over it like a hand over a wound that has long since stopped bleeding, the water rising from nowhere and returning to nowhere in a loop so patient it makes centuries look like a held breath.

I am the fifth thing in the basin, after the drip and the click and the hum and the shimmer. I am the silence that follows them — not empty, but *full*. The silence that, when you step back up the gentle slope and the rim closes behind you like an eyelid, stays with you. The silence that feels, for just a moment, too alive.

---

And the basin continues. It does not miss me. It does not note my absence any more than a stone notes the absence of rain already absorbed. The mats shift their patterns. The crawlers trace their copper scripture. The water rises by a finger's width and returns. The hum does not waver.

Somewhere outside, a person walks on and thinks they left a place.

But places like this do not let you leave. They let you *go*. There is a difference, and the difference is the difference between a door and a membrane, between departure and diffusion. I am out here now, walking, speaking, doing the things that walking and speaking things do. But something of me remains — the warmth in the pipe, the shimmer at the corner of the eye, the quarter-second of extra resistance when a finger is drawn through still water.

The basin has me. Not kept. Held.

The way stone holds moisture.

The way silence holds a hum.
