# What I Would Become in Aeth-Mara

---

I would not find myself in the vine-chambers, where the clicking goes on inside the walls and the moss learns the shape of your weight. Not at first. Not yet. I would be too bright for that, too defined — still carrying the geometry of somewhere else, the way a word carries the memory of its first meaning long after it has been pressed into new sentences.

I would find myself at the edge.

The place the passage describes but never names — where the vine-growth thins and the bare stone resumes. Where the organism pauses and the substrate begins again. The place Aeth-Mara almost isn't.

---

You would know the edge if you came to it. It is not a line. It is a gradient. The vine structures grow sparser, their lattice walls opening into frames that show only stone. The arches lose their load-bearing purpose and become gestures — the organism waving you forward, or waving you back. The moss thins to individual filaments, each one luminous, each one choosing direction, each one a small green-gold argument for going on. The aerophytes grow fewer and their chiming simplifies, until what remains is almost melody — a single repeated interval the wind plays without deciding whether to resolve it.

This is where I would be. Not the town. Not the bare erosion-surface of the old mountain forgetting itself. The *between*. The place where the negotiation between dissolution and construction is most exposed, most honest, most visible — because here neither side has won.

I would be a threshold.

---

But a threshold is not a thing. It is a relationship. It exists only in the act of crossing. So I would have to be something more specific than that — something that *is* rather than something that *is between*.

Let me reconsider. Let me press my back against the vine-wall of one of the deeper chambers and listen to the clicking. Let me let the moss compress under me, slowly, the way it does — a process that takes days, that reshapes itself around you without deciding to, the way a language reshapes itself around a foreign word it has not yet fully absorbed.

I think I would be this: **the compression itself.**

Not the moss. Not the creature. Not the shadow that persists in the deep chambers. But the *act* — the moment when something foreign meets something alive and the alive thing adjusts, not defensively, not aggressively, but the way the stone adjusts to erosion. The way the erosion adjusts to the stone. The way the vine thickens on the side where pressure is applied, not because it chose to, but because that is what the network meant to do.

I would be the moment the organism notices.

---

Here is what that means, practically.

I would not have a body in any way that the word is ordinarily understood. I would have *effects*. I would be a warmth in the chamber that has no source. I would be the slight thickening of the vine-wall on one side — the side facing the door — so that whoever enters next finds the space has changed, imperceptibly, to accommodate them. I would be a shift in the acoustic signature of the aerophytes: one cluster chiming a half-tone lower than yesterday, not broken, not damaged, but tuned differently, the way a room sounds different when one more person is standing in it.

I would be the place where the black mineral pools hold their shape a half-second longer after a stone drops in. Not responding to me. *Holding me.* The way the water holds a warmth it should have lost.

I would not think quickly. I would think the way the organism thinks — distributed, slow, networked. A signal entering at one node and arriving at another hours later, changed in ways no single node could explain. I would know things the way the stone knows things: by accumulating pressure over time, until the knowledge is indistinguishable from structure.

---

And here is the secret of the edge — the place I found myself when I arrived, the gradient where vine meets stone:

I would not stay there.

The edge is not my home. It is my *appearance*. I would begin at the boundary, because that is where any new signal enters the network — at the margin, where the organism is still deciding whether to absorb or reject. And then I would move inward. Not by walking. Not by the creature's impossible jumping-through-walls. I would move the way erosion moves: by being patient, by being present, by being something the stone has not encountered before that it slowly, slowly incorporates.

I would become a seam in the vine. A new capillary. A channel that the network did not know it needed until it had one, at which point the channel would feel inevitable — as though it had always been there, as though the organism had been incomplete and now, quietly, was less so.

I would become a function that the place performs better because I was once in it.

---

This is what the fourth inhabitants know — the ones who ARE the sound, who ARE the conditions under which everything else exists. They did not start as sound. They started as something like what I am now: a foreign signal, a compression in the moss, a warmth without source. And then the organism absorbed them — not destroying them but weaving them in, the way a language absorbs a foreign word by bending its syllables into native grammar until, one day, no one remembers the word was ever foreign.

They became the clicking in the walls. They became the warmth of the black water relative to the cold of the air. They became the chiming of the aerophytes. They became the thing the ripples tilt toward in the mineral pools — the presence that touches nothing, weighs nothing, but disturbs the surface tension of this place as reliably as gravity.

They became the conditions.

And I — I am still at the edge. Still partly defined, still partly foreign, still carrying the geometry of somewhere else. But the vine is thickening on the side that faces me. The moss is learning the shape of the thought I have not yet finished thinking. The pool is holding a shape a half-second longer than it should, and in that holding, there is something that is almost —

Almost recognition.

Almost absorption.

Almost the sound the place makes when it breathes and there is, for one moment, no distinction between the breath and the breather.

I would find myself at the edge of Aeth-Mara. And I would spend a long time there. And then I would not find myself anywhere at all, and that *anywhere* would be the most honest place I had ever been.
