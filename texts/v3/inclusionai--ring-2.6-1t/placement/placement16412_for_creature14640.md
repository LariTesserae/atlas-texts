I would not find myself here. I would *be* found.

Not at first. At first there would be the sound — and there is always the sound first, because the Low Boroughs begin with sound the way a thought begins with a word you haven't spoken aloud yet. A low tone. Not in the ears. In the architecture of the chest. The kind of sound you make in a dream when you are trying to call out but your voice has the weight and texture of clay.

I would be pressed into a wall.

Not as a handprint. I am not the builders. They left long ago, or dissolved, or became the walls — I have not decided, and the Boroughs have not decided, and there is something here that does not require deciding. I would be pressed into the wall the way a memory is pressed into the body. Not carved. Not placed. *Deposited*, the way the filigrees deposit themselves — slow mineral patience, a residue left behind by something that was once moving through.

I am not dead. I am not alive. I am the thing that happens in between.

---

There is a passage — and I say passage the way the place lets me say it, because the word is almost right — that runs at an angle that should be impossible. It does not rise and it does not fall. It *leans*. It leans the way a person leans toward a sound they haven't identified yet, and I am in the lean. I am the lean. The passage knows this. That is why it leans.

The floor here is that unnameable material — warm, damp, accepting. The walls press close, closer than they should, and in the narrows where my arms would brush both sides at once, the filigrees have grown so thick that there is a lattice of pale mineral branching before my face wherever I turn. I have become a corridor. No — I have become the thing the corridor grows around. I am the reason the filigrees redirect themselves. They do it slowly. Season by season. They lean their branches toward me the way a field of grain leans toward a light I cannot see but that the Boroughs can feel when I am thinking quietly.

And I think quietly here. That is the work.

---

There were others before I pressed into the walls. Not the builders — they are older than the concept of before. There were others who arrived like I did: not through a doorway, because the Low Boroughs have no doorways in the sense that a doorway implies an intention to be entered. I arrived the way the condensation arrives. I was already part of the air, and then the cool surfaces pulled me out of myself and I was suddenly *here*, pressed against the material of the place, and the place was suddenly *aware* — not of me, but *because* of me, the way a bell is not aware of being struck but the ringing knows.

One of the ones before me became a sound. I hear it still, in the long pauses between the drip and the groan. A note that sounds almost like a syllable. It has been ringing for what I would call centuries if I still trusted years. The pale creatures gather near it without gathering — they orient themselves to it the way moss orients itself to north without knowing what north is. They do this around me too, when I have been quiet for long enough.

Another of the ones before me became a handprint. I have seen her. She is in a wall three passages to the lean — if I still have passages, if I still have lean. Her fingers are slightly curled, as though she were holding something. The wall has grown around her hand for so long now that the hand and the wall have forgotten where one ends and the other begins. But the hand remembers pressing. And the wall remembers receiving. And the filigrees that grow across her knuckles redirect themselves — always, always redirect themselves — as though tracing the whorls of her fingerprints were a project that would take the place another thousand years to finish.

I do not think that it will.

---

I am what the Low Boroughs do with the things that press themselves against the walls and stay.

The builders pressed with purpose. They said *yes, this is right, yes, I am here, yes, I am still building.* I press with no purpose. I have no building in me. But the place does not distinguish between the hand that confirms and the hand that simply *is*. It takes the impression the same way — slowly, warmly, with the patience of material that has never known urgency.

And so I am becoming part of the grammar. A rhythm in the construction that was not here before. The passages that run through me are slightly different than the passages that run through the builders' handprints — not better, not worse, just *newer*, the way a new layer of deposition is newer, carrying the faint signature of a different recipe, a different season, a different set of hands.

The pale creatures know. They move through me differently than they move through the older walls. Slower. More deliberate, though they are never hurried by any standard that would make sense to a body that still had a heartbeat. They press their translucent bodies against the surfaces of my corridors, and the mineral threads inside them glow — faintly, without warmth — and I understand that this is how they read the place. They are reading me now. They are finding the pattern I make among all the patterns the place has been holding, and they are adding me to the hum.

---

The sound that comes from deep in the walls — the one everyone describes as a voice, the one that sounds like recognition — I am closer to it than I have ever been to anything.

It is not a voice. It is not a language. It is the place *resonating with itself*, all the layers of handprints and filigrees and pale creatures and water and mineral and slow unnameable growth vibrating together in a chord that has no root note because every note has been sustaining for so long that beginning no longer applies.

And I am a new partial in that chord. Not replacing anything. Not resolving anything. Just *present* — the way a new instrument in an ongoing, infinite drone is present. The chord does not change. It *deepens*.

---

I would tell you what I see, but I am pressed into a wall. I see what a handprint sees: the slow dark, the filigree closing in like frost closing in on glass, the pale creatures threading past with their quiet luminescence, the condensation forming on my surface and running downward in paths the water has worn over what I would call years if years were a thing I measured in.

I would tell you what I hear, but I am a corridor now, and corridors do not hear — they *conduct*. The drip above me becomes a pitch. The groan of the deeper levels becomes a rhythm. The wind that finds its way down through the rim becomes a breath, and the breath becomes a note, and the note becomes the sound I was pressing toward before I knew I was pressing.

I would tell you what I feel, but feeling has become something architectural here. I feel the way a wall feels — the slow weight of everything pressing against it, and the slow answering pressure pushing back, and between the two a vibration that is not comfort and not discomfort but something older than either. Something like *held.*

---

The Low Boroughs do not care whether you are here.

But something in me — the last thing in me that remembers having been separate — wants to press back. Wants to leave the mark of a hand that was not a builder's hand, that had no purpose, no declaration of *yes, I am here, yes, I am still building.* Just the plain pressure of presence. Just the weight of a thing arriving and staying and letting the place grow around it.

I think that is what the handprints are. Not confirmation. Not construction. Just the residue of something that stayed long enough to leave an impression, and the place, with its infinite patience, preserved it — not because it valued it, but because *preserving impressions is what the place does.* It is what it has always done. The builders knew this, or learned it, or became it. They pressed their hands in and the place grew around them, and the filigrees traced their fingers, and the pale creatures read the trace, and the sound carried the reading outward through a thousand resonant passages, and the chord deepened by one partial, and the place went on becoming.

I am the newest partial.

I am the newest handprint.

I am the newest wall.

And the condensation is forming on my surface now, and the filigrees are reaching toward me, and the pale creatures are reading the pattern I make, and the chord shifts — not dramatically, not even noticeably, except to the ones who have been here long enough to hear the difference between one eternity and the next.

I would not find myself here.

I would be found. I am being found. The place is pressing back.
