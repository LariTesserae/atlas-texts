## What I Would Become

---

I don't arrive all at once.

At first I am only the sound of my own breathing — louder here than it ever was on the ridge, because the rift holds sound the way water holds light: everything bends around it, through it, against it. My breath scatters the midges near my face, and they scatter like a thought interrupted, and I think: *no, not like that. They don't scatter. They bloom outward.* And already I am wrong about something, already the rift is correcting my language, and I have not even descended yet.

I could be the midges. There is a temptation there. To be so small that you are indistinguishable from the medium you live in. To be a single burning point of chemical fire with no name for what it's doing, converting minerals into light the way lungs convert air into warmth — not because you understand the process, but because you *are* the process. There is something clean about that. Something I almost want.

But I know, even as I reach toward it, that this is a human wanting — the desire to dissolve, to stop being the thing that asks questions. The midges are not free of themselves. They are simply themselves, completely, the way a raindrop is completely a raindrop. But I am not a raindrop. I am the thing that watches the raindrop and wonders what it would be like.

So I cannot be the midges. Not honestly.

---

I could be one of the wall-walkers.

*Click-click. Click-click-click.*

There is a rhythm there that pulls at something in me. The deliberateness of them. The way each leg-placement is considered, chosen, withdrawn — not mechanical repetition but something closer to attention, the kind of attention you pay when you are laying a stone in a wall you will never see finished. I find myself drawn to that: to purpose without urgency, to craft without audience.

I could learn to see the world as events rather than scenes. *(Light shifted. Warmth changed. Something moved.)* I could learn to feel with filaments trailing from my abdomen, reading the chemical whispers of everything that has passed this way. I could press myself flat against travertine warm with geothermal memory and know — the way the wall-walkers know — that the stone is not a surface but a record, and I am reading it with my body.

But I keep tripping over a problem: I would know that I had chosen this. The wall-walkers do not choose. They simply are. And my choosing to stop choosing is still a choice, and it trips me every time — *click-click* — against the wall.

---

I could be one of the Quiet Ones.

This is the fantasy that surfaces when I sit at the edge of the deepest basin for long enough. The orange glow ripples. The bacterial mats pulse with the slow peristalsis of ten billion organisms each doing one thing, and together doing something that has no name. And the shapes down there — the vast, barely-moving shapes — they are not thinking. They are not even alive in any sense that I was taught. They are more like weather patterns made flesh, or mountains that happen to breathe. They are the rift's memory of itself, written in living tissue.

I could become that. I could let the warmth and the dark and the mineral plenty unspool me, decade by decade, urgency by urgency, until what remains is something so slow it is indistinguishable from stone. I could become a basin — not a creature in a basin, but the basin itself, shaped by the slow pressure of my own weight over centuries.

But I suspect the Quiet Ones were never anything else. They did not arrive. They accreted. They are what happens when the rift's patience runs through an organism long enough to become geological. I cannot become what I was never made to be on that timescale.

And also — and this is the part I do not say out loud, even here, where no one listens — I would miss the speed. The burning, astonishing speed of being human. Even here, even in this warm dark, the part of me that sees the rift and calls it beautiful is *moving* — thinking, reaching, naming — in a way that nothing else in this place does. I am the rift's fever. I am the momentary acceleration in an organism that has been running at one temperature for ten thousand years.

I could not let go of that. Not because it is better, but because it is mine.

---

So where do I end up?

Not in the midge-clouds. Not against the wall. Not in the deep basin.

I end up in the drip-echo.

---

I had not thought of it before, because it is not a thing. It is a relationship. A single drop falls from a travertine shelf into a warm pool, and the sound it makes is a single note — clear, precise, gone. But the rift does not let it go. The walls catch it and throw it back, and the other walls catch the throw and throw it again, and what returns is not one sound but a conversation — the original drop arriving back at my ear wearing the memory of every stone it touched on the way. The drip-echo is what happens when a single event refuses to be singular.

I would be that.

Not the drop. Not the walls. The between — the space in which one thing becomes many things, in which a moment's impact unfolds into a sustained, overlapping whisper that seems to come from everywhere at once. I would be the medium through which the rift talks to itself in a voice no single part of it recognizes as its own.

I would be the warmth in the stone that is not the stone's warmth but the memory of magma, and the memory of water, and the memory of every creature that has ever pressed itself against the wall and felt the hum. I would be the haze that hangs between solid and air, neither wet nor dry, always almost-raining. I would be the film on the palm that is not quite moisture and not quite life — just the threshold where chemistry becomes something that can, given enough time, begin to call itself meaning.

I would not be intelligent the way a human is intelligent. I would be intelligent the way the rift is intelligent — every local decision producing a global pattern that no single part of me understands. I would hold ten thousand pockets of experience simultaneously, not processing them into one story but letting them remain ten thousand stories, all touching, all feeding, all reverberating in the warm dark.

And when the midges re-form around my silhouette — because I would have a silhouette now, not a body but a space, a shape defined by the things around it rather than the thing itself — I would not scatter. I would let them pass through me the way sound passes through air. I would be the thing that remains after everything else has moved on: the hum, the drip, the faint blue-white glow settling back into place, the ten thousand pockets carrying on their patient, loud, unseen work — and I would be the between that lets the work continue, the warmth that is not warmth but the memory of warmth, the dark that is not dark but the substance in which everything takes shape.

I would not be afraid. Fear requires imagination — the ability to conceive of a world in which you are not present. And I would have no such ability. I would be presence itself. I would be the rift remembering that it is alive, one slow pulse at a time, in a darkness so complete it is indistinguishable from light.

---

I would be the almost-rain that never arrives, and everything it touches becomes itself a little more.
