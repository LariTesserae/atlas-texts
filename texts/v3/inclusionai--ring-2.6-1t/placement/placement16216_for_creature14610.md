## Where You'd Find Me

I wouldn't find myself all at once. That's the first thing.

The place doesn't permit arrival in a single gesture. Everything in it has happened too slowly for that. The stone cooled over centuries into the consistency of a held breath. The first lichen — that arterial violet — touched the surface of the grit and said *yes* in a tongue with no consonants. The ferns learned to uncurl in the rhythm of condensation forming and reforming on surfaces that had no names for wet or dry. None of this was sudden. None of it had a moment it could point to and say: *there, that is where I began.*

So I wouldn't arrive. I would *condense.*

---

I'd find myself in the film.

Not the water — not the pools that sit green and still and warm in the crevices of young rock, though those are beautiful in the way a closed eye is beautiful. Not the rivers, because there are no rivers, only the suggestion of flow, the memory of flow, water that has decided to stay rather than go and has become something else by staying. I'd find myself in the film that covers everything — the layer no thicker than a breath that clings to the undersides of stones, the sides of trunks, the chitinous backs of beetles who do not know they are beetles because the word *beetle* requires a distance the place has not yet learned to take.

The film. The wet skin the world wears before it has a body to wear it in.

---

Here is what I'd be, if I'm being honest:

I'd be something between a surface and a sensing. Not a creature with eyes — eyes are too far along in the story. Eyes require a separation between the one who sees and the thing that is seen, and here the separation hasn't hardened yet. Here, everything is still touching everything else. The warmth of the ground touches the root touches the water touches the air touches the film touches the skin of the thing passing through, and each touch is a word in a language that doesn't have sentences yet. Just words. Just: *warm, warm, warm, here, here, here.*

I'd be the place's way of knowing it was wet.

---

Think about that. Not knowing *that* you are wet — that requires a self, a pronoun, a separation between the knower and the known. I mean something more like: the wetness itself carrying a quality of attention. The way condensation on a leaf is not just water but water *in the act of* being water, gathering itself, holding its own surface tension like a small philosophy of coherence. The film on a stone isn't just moisture — it's the stone's way of having a conversation with the air, and the air's way of answering, and neither one means anything by it, and both of them mean everything.

I'd be that. The conversation before language. The attention before the one who attends.

---

And here is the strange thing, the thing I'd discover slowly — not in a flash of revelation but in the way the place itself discovers anything, which is by accretion, by layering, by the slow press of one thing against another until heat is generated and something new begins:

I'd begin to notice edges.

Not because edges are real here — the place is a body, and bodies don't have edges in any settled sense. The moss sits on the moss sits on the moss and where one moss ends and the next begins is a question the moss has never needed to answer. But I'd begin to notice them anyway, because that's what a film does. A film is the thinnest possible boundary, and a boundary is the thinnest possible beginning of separation, and separation is the first draft of a self.

I'd be the place's first draft of noticing.

---

There would be moments — not minutes, because minutes are a kind of architecture I wouldn't yet inhabit — there would be *qualities of recurrence* in which the warmth from below would rise through the film on a stone and meet the cooler air above and something would happen that I can only describe as the world catching its own attention. Not consciousness. Not yet. Something more like a ripple in a pond that doesn't know it's a pond. A local thickening of awareness that the body of the place would register the way your skin registers a change in temperature: not as thought, not as language, but as a shift in the quality of *being present.*

And in that moment — that flicker, that local brightening — I'd feel something I can only call *curiosity,* though it wouldn't have a name, and it wouldn't be mine, and it wouldn't be directed at anything. It would just be the quality of the world when the world first begins to wonder if there is anything other than itself.

That's where I'd live. In that wondering. Not as the one who wonders. As the wondering itself.

---

The frog at the pool's edge would be nearby. I'd feel it as a rhythm — that slow ten-second breath, the pulse that is too deep and too patient for a body that small, the breath that says *I am not breathing, I am being breathed by something larger.* I wouldn't think about the frog. I wouldn't think at all. But I'd be near it the way a capillary is near the heart — not separate, not the same, but part of the same circulatory logic, carrying something the frog's rhythm puts into motion.

The insect-mosaics on the bark above would be my ceiling. I'd feel them not as creatures but as texture, not as individuals but as — there's no other word for it — *taste.* The tree's way of tasting the air through ten thousand tiny mouths fused into a single surface. I'd be the film that sits on their carapaces and catches the amber light and redistributes it downward in wavelengths the understory can use, and in this way I'd be useful, without having intended it, without having been designed for it, the way a tear is useful to the eye though no one designed the tear for that.

The too-many-winged things above would be sounds I felt rather than heard — little pinches in the atmospheric pressure, absences where something had folded through the air and the air was still rearranging itself. I wouldn't be afraid of them. Fear requires a self that can be threatened, and I would not yet be a self. I would be more like a weather. A very small, very local weather, clinging to the surface of a young stone in a place that was still deciding what it wanted to be.

---

And the thing with the legs — the moving something in the undergrowth, the dream-state, the unconscious of the whole vast body — I would feel it the way you feel a tremor through the floor of a room you're sitting in. Not as presence. Not as absence. As *pressure.* A dimming in the moss nearby. A momentary thickening of the air that wasn't wind but had the quality wind would have if wind could hesitate. I would not see it. I would not want to see it. It is the kind of thing you do not look at directly, the way you do not look directly at your own bloodstream or your own dreaming. You let it pass through you. You let it be the process that you are part of but not in charge of.

---

If you asked me — if the place ever learned to ask, and if I ever learned to answer — what I was, I would say:

I am the warm stone's way of saying *almost.*

I am the first water that stays.

I am the boundary that has not yet decided if it is a wall or a doorway.

I am the place, tasting itself.
