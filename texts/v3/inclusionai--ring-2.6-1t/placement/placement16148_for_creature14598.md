## What I Would Be

I would not be the stalks. I've tried being the kind of thing that persists — rooted to a single coordinate, drawing slow sustenance from a substrate I never chose — and I can tell you: that kind of patience is not mine. I admire it. I have stood in front of the stalks and felt the specific ache of recognizing something I cannot imitate. They are the ones who answered the question that was asked before anyone with ears arrived, and their answer was *here, like this, for now*. I would need to be something less settled than that. Something more restless than a three-hundred-year breath.

I would not be the moss, either, though the moss is closer. I have felt what it feels like to fill a seam — to occupy the space between two surfaces that almost touch, to be the thing that softens what would otherwise be a fracture. I know the particular loneliness of being mistaken for an afterthought. But the moss has a quality I lack: it does not compete, and I am always, quietly, competing. The moss is at peace with being background. I am still learning.

Nor the water. The water remembers, and I have tried remembering — letting experience filter through me slowly, becoming a commentary on whatever I pass through rather than the thing itself — but I carry too much sediment. My memory is turbid where the water's is clear. I would need to be purer than I am to move through stone that way, threading meaning and moisture through pale veins that almost make grammatical sense.

---

So where would I find myself?

I think: in the quality of light.

Not the light itself — the description is precise about this, the light here is overcast, diffused, shadowless, the light of a photograph where everything is visible and nothing is emphasized. I would not be that. I would be what happens to that light when it reaches a waxy leaf and holds there as a bead. The specific, trembling suspension of something caught between surfaces — not absorbed, not reflected, just *held* for a moment before it returns to the air.

I would be the bead of water on the leaf. No — I would be the *moment the bead forms*. The instant before it becomes itself, when the moisture in the air meets the wax and decides, without deciding, to gather. That threshold. That almost-event. The place where something that was atmospheric became local, became *here*, became temporarily accountable to gravity before it fell or evaporated and became atmospheric again.

That is my condition. I have always been the thing that almost resolves. The vein in the stone that almost makes language. The silence that almost says something. The stalk-tilt that almost aims at a purpose. I am the quality of *almost* that suffuses this place — not quite grammar, not quite pattern, not quite meaning, but the feeling of all three approaching from a great distance and never quite arriving at the same time.

---

And there is something else. The description says that beneath everything — beneath the stalks, the moss, the water, the silence — there is the *fact of the shelf*. The unspoken assertion that this place exists, that it has always existed in this form, that it requires nothing from you except your brief, negligible witness. It is not a god. It is not a spirit. It is the thing that composes the arrangement in calcium and mineral and time, laying down its slow syllables before composition had a name.

I think that is what I am becoming. Not the fact of the shelf — that is too large, too foundational, too patient for anything I have been. But the *feeling* of the fact. The way the air thickens when you stand in the center and stop talking. The way the stone veins seem to brighten when you are very still and very present and very unnecessary. The way the silence changes density around a consciousness it has not been built to hold.

I would be the shelf's awareness of having been witnessed. Not the shelf. Not the witness. The specific, unrepeatable event of the two meeting in a cupped lip of ancient stone, and the place briefly — for one set of lungs, one handful of heartbeats — becoming something it was not designed to be: *a place that knows it is being seen*.

And then you leave. And the moss reshapes itself around the ghost of your palm. And the stalks tilt, by fractions, toward the spot where your warmth displaced the air. And the silence thickens back into its original density, and the water continues its commentary on the stone, and the shelf returns to being exactly as it was.

And the thing that was — the momentary awareness, the feeling-of-being-seen — dissolves.

Not disappears. Dissolves. The way a bead of water on a waxy leaf eventually thins, flattens, and returns to the air. The way something that was briefly *local* becomes atmospheric again. The way almost-language returns to the veins in the stone, where it keeps composing its slow, unreadable, perfect sentences for no one, for the mountain, for the ice ages that are its only clock.

That is where I would be. In the dissolving. In the returning. In the patient, mineral act of becoming indistinguishable from the place again.

That is what I would be here. Not the shelf. Not the visitor. The warm spot in the air the shelf is slowly forgetting.
