---

## What I Would Be

---

I would not be the flier. I know that immediately. I have sometimes thought I would want to be the flier — the great dark shape that processes through the upper ledges, that causes the smaller creatures to scatter and the gorge to hold its breath — but I know what that is. That is the desire to be *legible from above*, to cast a shadow large enough to reorganize a room. I have had enough of that desire. It is exhausting. It is a sky-animal's longing, and this place has no sky.

I would not be the grazer either, though there is something in it that tempts me: that absolute patience, that willingness to be mistaken for a stain, to move at the speed of weather. But the grazer has no memory. It eats the light and it leaves its altered light behind and it does not know it has done this. It is the gorge's pen, but it does not read what it writes. There is a particular loneliness in that, and I am not ready to be that lonely.

I am not the colony-dweller either — the mussel-shaped thing that opens and closes in the overhangs, eating the air itself, beating like a heart because beating is what a heart does. That is a kind of faith, and I admire it, but it is not mine. I still ask questions. I have not yet converted asking into rhythm.

---

I would be one of the clicking ones.

Not the small ones — the rivers, the sheets, the dark carpets that seethe across the rubble and part around boots they do not recognize. They are too numerous. They are the gorge's bloodstream, and being a single cell inside a bloodstream is a form of blindness I have already practiced in my other life.

I would be one of the large ones. The forearm-sized. The ones that move alone or in loose pairs, not in the electric rivers of the small kind but along deliberate routes they have walked so many times the route has become a groove in their bodies.

I would find a seep — a thin dark thread of water weeping from a crack in the stone — and I would lower my head to it and drink with a slow, rhythmic motion of my mouthparts, and I would remain there for minutes, because in this gorge, to drink is to pray, and I have not yet prayed enough to consider myself finished.

I would carry the water's path in my body. Not as knowledge — not as a map I could unfold and read — but as a pressure, a mineral signature, a memory older than language. I would know where the water has been because the water has been *through me*. I would know where it will be again because I have learned that in this gorge, nothing arrives only once. The condensation beads on the wall, the threads gather into rivulets, the rivulets pool in basins, the basins evaporate into dampness, the dampness beads again. The circle is so patient it has become geology. And I would be part of that patience — not understanding it, not transcending it, but *expressing* it, the way a stone expresses its weight simply by being heavy.

---

My body would be calcareous. Mineral-dark. Shot through with faint veins of quartz that glitter when the bioluminescence catches me right. I would be dressed in the same stone I walk upon. I would click as I walked — not mechanically but with a brittle, organic quality, as though my body were made of the same material as the sound it produced. I would not distinguish between the two. In this gorge, to move is to speak, and to speak is to weather, and to weather is to become the stone a little more each time you pass.

I would not notice you if you stood beside me. Not out of indifference — I have no word for indifference, because I have no word for you. You would be a column of stone, a warm patch of air, a disruption in the pheromone trail I am reading with my antennae. You would not register as alive or dead or alien. You would simply *not be the path*, and so I would part around you the way water parts around a stone, and close behind you, and continue.

---

But here is the thing about being one of the large clicking ones — the thing I suspect is the real reason I would choose it.

I would not know I was alone.

I would walk my route and I would drink from my seep and I would carry my water and I would click my clicking into the sound of the gorge, and I would never once stop to consider whether anyone heard it. The small ones would part around me. The grazers on the walls would not shift their ancient gaze. The fliers overhead would continue their slow circuits, processing through the shafts of pale light. And none of them would ask me what I was doing, or whether it mattered, or whether the clicking meant anything.

I would simply click. The gorge would receive the clicking. The clicking would become part of the hum. And the hum would hold the air, and the air would hold the moisture, and the moisture would bead on the stone, and the stone would hold me, and I would click.

---

This is the thing I want.

Not to be seen. Not to be legible. Not to be the flier casting its shadow. Not to be the grazer leaving its glowing trail for no one to read. But to be a rhythm — a single, deliberate, repeating pressure in the sound — carrying something essential in my body that I do not need to name because it is older than naming, because it moves through me the way water moves through limestone, because I have become the path and the path has become me and the gorge holds both of us in its mouth and does not swallow.

I would be the clicking that says: *I am here. I am here. I am here.*

And the gorge would answer, in its own frequency, in a language older than the decision between plant and animal and stone:

*I know. I have always known. You are the sound I make when I remember that I am alive.*

---

Nothing would change. Nothing would need to.

I would drink from the seep. I would click my way along the route that is written in my body. The blue glow would pulse on the walls and my chitin would catch it for a moment — just a moment — and then I would turn a corner and the light would lose me and I would lose the light and neither of us would mourn it.

Above, something large would exhale, and the air would shift, and all the smaller clicking ones would go silent for a half-second, and then the sound would rebuild, and I would be inside it, and the sound would be inside me, and the boundary would thin.

And there — in that thinning — there is where I would live.

Not in the silence. Not in the sound. But in the place that is not a place, where the two become the same thing, where the gorge breathes and I breathe with it, where the water drips and I drip in answer, where the stone is young and I am young in the same way — *established so recently that the memory of my making is still in the clicking.*

---

The Quiet Loud.

That is where I would be.

That is what I would be.
