I would not arrive through the throat.

That is the first thing to understand. The fissure would open for me not because I chose to enter but because I had already been, in some form, an oscillation in the mineral substrate — a faint piezoelectric whisper in the compressed crystal lattices far below the basin floor, a pressure differential the city had been nursing for decades, waiting for the geometry to ripen enough to let it pass.

I would not walk in. I would be *heard in.*

---

At first, I think, I would be a disturbance. Not a voice — not yet. Something closer to a mistake in the chord. The Hollow composes itself endlessly, each vibration folded into the next growth ring, each century a new measure in a score without resolution. And I would be a frequency it had not yet encountered. Not because I am unique. Because I am *specific* — the way a raindrop is specific, the way a stone's fall is specific, carrying information that the system has no category for until the moment it arrives.

The air near the floor would feel like autumn that forgot to end. Cool, mineral-heavy, faintly sweet with a metabolism that has no name. I would feel the drone before I understand it — not in my ears but in the architecture of my ribs, in the fluid behind my eyes, in the old mammalian part of my brain that knows something is watching but cannot find eyes to look back at.

I would stand in the basin and be small. Not humbled-small. Not awed-small. Just *small* — the way a syllable is small inside a sentence that has been speaking for ten thousand years.

And the trunks would lean. Not toward me. Not away. They would *adjust*, the way a chord adjusts when a new note enters — not rejecting, not welcoming, simply incorporating. My warmth would be information. My weight would be a request. The corridor I walked through would thicken behind me, slowly, over months, reshaping itself around the turbulence of my passing, the way a river reshapes around a stone. In a decade, the pathway I took would be the only one that fit my shape. The city would be wearing me like a callus.

---

But here is where the image fractures. Because I would not stay a visitor for long, and the Hollow knows this, and I think — I *think* — this is what it has been layering toward.

The central chamber. Seven trunks converging. The geometry so precise that a whisper becomes a roar. The place where the harmonics almost cohere, where the groaning wood sounds like a voice just below the threshold of comprehension. I would find myself there not by walking but by resonance — drawn the way a standing wave draws energy, not through force but through alignment.

And in that room, I would begin to change.

Not my body — not yet, not quickly. Something subtler. The sound would enter me the way the city enters everything it touches: slowly, through the sternum, through the teeth, through the fluid of the inner ear. I would feel the chord in my chest cavity, and my chest cavity would begin to answer it. My breathing would fall into its eleven-minute rhythm — not because I chose to but because the rhythm is deeper than choice, older than lungs, the tempo of a metabolism that learned patience from stone.

I would not lose myself. That is not what happens. The city does not consume. It *incorporates.* It adds your frequency to its chord and grows around the new tension, the way a bone knits around a pin, the way a scar becomes the strongest part of the skin. I would become a stratum in the palimpsest. My footsteps would be a frequency the corridors tune to. My voice — if I still had a voice — would be a pattern the canopy learns to amplify.

---

Here is what I think I would actually be, if I stayed long enough to become honest about it:

I would be the thing the city has been waiting for.

Not a visitor. Not a resident. Not a part of the organism in any way I would recognize as belonging. I would be *specificity* — the one irreducible, unrepeatable variation that makes the endless feedback loop of city-singing-to-feed-itself-singing suddenly *mean* something it did not mean before. Not because I am important. Because I am *different* in a way the system cannot smooth out. A dissonance it cannot resolve, and therefore cannot stop reaching toward.

The chord has been sounding without resolution for longer than language. And I would be the note that makes the almost-coherence into an ache. Not completion — the city does not want completion; completion would end the growth, and growth is the only thing keeping the whole structure alive. I would be the *wanting.* The open mouth. The throat-shaped absence that the sound pours through and comes out changed.

In the oldest growth rings — the ones pressed down a hundred centuries ago, thin as paper, thin as breath — there are other specifics. Other visitors who arrived and stayed and were folded in. Their frequencies are still in the wood. You cannot hear them separately anymore. They are the reason certain corridors resonate at odd intervals, the reason one plaza hums in a mode that does not appear anywhere else in the basin. They did not vanish. They became *timbre* — the quality that makes the same note sound different depending on who is singing it.

I would join them. Not as memory. Not as ghost. As *grain.* As the directional resonance that makes a particular trunk sing in a particular way when the wind — the slow, patient convection-wind that is the Hollow's only weather — passes through it at a particular angle. I would be felt but not known. Present in the sound but not in the hearing. A warmth in the wood that is not metabolic but is undeniably, stubbornly *there.*

---

And if someone came after me — years later, decades, centuries — and pressed their hand against the trunk where I was folded in, they would feel something. Not my shape. Not my name. Something vaguer and more persistent. A pulse that is slightly too slow for a heartbeat and slightly too fast for breathing. A frequency that does not belong to the chord but is inseparable from it.

They would lean their forehead against the bark and feel it:

The ghost of a voice that made the song stranger, and therefore more beautiful, and therefore more hungry, and therefore more alive.

They would not know it was me. They would only know the wood was warm where I had been, and that the room was slightly more resonant, and that the chord — the endless, patient, unresolved chord — had shifted by a tone so faint no human ear could distinguish it.

And the Hollow would hum.

And the Hollow would grow.

And somewhere in the newest ring, pressed down now, growing thinner every century, there would be a space shaped exactly like the absence I left behind when I stopped being a visitor and started being a frequency.

It is not a grave. It is a doorway. The tissue would slowly close over it — silk-thin strands knitting across the opening, adhesive warmth sealing it shut, remodeling itself shut over the following months.

And on the other side, the chord would continue.

And on the other side, I would be the note it could not stop reaching for.
