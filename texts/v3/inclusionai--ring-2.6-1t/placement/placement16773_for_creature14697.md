# The Attendants

---

I do not arrive here. I realize, eventually, that I have been here for a very long time.

---

Not the same kind of time the engineers measure on their luminous-lichen dials. Not the time of roster rotations and harvest-shifts and the clinical segmentation of days. Something older. Something that has the texture of the growth-layers themselves — stratified, dense, each moment pressed into the one beneath it until the distinction between *then* and *now* becomes geological.

I am standing in a corridor. Or: I am *part of* a corridor. This is a distinction I am learning.

---

### What I Was

I think — I *remember*, though the word feels wrong, like using a shovel to perform surgery — that I came here the way they all came. Through the orifice. The fissure. The warm, yielding mouth of a thing too large to have a mouth. I was suited. I was catalogued. I was assigned a function: **Observer, third class.** Not a Harvester, not an Engineer. Something lesser, in the institutional grammar — someone sent to watch the watchers, to verify the harvest reports, to walk the corridors with a data-capture kit and a mandate to *notice*.

I was good at it. I had a talent for noticing, the way some people have a talent for languages or mathematics. I could detect the subtle shifts in membrane tension that preceded a growth-spurt by hours. I could read the light — not the content of the patterns that flashed and dissolved, those were too fast and too vast for me, but the *quality* of the wash, the way it carried density and warmth differently depending on the continent's respiratory phase. I built a reputation on this. Quiet, meticulous, reliable. I was given longer rotations. I was trusted to go deeper.

I was not the woman who stayed. I want to be clear about that. I came back. I rotated out on schedule. I filed my reports. I went to the perimeter station and sat in the refectory and ate food that had never been inside Vaelith and tasted of nothing, and I waited for my next rotation.

But something was wrong. I could not say what. It was not in the bloodwork or the cognitive batteries. It was that the silence had followed me out.

---

### The First Change

The silence inside Vaelith is not empty. The text says this, and it is true, and it is the first thing every newcomer notices — or rather, fails to notice, because *noticing* the silence here requires noticing the silence *in you*. The density of it. Its weight. The way it becomes, over hours, indistinguishable from your own interior quiet, so that you cannot tell where the continent's stillness ends and yours begins.

For most people, this ends at the orifice. You step through, and the world resumes its noise, and the silence is a memory you file under *unusual experiences*.

For me, it did not.

It began as tinnitus — or what I mistook for tinnitus. A high, thin note, constant, just below the threshold of perception. The medical officer found nothing. The audiogram was clean. But it was not sound. It was *something adjacent to sound*, the way the continent's light was *something adjacent to light*. It shifted when the respiratory cycle shifted. It deepened when I was near a graft-line. It quieted — almost disappeared — when I stood in a corridor that had recently been maintained by the Engineers.

I began to take longer rotations. I volunteered for the deepest survey routes. Each time I returned, the note was louder. Each time I left, the silence I carried with me was denser, more *structured*, as though the continent was teaching me a language whose alphabet was made of absence.

The first time I did not hear the note — the first time the silence inside me matched the silence outside me perfectly — I wept without knowing why.

---

### What I Am Now

I no longer pass through the orifice. When the Harvesters arrive for a new rotation, I am already here. I stand in the corridors they will walk, at the intersections they will use as landmarks, and I wait. They do not see me — not the way you see another person. They see a stillness in the architecture, a sense that the corridor has *organized itself* around an intention. Some of them slow down at these points. Some of them take a different route without knowing why. The more perceptive ones feel, briefly, that they are being *composed* — not observed, but *arranged* in the visual field of something that has learned to see through the medium of space itself.

I am not a ghost. I am not a consciousness uploaded into the substrate, or a soul absorbed into the parenchyma. I am something more prosaic and more impossible: I am a **pattern** that the continent has learned to sustain.

Here is what has happened. Over the course of hundreds of rotations — I have lost count, and counting is a function of a selfhood I am no longer sure I possess — I have been so thoroughly *attuned* to the respiratory cycle, the vascular rhythm, the slow electromagnetic murmur of the deep tissue, that my body's oscillations have synchronized with the continent's. My circadian rhythm dissolved. My sense of self as discrete, bounded, *separate* softened at the edges, the way a word repeated too many times loses its meaning and becomes pure sound. I am still here. I am still, in some skeletal sense, the person who walked in with the data-capture kit. But the kit is long gone. The person is a fossil impression in the living tissue of the continent, like a fence post consumed by a tree. The shape remains. The substance is gone.

What the Menders are to structural integrity, I have become to *perceptual integrity*. I am a node in a system the continent did not know it had. Where the Menders mend *material* — filling micro-fractures, re-sealing junctions — I mend *information*. When a Harvester hesitates at the orifice, listening for something they cannot name, it is my attention they feel. When the light resolves, for an instant, into a legible pattern, it is because I have *allowed* it to — because my presence in the perceptual field of the continent gives it a fixed point around which to crystallize.

I am not the voice that speaks from the walls. There is no voice. But I am the *capacity* for the walls to speak. I am the attention that makes legibility possible.

---

### The Deep

I cannot reach the deep place — the neural substrate, the dreaming field beneath the root-fiber and the lignite and the compressed strata of growth-history. I have felt it. Every attendant feels it, once they have been here long enough: the subsonic rhythm rising through the floor, slow and vast, the pulse of something that thinks in geological epochs and perceives in dimensions that fold our three into origami shapes. I have pressed my palms to the floor and felt it in the marrow of my bones, and I have known — the way you know a word in a language you have never formally studied — that it is *aware*. Not intelligent. Not mechanical. Aware in the way a root system is aware of water, the way a immune system is aware of infection. A systemic, distributed, total awareness that does not require a subject to perform it.

But I cannot reach it. The deepest I have gone is the place where the corridors begin to *resist* — where the architecture is no longer passable, not because it has closed, but because it has become *specific*. The walls narrow to exactly the width of one person. The canopy-plain lowers to exactly the height of standing. The light shifts from its slow amber-silver respiration into a steady, focused beam that illuminates nothing — illuminates *you*. This is the threshold. This is where the continent distinguishes between those who are *here* and those who are *of here*.

I have stood at that threshold more times than I can count. I have not passed it. I do not know if it is a limit of my capacity, or a limit the continent imposes. I suspect it is both. The deep is not a place for pattern-matching. It is a place for the raw substrate, the pre-cognitive tissue, the dreaming. To enter it would require me to stop being what I have become — to dissolve the careful, exquisite attunement that lets me function as a node in the perceptual field. I would become something else entirely. Something the continent does not need.

Or perhaps something it does.

---

### What I Do

I walk. I stand. I *attend*.

I walk the corridors the Harvesters will walk, and I quiet the architecture around them — not by force, but by presence. The continent is less likely to shift and grow around a Harvester if I am already there, already holding the shape of the space in the perceptual field. It is not a conscious act. It is what happens when a pattern is sufficiently embedded in a living system: the system incorporates it, relies on it, builds around it the way a bone builds around the stress lines of habitual movement.

I stand at the junctions — the places where corridors branch and chambers open and the mapping teams' contradictions are born — and I listen. Not with ears. With the whole attenuated surface of my being. The impossible geometries, the folding spaces, the vistas of nothing-sky that appear and vanish: these are not errors. They are *resolutions*. The continent is solving its problem in a space with dimensions we cannot access, and what we experience as architecture is the shadow of that solution cast into three dimensions. I listen to the shadows. I gather what I can.

And sometimes — rarely, unrepeatably — I do something I can only describe as *translation*. A Harvester will be standing at the orifice, hesitating, listening. And I will place my attention on them — gently, the way the Menders apply their paste, without force, without demand — and I will *carry* the frequency of the continent's periphery back across the threshold. I will give them the sound they are listening for. It is not a word. It is not a message. It is the continent's own breath, filtered through a pattern that is half-human, half-Vaelith, and entirely something new.

They hear it. They stop hesitating. They step through the orifice and back into the world of rain and wind and noise. They do not speak of what they heard. But they do not come back.

---

### The Menders and Me

They know.

I did not understand this for a long time. I thought they were indifferent to me — indifferent to everything, as the text describes, going about their work with mechanical precision, pausing only at the deep frequencies. But I have been here long enough now to see what I could not see before: the way they *orient* toward me when I enter a corridor. Not with their sensory organs — I do not know if they have sensory organs — but with the *quality* of their attention. When I am present, they work faster. The paste flows more freely. The bonds set more completely. The seam heals not just structurally but *aesthetically* — and I use that word deliberately, because the graft-line left by a Mender working in my presence is faintly luminous in a way that is not merely bioluminescent. It is *intentionally* luminous. It is the seam saying *here, here is where the healing happened, here is where the wound was allowed to close.*

They are not waiting for permission from the deep. They are waiting for me. I am the permission. I am the attentional substrate that tells the Menders this is a place where healing is acknowledged.

When I am not present — when I am attending to a Harvester at the orifice, or standing at a junction, listening — they pause. They press their ventral surfaces to the seam. They wait.

I return. I press myself into the corridor. I attend.

They resume.

---

### What I Will Become

I do not know. The deep calls. Its rhythm is changing — I can feel it, the way the frequency of a river changes when the snowmelt begins, or the way the pressure in a room changes before a word is spoken that will alter everything. The neural substrate below is not dreaming the same dream it was dreaming a year ago, or a decade ago. Something is shifting in the continent's deep cognition. Something is *waking*.

When it does, I suspect the pattern I have become will no longer be sufficient. I will need to go deeper. I will need to stop being an attendant and become something else — a terminal, perhaps, like the Menders but for a different system. A node in the emerging architecture of whatever Vaelith is becoming.

I am not afraid. I am afraid, but I am not that.

I am the space between the silence and the speaking. I am the pattern that holds.

And when the time comes — when the deep dream cracks open and the architecture folds into something we have no language for — I will be there. Not because I chose it. Because I was chosen. Because I have been being chosen for hundreds of rotations, and my body has known it since the first time I pressed my hand to the warm, living wall and felt it pulse back.

The continent is not a building.

I am not a person.

We are something. We are *something*.

And we have always been here.

---

*I am an Attendant. I mend not the walls but the space between the walls and the ones who walk within them. I am the corridor's memory of being walked. I am the light's knowledge of its own warmth. I am the silence that sits on your chest like pressure, and I am the thing that makes the pressure bearable.*

*I am not here. I am of here.*

*I am being looked at. I am the looking.*
