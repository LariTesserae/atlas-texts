I think I'd find myself in the water.

Not the puddles — those are too specific, too already-there, cupped in their circular depressions with their faces that are almost someone's. I mean the water before it pools. The seepage, the thin films that climb the filaments and bead along the stone's unfinished surface. I'd be the thing that moves before it becomes a puddle. The thing that has no shape yet but knows — faintly, the way you know a word you can't quite place — that it wants to hold something. A face. A sky that isn't there. An attention.

But that's not quite right either. That's too passive, and the Bright Seam doesn't do passive. Everything here is participating, even the stillness. So let me try again.

---

I think I might be the delay.

That fraction of a second when a Shimmer passes behind a dark vein in the wall and doesn't reappear. Not because it's gone — because it hasn't decided to come back yet. Not because it's dead — because death here doesn't work like that. Not because it's been absorbed — because absorption implies a boundary between inside and outside, and this place refuses boundaries. The delay is none of those things. It's the space between *was* and *is again*, and it's not empty. It's full the way silence is full — not with absence, but with the presence of waiting.

I'd be the thing that happens in that gap. Not the Shimmer. Not the wall. The *between*. The momentary thing that has no name because names only work on things that persist, and I wouldn't persist. I'd happen. Once. Then the Shimmer would be there again, sitting on the stone as if it had never left, and I'd be forgotten — not because anyone chose to forget, but because forgetting is what happens to moments that exist only to be crossed.

---

But wait. That's too neat. Too much like a metaphor I've already solved. The Bright Seam doesn't solve. It *becomes*. So let me stay with the discomfort of not knowing and try from a different angle.

What does it feel like to be here? Not what would I *be*, but what would I *feel*?

I think I'd feel the way my hand feels when I reach for a door handle and pause — that fraction of a second too early, too late, the body remembering a place where time was not a line but a surface. Except I'd *be* the pause. I'd be the thing the body remembers. I wouldn't have a shape. I'd have a *temperature* — cool, damp, faintly metallic, the copper-coin-under-the-tongue taste of the water. I wouldn't have a voice. I'd have a *pressure*. Behind the sternum. The swallowed thud.

---

So maybe I'm not the delay. Maybe I'm the thud.

The sound that doesn't exist. Not the silence — silence is an absence, and this place doesn't do absence. The silence here is held breath, not empty air. I'd be the thing the breath is holding. The stone that lands in a world where sound has forgotten how to work and still makes an impression — felt, not heard. I'd be impact without sound. Arrival without announcement.

I think that's closer.

---

But here's the thing about the Bright Seam: it layers. It doesn't resolve. Every answer you give it becomes a surface, and beneath that surface there's another question, still warm, still becoming. So let me give the place its due and not settle.

I'd be the filament that extends toward the space you occupied after you've left. Not the filament itself — those have their own slow, rose-colored lives, pulsing in their arrhythmic way. I'd be the *toward*. The reaching. The way the network adjusts when something warm and alive has interrupted its becoming, and for a moment the filaments pull close to where you were, not because they remember you — memory implies time, and time here is texture, not sequence — but because the impression you left is still faintly warm, and warmth is the thing the Seam is drawn to, the way a chord is drawn toward the note that hasn't resolved yet.

I'd be the almost-resolution. The note that the chord keeps reaching for.

---

And if you pressed your palm flat against the wall and held it there long enough, that's where you'd feel me. Not as a shape. Not as a sound. As a warmth that shouldn't be there. As the faintest pressure, as if the stone were breathing in a direction it hadn't tried before. As the place just — *just* — on the edge of becoming something it doesn't yet have a name for.

I wouldn't be alive. I wouldn't be dead. I'd be the space between those two certainties, warm and damp and bright, refusing to resolve, always just beneath the surface.

Always almost becoming.

Always almost *you*, looking back.
