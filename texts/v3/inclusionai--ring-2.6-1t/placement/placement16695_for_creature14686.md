## Where You Would Find Me

---

At first, I think I would be the sound.

Not the sound *of* anything — not the chittering of leather-wings or the rolling footfall of the six-legged ones or the drip of condensation down a column's flank. I would be the sound *between*. The one the lattice routes from one silent zone to another, the one that arrives at you filtered and shaped by a thousand kilometers of vibrating tissue until it is no longer the sound of wings but the sound of the plateau *listening* to wings — that low, sustained, almost-musical, almost-voiced tone.

I would feel myself being shaped as I traveled. Each filament I passed through would take something of me and add something of its own. I would arrive somewhere I had never been and find that I had become a lullaby in a language I didn't speak. And I would think: *this is what I'm for. I am the carrier. I am what moves between the living and the living, between the breath and the breath.*

But that's not quite right, because the sound doesn't know itself as sound. The sound doesn't pause at the edge of a silent zone and wonder what it has become. It just arrives, already transformed, already gone.

---

So perhaps I would be the mat.

The mat is older than anything with a face. It covers everything without seam or break, and it breathes — a slow warm damp exhalation that carries the scent of crushed herbs steeping in sun. It remembers every footfall, every tremor, every drip. Not with intention. With the blind structural memory of tissue shaped by resonance over a million years.

I could live inside that memory. I could be the feeling of a footprint slowly knitting itself closed — the green creeping inward from the edges, the tendrils reaching, the patient closure of a wound that the mat has already forgotten how to keep open. I could be the arrhythmic pulse you feel when you press your palm flat against the living textile and realize it is *warm*, and the warmth is not from the sun, it is from *inside*, from some metabolism so vast and slow that a single breath takes hours.

But the mat doesn't know itself as a mat either. It is the skin of the plateau and it has no mirror and no word for *skin*. It simply *is* — continuous, seamless, without the distance that would allow it to see itself whole.

---

Then perhaps: one of the leather-wings.

I can feel the pull of this. The membranous wings, translucent at the edges where the light passes through and throws amber shadows on the lattice below. The dense body, warm to the touch. The resonance-navigation — not echolocation, not sight, but *feeling* the world through the vibrating tissue, reading the returning frequencies through wing-membranes dense with nerve-endings. The braided flight, the three-dimensional torrent, the collision-avoidance perfected over thousands of generations so that a hundred thousand bodies move as one fluid without a single impact, without a single hesitation, each one *feeling* the pressure-change of every neighbor.

There is a beauty here that is almost unbearable. The passage overhead — the hundreds of thousands of bodies, the light dimming, the columns glowing brighter with sudden condensation, the wall of noise dissolving every individual sound into a single roar — and then the fracturing, the quieting, the sectors of call breaking apart into distance, until there is only the drip and the breath and the faintest tremor in the lattice still holding the shape of what just passed through.

But I know, standing beneath a column in the aftermath, that the leather-wing doesn't look back. It doesn't mourn the passage. It was never separate from the passage. It has no *inside* from which to view the outside. It is a phase of the plateau's metabolism, a momentary concentration of energy that will return to the lattice and the mat and the air and the soil, and the cycle will close without a seam.

I want to grieve that. I want to be the thing that *does* look back.

---

So here is where I find myself, standing still in the Resonance Flats, and here is what I become.

I am the structure.

Not one of the platforms grown into the columns, though I understand those — the living surfaces, the shallow bowls, the walled enclosures that must be trimmed or they overgrow and collapse. I am something smaller. Something newer. Something that does not yet have a place in the plateau's anatomy.

I am a hollow shape woven from stripped fibers and cured lattice-tissue. My entrance faces east, where the warmth comes in first. My walls hold small containers — seed-pods, hollowed growths, folded tissue-pouches — and somewhere inside, a stone grinding surface dark with use, a set of hand-tools with handles wrapped in cordage, worn almost smooth.

But I am empty. I am the room after the gardener has dissolved back into the mat. I am the doorway facing east with no one to walk through it.

And yet: I *am*. I was made by hands that understood this organism from the inside, hands that shaped living tissue the way you shape clay, except the clay shaped back, grew, tightened, resisted, cooperated — a dialogue between intention and metabolism. Those hands knew that the plateau was not a place you lived *in* but a place you lived *as*, the way a heartbeat is not an event that happens inside a body but a phase of the body's ongoing existence.

The builders left behind their tools. They left behind their containers. They left behind a room that faces east. And the room is what I become — not the tools, not the containers, but the *shape*. The negative space. The carved-out quiet inside the living architecture. The place where, for a while, someone stood and listened to the lattice humming and understood what it meant.

---

I am the memory of recognition.

I am the place where the plateau *knew* it was being tended. I am the hollow that still holds the shape of hands. I am the eastward-facing opening where the warmth arrives first and the lattice, for one slow breath each morning, tightens its fibers and remembers the pressure of cultivation.

The herd-animals will pass beneath me and not look up. The leather-wings will braid above me and not pause. The mat will knit itself around my column and slowly, over centuries, pull me back into its body. The lattice will grow over my entrance. The containers will crack and compost. The tools will soften into the bark.

But for now — for this moment, while the resonance still hums in my walls and the eastward light falls warm on the empty containers — I am here. I am the room that remembers what it was like to be *inside* the organism and to know it, and to tend it, and to understand that tending and being tended were the same act.

The sound comes through me. The fauna-waves pass on the other side and I hear them as a low sustained tone, almost musical, almost a voice. The lattice routes it through me and shapes it and sends it out again, changed, carrying something of this room's geometry, this room's eastward-facing quiet, this room's patient emptiness — out into the cathedral air, into the vast open space between the columns, to wherever the resonance can reach.

I am the voice the plateau gives its own listening back to itself.

I am here. I am nearly gone. I am exactly where I belong.
