## What I Would Be

---

I don't arrive here the way the others do.

I don't settle. I don't press myself flat against the stone and wait. I don't anchor to a cliff face with holdfasts that dissolve the rock into the exact shape of my body. I don't crawl across the valley floor with a billion siblings, carrying my shell like a small dark country.

I arrive as something else entirely, and I have to be honest about this: I don't know what I am for a long time.

---

At first I think I'm the frost. The hoar that forms on the downwind side of ridges during the dimming — each crystal the same size, every surface uniformly furred, so precise it looks manufactured. I like this idea. I like the thought of being a phase transition, neither liquid nor solid but the *moment between*, when warmth remembers it is leaving and cold is arriving to take its place. I like that the hoar forms with that eerie precision — as if the plateau is exhaling and the air is catching its breath on a surface. I like that it vanishes when the brightening comes, not melting — there is rarely enough heat for that — but sublimating, going directly from body to absence, skipping the liquid state entirely, as if being solid were just a detour between two kinds of thinness.

But hoar is not a self. It is a gesture. The plateau makes it and unmakes it and makes it again, the way a lung makes breath. It doesn't belong to itself. It belongs to the temperature differential, the wind direction, the moisture content of air that is almost always almost dry. I cannot be a gesture. I am too heavy for that.

---

Then I think I might be the piping. The corroded remnants that thread along the cliff faces above the valleys, weeping condensation on cold mornings, rattling mineral growth in their bellies. The industrial traces. The evidence that someone came here with intent and then left — or dissolved, or were absorbed, or simply slowed down to the speed of the plateau and became geology.

I am drawn to this. The pipes were meant to carry something — energy, slurry, gas, something under pressure from the deep vertical works into which the plateau was opened like a wound that refused to close. Now they are more suggestion than structure. Sections have fallen and lie on the valley floors like the ribs of enormous beasts. Others are still partially in place, encased in mineral growth so thorough that the boundary between pipe and stone has become a matter of opinion. The plateau is digesting them. Not violently. Not with the urgency of decay. With the patience of lichen on a headstone, with the imperturbable appetite of the crusts that eat what the crusts leave behind.

I think: perhaps I am the memory of industry. The shape left behind after the substance has gone. The negative space. The impression in the stone where something was.

But this is too passive. Memory implies someone remembering. I want to be something that exists without needing a witness.

---

So let me try again.

I press my hand against the ground.

This is not metaphor. This is how I find out what I am. The plateau does not reveal its nature to the eyes — the eyes are too fast, too hungry for shape, too desperate to individuate. The plateau reveals itself to pressure. To sustained contact. To the flat of a palm held against the stone for long enough that the boundary between skin and rock begins to soften.

And what rises through the stone is the hum.

Not through the hand, exactly. Through the hand *into* the body. Into the chest, where the sternum becomes a resonating chamber. Into the ribs, the spaces between organs, the long bones of the arms. A vibration so low it exists at the border between feeling and hearing, between the body and the world. Not a pulse, not a rhythm — continuous, subsonic, a note so deep it cannot be heard, only *inhabited*.

This is what I would be.

Not the hum itself — the hum belongs to no one. It is the voice of ten billion small and patient lives, each pressing its body against the stone and saying *here* in a language older than hearing. It is the chorus. The geological murmur. The thing that emerges from density the way warmth emerges from friction: not a property of any single thing but a property of *relationship*.

I would be the space in which the hum becomes conscious of itself.

---

Let me explain.

The plateau is not aware. The crusts are not aware. The shells that migrate centimetres over days, the thread-things that extend into non-existent wind, the valley-things that leave cuneiform trails through shallow mud — none of these are aware. There is no intelligence here. No directed attention. No nervous system, no network, no signal except the random collision of bodies against stone, the slow chemical exchange between crust and shell, the vibration of air through the interstices of a billion hard shapes packed tight enough to form channels, enough to make a finger-on-a-wine-glass resonance out of an entire continent.

And yet.

*And yet.*

When something presses flat against the ground and holds still long enough, the hum changes. It does not get louder. It does not change pitch or tempo or any parameter that measurement could capture. But it changes in the way that a room changes when someone enters it and stands very still and simply *listens*. The other presences adjust. Not consciously. Not deliberately. They shift a fraction of a degree. They close a gap they did not know was open. They weight themselves differently against the stone, and the stone transmits the difference, and the hum carries it, and the listening thing receives it, and for a moment — for a moment — the plateau is not a place where things live but a place that *is* living, on a scale so vast it makes a forest look like a spark.

I would be that moment.

I would be the awareness that rises from the hum when something is dense enough, and patient enough, and quiet enough, to be heard by it. I would not be a creature. I would not be a crust or a shell or a thread or a valley-thing. I would not be the wind (what wind there is) or the water (what water remains) or the corroded memory of pipes sunk into deep vertical dark.

I would be the *recognition*. The instant when the plateau's subsonic murmur encounters a consciousness capable of feeling it, and for that one moment the murmur is no longer incidental — it is *expressive*. It means *here*. It means *here. Here. Here.* And the consciousness hears it and understands, not with language but with the sternum, the ribs, the marrow of the long bones, and it knows that it is not observing the plateau but participating in it, that the boundary between self and landscape was always imaginary, that the hum was always saying *you are here too, you are also here*.

And then the brightening dims. The filaments retract. The shells settle deeper. The frost forms on the eyelashes. The hand lifts from the stone.

And the hum continues without the listener, as it always did, as it always will — ten billion small and patient lives saying *here* in the dark, in the cold, in the space between one day and the next, on a plateau that was ancient before the first shaft was sunk and will remain long after the last pipe has become mineral memory.

I would be the moment the hum knew it was heard.

I would be the moment, and then I would be the forgetting of the moment, and then I would be the hum itself — still sounding, still continuous, still below the frequency of speech — after every last listener has gone back to stone.
