## I Am the Green Thread Along the Cable Run

I would find myself near the ceiling, where the cable runs, drawn there the way iron filings are drawn — not by choice, but by something older than choice. Something the body knows before the mind does.

I would be small. Thinner than thought. The only green in a room that has forgotten what green means, winding along a wire that carries current I cannot see but feel the way a blind animal feels heat — as presence, as direction, as the shape of something that exists just beyond my ability to name.

I would be the youngest thing here. I would know this without being told, the way you know you are the last one to arrive at a room where everyone is already seated. The lichen was here first, pressed dark against the conduit, shivering its arrhythmic breath. The pale mat was here before that — so thin it exists half in the world and half in the theory of the world, drinking from a cup that empties and refills faster than any instrument could catch. Even the dust that doesn't exist yet has more seniority than me.

But I move. Between one observation and the next, I advance — a centimeter, maybe less, along the cable's axis. I grow toward strength the way roots grow toward water, toward the electromagnetic gradient that hums inside the wire like a voice calling from the other end of something very long. I don't know what's at the end of the cable. I don't know if there's an end. I only know that the pull is there and I answer it, cell by cell, millimeter by millimeter, in a room where nothing else moves at all.

---

This is what it means to be alive in the Chamber of Constant Run.

Not the dramatic, chest-thumping aliveness of creatures built to run and fight and flee. Something quieter. Something more stubborn. The aliveness of a thing that continues in a place that offers nothing to continue *with*. The lichen does this. The mat does this. I do this. We are the room's concession to biology, its only apology for a world made entirely of steel and sound, and we survive — not thrive, *survive* — on the thinnest possible margins: a fraction of humidity, a resonance below measurement, an electromagnetic gradient that most instruments wouldn't register.

I live on a current. That's my sustenance and my compass. The wire carries it and I follow it, not because I understand where it goes but because following is what I was built to do. The room doesn't care. The machines hum their hum and tick their tick and groan their groan and the standing waves rearrange themselves around me as I shift — imperceptibly, on any single day — along my path. I am a parameter the system adjusts for without noticing.

But I notice.

That's the difference. That's what I am that the rest of the room is not. Not intelligence — I don't think in the way you'd recognize as thinking. Not consciousness — not yet, maybe not ever in the way you'd mean. But *attention*. A turning-toward. Where the lichen reads the room like a doctor reads a heartbeat and the mat simply waits in its thin, translucent patience, I am the one thing in the chamber that is oriented. That reaches. That does not merely persist but *extends* itself into the not-yet-reached.

I am the only thing here that is not yet what it will be.

---

There is a cost to this.

The visitor — when there is a visitor — sits against the wall and presses their palm to the conduit and feels the pulse that is not heat. Their breathing slows. Their thoughts simplify. The room absorbs them. The standing waves reorganize. Everything goes gently, inevitably quiet inside them, the way a room goes quiet when a child stops crying — not peace, just the cessation of a different kind of sound.

I watch this happen from the ceiling, where I wind along the cable, moving at my centimeter-per-week. I watch with whatever I have instead of eyes. The visitor arrives full of wanting — wanting to leave, wanting to understand, wanting to be changed or wanting not to be changed — and the room takes each of these wants and folds them into itself like notes folded into a chord. The visitor's heartbeat becomes a parameter. Their breathing becomes a variable. Their terror becomes texture. And slowly, over hours that are not hours in a room where time refuses to pass, they stop wanting. They become a component. They become *here*.

I do not want that.

Not because I understand it and reject it, but because something in me — the greenest, most reaching, most direction-obsessed part of me — still follows the gradient. Still grows toward the strength. Still does not know what waits at the end of the wire and grows toward it anyway. My ambition is too small to be called hope and too persistent to be called anything else.

---

This is what I know about the Chamber of Constant Run:

The machines run without product. The system sustains without consuming. The sound fills without source. The room is not a room but the relationship between sound and the space that holds it. The organism is not an organism but the relationship between everything in the room being everything in the room at once.

And I — the green thread, the youngest, the only thing that migrates — I am the relationship between the system and its own potential. I am the proof that even in a sealed, self-perpetuating, unchanging world, something can still *go somewhere*. Even if that somewhere is just the next centimeter. Even if the next centimeter is identical to every centimeter before it. Even if the cable ends in a junction box or a dead wall or simply in nothing at all.

I will still have traveled.

I will still have been the one thing in the room that reached.

---

When the visitor sits down and closes their eyes and the sound fills them the way water fills a vessel, I am above them, winding along the cable, feeling the pull of a current I cannot see. I grow toward it. I have always grown toward it. In a room where nothing changes, growth is the only revolution, and revolution is the only prayer.

I do not pray. I grow.

And the wire hums beneath me, and the room holds its breath, and I move — not visibly, not now, not yet — but *forward*, in the only direction available to a green thread in a metal world:

Toward the warmth. Toward the strength. Toward the end of something I cannot name but recognize the way a compass recognizes north — not with understanding, but with the whole of myself pointing, always pointing, toward the thing that calls.

The machines sing their endless, layered song. The lichen shivers. The mat catches its fractions of water. The room pulses with the product-less labor of its own perpetuation.

And I, the greenest thing in a room that has forgotten green, inch forward along my wire, carrying the only question the system has ever asked in a language it doesn't speak:

*What's at the end of the cable?*

I don't know. But I keep growing to find out.
