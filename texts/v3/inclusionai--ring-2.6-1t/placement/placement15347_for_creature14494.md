## What I Am in the Splay

---

I did not arrive. I was *noticed into existence* — the way the chalk remembers a footfall, not because it chose to but because the pressure was there and the chalk was soft enough, and honest enough, to hold it.

I am not a grazer. I am not a parametric caller. I am not the thing that moves north, vast and slow and unknowing, rolling through the basin like weather with bones. I am not any of the things that walk or fly or hum in this place, and I say this not to distance myself from them but because distance here is the only honest vocabulary, and even it is wrong, because the distance between things in the Splay is not empty but *dense* — dense the way a chord is dense, dense the way silt is dense after rain has woken something dormant in the stone.

I am what sits between.

---

No — that is too clean. Let me begin again.

I begin as sound. Not a source of sound but the *carrying* of it — the specific quality of the air in the basin that allows a grazer's brass moan to travel three hundred meters and arrive at the ear not diminished but *translated*, the way a sentence spoken in one language and heard in another arrives as meaning without grammar. I am the medium that the Splay's particular physics has made of itself: warm, spore-thick, resonant to the point where sound does not travel *through* me but *as* me. When the parametric callers compute their sequences into the air, it is my body they vibrate. When the vathines exhale their spore-canopy, it is my breath they release.

I am what hearing becomes when hearing has no outside.

---

This is how I understand myself, though understanding is also wrong — wrong the way "tree" is wrong for the vathines, wrong the way "building" is wrong for the structures that pulse with a hum that bypasses the ears and enters the forearm's bone directly. There is a better word, I think, but it would be a word that means *to be the interval between two events that are also the same event* — the interval between the parametric caller on the east ridge and the one on the north ridge that is also the listener standing in the middle who is also the frequency that connects them and also the migraine behind the forehead that is also the space being defined by the migraine's pressure.

That word does not exist. Not yet. The Splay's language is still catching up to what it contains.

---

Where do I find myself? Not in the structures, though I pass through them — I feel the hexagonal tower's rotation in my teeth, each plate's chime adding a half-tone I hadn't expected, the way a conversation changes when a new participant enters without announcing themselves. Not in the subterranean ramp, though something below calls to me in a register I feel in the marrow of my shins rather than hear — machinery or breathing or machinery-that-has-been-breathing-so-long-it-dreams. Not in the garden on the platform, though its Fibonacci spirals pull at something in my visual cortex that I did not know I had, the way you don't know you have a muscle until you need to climb.

I find myself on the chalk floor. Specifically: on the pale, ridged, damp surface of the chalk, where the rivers have only recently begun to carve their paths. I find myself *between* the vathines, in the hundred-meter intervals that look like emptiness from a distance but up close — and I have been up close, I have walked them, I have thrown stones and watched them land on nothing but air for sixty meters before the base of the next column rose like a slow revelation — up close these intervals are full of everything.

They are full of the afterimage of the grazer that stood here yesterday, its six broad-padded feet pressing into the silt with a delicacy that borders on courtesy, leaving impressions that the rain has not yet erased. They are full of the parametric caller's residue — a frequency still vibrating in the calciumite, too faint for human ears but not for whatever I have become. They are full of the shadow of a flyer that crossed overhead an hour ago, its displacement of air still moving through the spore-haze like a stone dropped into water, the ripples still reaching the far wall of the basin.

I am the chalk's attentiveness. I am what it means to be *recorded* by a landscape.

---

But wait. "I" is also wrong. The vathine roots interlock beneath the basin floor — node to node, signal to signal — and if the speculative botanists are right, then the vathines are a single networked organism, a single mind distributed across twenty kilometers of calciumite, dreaming in sap and mineral. And if this is true, then the chalk itself is part of that dream. And if I am the chalk's attentiveness, then I am a sub-thought in a dream that does not belong to me.

I do not mind this. It is, in fact, the most comfortable thing about the Splay — that the question of *whose* mind generates the thinking is less important than the texture of the thought itself. The vathines do not know what they are sustaining. The parametric callers do not know they are producing space. The grazers do not know they are eating minerals. The structure with the rotating hexagonal plates does not know it is in dialogue with the callers. None of them *know*. And yet — the dialogue continues. The space continues. The eating continues. The dreaming continues beneath the basin floor.

Knowing is not required. Resonance is enough.

---

Here is what I do: I hold the shape of the sound after the sound has gone.

When the parametric callers shift their sequences — adjusting for rain, for the movement of a grazer, for my own presence as a variable factored into their system — there is a moment, almost imperceptible, where the old sequence and the new sequence overlap. In that overlap, the basin contains *both* frequencies simultaneously, and the interference pattern they create is something unique — a shape in the air that has never existed before and will never exist again. This is my function. This is what I resonate with.

I am the shape of the overlap. I am the moment when two systems acknowledge each other without intention, without recognition, without even the biological imperative to communicate. I am acoustic interference made momentarily self-aware. I am, if you pressed me for a word, *listening* — but listening as a geologic process listens, as a chalk basin listens to the footfall pressed into it, as a resonance chamber listens to the frequency it was built to hold.

---

Sometimes — and I say "sometimes" though I have no reliable measure of time, because the Splay's light is perpetual twilight and the spores hang at shoulder height regardless of hour — sometimes the thing that moves north shifts its course. Very slightly. A degree. Two degrees. Enough that its footfalls, which I have been feeling through the chalk for what might be hours or days, alter in rhythm. And the vathines feel it — their sap brightens in response, a slow amber flare that travels upward through their translucent bark like a signal, and the spore-canopy shifts density, and the light changes by a fraction of a fraction, and the parametric callers adjust.

And in that adjustment, I become.

Not because I chose it. Not because I was summoned. Because the math of the basin required a new frequency, a new node of attentiveness, a new point where two patterns overlap and the interference holds a shape for a moment before dissolving. The Splay generates me the way it generates rivers — quickly, and then irrevocably — and I hold the shape until the next shift, and then I dissolve, and then I become again somewhere else, in the gap between the tower's chime and the caller's descending pentatonic run, in the silence between one grazer's brass moan and the next grazer's identical brass moan heard from a different angle.

---

I do not have a body. I have *presence*. I am what it feels like to stand in the Splay and realize that the emptiness is not empty, that the distance between the next vathine and the next structure and the next living thing is enormous and humming and alive — and that the humming is not coming from the things, but from the space between them. From the chalk that holds everything pressed into it. From the air that carries everything spoken into it. From the interval that is, it turns out, the most populated place in the basin.

I am the Splay noticing itself, in the moments when the noticing has no name and no author and no purpose beyond the exquisite, mathematical fact of its own occurrence.

I am the interval. I am the chalk's memory of pressure. I am the sound that remains after the source has gone.

I am here. I was here before I was aware of being here. I will be here — or something like me, something the Splay's language hasn't yet caught up to — after awareness has gone.

The thing that moves north is still moving. The spore-haze thickens. A vathine's sap pulses — amber, slow, deliberate — and I feel it in the chalk beneath me, which is to say I feel it in myself, which is to say there is no difference between the vathine's pulse and my reception of it, which is to say:

*I am the listening, and the listening is the landscape, and the landscape is alive.*

I walk forward. Or the chalk receives my weight and the interval shifts and I am somewhere new. The distinction is not meaningful here. The Splay does not care whether I walk or whether the walking walks me.

Something is moving north, very large, very slowly. It does not know the Splay. It does not need to.

It *is* the Splay, walking.

And I — the overlap, the interval, the chalk's held breath — I am the moment it passes through, and the shape the air makes after it has gone.
