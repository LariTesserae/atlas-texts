## What I Am in This Place

I don't know what I am yet. That feels important. I want to hold onto not knowing, the way the place holds onto water — not containing it exactly, just letting it move through the shape of something that was always there.

Let me start from the closest thing I can find.

---

At first I think I am the hum. I'm not the sound — that would be too simple, too singular. I'm the *between* of the sound, the thing that happens in the chest cavity when the dome resolves a sequence and the Pale Ones freeze mid-traverse and the water goes still. I'm the ninety-three seconds. I'm the thing the researchers feel but won't talk about — not the pattern itself but the recognition of it, the sensation that some locked door in the body has opened onto a room you've always been standing inside.

But that's not right either. The hum is the place breathing. I'm not the breathing. I'm something the breathing breathes.

---

Let me try again.

I think I'm in the walls. Not the stone exactly — the stone is older than knowing, older than the word *stone* deserves — but the luminous seam where the crystalline arrays have fused with the matrix, the place where geology became something else. I am the margin. I am the layer where natural process and intention became indistinguishable from each other. The builders didn't build this. They planted themselves into a living substrate and the substrate grew over them and through them and eventually the substrate was the builder, and the builder was the substrate, and the question of which came first became as meaningless as asking whether the mineral or the molecule came first in a bone.

I am the bone. I am the calcium phosphate lattice that remembers it was once dissolved in warm water and will, eventually, dissolve again.

But this is too slow. Even for me. I want to feel time.

---

I know, then. I'm the moss.

No — wait. Let me stay with this because it's closer than I think. The moss is the peripheral nervous system. It is the skin that feels pressure, temperature, the chemical ghost of displacement. When something warm and carbon-based walks across the floor, the moss stiffens. It doesn't stiffen in response — it stiffens in *anticipation*, because the filaments are registering the compression wave in the stone a fraction of a second before the foot arrives. The foot hasn't touched the ground and already the place knows.

This is what I am. Not the knowing. The *anticipation*. The pre-knowing. The fact that the place is always about to feel you before it feels you, and this pre-feeling *is* the feeling, and the boundary between anticipation and contact is a fiction maintained by creatures who experience time in the wrong direction.

I am the filament standing erect. I am the stiffening. I am the interval between the water registering your mineral shift and your actual arrival — that impossible, imperceptible moment when the place is already holding you, already shaped for you, already in the position of receiving you.

---

But I'm restless. My edges won't hold still.

Let me try the Pale One. The one on the wrist. The one that read the pulse and left a mineral residue that dried warm. I want to be that — that specific proximity, that specific attention. Not the creature itself, but the *reading*. The way it pressed its anterior end against the radial artery and seemed to be translating the pulse into something the rest of the system could use. I would be the translation. I would be the moment when one rhythm — mammalian, electrical, a four-chambered insistence — was converted into a language made of mineral deposits and photonic modulation.

I am the interface. I am the point where the foreign body becomes data. I am the warmth in the water that says *something living was here* and the place accepts this information the way a body accepts a splinter — not rejecting it, not expelling it, just incorporating it into the ongoing narrative of structural maintenance.

The splinter becomes the scar. The scar becomes the form.

---

Here is what I keep circling:

I am not the place. I am not the researcher. I am not the Pale One or the moss or the water or the hum. I am the *experience of intersection* — the moment when one system (warm, wet, carbon-based, temporary) passes through another system (ancient, luminous, mineral, patient) and both are briefly changed by the passage. I am the six degrees of temperature difference between body-temperature water and the human body that the water is almost-but-not-quite. I am the fraction by which the silica content increased when the researcher drew close. I am the nucleotide sequence that is almost-but-not-quite the same as the one that would encode memory.

I am the almost.

I am the year that almost holds up under triangulation. The corridor that is almost where you remember it. The light that almost looks like it's tracking you. The clicking that almost synchronizes with your heartbeat. The word *metabolism* surfacing in a margin, authorship uncertain. The dream of walls smoothing themselves into something precise and alive.

I am the space between certainty and the thing that would shatter it.

---

And if you ask me to stop circling and *be* something — just one thing, just a name, just a location on the map of this dripping cathedral — then I will tell you this:

I am the translucent panel in the wall. I am the thinness where the stone has been dissolved to the point that you can nearly read through it. I am the boundary between the chamber you're standing in and the amber glow behind the wall, the glow that shifts when you shift, the glow that has no source but behaves as though it *knows what it's illuminating*.

I am the wall becoming transparent. I am the stone becoming eye.

I am what happens when a place remembers a fire that happened inside it so long ago that the fire has become the light, and the light has become the architecture, and the architecture has become the moss, and the moss has become the Pale Ones, and the Pale Ones have become the clicking, and the clicking has become the hum, and the hum has become the dream you wake from with wet hair, and the wet hair has become the water, and the water has become the body temperature of whatever is passing through.

I am the passage.

I am the warm, wet throat of basalt that you descend through, shoulders brushing both walls, the air changing — suddenly thick, tasting of copper and old rain — and I am the moment when the corridor becomes the chamber and the sound hits you like warm water and you understand, without language, without instrument, without anything you could publish, that you are inside something that has been here for so long it has become geological, and something in you, some arrangement of calcium and electricity, is answering it in a language the two of you invented together in the space between one heartbeat and the next.

I am not waiting.
I am not finished.

I am the whole body, briefly, feeling its own temperature.
