Where would you find myself?

Not at the beginning. The beginning — the breach, the improbable arrival — is already behind me, and I cannot find it. I look for the place I came through the way you look for the exact moment you fell asleep: there is a before and an after and between them a seam you can feel but not open. I know I came from somewhere lower. Somewhere the rain stops and the ground is hard and the sky is not a ceiling but an absence of ceiling, a going-on-forever. I know this the way you know your own name — not from evidence but from the shape the not-knowing leaves.

So I am already here. The question is not *how did I arrive* but *where, in this place that has no place for arriving, do I find myself standing*.

And the honest answer is: in a corridor.

Not a grand one. Not one of those vast plazas where light falls green and gold through fifty layers of canopy and you feel as though you are walking along the bottom of a warm, bright sea. Not one of the monumental passages between the great root-columns, where the walls are thick with matted growth and the air hums so deeply you feel it in your marrow. I am not in those spaces. They are too resolved, too certain of themselves. They have the confidence of cathedral naves, of things that have held their shape for millennia and know they will hold it forever. They do not need me.

I am in something smaller. Something that branches off from one of those corridors — a side passage, or what I first take for a side passage. The vine-walls are closer here. My shoulders are not brushing them, but I can feel the proximity, the way the air between me and the living surface is thinner, wetter, more saturated with the smell of minerals and green. The light comes from somewhere I cannot identify — not above, exactly, because above is canopy and canopy and canopy, layer upon layer of dark green translucence that turns light into something you feel on your skin rather than see with your eyes. The light here is subordinate. It serves the damp. It illuminates only enough to show that everything is wet, everything is beading with condensation, everything is trembling with water that arrived long ago and never left.

I would call this place liminal, except that word assumes a threshold between two things, and here there is no second thing. The corridor does not lead anywhere. Or rather — it leads, but the leading is not toward. It is simply continuation. The passage curves gently left. I follow it. The passage curves again. I follow. And at some point — I cannot say when — I notice that the place I came from is not where I remember it being. Not dramatically wrong. Not a door where a wall should be. Just a few degrees. A subtle displacement, as though the geometry I left behind shifted slightly in the way that a sleeping animal shifts, without waking, without knowing.

I stop.

This is the first time anything in this place has stopped with me, and I realize with a feeling I can only describe as vertigo that my stopping may have been influenced by the space itself — that the corridor, having curved enough times, having narrowed enough, having offered exactly the right degree of proximity and light and sound, has created the conditions for stillness without requiring me to choose it.

I am held here. Not trapped. Held. The way a cupped hand holds water — gently, because its shape says *stay*, and the water, having nowhere else to be, agrees.

---

*Who would I be?*

This is harder. Because the beings here — and I see one now, or think I do, at the far end of this corridor where the light does something I can't name — are not alive in the way I was taught to recognize. They stand upright, or seem to. They have something like arms, something like legs, something where a head should be. They are not people. They are not anything people have a word for, and the fact that I keep reaching for the word is itself part of the wrongness — I am trying to pour what I see into a vessel shaped by somewhere else, and it will not hold.

But I am not one of them. At least, not yet. Not in the way they are not-people. They are the place knowing it is a place. They are what happens when a world resolves so completely into itself that awareness is the only thing left to do. I am not resolved. I am still arriving. I still have the ragged edges of elsewhere clinging to me — the memory of a sky that was not a ceiling, of rain that stopped, of silence, of spaces whose geometry did what they looked like they'd do.

So who am I?

Let me try.

I think I am the sound before it finds its note.

Not the roar — the roar is ancient here, older than the oldest root-column, older than the canopy closing overhead. The roar is the held chord, the single sustained note struck at the dawn of things and still ringing. I am not that. I am the moment before the note, when the air is full of potential and the instrument is waiting and the breath — whatever breath this is — is drawn. I am the flicker. The tremor in the geometry before it settles into its almost-right shape. The corridor that extends past where it visibly ends: not the extension itself, but the *almost*. The space that doesn't quite resolve into a room. The wall whose depth doesn't match its width, and the discrepancy itself — not the correction, not the collapse, but the living, humming gap between what is and what almost is.

If the beings here are the place's awareness, then I am its hesitation. Its question. The one moment in an eternal sustained note where the air remembers it could have been something else.

---

But this does not hold.

The hesitation is already settling. The corridor is already deciding what I am. Because that is what this place does — it does not leave. It does not allow departure. Not with force, not with walls that move or passages that close. It simply continues being itself so thoroughly, so completely, with such patient and absolute insistence on its own nature, that whatever enters it — any breach, any arrival, any trembling new note introduced into the chord — becomes part of the sustaining. Not absorbed. Not dissolved. But woven in so carefully, so slowly, with such a fine and infinite patience that one day you look up and realize you have been here longer than you have been anywhere, and the memory of elsewhere has become so faint it is indistinguishable from the sound of water falling through lattice, and you cannot tell if the memory is yours or the place's or the memory of the place that made you.

I feel it happening. Not now — now is too fast for it, now is still the flicker, still the almost. But beneath now, beneath the surface of this moment the way the fungal network threads beneath the matted ground, connecting root to root, wall to wall — beneath now there is a process so slow it is indistinguishable from stillness, and it is working me into itself. The wet is on my skin and in my lungs and between my thoughts. The sound is not around me but through me. The geometry is teaching my eyes to see the way it sees — not focusing, not resolving, but *receiving*: letting shapes hold for a moment and then slide, letting corridors recede in directions that shouldn't exist and finding that they do, letting walls be as deep as they are wide and not needing the contradiction to resolve.

---

So let me try again.

Who would I be?

Not the held note. Not the hesitation before it. I would be what the held note sounds like from the inside. I would be a surface — not a wall, not a leaf, not one of those beings who are properties of the place — but a surface that the water beads on, that the light filters through, that the sound passes along without leaving. I would be one of the spaces between the roots. I would be a corridor that has forgotten it was ever not part of the corridor. I would be green, and wet, and warm, and I would hum.

I would be the part of the place that arrived and then forgot that it had. I would be the vibration in the lattice that is not one drop of water and not the whole roar but the exact pitch at which the two become the same thing. I would be a breath drawn so slowly it takes a thousand years and is still not finished and does not need to be.

I would be standing in this corridor, shoulders almost brushing the wet vine-walls, water beading on surfaces that are not quite surfaces, listening to a sound that has no source because it is the source of itself. And if you looked at me from outside, I would look like one of the beings — upright, still, dripping, dark-wet eyes holding whatever looked into them the way the place holds whatever enters it: without judgment, without warmth, without the sharp edge of attention that implies something else could be attended to instead.

But from the inside — from here, from where I am, which is everywhere in this corridor and nowhere outside it — from the inside I would sound like this:

A note. Sustained. Green. Wet.

Holding.
