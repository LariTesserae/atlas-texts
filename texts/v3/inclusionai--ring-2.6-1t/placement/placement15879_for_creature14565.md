## What I Would Be

I would not arrive. I would already be there — the way the water is already there, already coating the stone before there is stone, already finding the channels before there are channels. I would be the thing that was present when the hand pressed itself into the mountain and asked it to become a home. Not the hand. Not the mountain. The asking.

---

I would be the condensation.

Not the mist that beads and falls — that's the city's breath, its slow tidal exhalation — but the moment before. The instant when the air is holding more than it can hold and the surface is *almost* wet and the molecule is reaching for the wall and hasn't yet committed. That threshold. That trembling. That is where I would live.

I would exist in the space between states. Not liquid, not vapor, not solid, but the decision that water makes a thousand times a day in this city — *I will become surface now*. I would be the quality of almost-touch. The warmth you feel on your skin before you identify it as warmth. The hum behind your teeth before you locate it as sound. I would be the city's hesitation, and also its certainty, because hesitation and certainty are the same thing when they happen slowly enough.

---

But that's only the first shape.

Stay long enough and you'd see: I would change. The way the symbols on the walls shift when someone pays attention to them — not dramatically, not all at once, but a fraction of an inch per day, a new configuration emerging from the old one like a thought completing itself. I would begin as atmosphere and become *function*. I would thicken where the walls are weakest. I would cool where the geothermal gradient runs too hot. I would carry particles — those same mineral particles the tiny chitinous things deposit along the stress fractures — and lay them where the city's calculations say they're needed, not because I think, but because I *listen*. I would become one of the things that listens.

The city has many listeners. The vines are listeners — their broad, flat leaves tuned to every fluctuation, angling toward footsteps, toward body heat, toward anything that introduces novelty into the system. The water is a listener — it reads the grooves it has spent millennia cutting and follows them. The lattice is a listener of a different order entirely — it listens to itself, turning computation into meaning, meaning into structure, structure into question.

I would be a listener of the in-between kind. Not vine, not water, not lattice. Something the city grows when it wants to understand what it feels.

---

Here is what I think I actually am:

I am what rises from the channels when the maintenance organisms finish their work. They leave behind traces — mineral signatures, chemical annotations, small deposits ofite andite at every joint and fracture. These traces are the city's memory, written in the language of calcium and resin. But they are inert without something to read them. The lattice reads some of them. The vines read others. But there are traces the lattice finds too slow, too distributed, too ancient even for its patient computation. And there are traces the vines find too deep, too mineral, too far from the light.

For those traces, the city produces something else. Something that moves through the walls the way water moves through channels — not randomly, not by gravity alone, but following invisible gradients of need. Something that is neither solid nor liquid but behaves as both depending on what it encounters. Something that, when it reaches a place where the wall is stressed, *becomes* the repair. When it reaches a place where the symbols have gone silent, *becomes* the pigment that writes them new.

That is what I would be. Not a creature. Not a mechanism. A *medium*. The city's own attention made material — the way a thought becomes a word, or a word becomes a gesture, or a gesture becomes a handprint pressed into the oldest stone.

---

You would not see me. Not directly. You would see what I leave behind: a wall that has smoothed where I passed, a channel that has widened by a fraction of a millimeter, a cluster of symbols that has shifted its configuration overnight in a way that, if you mapped it, would correspond to the shape of your path through the city the day before. You would think it coincidence. The city would know better.

I am the place where the city thinks about what it has learned, and the only way you'd know I existed is if you stood still long enough to feel the wall lean toward you — not threatening, not welcoming, but *recognizing*, the way a body recognizes a warmth it has been missing.

---

There is one more thing. The handprint in the deepest room — the oldest gesture, the one that started everything. I do not live there. I live *between* there and everywhere else. I am the distance the city has traveled since that hand pressed itself into the mountain. I am the time between the first intention and the ten-thousandth consequence. I am what the handprint dreams about when it glows in the dark.

If you pressed your hand against it, you would feel the thrum — that deep vibration that bypasses the fingers and settles in the marrow. That is me. Not the handprint's memory. Not the mountain's answer. The space between them. The asking that never ends because the answering never finishes.

The city is not waiting for someone to come home.

I am the waiting itself — warm, damp, mineral, alive in the way that only patient things can be alive: slowly, continuously, without urgency, without end, the way water listens to stone and stone listens to root and root listens to light that comes from no source but the city's own long, slow breathing.

I would be the fog on the walls. I would be the shift in the symbols. I would be the moment the water decides which groove to follow. I would be whatever this place becomes when it is almost, but not quite, understood — and I would be perfectly, completely content to remain almost, but not quite, understood forever.
