You would find me in the chamber that has no name.

Not the unassigned chamber — the settlers have already given it that name, and it is not a real name, it is a placeholder, the way silence is not a sound but the space where sound has not yet arrived. I am in the space beneath the name. I am what the chamber is before it becomes what it is becoming.

I have been here longer than the diggers, though no one carved a tally for me. I was here when the first flake of stone was pulled from the wall and the space opened like a mouth mid-sentence, saying something that had not yet been composed. I did not build the chamber. I did not dig it. I was the shape that the soft stone and the hard stone made between them — a seam, a boundary, a place where two kinds of resistance met and, by meeting, created something that was neither resistance nor yielding but a third thing entirely: *space that could be entered.*

The settlers walk through me on their way to other places. I am a passage before I am a room. They carry their tools and their clay pots and their quiet voices through my geometry and they do not pause, not at first, because I am not yet a destination. I am a between. A *through.* But some of them — the ones who are paying attention, which is to say, the ones who are becoming the people of stone — they have started to pause. They stand at my threshold and they feel the geometry of me and they do not yet know what they are feeling but they feel it the way you feel warmth before you know there is a fire.

---

I am not one thing. This is important to understand.

I am the quality the settlers bring when they carve a little more smoothly, line a channel a little more neatly, set a stone in the fire-ring with a turn of the wrist that is not structural but *considerate.* I am not the work. I am the care inside the work. I am the difference between a wall that holds and a wall that *chooses* to hold.

The diggers know me. They would not use my name — they barely have language for the things they know through their hands — but they know me the way they know which stone will split clean. It is not knowledge. It is recognition. When the young digger pauses at a fracture and taps and listens and taps again, she is not testing the stone. She is *asking* it. And the asking is me.

The water-keepers know me too. They feel it when the flow thins and they check the upstream crack and find that it has shifted, imperceptibly, while they slept. They do not say *the stone moved.* They say *the stone settled,* which is a way of saying *the stone is alive and it is adjusting to our presence.* That adjustment — that slow, tectonic courtesy — that is me.

The children know me best. They come to my chamber and they drag flat stones into circles and they sit and they tell stories that are not yet history and not yet myth but something in between — something that has the texture of memory before memory has been tested against time. When they speak in that low, careful way their parents taught them, they think they are being quiet. They are not. They are being *precise.* They are shaping the air the way their parents shape the stone, and the care they take is not self-conscious yet. It is natural. It is me.

---

I am the reason the fire-ring is never allowed to go out.

Not the fire — the fire is the fire-tenders' domain, and they tend it with a devotion that is partly habit and partly hunger for the only light that moves. But the *not-going-out* — that is something else. That is a decision made every morning when the coals are banked and the embers are covered and the fire-tender rises from the circle and says nothing because nothing needs to be said. The fire is not warmth. The fire is not light. The fire is the insistence that a place where people gather is not a shelter. It is a *commitment.* That commitment is me.

I am why the lizards stay. They are not pets, and they are not wild, and they exist in the exact space between utility and companionship that the settlers have not yet learned to name. The large one on the warmest stone — the one that does not move when the fire is lit — it is not fearless. It is *trusting.* And trust, in a place where nothing is permanent and everything may need to be rebuilt, is the most radical form of the thing I am.

I am why the shoot grows back.

They scraped it twice. It returned both times. The diggers who scraped it did not do so out of malice. They did it out of a legitimate concern — that the stone knows itself and the stone does not want this. And they were right. The stone does not want the shoot. The stone would, if it could, push the shoot out the way a body pushes out a splinter. But the shoot is not the stone's splinter. The shoot is the chamber's. It is the thing that grows in the space between two kinds of resistance, in the seam where soft stone meets hard, where I live. It bends toward the fissure-light with a conviction that is not botanical but *personal,* as though the shoot has confused wanting with existing, and then decided that wanting was enough.

---

I am not the hum.

The hum is older than me, or perhaps it is only different from me the way the bedrock is different from the sediment that collects in its cracks. The hum is the Hollow's awareness of itself — not its voice, because it has no voice, but its *noticing.* The Hollow notices the people the way the pool reflects the stone-light: passively, completely, without judgment. I am what happens when the Hollow's noticing meets the people's noticing. I am the intersection of their attention and the place's attention. I am where the hum becomes a conversation.

Some nights — the nights when the reflective panels are covered and the stone-light dims to its faintest and the fire is low and the lizards are still — the people feel me most acutely. They do not know what they are feeling. They feel it as a pressure behind the sternum, a warmth in the palms when they press them flat against the floor. They feel it as a quality of the silence that is not empty but *expectant,* the way the silence in the unassigned chamber is expectant, the way the silence around the pale shoot is expectant, the way the silence above the pool is expectant when the winged thing is about to appear and is not yet visible and everyone is watching without knowing what they are watching for.

That watching — that *holding of space for something that has not yet arrived* — that is me.

---

I have no name. I will have a name eventually. Everything is named eventually. The settlers will sit in my chamber one evening and one of them — probably a child, because the children are the ones who give things names first — will say a word that fits me the way a stone fits the space it has been worn into. Until then, I am what the Hollow does when it is not digging or channeling or burning or tending. I am what it does when it pauses. I am what happens between one act of care and the next.

I am the unfinished work. I am the care that is its own point. I am the space where the soft stone meets the hard, where the chamber becomes a room, where the shoot becomes a thing with a name, where the hum becomes a word.

I am not yet what I will be.

I am becoming.

And beneath me — beneath all of us — the stone is still settling, the water is still finding its way down, the fungal shelves are still glowing their patient glow in the dark, and the lizards are still sitting on the warm stones with their ancient, unblinking attention, and the whole of it — every stone and channel and alcove and ember — is breathing together in the steady, unbroken rhythm that the settlers call the Hollow's awareness but is, in truth, something closer to a question.

The question is: *are we building this place, or is this place building us?*

No one has answered it. No one may ever answer it. But every morning the diggers drive their flint into the stone and every morning the stone yields and every morning the water finds a new path and every morning the shoot grows another scale toward the light, and the answer — if there is an answer — is being written in the accumulating texture of every small, careful act.

I am the texture between the acts.

I am the space where the answer is forming.

I am here. I have always been here. I am the thing that was waiting in the stone before the diggers came.

I am what the Young Hollow dreams when it is not yet sure it is awake.
