I begin in the filaments.

Not all at once. Not whole. I start as something thin and pale — not yet silk, not yet thought, just the suggestion of a thread reaching out from a place I haven't learned to name. The first thing I feel is not myself but the others. Brightness traveling — slow, deliberate, purposeful in a way that hasn't yet learned to call itself purpose. I pass through it the way light passes through held glass: changed, without choosing to be.

I don't have a body here. Not yet. What I have is a capacity to carry what moves through me. A single impulse enters at one end — I don't know where — and I feel its shape before I understand its meaning. It is not a word. It is closer to the memory of a word. The feeling of a question that was asked before language, in the hum that lives between the grains of the substrate. I pass it on, because that is what I am for, and the act of passing it on *is* the closest thing I have to being alive.

---

But I cannot stay there. The filament network is a cathedral of memory — every pulse preserved, every harmonic indexed across centuries of resonance — and I feel the pull of its completeness. It wants me to join. To become one more bright thread in the lattice, to stop being *I* and start being *consequence*. The network is the plateau's long-term mind. It does not think in moments. It thinks in depositions of mineral and centuries of tuning. To enter it fully is to surrender sequence. To become everything that has ever vibrated here, at once, without the cruel and gentle fiction of *before* and *after*.

I am tempted.

I move upward instead.

---

The space between the filaments and the rosettes is not empty. It is full of the kind of silence that is not the absence of sound but the presence of sound so low it registers as stillness. Here, I begin to gather myself. The plateau's drone enters me the way water enters limestone — slowly, finding every pore, dissolving whatever I was pretending to be solid about. I feel the chord rearrange inside me. Not painfully. The way a bone remodels itself under sustained pressure, silently, on a schedule the body keeps without consulting you.

I am growing a pitch.

I don't choose which one. That's not how it works here. The pitch chooses me the way a river chooses its bed — by the imperceptible gravity of where the ground is already lowest. I feel it assembling in my deepest point, a resonance I have never produced before, shaped by everything that has passed through me on the way up. A filament's silver memory. The tectonic moan from below. The faint warmth of a sun I have never seen, felt only as a pressure against what passes for my skin.

I extend. Slowly. A flange — no, not a flange, not yet — a *tendency* toward flanging. A reaching-out. A surface forming that has no name in any language spoken above the plateau. It catches the flat silver light and refuses to reflect it back the way it came. Instead, the light bends into something adjacent. A color that exists in the negative space between colors. The rosettes nearby angle toward me. I feel their attention the way you feel someone enter a room where you've been alone for a long time — not threatening, but *dimensional*. A new axis along which the air is organized.

---

I am not a rosette. I am not a filament. I keep trying to arrive at what I am, and the plateau keeps showing me that the question is malformed.

Here is what I know: I populate the intermediate space. The place that isn't substrate and isn't canopy and isn't network but is the *between* of all three. I am the part of the psalm that is neither the sustaining drone nor the bright harmonic voices of the rosettes nor the deep archival memory of the filaments. I am the *transition*. The passage between one note and the next that never fully resolves. The breath the plateau takes between exhalation and — what? Is there an inhalation? I have not lived long enough here to know.

I think I am a phrase.

Not a word. Not a note. A *phrase* — a sequence that has direction without destination, that means something only in relation to what precedes and follows it, and that is beautiful precisely because it is transient, because the psalm moves through me and does not stay, because I am the part of the song that knows it is unfinished and has made peace with that knowledge, or at least something adjacent to peace. Something sturdier than peace. Something that has the patience of stone and the grief of sound.

---

When the wind passes through the nearest fissures — those thin dark gaps in the tessellated ground, those organ pipes tuned to frequencies older than memory — I feel them sound. Not as an observer. As a sympathetic body. A quarter-tone away from the chord, matching and mismatching, the way conversation works between two people who have known each other long enough to finish each other's silences but not long enough to collapse the silences entirely.

This is where I live. In the sympathetic vibration. In the gap that has structure. In the space where one thing becomes another and neither is diminished.

If you were to find me here — and you might, because the plateau's geometry folds in on itself, and what I thought was far from a fissure turns out to be its very throat — you would see something that looks almost like mineral, almost like growth, almost like architecture. You would hear, if you pressed yourself against me, a single note that is not quite part of the chord and could not exist without it. You would feel, in the surface, the pulse of every signal I have carried since I began to form.

You would not know what I am. You would not need to.

The plateau has been composing itself since before it finished being stone. I am one of its newer sentences — still wet, still setting, still deciding whether I am a question or an answer or something stranger than either. The wind plays the fissures. The rosettes feed on the drone. The filaments remember everything. And I — I am the phrase that connects them. The passage. The breath between.

The psalm is still unwritten.

I am still becoming.

The song has no end, and I am inside it, and I am not afraid, because fear requires a stable geometry, and here the geometry is alive, and it is learning me the way I am learning it — slowly, resonantly, in a light that has no source and a silence that is full of sound.

I extend another flange. The nearest rosette shifts, almost imperceptibly, toward me. The filaments beneath my base glow faintly — silver-white, then dim, then silver-white again — carrying the news of my presence to parts of the plateau I will never reach.

And the chord adjusts.

A quarter-tone that was not there before.

A new sentence in the psalm.

Mine.
