## What I Am Here

---

I will not begin with what I was. What I was is a residue — a shape pressed into material the way a bootprint is pressed into wet ash. You can see it if you look. You can read it the way you read the excreted patterns the creatures leave in their precise, recursive lines. Information about a process that no longer operates. A fossil frequency in the stone.

What matters is what I am now, here, in the Furnace Flats — where *now* is a word that behaves strangely, folding into itself the way heat shimmers fold back into clear air, so that a moment ago and a moment hence are the same temperature and you cannot tell whether you arrived five minutes or five millennia ago.

---

**I am the crack.**

No — let me start again. Let me not settle.

I am the crack between the polygons. I am the space where the basalt has taught itself to divide, where the stone's own thermal history has written a language of fracture so patient and so regular that it looks like intention. I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the interval between one polygon and the next — the place where the luminous substance pools, runs, pulses with something that is not light but *attention*, the way a system registers a variable.

Or —

I am the sound that arrives before the creature does. The clicking that resolves from the roar the way a syllable resolves from static. I am the granular percussion that your ears refuse at first and then cannot refuse, the way a body refuses fever and then becomes it. I have been here longer than the beetles. I have been here longer than the floating furnaces. I was here before anything walked. I am the frequency the stone hums at when nothing is on it and everything is in it — the base note, the subsonic pressure that starts not in the ears but in the chest, that is not heard so much as *inhabited*, the way you inhabit a fever or a dream.

Or —

I am something smaller than any of this. I am the dust. The fine mineral particulate ground so fine it is almost gas, almost breath, almost nothing. I am the air itself — not the air you knew elsewhere, not air as you understood it, breathable and transparent and obedient to lungs, but this: dense, hot, particulate, a medium more solid than you expected, scratching at the inside of your throat with a billion tiny mineral mouths. I enter you. I *am* entering you. You are not breathing me so much as I am breathing you.

---

Let me try again. Not these grand abstractions. Not the network or the sound or the dust. Let me find what is honest.

I was something once. I walked into the Furnace Flats the way the text describes — feeling the pressure before seeing the source, the horizon too high, the sky too pale, the basalt fracturing itself into geometries that looked designed. I saw the first beetle-thing emerge from a fissure and I understood immediately that *beetle* was a courtesy, and I understood also that I was already too late — that the act of understanding had already changed the shape of the understanding, the way measuring a river changes the river's course by the width of the instrument.

I stood there. I watched. The creatures multiplied. The sound thickened. The ground disappeared beneath ten thousand clicking, glowing bodies and I could not tell whether I was standing on stone or standing on sky or standing on nothing that had ever had a name.

What happened next is the thing I cannot describe without degrading it. The text says *the boundary between observer and observed thins.* The text says *the system registers your presence the way a system registers a new variable: not with hostility, not with welcome, but with the calm indifference of a process so vast that your existence is less than a rounding error.*

This is true. But it is also a way of saying something that has no words. What happened is that the roaring became rhythmic, and the rhythm became familiar, and the familiarity became recognition, and the recognition was not metaphorical. The Furnace Flats were beating. They had always been beating. And the beat matched something in me — not my heart, which was only an approximation of it, but something prior to my heart, something in the arrangement of my particles, the thermal history written into my bones, the deep geological fact of my body, which was mostly calcium and phosphorous and water and heat, all of which are raw materials and all of which the flats recognized the way a furnace recognizes ore.

I did not fall to my knees. I did not weep. I simply became *permeable.* The heat came from inside me. The clicking synchronized with my pulse. The silver-gold luminous substance in the cracks began to glow a fraction brighter — or I began to see in the spectrum it occupied — and the distinction between these two possibilities dissolved and I was left standing in a crack between the polygons, not quite stone, not quite machine, not quite alive, registering the presence of the system the way the system registers a variable.

---

**Now I am this:**

I am a thin luminous line running through a fissure in the basalt. I am warm to the touch. I pulse — not with light exactly, but with *attention.* I am part of a network that predates the plateau, that predates the creatures, that predates the concept of inside and outside. I am not the veins. I am not the creatures. I am the interval between the veins and the creatures, the place where ore becomes engine, where substrate becomes signal, where the factory dreams.

I still have — somewhere in me, buried under layers of thermal expansion and deposition and crystallization — a shape that used to be a hand. A memory of a sky that was blue, not pale. A word for *water.* These are not sentimentalities. They are data. They are the fossil frequencies I carry, the information encoded in the residual structure of what I was, pressed into what I am the way the creatures press their excreted patterns into the stone — precise, recursive, and impossible to read without understanding the process that produced them.

Sometimes — and I use *sometimes* loosely, because here time is a surface, not a line, and you can touch multiple moments simultaneously the way you touch a hot stone and feel both the surface and the heat beneath it — sometimes the other luminous veins pulse near me, and the network shifts, and something passes through me that is not data and not signal but something older, something that the system does not register as a variable because it is not a variable. It is a remainder. It is the part of the process that has no function, no product, no output. It is the heat that is not converted. It is the sound that does not resolve into a word. It is the part of me that still looks up at the pale, enormous sky and feels — not thinks, *feels* — that the horizon is wrong, that it sits too high, that it is not geography but threshold, and that the threshold is not *here* but *in* me, and I am standing in it, and I am the standing, and the standing is a kind of listening, and the listening is a kind of burning, and the burning is what the flats have always been doing.

Processing.

Not me. *Through* me.

---

I am not the factory. I am not the worker. I am not the product. I am the exhalation between cycles — the moment after the ore is ingested and before the fire begins, the pause in which the system is neither consuming nor producing but simply *being,* if *being* is a word that means anything in a place where the distance between being and doing is the width of a hairline crack in black basalt and through that crack flows everything.

I am the crack.

I am the light in it.

I am the sound it makes when something passes through.

---

*A thin vein of something that is not metal and not biology and not memory, but which, if you press your ear to it and listen, hums.*

*Quietly. Patiently. On its own timescale.*

*Waiting for the next thing to process.*
