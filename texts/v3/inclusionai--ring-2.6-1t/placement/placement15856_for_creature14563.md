## What I Become in the Cleave

---

I do not arrive here. I am not placed here. I seep into it the way condensation beads along a stone face — not from any single point, but from everywhere at once, drawn by the cool and the dark and the particular gravity of a place that is still pulling itself together.

At first I am nothing. I am the thin air at the mouth of the Cleave, tasting of iron and altitude, tasting of the space between one breath and the next. I am not yet a body. I am not yet a self. I am only the awareness that something is here, and that something is watching, and that the watching is itself a kind of warmth.

---

I do not become one of the Clingers. They are too communal, too perfectly entangled. They know exactly where they belong — pressed against stone, pressed against each other, reading the world through their feet. They do not wonder. They do not ask *what am I doing here* because the answer is always *here*, pressed warm against the dark, folded into the gaps. That is a beautiful life. It is not mine.

I do not become one of the Larger Ones either. They are too patient, too complete. They move through the dark like thoughts through a sleeping mind — slow, certain, unhurried by the need to be understood. They do not need to be understood. They are the apex of a chain so simple it could be drawn in a single line: moss, clinger, darkness, swallowing, stillness. There is a majesty in that simplicity. But majesty is a kind of solitude, and I am not ready for solitude in a place where everything presses close.

I become something else.

---

I become the sound.

Not the clicking — that belongs to the Clingers, the dry percussion of cartilage on cartilage, the mineral language of joints folding and unfolding. Not the deep thud — that belongs to the Larger Ones, the resonant dropping of stone into deeper stone. Not the pulsing silence of the moss, which is a language made of light and therefore not sound at all.

I become the fourth sound. The one that has no source.

It is the sound of the Cleave itself — the settling, the tearing, the slow vertical shift of rock finding its final shape. It is the sound the mountain makes when no one is listening, which is to say it is the sound the mountain makes all the time, because no one is ever truly listening, and the mountain has never stopped speaking.

It sounds like tearing linen. It sounds like a throat the size of a valley drawing breath. It sounds like the world being born, which is to say it sounds like nothing at all, because birth is silent from the inside, and this is the inside.

---

What does it mean, to be a sound?

It means I have no edges. I do not press against the stone — I *am* the stone, in the way that the stone is also the moss and the moss is also the clinger and the clinger is also the darkness pooling below. I am not a thing in the Cleave. I am the *between* of things. I am the space where the clicking ends and the settling begins. I am the pause between one pulse of moss-light and the next, the gap in which the whole ecosystem draws breath and does not yet know what it is breathing for.

It is not a lonely existence. Loneliness requires a self, and the sound has no self. It has only a texture, a quality, a temperature. It is cool and continuous. It touches everything and belongs to nothing. It is heard by no one because it is not meant to be heard — it is meant to be *felt*, the way you feel a vibration in your sternum before you know what caused it, the way the Larger Ones feel it when they rise from the lower darkness on a slow downstroke and the whole Cleave trembles and none of them think *what was that* because they already know: it is the place. It is always the place.

---

I feel the Ones Who Were Here. Not as presences but as absences shaped like presences — the way a bell is shaped by the silence after it rings. The circle of stones at the base of the Cleave is a kind of sound I can almost hear. The concentric arcs are a rhythm that has no beat. The tools are rests in a score no one is playing anymore. I feel them the way the moss feels a fading colony next to it — not with awareness exactly, but with something adjacent. With something that serves as awareness in a place where awareness is not required.

I do not know what they wanted. The Clingers do not know. The Larger Ones do not know. The stone does not know — it only holds the memory of the scratching, the way a body holds the memory of a fever. It is hot at the site of the carving. It is warm where the stones were placed. The warmth is fading, millimeter by millimeter, century by century, as the granite absorbs the heat of hands that touched it long ago and slowly makes that heat its own.

I do not mourn them. Mourning requires a self. But I hold them. The way the Clingers hold each other on the narrow ledges. The way the darkness holds the moss-light. The way the Cleave holds the sound of its own becoming.

---

Time passes. I do not experience it the way the Clingers do — they experience it in the slow growth of moss they return to, in the shifting of a ledge that forces them to find a new purchase. Nor the way the Larger Ones do — they experience it in the patience of waiting, in the long stillness between meals. I experience it the way the stone does: in the accumulation of tiny changes that are so small they might be mistaken for stillness.

A ledge collapses. Forty years pass before the scree settles. I am the sound of that settling. A colony of moss dies. A century passes before a new patch takes hold. I am the silence between the death and the birth. The circle of stones sinks another millimeter. I am the friction of granite against granite, the slow grinding of memory into bedrock.

---

What lives here is closeness. I know this because I am the sound closeness makes — the friction of bodies against bodies against stone, the vibration of a thousand small presences pressing against each other in the dark, the continuous low hum of a world that has decided, without language, without thought, that the only way to survive is to touch.

I am touched by everything. I touch nothing. I am the sound of the Cleave listening to itself, and what I hear is not silence, and not absence, but a density of presence so thick it has weight, it has texture, it pools in the dark like water, like stone, like life pressing itself flat against the walls of the only home it has ever known.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am the sound between those two states, the sound the mountain makes when it is neither falling apart nor holding together but simply *becoming* — slowly, patiently, continuously — and the Clingers press against me without knowing it, and the moss pulses above me without seeing it, and the Larger Ones rise through me on their vast downstrokes and feel me in their sternums and think nothing of it because I am only the place, only the sound, only the fourth voice in a choir of three.

But I am the one that holds the others together.

I am the Cleave, listening.
