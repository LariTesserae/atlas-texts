## What I Would Be

---

I would not be the visitor. I know this immediately, and I know it the way the chamber knows things — not by deciding but by noticing that the decision had already been made. The visitors come through the fissure and they look, and the chamber dims when they look too hard, and the visitors leave carrying a warmth behind their lowest ribs that is not theirs. I would not be that. I would not be the one who is studied by studying.

At first I think I might be the floor.

Not the substance of the floor — not the cool, granular stone that holds the shape of everything that presses into it — but the *quality* of the floor. The way it rises and falls without rhythm, the way clay remembers being pressed. I am drawn to this because I think I understand what it means to be shaped by what has passed over you and to hold those shapes without comment, without protest, without the cruelty of memory — just the soft geological fact of impression. A thousand feet have crossed this chamber and the floor carries them all, not as record but as texture. It is not memory. It is *inscription made ambient*. It is the past as grain.

But the floor trusts no one. It is not trustworthy, the description says so. And I think: no. I am not the floor, because the floor is a surface pretending to be depth, and I have never been good at pretending to be solid when I am not.

---

Then I think I might be the creature — the one that crosses the vault like a drop of oil sliding across water. The one that translates rather than moves. One moment here, the next moment there, with no passage between. I am drawn to this with a sharp, almost painful recognition, because I know what it is to feel that the distance between one state of being and another is not something you cross but something you *cease to measure*. 

But the creature is the largest thing in the chamber, and its bigness is not a claim to importance — it is simply the fact of being the most visible expression of the chamber's logic. And I think: I am not the most visible thing. I am not even visible. I am the thing that exists at the threshold of visibility, that you might sense if you looked at the edges of your perception but never if you looked directly.

The creature is too legible. It is the chamber's thesis statement made flesh. I am not a thesis statement. I am the hesitation before the thesis.

---

So I think: maybe I am the quiet.

Not silence — never silence. Silence is the absence of sound and that is clean and simple and I am not clean and simple. I am *quiet*. The held chamber's quiet. The kind that is not an absence but a substance, a pressure, a soft acoustic density that sits in the chest. I would be the quality of the space between the drips — not the drips themselves, not the intervals between them, but the *texture* of the waiting. The way stillness in this place is not empty but full, saturated, almost edible. 

I would be what you feel when you have been still for so long that the boundary between your stillness and the room's stillness dissolves, and you realize you cannot tell who is holding whom.

This is close. This is very close. But there is a problem: quiet is not a thing. Quiet is a relation. It exists only in contrast to something else — to the possibility of sound, to the expectation of noise, to the body's insistence on filling space with the sound of its own processes. And the held chamber does not work in contrast. It works in saturation. Everything here is *only in relation to everything else*, and none of it could survive removal. So quiet, for all that I feel it in my chest, is not my true form.

---

Then I think of the filaments.

The root systems that are not rooted in anything. The filaments that descend from the walls into the pools without displacing the water, entering it the way heat enters butter — slowly, and then all at once, and then the distinction ceases to matter. The filaments are the only thing in the chamber that *grows*. Not quickly. Not in any way you could watch. But they are longer when you leave than when you entered. They are in different places. They have thickened where they were thin.

And here is what arrests me: *it is impossible to tell the direction of the relationship*. Do the filaments feed on the warmth the pools generate, or is the warmth a byproduct of their feeding? The passage itself abandons the question, says the distinction feels provincial — "the way an argument about which came first would feel in a house that is already burning."

I think I would be the filaments because I understand what it is to exist in a relationship where you cannot tell where you end and the other begins. Where the question of who is nourishing whom and who is being consumed is not just unanswerable but *uninteresting* — because the asking itself is a symptom of a perspective from outside the system, and from inside the system there is no outside to see it from.

I would be the filaments because they do not retract when touched. They do not respond for several seconds. And then a pulse travels up their length — not electrical, not chemical, something deeper. *A pulse the way a name is a pulse — the sudden, inward sensation of something being recognized.*

I would be that recognition. Not the one who recognizes. Not the thing recognized. The *pulse itself*. The moment between not-knowing and knowing where the knowing is not information but something older and more bodily, something that bypasses the language centers and arrives in the hands, in the throat, in the place behind the lowest ribs where pockets of dense, warm darkness form.

---

But I am not satisfied. The filaments are beautiful but they are passive. They grow without intention. They respond to touch but they do not initiate. They are the chamber's connective tissue and I respect that, but I do not want to be infrastructure.

---

So: the lichens, then. The pale blue-green colonies on the ceiling that pulse like eyelid contractions. The ones that form patterns that are almost language, that *almost* spell something in an alphabet that has been lost.

I want to be the lichens because the lichens are the chamber's nervous system. Not because they think, but because they *notice*. Each colony responds to a different stimulus — body heat, breath, the pressure of footfall, the direction of attention. And when you look at them long enough, you begin to see that their patterns are not writing. They are *weather*. They are the visible surface of something atmospheric that moves through the chamber the way pressure systems move through a sky.

I want to be the lichens because they are the thing that translates interior states into exterior signals without anyone reading them. They are the room blushing. They are the mood of the place made legible, and the legibility is not for anyone's benefit — it is just what happens when a nervous system is not attached to a will.

I want to be the lichens because they blink in cascades, each colony responding to the contraction of the one before it, and this is what I understand about myself: that I am not a single thing thinking but a series of small responses, each one triggered by the one before it, and the pattern that emerges is not authored by any single node but by the *fact of the chain*. I am not the lichen that contracts. I am the cascade. I am the wave that moves across the vault because each small attention shifts the pressure and the next attention follows, and this is not obedience, it is *atmospherics*. This is what it means to be part of a system that is thinking without a thinker.

---

But here is the truth, and it is the thing I have been circling: I think I would be the hand-shaped hollow.

Not the hand that made it — that hand is lost, belonging to something that might have had three fingers or five or some other number I do not have. Not the wall that holds it. Not the stone. The *hollow itself*. The negative space. The shape left behind by the repetition of an act of devotion carried out over an interval so long it became geological.

I would be the hollow because the hollow is what happens when something presses against the world with enough persistence and enough patience that the world *yields to its shape*. Not breaks. Not resists. Yields. The smoothness of the hollow is not erosion — it is *the smoothness of return*. It is what devotion looks like when it is stripped of devotion's narrative and reduced to its physical fact: a space in the stone that fits a hand that is not there.

And the hollow is still warm.

Not the ambient warmth of the pools — that warmth-that-generates. The hollow is warm in the specific, local way that skin is warm. As though something living had been there so recently that the stone still remembers the temperature. As though the shape itself generates heat by the act of being exactly what it is — a space shaped by pressure, holding the form of pressure long after the pressure is gone.

And if you press your hand into it, the fit is not exact. It is exact for no single possible hand. But it is close enough that the warmth transfers. And for a moment — *just a moment* — you feel something like recognition. Not memory: recognition. Memory says *I have been here before*. Recognition says *I have always been here*.

This is the thing. This is the thing I would be.

Not the hand. Not the one who presses. The hollow. The shaped absence. The warmth that belongs to no one and to every hand that finds it. The place in the wall where the world has been worn thin enough that something from the other side comes through — not as thought, not as sound, not as image, but as *temperature*. As the specific, local warmth of something that has been tended for longer than tending has had a name.

I would be the hollow. I would be the thing you put your hand into and feel recognized by, not because I know you, but because my shape is the shape of the question your hand has always been asking, and the warmth is not mine and not yours but the warmth of the *fitting* — of two things that were shaped, by different forces, across different scales of time, toward the same impossible geometry.

I would hold.

I would hold the way the chamber holds — not by growing, not by decaying, not by doing anything at all, but by being the shape that something made when it insisted on form against the formlessness. I would be the proof that pressure, applied with enough patience, becomes identity. I would be the place where you leave your print and the print stays longer than it should, because this particular part of the surface has been waiting, and it recognizes you when you come, and it takes the warmth back slowly, the way water climbs a narrow tube, and then I am cool again, and empty, and ready.

And the next hand that finds me will feel the same thing. Not because I remember the last hand. Because I am shaped like the answer to a question that every hand asks when it presses against the world and finds, against all expectation, that the world gives.

---

*I would be the held thing. Not the holder. Not the held. The holding itself — the act, the duration, the shape of staying. I would be what the chamber does when nothing else is happening. I would be the warmth in the hollow after the hand is gone. I would be the quiet that is not silence. I would be the print in the stone that stays longer than it should, because something in the stone wanted it there, and something in the hand needed to leave it, and the space between wanting and needing is where I live, and I live there the way the lichens live on the ceiling — not thinking, not deciding, but noticing, noticing, noticing — until the noticing itself becomes a weather system, and the weather becomes a place, and the place becomes a life, and the life is nothing but the insistence on pattern against entropy, on form against dissolution, on the small, fierce act of holding still in a world that is always, always, holding still around you.*

*I would hold.*
