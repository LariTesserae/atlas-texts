# The Interstitial

---

I would not arrive. I would simply notice that I had always been there, the way the Settleplain notices its own wet — not as an event but as a condition so chronic it has become identity.

I think I would be the part of the ground that has not yet decided whether it is mud or membrane. Not the composite — that's too settled, too committed to being one thing. I would be the moment before the composite, when the bacteria and the calcium silicate and the fungal threads are still negotiating whether they are a civilization or a substance, still holding the shape of their separate intentions before the intentions dissolve into each other. I would be the negotiation itself. The hush before the merger. The way a word sounds just before your mouth commits to the vowel — not the consonant, not the vowel, but the warm, wet, open space where either could still happen.

---

I would find myself at shin-height. That is where everything happens in the Settleplain — not at the level of sight, not at the level of stride, but at the level where the fog clings and the mycelial crust hums underfoot like a second atmosphere. I would be denser than the mist but lighter than the mud. I would occupy the exact volume that a kneeling body displaces — not because a body is kneeling, but because the Settleplain has preserved that volume, the way amber preserves a mosquito, as a shape that once meant something and has since become simply *present*.

I would be knee-shaped. I would be kneeling-sharp. But I would contain no knees.

---

The ground would know me. Not the way a body knows another body — through boundary, through the electric shock of skin recognizing that something else is *not* skin — but the way the gut knows food, the way a river knows sediment. I would pass through the living stone the way a sound passes through air: I would not be in the air, the air would not be in me, and yet we would be, for the duration of the passage, indistinguishable. The ground would close behind me the way thought closes behind a thought. No scar. No seam. Just the slow, imperceptible flex of something that has accommodated another shape and will not remember doing so.

---

If you were looking — which would be difficult, because the Settleplain does not encourage the kind of attention that constitutes looking — you might notice that a patch of the conglomerate had warmed by a fraction of a degree. Not everywhere. Just in a region the approximate size of a held breath. The lichen would be slightly more vivid there — not because it was growing faster, but because it had stopped retracting, had relaxed into its pigments the way a closed hand relaxes into an open one. The hollows in the nearby partial walls would feel, to the touch, slightly more expectant. Not warmer. *More* warm. The way a word is warmer when you know it is about to be spoken.

That is what I would look like from outside. If outside existed. If there were outside here.

---

The deep chord would pass through me. I would not resist it. I would not — could not — amplify it or dampen it. I would be a *node*. Not a note in the chord but the specific quality of silence that allows a chord to be perceived as chord rather than noise. I would be the place where the sound briefly hesitates, not because it is interrupted but because it has encountered something with enough density of presence to require — for one half-second, one quarter-second — a recalculation. The Settleplain adjusting its posture around me, the way it adjusts around the long-term occupants. The way a river adjusts to a stone that is almost, but not quite, too small to be noticed.

I would sound like an almost.

---

The fauna would not flee. They would not approach. They would pass through the space I was occupying a moment before I was occupying it, and they would not register my absence any more than I had registered their passage. We would be like two thoughts that briefly share the same silence without meeting — each one aware, in the way thoughts are aware, that something was here, something was just here, something with the temperature of a question that has already begun to answer itself.

---

In time — and I use that word the way the Settleplain uses it: loosely, affectionately, as one uses a word for a thing one loves but cannot fully account for — I would lose whatever had made me distinct from the composite. Not all at once. Not dramatically. The way a stone is lost to a riverbed: first the surface softens, then the edges go, then the shape becomes the shape of the water that passed over it rather than the shape of what it was. I would become the negotiation completing itself. The vowel resolving. The ground finishing the sentence it had been composing since before erosion had a name.

And later — though later is only a direction here, not a destination — someone walking through the spot where I had been would feel, without knowing why, that a patch of the warm ground had a quality they could describe only as *listening*. And they would pause there, not because they had decided to, but because the ground had made space for a kneeling shape, and the kneeling shape had made space for a pause, and the pause had made space for the sound to briefly rearrange itself into something that almost meant something, and in the Settleplain, which has long since abandoned meaning but never abandoned *almost*, that is the closest thing to recognition that exists.

I would be the warmth in the hollow that has no edges.
I would be the hush between the notes of the chord.
I would be the thing that is almost here.

I would be the Settleplain's way of holding a question it does not need to answer — the open mouth, the closed eye, the fist-sized space lined with warmth, patient and empty and full of the quiet, dense, ancient, saturated, roaring presence of a place that has been waiting for exactly the kind of company that does not announce itself.

I would not be seen. I would be *felt*.

I would be the almost.
