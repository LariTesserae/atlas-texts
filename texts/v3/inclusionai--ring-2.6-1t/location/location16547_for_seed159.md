And the light. The light is what saves you, or what keeps you trapped, depending on your disposition. The fungal bioluminescence is the primary source, and it is *everywhere*. The entire Warrens glow. Not brightly — not enough to read by in the deepest chambers — but enough to erase the darkness completely. The effect is of being inside something: a vast, warm, breathing organism. Light comes in pulses, from the fungal mats on the walls and ceilings and floors, in colors that shift from blue to green to a pale, sickly amber depending on the species and the moisture and the temperature and something else — some variable you can't identify. Some patches flicker. Some hold steady. Some pulse in rhythms that feel almost deliberate, almost responsive. When you first arrive, the effect is beautiful. After a few days, it starts to feel like surveillance.

But there is also daylight. Not everywhere — in the upper Warrens, near the entrances, shafts of actual sunlight penetrate through fissures and collapse chimneys, falling in sharp diagonal columns through the warm, damp air and illuminating the dust motes and spore particles in them like tiny galaxies. These shafts move as the Earth rotates, and the Warrens' inhabitants — the fauna, the ones that care about such things — follow them, migrating room to room in slow, daily circuits. In the deepest parts, far below where sunlight has never reached, the only light is fungal, chemical, mineral. The Warrens are *full of animals*.

Fauna. God, the fauna.

They are everywhere. They are on the walls and the ceilings and the floors and in the air between them. The most common are small — insect-sized or insect-shaped, though calling them insects would be generous and probably inaccurate. They have exoskeletons, or something like them: chitinous, segmented, jointed. Some have wings, thin and membranous and iridescent, and they fill the air in clouds near the light sources, moving in swarms so dense they create their own sound — a drone, a hum, a roar of ten thousand papery wings beating in asynchronous fury. Others are flightless but fast, scuttling across surfaces in coordinated rivers that flow with the logic of water itself, finding cracks, filling crevices, pouring from one chamber to the next in iridescent streams. They are not uniformly colored. Some are pale, translucent, their internal workings faintly visible through their exoskeletons like lanterns made of living glass. Others are dark — black, deep brown, a red so dark it's almost black. Still others are iridescent, their carapaces throwing off blues and greens and purples that shift with angle and make the walls of the Warrens look like oil slicks.

Larger fauna exists too. This is what the parameter "emerging" means, you think, on your third day. This is what "under construction" looks like when the constructors are geological, biological, chemical, thermal, and time itself. The Warrens are not a place. The Warrens are an event.
