### The Flora

The plants — and "plants" is a word you reach for out of desperation, because what grows here has no relationship to rain or root or the slow patient drinking of soil — rise from the cracks in the stone in a sparse, deliberate arrangement. They are not crowded. Each one occupies its own portion of the floor or wall as though by negotiation, with generous emptiness left between. In a space this small, the emptiness is conspicuous. You feel the distance between each living thing the way you feel silence between notes in music that is almost, but not quite, a song.

Vibration. A low hum that enters through the bones of your wrist and settles into the hollow of your elbow.

Every one of them sounds.

Not all at the same pitch. Not all at the same volume. They emit a tone — continuous, without beginning or end — that sits in the chest like a second heartbeat. The tones overlap, interfere, harmonize in ways that are not harmonious but are at least consistent. Standing among them is standing inside a chord that has been sustained since before the stones settled. Since the stones were new. Since this place was opened and the air rushed in and the plants, wherever they came from, simply began.

They do not grow. They do not wilt. They are lush in the same way a coral reef is lush — dense with life and structure, but alien to anything fed by rain. Their colors are muted: greengray, brownwhite, the occasional vein of something violet that pulses faster when the fauna pass nearby.

### The Fauna

The creatures are small, plentiful, and wrong in the specific way that things become wrong when they have had no water to constrain their design. There are dozens of them in the Hollow at any given time, visible and just barely visible, moving through the spaces between the plants with a confidence that suggests they have never known a world larger than this.

None of them drink. None of them seek water. Their mouths, when you observe them closely — crouched for a long time among the stones at the edge of the Hollow's central clearing — do not open to take in anything. ### The Built Things

There are structures. They are few and scattered, and they do not belong to the fauna as far as you can tell — the creatures move around them the way birds move around fence posts, with no attachment, no use, no avoidance. They simply exist alongside.

The largest is a ring of seven stones, each about knee-high, placed in a rough circle in the center of the Hollow. The grooves are not deep. They could be natural, except they are not. They converge in places, diverge in others, and in two of the stones, a single groove spirals inward to a point so precise it could only have been made by intention.

There is a mound, too — low, about a meter high, made of the same fine mineral dust that covers the floor but packed tighter, shaped, as though someone had carried it in handfuls and pressed it into a dome. At its peak is a single flat stone, placed with what you can only describe as care. Under the flat stone, if you are foolish enough or curious enough to lift it, there is a hollow space containing nothing but air — still air, ancient-feeling air, air that has not been disturbed because nothing disturbs anything here, because nothing ever changes.

### The Sound

It bears repeating, because the sound is the thing that will stay with you.

The Hollow is loud. Not with aggression, not with chaos, but with a dense, layered presence — the way a forest is loud with insects, but dry and mineral and resonant instead of wet and buzzing. The plants hum their sustained tones. The clickerite click. The gliders emit their low andite andite andite andite drones. The burrowers rattle beneath. And beneath all of it, underneath every individual sound, there is a vibration in the stone itself — a frequency so low it is less a sound than a pressure, a thing you feel in the fillings of your teeth and the soles of you feet. The cave resonates. The young walls, unweathered, sharp-edged, still holding the memory of the force that broke them open, carry every vibration from every source and fold it back into the space. The Hollow listens to itself.

And because nothing changes, this sound has not varied for — you don't know how long. Minutes, years, centuries, it doesn't matter. The equilibrium is perfect. No plant grows or dies. No clicker is born or perishes. The dust on the floor does not deepen. The grooves in the dark stones do not wear. The sound that fills the space now is the sound that filled it a thousand years ago and the sound that will fill it a thousand years from now, assuming anyone is here to hear it.

### The Feeling

You will not want to stay. You will also not want to leave quickly.

The Hollow asks something of you. Not danger, not effort — attention. You feel observed, but not by the fauna. The mineral glow is too even to be indifferent. The resonance is too structured to be accidental. You are inside something that knows it is a thing, that holds its own shape by not changing, that has found a state so stable it resembles intention.

The air is warm and dry and tastes faintly ofite anditeite andite —iteite copper, orite perhaps something older. Your eyes adjust quickly; the glow is sufficient, if you never need to see sharply. The edges of things are soft in this light, as though the world is a drawing done in chalk that hasn't been fixed. Every sound reaches you slightly after it is produced, because the air is dense with vibration and the stone slows the wave. You are always hearing the recent past.

No footprint you make in the mineral dust will last. Not because it will be erased — nothing is erased here — but because the dust is so fine and so still that a footprint simply sits, visible, undeniable, beside the footprints of you that are already there. Whoever came before you stood in exactly this spot and felt exactly this silence-that-is-not-silence and left the same impression in the same pale powder, and that impression has not changed because nothing changes, and yours will not change either.

You are in a place that is complete. It does not need you. It did not need the ones who built the ring and the alcove. It simply is — warm, resonant, luminous, dry, alive in a way that asks nothing of water and gives nothing of time.

You will leave. The climb back to the surface is possible — a chimney of fractured stone that opens to the flat pale world above. The light up there is different. The air is different. Everything moves.

But you will remember the sound. The chord, sustained, unresolved, perfect.
