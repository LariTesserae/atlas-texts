## The Bright Seam

You find it by touch before you find it by sight — a thinning of the air, a loosening in the rock around you, as though the mountain has exhaled and left a single small room inside its ribs.

The chamber is no larger than a chapel. No larger than a throat. You could nearly span it with outstretched arms if you were tall enough, but the ceiling is close, only a body-length above your head, and it is *dripping*. Not steadily — that would be predictable, and nothing about this place behaves in a way you can predict even after you've memorized it. The drops come in clusters. Three, then silence. Then seven, rapid, like knuckles on a door. Then nothing. Then everything at once.

Each drop is warm. That's the first wrong thing.

They fall from a ceiling that is alive — not metaphorically, not in the way a forest is alive, but actually, visibly, *growing*. The stone hangs down in columns that are too regular to be accidental and too irregular to be carved. They are pale, almost translucent at their tips, and they are extending themselves downward at a rate you can almost perceive if you watch long enough and stop yourself from blinking. Millimeters per hour. Maybe less. They are not stalactites. They have no mineral sheen. Instead their surfaces are faintly tacky, and where the water condenses on them, it beads and holds, trembling, before finally pulling free and falling toward the floor.

The floor is what takes your breath.

It is covered. Not carpeted — *covered*, the way a wound is covered, the way a surface is colonized. Pale creatures the size of your smallest fingernail cling to every inch of stone. They have too many legs — eight, ten, you can't count because they move. They shift constantly, a slow churning aggregate that breathes in and out, contracting toward the center of the room in a slow pulse and then expanding again, pressing outward to the walls like a tide that has forgotten the moon. They are cool to the touch when one climbs your boot, then warm for a moment where its body meets your skin, then cool again as it passes.

The light. The light is the thing you cannot account for.

There is no source. There is no opening to the sky — you checked, immediately, reflexively, and there is only seamless stone above and behind you. But the chamber is bright. Not brightly lit — *bright with itself*, the way a fever is bright, the way a bruise is bright from inside. The walls glow a color that sits at the edge of naming. It is not blue. It is not green. It is the color of a sound you almost heard once and can't remember. The creatures on the floor contribute to it — each one carries a faint phosphorescence on its back, a small oval of pale luminance, and when they move in their great collective tide, the light ripples across the cavern walls like reflections on water, like something is moving beneath the stone itself.

The sound is the second thing that is impossible.

It is enormous. In a space this small — this *intimate* — the noise should be muffled, contained. Instead it fills you. The creatures make sound. Not individually; they do not chirp or click or sing one at a time. They make sound the way a crowd makes sound — a murmur that is not speech but has grammar, has rhythm, has intent. It rises from the floor in a low sustained thrum and then breaks apart into thousands of tiny chittering voices, each one distinct for a fraction of a second before dissolving back into the whole. Sometimes, if you stand still and stop breathing, you can hear something underneath it: a deeper tone, almost subsonic, that does not come from the creatures at all. It comes from the walls. It comes from the stone itself. It sounds like the chamber is *humming a note it has held for so long that it has forgotten it is holding it.*

There are bones here. That's the built part — not built, really, but placed, or left, or arrived. A femur the length of your forearm, ivory-white, resting across two of the lower columns. It has been here long enough that the creatures have colonized it too, tracing its surface with their slow procession. A shard of something metallic is embedded in the far wall at chest height — not rusted, not corroded, but *healed over*, the stone having grown around it the way skin grows over a splinter. It is the only thing in the room that is not alive or becoming alive, and it is the only thing that looks like it came from somewhere else. It looks like the memory of a tool. A knife, maybe. Or a key. You can't tell. The stone has eaten its edges.

Nothing here changes.

This is the thing you understand without being told — the way you understand gravity or thirst. The dripping is the same dripping. The hum is the same hum. The creatures' tide rises and falls at the same interval it has always risen and fallen and will always rise and fall. The columns grow, but they grow at the rate that they have always grown, and the ceiling descends at the rate that it has always descended, and if you waited here long enough — years, centuries — you would see no difference. The chamber is complete in its incompleteness. It is a process that has achieved permanence. It is still being born and it has always been born and it will never finish being born.

You are standing in a place that does not experience time. You are standing in a place that has one moment, stretched thin and bright and loud and full of legs and light.

A drop falls from the ceiling onto the back of a creature. It does not notice. The light ripples. The hum sustains. The stone, pale and tender and young, keeps growing downward into itself.

You realize you have been holding your breath. You exhale. The air tastes like copper and rain. The creatures do not part for you. They do not notice you. You are standing in a place that was never built for visitors, in a space that was never meant to contain anything as large and temporary as a person, and yet here you are — an accidental witness to a room that is perpetually completing itself, a bright seam in the dark body of the earth, holding all its noise and light and slow wet life inside a space no bigger than a grave.

The warmth on your skin, where the creature passed, has not faded.

The drip is beginning again. Three, then silence.

Then seven.
