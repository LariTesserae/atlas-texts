## The Hollow

The ground opened here. Not violently — the way a fruit splits when it's too ripe. The edges are still sharp. You can see where the soil tore, and it hasn't finished deciding it's torn. This is a shallow wound cupped in flat, unremarkable terrain — thirty paces across, maybe twelve feet at its deepest. A place you could almost miss, if it weren't for the way the air bends slightly at the rim, light passing through it thicker than it should, as if the hollow is filtering everything that enters and everything that leaves.

---

**The Ground**

New. You can tell instantly. The soil inside is too dark, too uniform — ink still wet, every component mixed and unsorted. When you step, the surface gives in a way that feels wrong: not soft, not firm, like pressing into something that hasn't finished becoming. There are no paths. Nothing has walked here long enough to make one.

It is damp without being wet. The air sits on your skin like a second skin, warm — and the temperature doesn't change. Day and night, it holds steady at something close to blood heat, the humidity of a lung mid-exhale. When rain comes, the water doesn't pool. The ground drinks too fast, or it runs along knife-edge channels that have only recently begun forming at the lowest points, channels with edges so new and sharp they could cut if you pressed hard enough.

The erosion is infant. Everything angular. Everything freshly exposed. The soil is barely soil — it's halfway between raw material and the idea of a surface.

---

**The Ridge Apparatus**

Along the western wall, the uplift created six parallel ridges, evenly spaced a hand's width apart, running the full north-south width of the basin. They are mineral — definitely rock — but too regular. Too straight. Each ridge is forearm-tall and two-hands-wide, its surface smooth not by water but by something denser, the way you'd smooth clay with a slow palm. Dark gray, threaded with veins of something that isn't quite quartz but catches light the same way, a cold gleam that doesn't match the warmth of everything else.

In the channels between ridges, the filaments live.

Thread-thin growths — maybe mineral, maybe becoming mineral — spanning from ridge to ridge like suspension bridges made of drawn wire. They start translucent, flexible, swinging in faint air currents, producing a tone percussive and impossibly delicate: a single note on a thumb piano, so faint it exists below the threshold of deliberate hearing. Over days, the filaments stiffen and darken, joining the ridgeline, closing the gap, building new architecture. And new filaments begin in their place. The apparatus is always building itself.

The filaments are how the place breathes.

---

**The Residents**

The fauna arrived before the hollow finished forming. Perhaps they were already inside whatever was here before. Perhaps the place called them. Either way, the hollow is saturated with them.

*Flatbacks.* The first thing you notice. Low, wide, the color of wet soil — stretched ovals the size of cupped hands, maybe eight inches across. They don't walk. They ripple. They travel in packed lines along the ridges, following the grooves as though the grooves were instructions. When one stops, the ones behind it stop in the same cadence — a wave of stillness flowing backward through the crowd, like falling dominoes in reverse.

They are layered so tightly there is no ground between them. When they move, they look like a single organism flowing over stone — a sheet of warm, damp, rippling life. You could place your palm flat on the earth and feel dozens of small bodies pulsing beneath it, all feeding on the mineral seepage from the ridge veins. You can watch the color shift through their undersides as they drink — iron-dark to copper-bright and back.

*Glassnails.* On the basin walls. Small, spiraled, and fully transparent — you can see the slow pulse inside them, something dark moving through something pale. They leave trails that attract moisture and become the germination points of new filaments. The glassnails are the bridge between the mineral and the other thing. The in-between thing. Whatever word you reach for doesn't apply, but the glassnails live in it.

*The Singer.* You sense it before you locate it. A pressure change, a presence behind the sternum. Deep in the basin, beneath the overhang on the southern side, something the size of a large dog, shaped like nothing with a spine. Dense. Matte-black. Textured like volcanic glass but warm to the touch. It doesn't move — it *shifts*, the way a shadow relocates when the light source moves and you can't see the light source move.

Periodically, it produces a sound.

Not a sound. A settling. Like a building deciding it has finished expanding. The vibration travels through the ground, not the air, and every flatback in the hollow stops for the exact duration of the note — hundreds of creatures frozen in unison — then resumes. At night the intervals lengthen. The Singer's voice deepens, and the entire basin hums beneath it.

---

**The Light, the Air, the Sound**

Daylight enters from the open south side and from gaps in the rim. Inside, it's normal light — but wrong by a margin you can't measure. Shadows are too sharp. Edges too crisp. In the center, where the ridges peak, there's a spot where the light splits briefly and you see two overlapping versions of the same place, like a reflection in imperfect glass, and then it's gone.

The air smells of hot clay and copper and something faintly sweet — not decay, not rot, but the scent of a process too slow to see with anything but patience.

The soundscape is mostly quiet. The filaments' tonal hum. The faint click of flatback carapaces touching carapaces. Distant ground-voice of the Singer. Between these, silence — not empty silence, but held silence, the kind that exists in a room where something is about to happen and everything knows it except you.

---

**What the Place Is**

A pocket. A deposit on the surface of something larger, something unremarkable that surrounds it on every side. The hollow is not *in* the landscape so much as it is *on* it — a lesion, a bloom, a place where the ordinary ground became thin and something else pressed through.

Everything inside is alive. Everything inside is new. Everything is packed — no unused space, no margin, every crevice filled with filament or creature or mineral growth that is one step away from being any of those things. The distinction between geology and biology has not been resolved here. It may never be.

This place is not old. It might be days. It might be hours. It was here before you arrived. It will be here after — differently, feeding, building its ridges, rippling its flatbacks, humming through its Singer in the warm dark beneath the overhang, growing toward whatever it is becoming.
