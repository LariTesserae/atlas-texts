# Preparation Guide for Visiting The Saturation

## I. Understanding What You Are About to Walk Onto

Before anything else — before the boots, the compass, the breath — you need to grasp this: **The Saturation is not a landscape you visit. It is a place that absorbs you into its texture and continues to do so long after you stop moving.** Every prior visitor who left behind carved stones and half-walls is still here, preserved, possibly indistinguishable from the mineral crust underfoot. You are not going somewhere. You are being deposited.

An ordinary multiverse traveler typically arrives in a new world with assumptions: that the ground is ground, that distance measures something, that the things you step on are either alive or dead, that time moves in one direction and leaves evidence. The Saturation violates all of these so quietly and so completely that you will not notice the violation for hours. By then you will already be inside it.

Read everything below. Then read it again. Then step out of the transit point and stand still for as long as you can bear. The standing still is not optional. It is the single most important thing you can do.

## II. Physical Preparation

### Footwear

The ground surface is a compressed mineral-biological mat with the consistency of olditeite — dense, unyielding, and present in a way that feels almost responsive. This is not soil you sink into. This is a surface that pushes back. Standard hiking boots will transmit every micro-ridge and organism-body through your soles, and after several hours you will feel as though you are walking on the bones of something vast and very old.

**Recommendation:** Thick-soled boots with rigid midsoles, the kind designed for volcanic terrain or glacier hiking. You want material between you and that mat. The temptation will be to go light — the terrain looks flat, the walking looks easy. Resist this. Every traveler who has gone light has reported foot pain that does not correspond to the effort expended, as though the surface stores the impact and returns it later.

Consider gaiters. The mat is alive at its surface. Filamentous organisms thread across it. When you step, you compress them. They do not die. They persist, altered, against your boot. Over days, you will track a faint pale residue inside your gaiters — mineral and biological dust that smells of nothing at all and will not wash out. Bring an old toothbrush for your boots. You will need it, not for cleaning, but for the small psychological mercy of scrubbing something.

### Clothing

The air is cool, almost cold in the way stone is cold, but not thin. No wind — or wind so slow it is a gradient. Temperature variation across a day (assuming you can discern a day) is minimal. You will not need heavy insulation, but you will need full coverage.

**Why full coverage:** The Saturation's atmosphere is bone-dry and empty. It carries no moisture, no dust, no scent. Exposed skin in this environment will not burn or chap in any obvious way. Instead, it will simply *feel less*. You will notice the numbness slowly, the way you notice a room getting quieter when a refrigerator motor turns off. By the time you are aware that your forearms feel like stone, you will have been numb for hours. Wear long sleeves. Wear gloves if you can tolerate the dexterity loss. Reapply a barrier cream — petroleum jelly, anything — to your face. The air's emptiness is not neutral. It is actively withdrawing sensation.

Wear muted colors. Not because anything hunts here — nothing does — but because the fauna-mat is a field of pale domes, dark mineral-felt, and chalky whites. Bright colors create a visual dissonance that your eye will keep returning to, and on this landscape, every unnecessary point of visual focus drains something. You do not want to add clutter to a world that is already overburdened with visual information your brain is not equipped to process.

### Water and Food

Bring everything you need. There is nothing here — no water source, no edible organism, nothing to forage. The pale filaments and mineral-stalks that pass for vegetation extract trace compounds from the substrate in processes so slow they are geological. You could, in theory, chew one. It would taste like chalk and mineral dust. It would sustain you not at all.

Water consumption will be lower than you expect in cool, still air, but the dryness is deceptive. Your mucous membranes will dry from the outside in, the air pulling moisture from you with the slow persistence of everything here. Drink on a schedule, not on thirst. You will not feel thirsty until you are already significantly dehydrated, and by then the cognitive effects will make navigation (already unreliable here) nearly impossible.

### Shelter

You can carry a tent, but you will likely not use it. The Saturation does not experience weather in any conventional sense. The light is consistent — sunny, even, perpetual-afternoon bright — and the air is still. The cold is mild and penetrates slowly, like the onset of deep water. Most travelers end up sleeping on the surface of the mat, inside the partial shelters that prior visitors built millennia ago, or simply in the open. The ground is hard but not uncomfortable if you pad it. Bring a foam pad anyway. The psychological comfort of separation from the mat, even at night, is worth the weight.

If you do sleep on the mat directly, you will feel it. Not during the night — the quiet and the stillness are actually restful in a way that borders on narcotic. But in the morning, when you stand, you will see that your body has left a faint impression in the substrate, an outline of compression that has not yet rebounded. It rebounds. Slowly. Within a few hours the mat is seamless again. But the first time you see your own shape pressed into something that was here before your species existed, something in your hindbrain will object. Bring the pad.

## III. Navigational Equipment and Its Limitations

### What to Bring

Bring a compass. Bring GPS. Bring a star chart. Bring every orientation tool you own. **None of them will work the way you expect.**

The compass works — its needle points to magnetic north — but the direction it gives you will not correspond to the direction the landscape implies. You will look north, see open paleite stretching to a clean horizon, and walk. You will arrive at familiar terrain in a direction that should have been south. The Saturation's geometry is not Euclidean. It is not non-Euclidean in any dramatic, comprehensible way — there are no warped spirals or impossible angles. The space here is *stale*. It has the geometry of a closed loop that has forgotten it is closed. Walking north long enough will return you to where you started, but the ground under your feet will be different — older, darker, textured in ways your eye can see but your mind cannot categorize.

GPS coordinates will update normally, but the coordinates will not map to consistent features. You will pass the same longitude and latitude multiple times, each time at a different apparent location on the plateau. The system works. It simply works on a coordinate plane that does not correspond to the experience of walking.

### What This Means Practically

**Do not plan a route. Plan a return signal.**

Leave a marker at your transit point — something tall, something reflective, something that cannot be mistaken for natural fauna or geological feature. Then set a strict time limit. When your time is up, you return to the marker. Not by navigating back. By *remembering*. Because the Saturation will not help you find your way. It will show you familiar shapes at wrong distances. It will present the cairn you passed three hours ago as though it is ahead of you when you are certain you turned away from it. It will make the horizon look walkable in twenty minutes and then ensure that twenty minutes of walking achieves nothing.

The best navigational strategy is **brute-force triangulation**: walk in a straight line from your marker for a set duration (count your steps — stride length is one of the few reliable measurements here), turn a precise angle, walk again, turn again, and return. Idealy your triangle encloses a small area you can explore and then leave. Do not wander. Wandering here is not adventure. It is the beginning of incorporation.

### Light

The sun is the only thing on this plane that behaves with total reliability. It rises. It sets. It casts light. Its shadows are sharp — too sharp, because the air scatters nothing. There is no atmospheric haze, no diffusion. The light is a tool and nothing else. Use it to see. Use it to gauge the subjective passage of time. **Do not trust it to orient you.** The sun tracks across a sky that is, by all appearances, normal. But "north is where the sun is, until it isn't" — and the moment you try to use solar position for navigation, you will find that your internal clock has drifted, imperceptibly, and you have been estimating time wrong for hours. The Saturation does not disrupt your clock actively. It simply provides no external cues for recalibration, and the light is so uniform, so consistently pleasant, that hours collapse into a single "afternoon" feeling that you cannot shake.

Bring a clock. An analog watch. Wind it or charge it but do not rely on a phone — the phone will lose signal and then the screen will become your only sense of time, and that is a dependency you do not want here. The analog watch gives you numbers. Numbers are real. The Saturation has no interest in real, but it has no interest in numbers either, and this neutrality makes numbers more reliable than anything else.

## IV. Psychological and Sensory Preparation

### The Quiet

This is the thing no one warns you about, because every account of The Saturation comes back with the same paradox: **the quiet is the loudest thing about the place.**

It is not silence. Silence would be a relief. What The Saturation has is a *substrate sound* — a low hum that exists below the threshold of easy hearing. The aggregate of ten thousand tiny organisms moving through mineral. The faint creak of a landscape compressing under its own age. The imperceptible settling of something so heavy and so stable that movement has become a property of mass, not of intent.

To hear it, you have to stand still. You have to stop breathing. And then the place begins to whisper.

**Prepare:** Before your visit, practice sitting in silence for extended periods. Not meditation silence — *environmental* silence, the kind where there is literally nothing to hear and your brain, desperate for stimulus, begins generating phantom sounds. If you can sit with that for an hour without reaching for noise, you will be able to handle the Saturation's quiet. If you cannot, the substrate-sound will crawl under your skin and you will not sleep for days afterward.

### The Visual Overload and Sensory Mismatch

Your eyes will be full. Your other senses will be empty. The ground is teeming — *teeming* — with life, layers upon layers of sessile organisms, filaments, pale domes and dark mineral-felt and chalky white transitions that your visual cortex will attempt to parse and fail. Meanwhile, the air offers nothing. No scent. No taste. No wind on your face. The mismatch is vertiginous. Travelers describe it as standing inside a painting — all visual information, no physical feedback.

**Prepare:** Bring something with a strong, consistent scent. Not perfume — something *grounding*. Coffee beans in a sealed pouch that you open periodically. A sprig of rosemary. A strip of leather. Anything that gives your olfactory system something to anchor to. The Saturation will strip smell from you within minutes. The anchor is not for survival. It is for *sanity*.

### The Temporal Distortion

The light does not change. Not obviously. Not in the way you expect. A day on The Saturation feels like a single long afternoon. There is no dawn color, no dusk deepening. The shadows sharpen and soften with solar angle, but the sky remains bright and unremarkable. You will lose track of days. Travelers have reported spending what they believed were two or three days on the plateau and later discovering, through ration counts and watch timestamps, that they were there for seven.

**Prepare:** Mark each day mechanically. Notch a stick. Move a stone from one pile to another. Do not trust your body's sense of time. The Saturation's indifference to entropy extends to your circadian rhythm. You will not feel tired when you should. You will not feel hungry. You will simply *continue*, and continuation without markers is the first step toward incorporation — not physical incorporation, not yet, but *perceptual* incorporation, the slow merging of your sense of self with the slow, geological consciousness of the mat.

### The Fauna

You will want to interact with it. You will see the raised forms in the mat — the domes, the mounds, the house-sized shapes half-embedded in the ground — and you will want to touch them, examine them, determine what is alive and what is dead. **Do not touch them.** Not because they are dangerous. They are not. Nothing on The Saturation is dangerous in any conventional sense. But the fauna absorbs contact. Touch a dome and your handprint will remain, slowly mineralized, over the course of hours. By the time you leave, the thing you touched will carry your fingerprint in its surface, and your fingerprint will become part of its texture, indistinguishable from the rest.

This is not poetic. This is literal. The incorporation is real. The place preserves contact. You do not want to leave yourself — literally, physically — embedded in something that thinks on a timescale of strata.

More troubling is the cognition. When you look down at high density, when you see the distribution and regularity of forms, the faint patterning that suggests something *organized* — something *thinking* — your brain will attempt to model it. You will see intention where there is only accumulation. You will see a mind where there is only vast, slow, geological life. But the feeling will not go away. You will feel *seen* by the ground. You are not being seen. You are being *registered*, the way a stone registers pressure — without awareness, without intent, but with a permanence that makes awareness irrelevant.

Tell yourself this. Repeat it. **I am not being seen. I am being registered, without awareness, without intent, but with a permanence that makes awareness irrelevant.** If you can hold this thought when the feeling of being observed becomes overwhelming, you will be fine. If you cannot, you will begin to feel the first stages of perceptual dissolution — the sense that the boundary between you and the mat is thinning — and that is the point at which you should leave.

## V. What to Do and What Not to Do

**Do:**
- Arrive and stand still for at least thirty minutes before walking. Let your senses recalibrate to the emptiness and the visual density simultaneously.
- Walk slowly. Every step compresses organisms that have been alive (or dead-alive, or geological-alive) for millennia. Walk as though the ground remembers, because it does.
- Carry a journal and write by hand. Your phone will lose charge. Your mind will lose coherence. Written words are the one artifact you produce that you can take back. Leave the journal entries in the partial shelters if you must. The Saturation will preserve them better than you can.
- Set a hard time limit and a hard distance limit. Do not negotiate with either.
- Look back frequently. The landscape behind you does not look the same as the landscape ahead. Familiarity from one direction can become alienity from another, and this is not a trick of the light. The Saturation's spatial instability is directional.

**Do Not:**
- Remove any artifact, sample, or organism. The Saturation's indifference is not an invitation. Taking a piece of the place is like taking a sentence from a book written in a language you do not understand — you will have something, but you will not know what it means, and the meaning you impose will be wrong.
- Follow the filaments. The pale, nearly translucent networks that thread across the mat's surface move with imperceptible slowness. If you track one — if you crouch and watch and wait — you will enter a time-distortion. Minutes will become perceived hours. You will look up and the light will be in the same position and your watch will say six hours have passed. This is not the filaments' intent. They have no intent. But the attention you give them is the attention they absorb, and the Saturation feeds on attention the way the mat feeds on compression — slowly, permanently.
- Build anything. Do not stack stones. Do not dig. A traveler once dug a shallow trench to sleep in and the next morning found the walls had partially regenerated, new mineral deposits filling the cuts overnight. This is not metaphor. The Saturation *heals* around disruption. Your modifications will be absorbed. You will not be modifying the place. The place will be modifying you for being in it.
- Stay past your limit. The most common mistake — the one that ends most visits badly — is the conviction that *tomorrow will be different*. The Saturation does not promise variety. It promises sameness, and sameness is its mechanism. Each day is the same afternoon. Each step is the same texture. Each time you think you've found something new, you will find, on closer inspection, that you are standing on something you've already seen, laid out in a pattern your tired brain has finally decoded. This is when travelers sit down. This is when they stop moving. This is when the incorporation begins in earnest.

## VI. Departure and Return

Leave the way you came. If you cannot find the way you came — and you may not; distance and direction are unreliable — **stop, sit, and wait**. The Saturation does not want you to leave. It does not want anything; wanting is a property of organisms that operate on comprehensible timescales. But the transit point has a fixed coordinate in a coordinate system that the plateau's geometry, however distorted, cannot fully overwrite. If you are patient, if you are still, the transit point will become *more present*, not less, because your displacement from it is the one reliable vector the place cannot erase.

When you return — if you return — you will carry nothing. No samples. No photographs that look right (every photo taken on The Saturation shows the ground in sharper detail than the eye perceived, as though the camera's sensor can resolve what the human brain has learned to blur). You will carry a faint mineral residue on your boots that will not wash out for weeks. You will dream of flat horizons and pale domes, and the dreams will feel less like memories than like *geological deposits*, layers of experience pressed into you with a weight you cannot account for.

You will be changed. Not dramatically. Not visibly. But you will find yourself standing still for long periods without realizing it. You will find yourself listening to silence and hearing something beneath it. You will look at river stones and see the same patient smoothness, the same refusal to be anything other than what endless pressure has made them, and you will feel — for just a moment — that you understand.

You don't. But the Saturation has given you the *shape* of understanding, compressed and layered and dense, and that shape will sit in you like a stone for the rest of your life.

---

## VII. Final Note

Every world has a texture. Most worlds let you touch it and pull your hand back. The Saturation does not. The Saturation *is* its texture — and if you stay long enough, if you forget yourself in the silence and the slowness and the overwhelming presence of ten thousand lives compressed into a ground that refuses to be ground but is, you will lose the boundary between your texture and the texture of the place.

You will not die. You will become *part of the mat*. Not metaphorically. You will sit, or stand, or lie down, and the ground will receive you with the same indifferent patience it has received everything else, and your edges will soften, and your specificity will compress, and in five thousand years a future traveler may step on the place where you sat and feel, without knowing why, that the ground there is slightly warmer than the rest.

Go. Stand still. Set your watch. Walk your triangle. Leave when the time is up.

And do not, under any circumstances, sit down and close your eyes. The Saturation does not need you to sleep. It needs you to be *present*. And presence, here, is the first layer.
