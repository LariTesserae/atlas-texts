# Preparing for the Warrens of Karstheld

## I. What You Are Walking Into

The Warrens are not a ruin. They are not ancient. They are not abandoned. They are *new* — weeks old, perhaps less — and they are running. Before you pack a single thing, understand this: you are entering a living system that was not built for visitors, was not built by anything you can easily recognize as human, and has not yet decided if it is going to keep existing. That combination — newness, purpose, and uncertainty — is what will define your experience. This place has the silence of a held breath.

---

## II. Physical Preparation

### Heat and Humidity
The Warrens carry a warmth that is not furnace-dry but *biological* — the soaked heat of a fevered body under blankets. Humidity will saturate your clothing within minutes and cling to your skin for the duration of your stay. Dress in materials that wick and dry: synthetics or treated wools, never untreated cotton (it will become a second skin of damp fabric and will not let go). Bring more water than you think you need — you will be sweating constantly, and the warm, mineral-heavy air will deceive your thirst response. You will not feel how much you are losing. Drink on a schedule, not on sensation.

Expect condensation on every surface. Metal equipment you carry will fog and bead. Sealed containers will accumulate moisture on their exteriors. Lens equipment — if you carry optics of any kind — will need constant wiping or anti-fog treatment.

### Air Quality
The air is thick, mineral-scented, and potentially compromised. The place runs on steam vents and industrial infrastructure that exhale gases you cannot identify by smell alone. The sweet-melon note in the air may be a harmless mineral off-gassing or something more insidious. At minimum, carry a respirator rated for unknown industrial atmospheres — not a dust mask, a proper half-face respirator with multi-spectrum cartridges. If you have no way to test the air composition before entering, treat every breath as potentially bioactive. The stillness of the air underground means nothing is dispersing; everything is accumulating.

### Terrain and Architecture
The Warrens are dense. Everything is close, nested, stacked — staircases that spiral both directions, catwalks bolted between walls shoulder-width apart, rooms that are octagonal for no reason you can determine. Ceilings are low. Pathways are narrow. If you are tall, you will be uncomfortable. If you are claustrophobic, you will be in distress. Wear close-fitting clothing and secure any loose gear — protruding straps, dangling tools, anything that will catch on the constant jutting edges and half-finished weld seams that line every passage.

Bring light, but understand that it will feel redundant. The walls glow with a pale amber-white mineral paste that provides pervasive, sourceless illumination — the quality of light inside a tooth, perpetual dusk, never bright. Your eyes will adjust to this strange spectrum, but your depth perception may be subtly wrong under it. Carry a secondary light source with a color temperature you trust (daylight-balanced, 5000K or above) for tasks where accuracy matters: reading instruments, checking seals, examining the fungal growths.

The floor is uneven. Surfaces are freshly cut and still sharp. Chisel marks are legible on bedrock; weld seams are raised and uncured in places. Wear boots with thick, puncture-resistant soles. Do not kneel. Do not brace your palms against the walls without checking for heat — some pipes run hot enough to blister, and they are not always visible under the scaffolding and pipe-bundles that drape every surface.

## III. Sensory and Psychological Preparation

This is the part most travelers underestimate, and it is the part that will matter most.

### The Sound

The acoustics of the Warrens do not behave like acoustics should. Sound does not echo — the surfaces are too fresh, too angular — but it *persists*. When you speak, a ghost of your voice remains for a half-second after the words end, not bouncing back but *staying*, hanging in the air like a word you almost said. Every noise follows this pattern: footsteps, pipe-clangs, the sigh of a vent. The place is acoustically *reluctant to let go*.

In a place with ambient biological noise — birds, insects, wind, the general hum of living things — this would be unsettling but manageable. But there is no biological noise in the Warrens. None. No insects in the water, no rodents in the walls, nothing. The silence this produces is not peaceful. It is a vacuum. You will hear your own heartbeat. You will hear your breathing. You will hear the faint crack of your neck when you turn too quickly, and it will sound enormous, and the ghost of it will linger after it's done.

**Prepare for this specifically.** Spend time before your departure in silence — not comfortable silence, but *deliberate* silence, in a space where you can sit with nothing but your own body sounds and learn to tolerate them. If you have a metronome or a steady rhythmic sound source, practice focusing on it until it becomes background. The worst thing that can happen in the Warrens is that you become hyperaware of your own physiology and cannot escape it. Some travelers report that the acoustic persistence makes them feel *accompanied* — as though the lingering sounds are traces of someone else, just out of sight. This feeling is a trick of the architecture. Do not engage with it.

### The Architecture's Wrongness

The buildings in the Warrens were built by someone who understood construction but not *habitation*. The structures are competent — sound welds, true foundations, functional plumbing — but they feel *studied*, as though designed by someone who watched humans build and then tried to replicate it from memory. Doorways are slightly wrong in dimension. Rooms are octagonal where rectangular would suffice. Staircases change their spiral direction without signal. The spaces do not feel accidental, and human-built spaces are supposed to feel at least a little accidental.

You will feel this as a low-level wrongness that you cannot articulate. It will make you uneasy in ways you cannot justify. Acknowledge this when it happens. Name it: *the architecture is not for me.* It was not built for anyone from the surface. The discomfort is informational, not threatening.

### The Absence of Life

No animals. No insects. No signs of anything biological beyond the sparse mosses and the pulsing fungal bodies that rise from cracks in the floor. You are accustomed to a world saturated with other organisms. Their absence will register in your body before your mind can process it — a primal alertness, a feeling of exposure, the sense that you are the only living thing in a system that was not designed around life.

**This is correct.** You are likely the only biological visitor this place has received. The Warrens are a machine, not an ecosystem. Approach it as such.

## IV. Hazard Assessment

### Industrial Infrastructure
The Warrens are threaded with pipes carrying unknown fluids. Some run hot and emit a steady hiss. Others are cold, beaded with condensation, and shudder when something passes through. These are not labeled. They are not organized by any system you recognize. Do not touch pipes you cannot verify are cool. Do not taste, inhale, or make skin contact with any fluid that leaks from joints — the ceramic insulation on the electrical conduits is still curing, and the hand-applied sealant on junctions may be chemically active.

The three generators produce a subsonic throb you feel in your sternum rather than hear. **This is infrasound.** Prolonged exposure causes nausea, anxiety, a sense of prescient dread, and visual disturbances at the periphery of your field of vision. If you carry any infrasonometer, deploy it immediately upon arrival to establish baseline readings. If you do not have one, monitor your own emotional state: if you begin to feel inexplicable unease, dread, or the sensation that something is approaching from behind you, it is likely a physiological response to the generators, not evidence of a threat. Limit sustained exposure. Do not sleep near generator infrastructure.

### Biological Hazards
The fungal fruiting bodies — squat, ovoid, bone-colored, warm to the touch, pulsing on a rhythm so slow you question whether it is real — are unclassified. **Do not touch them. Do not inhale near them. Do not consume anything that grows in their proximity.** The fact that they are warm suggests metabolic activity, possibly a chemotrophic or thermogenic process unlike anything catalogued in surface biology. Their spores, if they produce spores, are unknown. Treat them as hazardous until proven otherwise.

The translucent mosses along steam vents and the greenish-grey crusts on water-basin rims are similarly unclassified. They appear to photosynthesize from mineral-spectrum light. They may be harmless. They may not. Observe without contact.

### The Water
The water in the Warrens is warm, tastes faintly mineral, then sweet, then like nothing at all. This three-stage taste profile suggests dissolved minerals followed by some volatile organic compound followed by a desensitizing agent. It may be safe. It may be mildly psychoactive in cumulative doses. **Do not drink it without filtration and chemical testing.** The fact that it forms in beads that defy gravity — clinging to undersides of ledges, forming in cracks where it should not collect — suggests unusual surface tension properties, possibly due to dissolved substances you do not want in your bloodstream without understanding them.

### Dimensional Considerations
The wrongness of the water, the persistence of sound, the light that "holds wrong," the sense that the place has not fully committed to existing — these may be signs of dimensional instability. If you are traveling between worlds, carry your standard dimensional-anchor equipment. Check your bearings upon arrival and at regular intervals. The Warrens sit in a space that was "not meant for building" — the stone remembers being stone. There is a chance that the local reality is still negotiating with the presence of a constructed environment inside it, and that negotiation may produce localized physics anomalies. Be prepared for the unexpected: rooms that should connect but don't, passages that seem longer on the return trip, time perception shifts near the glowing mineral paste.

## V. Practical and Strategic Considerations

### Navigation
The Warrens do not follow a logic you can intuit. There is no cardinal orientation underground. The architecture loops, doubles back, and presents choices that feel arbitrary. Bring physical marking supplies — chalk, biodegradable flagging tape, UV-reactive markers — and mark your route obsessively. The place is small, roughly the size of a market square, but it is *deep*, and the stacked levels, spiral staircases, and shoulder-width catwalks make spatial reasoning unreliable. What feels like a loop may not be. What feels like a dead end may open into a lower level you didn't know existed.

### Social Contact
The Warrens are built for inhabitants — a hamlet's worth of structures, active infrastructure, industrial-scale generators. But you may not see anyone. The place may be occupied by beings who avoid visitors, or by workers who are indistinguishable from the infrastructure they maintain, or by something else entirely. Do not assume the settlement is empty just because you see no one. Everything here is close and enclosed; observers could be three feet away, behind a pipe bundle, and you would never know.

Do not be hostile. Do not be overly friendly. Be calm, be observant, and be prepared to explain what you are and why you are here in terms that a non-surface intelligence might understand. If you do encounter inhabitants, note their behavior toward each other, toward the infrastructure, and toward you. Their relationship to the system they built will tell you more about the Warrens' purpose than any instrument can.

### Duration
The place is young. It is in its first months, still running up to equilibrium, still becoming. This means it is also at its most unstable. Systems that have not settled are more prone to surprises — pressure fluctuations in unknown pipes, steam vent cycles that are not yet rhythmic, structural settling that may produce cracks, shifts, or collapses in freshly excavated spaces. **Do not stay longer than necessary.** Gather your data, make your observations, and leave. The Warrens will outlast you whether you stay or go, but there is no reason to test whether a place that hasn't decided if it will last will decide to stop while you are inside it.

### What to Bring
- Respirator with multi-spectrum cartridges
- Daylight-balanced portable light
- Insulated gloves (heat-resistant, cut-resistant)
- Thick-soled boots, close-fitting clothing
- Water and food from outside — do not rely on Warrens water
- Infrasonometer or equivalent frequency-monitoring equipment
- Dimensional anchor and navigational instruments
- pH strips and basic chemical assay kit (for water and surface deposits)
- Marking supplies for navigation
- Recording equipment (audio and visual) — document the acoustic persistence, it may be diagnostically useful
- A compact emergency shelter — if you need to stop moving and be enclosed, you want it to be *your* enclosure, not a Warrens room

### What to Leave Behind
- Assumptions about how physics works
- Any expectation that this place will make sense to you
- The belief that newness equals fragility — this place was built with industrial-scale infrastructure by someone who knew what they were doing, even if their aesthetic is *other*. Treat it as structurally sound until demonstrated otherwise.

## VI. The Feeling of Being There

In the end, what you need to prepare for is not a hazard. It is a *feeling*. The Warrens will make you feel like a cell that has wandered into an organ it was never part of. Everything is functioning. Everything is interconnected. Everything is alive with process and purpose. And none of it is for you. You will walk through humming corridors and past pulsing organisms and along catwalks that vibrate with the passage of fluids you cannot name, and you will feel — with a certainty that lives below thought — that you are inside something that knows you are there and does not care.

That is not hostility. It is indifference, which is worse. The Warrens of Karstheld do not welcome you. They do not exclude you. You are simply not the category of thing this place was built for. Find what you came for, mark your path back, and surface into a world that will feel strange and bright and full of space in ways you did not notice before.

Go in prepared. Come out changed, if that is what happens. Either way, come out.
