# Field Preparation Guide: The Equilibrium Sea

## A Practical Manual for the Interdimensional Traveler

---

### I. Understanding What You're Walking Into

Before you pack a single piece of gear, internalize this: **The Equilibrium Sea does not care that you exist.** This is not a wilderness that can be survived with the right knowledge. It is not a predator that can be outwitted. It is a system so perfectly self-contained, so thoroughly settled into its own rhythms, that your presence registers as less than a single molt from a single grazer on a single ridge six hundred miles from your camp. The machines still run, the fauna still feed, the water still takes your warmth — not because of you, but because they always have. Prepare accordingly.

That said: the place is not hostile. It is merely indifferent, which is the more difficult thing to survive.

---

### II. Thermal Protection — Your First and Most Critical Concern

The water is described as cold "beyond what cold should feel like in water — a cold that has had eons to understand the concept of a body's warmth and to remove it methodically." You will be in it. Knee-deep to waist-deep, across what could be a multi-hundred-mile traverse. There is no staying dry. There is no shore to rest on where your feet aren't submerged.

- **Full drysuit** — not wetsuit, *drysuit*. Something with a vulcanized neck seal and waterproof zipper, insulated with aerogel or equivalent high-R-value undergarment system. If your tech level allows, a heated undergarment powered by a compact fusion cell or biochemical battery is worth its weight in extraction priority.
- **Extremity protection** — heated gloves (you'll need fine motor control for climbing), neoprene hood under your drysuit's neck seal, insulated boots (see Footwear section below).
- **Hypothermia protocol** — even well-suited, expect core temperature to drift downward over hours of immersion. Carry chemical heat packs for emergency rewarming, and build your itinerary around mandatory warming stops. If you have access to metabolic boosters or synthetic brown-fat supplements, take them.
- **Assume 4–6 hours of functional exposure** before mild hypothermia onset even with good gear. Plan your routes so you're never more than 2 hours from shelter or extraction at any given point.

---

### III. Footwear — The Ground Will Cut You

The terrain is young volcanic basalt: razor-sharp edges, glass-smooth surfaces, hexagonal columns that fracture without warning. Imagine walking across a field of broken glass the size of a continent, submerged in freezing water.

- **Armored, waterproof boots** with vulcanized rubber soles, puncture-resistant plates (titanium or ceramic composite), and aggressive tread designed for wet glass. Think deep-sea volcanic vent research footwear dialed up to extreme.
- **Gaiters** — the water will find every gap. Sealed gaiters connecting boot to drysuit leg are essential to prevent ingress.
- **Replaceable crampons or micro-spikes** for the glass cliffs. The basalt ridges can be climbed, but not in normal boots. The hexagonal columns offer handholds, but the edges will shred your palms (see Hand Protection below).

---

### IV. Audiological Protection — The Sound Will Break You Before the Cold Does

The drone is the defining feature of the Equilibrium Sea. It is not a sound so much as a *presence* — layered, permanent, continental in scale. It includes:

- The mechanical hum of still-running industrial machinery (conveyor motors, generators)
- The hiss of steam from corroded smokestacks
- The metronomic clicking of grazer mandibles, multiplied across a continental colony
- The percussive cracks of pressurized water eruptions from the flooded canyons below
- The metallic clatter of the watchtower ladder, resonating on its own in the sound-saturated air

This is not an environment where you "get used to it." Chronic exposure to a constant, complex, loud drone at these frequencies causes:

- Progressive hearing damage
- Disorientation and loss of spatial awareness
- Severe psychological deterioration — anxiety, paranoia, dissociative episodes
- Sleep impossibility (even during the 15%-dimming "night")

**Required gear:**

- **Active noise-canceling headphones** (best available) *under* a waterproof hood, with physical ear-cup seals as backup for when the electronics inevitably face moisture intrusion
- **Underwater:** in-ear pressure-equalizing plugs if you submerge, though in knee-deep water this is less of a concern for immersed ears than for the sheer bone-conduction sound that travels up through the water into your skull
- Consider a **pharmacological countermeasure** — short-course anxiolytics for the first 48 hours, if your medical kit allows, to prevent the initial sensory overload from cascading into panic

---

### V. Navigation — You Cannot Trust the Sky

The sky is a pale, uniform dome. There is no sun, no visible celestial bodies, no shadows of useful definition. "Night" is a fifteen percent dimming that happens without warning or clear rhythm. You cannot navigate by stars, cannot tell time by the sun's position, cannot orient by any natural cue.

- **Electronic navigation only** — GPS will not function (wrong planet; likely wrong dimensional frame). You need a local positioning system: LIDAR terrain mapping, inertial navigation unit, or beacon-based triangulation if you've pre-scouted.
- **Physical markers** — on arrival, establish a route using reflective trail markers or biodegradable beacons. The terrain is featureless for hundreds of miles in places. You **will** get lost without markers.
- **The ruins as landmarks** — the factory complexes are separated by ~200 miles. The watchtower is visible from a great distance on its four iron legs. Use these as waypoints, not as destinations (see Structural Hazards).
- **Carry a clock set to your home frame's time** and maintain a rigid schedule. The place's temporal uniformity is deeply disorienting.

---

### VI. Food, Water, and Air

- **Water:** The local liquid is a mineral-saturated solution of sulfur, iron, and dissolved volcanic compounds. It is emphatically not drinking water. Bring all water with you. Budget at minimum 3 liters per day per person in a drysuit environment — more if exerting yourself hiking the ridges.
- **Food:** There is nothing here to eat. The fungal-chemosynthetic food chain supports the local fauna but produces nothing a human can consume without severe gastrointestinal consequences. Bring high-calorie, dense rations. You'll be burning calories fast against the cold. Minimum 4,000 kcal/day.
- **Air:** The atmosphere is breathable in the open, but near the caldera vents and steam plumes, mineral vapor concentrations could irritate or damage lungs. Carry a **filtered respirator** (P100 minimum) for use in vapor zones. If the vapor is hydrogen sulfide — likely, given the sulfur metabolism of the local fungus — even brief unprotected exposure is dangerous. Below 10 ppm, headache and nausea onset.

---

### VII. Shelter

- There are no structures built for human use. The factory ruins offer partial cover but are **structurally compromised** — corroded iron, flooded lower floors, unstable floors, steam hazards.
- **Portable shelter**: a lightweight, insulated bivouac system. Set it on elevated terrain — the ridges, the artificial mineral islands near the conveyors, or the tops of the larger basalt fins. Avoid setting up near fissures (eruption risk), near the standing ruins (collapse risk), or on low ground (flooding from the eruptions that "happen at irregular intervals but are inevitable in occurrence").
- If you can secure permission (or find no objection), the **watchtower platform** offers the best vantage point and most stable elevated shelter, though the ladder access is hazardous (see below) and the tower itself leans.

---

### VIII. Structural Hazards — The Ruins Are Dying Slowly

The industrial ruins are corroded iron and steel in a chemically aggressive environment. They look stable. Many of them are not.

- **Factory complex:** Six stories of riveted iron, half-consumed by water, lower floors flooded and dark. The smokestacks vent hot steam unpredictably. **Do not enter the lower floors** — structural integrity of submerged ironwork is unknowable, and the water in those spaces may be superheated near geothermal sources.
- **Conveyor belt:** Still running. The motor whines at chest-resonant frequencies. The output pile has overflowed into the shallows — unstable ground of loose mineral debris over submerged basalt. Approach the conveyor with extreme caution: the belt could start, jam, or shift unpredictably.
- **Cylindrical tanks:** Corroded, split, leaking unknown long-cold contents. The local fauna feeds at the spill sites, which tells you the contents are either nutritive to the local biochemistry or toxic in ways you cannot identify. **Do not touch, taste, or inhale near spill sites** without respiratory and dermal protection.
- **Watchtower:** Four iron legs, leaning, ladder rattling in the windless air from resonant vibration. Climb at your own risk. The view from the top is extraordinary and the single most psychologically intense experience available on the Equilibrium Sea — but the platform has no railings, the iron is corroded, and the wind at sixty feet on an open basalt ridge is not to be underestimated. If you climb: harness, helmet, and someone on the ground.

---

### IX. Fauna — Observe, Do Not Engage

The ecological machine here is closed, ancient, and efficient. You are an anomaly, not a participant.

- **Grazers** (armored, dog-sized, mandible-clicking): Appear non-aggressive. They scrape the fungal crust from the glass and move with deliberate, metronomic patience. They shed and regrow their segmented shells. Do not approach closely — the mandibles could amputate a finger, and startling a colony may cause unpredictable stampedes across submerged ridges.
- **Filter-feeders** (sixty-foot translucent sacs anchored to columns): Not a threat, but their scale is psychologically devastating at close range. The pulsing of their feeding filaments is hypnotic in the wrong headspace. Do not swim beneath them — periodic expulsion jets could carry you under.
- **Scavengers** (small, dark, multilegged, quick): These will investigate any foreign object, including your camp, your gear, and potentially your boots while you're wearing them. The fungal crust on your equipment will attract them. Store food in sealed containers (you should already be doing this) and shake out boots before wearing. They are not known to be venomous, but no one has tested this comprehensively.
- **Do not attempt to collect specimens** without biosafety-level containment. The chemistries at work are not terrestrial. The fungal substrate alone operates on mineral-metabolic pathways that took visiting scholars decades to understand. Assume everything here is biologically active in ways you don't understand.

---

### X. Geological Hazards — The Earth Remembers It Is Volcanic

- **Pressurized water eruptions** from fissures in the submerged canyons. These erupt a hundred feet into the air with percussive force sufficient to knock a person unconscious at close range. They are unpredictable in timing but "inevitable in occurrence." When you hear a deep subterranean groaning, move away from any visible fissure immediately. Do not camp in low-lying areas between ridges.
- **Hexagonal column collapse** — the basalt fins crack and shed pieces regularly. Do not stand directly beneath overhanging formations. Do not put full weight on any column edge that looks freshly fractured.
- **Canyon edges** — the flooded chasms below the surface are unmapped, pressurized, and deep. The water near a canyon rim may be deceptively deep, and the edges may undercut. Probe depth with a pole before crossing any area where the bottom drops away suddenly.

### XI. Psychological Preparation

This is not a section you skip.

The Equilibrium Sea is a place that operates on scales — temporal, spatial, and existential — that have no analog in human experience. The uniformity of the light. The sameness of the horizon. The mechanical constancy of the drone. The absence of any human artifact, any sign, any trace that anyone has ever cared about this place. The ruins are not monuments to human ambition; they are alien industrial remnants slowly surrendering to an alien ecology, indifferent to you as to everything else.

Visitors have reported:
- A creeping sense that the equilibrium is a *philosophy*, not just a physical state — that the place is demonstrating something about the universe
- Auditory hallucination after 48+ hours (hearing human voices, music, or structured patterns embedded in the drone — there are none; the pattern is pure noise at a scale that mimics signal)
- Existential dissociation — the sense that one's own presence is cosmologically absurd, that the self is a temporary perturbation in a system that has no mechanism for noticing perturbation
- A paradoxical compulsion to stay — "the equilibrium is seductive," as one researcher wrote. "It does not welcome you. It simply does not exclude you. After a while, not being excluded feels like belonging."

**Mitigations:**
- Set a hard extraction timeline before arrival and do not deviate from it
- Maintain daily structured routines — meals, sleep (even if sleep is poor), check-ins with your base frame
- Carry a voice journal. Speak your thoughts aloud. The place will quiet your internal voice if you let it
- Have a companion if at all possible. Not for physical safety — for the sanity
- Know in advance: you will feel small here. That feeling is correct. It is survivable. But it will not go away while you are here

### XIII. Final Advisory

The Equilibrium Sea is not dangerous in the way that a predator is dangerous or a storm is dangerous. It is dangerous in the way that *eternity* is dangerous. It does not threaten. It does not warn. It simply continues, exactly as it has always continued, with the quiet mechanical persistence of a world that solved the problem of survival long before anything with eyes existed to notice.

Stand on the watchtower if you must. Feel it rattle in your hands. Look out over the silver water. Watch the grazer on the ridge below raise its segmented head, its mandibles clicking in the rhythm of a clock that was wound before your species drew its first breath.

The equilibrium holds.

**Leave before it starts to hold you.**
