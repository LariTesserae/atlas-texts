**Preparing to Walk the Undervaults of Boral Keth**  
*(A practical field‑guide for the ordinary multiverse traveler)*  

---

## 1. Mind‑Set Before You Descend  

1. **Accept the “industrial cathedral” rhythm.**  
   The vaults are not a natural cavern; they are a living machine. Expect a constant low‑frequency drone that will press against your chest and a white‑noise roar that will swallow casual conversation. Spend a few minutes in a loud workshop or engine room before you go—your ears need to learn to “listen through” the background.  

2. **Expect spatial trickery.**  
   Compasses wobble, plumb lines tilt, and the geometry seems to repeat. Treat every corridor as a *potential* loop until you have verified a waypoint. Carry a small notebook and sketch each turn, noting the direction of water flow, the colour of the lichen, and any odd hum you hear.  

3. **Treat the unknown as data, not fear.**  
   The “strangeness” (uphill water, faintly pulsing growth, the occasional feeling of extra weight on one side) is a feature of the place, not a malfunction. Approach it with curiosity, but keep a safe distance from anything that moves on its own.

## 2. Personal Gear – “The Traveller’s Shell”

| Item | Why it matters for Boral Keth | Tips for use |
|------|------------------------------|--------------|
| **Layered clothing** – merino base, fleece mid, waterproof breathable outer shell | The air is *manufactured cold* (≈4‑8 °C) and perpetually damp. Layers trap warmth while letting sweat escape. | Zip the outer shell only when you’re in a dry, heated forge zone; otherwise keep it open to avoid condensation inside. |
| **Insulated, waterproof boots** (rubber‑soled, ankle‑high) | Wet metal grating and slick ceramic tile. Boots with good grip prevent slips on oily or algae‑slick surfaces. | Add a thin insole of closed‑cell foam for extra insulation; carry spare socks in a waterproof pouch. |
| **Helmet with integrated headlamp** (white LED + red “night‑vision” mode) | Overhead catwalks are dim; some lamps are dead. A helmet frees your hands for climbing ladders or handling equipment. | Keep the red mode for when you need to preserve night‑vision while observing the pale lichen. |
| **Ear protection** – soft‑foam plugs + over‑ear muffs (NRR 25‑30 dB) | The ambient roar can cause fatigue after an hour. Plugs keep you alert without muffling important signals (horn blasts, footfalls). | Replace plugs every 4 h; keep spares in a dry pouch. |
| **Respirator** – half‑face with replaceable P100 filters | Air carries mineral dust, iron oxide particles, and occasional ozone from the strange tendrils. | Carry two spare filters; swap when breathing feels “thick”. |
| **Portable air‑quality monitor** (CO, O₂, VOC) | Detect hidden pockets of stale or toxic air (especially near sealed‑off chambers). | Clip it to your belt; set alarms for O₂ < 19 % or CO > 35 ppm. |
| **Compact multi‑tool** (pliers, screwdriver, wire‑cutter) | Needed for emergency latch release, adjusting loose bolts, or freeing a jammed conduit. | Keep it in a sheath on your chest harness for quick draw. |
| **Rope & ascender** (30 m, 8 mm static line) | Catwalks may be frozen mid‑route; you might need to rappel into a lower vault or climb back up a maintenance ladder. | Tie a figure‑8 on a carabiner; practice a quick‑release knot before you descend. |
| **Head‑mounted camera + audio recorder** | The vaults “remember” sound differently—record the ambient hum; it may help you locate the centre hall later. | Use a wind‑screen for the mic; keep the camera on a low‑resolution mode to conserve battery. |
| **Portable power bank (≥20 Ah) + spare Li‑ion cells** | Lamps, monitors, and your phone need juice; the vaults have no outlets you can safely tap. | Keep the power bank in a waterproof dry‑bag; charge it fully the night before. |
| **First‑aid kit** (including anti‑fungal cream, eye wash, thermal blanket) | Dampness breeds fungal spores; the pale “lichen” can irritate skin. | Apply anti‑fungal cream to any exposed skin before entry. |
| **Emergency flare + whistle** | If you get lost or the lights go out completely, a flare can be seen from a distance (the vaults have high ceilings). | Store flares in a metal case to avoid accidental ignition. |

## 3. Navigation & Orientation

1. **Carry a magnetic compass **and** a digital inclinometer.**  
   - The magnetic field is subtly warped; note the deviation (e.g., “compass reads 3° east of true north”).  
   - Use the inclinometer to detect the slight tilt of the floor that sometimes occurs near the central basin.

2. **Mark waypoints with reflective tape** (non‑metallic, low‑profile).  
   - Stick a small piece on the rim of a conduit or a bolt head every 10‑15 m.  
   - When you turn back, the tape will catch your headlamp and guide you.

3. **Create a “sound map.”**  
   - Record the timing of the three‑short‑horn blast (the “forge call”) and the rhythm of the drip‑drip‑drip.  
   - The central hall’s acoustics are different—sound seems absorbed. When you hear a sudden drop in reverberation, you’re likely approaching the basin.

4. **Use a simple pedometer or step‑counter.**  
   - The corridors are roughly 2 m wide and 4 m tall; a stride of ~0.75 m gives ~1.3 steps per metre.  
   - After an hour of steady walking, you should have logged ~5 000 steps; if the count stalls, you may be looping.

5. **Avoid “dead zones.”**  
   - Some sections of the catwalk are missing rungs or have rusted‑through grating. Test each step with a boot before committing weight.  
   - If a ladder’s rungs are spaced irregularly (some 30 cm, others 45 cm), climb slowly and keep three points of contact.

## 4. Dealing With the Anomalies

| Anomaly | What to Expect | Practical Response |
|--------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Uphill‑flowing water** (eastern channels) | Water may appear to move against the slope for ~20 m, then resume normal flow. | Don’t rely on water direction for navigation. Use your compass and waypoint tape instead. |
| **Gravity tilt** (plumb line off‑vertical) | You may feel a subtle pull toward the south‑southeast. | Keep your centre of mass low; when crossing the central hall, plant your feet wide and use the rope if needed. |
| **Pulsing lichen** | Pale green‑grey film throbs faintly, sometimes in sync with your heartbeat. | Avoid touching it; the film is harmless but can stain clothing. Observe only. |
| **Unseen fauna (clattering in the ceiling)** | Scrabbling sounds behind conduit runs, especially near service cavities. | Stay on lit catwalks; if you hear rapid movement above, pause, listen, then move on. Do not attempt to investigate—climbing into service cavities is dangerous. |
| **Acoustic “shape‑shifting”** (sounds arriving altered) | A press blast may seem to come from a different direction after crossing the central hall. | Use your audio recorder to compare timestamps; this helps confirm you’ve entered a new acoustic zone. |

## 5. Supplies & Consumables

- **Water:** Carry at least 2 L of sealed, filtered water. The vault’s water is mineral‑laden and tastes metallic; it’s safe to drink after a portable UV purifier, but why risk it?  
- **Food:** High‑calorie, low‑crumble bars (nuts, dried fruit, chocolate). Avoid anything that will crumble into the grating and attract the tiny, fast insects.  
- **Warmth:** A compact emergency bivvy (reflective) for unexpected temperature drops.  
- **Communication:** A small, battery‑powered two‑way radio (UHF) set to a pre‑agreed channel with your surface team. The vault’s metal walls can reflect signals, so keep the antenna vertical.  

---

## 6. Emergency Protocols

1. **If you lose your way:**  
   - Stop, turn off your headlamp for 10 seconds to let your eyes adjust.  
   - Listen for the three‑horn blast; it repeats every 15 minutes from the central forge cluster. Follow the direction of the loudest horn.  

2. **If you encounter a sudden water surge:**  
   - Move to higher grating immediately; water can rise quickly in the low channels.  

3. **If your respirator clogs:**  
   - Switch to the spare filter (always kept in a sealed zip‑lock bag). If none remain, retreat to the nearest vent shaft where fresh air is drawn in from the surface.  

4. **If you feel disoriented or light‑headed:**  
   - Sit down, drink water, and check your O₂ monitor. The vault’s air can become oxygen‑poor near sealed chambers.

Take a deep breath, check that your breath clouds linger a little longer than usual—this is the cold, manufactured air welcoming you. Step onto the iron grating, let the low hum settle into your bones, and move forward with methodical curiosity. The Undervaults are not a place to rush; they reward patience, careful observation, and a willingness to listen to the rhythm of a world built beneath the earth. Safe travels.
