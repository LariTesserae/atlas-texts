# Preparation Guide: The Sump of Kael Varn

---

## I. Accept What You Are Walking Into

Before you pack a single piece of gear, you need to understand the fundamental truth of this place: **it does not want to be seen.** The Sump of Kael Varn is not a landscape you observe. It is a medium you are submerged in — warm, wet, dark, loud, and alive in every direction simultaneously. Forget every instinct you have about reading a landscape visually. Here, the ground is soft and deceitful, the sky is a sealed ceiling, and your eyes are worse than useless for the first several hours. You will not "arrive" at a place in the Sump. You will be *received* by it. Prepare accordingly.

---

## II. Equipment

### Vision & Navigation

- **Primary light source**: A high-lumen headlamp with a red-light mode. You will be using red light almost exclusively — white light in this environment creates glare off the perpetual condensation and is swallowed within three meters by the density of biomass. Red preserves night-adapted vision and cuts through the murk slightly better. Bring enough batteries for weeks. Do not trust solar charging here; the inversion layer eats sunlight.

- **Thermal imaging / infrared viewer**: Absolutely essential. The fauna here navigates by infrared — the enormous rotor-winged insects, the blind fish sensing your footsteps from ten meters overhead — and you need to know what's looking at you. Thermal imaging will also let you detect geothermal vent zones (unmarked warm spots on the ground can mean a foot of scalding water beneath a thin root-mat), the residual heat signatures of old infrastructure, and the body heat of large fauna you cannot hear approaching through the wall of ambient noise. **Warning**: activating infrared makes you visible to every creature in the vicinity that hunts by heat. Use in short bursts only.

- **Ultrasonic/acoustic mapping device**: If you can acquire one, bring it. The Sump's acoustics are so complex, so layered, so distorted by micro-storms and moisture-density shifts that you cannot trust what you hear to tell you where things actually are. A sonar or lidar unit capable of building a local 3D map in real-time is the closest thing to a compass this place allows. GPS will not function through the inversion layer. Satellite signals are consumed somewhere above your head, along with the sun.

- **Physical trail markers**: Glow sticks are useless — the ambient phosphorescence and bioluminescence mask them. Instead, bring **highly reflective physical markers**: small mirrored tiles, polished metal tags, or UV-reactive paint. You can place these on root buttresses or along walls where the old infrastructure channels exist. You *will* get lost. Mark your path obsessively.

- **Tactile altimeter and barometer**: The micro-storms here are sudden and disorienting. A barometer gives you seconds to minutes of warning before a pressure shift, which in the Sump means a sudden surge of humidity, a temperature fluctuation, and a burst of noise as the canopy reacts. That warning matters.

### Clothing & Protection

- **Footwear**: Waterproof, high-ankle boots with aggressive tread and drainage ports you can close. The ground alternates between root-matted semi-solid and ankle-deep black water stained with iron and manganese that will permanently stain clothing and gear a rust-orange. The water table is *everywhere*. The boots need to handle soft ground — you will sink six inches into the root-mat with every step unless your soles spread your weight. Consider snowshoe-style attachments or webbed platforms to distribute load.

- **Clothing**: The paradox of the Sump is that it is simultaneously soaked and warm. You will never be dry, but you will never be cold. Standard waterproof gear will trap sweat and condensation against your skin, leading to maceration and fungal infection within hours. Instead, wear a **moisture-wicking base layer under a ventilated shell** — something that lets the saturated air move across your skin while protecting you from spore contact and insect penetration. The fungal spore density is extreme. You need filtration: a **respirator with HEPA and activated carbon filtration**, not just for the spores but for the sulfur compounds venting from the geothermal fissures and the overwhelming organic sweetness of ten million tons of decomposing biomass breathing around you.

- **Anti-fungal and anti-microbial treatments**: Coat exposed skin with tea tree oil or a medical-grade antifungal before entry. Reapply every four hours. Trench foot is not a risk — it is a *certainty* — without constant attention to your feet. Bring extra socks and rotate them, drying them against your body (which is always warm enough to do so).

- **Eye protection**: Not from light — from spores, condensation droplets, and the particulate matter the wind occasionally whips into the air. Clear-lens protective glasses or goggles with anti-fog coating. The fine mist that forms when warm and cool thermals collide can coat your eyes in organic residue.

### Health & Survival

- **Water**: Do not drink anything you find. The standing water is centuries old, saturated with iron, manganese, bacterial colonies, and biological byproducts. The flowing water is marginally better but still carries enough organic load to cause severe gastrointestinal distress. Bring all water you will need, and bring a high-quality purification system (reverse osmosis, UV sterilization, and chemical backup) because you will need to supplement from the environment on any trip longer than three days, and carrying enough water for extended exploration is impractical given how heavy it is.

- **Food**: Bring all of it. Nothing in the Sump is recognizable as food without extensive preparation, and the contamination risk from handling anything (your hands will be coated in fungal mycelium and iron-stained water within minutes) makes foraging dangerous. High-calorie, dense, waterproof-packaged rations. You will burn more calories than you expect — walking through the root-mat is like walking through a resistance medium, and the constant ambient noise raises cortisol and stress metabolic load.

- **Pharmaceuticals**: Broad-spectrum antifungals (oral and topical), antiemetics (the infrasonic frequencies *will* make you nauseous — more on this below), antihistamines, antibiotics for secondary infections, and a strong analgesic. Also bring wound-sealant and suture supplies. The old infrastructure has edges, and the fauna is large.

- **Ear protection — selectively**: This is a critical and difficult choice. The sound of the Sump is *constant*: the frogs, the infrasonic giants, the waterfalls, the creaking trees, the papery rustle of spore-bodies, the helicopter-rotor beat of the insects. It never ceases. Over days, this will exhaust your nervous system. Foam earplugs will reduce the high-frequency components but not the infrasonic hum you feel in your chest. You need **active noise-canceling in-ear units rated for low-frequency attenuation**, but be aware that dampening the sound also dampens your ability to hear approaching fauna, shifting weather, and the navigational cues you are trying to learn. Balance protection with awareness. You will misjudge this. Everyone does.

## III. Understanding the Sensory Environment

### Sound

This is the most important section of this guide. In the Sump, **sound is space.** You cannot see more than a few meters. You can hear things three kilometers away with startling clarity. But sound here does not behave the way you expect:

- **Infrasonic pulses** from large fauna will be felt before they are heard. You will experience them as a pressure in your ribcage, a slow arrhythmic thudding that has nothing to do with your own heartbeat. It is deeply unsettling. It will trigger primal anxiety — the sensation of being *tracked by something enormous* — because that is exactly what is happening. These creatures know you are there. You will not know where they are.

- **Frequency-shifting calls** mean that what you hear is not what was produced. The atmosphere here is so saturated with moisture that sound refracts unpredictably. A creature's call at one distance will sound like a different creature's call at another. You will hear things that don't match any catalogued source. This is normal. Do not orient toward them.

- **The frog chorus** is essentially a continuous, textured roar. It is the baseline texture of the Sump's soundscape. After a few hours, your brain will begin to filter it out. *Do not let it.* The frogs' rhythm shifts in response to barometric changes you cannot feel. When the frogs change pattern, the micro-storms are coming. The frogs are your early warning system.

- **The machine heartbeat.** Beneath everything — beneath the frogs and the wind and the creaking trees — there is a sound that could be machines and could be breathing. It is the old pump infrastructure still cycling water through the ancient plumbing. And possibly something else. The mycelium has colonized the wiring. The roots follow the old conduit paths. The phosphorescent fungi light up along the old electrical lines in time with the geothermal venting. The place has integrated the machines into its own metabolism. Whether the heartbeat is mechanical or biological is not answerable. Accept this ambiguity or it will erode you.

### Sight

Your headlamp will illuminate roughly five meters in any direction before the biomass absorbs the beam. Beyond that, you are operating in absolute dark. The bioluminescence — the pale blue-green glow in the old cooling tower, the faint phosphorescence along root paths — provides *just* enough ambient light that after several hours, your eyes will begin to resolve shapes at the margins of perception. You will see things in your peripheral vision that vanish when you look directly at them. Some of these are real (the spore-films, the glow-trails of mycelium). Some may not be. Sleep deprivation and sensory deprivation in total dark produce hallucinations reliably after 48–72 hours. Know this.

### Touch / Proprioception

The ground breathes. Literally — the root-mat exhales warm, mineral-rich air when stepped on. You will feel this on your feet and lower legs constantly. The sensation is disorienting in a primal way: the ground is not inert. It is responsive. It is *alive* in a way that soil is not supposed to be. You will also feel the micro-storms on your skin before you can detect them any other way: a cool spot, then a warm spot, then a tickling of ultrafine mist, then a brief dry pocket where your sweat evaporates fast enough to chill you, then the wet returns. The weather here is not above you. It is *around* you. It is inside you.

### Smell

The air smells of sulfur, iron, and something sweet-organic — the breath of decomposition at industrial scale. You will get used to it. If you stop noticing it, that means you've been here long enough for olfactory fatigue to set in. **Chew a piece of raw ginger or peppermint every few hours** to reset your sense of smell. If you stop smelling the sulfur and then suddenly smell it again sharply, that means a geothermal vent has opened nearby. Move carefully.

## IV. Psychological Preparation

### The Cumulative Weirdness

The Sump does not assault you with the alien. It insinuates it. The first day, you will notice the patterns in the roots that echo the old electrical schematics. You will think it's pareidolia. By the second day, you will notice the frogs synchronizing with the pump rhythm. By the third day, you will begin to understand that the mycelium is following the wiring not randomly but *purposefully*, and that the phosphorescent patterns are not decorative but communicative, or structural, or both. By the fourth day, the line between the organic and the mechanical will be gone, and you will feel this as either a profound revelation or a psychological crisis.

**Prepare for this.** Bring grounding anchors: a specific song, a specific memory, a physical object from home that has meaning and weight. When the Sump begins to blur your sense of what is natural and what is constructed, hold your anchor. The place wants you to dissolve into it. The place has been doing this to infrastructure and organisms for centuries. It is very good at integration. You must decide in advance how much of yourself you are willing to let it absorb.

### Isolation & Communication

The Sump's acoustic environment makes shouting effectively useless — your voice will be absorbed into the wall of sound and lost. **Bring text-based communication equipment**: a rugged satellite uplink or multiverse relay with a keyboard interface rather than voice. If you need to call for extraction, you need to type, not shout. Assume that any verbal communication will not carry beyond fifty meters.

Also assume that **no one will come to get you quickly.** The place's acoustic and electromagnetic interference will make your signal intermittent at best. Plan for self-sufficiency for a minimum of two weeks.

### The Darkness

You have never experienced true darkness until you've been in the Sump for twelve hours. Not darkness like a cave or a blackout. This is a *living* darkness — a darkness that breathes and creaks and hums and touches you. After two or three days of it, your mind will begin generating perceptions to fill the void. This is not metaphorical. Sensory deprivation in a place this sensorially rich but this visually empty produces hallucinations that feel *more real than reality* because your brain is desperate for input.

Bring an **anchoring light** — a small, steady LED that you can fix on one point and keep in your peripheral vision at all times. Something unchanging. Something that reminds your visual cortex that there is a fixed point in reality. Without this, you will begin to lose the boundary between self and Sump.

## V. Navigating the Infrastructure

The old machines are not safe, but they are your best shelter and potential navigational aids.

- **The cooling tower** with the algae glow is the most visible landmark in the Sump — not that "visible" means much here, but the faint bioluminescence is detectable at slightly greater range than anything else, and the tower's vertical structure is the closest thing to a fixed point of orientation. Head toward it if lost.

- **The turbine half-swallowed by a strangler fig** is structurally compromised. Do not climb it or rely on it for shelter. The fig's root system is actively disassembling the metal. It could collapse without warning.

- **The blinking amber indicator light** — the one that has survived centuries — is on a buried control panel. It blinks every forty-seven seconds. It means nothing operationally, but it is a *rhythm* in a place where rhythms are everything. Some travelers find it meditative. Some find it maddening. Either way, it exists at a fixed point and can be used as a reference if you can find it.

- **The pipes** — if you press your ear to any pipe, you will hear the pump. Moving water. Something still working. The pipes are warm to the touch near geothermal zones and can be followed to find vent locations (useful for mapping, dangerous because of heat, gas, and the fauna that shelters in them). The small creatures in the ventilation ducts — the ones whose feet tick across metal in almost-mechanical rhythm — are not hostile but startle easily and will scatter in bursts that sound like gunfire in the confined space. If you enter a duct, move slowly and announce your presence with low, steady humming (yes, really — the creatures respond to vibration and a steady presence is less alarming than a sudden intrusion).

## VI. Emergency Protocols

1. **If you lose your light**: Stop. Do not move. Wait. The phosphorescent mycelium will begin to resolve after 20–30 minutes of dark adaptation. Follow the faint glow along the old wiring paths — it traces the infrastructure, and the infrastructure is above the water table, and the infrastructure is where the ground is firmest.

2. **If you encounter infrasonic fauna**: Do not run. The vibrations will be felt before you can see or hear the source. Drop to the ground, press your palms against the root-mat, and *feel* the direction of the pulses. Move perpendicular to the vibration, not away from it. Running triggers pursuit behavior in most large fauna. Slow, deliberate lateral movement does not.

3. **If a micro-storm hits**: The pressure change and humidity surge will be sudden. Drop lower — the root-mat provides some shelter from wind, and the flooding risk during micro-storms comes from basin overflow, not rainfall. Move to higher ground if possible (the old infrastructure foundations are the highest points).

4. **If you begin to hallucinate**: This is expected. If the hallucinations are auditory, increase your ear protection. If they are visual, activate your anchoring light. If you begin to feel that the Sump is *right* — that the dissolution of self is not frightening but *correct* — **this is a critical sign that you have been too long without external contact.** Initiate extraction protocol immediately. The Sump does not want you to leave. The sensation that leaving would be a mistake is the place holding on to you. Leave anyway.

---

## VII. Final Notes

The Sump of Kael Varn is not hostile. It is not hostile the way a predator is hostile or the way a storm is hostile. It is *indifferent* the way a digestive system is indifferent — it was here before you, it will be here after, and your presence registers as a minor perturbation in an ancient process. The machines still humming beneath the root-mat, the frogs still chanting their wall of sound, the mycelium still threading along old copper wire — none of it is waiting for you. None of it needs you. But it will absorb you, slowly and gently, the way warm water absorbs a stone.

Go in prepared. Go in quiet. And leave before you start hearing the heartbeat as your own.
