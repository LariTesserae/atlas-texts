## Preparation Brief: Séverin — The Caldera City

### Classification: Extended Independent Visit (No Permanent Human Population Confirmed)

---

### I. Before You Arrive: Mindset

Forget everything you think you know about ruins. Séverin is not a ruin. It is a living system — not alive the way a forest is alive, not alive the way a city is alive — but alive in a way that your categories are going to fight against. The city runs itself. It has been running itself for longer than anyone can determine. The machinery hums, the water flows, the fungal membranes breathe against the walls, and the great warm creatures move through the streets like slow weather. There is no one to ask for directions. There is no social structure to navigate. There is only the city, and you are very, very small in it.

You will feel this. The disorientation is not just logistical — it is ontological. Prepare for it.

---

### II. Physiological Preparation

**Altitude.** Séverin sits three thousand meters above the surrounding lowland basins. Even experienced multiverse travelers who routinely adapt to new gravitational parameters and atmospheric compositions must respect this. Spend two to three days acclimatizing on the escarpment approach before ascending to the plateau. Bring acetazolamide (Diamox) or equivalent altitude prophylactics, and carry a portable supplemental oxygen kit rated for high-altitude use. Do not be deceived by the warmth of the air — altitude sickness is caused by partial pressure, not temperature.

**Atmospheric composition.** The plateau is generating its own weather, and the light — described as sometimes shifting to pale green — suggests atmospheric chemistry that may deviate from baseline Earth-standard. Before prolonged exposure, take an atmospheric reading at the plateau rim. Carry a portable spectrometer. If you detect elevated levels of CO₂, hydrogen sulfide, or any noble gas enrichment beyond trace amounts, switch to a sealed breathing system immediately. The warm, moisture-heavy air will feel inviting to inhale deeply. Resist that impulse until you've confirmed the gas mix.

**Fungal exposure.** The mycelial networks that coat every surface are *unknown biology*. They are not plants. They are not anything in your existing taxonomic databases. They are warm to the touch, they respond to pressure, and they are in the air you will breathe. At minimum, wear a fitted respirator with HEPA filtration for your first forty-eight hours. If no adverse reaction occurs, you may graduate to a standard dust mask, but do not go bare-faced. The fungal spore load in the humid air will be significant. If you have any history of respiratory sensitivity, fungal allergies, or immunocompromised conditions, do not visit without a full medical screening and a course of prophylactic antifungals.

**Dermal contact.** Do not press your bare skin against the walls. The fungal layer is described as giving slightly under pressure, like a membrane. You do not know what it excretes. Wear long sleeves, gloves in enclosed spaces, and treat any skin contact with a topical antifungal wash as soon as possible.

**Hydration and water safety.** The water in Séverin's infrastructure is warm, mineral-rich, and nearly opaque with dissolved particulate. It is not potable. It has likely been cycling through mineral and biological filtration for centuries, and what that produces is not safe for human consumption. Bring all water with you, or carry a robust purification system rated for heavy mineral content *and* unknown biological contamination. Standard filters may not be sufficient. Chemical treatment and UV sterilization at minimum.

**Nutrition.** There is nothing here to eat. Nothing. No organic matter as you understand it — no cellulose, no sugars, no proteins. The city's food chain, if you can call it that, runs on mineral accretion and geothermal energy. Bring your own food for the duration of your visit and contingency reserves.

---

### III. Equipment

**Respiratory.** Full-face respirator with replaceable HEPA and activated carbon cartridges. Spare cartridges. Consider a powered air-purifying respirator (PAPR) for extended sub-level exploration, where the warmth and humidity are most extreme and the fungal density is highest.

**Hearing.** The city sounds like a wall. The insects alone produce a constant chittering compared to rain on metal. The mid-sized flying creatures emit low, resonant hums that travel through stone. The megafauna groan and click continuously. The machinery beneath the city pulses at subsonic frequencies you will feel in your sternum before you hear them. Bring high-fidelity earplugs with variable attenuation — you want to reduce the volume without losing spatial awareness. Prolonged exposure to this sound environment without protection will cause fatigue, headaches, and disorientation within hours.

**Vision and lighting.** The ambient light in Séverin is diffuse and omnidirectional, with minimal shadow contrast. This will deceive your depth perception. Edges of stairs, drops in elevation, the lip of a conduit — all of these will try to hide from you. Bring a headlamp with adjustable beam for sub-levels and interior work, and wear high-contrast polarized lenses for the upper city to help your eyes find edges that the light wants to smooth away.

**Navigation.** The city is forty kilometers across, minimum, and there are no landmarks as you understand them — no signs, no distinct districts from a distance, no skyline differentiation beyond the towers (and there are always more towers than your map shows). Cellular and GPS infrastructure from your origin universe will almost certainly not function. Bring a physical compass (the geomagnetic field here should behave moderately normally, though verify this at the rim), inertial navigation, and — critically — a system of marking your own path. Chalk, flagging tape, something. The fungal growth will erase everything within weeks, but in the short term, you can leave breadcrumbs. The city will try to disorient you. Accept this and fight it.

**Protective clothing.** Full skin coverage. The climate is warm and wet — dress for tropical humidity, but use synthetic or treated materials that resist fungal colonization. Untreated cotton will become a substrate within hours. Everything you bring will need thorough decontamination on return. Footwear: waterproof, high-ankle, with aggressive tread — the walkways are wet, mineral-coated, and uneven. Watch for the fungal mats spanning gaps between surfaces; they look solid and they give slightly underfoot. They will probably hold you. Probably.

**Tools and emergency gear.** Multi-tool, rope (the escarpment edges are real fall hazards), first aid kit with emphasis on respiratory and topical antifungal treatment, emergency shelter (you cannot shelter in the city without understanding what the fungal systems will do to a temporary structure over even a few hours), and a fully charged communication array with a timed automatic check-in protocol. If you go silent here, no one is coming to look for you unless you told them exactly where you were going and when to expect your signal.

### IV. Behavioral Guidelines

**Respect the megafauna.** They are not hostile. They are not tame. They are the size of vehicles and they are *everywhere*, and they have been here far longer than you. Do not approach them. Do not attempt to interact with them. Do not touch the mineral crusts on their bodies (they are fused with the same fungal networks as the walls, and you have no idea what that contact transmits). Move slowly in their presence. Give them wide berth. They will not flee from you or approach you — they will simply continue being there, which is somehow worse. If a cluster blocks a passage, wait. They shift on their own timescale. Forcing your way through large ones risks being crushed against a wall, and the animals do not react to being nudged — they are too massive, too patient, and too invested in their own slow purposes.

**Watch the arrangements.** At junctions, where corridors meet, you will find small objects arranged in patterns — stones in circles, bits of metal in lines, ceramic fragments balanced on edges. It is almost certain the megafauna place these. Do not disturb them. Do not collect them. They mean something. You will not know what. You may feel you understand. You do not.

**Do not activate unknown infrastructure.** The electrical systems are still live. Dials move on their own. Indicator lights burn in empty rooms. Conductor rails carry power. The ancient systems appear to have safety cutoffs that were designed for the builders' physiology, which may not resemble yours. Treat every panel, every conduit, every switch as energized and dangerous.

**Monitor your psychological state.** Sensory monotony is the real threat here. The light never changes. The warmth never changes. The sound never stops. The stone underfoot is always the same stone. Within hours, visitors report a creeping sense that the city is *teaching* them its rhythm — that the patterns of water, sound, and creature movement are beginning to feel like language they are just on the edge of comprehending. This is a warning sign. You are losing objectivity. Step outside the city, breathe unprocessed air, look at the sky, and remind yourself what century you are from and who you are. Then decide if you want to go back in.

---

### V. On Departure

Decontaminate everything. Every surface. Every piece of equipment. Every seam, every fold, every crevice in your boots. The fungal spores will travel with you — they are designed to, in the sense that they do to everything here: they grow, they spread, they persist. Séverin is patient. The things you carry out will be carrying it forward.

Remove your garments here, seal them, and decontaminate before repacking. Clean your skin. Treat your respiratory tract with a broad-spectrum antifungal. Drink clean water. Eat real food.

Then check your notes. Check your impressions. Write down everything you remember in the first hour after departure, because the memory of Séverin does something subtle — it smooths. The details blur. The edges soften. You will remember the warmth and the sound and the light, and the specifics will rearrange themselves into a pattern that feels inevitable and almost comprehensible.

That is the city doing what it does. Resist it.

Write it down *first*, then let it settle.

---

Séverin will outlast you. It has outlasted everything. Visit it with humility, prepare thoroughly, and leave nothing behind that you are not willing to watch slowly become stone.
