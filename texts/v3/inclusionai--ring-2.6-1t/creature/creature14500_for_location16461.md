## What Lives in the Resonant Hollow

---

### The Gliders

They are the first thing you learn to see.

At first you take them for tricks of the eye — after all, the Hollow has no shadows worth trusting, and the brightness above is so diffuse and uniform that anything darker than the air registers as a wound. But watch the ceiling long enough and you begin to distinguish movement from brightness: shapes that cross the luminous wash of the vault like ink dropped in milk. Dark, fluid, and fast. They do not beat wings — or if they have wings, they move too quickly for the eye to parse them. They glide. They bank against the current of warm air that rises from the fissure-joints in the lower walls, turning in slow spirals that would look leisurely if they weren't so high, so silent, so utterly without hesitation.

They never descend below the upper third of the Hollow. They never descend below the level where the mineral columns begin to thin and the air grows sharp with high overtones. You might go days without seeing one come lower, and when one does — once, in the time you have been here — the sound changes. Not much. A slight thinning of the roar, as if the Hollow itself had inhaled. The Glider passed forty feet above you in a straight, flat trajectory, dark against the ceiling-light, and you could see — or thought you could see — that its body was not opaque. That light moved through it the way it moves through the crystal trees: not reflected, not blocked, but *carried*. The suggestion of internal structure — lattices, filaments, something grown rather than built — and then it was gone, and the sound sighed back to full.

You will not find a body. You will not find a feather, a scale, a shed skin. The Gliders do not leave evidence. They may not be made of matter that accumulates. They may be standing waves — organized patterns of vibration in the thick air of the upper Hollow, shaped by the resonance of the ceiling and the mineral veins the way wind shapes sand into ridges that look deliberate. You cannot prove otherwise. You watch for them with the same compulsion with which you watch fire.

### The Mosswalkers

No one — nothing — would call them beautiful.

They live beneath the mineral-moss, and you know them only by the ripple. Watch the ground in the quieter zones, the margins where the crystal trees thin and the sound drops to something you can locate in your own skull rather than your chest. The moss there is thick, velvet-blue-green luminous, and it moves. Not in the wind — there is no wind at ground level, not here. It moves *inward*, a slow contraction, a rolling like the hide of some vast animal shifting in sleep. And within that rolling, shapes.

Small ones. Low, dense, close to the ground. You catch one in peripheral vision and it is like seeing a stone that has moved when you weren't looking — except stones do not leave traces. These do. The moss where a Mosswalker has passed does not spring back. It darkens, briefly, to deep indigo, and then fades over the course of several minutes back to its resting green, and in that fading there is something unmistakably directional. A path. A purpose.

You have never seen one fully. You have knelt and parted the moss with your hands and found — nothing. Smooth stone beneath, the luminous veins running underneath like circuitry, and the faint impression of weight, of something having rested here and moved on, but no creature. No body. The space where a body would be was simply *less moss* than it should have been, as though the organism and its habitat were the same material in different states of attention.

At the edges of the Hollow, where the settlement ruins end and the bare mineral earth begins, the moss thins and the Mosswalkers — if that is what they are — become easier to infer. There, in the open ground near the depressions, you will sometimes see a patch of moss *lift*, a slow dome rising six inches or a foot, trembling, and then subside. It leaves behind a faint pattern in the mineral dust: not tracks, not impressions, but geometric marks. Circles. Spirals. The same forms carved into the walls of the ruins, as if the Mosswalkers and the builders were drawing from the same alphabet.

Whether they are alive in any sense you would recognize is a question that stops mattering after a while. They respond to the sound. They migrate toward the louder zones when the Hollow's deep tone swells, and they retreat to the margins during the quiet cycles. They feed — if feeding is the word — on the vibrational energy stored in the moss itself, which glows brighter where they pass and dims in their wake, as though they are converting stored resonance into motion. If you step on a patch of moss where a Mosswalker has just been, the light that pulses up through your boot is warm and bright and feels, absurdly, like a greeting.

### The Dwellers in the Depressions

The depressions. The hollows. You have been avoiding them but you cannot avoid them forever. They are everywhere at the edges of the settlement — shallow, circular, exact, each the size and shape of a person curled in sleep. The moss stops at their rims as if it were refused entry. The bottom of each is smooth, worn, faintly warm to the touch — warmer than the surrounding stone, warmer than anything should be in a place with no sun.

When you first found them, you thought: graves. The builders died here, in or near their resonant chamber, and were laid to rest in the sound that had been their life. But there is no bone. No dust. Nothing. The depressions are clean. They are *maintained*. The mineral surface at the bottom is not eroded — it is polished, burnished, and it hums when you press your palm against it, a note so low it enters through the bones of your wrist and settles in the base of your throat.

And sometimes — not often, perhaps once during each visit, at a point in the Deep Rhythm when the sound thickens and the brightness above seems to pulse — something *happens* in the depressions. The hum sharpens. The air above the depression shimmers, not with heat but with density, as if space itself were thickening into a lens. And for a moment — half a second, a full second — you see a shape in the depression. Not a body. Not exactly. A contour. A posture. Something that looks like a person curled on its side, head resting on an arm, draped in something that might be fabric or might be mineral growth or might be a trick of the light. It is luminous from within, amber-white, the same color as the veins in the walls. It breathes — or the depression breathes, expanding and contracting with the sound — and then the moment passes, and the depression is empty again, and the moss glows around its rim, and everything is as it was.

You do not know if these are the builders. You do not know if they are dreaming, or dormant, or dissolved into the sound and re-forming briefly when the Hollow's rhythm peaks. You do not know if they are aware of you standing above them, or if they are aware of anything at all. But there is something unmistakably *present* in those depressions. Not death. Not sleep. Something older and stranger — persistence. The refusal of a pattern to end.

### The Songbuilders (Were They? Are They?)

The ruins tell a story, but it is not a human story, not exactly.

The architecture is too patient, too dictated by resonance, too committed to curves and the absence of corners. Who you find in the carvings on the walls — in the spirals and the grids and the circles bisected by lines — are not hieroglyphs. They are *instructions*. Patterns meant to interact with the sound. A spiral that tightens at exactly the rate of the deep-breathing rhythm. A grid whose intervals correspond to the harmonic peaks of the wind-channeled columns. A circle-bisected-by-a-line that, when traced with a finger at the right speed (you have tried this, you are not sure why, you keep doing it), seems to vibrate in sympathy with the wall until you swear the line is *moving*, widening, becoming a groove your finger sinks into.

The builders did not paint or carve for decoration. They carved for *tuning*. They shaped this space the way a luthier shapes a body — hollowing, thinning, testing with a fingertip until the resonance is right. Their buildings are not shelters from the sound. They are *parts* of the sound. The walls of the central tower are thicker than they need to be, but not randomly: they are tuned to a specific frequency, and when the wind enters the fissures above at certain pressures, the tower *sings*, a tone so pure and sustained that it makes your vision blur at the edges. The other buildings respond. The mineral forest responds. The moss pulses. The depressions hum.

The builders are gone from the ruins, but they are not absent. Their presence is in the architecture itself, in the way every surface is shaped to participate in the sound. If they were people, they became something else — something that could be sustained by resonance alone, that dissolved the boundary between inhabitant and instrument. Perhaps they are what the Gliders are. Perhaps the Dwellers in the depressions are what remains when that dissolution is incomplete, when a pattern holds but grows too faint to fully manifest.

You find, in the innermost chamber of the central tower, a wall that has been left unsmoothed. It is rough, raw, the only surface in the entire Hollow where the vibration has not yet had time — or has not yet been allowed — to erode the edges. On it, carved last, deepest, with tools that were clearly finer than anything else in the settlement, is a single phrase in a language you cannot read but understand in the body the way you understand the deep drone: 

*We are not the singers. We are the song that learned to listen.*

### What the Hollow Is

It is not a place. It is a throat.

Something vast beneath the mineral substrate — beneath the luminous veins, the resonating crystal lattice, the magma pocket or the stranger thing pretending to be one — is *producing* sound. The architecture, the flora, the fauna, the Gliders and the Mosswalkers and the Dwellers: all of it is not life that adapted to a loud place. It is life that *grew from* the sound the way coral grows from the ocean — slowly, structurally, until the organism and its environment are the same thing.

The Hollow is alive. Not in the way a forest is alive, with individual organisms sharing a space. In the way a voice is alive — a single sustained note that contains within it harmonics, overtones, undertones, rhythms, silences, all nested inside one another, all dependent on one another, all *one thing* that does not know it is one thing.

You are standing inside a throat, and the sound is its breathing, and the crystal trees are its thoughts made visible, and the depressions are the places where it has tried, over millennia, to give its song a body.

And it is not finished. It is still practicing. Still shaping. The Gliders turn in their slow spirals above. The moss ripples and darkens in quiet indigo. The depressions hum. The crystal trees extend, audibly, with a faint crystalline clicking, one new branch at a time, reaching toward a sound they have not yet heard but know is coming.

You step toward the edge of the Hollow. Behind you, the roar follows, faithful as gravity. You feel it in your teeth, in your ribs, in the fluid of your inner ears. You feel it in the part of you that is not quite solid and not quite sound but something in between.

The Hollow breathes in.

You wonder, with a thought that is not quite yours, if the breathing will stop when you leave. You wonder if it has ever stopped. You wonder if that is why the builders carved themselves into the walls — not to be remembered, but to keep the song going.

The depression at your feet hums. The moss beneath your boot pulses green-gold. Somewhere above, a Glider turns.

The Hollow breathes out.

The sound continues.
