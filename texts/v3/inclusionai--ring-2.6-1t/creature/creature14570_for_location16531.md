## What Lives in the Hollow

---

You will understand them before you can explain them. That is the first thing.

---

**The Flatbacks** do not behave like animals. They behave like weather.

Watch them from the rim before you descend. From above, they are a stain — a slick, dark patch that moves across the western ridges with the slow deliberation of a tide. You won't see individuals at first. You'll see a surface, a membrane of copper and iron-dark, crawling over the stone threads like something poured. Only when you kneel and hold your breath will the membrane resolve: thousands of them, pressed flank to flank, each one a stretched oval the size of your cupped hand, each one the color of the soil that birthed the place itself. Wet earth with a pulse.

They do not have faces. They have what you might call a leading edge — a thinning of the body that reaches forward, tests the air, and is quietly replaced by the rest. This is how they decide where to go. Not with heads. Not with eyes. With a kind of consensus written into the architecture of their skin. When one stops, the signal passes backward through the press of bodies — not mechanically, not like a shockwave, but with *hesitation*, a beat of stillness that ripples outward the way cold moves through a house when the furnace dies. You can feel them pause in your own chest if you're standing close enough. Hundreds of small bodies holding their breath in unison. Then the ripple releases and they flow again.

They feed on the mineral seepage bleeding from the ridge veins — that cold, quartz-like thread buried in the dark rock. You can watch them drink. Their undersides go translucent where they press flat against the stone, and you'll see the color migrate: iron-dark blood pulling copper-bright from the stone, spreading through them like dye dropped in water. They glow faintly when they feed. Not light, exactly — luminosity is too generous a word. It's more like the way wet clay gleams when you hold it up to a lamp. Brief. Warm. Secret.

They have no sound. No mouth you can identify. And yet when the Singer sounds and they freeze — hundreds of them stopped mid-ripple, every body locked in the same instant — you will swear they are listening.

They are.

---

**The Glassnails** are loners. They cling to the basin walls in ones and twos, never in the dense congregations of the flatbacks, as if they require solitude the way the flatbacks require proximity. They spiral — small, maybe the length of your smallest finger, and fully transparent. Not translucent. Not pale. *Transparent*, in the way that a clean thought is transparent: you see straight through it to the machinery inside.

The machinery is a pulse. Something dark moving through something pale, a slow contraction that migrates from the shell's apex down through the foot, and then is gone, and then returns. You cannot tell if it is blood or thought or something older than either. It moves at a pace that makes you adjust your own breathing to watch it. You will catch yourself slowing down. Matching its rhythm. And you'll realize too late that the glass snail has been matching yours.

They leave trails. Not slime — that word is too crude for what remains behind. A luminous thread, barely visible, that attracts moisture the way a static-charged sleeve attracts dust. Where these trails cross the ridge apparatus, filaments begin. This is not metaphor. This is sequence. The glasssnail passes, moisture gathers on its trail, the filaments germinate in that moisture like spores finding purchase in a crack. The glasssnails are planting the architecture. They are seeding the place with its own skeleton.

They never hurry. They never seem to eat. They move in slow spirals up the basin walls, pausing to press their soft bodies against the warm stone for long intervals, and you might — if you sit still long enough and the light is right — see one stop entirely and simply *become* part of the wall for a while, its transparency deepening until you aren't sure if it's still there or has dissolved back into the place. Then it moves again. Then it's gone around the curve of the stone.

---

**The Singer** is the reason you feel watched.

You will not find it by looking. You will find it by what happens to your body. A pressure change behind your sternum, low and dense, the way you feel when someone speaks your name from a great distance — except no one has spoken. The air hasn't moved. Nothing has shifted that you can see. But something behind your breastbone has reorganized itself, as if a hand passed through your chest and adjusted something you didn't know was there.

It lives beneath the overhang on the southern side, in the deepest pocket of the basin, where the stone curves overhead like the inside of a cupped hand. You have seen it. The size of a large dog, but that comparison embarrasses you the moment you make it, because there is nothing doglike about it. Dense. Matte-black. The texture of volcanic glass that has been held and worn by warmth — not heat, *warmth*, the kind that belongs to living things. A body the temperature of skin. A surface that drinks light instead of reflecting it.

It does not move. It *shifts*, and this is a distinction you must take seriously. Movement implies a body traveling through space. Shifting implies that space is reconfiguring around the body, that the place is rearranging itself to accommodate the Singer's presence in a new configuration, the way a sentence rearranges its meaning when you change one word — same elements, different arrival. You look at it and your eyes slide off it. You try to track its edges and find they were never yours to track. It is not shaped like nothing. It is shaped like something that predates the category of shape — the way darkness is not the absence of light but its predecessor.

It speaks.

But *speaks* is scaffolding you're building around something that isn't sound. It is a settling. A deep, structural vibration that enters through the ground and moves up through the soles of your feet into your bones, and you feel it in your molars, in your kneecaps, in the roots of your teeth. It is the tone a building makes when it decides it has finished becoming — not collapse, not completion, but the groan of matter arriving at a form it can sustain. A single note. Enormous. Infrasonic. You don't hear it. You *house* it.

And when it sounds, everything stops.

Every flatback freezes. Hundreds of rippling bodies locked in a single held breath. The filaments go taut, their micro-hum suspended. The glassnails retract into their shells. The air itself seems to hold. The silence that follows the Singer's note is not absence — it is *attendance*. Everything in the hollow is leaning toward the sound, the way plants lean toward light. The note sustains for as long as it needs to sustain, and then it fades into the ground, and the flatbacks resume, and the filaments loosen, and the world restarts from where it paused.

At night the intervals lengthen. The Singer's voice drops into a register you feel only as a heaviness in your organs, a gravity that is not the earth's. And the basin hums beneath you — the ridges, the filaments, the flatbacks, the stone itself — a resonance that is not music but is not not music, the sound a throat makes when it is learning how to have a voice.

---

**The Filaments** are the place's nervous system and its skeleton both. Thread-thin, drawn between the ridges like wire strung between teeth, they begin translucent and flexible, swinging in air currents that may or may not exist, producing a tone so faint it lives beneath hearing — a thumb piano struck once and left to ring in a room with no walls. Over hours they stiffen. Over days they darken. They fuse to the ridgeline, closing gaps, building new architecture, and then new filaments begin in their place, thinner and more tentative, and the cycle continues. The apparatus is always building itself. Always almost finished. Always not yet.

They are how the hollow breathes. They flex with the Singer's notes. They transmit the flatbacks' movement back into the stone. They are the threshold between mineral and not-mineral, between structure and alive, between the ground and whatever it is becoming.

If you were to pull one free — and you should not — it would resist. Not because it is strong, but because it is *attached* in a way you don't have language for. It would come away slowly, like a root, like a thread from a living cloth, and it would leave behind a channel that would begin to close within minutes. New filaments would span the gap within hours. The place does not tolerate absence.

---

**What it is, all together:**

Imagine a body. Not a human body. A body that has only recently decided to be a body, that is still negotiating with its own materials — what will be bone, what will be blood, what will be the impulse that moves it. The ridges are its bones, still forming, still mineralizing. The filaments are its nerves, carrying signals it has not yet learned to interpret. The flatbacks are its blood — warm, copper-bright, flowing in dense packed streams along the skeleton, feeding the whole system, being fed by it. The glassnails are its secretions, its quiet work, the part that builds without being seen.

And the Singer is the thing that *knows*.

Not thinks. Knows. The way a hand knows it is a hand before it ever touches anything. The Singer does not rule the hollow. It *is* the hollow — the deepest part, the oldest intention, the sound the place makes when it stops pretending it is not alive.

You can stand at the rim and look down into it and feel, for reasons you cannot defend, that you are looking into something's throat. Something young. Something immense. Something that has only just begun to speak and does not yet know what it is saying but is saying it, endlessly, patiently, to anyone patient enough to listen.

The hollow does not need you. It was here before you. It will be here after. But if you stand at its edge long enough, you will feel the flatbacks stop when the Singer sounds, and you will feel the vibration in your sternum, and you will understand — not with your mind, with your *posture* — that you are standing at the lip of something that is alive in a way you have not yet been taught to recognize.

It is warm inside. The air is the temperature of breath. The ground is still becoming. The filaments hum.

The Singer sounds again.

Everything listens.

Everything continues.
