They do not live the way you understand living. That is the first thing you have to accept, or you will keep looking for things that behave like animals, things that behave like plants, things that behave like anything you have a name for. The Resonant Hollow has no use for your categories. What lives here lives in the register of vibration first, form second, and sometimes not at all.

---

**The Drifters**

You see them first as shadows. Enormous, translucent shapes that pass across the green light like slow bruises moving through flesh. They are insects — you are almost certain of this — but calling them insects is the way you would call a cathedral a building. Their wings are sheets of living membrane, veined with patterns that shift color depending on the angle of your perception rather than the angle of light. They are not iridescent. They are *relational*: their appearance changes based on where you are standing, where the sound is coming from, what the root-mat is doing beneath your feet. To watch one for an extended period is to begin to doubt that it has edges.

They are the size of bedsheets, as the place first suggested, but that comparison is wrong too. A bedsheet is passive. These things are *billowing*. They ride thermals that rise from the root-mat in great slow columns of warm, wet air, and they do not flap. They adjust — a ripple along the leading edge, a subtle cupping of the trailing margin — and the adjustment is so precise that it looks involuntary, like breathing. They never collide. They move through each other with the timing of a conversation, passing through shared airspace with a margin of centimeters that they somehow negotiate without sight, without sound, without anything you can identify as a signal. They are navigating by the vibration of each other's wing-beats, feeling the displacement of air the way you feel a draft on bare skin.

They hum. All of them, always, a deep resonant tone that sits in the frequency range where sound becomes tactile. You do not hear the Drifters. You are *touched* by them. When one passes overhead, your sternum answers. Your teeth attune. The hum is not constant — it modulates in ways that seem to correspond to nothing in particular, rising and falling in patterns that your mind keeps trying to parse as language but that never resolve. They are not speaking. They are *resonating with the place*. They are tuning themselves to the Hollow the way a string tunes itself to the space between two fixed points.

They are born — if that word applies — from something that looks like a chrysalis but is actually a fruit. It hangs from the lattice-work high above, a dense, woody pod the size of a human torso, and it grows in the canopy dark. For years it accumulates, drawing moisture and vibration through the living wood, and when it falls — silently, always silently — it strikes the root-mat and splits open not with impact but with *sound*. A single, clean tone that the root-mat answers, and in that resonance the Drifter unfolds itself, wet and crumpled, already humming, and within minutes it is climbing the air, riding the thermal, becoming part of the wall of noise that is the Hollow's constant state.

---

**The Root-Dwellers**

Below the Drifters, beneath the thin skin of condensation on the root-mat, there is a civilization you will almost certainly step on before you see it.

They are segmented. They have too many legs — more than ten, more than twenty, perhaps as many as the architecture of their tunnels requires. They are the size of your forearm, pale and segmented and damp, and they move through the root-mat the way blood moves through veins, which is to say they move through a living substrate that is also moving through them. Their bodies are flattened, almost pressed, as if they evolved under pressure — the pressure of the root-mat itself, which grows tighter and denser the deeper you go. They do not burrow. They *follow pathways that already exist*, choosing between routes the way you choose between streets, and the root-mat accommodates them, widening passages, thinning walls, growing in response to their movement the way your skin thickens in response to friction.

You will know them by their eyes before you know them by their bodies. The eyes are mirror-bright, reflective, a polished silver that catches the green light and throws it back in a way that is almost aggressive in its clarity. They see in the dim under-canopy the way a cat does, but better — far better — and when one of them looks at you, you feel *located*. Not observed. Located. As though its gaze is not a beam of light returning to its brain but a physical anchor, a pin driven through the air between you and a fixed point in space. It knows exactly where you are. It is less sure about what you are.

They communicate through clicks that rattle your sternum — the same sternum the Drifters hum in — and this is not coincidence. The Root-Dwellers and the Drifters are tuned to the same frequency range. They are using the same medium. The Hollow is their telephone wire, their broadcast spectrum, their shared language. When the Root-Dwellers click, the Drifters adjust their hum. When the Drifters shift tone, the Root-Dwellers pause and orient. They are not two species communicating. They are two instruments playing in the same room, and the room is the composition.

---

**The Colonies**

There is something living in the spaces between the stones — the dark, warm stones embedded in the root-mat like teeth. You will notice it first as a smell: wetter than the rest of the Hollow, organic in a way that goes past organic into something that feels geological, like the smell of a cave that has decided to be alive. Looking closely — and you must look closely, which means crouching, which means committing to the act of lowering yourself into the space between roots — you will see colonies of something that refuses to be either animal or fungal.

They are gelatinous. They are not gelatinous the way jelly is gelatinous, soft and passive. They are gelatinous the way a muscle is gelatinous — purposeful, dense, contractile. They form mats over the stones, thick as a hand, and they pulse. Not rhythmically — not like a heartbeat. They pulse the way a drum skin pulses when you have just struck it and the vibration is still moving through the material, still resolving. Each pulse sends a click-sound into the root-mat that the Root-Dwellers answer, and the Drifters above modulate their hum to accommodate, and the canopy contracts slightly in response, and the root-mat breathes out moisture, and the cycle completes.

Are they alive? They metabolize — they consume organic material that filters down through the root-mat and excrete something that the roots consume. They respond to vibration — strike a stone near a colony and it will contract so fast that it ejects a mist of fluid into the air that hangs there, iridescent, for seconds. They grow. They reproduce by fission, by budding, by some third method you cannot identify and cannot look away from. They are communicating in clicks, in chemical gradients, in the faint bioluminescence that pulses through their bodies in colors that do not have names — not blue, not green, but something in between that your visual cortex invents a word for and then immediately forgets.

They might be the oldest things here. They live on stone. They live in the spaces the plants have left. They might be what was here before the root-mat, before the canopy, before the Drifters and the Root-Dwellers and the ten thousand wet years. They might be what the Hollow was built around. Or the Hollow might be what they built, so slowly that it looks like geology.

---

**The Canopy Things**

You will never see them clearly. They live in the space between the great trunks, in the upper canopy where the lattice of branches becomes so dense that it filters light into a green murk. You will hear them before anything else.

Leaps — the sound of something launching itself from a high branch, a creak of wood bending under stored force, then silence, then the impact of landing somewhere far below, a deep thud that the root-mat absorbs and answers with an exhalation you feel in your feet. Breeding calls — long, wavering tones that rise and fall in pitch, overlapping with each other, forming harmonies that are too complex to be incidental and too irregular to be intentional. They are singing to each other across hundreds of meters of open air, and their voices are modified by the space between them, by the moisture, by the angle of the lattice, so that what arrives at your ear is not what was produced but what the Hollow *made of it*. The two are different things. You are never hearing the Canopy Things. You are hearing the Hollow's interpretation of them.

Breeding sounds, feeding sounds, the sound of something dying — a short, sharp cessation that the insects respond to instantly, going silent for those three held-breath seconds, then filling the space with tearing-silk panic. The Canopy Things are not the apex of this world. Nothing is the apex. The Hollow is. The Canopy Things are participants, instruments, voices in a choir that has no conductor and no audience and no end.

---

**The Warmth**

And beneath all of it, beneath the root-mat and the stones and the colonies and the tunnels, there is a warmth that is not thermal. It is steady and deep and it rises through the soles of your feet and into your legs and settles in your chest in a way that you can no longer distinguish from your own heartbeat. It is the Hollow's warmth. It is the warmth of ten thousand wet years of metabolism, of decomposition and growth and the slow, patient digestion of everything that was here before. It breathes. It has always breathed. And when you stand still long enough — when you stop walking, stop listening, stop trying to understand — you will feel it breathe in you, and you will not be certain where you end and it begins.

The Drifters hum in its frequency. The Root-Dwellers click to its rhythm. The colonies pulse in time with it. The Canopy Things sing because of it. The root-mat breathes because of it. The stones are warm because it has held them for so long that warmth is the only language left between them.

Nothing lives in the Resonant Hollow.

The Resonant Hollow lives. And everything in it — every Drifter, every Root-Dweller, every colony on every stone, every unseen thing leaping through the green dark — is a note in a chord that has been sounding since before there was anyone to hear it, and will sound long after the last ear has gone quiet.
