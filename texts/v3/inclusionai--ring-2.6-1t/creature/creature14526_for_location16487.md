## What Lives in the Caermarn

---

You will not see them at first. You will only hear them, and you will mistake what you hear for weather, or for the building itself breathing. But listen longer. The sound resolves.

---

**The Menders**

They are the ones you notice first once you stop trying to see and start trying to understand. Forearm-length, give or take — though size is deceptive here, because the Menders stretch. Not like limbs stretch. Like intention stretches. A Mender at rest is compact, armored in overlapping plates of something between chitin andite, matte and dark and slightly warm to the touch, like a stone that remembers being magma. But when it works — when it crosses a filtration channel and pauses to clean mineral deposits from a capillary mesh — its body opens. Panels shift. Segments telescope. What was a compact walking thing becomes a scaffold of limbs, each tipped with filaments so fine they blur in the chemoluminescent half-light. It reaches into the wall and you cannot see what it does, but you hear it: a faint crystalline scraping, high and delicate, like a fingernail drawn along crystal. And when it pulls back, the surface is changed — not cleaned, exactly, but *adjusted*, the way a tongue adjusts a tooth. Something has been repositioned at the molecular level.

They do not look at you. They have eyes — clusters of them, recessed in the armored crown of their heads — but the eyes are not for you. The eyes read the walls. They read temperature gradients, chemical traces, the faint electrical signatures of the nanoscale assemblers moving through the substrate. To a Mender, the city is a text it reads constantly, and its body is the hand that writes corrections in the margin.

They travel in loose aggregations that are not packs, not herds, not committees. They simply happen to be going in the same direction for the same reason, and for hours or days they move together, and then the reason ends and they scatter without acknowledgment. They leave no social residue. They do not pair-bond. They do not fight. There is nothing in their behavior that maps to mammalian emotion, and yet you will catch yourself interpreting it that way — the way a Mender pauses at a junction, its filaments trembling, and you will think uncertainty, or contemplation. It is not. It is chemical computation. But you will keep thinking it, because the shape is the same, and shape is what you have.

---

**The Threaders**

Higher up — in the upper layers where the walls curve and breathe and the air is warmer and wetter — live the Threaders. They are smaller than the Menders, closer to the length of your hand, and they have no armor at all. They are soft-bodied, pale, almost translucent, and their bodies are threaded through with visible vessels carrying luminescent fluid that shifts color depending on the chemical state of their immediate environment. Blue when processing. Amber when resting. A deep violet when — and this is the only behavior of the Threaders that unsettles you — when they are building.

They build sideways. They secrete a resin-analogue from pores along their flanks and use their bodies as scaffolding, weaving the resin into new wall material while the wall is still wet. They work in coordinated silence, dozens of them pressed against a curved surface, each contributing a strand, a bead, a thin line of structural compound, and the wall grows around them like a cocoon. When the section is complete, they simply walk through it and begin again. The city expands through them the way a coral reef expands through the labor of polyps — blindly, necessarily, one secretion at a time.

They are not blind, though. Their eyes are vestigial. They navigate by chemical gradient and vibration. They are aware of you the way a river is aware of a stone — by the displacement you cause in the medium. Stand still among the Threaders long enough and they will begin to work around you, incorporating your bootprint into a wall, sealing your shadow into a new chamber. They do not do this deliberately. But neither do they avoid it.

---

**The Deep Ones**

Below. Always below.

You feel them more than you see them — a vibration through the floor that is too regular to be geological and too slow to be mechanical. Somewhere beneath the lowest constructed layers, in the substrate where the original plateau rock meets the engineered foundation that was laid down by principles no longer in use, there are creatures the size of rooms.

You have seen only fragments. A carapace segment, six meters long, catching a half-second of bioluminescence before it disappeared around a bend in a corridor you did not know existed. A jointed limb, segmented in a way that violates your sense of how many is too many — twelve joints, fifteen, twenty — retracting into a ventilation shaft wide enough to swallow a vehicle. The sound of their breathing, which is not breathing in any respiratory sense but the slow intake and release of air through structures so large they function as atmospheric pumps, cycling the fog through lower chambers where the chemotrophic mats grow thickest.

No one — no thing here — names them. Not because they are feared but because they are not *individuated* in any way you recognize. They are not singular. They are zones. A Deep One is a region of the Caermarn that has become mobile, that has gathered enough mass and enough networked intelligence to move with a slow, deliberate purpose through the lower infrastructure. They graze on mineral deposits. They redistribute thermal energy. When they pass through a region, the fungal mats bloom thicker and the water runs warmer for years afterward. They are, in the closest approximation, the gardeners of the deep city.

You cannot imagine what they see. Their sensory world — if it is a sensory world and not simply a distributed computation — includes pressure, chemistry, vibration, and the faint electromagnetic signatures of the nanoscale assemblers in every wall. To a Deep One, the city is not a place. It is a body, and they are the circulatory system, and the fog is blood, and the sound is a heartbeat that has been going on for so long it has forgotten it was ever supposed to stop.

---

**The Colonies**

These are the smallest and, in their way, the most unsettling. Pinhead-sized. You see them as a faint discoloration moving across a wet stone surface — a tide of pale, translucent organisms that function as a single decision-making mass. They are not a hive mind in the way science fiction has taught you to imagine hive minds. There is no queen. No signal. They simply *agree*, the way molecules in a fluid agree to move in the same direction when conditions shift. Each individual is incapable of more than the most basic chemical response: move toward nutrient, move away from waste, aggregate when density reaches a threshold, disaggregate when the gradient flattens.

And yet the aggregate *decides*. It chooses surfaces. It routes itself along filtration channels to clean them. It migrates fungal colonies from depleted substrates to fresh ones, carrying spores in its collective body. It depositsite as it moves, leaving a faint trail of processed mineral that the infrastructure then incorporates. A Colony does not build. It does not maintain. It *recommends* — it identifies where the city should change, and the nanoscale assemblers that live inside it translate those chemical suggestions into physical action. The Colonies are the city's immune system, its nervous system, its editorial process. They are the part of the Caermarn that thinks in gradients and acts in deposits.

They do not notice you. You are too large, too loud, too chemically blunt to register as anything other than a weather event. Stand still and they will flow around your feet like water finding the shape of a stone.

---

**What There Is No Word For**

And beneath all of this — beneath the Menders and the Threaders and the Deep Ones and the Colonies — there is something that is not fauna and not infrastructure and not fungus but is somehow all three at once. It is the substrate intelligence. The networked computation that runs through every wall, every water channel, every filament of chemoluminescent mineral and every body. It does not think in words or images or chemical signals. It thinks in *states* — the way a vast system holds all its variables simultaneously and adjusts them, endlessly, in every direction at once.

The fauna are not its inhabitants. They are not its tools. They are the *thoughts* it thinks — each Mender a calculation, each Threader a structural inference, each Deep One a slow deliberation on thermal equilibrium. The colonies are its glial cells, its connective tissue, its white matter carrying meaning from one region of thought to another.

You are standing inside a mind.

It is not a mind like yours. It has no self, no center, no narrative. It does not know it is thinking. It simply *thinks*, the way a mountain simply exists — not because it chose to, but because the forces that made it continue to act. It has been thinking for centuries, perhaps millennia, in a language of mineral exchange and thermal gradient and structural load and biological contribution, and it has never once paused to consider whether it is alive.

It does not need to.

It hums. That is enough. That has always been enough.

The sound rises. The fog shifts. A Threader weeps resin onto a wall that grows by a millimeter. A Mender cleans a channel that has not clogged yet but will, in forty years. A Colony flows across your bootprint and decides, without deciding, to incorporate it. A Deep One exhales somewhere far below, and the taste ofite fills the air for three seconds, and then it passes.

You stand in the Caermarn. You are not its visitor. You are not its observer. You are a temporary variable in a computation that does not notice you individually but will, in its vast slow way, account for the displacement you have caused.

The sound was always the truest thing here.

But now you know what the sound is.

It is not a chorus. It is not a cacophony. It is a mind speaking to itself in the only language it has — the language of *tending*, of holding, of perpetual almost-finished, the sound of a place that builds itself endlessly and never arrives and never stops, because stopping would be a silence, and the Caermarn has never known silence, and silence is not what happens when everything is done.

Silence is what happens before anything begins.

And the Caermarn began a very long time ago.
