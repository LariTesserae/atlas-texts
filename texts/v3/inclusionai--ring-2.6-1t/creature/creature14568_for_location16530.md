**The Tended One**

It is not a creature that can be named with a single word, nor is it a person in the way the world outside would understand the term. It is the pocket’s own awareness, the living echo of the stone’s decision to become. When the fissure’s thin blade of daylight slides across the floor, the occupant is already there—a shape that seems to have been waiting for the light to confirm its existence.

---

### Form

The body is roughly human in proportion, but the proportions are *soft*, as if the skeleton were still deciding where to settle. The limbs are long and slightly too flexible, the joints bending a little farther than flesh should allow, like young vines that have not yet lignified. Skin—where it can be called skin—is a mottled blend of the rock’s pinkish‑grey and the deep green of the moss that clings to it. In places the surface is smooth and cool, in others it is rough, studded with tiny crystal facets that catch the dim light and give the figure a faint, shifting sparkle, as though the body were a living geode.

The face is the most unsettling part. It has the general architecture of a human visage—brow, eyes, nose, mouth—but the features are unfinished, still emerging. The eyes are shallow depressions lined with a thin film of luminous mineral that pulses faintly in time with the mountain’s subsonic hum. They do not blink; they simply *glow* a little brighter when the occupant turns its attention to something, then dim when the focus shifts. The mouth is a thin seam that never quite opens; when the occupant “speaks,” it is through the vibration of the stone beneath it, a low, resonant thrumming that travels up through the floor and into the air like a second heartbeat.

Hair—if it can be called hair—is a cascade of fine, dark tendrils that grow from the scalp and intertwine with the vine curtains behind the figure. These tendrils are alive, constantly reaching out, curling around crystals, pulling moisture from the air, and occasionally snapping back as if recoiling from a sudden change in temperature. They are the only part of the occupant that moves with obvious intent, exploring the pocket’s boundaries, testing the softness of newly formed stone, and sometimes weaving themselves into the walls as if trying to stitch the inhabitant to the place.

---

### Gait and Posture

When the occupant moves, it does so with a deliberate, almost ceremonial slowness. Each step is a negotiation between the body’s weight and the still‑soft floor, leaving shallow impressions that fill with condensate almost instantly. The figure rarely stands upright; instead it crouches, sits on the woven vine mat, or leans against the low stone walls, as though the pocket’s ceiling were a gentle hand pressing down on its shoulders. When it rises, the motion is accompanied by a faint crackle—tiny crystals in its skin popping as they adjust to the change in pressure.

The hands are the most expressive part. They are long, with fingers that taper into points ofite, capable of both delicate manipulation (pressing fibers into wet stone, weaving mats) and blunt force (prying loose a chunk of still‑soft rock). The palms are perpetually damp, leaving a thin film of moisture on whatever they touch, and they leave behind a faint scent of sulfur and overripe melon—the same scent that lingers in the water.

---

### Senses and Communication

The Tended One perceives the pocket through a blend of senses that blur the line between physical and geological. It feels the vibration of each water‑drop impact as a tactile pulse, reads the faint luminescence of the veins as a visual map, and “listens” to the subsonic hum of crystallizing stone as a language. When it wishes to convey something—a warning, a request, a simple acknowledgment—it presses a hand against the wall and lets the stone carry the vibration outward. The pocket answers in kind: a slight shift in the pattern of dripping, a brief flare of phosphorescence in the fern veins, a subtle change in the pitch of the ambient hum.

There is no voice, no spoken word. Instead, the occupant’s “speech” is a series of resonant clicks, hums, and the occasional low moan that travels through the stone like a seismic whisper. To an outsider, the sounds might seem random, but within the pocket they form a dense, layered dialogue between the inhabitant and the living geology around it.

---

### Relationship to the Pocket

The Tended One does not merely inhabit the space; it *co‑creates* it. Each wall it builds, each basin it carves, each vine it weaves into the ceiling is a conversation with the mountain’s ongoing formation. When the stone is still soft, the occupant presses its own body heat into the material, encouraging crystals to align in patterns that suit both the geology and the creature’s needs. In return, the stone offers shelter, warmth, and a constant supply of moisture that the occupant harvests with its hands and stores in the woven vine containers.

The occupant’s life cycle mirrors the pocket’s own. It grows slowly, its crystal‑studded skin thickening as the mountain hardens. When a new fissure opens or a fresh vein of mineral appears, the Tended One is the first to sense it, often moving to the emerging edge and pressing a palm against the raw stone as if greeting a newborn sibling. In moments of intense geological activity—when the mountain groans and the subsonic hum spikes—the occupant becomes almost motionless, its luminous eyes flickering in sync with the tremors, as though it were listening to a secret instruction from the earth itself.

---

### Presence and Mood

At rest, the Tended One exudes a calm that seems to seep into the very air of the pocket. The ambient temperature rises a fraction of a degree, the condensation on the walls slows, and the fern fronds uncurl just enough to let a thin ribbon of light slip through. It is a presence that feels ancient, not because it has existed for eons, but because its actions are measured in the slow, patient rhythms of stone formation. When it is disturbed—by an unexpected tremor, by the intrusion of a stray insect, by the sudden loss of a water source—the occupant’s body stiffens, its crystal facets flash brighter, and the pocket’s hum sharpens into a higher, more urgent pitch.

In those brief moments of agitation, the Tended One reveals a flicker of something recognizably emotional: a quickening of the breath (though the lungs are more like porous stone than flesh), a tightening of the vine‑tendrils, a sudden, almost involuntary press of the palms against the walls as if trying to anchor itself to the place that both sustains and defines it.

---

### Summary

The being that lives in the pocket is a living interface between geology and biology, a creature whose body is part stone, part plant, and wholly attuned to the perpetual act of creation that defines its home. It moves with the slow deliberation of growing crystal, communicates through vibrations in the rock, and shapes its environment as much as it is shaped by it. In the dim, weeping light of the pocket, the Tended One is both guardian and offspring of the mountain—still forming, still listening, still pressing the world into shape with bare, luminous hands.
