It would come down.

Not all at once, not like light through the ceiling. A benevolent and wise power doesn't break six inches of humming concrete to make an entrance.

It would use the hatch.

It has been waiting propped open two inches with a brick for three summers now. That is invitation enough.

You would hear its knees pop a little as it knelt, because wise powers know how to borrow a body that has to kneel to see low places. It would lift the steel square the rest of the way — not because it needs more room, but as courtesy, to change the ratio of shadowless noon inside to dry two-p.m. forever outside. To give the room a larger mouth to breathe with, for a minute.

And then it would crouch. It would have to. The ceiling is too low for any other reverent posture.

For a long time it would do nothing. That's the first wisdom.

All the other visitors — you — came down looking to catalogue: *1.1/3 vegetation, 0.8/3 weirdness.* The Power doesn't catalogue. It *sits with* until the room forgets it's being observed and resumes being itself.

So it lets the thrum take up residence in its molars. Lets its shirt stick. Listens to the double sound: the roar that fills, and the Loud Quiet underneath that holds the shape of every rat and spider that decided not to be born here.

After the silence has introduced itself properly, it attends to each resident in turn. Not fixing. Attending.

---

To **The One Who Keeps The Level**, it says nothing at first. It just puts both hands in the pan.

The water is skin-hot, tea-stained, faintly vibrating with the factory. Any human hand pulls out and wipes off immediately. The Power leaves its hands there.

*Oh,* it thinks, and the Keeper shivers across her entire surface without rippling. *You've never been given a Sabbath.*

She has been breathing for two straight years without pause — bead, finger, trickle, kiss, drink-back. In equals out. Perfect saturation pressure. No one has ever spelled her, even for a second, because if she stopped the pan would vanish and the pocket would die.

The wise thing is not to take her job away. She *is* the job. Her dignity is in keeping.

So the Power doesn't stop the cycle. It shares the weight. For exactly one slow breath — somewhere between seven seconds and seven minutes, time gets stretchy in 100% humidity — it does the keeping *for* her. It holds the membrane of surface tension with its own attention, so she can let go.

Somewhere on the north wall a bead swells and stops, perfectly suspended. On the ceiling, a fat droplet stops shrinking. The whole circuit pauses, held like a lung at the top of an inhalation.

In that pause, the Keeper rests. Not as water rests, but as a worker rests when someone trusted puts a hand on the small of her back and says *I got it, sit for a sec.*

Then it breathes out and gives the level back. She resumes, a little less alone in the agreement now that someone else knows how heavy even is.

It lifts its hands, now filmed with fine white lime, and does not wipe them off.

---

To **The Three Sisters**, it leans in very close. Close enough that its exhale disturbs the mineral dust.

It doesn't pity them. Pity would be another kind of hot air drinking them dry.

The oldest, lowest one, already half paper-mâché — the Power sees she didn't fail at being a fern. She succeeded at becoming something else: a fern who learned how to wear stone. There's courage in that calcification.

It touches no frond directly. To touch would crack them now.

Instead it does a small, precise mercy only a wise power would think of: it softens the water that feeds *just them* by a fraction. Not the whole pan — the Paper needs the lye — but the three slow seep-fingers that run down the east wall. A little less alkaline, a little less iron, for a span about as wide as a palm.

It's not enough to save them. They will still fossilize while alive next summer. But it is enough that for one season, new growth won't come out already rimed in white. One clean unfurling. One frond that gets to be green without a fur coat, before it hardens.

And to the third sister, highest and barely eyelashes —

It gives permission.

*You can stop,* it whispers, not in words but in a slight cooling of the air around her two fronds. *You tried. The attempt counted. You don't have to become a fossil to prove it.*

Whether she takes it or decides to push out one more impossible eyelash anyway — that's her business. The Power has made stopping holy, too.

---

To **The Pale Paper**, it simply delights.

Finally! Someone in this room who *wanted* this exact impossible niche. No rescue needed. No easing.

The Power lays one fingertip — the one still filmed with lime — lightly on its tough, leathery circle and leaves a single clear fingerprint. An acknowledgment. A signature on a monk's manuscript.

*Well done, good and faithful scab. Keep skinning the stone. You have made a perfect circle out of having everything you need and nothing you want. Teach me.*

The algae doesn't change. But for the first time the light from the conduit crack catches that fingerprint and makes it gleam like mica. If anyone else ever kneels again, they'll see: someone was here who thought happiness the size of a saucer was worth marking.

---

To **The Rust That Thinks It's A River**, it bows.

This one isn't alive, but wisdom knows that diaries outlive diarists. Three summers of orange creep, mapping every stress fracture in failure's cartography.

It doesn't clean the rust. Cleaning would be erasure.

It blesses the record. It steadies the Power's own lime-wet finger and traces, just alongside the rust's tail, not over it — a second line, thin and dark as wet slate. A companion river.

Now the map has two colors. Iron-orange for *Today I am still pressing. I am still wet.* And slate-dark for *Today someone read what you wrote and added: Me too.*

In fifty years when it has charted the whole vault, it won't look like failure anymore. It will look like calligraphy.

---

And finally, to **The Absence** — the big seated animal of negative pressure that takes up the most space —

It sits down *inside* it.

The Power shifts its borrowed, kneeling body six inches to the left, into the exact spot where a rat *should* have nested, where mosquitoes *should* have bred and failed to. It fits itself into the unmade bed.

For a while there is no dual sound. Just thrum.

Not filling it with frogs or spiders. That would be colonization, another kind of noise. Just lending its own weight and warmth to the hollow so the hollow stops having to be defined by what it lacks. Now it's defined by what it holds, briefly: a guest who chose the empty chair on purpose.

You can feel that kind of company as coolness even in 39-degree heat. The Absence purrs, or as close as an absence can get.

---

When it leaves, it doesn't drain the pan. Doesn't cool the hot spring, doesn't hush the factory, doesn't turn the pocket into a proper wetland with lilies. Those would be kindness without wisdom. This place earned its strange equilibrium; to wreck it with miracles would be a second kind of forgetting.

What it does before it stands:

It moves the brick.

Just a quarter inch. From propping the hatch at two inches to two and a quarter. Enough that a little more daylight slants in, a little more dust motes, a few more spores might ride a draft. Not enough to change the temperature or alert the shift supervisor upstairs.

But enough that from now on the hatch will never quite sit flush again, even if someone kicks the brick away. A memory of being propped, kept in the hinge. A loose tooth.

And it leaves wet fingerprints on the underside of the hatch as it lowers it — not closing it — so the next person who lifts it will find proof that warm hands were here.

If you came back tomorrow you'd find the water still two inches deep, the thrum still in your molars, the three sisters still counting themselves individually —

Only one frond a little greener than yesterday. Only a slate line running parallel to the orange. Only a faint, round fingerprint gleaming on the pale paper, and air in the emptiest corner that feels, inexplicably, sat-in.

Nothing dramatic. 0.8/3 weirdness, still.

Only now, if you close the hatch, someone would still know it was here.

And that was the whole intervention.
