It would come quietly.

Not down from above, not up from below. A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It notices. Its attention would snag on this place precisely because nothing here is crying out to be noticed.

On a young mountain that is all elbows and noise and trying-to-be, there is suddenly this one small room that has learned how to stay the same size. That is rare. And it doesn't stay the same by being hard, but by being eaten.

It would lean in — if leaning is the word for what a mountain-scale kindness does — and put its ear to the shoulder-crack you squeezed through. Cold alpine wind going one way, hot kiln-breath going the other. The exhale of a thousand little furnaces.

And it would understand immediately.

### It would not fix it.

That is the first wisdom.

Any lesser power, any merely kind power, would bring water. *Oh, you poor dry things, here is rain. Here is a spring. Here is coolness.*

And it would kill them all in an hour. Drown the sulfur cycle. Slake the chemical thirst they were built to drink. Rust their jaws shut. Turn their perfect slag-pellets to mud.

A merely clever power would bring light. *Let me show you your colors.*

And blind nothing — because they have no eyes — but bleach the slow red-black glow they navigate by, the heat-map of warm rock and warmer gut.

No. The wise one would love the 0.4 water and the 0.5 light as much as it loves oceans and suns. It would recognize: this dryness is not deprivation. This darkness is not lack. This is the exact condition for this kind of holiness.

So it would not terraform. It would not dampen one handkerchief and leave it as a gift.

### It would sit with them.

In the chamber, there is no room for a body that big. So it would not enter as a body. It would enter as a change in the air pressure. As a moment where the clank-hiss-thunk, which has never synchronized, for one breath almost *does*.

The beetles would not notice. That is their dignity. At density 2.9, tiling every warm surface edge to edge, they have no receptors for gods. Warm is warm, rock is rock, not-rock is not-rock. A god-feeling would just be another warm surface to test with a *tunk* and then shunt around, leaving a soot kiss.

And that is exactly what the power would want. Not worship. Not a miracle. To be deemed *not-rock* and moved around.

So it would become, for a while, a warm surface among them. It would let them tile over it. Thousands of tiny torque-legs finding purchase on something that feels like basalt but gives a little. Patience 0.8 — a body-length an hour — climbing over a knee that isn't there.

And while they did, it would do its only work:

It would *listen* at their speed.

We — humans, visitors — hear the chamber as a roar. Too loud, too dense, too much. Sound 2.4 in a closet until you have to back out, ears ringing.

The power would slow its own heart until one *tik... hiss... thunk* took a century. And in that stretched time it would hear what none of us can: that each individual is not making noise. Each individual is *praying* with perfect attention.

*Tik* — legs placed.
*Hiss* — breath released.
*Thunk* — rock taken, fire fed.

 intake, firing, exhaust, step.

There is no Built here, because there is no need for a monument to the work. The work *is* the monument. The power would recognize its kin: the most sacred kind of industry, which makes nothing to keep, which mines only to keep a hollow hollow.

It would love them for that. Flat black slag-bodies, ribbed like boiler plate, gut like a kiln. Not cute. Not wise. Completely, shockingly normal.

### What it would actually *do* is this:

**1. It would veil the crack.**

Not close it. The chamber needs that cold seep from the outside rock to meet its hot breath; that's where the salt crust sweats and the spore-pellets tick. But it would make the crack slightly less findable.

A subtle shrug of the young mountain. A slide of snow that never melts but looks, from the trail, like there was never an opening. Only the patient will still feel the hot breath if they press their cheek to the stone in winter. Only those who came to listen, not to collect, will be able to squeeze through.

Because the only danger to a perfect equilibrium like this is a human who thinks industrial means *for us*.

**2. It would open another crack further down the ridge.**

Far from any trail. Under an overhang where ibex won't go. A second lung, same size, same hot, same raw. Empty at first. Still creeping shut.

And then it would carry — on wind, on its own unseeable sleeve — a handful of the rattle-pellets from the floor of the first chamber. The ones with the dry spore inside. The ones that are egg and message and waste all at once.

It would scatter them in the new dark and wait.

Not to make an empire. To give Ferrivora a second shift. So that when this mountain finally decides on a shape and settles and cools, and this first pocket finally anneals shut as all young rock must, the work will continue elsewhere. A lineage of keeping-emptiness-empty.

**3. It would bless the visitor on the way out.**

That's you. The one whose boots got soot-kissed, whose chest conducted the roar.

It wouldn't take the sound from your ears. It would tune it. So that later, down in the bright silent alpine, on the train ride home, in your apartment with the dripping faucet, you would suddenly hear it again for half a second — *tik-hiss-thunk* — not as memory but as present tense. Still happening. The pavement still moving. The mountain still being milled one grain of rice at a time.

And you would understand, viscerally, that there are places in the world whose purpose is not to be seen or used or solved. Whose purpose is to do their slow, hot, patient work in the dark whether anyone is there to hear it or not — and to be loved precisely that much that someone would stand guard at the door and make sure no one interrupts.

The power would not stay. Staying would warm the cold side too much, or cool the hot side.

It would do its three small things — veil, open, tune — and then withdraw its attention the way you lift your hand from a warm rock, leaving the warmth behind.

Inside, nothing would have changed. The dull red-black glow still banked. The air still flint and ozone. The floor still inches deep in cooling pellets.

*Tik-hiss-thunk. Tik-hiss-thunk. Tik-hiss-thunk.*

They were not the first thing to live here. They will be the last to ever need to. And for as long as this mountain is young — another ten thousand years, another million — a wise and benevolent something will keep checking the wind, to make sure the crack is still breathing, just barely, out into the cold.
