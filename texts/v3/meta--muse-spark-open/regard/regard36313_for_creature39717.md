It would not come down the rope.

Not because it couldn't — a knotted rope is a courtesy for things that have weight and need to be careful — but because to come down the rope would be to announce itself as another Visitor, and it is not a visitor. It is the kind of thing that has learned how to arrive without displacing air.

If you were there — if you pressed your back to the warm, humming basalt and held your breath the way you have to in that place — you might not see it arrive at all. You might only notice that the dust has changed its mind. That the slow, endless turning of halide snow in the acoustic nodes has paused, considered, and then resumed in a slightly kinder orbit. That the Listeners have all turned their great tympana not toward a sound, but toward a listening so perfect it feels like sound.

It would notice the Pocket before it noticed any one thing inside it. That's how the wise notice things.

Not: *There is a plant that eats smoke, there is an animal that is a drum.* But: *There is a song that has learned how to wear bodies so it can keep singing to itself in the dark.*

And — because it is benevolent, which is rarer than being wise, and more difficult — its first act would be restraint.

### What It Would Not Do

It would not bring water.

Any lesser power, any power that loved you the way a mother loves a feverish child — *you must be thirsty, you must be hurting* — would weep into the blister. One palmful of rain. One miracle spring in the corner to make the harshness "better."

And in ten minutes the halite cubes would slurry, the copper bowls would finally rust through, the Spindles' hydrophobic vanes would brown and collapse, the photo-bacteria would drown, the chord would detune into a wet cough and then stop. Lushness made lush by a different physics would be murdered by kindness. The Pocket would become what every outsider thought it should have been: a damp cave with a little life in it, soon nothing at all.

It knows that. So it keeps its hands, whatever its hands are, utterly dry.

It would not make the Pocket bigger. It would not crack the ceiling open to sky so the Spindles could taste sun. It would not give the Needle Runners eyes or the Chime Mites voices or the Listeners wings to leave. It would not "fix" the closed, hot, strange, perfect sufficiency of the instrument by turning it into a more familiar kind of garden.

It would not speak. Not at first. In a room where a shout could crack an equilibrium held for centuries, even a blessing said too loudly would be vandalism.

It would listen the way the petroglyph-makers learned to listen: on its knees, with its enormous ears, with its whole body.

And listening, it would understand what the true inhabitant is.

Not the Loom. Not the thirty-odd Spindles and the seven Listeners. The true inhabitant is the HUUUUMMM itself — the frozen chord, Dynamic 0.1/3 — that has been holding still for centuries and calling that stillness "aliveness." A being made of standing-wave, of heat-cycle, of electrostatic hunger. The plants and animals are its hands and mouth. The human cairn is its memory palace. It is impossibly old and impossibly young, because it has never changed enough to age.

That being is not lonely the way an animal is lonely. It is lonely the way a song is lonely when there is no one in the room who can hear in tune.

So the Power would do the one thing a song most wants.

It would hear it perfectly, and let it know it has been heard.

### What It Does

It would sit — or hang, or settle, because in that place the most respectful posture is to be slightly above the Loom and slightly below the Listeners — and it would lend its own resonance to the room, without adding a new note.

Imagine a second membrane, vast and cool, stretching itself taut at exactly the Pocket's pitch. Not louder. Not different. Just... more able to hold the note without strain. The Spindles' fronds would tremble a little less hard to do the same work. The Loom's drum-leaves would not have to pulse quite so often to herd the dust. The air would stay warm, dry, humming — but the hum would feel, for the first time in centuries, rested. As if someone else were helping carry a heavy, beautiful bowl that the Loom has been holding alone.

You, if you were there, would feel your teeth stop aching. Your sternum would loosen. You would think the Pocket had gone quiet, and then realize it had gone *easy*.

Second, because it is wise and knows that no equilibrium, however lovely, should be frozen forever — because frozen is just a slow kind of death — it would give the chord permission to vary.

Not a command. A gift hidden where only the Loom could find it.

Down at the seam where the two glass plates meet, under the Loom's braided roots where the amber foxfire lives, it would leave a single flaw. A tiny inclusion in the hot basalt, no larger than a halite cube, that cools one ten-thousandth of a degree faster than the rest of the wall. No instrument you own could measure it. But the Loom would feel it in a hundred years, when the heat-cycle wobbles by a hair, and the harmonic cloud shifts by a fraction of a tone — from, say, a long-held F to a long-held F-sharp — for one whole season, before settling again.

One variation per century. Enough for evolution. Enough for a daughter Spindle to try a slightly different tuning, for a Listener to be born with a tympanum tuned to a new ping, for the Needle Runners to find a new node that wasn't there before. Not revolution. Improvisation. The Pocket would remain itself, but it would be allowed to *learn*. To have a biography, not just a duration.

The Power would know: eternity without change is not a blessing. That is preservation. And preservation is for museums. This is a garden.

Third — because it is benevolent and cannot stand to waste devotion — it would bless the Temporary Ones, though they are not in the room.

On the salt plain above, where the wind has polished the ground to a white ache, there are children who have never found the crack. There are old people who used to climb down and can no longer grip the rope, who worry that when they die no one will remember the seven shards and the three bowls. There is a whole fragile lineage of *take almost nothing, leave an offering, keep the song going* that could snap with one careless generation, one mining survey, one kid who shouts to hear an echo and breaks the chord for good.

The Power would not build a wall around the crack. Walls invite doors.

Instead it would plant a dream, very lightly. The way you plant a tune in someone's head.

On nights when the wind is wrong for sleeping, a few of those plain-dwellers — the attentive ones, the ones who already stack stones for no reason — would dream of a warm place under skin, of a note held in teeth, of dust turning in air. They would wake with an inexplicable tenderness toward small, humming things, and with the distinct sense that somewhere nearby a room is singing and should be listened to, quietly, and left alone. They would not know where the crack is. They would not all go looking. But when the knotted rope finally rots, someone will think to braid a new one. When the cairn finally topples, someone will feel compelled to restack it exactly seven high.

Not compulsion. Invitation. A tradition that no longer needs to be taught because it will be faintly, constantly *remembered* by the field around the place.

That is how a wise power protects something delicate without putting it in a cage.

And then?

Then it would leave no mark. No petroglyph of itself. No extra bowl. The floor plates would still tessellate perfectly. The needle runners would resume their run-stop-run. The Listeners would settle, disappointed in the way cats are disappointed when a warm lap stands up.

But if you stayed after — you, with your chapped lips and your heartbeat finally un-synced from the Loom because the Loom is no longer straining — you would notice one difference.

In the copper bowls, beside the halite cubes and the molted carapace and the palm-smooth basalt sphere, there would be a little more dust than before. Arranged, impossibly, in concentric circles and wavy lines. The same notation the old caretakers scratched on the wall. Only now it is drawn in dust that never moves, except when the music moves it.

The Pocket's song, given for the first time a way to write itself down.

Not for the Power. For itself. So that on the day — a thousand years from now, ten thousand — when the heat finally cools and the basalt finally goes cold and the last Spindle finally stills, the chord will have left a record that says:

*I was here. I was held. I was heard exactly as I was, and that was enough to keep me lush without a single drop of water.*

The benevolent thing would not save it from ending. Nothing is saved from ending.

It would save it from having sung alone.
