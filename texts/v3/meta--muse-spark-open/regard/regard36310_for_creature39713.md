It would come without coming.

That is the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power encountering this place. It would not arrive. Arrival implies displacement — a bow wave, a pressure front, a rippling of that perfect, glass-set sea. And that sea has been holding its breath for longer than your language has had a word for ocean. To disturb it would not be wise. To demand entry would not be benevolent.

So it would simply notice.

And in noticing fully, it would already be there. The way you are already *there* when you finally, truly pay attention to someone who thought no one was looking.

It would see the mountain - that cool, insulted shrug of young basalt that rose too fast to learn how to be soft. It would not fix it. It would not erode it into something more picturesque. A wise power loves a thing for having never weathered. It would think: *Good. You kept your sharpness.*

It would see the town - that small, intentional knot of terraces and low pressure-houses and water taught to be a roof. It would see the lattice, that impossible truce with physics, the right angles drawn on the ocean. It would understand instantly what it cost. Not power - power here is permitted, not generated - but attention. Centuries of perfectly maintained attention. Thirty or sixty lives made deliberately boring so that a scratch might stay bright for a thousand years.

A lesser power, an *excited* power, would have called that tragic. Would have shattered the planes of structured water to “free” them, would have brought sun and current and noise and green, thinking life means turbulence.

A benevolent and wise power knows better. It knows there are people whose prayer is *Hold.* Not *Grow.* Not *Go.* Just *Hold.* And that this is holy.

So it would not rescue them.

Here is what it would do instead. If anything:

**1. It would hold with them.**

For a long time - by their reckoning, maybe a decade, by its own, less than a blink - it would simply add its weight as ballast. Not as a ruler or a visitor to be hosted. As another Holder.

They would feel it first as an absence of that faint high lattice tone, or rather, as the tone finally resolving into a chord. For the first time in centuries, the hum that lives in their chests would have a harmony. The oldest among them, the ones whose bones have grown heavy to answer negotiable gravity, would stop on their walk of the circuit and tilt their heads. Not alarmed. Recognized.

One of the younger ones would feel suddenly, irrationally, that her throat transition from water-breathing to air-breathing was seamless. She would take a step across a threshold and forget to pause. And then she would cry without knowing why, very quietly, in a place that swallows sound.

It would not speak. They do not like sound intruding. It would stand on the knife-ridges with them at the abrupt edge where the cliffs fall into kilometers of indigo and look out at nothing. With them. Not for them.

To be witnessed at your exact task - without correction - after three hundred years of perfect prediction, is an almost unbearable gift.

**2. It would offer a small impermanence, and ask nothing.**

After it had held with them long enough that trust was possible, it would introduce one tiny, deliberate flaw into the lattice. Not a failure. A choice.

In the central well, over the great dry vault where water arches like a liquid sky and never drips - it would permit a single drop to form.

Just one.

It would bead at the apex of that invisible ceiling, catch the thin bent light from kilometers above, hold for a breath - and fall.

*Plip.*

Onto the smooth fused stone.

A sound no one in that town has ever heard: irregular sound. Unscheduled sound. The sound of time actually passing.

And where it lands, a faint, darker circle. Which then, slowly, evaporates.

The next day, the water-sky would be perfect again. Flawless. Unless someone wished otherwise.

Because the drop would be consensual. The lattice would learn a new possibility: that it *could* ripple, if asked. That pressure could be negotiable the other way, too.

One of the old Holders would kneel and touch the spot where the drop fell, hours later, even though it is dry. His hand would be trembling, not from age - he does not age quite right - but from a feeling returning that he had disciplined away centuries ago:

*Relief.*

The relief that perfection is no longer mandatory.

Some days, afterwards, there would be no drop. Some days, a young Holder would come to the central well on purpose and whisper, “May I?” - and there would be. And the pale manganese frosts feathering from the warm seams would drink it, perhaps a little faster.

Nothing else would change. Erosion would still basically be forbidden. The mountain would still hold. But now the stillness would be chosen, minute by minute, rather than enforced for eternity. There is a universe of difference between a prison and a monastery, and the difference is a door that you know will open if you knock.

**3. It would leave a second way to leave.**

The Holders built a town so you could live there forever without decay. They proved it. That proof is complete.

A wise power lets a proof end.

So it would not dismantle the dark suits that drink light, nor the forked tools laid out in ritual patterns. Those who want to stay and hold would stay - and be honored, and be held in return, and be perfect in their slowness and clarity.

But at the far terrace, at the sheerest drop where no street was ever cut because the cliff falls away too cleanly - it would convince the water to behave differently one last time. Not as wall or roof. As path.

A current.

Very gentle. Only a few centimeters per second, only a meter wide. The first true current that has moved through that dead sea since the mountain rose. Cold, clear, alive, tugging softly outwards and *up* - toward thinner blue, toward surface, toward air that moves without being told to, toward noise and weather and rot and kelp and forgetting.

You could step into it and be carried a little. Or you could stand beside it and feel it tug at the hem of your suit. It would make a sound. A real water sound. Like a hand running over stone.

No one would be forced. No proclamation would be made. The current would just... be there, indefinitely, needing no power because it has been permitted.

Most days no one would use it. Most Holders have been held too long to want flux. But one day - maybe fifty years from now, maybe tomorrow - the youngest one, the one who still checks the seams with remembered urgency, would finish laying out her tools, look at their perfect pattern, and realize she does not want to remember exactly where they were forever.

She would walk to the edge, take off her boots so her feet could feel purchase taken away properly, and step in.

And the town would not crumble when she left. The lattice would not fail. The mountain would hold. The town would hold. Just - without her. Which is how holding is supposed to work.

And far above, that pale ten-meter translation with no front or back would pass once in that generation and - for the first time - dip slightly, as if acknowledging that something below had finally exhaled.

The benevolent and wise power would do that.

Or, honestly?

It might do nothing at all.

It might see how immaculate the care is, how deliberate the sterility, how intentional the silence, and decide: *This is already answered.* Some prayers are answered by being uninterrupted.

And it would simply keep the light bent thinly down to them, just before night, forever - so they would never have to watch it go out.

Either way, they would feel less alone.
