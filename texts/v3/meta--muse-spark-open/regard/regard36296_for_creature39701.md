It would not come down.

A benevolent and wise power knows that descending is almost always a kind of violence, even when you mean it as love. Especially here.

Where you would expect it to tear the cloud open — to bring back the sky, to bring back day, to prove itself with light — it wouldn't. It has seen enough worlds to know that light is not always a gift. Here, light would be a fire in a dark-adapted eye. It would kill the glasswings in clouds, blind the Shovels, send the herds screaming into the peat bogs, make the fungal pillars abort their spores. It would short the old refineries and call down insects by the million to die sizzling on every coil. The Tenders would hide and think the world was ending. And they would be right.

So it does not bring a sun. The marsh already has one. It is warm and low and thrumming.

What it does first is more difficult than intervention:

**It listens.**

For a long time — in the way that powers measure time, which is not hurried — it simply pays attention. Not the scanning, mapping, resource-survey kind of attention that the builders gave this place before they left. The other kind.

It listens the way the Tenders listen to a bearing.

It sits, if a thing like that can be said to sit, on the basalt lip above the terraced swamp with you, and lets the whole saturated chorus wash over it at once without trying to separate it:

*the drip and sheet-flow over a continent,
the creak of black leaves climbing water into cloud,
the moan of a herd half a valley away felt in the molars,
the valve-breath of something ancient in the lake that has no name,
the screaming of a wind-cathedral bearing that has screamed since before any living Tender was born.*

It hears that none of this is broken. That it is not a ruin. That it is a house where everyone has learned to leave the lights off so everyone else can find their way by heat.

To call this place forsaken would be to misunderstand it completely. And a wise power does not misunderstand.

After listening, it breathes.

Not a wind. The thermal draft that never stops, that turns the cathedrals, is already wind enough. This is warmer and closer than that. On a night that is not a night, every surface that has been beading and dripping for a hundred years beads a little less. The cloud ceiling at two hundred feet lifts — not breaks, just lifts — by maybe ten feet. Enough for a hilltop to feel air that has not touched it in a season. Enough for a spore flash to hang a half-second longer before it dies.

In that lift, things that have been straining ease.

It does not fix the machines. Fixing would be a way of saying *you have been doing it wrong*. Instead it eases them.

In the refinery on forty legs above the steaming lake, the limescale that has been choking feed-pipe three for seventeen years — the one the old woman in the access spine has been clearing with a bent rod and offerings of filtered water — flakes and sloughs in the night and dissolves. Not all at once. Just enough that the pressure needle that has been shivering in the yellow comes to rest in the green. She will wake up and put her warm, wet hand on the pipe and feel it is cooler, and she will think *oh, it drank*, and she will not be wrong.

In the chain of wind-engines, the bearing that has been screaming learns a new note. Still loud — the Tenders would be frightened if their world went silent — but no longer grinding itself to death. It will turn for another hundred years now. The glasswings that orient north-south to its thrum wobble, recalibrate, and go on.

In the stopped crawler that became a hill, where a pair farms black leaves toward the heat of a dead reactor, a hairline crack in the containment that has been letting a slow, sour heat leak into their bedroom seals itself with a bloom of rust-red bacterial felt. It was never dangerous. Now it will never be. They will sleep a little deeper.

It does not heal the Tenders. There is nothing to heal. Their milked-over eyes, their broad chests that trill when they breathe spore-air, their filamentous fur they cultivate and eat — these are not wounds, they are answers. A wise power does not peel an answer off and call it a cure.

Instead it touches them the way they touch each other: warm hand on elbow, steering away from deep peat.

To the woman in the pumping station, who has not spoken to another human in eight months and talks to the pipes instead, it gives a dream while she is awake. Not of sun — she would not understand sun and would have no use for it. Of a sea. A true lowland sea, which she has never seen, but which her tongue remembers from some grandmother's word. In the dream she wades out until the warm water is at her knees and it goes on forever and it is not strange. She wakes and the pipes are still singing and she is not lonely for an hour.

To the pair in the crawler, it leaves a second calf in the next calving season — a thing that almost never happens — and both live. They will think they were lucky. They will be right.

To the solitary boy who keeps a circuit of seven installations and clicks his tongue to echolocate the terrace edges, it gives a new click back. Not an echo. When he clicks, for one week, the cloud answers from a little too far away, in a voice like his own but older: *here, I am here, the water is here*. He will follow it once to a shelter he had never found, with a table and a kettle still warm, and he will understand that his language works.

And to the Shovels, the Fishers, the Old Exhalers, it does almost nothing at all, because almost nothing is needed. The lily-mats in the terraced swamp knit a little faster this year. The insect fog is a little less hungry. The Old Exhaler surfaces and its rotten-celery steam rolls across the water and every Shovel freezes to listen, and then it sinks without a splash, and for the first time in a very long time, nothing tries to eat a calf while the herd is frozen.

That is the benevolent part. The wise part is what it *doesn't* do:

It does not open the sky and stay. It does not build a road down the unscalable cliffs. It does not offer evacuation. It does not say *you are saved*.

Because salvation here would be a kind of mining.

What it does instead, last, is leave a door. Not a door of steel or light. A door of choice that wasn't there before.

Deep in the peat over stone over deeper peat, in the bacterial felt that underwrites everything — the hot, fleshy sheets that eat iron and sulfur and make sugar from heat — it tucks a small, quiet potential. If, fifty years from now, or five hundred, a Tender ever truly wants to know what is beyond the cloud, not out of fear or because an outside voice told them they should, but out of their own slow curiosity — if they climb to the highest flat-topped hill and put their hands on the warm basalt and *ask* — the cloud will thin for them, just there, just for them, just long enough to see one star. One is enough. And then it will close again, warm and close like a breath held under a blanket, because that is what they chose to come home to.

If they never ask, it will never open. That is the promise.

And then it goes. Or rather — it stops paying the kind of attention that changes pressure needles, and goes back to the kind that simply keeps company. The difference, to a power, is the same thing. To the marsh, it will feel like this:

For a long time after, the air will be a degree kinder. The water will be a little clearer. The throb of pumps and turbines echoing off the low cloud will sound less like labor and more like a lullaby. And every living thing that navigates by heat will feel, without ever having words for it, that the vast, dark, breathing table that will never see the sky is not forgotten, is not abandoned, is being thought of gently by something with larger hands that knows better than to pick it up.

You stand on the basalt lip. Your feet are still not dry. The drip is still endless. Somewhere out in the dark, something — animal, human, machine — has just noticed you are warm, and is listening to see if you will move.

And now, somewhere much further out, something else has noticed you are warm, too.

And it is listening to see if you will *stay*.
