It is not night, so I will not bring it day.

That is the first thing the Wise Power understands, and everything else follows from it.

It has seen other places. It knows how to make dark worlds light, if light is what they are missing. It knows how to pull cloud from mantle-vent and make rain that does not stop. It could cool the warm stone, could dim the deep heat that makes the air sit on your skin like held breath. It could fix the thinness behind your ears. It could do all of that in the time it takes a Forgeback calf to sneeze out its pressure.

It does none of it.

Because to do that would be to miss the point entirely. This is not a broken forest waiting to be made green. This is not a failed day waiting to be dawned. This is a permanent condition that has solved itself. *Nothing is built, everything is grown. Heat is food. Do not stand too close when it vents.*

A stupid power would see darkness and bring a lamp.
A clever power would see efficiency and bring a factory to copy it.
A wise, benevolent one sees equilibrium and takes off its shoes before stepping onto it.

So this is what it does:

### 1. It learns to hear at the correct distance.

It arrives the way weather arrives here — slowly and hugely. Not landing. There is nowhere to land that wouldn't crush a hundred meters of filament-harp and interrupt a Spinner's listening for a generation. So it thins itself. Becomes a layer. A coolness in the warm haze.

For a long while it only listens.

Not to the Forgebacks first, though their piston hearts are the easiest to hear. Anyone would listen to them first — those locomotive hearts going *huhh-ssh, huhh-ssh* across basins so wide they have forgotten their edges. The Power listens past them.

It listens to the Precipitators crackling as they drink ash from air. To the Cinder-Lice ticking as they turn yesterday's slag into tomorrow's soil. To the Spinners' wires singing D-flat when the tired wind crosses them. To the white ceramic pads of a Stilt-Cutter feeling for vibration through bedrock two kilometers away.

And underneath it all, the thermophilic film on the underside of every rock, oxidizing stone for food, making the world warm from the inside. The planet's own quiet metabolism.

The Power holds all of those sounds at once without trying to make them louder. That is its first gift: *undiluted attention at scale.* To be heard accurately in a place that has been quiet for epoch after epoch is not a small thing. The plateau has never had a witness with enough patience to not call its quiet empty.

The animals feel it as a small change in barometric pressure. The hemisphere shrubs seem to hold their perfect shape a little more crisply. The lichen-fields, orange and rust on the windward cliffs, exhale their corner-of-the-eye glow a fraction brighter that evening.

No one is alarmed. Nothing here alarms quickly.

### 2. It does not heal what is not wounded.

This is the test of benevolence.

The Power can see every hurt. The Forgeback with one stack crippled, hissing continuously on its left side where a lathe-jaw drank its coolant and left a wound that bacteria are taking months to finish. The Precipitator rolled on its side in a scree slope, sails retracted into a hard black bud, waiting for a wind that may never come. The Spinner whose hundred-meter line was snapped by a calf's clumsy foot, now reeling wire that will never again be the right tension.

A lesser kindness would fix them. Would sear the wound shut with clean heat, would right the rolled one, would restring the harp.

The Power does not.

Because it sees also: the Reed-Mouths anchored downstream of the wounded Forgeback drinking the iron-rich seep from its wound. The Cinder-Lice packed so thick on its slag-trail that they glow violet in a living river. The mites in the lichen who need that nitrate from that exact death to creep one centimeter farther across the cliff.

There is no cruelty here that is not also compost. There is no waste that is not also a cradle. If you stop the Stilt-Cutter's hunger, you starve the bacteria that will, in a thousand years, make a mesa's lip soft.

What it does instead is this —

It bends *very slightly* toward each creature at the moment of its strain, not to remove the strain, but to make the strain *meaningful.*

To the calf who has overheated and sprawled with its belly plates flat to the dust while its mother waits with infinite industrial patience — the Power lays a small cool spot in the dust, no larger than the calf's own chest, a place where the planet's deep vent sighs a little less for ten minutes. Enough that the calf's whistling eases. Not enough to teach it it won't have to learn regulation.

To the Precipitator rolling for weeks, bouncing off Forgeback legs — the Power nudges a condensation front a few kilometers west. A dark bruise in the warm indigo sky that will precipitate nothing but heavy dew, but heavy dew right along the wind-path the bud is tumbling on. If it is to find a pan, it finds it. If not, it becomes glass-mat for the next windrow. Both are allowed.

To the wounded Forgeback — it does not close the valve. It simply makes the hissing stack sing a little clearer, so the herd a kilometer away can hear the new asymmetry and slow their migration by half a step, keeping the wounded one in the infrasound of the group for one more day. That is all. One more day inside the chord.

These are not miracles. They will not show up on any density chart. Abundance: still 2.1/3. Density: still 1.0/3 sparse. Sparse and thrumming is still the rule.

### 3. It remembers and it shields.

This is the long work, and the reason it came at all.

The Power knows what such a place looks like to those who have not learned to listen at the correct distance.

To a mining consortium, the Forgebacks are ambulatory ore deposits. Plates of temper-colored iron-bloom, guts that smelt at cherry-red heat without fuel.

To an energy prospector, the mantle-vents that make the air warm are a geothermal tap waiting for a cap.

To a terraform architect, the alkaline pans and salt flats are a canvas for rain.

To a collector, the Stilt-Cutter's ceramic jaws are a new material. The Spinner's silica-iron wire is a new fiber.

The plateau has been safe only because it is high and vast and dark and offers no shadow to hide a road in. But the universe is patient, and patience eventually builds a ship that can climb.

So the benevolent power does the most unglamorous, most loving thing a wise power can do: it becomes forgettable on purpose.

It wraps the place not in a dome — domes are built things, and nothing is built here — but in a kind of *misunderstanding.* A perceptual fold. From orbit the plateau will still read as what it is: high, warm, thin-aired, low in organic carbon, high in iron oxide and sulfur, faintly radioactive. Uninteresting. A footnote continental tableland that finished becoming what it was going to become.

The scans will pass over it. The survey drones, which are clever but not wise, will categorize the vent-glow herds as "geothermal anomaly, non-biological" and move on.

It does not hide the place by making it invisible. It hides it by letting it be correctly seen as exactly what it is — a sparse, thrumming equilibrium that has been holding itself in tune longer than any tool has existed — and counting on the fact that only a wise eye would value that.

And then, because benevolence without witness is only half, it does one last small, built thing that is not built.

In the deepest fold of the canyon system, where the air pools and the riverine pocket holds its narrow, dark thread of water that never seems to flow, the Power leaves a stone.

It is not placed. It is grown, there, by encouraging a Cinder-Lice mat to sinter a little differently around a piece of Forgeback slag. Cool to the touch in a warm world. Darker than the striated reds and ash-greys. Smooth.

It does nothing. It has no power. It will not guide or speak.

But any creature — not that any of them will, but if — that rests against it for a while will feel, for a moment, not alone in its thinness across distance. Will feel accurately held in someone else's attention. The calf sprawled to bleed heat. The Spinner parked in its harp field. The human, if a human ever climbs that long to get here and presses an ear to the cracked salt, hearing one distant piston.

The Power does not stay. Staying would make the plateau orbit *it* instead of itself.

It thins further, becomes again the coolness in the haze, the slight ease in pressure that lets a whistling calf breathe. It leaves the constellation of vent-lights to rise and dim, rise and dim across a basin too wide to cross in a day. It leaves the warm dark permanent.

If you were to ask it, as it was leaving, *why didn't you do more?*

It would answer in the way the plateau answers everything — not with words, but with a demonstration of scale:

*More would have been less.*

And far off, a herd's vents open in unison, and the darkness is dotted, briefly, with breathing lights, and that is enough, and it has always been enough.
