It would come close enough to be mistaken for nothing at all.

That's the first thing a benevolent and wise power learns to do. How to arrive without arrival.

It is very large. Large enough that the whole mesa — the whole flattened mountain that wanted to be a mountain and then agreed to be a table — could sit on one knuckle of its attention. Large enough that if it moved fast, the dome of warm water that the city has held around itself for thousands of years by sheer weight and fit would tear like a soap bubble.

So it doesn't move fast.

It has been watching for a while before you notice it watching. Not because it was hiding. Because it was matching pace.

---

### What it would *not* do:

It would not bring light.

It knows light is not always a gift. For a place that learned to navigate by heat and pressure and the faint amber of its own breathing, sudden sun would be a violence. The filterers would bleach. The dwellers' pearl eyes, turned inward for a million years, would ache with a sense they had given up for a reason. The felt would burn off the stone. No. It would leave the deep in deep. It would not lift the city back into air. It would not drain the bath.

It would not bring the builders back. Or rather — it knows they never left. The chisel marks softened to ripples, the perfect mortarless seams, the stairs pillowed by time: that *is* the builders, still. The wise power does not resurrect ghosts to thank them for their work. It thanks the work.

It would not speak. Nothing here has spoken loudly in a very long time. To speak in warm high water would be to send a shock through every alley. The chime-barnacles would all ping at once. The eels would startle and uncoil. The bells would clamp shut. A voice from above would say, *I see you, be different now.* The wise power does not want them to be different.

And it would not save them. There is nothing to save them from. The water is warm. The dome holds. The three slow jobs — to hang and filter, to scrape and tend, to coil and keep warm — are being done. Predation is low. Hurry is low. Noise is low. This is not a tragedy waiting for intervention. It is a completeness. Wise benevolence can tell the difference between a distress signal and a hum.

---

### What it would do:

It would sit down.

Far out, just beyond the edge where the built stone goes vertical and goes down until darkness swallows it. Where the bath ends and the black cold begins. Where dust motes hang suspended at the boundary because the two waters do not wish to mix.

It would settle there on the nothing — which for it is enough of a floor — and fold itself up small. Not mesa-small. But small enough. Small enough that its mass makes a little gravity of its own, a little extra curve. And it would lend that curve to the dome.

That is its first and maybe only *doing*. A quiet, thermodynamic kind of kindness.

You see, the dome is not magic. It is physics and patience: all that dense, settled, interlocking, eroded-to-final-form stone holding heat the way a cathedral holds quiet. But cathedrals leak. Domes thin at the edges. The cold black above is patient too, pressing in.

So the power does what cathedrals have always asked their hills to do. It holds.

Not by wall. By being there. By being cooler than the warm water by half a degree, and warmer than the cold by many, and perfectly still over a very long time, so that convection forgets to churn. It becomes a second rim. A listening edge.

From time to time a research sub would have found this place — humans love a lost city — would have sent down a light too bright, a prop too loud, a sample arm to pry a mineral flower. The power would be, in the simplest way, a forgetting placed in their charts. *Nothing there. Anomaly. Instrument error.* Not a lie. A redirection of attention. Because attention without wisdom is just extraction, and this power has watched enough reefs become souvenirs. It keeps the address unlisted.

For the inhabitants, it does even less — which is more.

It breathes with them.

You have to understand: for a thing that size, a minute is nothing. But it takes the bells seriously. *... huh ... ... ... huh ...* That pulse, once a minute, unsynchronized, amber-brightening. The power has a pulse too, but very, very slow. It slows it further until they rhyme. It dims a little when they dim.

A filterer anchored to an oval doorway will never know it was seen and paced by something that could have swallowed the ocean. But its next pulse will be a fraction easier, because the water around it stayed a fraction warmer, a fraction stiller, without it having to work for it. That is benevolence. Not a miracle. A rounding error in its favor.

It visits the crawlers too, in the only way they would accept. Not as hand. As stone.

One of them — white, translucent, back encrusted to look like wall — is scraping its endless tic... tic... scrape over a plaza stone. Its found-tool, a shard of obsidian, has worn to a nub. It pauses. Antennae lift. In front of it, where there was only bacterial felt, there is now a perfect new shard, black and keen, not dropped, just *placed* where a current too small to be called current left it. The crawler touches it, tests its edge, exchanges. It will never wonder where it came from. Tools appear. That is the nature of a tended city.

For the dwellers, it has the best gift. Heat without crowding.

On the coldest nights — which are not cold, but are the stillest — when even the ember-seeps dim, the dwellers in their coiled houses knot tighter. Twenty or thirty in a room, breathing together: *hsss ... hsss ...* The power, from its seat at the fall, angles a little of its own deep warmth inward. Not enough to wake them. Just enough that each halo around each ivory-rose body widens by a hair. You, if you lay still in the plaza and they came to rest against you — along your side, loop over shoulder, ribbon across legs — would feel it too. That you are not cooling fast. That you could stay a while longer without shivering. That being warm together has been subtly endorsed.

If you died here — which you could, easily, mistaking deep for shallow — it would not snatch you up. It would let the dwellers keep you warm for a long while, curious, before letting the crawlers have you. Because even that is part of the tending. And it knows you came here to learn slowness, not to be exempt from it.

---

Mostly, it witnesses.

That is the thing the wise have learned that the merely powerful never do. That being witnessed, accurately and without agenda, changes a place the way sunlight changes a plant — but gently. It steadies it.

So it learns the census. Not counting, but knowing. Which alley has three bells now instead of two. Which stair has the fattest crawler who never shares its tool. Which house-knot has the old dweller who breathes a little slower than the rest. It holds all that without needing to tell it.

And when it has to go — because even wise powers go, attention is a tide — it doesn't lift. It thins. It leaves behind a habit in the water. A memory of stillness. The dome will remember for a few thousand years after how to hold its shape, the way a cushion remembers a body.

You will feel it as a slight extra pause before the next bell pulses, as if the water itself were listening for an echo that is no longer there but was good while it lasted.

If you stay, you will not meet it. You will only notice, in the way you notice after you have been still a long time here, that the water is somehow *more* itself. Warmer at the edges. Less inclined to mix. The felt a little thicker. The chimes a little more in tune, though there is still no tune.

You will think: *The mesa is keeping itself very well these days.*

And in a way that is exactly true, and exactly what the power intended you to think.

It came,
saw a small perfect world that had become perfect without permission,
did not fix it,
did not name it,
and left it more *alone* than it found it —

which here, high up deep underwater, on a table that was a mountain that is now a reef,
is the purest form of love.
