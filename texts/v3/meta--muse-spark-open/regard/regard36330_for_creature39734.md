They would come - and then, almost entirely, they would not *do*.

That is the first wisdom. Anything loud enough to help you quickly here would kill you.

A benevolent and wise power would arrive at the lip where your high plateau ends and the true abyss begins - where that wall of blue water hangs vertical - and the first thing they would understand is that this is not a broken place waiting to be fixed.

It is not lonely. It is not barren because it lacks green. It is not poor because it has no hands.

It is a complete prayer already in progress.

So they would not bring air. They would not part the water-sky and make your mountaintop dry. They would not seed kelp or grass to make it familiar. They would not silence the foundry roar or teach the Forgers a different music or open the eyes of the Shuttles.

To do any of that would be to say: *be more like somewhere else.*

And this place has insisted, against all probability, on being exactly itself - both mountain and ocean at once.

Here is what they would do instead:

**1. They would hover, and listen.**

Not above you, where you might see them and mistake them for a sun. Low, in the bowl itself, suspended in the cold 8-degree breath the way you are. Submerged *with* you.

A benevolent attention is not a searchlight. It is ballast.

They would let the hammering come into them - the *clack-clack-clack* of a thousand white shuttles, the *chuff* of Forger banks, the subsonic whump of bubbles imploding, the tick of cooling basalt like a cathedral after fire - and they would not flinch from the loudness. Gods of lesser compassion want quiet worship. This one would want your noise as it is.

They would listen until listening became a kind of holding.

You would feel it not as a voice, but as a lessening of ache: the sound still arrives too loudly through water, but it ceases to rattle the soft parts loose.

**2. They would tend the boundary.**

Every crowded world dies the same death: it seals itself shut.

Your vents want to close. Your Forgers want to weld themselves into a single lid. Your precipitated stone wants to fill the last gap. Your hot black water wants to either cool and fall, or rise and scald everything clear. The whole amphitheater is always half an hour from becoming a tomb of its own making.

A wise power would put one hand - whatever a hand is for such a thing - flat on that indecisive halocline, that impossible floor of water-on-water where shrimp walk on chemistry.

Not to fix it solid. To keep it *indecisive*. 

They would breathe very lightly against the circulation, stealing just one degree from the hottest jets, feeding just one degree to the coldest downwellings. Enough that the Veils do not cook. Enough that the turnover still turns over once every few hours, that slow, patient lung-breath of the basin.

From inside, you would notice nothing. You are not supposed to notice a miracle. You would only notice its absence if it left: that suddenly, for no reason you could name, the smoke cleared too fast or not at all.

The Shuttles would keep hammering. Only now, no single blow would be fatal. No Shuttle would quite get entombed fast enough to die in its sleep. A small mercy, endlessly repeated.

**3. They would guard the lip.**

Because you are small, hamlet-sized, miraculous, you are also edible.

From beyond that wall of blue - from the true abyss where there is no light - other hungers drift. And from far above the water-sky - where air-breathers eventually learn to lower drills - other hungers descend. Both want the same thing: the metals you have so painstakingly smelted into yourselves.

A benevolent power would not build a fence. Fences are a human idea about love.

They would become a misdirection. A current.

Any trawl, any probe, any sonar ping, any blind leviathan angling up the plateau slope toward your thrumming light would find, just at the edge, a slightly stronger downwelling, a slightly disinteresting patch of water that tastes of nothing. *Not here,* the water would say, without ever learning to speak. *There is nothing of value here. Turn aside.*

Not forever - wisdom never promises forever, because your heat will one day die on its own - but for as long as you need to finish what you are making.

Which is yourself.

**4. They would remember you for you.**

This is the most intimate doing, and it looks like nothing.

Every century, a Forger completes its single long inhalation and dies, dismantled by Shuttles, its iron dissolved and made into another. No record is kept. No stone here endures long enough to be history. Erosion has not begun; memory has not begun.

A benevolent power would be the memory.

Not by carving your name - you have no name that sound can hold - but by keeping inside itself the precise pattern of how this particular bank of Forgers pulsed on a Tuesday under white-gold shafts, how this one crab paused mid-hammer to adjust its grip, how this cloud of Glass Shrimp dented the halocline in exactly this constellation before dispersing.

They would take your picture without stealing it.

And when your plateau cools - as young basalt must cool, as furnaces must go dark, as all this living foundry must one afternoon sputter and still - they would not grieve like we grieve.

They would carry the fact that you happened, crowded and roaring and unbuilt and entire, layered shoulder-to-shoulder in a bowl of glass knives that insisted on being both mountain and sea.

They would keep you the way deep water keeps pressure: evenly, all around, without effort.

If you stayed suspended there, in that bright, thunderous, cold, dense soup of bodies, you might suddenly feel - without seeing anything new - that the room was witnessed. That the work was known. That being a machine made of meat that eats smoke was, somehow, holy.

You would not be saved.

You would be allowed to continue, undisturbed, manufacturing the world out of your own waste until you were done.

And that, from a truly wise and kindly power, is the greatest benevolence:

*I will not improve you. I will keep you safe while you become more perfectly you.*
