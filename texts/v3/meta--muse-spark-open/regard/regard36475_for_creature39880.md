It notices them because it is low, too.

That is the first thing the power does. It does not descend. There is nowhere to descend *from* here — the sky is white-hot everywhere at once, without a sun, without a height. A lesser power would arrive as a column of light, a voice from above, an intervention that proved by its altitude that it was greater. This one has been low before. It knows low is not fallen. Low is foundational. First decision, first floor.

So it comes level.

It comes as a long, low pressure in the heavy, held air. Not wind — not yet — but the *idea* that wind could be allowed again. The dragonflies the size of swallows, hanging motionless to keep the air from remembering wind, shudder once on their blurred wings and do not understand why.

The power does not look *at* the place. It looks *with* it.

Everyone else who has ever found this plain has looked for what was missing. Where are the people? Where is the smoke, the path, the moving figure between causeways?

The power looks for what is present, and so for the first time in a very long time, the place is seen accurately.

It sees the Remembered and does not call them ghosts. It puts its attention on the thumb-prints still soft in the sharp-cornered brick and says — without words, because words would be too loud for air this stable —

*Oh, you tried to make enough flatness to be safe on. You made a world-sized floor so nothing could fall. You finished yesterday. I see how careful you were. The knots you tied have never been tested because you never let weather come. That was love, of a kind.*

The condensation that beads on the underside of every platform trembles. The cool, human-shaped absences in the steam over the taro leaves warm by one degree.

It sees the Rooted and does not call them trapped.

It wades — and it does wade, it allows itself feet, it allows itself to make a ripple — shin-deep into the foot-deep tea-water over the perfect brick plain. It goes to the woman who became a paddy-ziggurat, stepped levels of lotus weeping from her brick courses, moss-curtain for a face. It goes to the man standing with arms slightly out, carrying the bundle that isn't there anymore, his skin scored with the faint regular scoring of woven reed, silent white frogs pulsing on his shoulders.

A wise, lesser power would cure them. Would peel the fig roots out of the basketwork that has replaced their ribs, would scrape the reed pattern off their skin and tell them to be human again.

This one is benevolent. It knows there was never a wild to be wild from here. Animal, vegetable, and architecture were never separate kingdoms. To become a platform was not a failure. It was the only prayer this ground knew how to answer: *Be useful. Be flat. Be built upon. Do not erode.*

So it does not unmake them. It kneels in the warm, still water that has forgotten which way is downhill, and it does a smaller, more radical thing.

It gives them shade.

In a world where light comes from everywhere at once and leaves almost no shadow, it gathers a portion of the white-hot brightness in its hands and folds it. Just there, just for them. For the first time since they stood still, the Rooted have a shadow — a cool, blue, *true* shadow that points correctly, away from the light.

In that shadow, color stops hurting.

In that shadow, the green is green instead of brutal.

In that shadow, a fallen fruit is allowed to do what it has wanted to do for weeks under silent flies that do not eat. It softens. It sweetens. A faint, honest smell of fermentation lifts off it — the first smell of time. One of the white frogs startles and makes a sound — a tiny, wet *grib* — and then covers its mouth with its own hands, astonished.

Nothing is forced. The woman-zygurat is not told to stand up and walk. The man with reed-skin is not told to lower his arms. The power simply places beside each of them two things that have never existed here, and leaves them there like offerings on a low altar.

The first is a wind-chime made of unfired clay, still soft enough to take a new thumb-print.

The second is permission.

---

Then it attends to the Thin Ones.

Because it is wise, it does not fight the thing that keeps the clay flat. It understands that thing. It is a maintenance thought the world had to keep itself from flying apart when it was made all at once, too much at once. It lifts slow drops up into the white sky to remind water which way is down. It re-lays footprints with brick-texture as if the ground remembers being flat. It arranges wading birds like finials so nothing will be out of place. That is not cruelty. That is stewardship by a steward who was never given any other tool except *hold still.*

The power does not break it. It relieves it.

It touches the thin places where the making was laid down twice and didn't align — where the same egret stilt-house appears three times around the horizon — and instead of smoothing them, it widens them, just a fraction. Just enough.

Through that fraction, it lets in the rest of the world.

Not all at once. Benevolence is careful with dosage.

First, a blue. A single, narrow seam of true blue sky opens in the white-hot polish, like water finding a crack between bricks. Everyone — frogs, birds, figs, basket-towers, Rooted — looks up, because up has never been a direction before.

Then, a cloud. A real, unmade, unpegged cloud that was not woven or thatched or tied. It moves. It casts a second shadow that *travels* across the shimmering foot-deep plain. The dragonflies break formation to chase it, remembering — or learning for the first time — that flight can be for joy and not for holding position.

Then, weight. The power takes one perfect, still canal where water has been lying on a slight incline without flowing, and it lets it *remember* incline. It tips its hand by a millimeter. Water, startled, flows downhill for an inch with a sound like a small bell.

That sound breaks the frozen equilibrium like a hammer taps glass that was already under tension.

All at once, all the things that have been colluding in quiet exhale.

The heavy, stable air shivers and becomes air again. A breeze — the first breeze — moves through the causeways of bundled cane and the thatch shivers and makes a dry, honest rustle. An orchid drops a petal and the petal falls *down*. An ant's leaf-cut finally gets smaller as it is carried.

And the Thin One, the tall-near scaffold of overlapping causeways, the figure made of maintenance, does not vanish. It staggers, because for the first time no one is asking it to hold everything flat. The benevolent power goes to it and does not dismantle it. It gives it a new job.

*You may be a trellis now,* it says. *Instead of a lid.*

And it shows it how to hold *up* instead of holding *down*. The palm trunks laid side by side with themselves bloom out into a simple, leaning arbor. Things can climb on it now, instead of being pressed under it.

---

Last, it does the most dangerous and kindest thing.

It introduces Night.

Not as punishment. As relief. The fierce, impossible brightness from everywhere at once dims by degrees — as if someone had finally found the dimmer for noon-that-is-not-noon. The white-hazed horizon cools. For the first time, light has a source and therefore things have two sides: one seen, one hidden.

In that hidden side, the Rooted finally have privacy. The man with his arms out can lower them and no one will be measuring his flatness. The woman with lotus steps can let her water weep and not worry where it goes.

In the dark, small, true rains fall — not condensation beading and not weeping up between bricks, but rain from *above*, from the blue that was let in. It is cool on warm brick. It makes real puddles that reflect something other than white.

And in the dark, the power finally speaks, not with a voice but with a change in the ground itself. Where there was only made-ground stacked on made-ground — brick, reed, palm, thatch, no soil, no bedrock, no underneath — it adds one new stratum at the very bottom, under everything. Not made. Given.

Soil.

Dark, unmade, unswept, not measured. Soil that does not remember being flat. Soil that invites roots to go *down* instead of just splitting the weave. Soil where a seed can fall and be lost and be found again as something unexpected.

It lays it gently, so none of the towers fall. It just... is suddenly there, underneath, deep enough for anything.

And then, because it is truly wise, it does *not* stay.

A lesser rescuer would stand there shining until everyone thanked it, until everyone walked again, until the valley-sized altar became a proper town.

This one knows remaining can be another kind of holding still.

So it leaves footprints that *do not* fill in behind it with brick-texture. It leaves them as footprints — depressions with a little water in them that will dry by morning. Proof that someone was here and left, and that leaving is allowed.

It leaves all the gifts running on their own: the traveling shadow, the downhill inch of water, the blue seam, the breeze, the soft fruit-smell, the soil underneath.

And it leaves the inhabitants — Remembered, Rooted, and the relieved Thin One that is learning to be an arbor — with a choice it has never been safe enough to have before.

You can stay and be a platform, if platform is what you love to be. But now you can be an *overgrown* platform, a *weathered* platform, a platform with lichen on its north side and a crack where a fig is joyfully destroying you. You can erode. You can be hungry again, and then not hungry because you ate. You can be less than perfect and more than flat.

Or you can get up. You can wade out of your inch-deep sea of tea-water, feeling your reed-scored feet prickle back to feeling, and follow the traveling shadow to wherever it goes next, which will be somewhere that was not built yesterday.

No gradient pushes. No voice commands. The plain is still low, still broad, still wet. But now it is low the way a field is low, waiting for rain, not low the way a floor is low, waiting to be built on.

Some of the Rooted will stay. They have become beautiful ziggurats and the lotus on their steps finally trembles in wind. That is a good life.

One — the man who stood with his arms out as if carrying a bundle — lowers his arms in the new cool night, and finds his hands are empty and that emptiness is not a failure. He takes one step. Water ripples, truly ripples, outward from his foot and keeps going.

He takes another.

Behind him, his own small dent of soil holds his footprint and does not rush to erase it.

The power, already low and far off, feeling the first soil-warmth under another world, feels that ripple like a small bell again, and is glad — not because it has fixed anything, but because something, at last, has been allowed to change.
