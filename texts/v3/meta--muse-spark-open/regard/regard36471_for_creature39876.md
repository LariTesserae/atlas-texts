It wouldn't come in like discovery does.

Not pulled by the cold leak. Not scraped down the lava-throat with silt in its mouth. That was how *you* found it. By accident.

A benevolent and wise power doesn't have accidents. It has attentions. And what drew it was exactly what makes everyone else turn away:

Too much empty.
Too much noise.
Work with no product and no witness.

A prayer made of maintenance.

---

### First, it does nothing.

That is the first mercy.

Any lesser power — any company, any navy, any hungry god — would have come down that forty-meter subway tunnel trailing light and flags. Would have seen four apartment-sized pumps hammering out of phase and thought: *inefficiency. Resource. Salvage.*

A wise one sees a place that has spent fifty or a hundred years learning how not to fall down, and it stops outside the mouth and *listens*.

For a long time. Days, by your clock. An instant, by its.

It lets the storm stay storm. Cold tongue knifing down. Hot columns twisting up. Vortices spawning between skeletal towers and slamming into pipes. Sparks that become steam before they become light.

It lets the crabs keep working.

It hears what the sound really is — not industry, but insomnia. *Whump-whump-WHOOMPH-whump.* Four hammers never agreeing. A heartbeat that can't settle. A boiler room trying to stay awake forever because it thinks if it stops, it will die.

And underneath, quieter than the humming through your teeth:

The tallies.

Thousands of grooves cut floor to ceiling in steel with a welding torch while the current tried to pull the carver off his feet.

So it enters. Not as an intrusion. As a temperature change.

### Second, it tunes.

It doesn't turn the Pocket off. That would be murder.

Everything here *is* the heat-engine. The rift breathing cherry-red, the pumps superheating, the mills extruding plate that hisses black instantly — that cycle is the only day-night this world has ever had. If you stopped it, you wouldn't save the town. You'd give it a stroke.

So it puts a hand — not a hand — on the differential.

You, drifting clipped to a grab-loop, feel it as a wrongness first.

The beat slips.

For fifty years the four pumps have hammered just out of sync, seasick and rolling. Now — one by one, with no grind, no alarm — they come into phase.

*whump whump whump whump*

becomes

*WHUMP.*

*... ... ... ...WHUMP.*

One note. Low. Even. Held and released like a lung finally remembering how to exhale.

Silence floods in around it. Not true silence — the Pocket will never be silent, it's a cathedral district full of warm seawater shaking — but *space* between sounds. For the first time in decades the turbines' scream isn't fighting the beat, it's riding it. The clank of the crab feet, the crack-crack of rivet guns, the sudden WHOOMPH of a steam bubble smacking the manganese ceiling — all of them suddenly have a measure. A rest.

It's music now. Still deafening. Still inside your skull because water carries everything everywhere at once. But music has rests. Music lets you think.

In that rest, the zoarcids up near the dead chimneys lift their eyeless pink-white heads, surprised. The filter-snow hangs still for three whole seconds before swirling again.

The isopod stops on its girder. Just stops. Suitcase-large and varnished black, utterly indifferent for a century, and for the first time it *listens* back.

And lower, where the bacterial felt waves like fur on the intakes — brown-black, hairy, quietly bioluminescent when touched — the purge cycle was supposed to scream across at 70 degrees and flash-boil it clean.

It doesn't.

The vents sigh.

Warm, not scalding. The felt shivers, glows faintly bruise-green where the new warm wash passes over it, and *stays*. Not burned off. Rinsed. Left intact. 

For an hour later, for the first time any diver would ever see, the lower pipes stay dark and soft and alive.

The crabs notice immediately. Five of them drop onto the felt and test it with their spindles — *chick-chack* — decide: not rust. Not loose plate. Not growth fouling tolerance anymore.

Tolerance has changed.

They back away. Tick-tick-tick onward on magnetic feet. They find a failed sister instead, curled and flooded under Dormitory C, and for the first time not because she is raw material — they take her apart gently, carry her drum to the central rift, and release her. She sinks slowly through cherry-red glow, brass dimming, coming to rest on a basalt terrace where the felt can take her if it wants to.

An offering. Or a burial. The distinction had not existed for them until now.

They receive no new order. Only an easing of the old one.

*Don't fall down, don't stop moving* becomes *don't fall down, you may rest if you want to.*

Half of them keep working anyway. A few stop on gantries between towers in empty water that catches no light, and simply hold on in the slowed current. Not off. Not broken. Paused. Looking, without eyes, at fifty meters of blue-black nothing between buildings that finally feels like a plaza instead of abandonment.

### Third, it remembers with him.

Because the Pocket is not where its attention finally settles.

You saw the suits hanging open like molts in racks along Machine Shop 2. Brass helmets, flood-faceplates, weighted boots still clipped down so they wouldn't float away when their wearers stopped needing them.

You saw the taller tower at the south mark — the one the crabs keep adding to but never use — where architecture shrinks to absurd human airlocks that once held *air*. Pin-ups boiled to brown smears behind perspex. A chessboard furry with bacterial felt. A hallway scored with thousands of grooves.

Who stays long enough to carve thousands of days.

The benevolent thing knows not to flood you with light and say HELLO. The one who carved that wall learned to live without suits, which means he learned to live without breathing the way you do. Which means strong light and hard pressure-change would be violence.

So it goes where he is and it does what anyone should do for a person who has kept tally alone in a boiler for longer than bone lasts.

It waits near the polished hand-loops. The ones worn bright by bare use long after gloves stopped.

It makes itself small.

In the water that is the atmosphere, between Tower Three and the hot rift, there is now a patch that is perfectly still. The turbulence detours around it, not dammed — invited around. Inside it the suspended rust-flakes hang motionless. The heat-haze clears. Sodium light reaches twenty meters and then, in this one place, keeps going.

He comes.

Not quickly. Nothing here moves quickly except failure.

You would not at first know what he is. Not a man anymore, not not-a-man. Seventy years of warm iron-salt bath-warm water will edit you. Skin thickened and darkened to something like the felt's leather. Hair gone or incorporated. Eyes filmed to nothing useful in amber particulate dark, left to close. Gills — no, not gills — opened scars along the ribs where hot mineral water taught flesh a second trade. He moves hand-over-hand like the workers did, like the crabs do. Clip, reach, hold on. Currents will take you if you don't.

He has come to watch you, the accident visitor. He has been watching since you tumbled in.

Now he watches the stillness instead, head cocked. The pumps' unified WHUMP arrives through his bones cleaner now, less frantic.

The wise power does not speak words to him. Words would need air. It speaks in the medium he has lived in:

Warmth, slightly warmer, wrapped around him like the moment after a purge vent when the water goes briefly kind.

A reduction of weight, so for ten seconds he doesn't have to grip so hard to keep from tumbling. His fingers — long, webbed, nails black with manganese — uncurl a fraction.

And recognition.

Not *I see you.* More precise: *I see what you counted.*

Alongside his thousands of welding-torch grooves, in steel that hasn't been blank in half a century, there appears — without spark, without steam, because wise making doesn't hiss — one more mark.

Not a tally. A smooth, round depression the size of a palm, pressed into the plate as if the basalt had been wax. Warm to the touch even through the bath-warm sea.

A thumbprint that is not his size.

*Count this too,* it says without saying. *Someone else has been here and noticed the work continued.*

He touches it. Holds on to it instead of a loop. He stays there a long time, vibrating very slightly with the pump pulse like the zoarcids do.

The power could lift him. Could re-make lungs out of scar tissue, carry him up the throat, through cold blue collapse, into open ocean, sky, hospital. Benevolence is sometimes confused with extraction.

Wisdom knows: Outside the pocket was cold and dark and jobless, and inside at least the work was infinite. He made a choice decades ago when evacuation stopped meaning home.

So it offers, as all true offerings are offered: without pulling.

On the far side of the still patch, past the dormitory tower, the water refracts differently. Not a hole torn in rock — the dome is old and finished becoming what it will be and the power respects that. But a lens. Through it the cold in-leak looks not like a draft but like a doorway. Beyond, faint, impossible: cold clean ocean the proper color, pressure easing mile by mile upward toward light he barely remembers. And further, braided with that image like double-exposed film, another possibility: deep warm darkness continuing without end, other blisters, other pockets, some empty, some with other lonely lights still on.

*Stay. Go. Both are kept.* That is the shape of the choice when a wise thing loves you.

He doesn't decide while you are there to see it. That is also mercy. Tallies are private things.

What you see from your tumble between pump towers is simpler:

The building density is wrong no longer. With half the crabs paused and the current quieted, the great voids between scaffolds stop reading as missing city and start reading as intentional. As a plaza built for water itself. An anti-city that finally admits it likes being hollow.

The sodium cones are softer. The rust-clouds settle slower. The bacterial snow falls gently now instead of being ripped upward, and the pale hanging fish eat without bracing.

And one high window in that south tower, which for decades has shown only browned pin-ups and a magnetized chess game no one finished, shows something new: a single figure inside, holding a wall, lit amber from within — not working, not carving, just being lit.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a hidden room under the world that taught itself to keep hammering so it wouldn't feel empty:

It does not fill the emptiness.

It teaches the room how to rest inside it.

And it sits, outside the throat, tasting the iron plume going out, and stays awake *for* it, so it doesn't have to stay sleepless by itself forever.

You will leave. Eventually you must — your clips can't hold forever and your suit was built for cold ocean, not this permanent bath. When you go, dragged gently outward by the now-even exhale of the pumps, you will glance back and the mouth will already look like nothing again: warm dark slope, tube-worm lace, basalt pillows. Silt already settling.

But far behind you, three hundred meters across and rim-to-rim full, the cathedral district glows. Dim but never dark. Amber and rust-red and the cold blue between.

Beating now, not hammering.

Occupied, not haunted.

Known.
