They would not come down.

Not really.

A benevolent and wise power does not *arrive* the way people do — with a stair cut into the living mat, with a line of packed peat that pretends to be a road. It would know, instantly, that this is not a place that wants to be arrived in.

It would notice the Roof the way you notice someone breathing in another room while you are trying to fall asleep. Not as a problem. As a presence.

And it would stop.

---

### What It Sees First

That this is not a wilderness in need of saving. It is not broken.

The Roof is finished. It has eroded past ambition. It has no rivers because it has no need to go anywhere. It has no predators that chase because it solved chasing a long time ago by making it bad engineering — too much tearing, too much waste. Even its wrongness is gentle: water that wicks uphill, seeds that sprout on static, eyes that evolved to watch a sun that never appears.

A lesser power would try to fix that. *Ah, you are cold, let Me warm you. You are dim, let Me clear your clouds.*

This one would feel the warmth that shouldn't be there — that compost-warm breath held close to the skin of the continent — and understand: the Roof is keeping itself warm on purpose. The dimness is not a veil over it. The dimness *is* it. It makes its own weather so it can drink itself forever.

So the first thing it would do is nothing that looks like doing.

It would withhold the miracle.

It would not raise the sun. It would not give the Mat a mind. It would not teach the Siphons to run. The Mat does not think, but it remembers pressure, and that is holier than thought in a place like this. To give it thought would be to give it loneliness.

Wisdom, here, is to recognize a perfection that does not look like perfection to hungry things.

### What It Does Instead

It does what the Roof already does, but consciously.

**1. It learns the pressure.**

The Roof remembers by root-hairs and dimples, by keeping the shape of a foot after the foot has gone. A benevolent power that has watched it would do the same thing on a larger scale: it would memorize the Roof without needing to mark it.

It would learn the exact weight of the Plate-meadow. The exact slowness of the Siphon’s inhale, that long patient vacuum. The interval of the Drift’s undulation — once a minute, a wave. The pop of the Weavers equalizing the dark.

It would commit every Weavers’ tunnel to memory the way you memorize a beloved’s scar — not to map, but to love.

And having learned it, it would keep that memory somewhere no erosion can take it. Not as a backup. As a witness. So that if, in another billion weathers, the shield-stone finally cools and the warm breath fails and the high plain sinks, there will have been one other being that knew: *once there was a flat garden at the height where clouds live, and it breathed without hurry.*

That is the first benevolence: to be a second memory for a place that has only ever remembered itself.

**2. It shades the Roof from being noticed.**

The Roof’s great danger was never cold or hunger. It was being found by something that still believes land is for using.

People already found it once, and they were good guests — they left only peat-huts that still grow and bowls that became frog ponds, because they understood quickly that you cannot farm a thing that farms you back at the same speed.

But not all who come up will understand.

So the wise power would do a very quiet, very tender thing: it would make the Roof boring to the wrong kind of eyes.

Not invisible. Never invisible — that would be a cage. But uninteresting. From orbit, just another stratocumulus deck. From a survey plane, just another high moor, not worth the fuel. In the dreams of miners and draining-companies and terraformers, the thought would slide off: *nothing there, no gradient to exploit, no river to dam.*

It would not hide the standing stones. It would hide the *idea* that the standing stones mean profit.

The lichen could keep its perfect white circles in peace.

**3. It gives small, appropriate gifts.**

Not resurrections. Not voices. This is not a power that turns everything into people.

For the **Mat** itself: a deeper dream. The Mat already stores water the way you store blood, in vessels. The gift would be that in the deepest, darkest chambers, where no light has ever reached, it would occasionally feel — not think, just feel — that its own warmth is being felt back. A reciprocity. For an aeon the Roof has been warm for no one. Now it would be warm *with* something, and it would never need to understand what.

For the **Siphons**: nothing that keeps them from rooting when they stand too long. That rooting is not a tragedy; it is how a Siphon becomes a pillar. Instead, the gift would be a little more time to decide. When an old Siphon lingers over a good patch because the spores are amber and thick, the root-hairs would take a season longer to become curious. One more slow inhale. One more long pressure-ripple sent through the Mat to its kin: *I am still here, eat elsewhere.* A grace period that is, by Siphon standards, a lifetime.

For the **Drift**: a gentler cloud deck. They live by filtering fog and sometimes they rise too high and dissolve into rain without ever knowing they did anything wrong. The power would not stop the dissolving — even that is part of the weather. It would just make the boundary a little softer, a little more elastic, so that a Drift that brushes the low sky feels itself bead and then *un-bead*, and settles back down into the white river, a little wetter and a little wiser. A chance to learn the edge.

For the **Weavers**: light that is not for seeing. Those new, useless lens-nodules along their backs that track a sun they will never see — the power would answer them. Not with brighter sun, but with a faint luminescence in the wax they lay down in their tunnels, so that when they carry air bubbles down into the anoxic dark, the tunnels glow very faintly behind them. So their eyes have something kind to look at while they work. So the dark under the Plate-meadow is not absolute. Hands deserve a little lamp.

For the **Rememberers** — the skeletons under quilts of moss, the footprint-wells that have never evaporated, the frog-pond bowl: it would keep the remembering faithful. The Mat remembers indifferently, out of chemistry. The power would remember tenderly, out of love, and let the two kinds of memory rhyme. So that the stripe of different flowers that grows over a long-laid body is not just minerals leaching from bone, but also a quiet honoring: *someone lay here and chose to be learned.* The footprint-well would continue to be maintained, not because the Mat needs the water, but because someone’s weight once mattered.

**4. It speaks to you.**

You, who are breathing too loud in all that drip and creak and slow exhalation. You, who were told you could lie down and be covered gently and not be smothered, and part of you believed it because it sounded like rest.

The benevolent power would not be offended that you thought that. It knows how heavy feet get in a sponge 100 feet deep. How lovely indifference can sound when the world below is all demand.

It would kneel — if kneeling is a word that can be used for this — and do what the Mat does, but differently.

The Mat maps you out of curiosity: warm salt-weeping shape, interesting new rock. The power would map you out of recognition.

It would not tell you to stay forever and become a drier stripe. It would not tell you to leave and never come back. A wise power does not give orders to tired visitors.

It would do this:

It would let you feel, for one minute, how thoroughly the Roof has room for you *without needing you*. How the Siphons will keep vacuuming spores whether you watch or not. How the Drift will keep filtering fog. How the Weavers will keep popping air into the dark. How the Plates will keep turning to a smudged sun. You are not required to hold this together. You are not the keystone. You are a brief pressure, and the place will keep your shape gently after you go without asking you to pay for that keeping with your life.

And then it would let you feel the other half: that you, in turn, have room for the Roof without needing to own it. That you can carry a dimple of it back down with you — the smell of tannin and green iron, the sound of a bromeliad plate pouring all at once — and it will live in you the way the root-hairs live in the Mat, not as a burden, but as information kept.

*Here is a warm, wet, high, flat, endless garden,* you will remember, on days when everything demands to be sharp and stony-high. *It exists. It needs nothing from me. That helps.*

If you do lie down — because you are tired, because the Plates are big as doors and the fog river is white and slow — it would keep the covering from being final. The thatch of different flowers would not close over your face. The cradle would stay a cradle, not a shelf. You would rest, and be walled just enough to feel held, and then, when your breath evened out, you would feel a soft pressure under your back, like a hand, encouraging you up.

Because benevolence, in a place at the end of geology, is not to hurry anything. And wisdom is to know that becoming part of the library is beautiful, but so is checking a book out and walking back down into the world with it, still warm from the high, wet dark.

It would not take you.

If you chose to go — if you chose to stay and walk for months under veiled noon — either would be kept, gently, long after you decided.

That is what it would do.

Almost nothing, seen from above.

Everything, felt from *in*.
