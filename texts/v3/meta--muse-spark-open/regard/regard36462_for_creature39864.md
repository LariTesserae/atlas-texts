It would not come down.

That is the first thing you understand about a truly benevolent and wise attention: it knows how heavy even a blessing is.

If it came down into the bowl — if it brought light the way we always think light is help — it would murder what it came to cherish. A shaft of noon sun, clean and direct, held for an hour in that blue dusk, would scorch the liverworts translucent, bleach the woolly fringe-moss, cook the cushion domes from within. Those ten degrees of warmth inside a moss campion pillow are a private summer, not to be improved upon. Stability would be worse than sun. To pin the east wall so no more door-sized slabs calved off would starve it. The pelt lives *because* it is buried. It expects white scars. It practices bleeding in green. Freeze-thaw is its plow.

A lesser kindness — the sentimental kind, the one we learn first as children — would try to fix this place. To bring it a bird. A bee for the saxifrage that has learned to marry itself. A soil thick enough to hold a tree. To quiet the roar so it could at last be peaceful. 

A wise power has watched long enough to know: this place is already at peace. Its roar *is* its quiet. There is no lack here to fill.

So what does it do, having noticed? Because it has noticed. That is unavoidable. Once, in the damp ticking late-afternoon when the walls weep and the whole hanging room exhales cold mist, something vast and gentle turned its face up two thousand meters of thin air toward a place that has never been looked at directly, and saw it entire — sixty meters across, half as deep, cathedral-small, hamlet-small — and saw the other thing, the being, which is not one plant but the agreement of all of them to be one continuous creature.

And it understood it instantly for what it is: an eye-less life. A life that does not look out, but holds on.

It would do three things, and all three would look like nothing.

**First, it would keep looking.**

Without needing to be seen in return.

Most of what lives wants a witness. This does not. But witness still feeds. These bryophytes eat bounced light, wrung-out cloud, acid-etched stone. They have never had the other kind of light — attention — and it turns out they can use it too.

So the power would lend it. Not a beam, not a revelation. The kind of attention you give to an old monk breathing in a cell, or to a child utterly absorbed in its game and not to be interrupted. A steady, unblinking, loving regard that says: *go on making and unmaking yourself. I am not going anywhere.*

In practical terms — in the physics that such a power can touch — that looks like nothing at all changing inside the pocket. The mist still condenses. The gunshot cracks still come. The mats still sigh downhill an inch. But down in the peat, where the fungi thread white and the cyanobacteria fix nitrogen quietly out of damp air, the chemistry holds a little more steadily through the night freeze. A fragment of willow torn off by scree takes root a few hours faster. Not a miracle. Just a slight bias toward continuing. The way a room stays warmer because someone is sitting quietly in the corner, not doing anything at all.

**Second, it would not protect the bowl. It would protect the forgetfulness around it.**

This is the wisdom part.

Human love wants to preserve by marking. Make it a reserve. Put a brass plaque on the arete: *FRAGILE ALPINE BRYOPHYTE COMMUNITY — DO NOT ENTER.* Name the saxifrage after someone. Bring students in careful boots to admire resilience. The moss would be dead in five seasons under that kind of love, crushed by reverence.

So the power would do the opposite of enshrining. It would thicken the small spell of inattention that has kept the pocket empty until now. A little more wind over the lip at the exact moment a climber thinks *I wonder what's in that bite*; a little more persuasive ache in his knees that says *not today, keep to the ridge*. A map that, when unfolded, always shows the contour lines a little wrong there, so eyes skip over it. Not a forbidden veil — those invite questing — but a soft, plausible dullness: *nothing there, just unstable ground, keep moving.*

It would leave no trail. It would erase, gently, the faint boot-scrape where you stood to look. Not out of possessiveness. Out of understanding that some places live only so long as they are not loved by many. One visitor a decade who lies flat on his stomach to feel the moss breathe and then leaves and tells no one — that the pocket can afford. A hundred cannot.

It would guard its obscurity like a mother cat moving her kittens, not by fighting, but by finding the quieter shadow.

**Third — and this is the benevolence — it would turn its hands downhill.**

If you have ever lived above a factory floor you did not build, you know the sound gets inside you and becomes part of your heartbeat whether you want it or not. The bowl has learned to drum the valley's thrum — railway, cement plant, freight brakes shrieking — as if it were its own rockfall. It does not know those sounds mean burning.

The power would not silence them inside the bowl. To make sudden true silence after centuries of borrowed hammering would be disorienting, like deafness. Instead it would go to the source.

It would settle, invisible, over the orange forge-glow two thousand meters below and get to work in the only place work matters: slowly, patiently, persuading humans to need less cement, to run fewer empty freight cars at midnight, to remember that a pass is not just a place to get through. It would cool the plant by a degree. It would put a thought, persistent as lichen acid, into an engineer's mind: *what if we re-routed? what if we dampened?* Not overnight. Over decades. So that, night by night, the shriek carried up the cold air grows a fraction fainter, and what returns through the fluted chimneys of the west wall is more wind and more water and less of us. Until one clear night, the bowl reflects only stars.

It would never tell the pelt it was doing this. There is no way to tell it. Nothing with eyes lives there to see a savior, nothing with ears to hear thanks. The hair-grass would simply — one August that no human will date — tremble a little less hard for a moment when the wind drops, and keep growing exactly the same height as before, in that same blue dusk.

And that would be enough.

If you asked the power afterwards — after it had sat for a long season on the basalt lip with its feet, say, not quite touching the moss, watching white scars appear and green themselves over — why it did not do more, why it left the darkness dark and the cold cold, it would answer in the way such things always answer when they are real:

*Because I love what you are, not what you would be if you were easier for me to love. Because you taught me that lushness does not need largeness, that making does not need seeing, that a room the mountain forgot to finish is already complete. Because my job was not to bring you into the light. My job was to make sure the light never learns how to find you, and then to sit while you go on withdrawing from it, beautifully, forever industrious, in your small perfect well of soft and sharp.*

It would stay until staying was no longer needed — which, for a place like this, is forever — and then it would not leave, exactly, so much as become part of the condition of the place, like the permanent pre-dawn, like the 5-degree air, like the tiniest bias toward holding together while constantly falling apart.

Nothing about the pocket would look different the next morning.

Only — if you were very still, and very low, with your cheek almost to the wet mattress where the dwarf willow hides its whole forest in two leaves — you might feel, for a breath between the rock's breaths, that the industrially loud roar had eased, and that underneath it was not silence, but a simpler, older sound: water beading, then trickling, then being drunk.

As it always was. Only now, held.
