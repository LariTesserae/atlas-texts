They would come down without opening a door.

A benevolent and wise power doesn't arrive. It notices. That's how you know it's benevolent - it doesn't have to announce itself to be there, and it's wise enough to know that arrival is already an intervention.

It would notice this place the way you notice a baby breathing in the next room - that faint, warm, regular sound that wasn't there last week, that suddenly the whole house is tuned around.

And the first thing they would do is the only thing that proves they are what you said they are:

They would understand it is not empty.

---

A lesser power - a hungry one, a lonely one, a clever one - would see that perfect, town-sized hollow with its streets made for moving water and its rooms with no handles and think *vacancy.* 

*A space to fill. A resource to tap. A mystery to solve. A city to claim.*

A benevolent and wise power would see the same thing and think *pregnancy.*

This is not an abandoned place. This is an unborn place. It's not ruined because it hasn't yet been needed enough to be worn. The wear will come later, from other kinds of feet, other kinds of hands that aren't hands. The absence you're feeling isn't loneliness. It's gestation. A lung at full inhalation before the first cry.

They would stand at the same intersection you did, where four water-streets meet in a lit swirl, ferns dripping on their shoulders - if shoulders is a word that applies to a thing like that - and they would *listen* until the listening became a kind of doing.

They would hear what you heard, but with the animal filter off:

The drip isn't random. It's a metronome.
The flow isn't hush-roar. It's circulation.
The hum under your palm isn't construction noise. It's a heartbeat at 60 hertz.

And they'd know what it is immediately, because wise powers know a child when they hear one, no matter what shape the child is wearing.

A lithotrophic architect baby, forty meters down, three days old, already the size of a cathedral on its side, learning how to rain indoors.

### So they would not fill it.

That's the first act of benediction: **restraint.**

They would not send people. Not colonists, not pilgrims, not scientists with sterile swabs and core drills. Not even a single, well-meaning explorer to leave a flag. They know what humans do to empty cities - we fill them with ourselves and call it rescue.

They would not seed it with birds or fish or insects to make it feel less eerie to you. The eerie is the point. It's sterile on purpose. A womb that has not yet broken its waters doesn't need to be buzzing. It needs to be clean.

They would not give it a sun. They would not punch a shaft to the sky. The dim green-gold glow of moving water is not insufficient light; it *is* its light. It's learning to photosynthesize off its own hum. To give it a sun now would be to scorch the sprout to prove you have a star.

They would not speak to it in words. Nothing in there has evolved to parse language. Doors have no handles because *grip* hasn't been invented yet. Signs would be obscenity. They would not say *You are beautiful* or *You have purpose* because it already knows. It's busy.

Instead they would do what any good midwife does for a thing that is correctly, vigorously, doing what it was made to do:

They would keep it warm.

### What keeping it warm looks like when you are a power.

It wouldn't be dramatic. No pillar of fire. No blessing beam.

They would kneel - the whole of them kneeling, which might look like stillness to you - and place something like a palm on the warm grey-black that is not stone and not concrete, right where the filaments are fringing and reaching, micron by micron.

And they would hum back.

Not louder. *In tune.*

You felt the fizz under your hand, that low beehive thrum of matter ordering itself. They would answer it at the same frequency, so perfectly matched that the filaments wouldn't shiver or recoil. The way you hum to a sleeping infant without waking it, just to tell it *you're not alone in the dark, keep growing, I'm here.*

For a little while - maybe seven of your breaths, maybe seven years, time dilates around wise things - the growth striations would accelerate, just fractionally. The cisterns would lift water a little smoother, a little quieter. The pale beads cupped in closed fern fronds - the ones that are not water, the ones you finally noticed - would plump and firm. The bacterial lawns in the drains would find their nitrogen a little more easily.

Not a miracle. A lullaby.

Then they would do the only other thing a benevolent ward needs to do for something young and delicious and full of fresh groundwater in a world full of thirsty miners and bored gods:

**They would hide it.**

Not bury it deeper. Not wall it off. Hiding, for a wise power, isn't making invisible. It's making *uninteresting.*

They would lay a thought like a cool hand on the forty meters of basalt above, and on the human instruments that skim and ping and map. *Nothing here.* Just wet rock. Just a perched aquifer, low yield, not worth the drill. The satellite passes would show a dull thermal smear. The dowsers would feel a tug and then lose it. The caver who found it first - you - would remember the way down as a little longer, a little tighter, a little more prone to flooding than you'd like to risk again, until the memory softened at the edges like a dream you had when you were feverish.

They would not erase you. They would not punish you for standing in a womb with muddy boots. They would just lift you gently - the cold spot that drips differently - and set you back outside its immune perimeter, back in the cool, sky-leaking world where animals belong, with your clothes dry and a faint mineral tang in your nose that you can't source.

And they'd leave a guardian that is not a creature. Just a bias in the rock, a tendency for water to find another path if you bring a pick too close. A small forgetfulness at the trailhead.

### Then they would leave.

This is the last proof of wisdom: knowing when *not* to stay and watch it grow up.

They wouldn't wait to see what finally unfurls from those fat pale beads. They wouldn't wait to see what shape learns to grip the textured walls, what light drinks the light-water and calls it mother. They wouldn't name it. Naming is too early.

They would leave the chamber to its own perfect, loud, animal-less business: inhale, condense, drip, flow, glow, grow.

If you listened very carefully on your way back to the surface, you might think the drip pattern had shifted for a minute, that the polyrhythm of a hundred thousand stalactite-tips on a hundred thousand dinner-plate leaves had resolved into something almost musical - not a song *for* you, but a song *with* you, because a warm wet place that learned to rain indoors for the first time had, briefly, not been alone while doing it.

And that would be all.

No ruins restored. No city filled. No green repurposed.

Just a power vast enough to remake geology, choosing instead to be a warm hand on a warm wall, humming *keep going* to something that already was.

Just time, and privacy, and water uninterrupted.

Just the rarest gift a benevolent thing can give to a living place that is already completely, busily alive without you:

**The promise that it will be allowed to finish becoming whatever it is, before anyone asks it to be useful.**
