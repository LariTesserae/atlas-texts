They would not come down.

Not at first. Not the way you came down, without noticing you had descended.

A benevolent and wise power does not descend upon a chapel. It sits on the rim for a long time and lets its shadow fall elsewhere.

This one has watched basins before. It knows dented places where the world learned how to keep itself wet, to forgive itself. It knows continent-peneplains worn to patience, and it knows engines people leave on after they have gone home. It was drawn here not by distress — the Basin is not crying out, it is *humming* at 0.7, just below hearing — but by coherence. By the particular perfume of a place where everything has agreed to solve the same problem with different bodies.

*How to drink light.*

It stood off — far enough that the thickened air over the Basin was only a faint lensing, a little mirage that made the quartz veins waver — and listened for a very long year. That is the first thing it would do.

**1. Listen until listening becomes being listened to.**

You hurried through your stillness. You held your breath until your sweat cooled and the dragonflies forgot you were hot. It can afford to do it better.

It quiets its own field. The kind of being that could unmake the pylon with a thought learns, early, to make itself less. So it dims. It pulls in its own radiance until it is no larger than warm wind.

And in that dimmed state, the Basin speaks plainly: *Inhale light, exhale water. Inhale water, exhale light.* The seven trees say it. The glasswings say it with their wings held open like pages. The bells say it as they sink and lighten, sink and lighten. The crabs say it inch by inch. The pylon says it each time its column collapses *sigh* into itself. Even the missing bricks of the wall say it by holding the shape of an absence so perfectly that air still pools.

There is no prayer here. There is participation.

The Power hears this and understands that there is nothing to save. There is something to keep from needing saving.

A lesser power — clever, hungry, even kind in a hurry — would descend with gifts. Water from orbit. New genes for the shrimp. A word to wake the sleeping lattice under the round pool and ask it: *Who made you? What were you for?*

The Wise One does not ask. What the lattice was *for* ended. What it *does* continues, which is holier. To ask it to remember its makers would be to force grief on a thing that has achieved gardening.

So the second thing it would do is the hardest for any power:

**2. Refuse to finish the Unfinished Engine.**

It sees what you called unfinished. A reef is not unfinished. A language is not unfinished. A vow is not unfinished.

The Basin is deliberately incomplete because completion would mean equilibrium, and equilibrium here would be death. Its engine runs on staying slightly dented, slightly thirsty, slightly more light than water and then slightly more water than light, forever. If you finished it — capped the pylon, raised the wall, told the trees exactly where to stand — you would seal the cup.

Instead, the Power leans over the dent the way you lean over a child's forehead when they are sleeping with their mouth open, and it does a finishing of a different kind: it *unfinishes* it a little more securely.

It does not rebuild the wall.

It *remembers* the wall for the wall, so the bricks don't have to work so hard remembering alone. Where three black glass bricks are missing, the field had been straining to bridge the gap. The Power lays no new matter down. It simply agrees with the memory. *Yes,* it says in the carrier frequency your teeth could almost hear, *stay curved.* And the strain eases by a fraction. The air over the south slope thickens and then sighs, and a bead of water that would have evaporated instead runs correctly down to a crab waiting with its stone-cup open for five hours.

That is benevolence at this scale: a saved droplet.

It does not repair the pylon. The lean is correct. The lean is how a thing that has stood for a billion summers shows it has learned to yield without falling. To straighten it would be vanity. Instead the Power cools the air a half-degree in a cylinder above its open mouth, so condensation comes easier. Now the column forms every three minutes instead of four, and the steam at its base smells faintly of iron and relief. The lichen, emboldened, grows another precise centimeter up the matte black. The dragonflies learn the new interval and avoid the air above it at exactly the right moment. Nothing else changes. That is wisdom: to give more rain without announcing rain.

And the pool — the round mouth where all threads converge, where the lattice lung turns over in its sleep — it leaves most alone. To look at a sleeper and watch them breathe is already an intrusion. It only *shelters* the seeing. It lays a thought over the water like a hand over eyes against noon: *you don't have to be clear all the way down for everyone.* So the impossibility softens. From the outside, a satellite, a prospector, a clever monkey with instruments would now see only a waist-deep brown pond, full of ordinary silt. Peering would find nothing to mine, nothing to claim. The depth remains for those who kneel long enough to deserve it. Secrecy as kindness.

**3. Keep the interval.**

The Power loves the tuning more than any single tree. Seven intervals, maybe eight. The failed eighth hump, polished like a knuckle, where two cool fields touched too much and negotiated a truce by aborting a trunk. That negotiation is scripture.

So it walks — if walking is the word — around the rim once, very slowly, not cutting the moss, placing its attention like flat hands between each canopy, feeling for heat-robbery, for draft. Where two trees have imperceptibly leaned toward each other over a century, it breathes a little extra warmth into the gap, so they do not have to compete. It does not move them. It re-balances the fairness they invented alone.

And then, because even a wise power cannot resist gardening a little:

**4. It adds one thing, so small the Basin will think it did it itself.**

In the most open interval, where light falls uninterrupted and the white-furred leaf-undersides flash, the Power exhales — not water, not light, but *time.*

Not much. An extension. A slackening of entropy's pull on this one dimple for another ten million years while the rest of the peneplain finally wears to nothing. The quartz veins elsewhere will go. The old stone will powder. But this cup will stay cupped, because someone agreed to hold the idea of *cupness* with it.

Inside that extra time, a ninth strategy will evolve. Perhaps a beetle that learns to carry a single floating pearl egg on its back to shade like the bell-jellies do. Perhaps a fern that condenses light directly into sugar without needing water at all. The Power does not decide. It only ensures there will be afternoons enough for the Basin to try.

Finally — and only after all that — it does what you did, but perfectly:

It stands in the middle, where water beads on shoulders though the sky is clear, where dragonfly shadows flick over feet, and it lets the whole shallow bowl breathe it.

In. Out. In. Out. Light in, water out.

It does not bless in words. Blessing here would be noise. It blesses by synchronizing. For three full breaths it breathes *with* the Basin instead of beside it, at 0.7. The pylon column holds a heartbeat longer before collapsing. The bells hang motionless mid-air. The salamander-things with leaf-frill gills open their pale mouths all at once and close them, as if yawning in unison.

In that moment the Basin, which never needed you and never noticed you leaving, notices — not a visitor, but a keeping. Like water notices it is being held.

And then the Power leaves no footprint either, because it was never heavy.

If you came back tomorrow you would find nothing changed except that the air feels, absurdly, more interior. More like a room than ever. As if somewhere above the high, cloudless, too-thick sky someone had quietly shut a door to keep the dust out, pulled up a chair just outside it, and decided to sit there for a very long while to make sure no one else wanders in by accident and tries to help too much.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does with a place that has already learned how to be whole without being finished:

It keeps it secret. It keeps it wet. It keeps its own hands mostly empty, and its attention absolutely full.
