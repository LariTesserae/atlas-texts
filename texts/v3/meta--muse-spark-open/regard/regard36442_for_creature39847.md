They would do almost nothing.

That is the first thing to understand about a truly benevolent and wise power.

Any lesser power would arrive.

It would see the Roof - that tired, lifted continent wearing its thatched ocean of leaves - and it would call it broken. *No rivers! No soil! The people so small and hungry, eating moths in a deafening dark!* And out of kindness it would try to fix it. It would split the stone to make a river. It would bring wheat that cannot grow. It would teach the Roof people to speak louder, to build higher, to burn. It would give them metal. Fire. Medicine they didn't ask for that would need medicine after it.

It would kill them with help.

The Wise One does not arrive.

It notices.

Attention, from such a being, is not a spotlight. It is a weight, like a hand on a fevered brow, like shade. The Roof people would not feel it as a visitation. The Aeonbroma would not feel it at all. It has been breathing for a hundred million years; it knows how to breathe without advice.

For a long time - maybe years, maybe a single long afternoon that stretches because time behaves differently around such powers - it would just *listen*.

It would listen to the Roof the way you listen to a heart.

It would stand - if standing is the word - at the cliff's edge where the mat frays and pours over the eroded mud-cliffs in green waterfalls, and look inward across that comprehensible, town-valley-sized infinity. It would hear the wall of sound - 2.7/3, deafening, that metallic cicada shriek of a million Veil Swifts - and it would not wince. It would hear underneath it the *plip-plip-plip* of the frog-cisterns, the creak of lignified leaves, the sigh of upward rain at noon. It would understand that this is not noise. This is a perfect conversation that has been going on without pause since before its own kind had language.

And it would listen down, through fifty feet of floating peat and root-arches, to the little hearths. To the murmur. The Roof people have learned the only way to speak under a screaming sky is to speak as if to a lover: low, close to the ear, necessary. The power would hear a mother telling a child which of the sixty cisterns near their leaf-tent is poison this season. It would hear a old man tapping a root - *tok-tok, tok-tok* - listening for the hollow drum that means *it will hold you*. It would hear them not praying for rescue, not cursing the damp, but naming. Always naming. *This is Wasp-Root. This is Frog-Throat. This is the dome where the Mantle sleeps, leave it alone.*

It would realize: they are not abandoned. They are not deprived. They are *fitted*.

This place did not make them by accident. It shaped them the way water shapes a cup - by being exactly itself for a very long time. Short, broad, wide-footed, cropped hair so moths won't lay, half-deaf and soft-voiced. Gardeners of rooms. People who have learned not to climb or dig but to slip sideways.

To change anything would be to unfit them.

So the Wise One would not heal. There is nothing sick.

It would not uplift. There is nothing low.

It would not even reveal itself. Revelation is a kind of violence here. To appear in fire above the felt prairie would scatter the mothfall for generations, deafen further the half-deaf elders, make the children worship a shape instead of learning to read a leaf.

Instead it would do four very small things. So small only the world itself would notice.

**1. It would become a second roof.**

Not over, but around. At the distant cliff-rims where the Roof falls away in slumped, vegetated steps toward the lowlands - where one day men with metal and shouting will come again, because lowlanders always come again to places that look empty on a map - it would deepen the strangeness a little.

It would make the fog a little thicker on the approach trails. The way a thought slips from your mind when you walk into another room. Compasses would wander by two degrees. A sense would settle on any expedition: *we have brought the wrong shoes for this place. There is nothing here for us.* They would turn back before they ever saw the first round clearing, and they would not know why, and they would not feel denied.

No wall. No wrath. Just an extra kindness of forgetfulness, so the Roof can continue to be incomprehensible on its own terms until the lowlands are wise enough to slip sideways too.

**2. It would answer the mat's breath with its own.**

At noon, when a billion stomata open and the Roof exhales upward rain in a great steaming sigh that the wind tears away - that moment when a million gallons are lifted and lost - the power would lend a fingertip of pressure. Not much. A slackening in the high wind for an hour. A hesitation.

So that a little more of that transpired water falls *back*.

Not as a miracle storm. Just as a heavier bead on the waxy leaves. A cistern that would have evaporated by three p.m. now lasts until dusk. A crab gets an extra hour to cross from pool to pool with water in its belly-hairs. A child dipping a root-fiber net finds one more fat white larva.

No one would call it a blessing. They would just say, *good fog this year.*

That is how benevolence prefers to be remembered: as good weather.

**3. It would leave a gift inside, not above.**

It would not give the Roof people tools - they have the only tool this place allows, that smooth imported grinding stone carried for generations because there is no stone left to knap. It would not give them writing; they already write on the world by knowing sixty cisterns by taste.

It would give them a little more *time inside the sound*.

When the dusk mothfall rises and the under-canopy becomes a blizzard of white wings and the birds rise to meet them and the sound becomes a physical wall that vibrates your sternum - the old ones cannot bear it anymore. Their half-deafness is not enough. They go inside leaf-flaps early and miss the hunt.

The power would teach the humus, very locally, under the three-leaved tents, to dampen just a little more. A property of the peat, a slight thickening of fiber. So that inside, with the flap pulled shut, the shriek softens by a fraction. Just enough that an old voice, low and close to the ear, can still be heard telling the story about the cliffs where the world drops away.

Not silence. The Roof should never be silent. Just enough quiet to pass knowledge hand to hand, throat to ear, one more generation.

**4. And finally, it would learn.**

This is the most important thing it would do, and the only thing it would do for itself.

A wise power knows that after a hundred million years of warm air, lichen, and patience, a mountain can become tired and soft and *tiredness itself can be a form of perfection*. That a forest can be 2.6/3 total vegetation and 0.5/3 empty - that overwhelming and empty can exist together if everything becomes scaffold. That people can live *in* a place, not on it, and be healthier for making themselves small.

It would sit - again, if sitting is the word - under a root-dome in the green cathedral where the light filters down like through stained glass, condensation beading on its shoulders - whatever shoulders a power has - and it would let a tiny vermilion frog climb onto its boot thinking it is a root, and blink.

It would not move.

It would let the birds forget it. It would let the condensation run down its neck. It would breathe when the Roof breathes.

And when it left - not departing, but diffusing, as fog diffuses at wind-tearing noon - there would be no sign it had been there.

Except this: a year later, a Roof child slipping sideways between two leaf-walls to find a new cistern would pause for no reason, tap a root-arch, hear a truer hollow drum than before, and choose a slightly better path. And far below her feet, fifty feet down through humming air, the tired old mountain would hold her up a fraction more lightly, as if it too had been listened to, and found adequate.

That is what love does when it has no limits and no need to prove them.

It does not build. It does not save.

It keeps the rooms empty enough for life to keep making them.
