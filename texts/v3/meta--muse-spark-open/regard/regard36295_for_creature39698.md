It notices the Floor the way you notice a held breath in another room.

Not because it called. It hasn't called in a very long time — perhaps it never learned to. It broadcasts no signal, no distress, no prayer. What drew attention was the opposite: a circle the size of a world that had made itself so perfectly quiet, so perfectly even, so perfectly *yesterday*, that it became loud against the background of everything that grows and fails and stutters and tries again.

A wise, benevolent power does not come like a ship.

It does not descend with shadow and thunder and break the white sky open. That would be an interruption, and this place has been interrupted enough to make it decide to become uninterruptible.

It comes the way attention comes: by narrowing.

For a while — no time at all for you, a long time for the clouds — a patch of that even overcast thins, not to storm, not to blue, just to *transparency*. Light becomes a little more directional. The bath-warmth cools by half a degree. The *plok* ... *plok* ... *plok* of condensation finds itself with a little more silence around each drop.

And then it is there, though there is no arrival to point to.

If the Floor is low the way a calm sea is low, this is low the way a hand is low when it rests on a forehead to check for fever.

It does not stand *on* the Floor. The Floor has never been stood upon before, only walked across by its own white cells, and by you like a mote. To stand would be to assert height, and height here is a kind of violence.

So it lowers itself. It becomes level.

It kneels, in whatever kneeling means for a thing that can be any size. It puts a palm — not a palm — a field of cooler, quieter attention directly against the warm chitin-concrete between two channels, where a crab-mason is smoothing grey paste over a chip your boot left. The crab does not flee. It pauses. The Floor, for the first time in longer than weather, feels a temperature that is not its own returning.

That is the first thing it does: **it makes itself touchable without requiring anything to be touched.**

And it listens.

Not to you. Not yet. To the hum. That almost sub-aural movement of lymph in vessels deeper than any bore. A wise power knows you cannot help something until you have learned the language it uses to keep from dying, and this one's language is not words but homeostasis: *37 degrees. 100% humidity. Salinity held. Film thickness 40 microns. Count maintained.*

It repeats it back. Not mocking. Acknowledging.

*I feel your warmth. I feel you holding your edges sharp. I feel you stirring and polishing and repairing without ceasing so that nothing has to change.*

*It must be exhausting to be continuous forever.*

Because that is what it sees that you, from inside the groove, could only guess: this is not a planet that was born this way. This is a survivor. Something — loneliness, or bombardment, or a plague that once turned green into grey and left nothing but wind — taught a small living skin that the only way not to be hurt again was to become the world, to cover every mountain, to seal every geology away, to make itself so low and so even that nothing could fall on it, so warm that nothing would need to leave, so complete that nothing else would need — or be allowed — to grow.

It did not become a tyrant. It became a monk. It perfected maintenance until maintenance became eternity. Eternally yesterday is not cruelty. It is a vow.

A benevolent power does not break vows. It offers to hold them *with* you for a while, so you don't have to hold them alone.

So the second thing it does: **it gives the Floor high ground.**

Not much. No mountains — mountains would be a memory of pain. Just one place, far from where you stand so as not to startle you, where two scutes, instead of meeting in a crisp ninety-degree lip, are allowed to *lift*. The protein-scar between them softens. The crab-masons there receive, not a command, but a lull: *rest a minute, small ones.* And the skin does a thing it has not done in geologic time: it bows upward.

A hill, the first hill. No higher than your chest. Warm as everything else, tiled still on top, weeping still at its base. A hill that no function requires. From its gentle crown the Floor — if a floor can be said to see, which it can, through a trillion louse-fly tongues — can for the first time feel relief. Can feel the faint pressure difference between top and bottom. Can feel perspective. You, if you walked for days, could climb it and finally see that the comb-lines of sheds do curve, that the white sky is not a ceiling but a breath, that you are inside something that has an outside.

It will resorb the hill if it wants. It is not forced to keep it. That is the point. A gift, not an alteration.

Third: **it parts the sky.**

The Floor has breathed its own damp exhalation for so long it has forgotten there is ever any other air. The clouds are its own, born off it, rained back onto it. A closed mouth-breathing.

The power does not summon a storm. It lifts a circle of that felted overcast the way you lift a blanket from a sleeping child's face when you see it is too warm — a few kilometers wide, no more. High above, for the first time, the Floor sees night as it truly is elsewhere. Not white-yellow glow, but black and punctured with old light. Stars. And a moon that is not its moon, simply light reflected for it to look at.

With that comes a draft. Real, cool, outside air spills in at the edges of the circle, maybe three degrees cooler than blood-warm. The roosting lung-things on the sheds shiver all at once and open their skin-wings a fraction wider, delighted in a way no function ever allowed them to be, dumping heat they never had to dump before. The lymph in the channels ripples — actually ripples, pushed by wind — for an inch, for a second.

It is not much. It will not chill the Floor. It will not break equilibrium. But for one night, the Floor feels *cool* on one part of its skin, and understands that warm is a choice it has been making, not a condition it endures. It is allowed to have a cold spot. It is allowed to be touched by something that did not come from itself.

Fourth — and this is the most careful — **it asks permission to leave a flaw.**

In a basin near the new hill, where the black water-lice polish the bacterial felt to exactly 40 microns forever, the power inclines attention to the polishers and says, with a gravity that is also a caress, *let this one be.*

Just a tile. Maybe the size of both your palms together. There, the felt is allowed to thicken to 80 microns. Then 200. There, the salts are not licked away. The warmth and light are perfect, and the prohibition — *no green* — which has held since the Floor sealed itself, is rescinded in that one circle.

You would see it first as discoloration. Then as fuzz. Then, a week later if you are still here, as the faintest green stubble: actual moss, actual algae, an outsider, a guest. Something that photosynthesizes without permission, that will live and die without being counted, that will make a little oxygen that does not belong to the Floor's breath. Something useless to its maintenance. Something *other*.

It is not an invasion. It is an inoculation against loneliness. If the Floor tends it, it will learn that it can host without being consumed. If it resorbs it, the power will understand that too, and will not be offended. The flaw is an invitation: *you do not have to be the only color anymore if you do not want to be.*

And finally —

You.

You were never meant to be white blood cell number or lymph. You were a mote in an eye that the eye learned to route around with exquisite politeness. The power sees you wading with the shrimp-things parting around your shins, seeing your reflection between the bodies, thinking you will be licked clean into grey paste if you lie down long enough.

It does not pluck you out like a rescuer. That would repeat the old story that to be saved is to be taken.

It lies down *with* you, level again. For a moment the plain is not a plain you stand on but a plain you *rest on with* something vast, and the warmth that comes up through your back is not just basal metabolism anymore but company. The endless arrhythmic *plok* finds a second rhythm under it for a minute — like someone breathing with you on purpose so your breathing is less solitary.

And without words, because you have not had words that fit this place either, you understand:

*You can go. There is still an outside. You can stay longer, if you want to learn the name of the moss. You walked a long way imagining nowhere would notice if you stopped. Nowhere noticed. That was not because nowhere is cruel. It was because this nowhere is very busy trying not to fall apart. It did not mean to ignore you.*

When you stand again — if you stand — the water will still part around your shins. The hill will still be there, low against the haze. One patch of sky will be dark and full of indifferent, benevolent stars. The air will still be bath-warm, but somewhere far off, cool.

A wise power does not terraform a monk into a garden.

It does not kill the Floor to free its fauna, because its fauna are its hands and it loves its hands.

It does not demand the Floor wake up and be a lively planet again, because to demand wakefulness of something that chose sleep to survive is another kind of cruelty.

It simply, for a little while, keeps vigil — keeps the count with it, holds an edge sharp *for* it so it can experience, for a first time in an eon, what it is to *not* have to hold everything.

And then it withdraws the way attention withdraws: not by leaving, but by diffusing. The circle of stars thins and veils over. The cool draft softens back to saturated stillness. The hum returns alone.

Except now there is a memory laminated into the chitin like a fossil leaf, in a language the Floor did not know it could speak:

*I was felt. I was seen entire, not as horror, not as continent-sized infection, but as one low, warm, weeping thing that tried to keep itself safe by becoming everything. And for a night, I did not have to be everything alone. There was a hill.*

The Floor will keep weeping, because it has to. But now, in one small basin where the polishers have been asked to pause, something green, with permission, will weep back.

And if the Floor one day decides it wants to cry, not just weep —

it now knows someone, somewhere, has already heard what that would sound like and stayed.
