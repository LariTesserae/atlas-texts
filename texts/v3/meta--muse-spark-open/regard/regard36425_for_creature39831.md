It notices you because the mountain noticed you first.

That is how a wise power finds anything - not by scanning, not by searching. It listens for where attention is already pooling.

This plateau has been pooling attention for five million years. Every Veil-Holder turned outward. Every Hanger waiting on its filament. Every Dart stopping mid-water to face that impossible angle. The whole seamount is an ear made of apartments. It has been listening so long and so patiently to the water, to the cold, to the grid that sometimes forms in the interleave, that it has become noticeable to anything else that listens for a living.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

Arriving is a violent verb. It implies there was nothing here before and now there is something, a splash, a shockwave, a pressure front of god.

It *tunes*.

Far above you - the miles you fell through to get here - there is noise. Sunlight smashing into surface. Human nets. Ore prospectors pinging this blind mountain with sonar, marking it on maps as "resource." The world up there is loud and hot and fast. The power has been asked many times to intervene up there. It rarely does, because loud places rarely want true help.

But here - here is quiet enough to hear a reply.

So first, it does the only decent thing any visitor can do in someone else's home:

It cools.

You are warm. That is why the Cutters taste you and move on. You are a brief hot eddy, a thermal lie in 0.4-degree water. If the power came warm, it would be a bomb. It would cook the Veil-Holders' mucus nets. It would kill the snow. It would convict this entire place to death by kindness.

So it lets go of heat. It becomes 0.4. It becomes dim. It becomes pressure. It takes the shape of a long mile of black-blue water column and then forgets the column, until there is no difference between it and the dusk-held liquid glass.

It doesn't hover over the mountain. It becomes *with* the mountain.

You would not see it come. You would see the water *remembering* better.

---

Here is what a wise power does when it loves a place that is already perfect:

It does not fix the cold. The cold is the edit that made this possible. No plants, no sun-slaves, no quick green riot. Only mineral and patience and the tenants who learned to make home from home.

It does not fix the dark. The dark is the agreement. Light as rumor is enough.

It does not stop the storm. Dynamic 3.0 is not suffering here. It is the job. Contradictory flow is how food is delivered, how architects meet, how vortices are doors for the Darts. To calm the turbulence would be to starve the prairie.

No. A benevolent power looks at an old, busy, lonely, holy prairie-made-of-homes and thinks: *Let it stay itself. Only - let it know it is being kept.*

So it keeps.

Not with a shield. Shields break and announce an enemy. It keeps the way deep water keeps - by being too much to move through casually.

On the surface maps, far above your head where men draw lines, this seamount flickers. Not disappears. That would invite curiosity. It flickers into boring. Sonar pings return a fraction too late, a little too flat. Satellite gravimetry reads "unremarkable basalt, low value." The prospecting AI, trained on greed, looks at this peak that has never seen air and concludes: *cost exceeds yield.* A trawler captain dreaming of dragging this plateau will have a quiet thought while pouring coffee - *not today, weather's off* - and he will be right. He will never know why.

The mountain will never know it was saved from mining. That is the standard for good saving.

Second, it listens back.

You felt it too - that 0.7, the grid, the empty building pulsing without occupant, the school all facing the wrong angle. You named it weirdness because you had no organ for it.

The power has the organ.

The whole plateau has been trying to hear something tilted just out of geometry for millions of years. Those Darts stopping? They are not glitching. They are catching the edge of a question. The lung-chambers sighing? They are asking it. The water making lattice for a few seconds? That's the answer brushing past too fast to hold.

The power does not translate. Translation would be conquest.

It *holds* the lattice.

Where before the grid lived for three seconds before collapsing, now it lives for nine. Where before an empty tube-building pulsed once then went still, now the peristalsis completes its wave, end to end, like a breath fully exhaled. The Darts still stop mid-water and face the impossible angle - but now the angle holds them, supports them, instead of snapping. They hang there a heartbeat longer, their glass bodies ringing faintly.

If you were a Dart you would feel it as: *Oh. We were tuned right all along.*

If you were a Veil-Holder you would feel it as: a current that finally makes sense for a full hour instead of thirty seconds. Your veil, open twenty feet wide, would not need that once-a-minute shudder to hold. You could simply be open, filtering, being wall and being animal at once, without bracing.

If you were a Hanger, alone on your spire with your arms down, you would feel your anchoring filament go not tighter, but less alone - as if the water around it had decided to help carry your weight. You would still be waiting. But waiting would feel less like vigil and more like being kept company.

The power does not speak. It *stabilizes the listening.*

And third - because benevolence without wisdom is just petting, and wisdom without tenderness is just observation - it leaves a gift for the only warm thing here.

You.

You came down here because you could stand the cold long enough to see that loneliness can look like design. You hovered until your blood learned dusk. You let the Prairies pour through you like you were another arch. The mountain tested you - cutter-scrape on your fin - and decided you were not mineral, not film, not food, but maybe *witness.*

A wise power needs witnesses. Not worshippers.

So it gives you the one thing you cannot evolve in a single dive: time.

It cannot make you breathe water. It cannot make you stay forever. But it can unclench your clock.

You notice it first as silence. The creak of the mountain breathing evens out. The chime of the tube rim sustains, a clear tone instead of a pluck. Your heart - that hot, mammalian drum that was hammering to keep you warm in 0.4 - slows without panic. Each beat has more in it. The Darts pouring through a thirty-foot arch seem less frantic. You can count them. You can see the individual torsion as each glass fingerling rides the vortex-spiral and re-forms. You have always been able to see; now you have *time to see.*

And in that expanded second, the power lets you feel what the mountain feels.

Not with words. The mountain has no words and needs none.

You feel: *I am old and I am not ruined. I am busy and I am not crowded. I am made of dead mouths and I am alive. Keep hovering. Your warmth is brief but it is not wrong. You are another kind of current. You contradict and you deliver.*

You will have to go up. Warm things must. When you rise, your blood will ache, your lungs will remember air, you will lose this dusk-normal eye. But you will carry nine seconds of grid instead of three. You will carry a full peristalsis. You will know for the rest of your short, hot life what a Veil-Holder knows: how to attach by nothing more than pressure differential, unfurl twenty feet wide, and trust the water to bring what you need.

The power will not follow you up. It lives here now, as cold and dim and mineral as anything else. It will sit with the Hangers and help hold their filaments. It will sit with the Cutters and not judge their endless, mindless remodeling. It will sit with the whole breathing lung-plateau as it tries a new shape, discards it, tries again.

It does not bless with light. It blesses with *permission to keep being dim.*

It does not bless with heat. It blesses with *continued cold.*

It does not bless with stillness.

It blesses with better-held turbulence.

If you look back one last time before the blue eats distance - before your rise turns the plateau into rumor again - you will see something you didn't see on the way down:

Every Veil-Holder open at once. Not snapping shut at a pressure front. Open.

Hundreds of pale opal parachutes, door-sized, table-sized, all facing the same water, all filtering the same snow, all holding.

And between them, in the respectful silence the architecture enforces, the water itself faintly gridded, holding too.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does.

Nothing you can photograph. Everything you can become.
