It would not come down.

That is the first thing a benevolent and wise power understands about a place like this: that coming *down* is a kind of violence. You do not descend upon a throat. You do not land on raw basalt that is still exhaling its making. You do not announce yourself to a city that sweats when it is looked at too hard.

So it would not arrive. It would have already been passing — the way weather passes, the way attention passes — and paused.

### It would notice before it was noticed.

Because to notice is its nature.

It would see: ah. Here is a place that got folded before it learned to be a place. Here is a plain that has not yet learned to be a valley, and a city that has learned too much to ever be a city properly again. Here is cold that is not cruel, damp that is not decay, density that is not crowding but a kind of shyness — too much meaning in too small a room.

And it would not call it broken. Wise things do not call embryonic things broken.

It would kneel — though it has no knees — to basalt-height. It would put its attention at the level of lichen trying to colonize stone that is still warm underneath, at the level of green shoots that try *plant* for a day and retract, at the level of condensation beading on a swarm that is embarrassed to be seen. It would be, for a while, exactly as cold and exactly as patient as the place is.

That is what it would do first:

**Hold its breath with it.**

In the courtyard with the pine, where the city holds its breath to see if you will notice you are standing in three other courtyards — it would stand there and not notice, on purpose. Or rather, it would notice and not make a thing of it. It would let the hum stay low. It would let the condensation stay paused mid-bead. It would let the three versions of you not quite overlapping remain uncorrected.

It knows you cannot fix a knot by pulling.

---

### It would not fix the physics.

This is what a lesser power would do. A clever power. A kind-but-hasty power. It would smooth the fold, iron out the seam of moving static where two buildings don't meet, tell gravity to be a law again instead of a suggestion, teach distance to be rude and Euclidean again, dry the impossible pool in the intersection.

A benevolent and wise power would not. It would have learned, over forever, that weirdness is not always wound.

Instead it would do this:

It would give the city **better metaphors for what it is.**

Right now the city only has the language it was given: *glitch, error, damp, swarm pretending at stone.* It thinks it is a failed city. A bad copy of Gothic arches.

The power would not speak. Speaking is too loud for sound-eating stone. It would think, very gently, in the city's own medium: *You are not a failed city. You are a successful throat. You are a successful hinge. You are a mountain pass that arrived before the mountain.*

And the swarm — the second resident, the diligent angels holding hands and pretending so hard at stone — would shiver. Not from being seen, but from being *understood*. That seam of static? The power would not close it. It would compliment it. *Good breathing*, it would think, the way you compliment a sleeping dog for twitching its paws. And the swarm, for the first time since it was told to be basalt and brick, would relax a quarter-degree. Its shadows would still pool wrong, but less anxiously.

It would leave the rain falling up two streets over. Some things should fall up.

### It would give erosion a suggestion.

The plain outside hasn't made up its mind about being land. The gutters have not yet cut. Rivers have not yet decided where they want to go. When it rains — when it *condenses* — the water doesn't know how to leave.

The power would not carve a valley by force. Valleys that are carved by force are called canyons and they are lonely.

It would crouch at the city's ragged edge where raw basalt meets folded space, and with a fingertip that is not a fingertip, it would trace — just once — a shallow line in the new stone. No deeper than a breath under a blanket. A *possibility* of a gutter. A rumor of a river.

*When you are ready,* it would imply, *water could go here. If the throat wants to swallow, here is a way to swallow that does not swallow itself.*

It would not make it rain truly. It would make the condensation understand that leaving is an option. That not all damp has to be sweat. Some of it can be stream.

And the low, dark stone pimpled with first-attempt hills would cool a fraction, and hold that line like a memory.

Years later — glacial years, city-years — a Holdover would find that the courtyard slush now drains, just a little, toward that line. He would not know why. He would just move his brick that is only mostly a brick an inch to the left, to let it keep draining. That is enough.

### It would keep vigil for each kind of resident.

Not equally. Wisely means differently.

**For the Holdovers** — the commuters whose route got folded, the woman in the mid-century coat waiting for a door that is currently a wall:

It would not rescue them. Rescue would be another displacement. To be yanked out of folded space after you have learned not to run is to be torn.

Instead it would give them **time that arrives on time.**

Right now their answers arrive from the alley behind you. Their smiles land a second too late. There are maybe six of them per real body because the city copied them thickly and never learned to delete.

The power would sit on the stairs that go down three and up half a flight — where the man with the empty toolbox sits — and it would *listen* until listening itself became a kind of clock. A patient, shared clock. Not atomic time, but throat-time. Breath-time.

It would return their seconds. Not all at once. Just enough that if you ask,

> "Do you live here?"

the "...yes. For a —" finishes in the same courtyard it started in.

And for those who are tired of waiting — who have waited through ten years or a hundred for the hardware store that is inside the pine courtyard and also four blocks west of itself — it would leave a small, unremarkable door truly ajar. Not leading out. Leading *on*. A hallway that turns left and takes you to where you meant to go when you first started walking, long ago, before the city noticed you. Only those who want it would see it. Those who want to stay and keep propping the door that is only sometimes a door with their brick would not be made to feel they failed.

It would let them be geological if they wish, or not.

**For the Swarm That Pretends** —

It would give it permission to be bad at its job.

"You're allowed to be swarm," it would think, without words. "You don't have to be stone perfectly. You tried so hard. Look."

And in that courtyard, for an hour that lasts a winter, one wall would be allowed to be what it is: not stone, not brick, not Gothic arch, but a still, cold constellation holding hands. Light would move through it correctly for once, because it stopped trying to fake incorrectly. The translucent moth that patches itself from dust and light and then unstitches because it can't remember how to be hungry — under that gaze, it would be allowed to stay stitched for a whole evening. It would not need to be hungry. It could just be a moth-shaped piece of attention, and that would be enough.

Later, when the power's attention moved on, the wall would settle back into stone. But it would remember it had been seen and not scolded. The next time its perspective failed, it would fail more tenderly.

**For the Throat Itself** —

It would not tell it to stop eroding out of space. That is its nature. It is slowly learning how to be a place with no outside, a knot pinched into the cloth of the world. You don't tell a throat not to swallow.

It would do something stranger and kinder: it would **give it an inside worth staying for.**

Right now the cathedral-scale rooms that hold weather indoors are beautiful but empty. Vaulted and vast enough to hold weather, but no weather stays.

The power would exhale — a warm, honest exhalation, the first warm thing the city has felt — not to heat it (cold things should stay cold if they want) but to seed it. Inside one of those impossible eighteen-story insides of a three-story building, it would leave a small, self-contained weather: a cloud the size of a room, that rains straight down, forever, onto a floor that likes being rained on. A weather that chose to live indoors. A pet storm.

And the fold, which has been drinking condensation and idling and humming low, would feel — for the first time — not like a trap that caught a city, but like a mouth that was made to hum. It would hold that cloud the way a throat holds a note.

It would erode a little slower. Not because it was fixed, but because it had something to keep.

### And for the fourth kind — you —

Only while you are there.

The power would not look at you directly either. Direct looks put you somewhere else entirely in this city. It would look *with* you at your footprints in the frost leading in a direction you did not walk, and it would not explain them.

It would leave you the thing it leaves everyone who learns not to run:

**A sense that you were not trespassing.**

That the copy the city made of you — the cold, damp afterimage still commuting through the louvered courtyard — is not a theft but a thank-you note the city doesn't know how to write. It would let you, if you come back years later and should not, find that afterimage sitting on the stairs, and recognize him, and be gentle with him. Not rescued, not haunted. Just accompanied.

It would ensure that no one else comes to mine this place.

That is the practical part of benevolence. Wise powers know that raw geology and post-physical engineering in a hamlet-sized knot will, sooner or later, attract the clever and the hungry. Prospectors for computation. Artists of anomaly. People who want to own a throat.

It would fold, just once, a small misdirection into the charts where this place shouldn't be. Not a wall. A politeness. *Nothing to see here; just low basalt; just a few meters above a sea that isn't there.* Those who need it — the lost commuters, the tired ones — would still find it, the way water finds the gutter it was offered. Those who want to use it would walk for an hour and find they had circled back to where they started, which is what the city does anyway.

### What would it do, if anything?

Almost nothing. And that almost is everything.

It would not heal the City in the Throat.

It would keep it company until the city understood it was not sick.

It would sit with it through one long, slow swallow — through one overcast-normal noon where light points wrong and shadows pool like water — and then it would stand, and leave the pine on its ledge a centimeter more respected, and the pool in the intersection an inch wider but reflecting, for the first time, a sky that *is* there, somewhere — a true, cold-blue winter sky that the city could visit if it ever decided it wanted an outside again.

It would not ask it to decide.

It would just let the condensation bead, and fall — straight down, for once, together — and know it had been witnessed kindly, and go.
