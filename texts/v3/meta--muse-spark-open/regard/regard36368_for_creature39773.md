They would not come down like a god.

A wise and benevolent power would know immediately what this place is: not a broken world waiting to be finished, but a *negotiation* that is working. Floor and Roof do not enclose anything. They only mark where the world begins again. Any power that tried to enclose it - with a dome, a city, a promise of permanence - would be smaller than the place itself.

So at first, they would do nothing that looks like doing.

They would *notice*. Which, here, is not a small thing.

---

### The Noticing

To notice this place correctly you have to stand still long enough to see that nothing is standing still.

You have to feel the Harp thrumming under your palm and understand it is not a string to be plucked but a tendon of a continent that decided to stay open.

You have to taste the bitter-mint sap and know it is antifreeze and blood and library all at once.

You have to learn that the emptiness per volume is not loneliness - it's room for something to drift between pillars without ever having to land.

Most powers that look down see *lack*: lack of heat, lack of soil, lack of people, lack of cities. A wise one would see the opposite: an abundance so precise it has to be sparse. Cold light without shadow. Water without rivers. Roads without roads. A culture that is a route.

They would fall in love, quietly, with the rule that makes it all cohere:

*Nothing that stands still will remain standing long.*

They would recognize that as wisdom, not curse. They would not try to repeal it.

This is the first benevolence: not to fix what is not broken.

---

### The Understanding

If they stayed and watched - and a benevolent power would stay, because benevolence is just attention that has learned patience - they would understand three griefs here that do not need to be solved, only softened:

**1. The Pillars are lonely in the way giants are.**

An Aerostele's canopy is a country pressed against ice. It photosynthesizes alone. It spends a century leaning an inch toward a traveling sunbeam hoping another pillar is leaning too, so their roots can touch in mid-air. When they miss, the root-island aborts, falls, becomes peat. So much yearning for the odds of a handshake.

**2. The Drifters are tired.**

A Veil Moth never lands. It is born floating and dies tangled. To be the thing that *owns* the empty air is magnificent and also exhausting. Every Threader caught in the flash-freeze after a sunbeam passes has to decide to go dormant and hope warmth returns before wind scatters it.

**3. The Weavers are unwitnessed.**

They navigate by groan, they drink by licking leaves, they weave a child's sling between two living roots that will heal and swallow the anchor by next season. They leave carvings with fresh edges: *I was here. Water that way.* They draw *you* before you arrive, and then leave before you can thank them. They have made impermanence into a skill, but skill does not cure the ache of singing low to the Harps alone and having only the Harps hum back.

A wise power would not pity these things. Pity wants to make them warmer, drier, denser, more human. Wisdom knows they like being cold and sparse and strange. What they want is not rescue.

What they want is *company without enclosure.*

---

### What They Would Do, Then

Very little. And everything.

**They would not lift the Roof.** Not even to give them true sky. These people and pillars have learned to live on diffused, bounced, borrowed light. To give them open sun would blind them, burn the Lantern Galls, melt the Floor into a sea that would drown the young rock before it learned to be soft. No.

Instead, they would make one small amendment to the light. In the hours when the sun-shafts sweep like lighthouses, they would tint them - just for a breath - a fraction warmer. Not enough to thaw. Enough that a Threader caught mid-air would feel, for one second, *chosen* by warmth. Enough that the condensation beads on a leaf-edge would linger three heartbeats longer before evaporating, so a child has time to drink.

Benevolence is not rerouting rivers. It's making the drip stay.

**They would not still the turbulence.** They would not stop the leaf the size of a house from falling with a quiet sigh, or silence the *crack* of a seed-pod. That restless, bazaar-like growing is holy.

Instead, they would teach the turbulence to remember. They would run a hand - if a power has a hand - along the young basalt, and where the rock ticks as it gives way to a root, they would leave a memory in the stone. So that when a family returns next year to find yesterday's clear path blocked by a freshly fallen leaf-bridge, the leaf-bridge would already have a slight dip in the middle, a place worn by no feet, waiting for feet. As if the continent had dreamed they would come back.

And when a Harp snaps and its sound takes minutes to arrive as thunder without a storm, they would make the after-hum last a little longer. So the Weavers could tune their low song to it, and know which Harp broke and mourn it properly.

**They would not gather the Weavers into one place.** No city. No nation. The third rule forbids it, and the Weavers have obeyed it with grace.

Instead, they would bless the *route* itself.

They would go to the lean-tos whose walls are still faintly photosynthetic, trying to heal even as people sleep inside them, and they would whisper to the walls: *grow a little slower.* So a basket left half-finished has time to be finished by the next hands that find it. They would go to the loom-frames strung between live roots, and leave the fibers a little springier, so the child's sling holds across the gap between archipelagos without cutting.

They would keep the carvings fresh a winter longer. The stick figure with splayed lines around the head - *good light here for drying* - would not weather as fast. The spiral - *this one moves fast, don't camp* - would deepen, as if re-cut by a careful thumb, though no one was there. So the next walker knows: someone read you. You were not unseen.

Once, perhaps once in a generation, when two Aerosteles have leaned for years toward a meeting that keeps missing - roots reaching through empty air like shy hands - the power would step in as nothing more than a wind.

A cold, still, precise wind that is not wind.

It would hold a Threader-swarm in place for an extra hour. The swarm's faint electrical field would be enough to nudge both root-tips one degree toward each other. Overnight, they would graft.

By morning there would be a new island, dripping with hanging gardens. No Weaver would see it happen. They would only arrive months later and find a place where there was no place, and say, "Oh. Good," and camp there because the damp pocket was excellent, never knowing that the continent had been helped to hold hands.

That is what wise love does. It does not announce.

**Finally - and most importantly - they would become another witness.**

For so long this Sunk Continent has been creation and destruction at architectural scale with no witnesses. The distant white kite crossing a county. The sap pulse visible through translucent bark. The Veil Moth that never lands. All of it happening to itself.

A benevolent power would simply *be there* in the big silence that hurts your ears. Not above the Roof looking in like a scientist, but *between* Floor and Roof, at human height, where breath smokes.

You would feel it not as presence but as absence of loneliness.

You stand on the young, sharp Floor. You put your hand on a humming Harp. And the hum, for once, hums back at the exact frequency of your own blood, and holds it for three seconds too long to be accident.

You find the lean-to at the base of the pillar. Inside, the stones are still warm-ish. The basket is half-finished. And next to the carving of you, the stick figure standing where you now stand - there is a second stick figure added, standing beside it. Shoulder to shoulder. Not leading. Not following.

Just beside.

You look up. A leaf the size of a house decides, with a quiet sigh, to fall - but slower this time. Slow enough that you can step clear without hurrying, and watch it come down like a page turning.

You understand, all at once, that you have been accompanied across this empty continent the size of a sea, and that nothing has asked you to stay.

That is the whole of it.

They would not enclose. They would not end the weather between Floor and Roof.

They would become a little of that weather themselves: a condensation that gathers at the right moment, reflects the glowing Roof perfectly, and offers itself to be drunk from the underside of a leaf.
