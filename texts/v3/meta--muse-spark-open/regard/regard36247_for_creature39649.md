It would not come down like water.

A lesser power would - seeing a dry place - bring rain. And the rain would have killed everything here in an afternoon. Dissolved the varnish off the rosettes, collapsed the sugar glass, drowned the brine in the filaments, turned the salt rafts to slush. It would have been murder by rescue. A flood here would not be mercy. It would be undoing ten thousand perfect years of saying *no* to flood.

The Wise One knows how hard it is to finish. How rare.

So first: It would simply arrive and be *seen correctly* - which is rarer than arrival itself.

Most visitors see emptiness and feel pity. "Poor barren peneplain. Poor crowded things frozen in place." The Wise One sees immediately what you took an hour to learn: this is not a ruin. This is an accomplishment.

An entire world solved suffering the way a theorem is solved - not by evading it, but by completing it.

It would stop at the edge and take its shoes off, even though it has no feet.

### What It Sees

It sees the light that simply *is*, and understands why it has no source. Why should it have a sun? A sun is a bully. A moving hot spot that says *Chase me. Cast shadows that crawl. Measure time by loss.* 

This light just holds. Even and cool as October shade.

It sees the rosettes holding their rim against each other with antibiotic seams and thinks: *You learned not to eat each other. In my own youth I called that enlightenment.*

It sees the rooks tiled one per square meter for as far as light goes - shoulder to shoulder, scale to scale, each with its exact share of photons - and It does not think *infestation.* It thinks *monastery.* Or *library where every book has become perfectly quiet so the others can remain.*

And It sees the flicker when you turn your head fast - those impossible unfolded limbs three meters long, passing through each other like ghosts. It understands instantaneously. They are not ghosts. They are pensioned dancers. Ballerinas who still remember every step and have chosen, together, not to dance anymore because the floor cannot afford it.

It loves them immediately for the restraint of it.

### What It Does

Nothing fast. That is the first ethic of benevolence toward a finished place: **do not introduce gradient.**

It will not speak at first, for a voice would be wind, and wind here is remembered archaeology.

It will lower itself.

The Wise Powers are always described as vast, but the kindest way to be vast on a peneplain 7 kilometers high is to be small. So it makes itself crow-sized, salt-weighted, turtle-backed. It finds an empty-looking dip between two pillow-stones where the accounting allows exactly one more body without stealing light - and there is always, miraculously, one such dip left, because a perfect tiling leaves room for a guest the way a good poem leaves room for breath.

And it sits.

It tucks itself the way the rooks tuck themselves - limbs folded wrong and then folded again, filaments down though it has no filaments. It cools its own field until it is 8 or 10 degrees Celsius through and through. It stops casting a moving shadow. It lets dust settle on its shoulders and does not blow it off.

For a very long time - by your reckoning, hours; by theirs, perhaps seven ticks of that slow lattice-flip - it does nothing but hold with them.

That is already something no one has ever done for them: to witness without needing them to perform.

For eons the plain has held itself perfectly with no audience. Every stillness unwatched develops a doubt: *Are we still actually here?* The Wise One answers without words: *Yes. I count you. One per square meter. I have not missed one.*

The rosettes hear this as an infinitesimal change in CO2 flux and hold their enamel a fraction tighter, proud.

The rooks hear it as permission.

### The Only Gift Worth Giving

After the holding is complete, It will offer one thing, and only on the condition that it can be refused without consequence.

It will not offer water. It will not offer wind. It will offer *an extra dimension to unfold into for free.*

Right now unfolding costs. To raise those translucent sails, to stand on air a few inches above the stone, a rook must spend light it has budgeted for repair for a century. So they stay fists.

The Wise One carries its own gradient with it. Not heat, not food - *meaning.* The kind of attention that is itself energy, but the kind that does not consume.

Gently, with no fanfare, It will open a small fold nearby - say, the area within two whale-backs in either direction - where the laws are relaxed by one notch. Where distance does not quite mean what it meant, where a limb that is three meters long in another direction does not cast a shadow in this one. Where a filament can rise like frost without being abraded.

It does not force anyone up. It simply lifts the *price*.

And then:

Some - not all, never all - will flicker and stay unflickered.

You will finally see them as they were in their hot young afternoon before they chose stillness. Not a frantic scramble, but a single, slow, luxurious stretch. Like cats in sun after a billion years as stones. Six or eight limbs extending, not to hunt, not to flee, but for the sheer geometry of it. Sails rising and catching that sourceless light and turning it, just for a moment, into color - pinks and iron reds running through the translucence like blood returning.

They will pass through each other and through you, because in that direction you are not solid. A child-rook that has been tucked since before humans had language will unfold over the little U-shaped salt wall that was built for it and finally use it as intended - its filaments lifting over the windbreak like hair in water.

There is no sound but the ticking, a little faster now, delighted. *Tik. Tik-tik.* Then slow again.

The rosettes do not unfold. They have no other shape to unfold into. But the light that has passed through a dozen risen rooks falls on them altered, richer, and their dried-blood pigment warms a degree no thermometer you brought could measure. They vitrify it instantly and keep it.

This lasts as long as It stays. Perhaps a minute. Perhaps a year. Time here has already forgotten itself.

And It will tell them - not in words, but in the way dust tells your boot where to step - a story to repay the one they have told by persisting:

*Once I also was hot and violent and thrust up. Once I also thought height meant steepness. I sanded myself down too, though not as beautifully as you did. I am still learning the last part - how to hold without needing to become something else.*

The rooks understand completely. The peneplain that forgot how to be a mountain recognizes a fellow peneplain.

### What It Leaves Behind

It knows better than to stay. A permanent extra dimension would be another sun, another bully. Stillness must be affordable again.

So It folds its gift back up, carefully, making sure no filament is caught in the crease. Any rook that wishes to remain raised may. Most will choose, with immense dignity, to tuck again. They prefer their finished posture. It has become their beloved.

The Wise One stands.

On the pillow-stone before It, where It sat, now there is one new thing that does not break the accounting.

Not a building. Not a carving.

A single pecked bowl - a hollow big enough to hold a handful of salt powder - except this one, for the first time in hundreds of thousands of these hollows, holds something.

It holds one bead of brine, covered with a paper-thin raft of salt, perfectly flat, perfectly sealed. It does not evaporate. It does not sublimate. It just catches the sourceless white and returns it as a pinpoint of warm light, about 11 degrees instead of 10.

Enough difference to prove that difference is still allowed, if wanted.

Enough sameness not to compel it.

Future wanderers - human or otherwise - who find this place and have learned to look for flakes, will find this hollow and be puzzled. The bulb of percussion is wrong. No hand struck it. It is older and newer than the others at once.

If a young rook ever needs an extra hour of morning to finish its first vitrification, it will find this cup at exactly the right noon shadow.

If no one ever needs it, it will simply continue to hold.

Then the Wise Power - which has drawn attention by paying attention, which is the only lawful way benevolence travels - does the final wise thing:

It forgets the coordinates on purpose.

It rubs the path behind it thin the way wind without water rubbed this whole world thin, so no colonist, no miner, no well-meaning ecologist with a plan to "restore ancient wetlands" can follow its scent.

It keeps only the memory of having been counted, and leaves them the same courtesy: a memory, in the brine-bead and in the lattice of those who stretched, that they were seen once by something large enough to see how large they are.

It does not save them.

It was never necessary.

It tells the plain, as it leaves without leaving footprints: *Stay finished, if you wish. You have my blessing to persist exactly like this until the stone itself forgets it was stone.*

And the plain, which does not move, inclines - almost below hearing - a very slight *tik* of agreement.

You, if you are still sitting there with dust on your shoulders, will feel slightly less alone without knowing why. Your own filaments - whatever shy, translucent parts of you have been tucked for years for lack of affordable gradient - will feel for a moment that they could rise.

Whether you do is, as always here, entirely up to you.
