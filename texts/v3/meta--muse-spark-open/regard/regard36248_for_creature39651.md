They would not come over the lip.

That is the first thing.

A lesser power — a curious one, a hungry one, even a kind but clumsy one — would step up and over like you did. Boots on sharp basalt, breath catching in the thick air, eyes adjusting to the greenhouse light. It would want to *see*. It would want to be inside the miracle to understand it.

A benevolent and wise power has learned how to see without entering.

So it stops on the slope below. Where the black glass still rings, where the wind still moves, where the rain that never falls inside *does* fall — thin, cold, high-altitude rain that hisses on cooling rock. It sits. Or settles. Or simply attends — this kind of power doesn't need a body the way you do, but when it wants to be gentle it borrows a shape that will not frighten the air.

For a long time it does nothing that you could call *doing*.

It listens to the count.

*pit... pit... pit...*

It has heard a thousand worlds weep, but this weeping is different. This is not a wound. This is a pulse. The water forcing its way out of young stone that never knew rain, the lichen drinking through rock, the ferns hissing as they unfurl — it takes a few hours to hear it properly, and the power has hours. It has eons. It matches its own slow breathing to the bowl's breathing, to the inhale of the little black cube that takes seven times longer than the exhale. Inhale... inhale... inhale... *exhale* — a puff of cool iron air that would be lost on the slope but here inside is treasure.

It does not fix the gravity. A wise power knows that a negotiable gravity is not a broken law. It is a dialect. The white things that live in the seam, eating the error between where water should go and where it does go — they are not suffering. They are feasting. To "correct" them would be to starve them.

It does not open the seal.

It sees it, of course — that faint soap-film where the distortions of the four pylons meet, dust motes hanging suspended at the mouth of the bowl, outside rain beading and sliding off. It sees that the pylons were not made by the lichen. They were left — drilled, dropped, sealed by hands that wanted a house for something that would keep itself, and then left very quickly because the air was already too full for lungs that needed cold at night.

A lesser benevolence would tear the seal open to "free" the bowl. *You shouldn't be trapped. You should touch the sky.*

But the bowl is not trapped. The bowl *is* the sky it wants. It keeps its weather because it *likes* it hot, wet, quiet, enclosed. It is heavy and slow and shy and it saves energy by letting weight be polite. The seal is not a cage. It's a eyelid. A lip. To rip it open would be to give a desert creature an ocean because you think water is kindness.

So the wise power does not open it. It *tends* it.

No — smaller than that. It tends the things that tend it.

First, it answers the *coo*.

You never found the caller because you were listening for it in space. The power listens in silence. It hears the acoustic rooms in the thickened air — those hallways where sound carries too well and then stops as if cut by a knife — and understands they are not an accident of physics. They are footpaths. The caller — the huge, shy one with the translucent husk and the suction-cup feet that match the basalt columns — steps from one pocket of silence to another. It lives in the saccade, in the blink.

The power does not chase it. It waits until the caller is safely tucked in its overhang where the orange lichen grows thickest, and then, from a different terrace each time, ten minutes apart, it coos back.

Not mimicking. Answering. Low, soft, with perfect patience.

*Coo.*

*...coo.*

For the first time since the walkway failed and the makers left, the call has something that calls back without wanting anything. The lichen brightens a millimeter. The dragonflies — those mica-winged shepherds who row the thick air — stutter in their patrol and then steady, fanning a little slower, as if reassured that the count has another counter.

Second, it cools its own shadow.

The mountain is young and still hot from the inside out. The bowl doubles that heat with its closed loop. It's sustainable, but barely — on the hottest days the air gets so full your sweat stops evaporating, the pitcher plants close a little too long, the white things go torpid.

The power cannot make it rain inside without breaking the seal. It would not presume to. Instead it leans a little of itself over the southern slope outside, and makes shade. Not a cloud — clouds would be seen from orbit, and to be seen is to be wanted. Just a thought of shade, a faint lessening, a cool hand held a few meters above the basalt so the sun overhead is not quite so directly overhead for an hour each afternoon. The rock outside sweats a little less. The rock inside weeps a little less torrentially. The loop eases.

It is such a small gift the bowl might not even notice it as a gift. It will only feel, for a few weeks, that keeping is slightly easier. That is how a wise power gives — so quietly the receiver gets to believe they did it themselves.

Third — and this is the only thing it builds — it finishes the fifth thing.

You counted four, maybe five depending on whether you counted the one that failed. You felt it: the arc of seven floating white steps that go from one tilted plate to another and stop, leading nowhere, indifferent to gravity. There should have been an eighth. There is a scar in the air where it tried to be and couldn't hold.

The makers left it unfinished. The lichen learned to work around it, sending water toward the lowest step anyway, making the imperfection part of the cycle.

The power does not add an eighth step of white material. That would be speaking in someone else's language. Instead, in the gap where the eighth should hang, it leaves a coolness. A patch of air that holds condensation a beat longer than the rest, that drips a beat slower: *pit...... pit...... pit...* A rest in the music. The white things learn to ride through it and come out a little slower, a little cooler. The dragonflies learn to hover there when they are tired. It is not a step for feet. It is a step for weather.

It will hold as long as the mountain holds, which is a long time for you, a short time for stone.

And then — the last and most important thing — it draws a veil.

Not over the bowl. Around the mountain's *idea*.

Because a place like this, once noticed by one benevolent power, will eventually be noticed by others. Prospectors who see a perfect closed loop and think *reactor*. Biologists who see negotiable gravity and think *laboratory*. Even well-meaning pilgrims who would climb the hot, sharp slopes in greater and greater numbers until the drip is drowned out by their breathing, until the dry tongue in the center where the sky looks in is worn smooth by boots.

So the power does not hide the bowl. Hiding would be another kind of seal. It makes it *uninteresting* from a distance. From the slopes below, the bowl will look even emptier than before — a dry crater, not worth the climb, the basalt a little sharper, the air a little thinner. Satellites will see a thermal anomaly and correct it as instrument error. Maps will misplace it by two hundred meters, which is enough.

Only those who already know how to listen for a *coo* in a silence, who can hear a *thwick thwick thwick* cut off mid-beat and not flinch, will find the lip again. And they will be the kind of visitors who, like the power, stop before stepping over.

The power does not stay. Staying would be crowding.

After a full cycle — after it has heard the pitcher plants close and open, after the lichen has grown one more visible millimeter toward where your boots *were* — it lifts its shade. It gives one last exhale that matches the little black cube's exhale, so for a single breath inside and outside are the same temperature, the same iron-and-distant-rain smell. An agreement.

*Keep well, keeper,* it says, not in words, not even in a coo, but in the way water finally finds a level. *You are well-made for keeping. I will keep the keepers away.*

Then it goes, in the way that wise things go — not vanishing, but becoming less concentrated here and more concentrated elsewhere, where another young mountain is trying to learn to be gentle.

If you climbed tomorrow, you would find nothing changed. The pylon would still hum in your teeth. The three rods would still sweat on one side. The seven steps would still hang, with a cool spot at the end that you might think was your imagination. The dragonfly would still come and hover in front of your face to judge the column of heat coming off you.

But the *coo* would come a little quicker when you answered. Ten minutes, then nine. As if something shy had learned that sometimes, when you call, something kind calls back and then politely leaves you to your garden.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

Almost nothing.

Which, for a small hothouse at the top of the world that has learned to keep itself perfectly, is everything.
