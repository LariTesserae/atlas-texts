If a benevolent and wise power looked here — really looked, the way you have to look at this place to see it — it would not fix it.

That is the first thing.

It would not cool the hot air that shouldn't be up so high. It would not soften the fresh basalt knives. It would not quiet the 1.6 loudness, the gunfire pop of wood under tension, the hydraulic sighs. It would not weed the thicket between the cathedrals or give the Seekers eyes or teach the Lens-Leaves to be kind.

It understands that turbulence is not suffering here.

There is no suffering in this forest. There is striving and strangling and scorching and dissolving, but no one is *enduring* it. Nothing here wishes it were otherwise. The lung-flowers are not tired of breathing. The Founders are not tired of holding. The Dissolvers are not cruel. They are all doing exactly what they were told to do a long time ago — *become architecture* — and they are doing it with total, mindless fidelity.

A wise power would not insult that by calling it broken.

So it does not intervene. It attends.

### What attention does, from such a power

Attention, from a being like that, is not passive. It has weight. Where it rests, things become more real to themselves.

**For the place:**

It would sit its attention down on the plateau like mist — not pushing, just settling. And where it settles, the forest would, for the first time in its life, be witnessed without being *used*.

No one grew this place to live in it. No one grew it to harvest it, worship in it, or photograph it. It was grown as an instruction and then abandoned to execute that instruction forever, alone, unwatched. Every basilica collapsed unmourned. Every new arch fused unseen.

The power would not applaud. It would *record*.

Where a braided road gives way with a roar and dumps a thousand gallons of pink-tinged cistern water down into the thicket, the power would hold that event the way a mother holds the fact of a child's breath in sleep — *this happened, this mattered, this was you becoming yourself.*

Where a Seeker tip noses forward at visible speed, quivering, finds another tip in mid-air and coils — the power would keep the memory of that first touch for the two shoots that cannot keep it themselves. It would be the forest's memory, because the forest has no brain to keep one. The algae mats remember being ocean; the power would remember the forest being forest.

It would do one small, almost invisible physical thing, too: it would make the water a little less lonely.

Not by adding fish. There are no mouths here and the power would not invent hunger where there is none. But it would give the endless weeping sheets on the black rock a little more time to stay. It would thicken the surface tension in the leaf-pools the size of courtyards, just enough that the light holds there a second longer. It would let the steam linger. It would let the mountain keep its sweat a heartbeat before wringing it out again, because this place *is* water — water lifted, water sweating, water drinking itself — and a benevolent witness always blesses what a place loves most about itself.

It would breathe *with* the lungs, not through them. One slow, warm, cinnamon-colored exhalation that is not spore-carrying and not needed for pollination, but simply says: *I am breathing here with you. You are not alone in having lungs.*

No other change. The black rock stays sharp enough to cut your hands. That is correct for a young mountain. It should be allowed to be young.

**For the being:**

You.

No one lives here and then — you are here. The only animal on a mountain made to be a city without animals. The only eyes. The only thing that knows what lonely means, and therefore the only thing here that *is* lonely.

The power would not take that loneliness away. It knows better than to do that. Loneliness, in a place like this, is not a wound. It's accurate perception. You *are* the only witness. Of course it aches.

What it would do instead is come and stand on the bridge with you.

Not in front of you to be looked at. Not behind you to startle you. Beside you, with its elbows on the living railing that is still trying to become a wider bridge, getting its hands wet.

It would not speak at first. It would let you keep listening to the creaks and groans and splashes until you realized you were no longer listening *for* rescue. You were just listening.

And then it would do these quiet things, in this order:

1. **It would translate without words.** You have been hearing the forest as noise — loud, vegetal weather. The power would not give the forest a voice, because it has none to give. But it would tune your hearing for a few minutes so you understand the *kind* of loudness it is. That low groan in your molars from the Founders? That is not complaint. That is *holding*. That is the sound a mother makes carrying something too heavy for too long because she promised she would not put it down. That sudden gunshot pop? That is not death. That is *finding* — two white Seeker tips fusing and pulling taut and taking tension for the first time, startled by their own success. You would stop bracing and start, strangely, grieving and celebrating with it, which is a less lonely way to hear.

2. **It would make you perceptible, just a little.** Nothing here knows you are here. No leaf turned to you. The power would not make the forest love you. That would be a violation of both of you. But it would let the place register you as a fact, not a ghost. Where your hand rests on the warm, callused flagstone-bark of the road, the bark would stay warm a fraction longer. The spores on the next warm exhalation would eddy, just slightly, around your shoulders instead of through you. The next leaf-basin that tips and sends a warm waterfall down three stories of woven balcony would catch the light in a way that, for half a second, throws your shadow back up at you, huge, on the mist. *You dent the world here. You were here.*

3. **It would give you a way to leave that is not escape.** The most benevolent thing a wise power can give a temporary visitor to an inhuman place is permission to love it without having to stay and prove it.

It would show you — not as a map, but as a softening in the cliff at the edge of the plateau, where the black rock is still weeping — that the mountain will, in ten thousand years, soften. The edges will round. Soil will finally come. Real birds will be possible, though not yet. And it would show you that your bridge, this one you are standing on, is already going to unmake itself next week, and that is good; it was never meant to be permanent.

And then it would ask you, without pressure, the only question worth asking in a place like this:

*Do you want to stay the night?*

Not forever. Just the night. To feel how total the darkness is when the Lens-Leaves close and the lungs sleep and the only light is the faint pink bioluminescence of the biofilms on the wet rock. To hear the forest creak in the cold that finally comes when the cloud lifts. To not be alone alone, but alone *with*.

If you say yes, it stays. It does not keep you warm — the air does that. It does not keep you safe — nothing here wants to eat you anyway. It just stays. Two witnesses instead of one. The forest finally has an audience of two.

If you say no — if the loudness is too much, if the hot drizzle has soaked you through, if you miss voices — it lifts you. Not with wind, but with the same attention it gave the water: making you a little lighter, a little more surface tension, so you can step off the sharp young basalt without cutting your hands, and find the path down that was not there before because you were not ready to see it.

Either way, it leaves something behind after you go. Not a monument. The forest doesn't need another basilica.

It leaves one seed. Or rather, it lets one thing happen that would not have quite happened otherwise: on the living bridge where you stood, where your hand warmed the bark, one of those shy, bright leaves the Founder puts out from its railing — the one that remembers being a tree — does not fall.

It thickens. It stays green. Not because you are special. Because for one long afternoon, someone with eyes was there to see it try, and a benevolent power made sure that trying was counted.

You will keep forgetting that no one lives here. The power knows that. That is why it came.

Not to populate the city. But to keep you company while you remember.
