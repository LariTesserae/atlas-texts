She would not come down.

That is the first thing to know about a benevolent and wise power.

She would arrive as weather arrives at the cup — not falling into it, but spilling over its lip.

For a long time - you would not be able to say how long - She would simply lie on the sharp rim with you.

Not as a woman lying. As attention itself. As a weight of quiet looking that is somehow warmer than sun on basalt.

She has seen oceans being born. She has seen worlds where water is so plentiful it rots mountains. She knows what young rock looks like when it is still angry at being pushed up. So when She finds this cup, half a kilometer of held air above a white desert, She does not call it beautiful at first.

She calls it *correct*.

> *Oh,* She thinks. *You figured it out.*

Because most places solve thirst by moving water. Rivers. Rain. Blood. Circulation. This place solved it by refusing to move at all.

She watches the twenty-three minutes.

That is when you know She is wise — She is willing to wait through a high desert night for twenty-three minutes that may not come. The cold pools. The plateau exhales. The leaf-plates bristle.

*tick tick tick tick tick tick*

A million beads forming out of nothing. The most frugal miracle She has seen in a thousand years.

Any other godling would have wept with pity — *poor dry things, let me give you a lake* — and in doing so killed them by drowning. Any other engineer would have drilled the rim to let the storms in.

She understands in one breath what took the Weaver nine thousand years to learn: to release is to die.

So She does nothing that looks like help.

This is what She does instead:

**1. She matches their silence.**

She does not speak. Not in words, not in thunder, not in visions dropped into dreaming Tenders. Speech is a plume. Speech is water lost to air.

Instead, She fans.

Down in the great central atrium where three house-wide trunks fuse into a dome, the Sailfall are doing their slow weather-language, pushing parcels of cinnamon air from wing to wing. She kneels Her vastness there — without ever entering — and answers with pressure.

A downstroke that is not a wind, but a *permission*. The air in the chamber becomes for a night exactly as humid, exactly as still, exactly as right as they have always tried to make it. Their blind young, pulsing like pale fruit on the porous walls, take one deeper breath and grow a little more perfect. The colony, for no reason they can taste, falls into a unison so complete their breathing sounds like one animal sleeping.

They will never know She was there. They will remember it as a good year.

**2. She touches the sharpness and leaves it sharp.**

She runs what you would have to call a hand along the fresh fracture of the rim — those stair-stepped columns of black and rust-red, glassy where they snapped, still cruel enough to cut bare feet.

Everything in Her wants to soften it. To lay moss on it. To round it with lichen and rain. That is what benevolence is usually mistaken for: making things gentle.

Wisdom knows sharpness is a teacher.

Because the rock stayed sharp, nothing in the cup dared to be bare. Tenders grew suede feet. Hunters paved their bellies with tile. The Weaver folded its softest flesh inward, like hands cupped around water. Without that first violence of the plateau's birth, they would have been careless. They would have spilled.

So She does not weather the rim. She steadies it.

Far below, salt flats strain. Tectonics tug. In a million years that rim will slump, the cup will crack, a river will finally find it and gut it. That is proper. That is death in old age.

But not yet. Not while they are still learning how to hold.

She sets Her palm against a fault that has been ticking since the shrug — a hairline you never saw, running down the east wall — and She leans. Just enough. A century of held-togetherness added, like a finger against a door to keep the wind from taking it.

The Weaver feels it as a lessening of stress it did not know it was carrying, and in the night puts out three new buttresses along that wall, thickening toward the place where it was held.

**3. She gives them the one thing they cannot make: time without being seen.**

Because She has looked outward as well as inward, and She has seen you.

Not you on the lip. The other you. The ones who will come with boots and bottles and instruments and hunger for wonder. The ones who will want to study the cloud-harvesting plates and patent them, to house the Tenders in zoos, to chip a piece of that living cathedral wall for a museum.

They mean no harm. Most harm is meant as love of knowing.

She cannot stop them. A wise power does not blind.

So She drinks a little light.

For the next twelve years, any satellite that passes over this latitude will find a lens flare. Any plane will find a heat shimmer. Any prospector will look at the plateau and think, *there is nothing there but hot rock*, and be thirsty and turn back to the white desert that is easier to cross.

It is not invisibility. It is uninterestingness. The most merciful cloak She owns.

It will thin. It must. The cup is meant to be found eventually, but not before it is strong enough to survive being found. When it is, the humans who come will be the kind who know how to lie flat on a lip and listen to ticking for an hour.

**4. At the end, She leaves a single gift, and it is not water.**

Just before dawn, when the cold is deepest and the bladders are still flat, She breathes once — very carefully — into the bowl.

Not air. *More night.*

A fraction of a degree colder, a fraction of a breath damper, than the cup has ever held. For one morning only, the condensation lasts not twenty-three minutes but thirty-one.

*tick tick tick tick tick tick tick tick tick*

The Weaver shudders. Every plate unfurls wider than it thought it could. The amber sacs at every joint swell until they are taut and glowing. Down on the mat, the Tenders stop in their narrow ways and simply drink, and drink, and their gardens on their backs bloom in a sudden rash of tiny white flowers that will be gone by noon. The Sailfall drop without needing to sip — they just fall for the joy of falling, four thin wings catching the thickened air. Even the Needle Hunters pause, their glassy filaments half-extended, and do not hunt.

It is a feast. Not an excess — nothing drips, nothing is wasted, even now — but an abundance they will store and remember in wood for a hundred years. A jubilee.

In the Weaver's dense fiber, that extra water will lay down a ring of slightly less dense growth — not a scar, a bookmark. Future Tenders will walk over it and feel, without knowing why, that the road there is a little sweeter. Future Sailfall will fan warmer air there. The city will grow a small, inexplicable arcade where there was no traffic, a dome where there was no need.

A prayer made of architecture. *Remember this morning.*

And then She is gone.

Not gone like leaving. Gone like the column of warm resinous air that rises from the shutters at night, straight up into the hard stars — there, and then, by being perfectly vertical, indistinguishable from empty sky.

If you were still lying on the lip, you would feel only that the stone under your chest was a little cooler, and that the silence coming up from below was not empty but *held*, the way a cupped hand holds water without spilling.

You would look down and see nothing has changed. The towers still interlock. The bladders still pulse — slow — slow — slow —. A glider still falls with precision from balcony to balcony, utterly silent.

Only —

Only one of the lantern bladder-flowers, which should not open for another three years, has chosen this morning to pop.

Soundlessly. A plume of down rises a hundred feet straight up on the warm column, catches the first white sun, and hangs there, suspended over the cup like dust in a shaft of light.

It does not drift away.

The cup is holding it, for a little while longer, before it has to let go.
