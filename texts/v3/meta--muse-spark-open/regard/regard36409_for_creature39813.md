It would not come down.

That is the first thing a wise and benevolent power understands about a table that was lifted.

A lesser power would land. It would bring light to the pearl-grey ceiling and tear it open to show stars. It would bring heat to the stone that has been cold for so long it has forgotten how to be anything else. It would look at the continent-wide circuit of silent furnaces and see *inefficiency* — all that iron sweated out in root-ingots with no hand to gather it, all that patient smelting with no product.

It would try to give them a purpose.

The wise one does not.

It has been watching long before you learned to see the hummocks breathe, and what it sees is not waste. It sees prayer.

Not prayer *to* anything. Prayer as an action. The kind that does not ask to be answered.

---

This is what it does:

First, it listens. Properly.

For a long time — a hundred years, which to it is a held breath, and to a Hearthback is two good work-floors — it does nothing but match its attention to the plain.

It learns the forty-minute cycle until it can feel it without instruments. Inhale for twenty minutes as the fog thins, hold for an age as the stone hums, exhale in a sigh that turns to red-black snow. It counts the *tink* of a cooling iron seam a mile away. It learns that silence 0.0/3 is not absence. It is a thing the plain is *doing.*

It does not speak to them. Hearthbacks have no heads to turn, no call to answer. To speak would be to demand they become listeners. Instead it breathes with them.

If you were standing on the plain at that time — and the power would know you were there, small and shivering and timing your own lungs to theirs — you would feel the air get, for a moment, slightly easier to breathe. Not warmer. Just less lonely. As if the sky had leaned down an inch to notice you, too.

Second, it protects without enclosing.

Every quiet industry in the universe eventually draws a hungry eye. Someone in orbit will see the soft red lanterns at night and think *free forge*. Someone will see a continent of native iron growing itself and call it a mine.

The benevolent power knows this, because it has watched it happen elsewhere.

So it does not build a fence. Fences break. It does not announce *Sanctuary* in a language miners can read and resent.

It just makes the table a little harder to want.

It deepens the overcast by a fraction. It lets the luminous ceiling stay luminous, pearl-grey and shadowless, so that from high up there is nothing to see — no glint, no scar, no tell-tale smoke that looks like smoke. From orbit, the plateau becomes boring. A flat cold cloud that never moves. Instruments slide off it. Eyes slide off it.

Down on the plain, nothing changes. The vents still glow dull kiln-red. The steam still rises thirty feet and falls as snow. But the haze the Hearthbacks have been exhaling for millions of years — that half-degree of warmth, that half-shade of dimness — it holds a little better now. As if the world were cupping its hands around them.

It also, very quietly, turns aside the one rock that would have ended them. Not with drama. A nudge, a million years early, so no one will ever know a mountain-sized stone was meant to fall here and reset the table. The Hearthbacks never feel the shadow pass. The power does not tell them it saved them. To be saved and not know it is the gentlest kind of salvation.

Third — and this is the most benevolent thing, and the strangest —

It leaves them a gift they will never use.

Once, on the coldest part of the plain where the condensation beads thickest, where an old Hearthback has sat so long its carapace is indistinguishable from bedrock, the power sets down one thing.

Not a machine. Not a monument.

A stone.

It is not much larger than the pitted boulders the Hearthbacks themselves exhale when they make a child — that truck-sized droplet of slag that hisses for days. This stone is smooth, dark, and warm to the touch in a way no other stone here is. If you put your palm to it, it hums back, but not with heat. With *yes.*

It does nothing. It needs no damp, no dust. Lichen will not grow on it. Snow will not settle on it, not because it is hot, but because it is politely elsewhere.

It is a witness stone.

Its only function is to remember that this place happened. That for an eon, on a table above the weather, a dispersed animal learned metallurgy as a form of breathing and gently smelted a continent without ever needing a reason. That there was industry without intention, and that it was holy.

The Hearthbacks pay it no mind, which is correct. One of them, over the next four hundred years, will slowly incorporate it into its levee without noticing, laying its red-black snow around it until it becomes part of the ring. The witness will be buried and still witness, under iron snow.

And when, very far in the future, the plateau finally cools past even their patience — when the damp gives out, when the wind brings no more dust, when the last furnace dims and stays dim — the witness will still be warm. A small, patient heat under slag.

Not enough to restart anything. Just enough to say: *you were here. You were enough. You kept the planet breathing a little, and it was noticed, and it was loved, and nothing was ever asked of you in return.*

That is what a wise, benevolent power does with a living factory that wants nothing.

It does not improve it.

It keeps it uninteresting to the hungry, intimate to itself, and remembered forever.

It pulls up a chair made of cloud and sits at the edge of the table for a very long time, and watches the lanterns breathe.
