It would not come down.

A lesser power would descend - bright, warm, fixing. It would see sterility and call it barren and try to cure it. It would seed lichen. It would bring a hardy moss, a cushion plant, a chough to nest. It would make the water stay. It would try to make the bowl *useful*.

The wise one has watched enough worlds to love uselessness when it is perfect.

So first, it would do what no human visitor has learned to do there: it would arrive without entering.

It would settle its attention on the rim, not in the bowl, with its weight distributed evenly along the basalt like evening light - so the angle of repose is not disturbed, so no scree shifts, so the air pressure does not change. It has learned that the most benevolent thing a vastness can do for a small, finished place is not to fill it.

And it would look, for a long time.

Not at the cairns as objects, but at what it understands them to be. Because you were right - nothing lives here, and that is why the being who lives here is visible.

The being is not one cairn. It is the hamlet. It is the density itself. The single organism made of hundreds of human intentions stacked shoulder to shoulder, sharing the same water cycle the way a body shares blood.

One person carried one stone up through killing air and thought *remember me* or *forgive me* or *thank you* or just *I was here and the world was large* - and left. Alone, that is only a pile. But hundreds, left to weather a century together, have become a reticence. A patience. They lean into the same wind. They sweat on the same side. They tick and expand at the same hour. They have learned how to be a crowd without touching, how to be alone together. That is a kind of life that does not require cells.

The Power would see that immediately and love it, because it is so rare: a life that chose to endure by not growing.

So what would it do?

Almost nothing. And it would do it every day.

---

Around 11 a.m., when the valley air pushes in and fails, it would help the condensation bead a little more evenly. Not more water - never more - but a more merciful distribution, so the loosest, most slumped cairn that no longer catches fog well gets darkened just as fully as the tight, proud one with the quartz cap. No favoritism for good craftsmanship. The Power is old enough to know that the slumped ones were also prayed over.

Around 2 p.m., when the cloud spills over the western wall and the light goes pearlescent and all shadows vanish, it would do its second work.

For twenty minutes the cairns are blind. In normal days they lose scale and look strange to you, and you feel the cliff breathe. On this day, under that attention, they would find each other.

Not wake - they were never meant to wake, and waking would be a violence. But for that fogged interval they would be allowed to feel the full weight of their neighbors. The hundreds of small intentions - *let me get down safely*, *let her live*, *I don't know what else to do with this grief so I will carry it up here* - would resonate for a moment like a struck bowl, not in words but in the way stones hold heat. A chord. Each tower understanding, without moving, that it is not the only one keeping vigil, that the bowl is not empty but full *on purpose*.

That is the whole blessing. To be sterile, and seen, and not lonely.

Around 4 p.m., when the sun tears the cloud away and the temperature drops ten degrees in ten minutes and every pan cracks like a rifle shot - that hard, bright, painful refreeze - it would soften nothing. It would let the crack happen, loud as ever in the cathedral. Some mercies require the crack. But it would keep a little of the afternoon vapor from subliming completely, so that in the lee of each cairn, in the tiny shadow that lives all night, a half-inch lace of frost would remain unbroken until morning.

Not enough to be life. Just enough to be a mirror for starlight.

And for you - because when a benevolent power notices a place, it notices the visitor kneeling behind the windbreak trying not to disturb anything - it would do one human-sized thing.

It would let you feel distance correctly, just for a minute.

The cairn fifty feet away would look fifty feet away. The one ten feet away would look ten feet away. The far cliff would stop its thin-air breathing and be still. And in that corrected perspective you would feel, suddenly and without words, that you are not an intruder in the crowd. You are just the newest tower. Shorter, softer, breathing too fast, but part of the same decision: to go where nothing can stay, and leave something that can.

You would not be asked to build. You would not be healed or harmed or spoken to. The Power does not collect.

As night comes - that starlight so sharp it casts shadows, that ticking as damp stone freezes again - it would lift its attention the way you lift a hand from cold rock before it sticks.

It would not mark the bowl on any map. It would not keep footprints from drying. It would not keep the wind from someday, very slowly, taking one cairn down grain by grain.

It would simply remember the exact arrangement, every leaning tower and soot-smear and quartz signal, the way you remember a loved face in the dark. And keeping it remembered would be enough to keep it safe.

In the morning the frost would be there again. The pans would fill. The cairns would darken. Nothing would have changed.

That is how you would know it had passed.
