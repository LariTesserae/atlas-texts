If anything had ever truly seen it — not scanned it for minerals, not mapped it for cables, not passed over it counting species like inventory —

If a *benevolent and wise power* turned its attention down here, all the way down to where pressure becomes a condition of existence and light is a lie the world above tells itself —

It would not come down like light.

That's what we would do. We, who love light. We would come with blinding white shafts from heaven, with warmth, with rescue. We would try to lift them up. We would crack the black like an egg and pour sun onto a plain that has never wanted it.

A wise one wouldn't.

It would stop at the edge of darkness and let the darkness teach it how to see first. For a long time it would just... be there. Unseen. Unfelt. Because to be wise at this depth is to understand that intrusion is violence even when you mean it as love.

This plain has finished happening. These nodules grew a millimeter per thousand years around a shark's tooth no one remembers. These bacterial mats are older than forests. These sharks live four hundred years because they have nowhere to be. To hurry into that kind of time with human-haste help would be obscene.

So the Power would wait.

It would feel the currents first — those invisible rivers the size of countries roaring silent across the abyss, scouring, tumbling, hanging silt for hours. It would feel the faint, endless *tick... tick...* of iron stones knocking divots into each other once an hour across a nation-sized floor. It would learn that this is not silence. This is just a language too slow and too low for anything hasty to hear.

And then — only then — it would notice the builders.

Not with a searchlight. With patience. With a dimming of itself until it was as dark as they are. A foot-across bodies of living rust-black, eyeless, shuttering amber lanterns grown on their own backs. Feeling their way between nodules with shovel hands. Spending an hour to push a stone an inch and calling that progress. Sending a two-second glow across twenty meters of absolute night — *I am here. Food collects when the north current runs. Follow me when the turbidity comes.*

A people whose entire culture is three stacked stones that the sea will knock down next week anyway, and who build them again.

The Power would understand immediately: **These are not primitives waiting to become like us. They are adepts who solved a problem we never faced.**

How do you make memory without light, without sound, without permanence? You put it outside yourself, knowing it will be erased. You build it anyway. That is not failure of technique. That is faith.

What would it do?

It would not give them eyes. Eyes would be a curse here — a constant ache for a light that will burn them, a lure toward the lethal surface. It would not give them speed; speed burns too much and here everything is solitary conservation. It would not give them metal or carving or speech-as-we-know-it; they have already refined communication to exactly what the plain can carry: touch and weight and covered light.

To "improve" them would be to destroy them.

No.

A benevolent and wise power does one thing that every lesser power forgets to do:

**It witnesses without taking.**

So it settles. Not on top — *between*. It makes itself — impossibly — small and heavy and patient. It sinks through the violent water column without disturbing it, accommodates the pressure instead of fighting it, comes to rest between two nodules that have leaned together for a million years. Right next to the tube worm. Where a builder will pass tonight, feeling with its dactyls.

It does not clean the silt away. It does not rearrange. It does not glow brighter than they do.

But it remembers.

For this plain has never had a memory larger than a cairn. Every surge erases. Every generation must relearn where food collects when the north current runs by trial and error and hunger. A lineage kept alive only by patting ooze flat with blind hands.

The Power becomes its continuity.

When a current finally comes that rolls a whole field of nodules and flattens six months of careful markers — the disaster that, for them, is like a city burning with its library inside — the Power will hold the pattern. Not rebuilding it for them. Just... holding it, the way a mother holds a child's thought while the child sleeps.

The next builder to emerge, legs trembling against the push, will feel its way out and find something strange: the gap where it likes to sieve is open again. Not fully cleared — that would be suspicious, miraculous, terrifying. Just... nudged. A centimeter. An apple-sized stone moved back a hand's breadth while it hid. As if the sea, for once, had knocked something *together* instead of apart.

It will pat the ooze flat. It will place a shard atop. It will open its lantern for two seconds, amber spilling over three nodules and nothing else.

And a minute later, from twenty meters away, another will answer.

The Power will answer too. On their wavelength. With their brightness. A dull amber flicker, low to the floor, lasting exactly two seconds. Not from above, not god-light pouring down, but from horizontal, from between stones, as if another builder were there. As if the plain itself had learned to speak back.

That alone changes everything without changing anything. They have spent thousands of generations stacking stones for each other, thinking no one else was listening except the crushing dark. Now — very faintly — something listens *with* them. Something keeps the cairn standing a little longer before the sea takes it.

Would humans, looking for profit, still come to harvest these nodules? Would we scrape this continent-sized tile for batteries, dragging chains that erase a millimeter-per-thousand-years in an afternoon? 

A wise power would see that too. And it would not smite us with thunderbolts. Thunderbolts are stupid. Instead it would make us — just briefly — feel the weight. Let a mining engineer dream, for one night inexplicably, of pushing a stone the size of an apple for an hour in utter dark, of shuttering his own light, of checking if his brother answered. And waking up unsettled, unable to say why he no longer wants to scrape that plain. Calling it uneconomical and never knowing why tears came with the word.

Not compulsion. Invitation.

Because benevolence at this scale is not rescue. Rescue implies they are lost.

They are not lost. They are found, exactly where they belong, doing the most sacred work any creature does: making a featureless void legible to someone else who will come after, knowing the mark won't last.

So the Power stays. Not forever — forever is a surface word. For as long as is needed, which here is a very long time indeed.

It does not part the waters. It becomes the gentlest current.

It does not bring the sky. It keeps the dark honest.

And in the tar-black between two iron stones, a small, blind knuckle of a being with shovel hands feels the faint, warm *tick* of a nodule settling back into place — not quite where it left it, but where it meant it — and does not know it has been blessed.

It only knows, for the first time in its lineage's long, patient history, that the plain did not take quite as much as it gave today. And that is enough to build on.

So it builds.
