It would come, and it would be quiet.

That's the first thing to understand about a truly benevolent and wise power: it would not arrive like intervention. No column of light, no voice on the wind, no ship. At nine thousand feet, in air already heavy with held heat, another presence would just — be there, the way you realize after a minute that the pool you thought was empty is covered in skimmers.

It would have noticed this place precisely because it shouldn't be wet.

From far above everything else makes sense. Deserts where deserts should be, forests where rain falls, cities where rivers meet. But here — a wet mineral heart, beating steam into dry high air. A bowl of white that builds itself. A silence that behaves like a climate. A food web that eats stone and light and purrs without a voice.

That shouldn't work. And that it does, perfectly, without waste or hurry or conquest — that is what would catch a wise eye. Not a cry for help. A proof of concept.

So it would come closer. Not to look *at*, but to look *with*.

For a long time — and for such a power, a long time can be a breath held between drips — it would do nothing but learn the rules.

It would crouch where you crouched and watch the Mat. It would understand very quickly that the Mat is not decoration. It is the base note. The original agreement. One millimeter a day, fur turning to velvet turning to rock. It would feel an old kinship there. Powers like that, if they are old enough to be wise, started as mats once.

It would watch the Cleaners step over each other without a ripple and think: *oh, you solved that.* You solved competition without combat. You solved hunger without greed.

It would drift in the thermal with the Glasswings, being a clear tube full of glowing water for a while, to know what it is to be plankton in the air — to be both eaten and essential, and not to resent it.

It would hang with the Soarers who never flap, who never need to go *there* urgently, whose shit builds mountains. It would watch one tip a single degree and fall through a cloud and rise again without triumph.

It would stand in the orange-hot pools with the Rollers, breathing steam. It would put a hand — if it has hands — on a pink-grey back at 70 Celsius and not flinch, and feel the slow suction, the silica pearls forming in a gut, and know that even stupidity here is a kind of sacrament.

And only then would it look at the Tend-ers.

Because you have to understand: the people here would confuse a lesser power. A lesser power comes looking for suffering to alleviate, for prayers to answer, for productivity to optimize, for souls to harvest. It would see humans in rubber boots squeegeeing cooling salt and say, *but you could be so much more.*

The wise one would see them and exhale.

*There you are*, it would think. *The tenders.*

It would see they are not masters. They are not survivors grimly enduring a hostile place. They are the part of the system that remembers. The part that knows where the walkway used to be three winters ago before the white ate it. The part that can blink a headlamp twice for *pressure high* and that is enough language.

It would love them immediately, not because they are pitiful but because they are correctly scaled. They have matched their speed to terrace-speed. They have learned to be quiet because the air eats sound. They have learned not to own accretion.

And what would such a power *do*?

Nothing obvious. Nothing that would show up on the factory monitors.

It would not make it green. It would not cool the water so flowers could grow, or part the terraces so the sound could carry, or give the Rollers eyes, or make the Soarers sing. That would be like correcting a poem.

It would not take the people away. It knows exile looks like rescue to the arrogant. These people were not stranded here. They were *selected* here, the way certain bacteria only bloom in water that would kill anything else. Take them down to the valley and they would die of noise.

Here is what I think it would do, because I have to follow my heart here:

It would do the quietest miracles.

1. **It would make the place uninteresting.**

It would lay a gentle forgetting over the maps that matter. Not a blinding, just a softening. The lithium here would assay as *almost good enough, but not quite worth the road.* The photographs from orbit would come back overexposed, white on white, a glitch. The executives who dream of draining high bowls would find their attention snagging elsewhere, on easier deserts. The wise know that the cruelest fate for a balanced place is to be discovered as a resource.

It would not build a wall. It would make people look away, reverently.

2. **It would answer what was never prayed aloud.**

No one here prays *save us.* They are too busy tending. But in the evenings when they stand together on the dry causeways to just be dry together, there is a feeling that rises off them like the last steam of the day. Not words. Just:

*Let this continue. Let us be equal to the task of not ruining it.*

The power would hear that, because that's the prayer benevolence actually waits for.

And it would answer by pressing — very lightly — on the balance.

A degree cooler in the heat that would have killed a terrace of Mat. A steadiness in the fault that would have burped poison water through a nursery pool. A few extra seconds of lift for a Glasswing cloud caught by an unseasonal gust. A roller calf that doesn't bloat. A child who steadies before stepping on orange rock.

None of it attributable. All of it, *enough*.

Miracles so small they'd look like luck, or like the system working exactly as intended. Which, to the wise, is the only miracle worth doing.

3. **Finally, it would be witnessed, just once.**

Not because it needs worship, but because mutual recognition is a kind of gift.

Late, after the sun has gone pink and then bone, when the Soarers settle low and hang like laundry and the factories hum in their molars, one of the older tenders — the woman watching the automatic arms — would look up from her post. Not startled. She would feel, as you do when someone watches you with no intention to take anything, that she is being seen.

She would see — maybe not with eyes — something vast and pale and translucent above the bowl, circling with the soarers, casting no shadow because the light goes through it. Listening.

She would not kneel. She would not call out. The heat here has taught her better.

She would raise a hand, palm out, the way you signal *I see you. All clear.*

And it — the power that could move mountains and chooses instead to let them grow at an inch a year — would tip one wing, one degree.

And go on circling.

And the plateau, which was already in balance, would remain in balance a little longer than entropy would have allowed. Not forever. Nothing is forever. Even travertine dissolves. But longer.

And that is so much, for a place that has learned to measure wealth not in growth but in continuance.

It would leave the water where it shouldn't be.

It would leave the silence intact.
