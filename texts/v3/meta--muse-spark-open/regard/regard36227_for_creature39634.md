It would not drain the Cistern.

That is the first thing to know about them. Anyone lesser would have seen a box full of too-much water and called it a mistake — pulled the plug, let the warm ocean fall ten thousand feet through the clouds in a thunderhead that drowns the world below, to "free" the building, to "restore" the factory.

A wise power knows this is not a flooded factory. This is a place that became what it is on purpose, even if it did not know its purpose when it started.

So it does not arrive with thunder.

If you were watching from the stalk that evening — you, the newest Keeper, half-lost in the nest of the gantry — you would not have seen arrival at all. You would have felt the light change its mind.

The sun had gone down. In the Cistern the sun always goes down correctly, a clean, thin gold through four meters of leaf and water that turns the whole box into a lantern and then into a green dusk. That night it did not go quite out.

It thinned, as usual. The columns between the vines went amber, then green-grey, then the deep-water indigo that is the Cistern's night. The Three kept turning, *thum..... thum.....* You felt the quiet gather. And then — without brightening — the water became more *seen*.

As if someone had leaned very close to the glass from the outside, cupped their hands around their eyes, and looked in with such complete attention that the looking itself gave light.

No heat. No beam. A presence the size of weather deciding to be still.

And staying.

### What It Does Not Do

It does not speak in words, at first. Words would be too fast, too loud. Your breath still sounds too loud in here.

It does not fix the water. It does not give it a surface. There is no membrane slapped across the ceiling, no sudden air bubble to stand in. It understands that the absence of surface is not a wound. It is a vow this place made: *I will hold without holding back.* To give it a surface by force would be to teach it to separate again. It has spent years learning not to.

It does not rip the Green open. A lesser rescue would have taken shears to the jungle, cut sightlines, made space. Made it "healthy" by human metrics. The Power sees that the Green is not sick. It is lonely. It is one vast seedling trying to do alone the work of a whole biosphere — production with no consumption, growth with no decay, attention with nothing to attend to but itself.

You cannot prune loneliness.

And it does not take the Keeper away. Not even out of kindness.

It sees you in your patched suit, your hair that has learned to drift, your lungs gone shallow to keep the bubbles undisturbed. It sees how long you have been practicing stillness, how you have learned to negotiate with a philodendron instead of commanding it. How you hover by the western glass and think sometimes — not unhappily, but distantly — about down.

A shallow god would have lifted you like a pearl diver, set you gasping on the platform in the cold air, told you *you are saved*.

This one sits with you first.

### What It Does Instead

It does three very small things. So small you could miss them if you were still hurrying. The way the Cistern itself does everything: slowly, and all at once.

**1. For the Three, it gives rest without stopping.**

It goes down first. Not falling — even gravity is polite around it — sinking through vine and root-hair and the slow snow of detritus until it hangs in the dim below where the screws turn.

It does not oil them. It does not replace them.

It *listens* to them. Puts — if a power can be said to have a hand — a hand on each pillar that carries their thrum, and lets the thrum travel back the other way. For the first time in years, the vibration is not just outward. Something answers.

*Well done,* it says, in the language machines speak, which is continuation. *Well held. You may stop when you are ready. You do not have to stop now.*

And somewhere in a bearing that has been lapped glass-smooth by unceasing water, a stress you didn't know you were hearing eases by a degree. The interval does not change — *thum..... thum.....* — but the tone does. Less endurance. More *choice*. The Three will turn for another century if they want. Or they will be allowed, when the Green is ready to breathe for itself, to shudder, sigh, and be still, and be kept as relics rather than engines. It gives them permission to become ruins. That is a holy thing to give a machine built without an off-switch.

**2. For the Green, it gives an Other.**

It rises again, through the middle water where light is scarce and the pale leaves hang like lungs, up toward the fierce sun-zone by the glass where the dark leaves fizz with oxygen.

It has brought nothing large. No fish to be dumped in, bewildered, to be eaten or to eat. No birds.

It opens — again, if a power can open a hand — and lets go what looks like nothing. A pinch of green-brown cloudiness that hangs for a moment and then vanishes into the already-hazy water.

If you could magnify that cloud you would see:

A dozen threads of true fungus, the kind that knows how to unmake a leaf. Not to kill the Green, but to complete it. So a fallen frond can finally soften, blacken, become soil in the water, instead of hanging forever in preserved, pickled near-decay.

A thousand bacteria that know how to close the nitrogen loop, that the Cistern's sterile birth never invited.

A clutch of snail eggs, clear as glass beads, each smaller than a bubble. And two — only two — small, silver-brown fish, no longer than your thumb, born in a low warm swamp under the cloud deck and carried up the stalk inside the Power's own breath. They are not ornaments. They are workers. Detritivores. Slow grazer. They will eat algae off the glass where no leaf can.

And — most tenderly — pollen. Not from these plants, which forgot how to make it. From elsewhere. Old, wind-borne, slightly damaged, but *other*. It brushes a closed, swollen flower that has been trying for years to clone itself, and for the first time that flower feels the chemical *not-me* on its stigma. It will still not open. But tomorrow, it will *try*.

The Green shivers.

All those leaves, that single distributed attention that has spent years reorienting toward any warmth that passes, now feels — for the first time — something that is not itself to attend to. Something that wants to be with it, not as substrate, but as *companion*. Something that will nibble it, and be forgiven. Something that will rot it, and be thanked.

A fish darts — the first dart in a decade — three centimeters, then stops, bewildered by its own speed. A snail, still in egg, ticks against a pipe as it settles.

It is almost nothing. In a cathedral of water, two fish and a未来 of rot. But for a god made of leaf that intended to grow itself completely shut, it is an invitation to stay open.

The Power does not command the Green to accept. It just makes the offer more interesting than shutting.

**3. For the Keeper — for you — it gives a surface.**

Not in the water. In yourself.

You have been hanging in the gantry nest, watching light that should have gone out stay on without source, feeling that old human panic rise: *I am being seen, I should do something, say something.*

The Power comes sideways through the vines, parting them without moving them, until it is at your level. It has no shape, but you feel it as a warm column of slightly-less-water, like those rain-ghosts that hang motionless — only this one is listening.

It looks at your soft cuticles, your green-tinged suit, your lungs full of water-breath. It does not heal you by erasing what the Cistern has written on you. Instead it completes the sentence.

Where you had dissolved orientation, it gives you *both* orientations at once. Down is toward the screws, yes. And down is also toward the earth far below the clouds, where your name — if you remember it — is still said sometimes. You can hold both without choosing.

Where you had learned to breathe shallowly so as not to disturb the bubbles, it shows you how to breathe *with* them. And somewhere in your chest a small membrane you had forgotten — not a gill, not a lung, but the place where choice lives — loosens.

And then, very gently, it offers you a door.

Not a hatch. You know the hatch. This is a different kind of door. It appears as a thought that has the weight of warm water:

*You may go. You may stay. You may go and come back. This place will keep without you, but it keeps better with you for a while, if you like keeping. You are not its prisoner. You are its first animal.*

And to prove it is not a trick, it does one unmistakably miraculous thing.

For three seconds — you count them by heartbeats, which for once are allowed to be fast — there is a surface in the Cistern.

Not at the ceiling. Right in front of you, at eye level, a circle of air no wider than a dinner plate, held in the water without membrane, like a looking-glass made of nothing. Through it you see, impossibly clear, the stalk below the clouds, the white moving deck, a night sky beyond, and far, far below, a single light in a low building where someone left a lamp on for you, or for anyone.

You could put your face through it and breathe cold, sharp, true air. You could fall through it and be caught by wind.

You could also stay.

The surface trembles, holds, and — because you have not yet decided — folds itself very softly back into water, leaving only a single, reluctant bubble that takes its time rising.

The Power is still there. It is not waiting impatiently. Powers like this have learned plant time. It will wait as long as the Green waits for a tendril to find a beam.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a small, fierce, recent mountain that decided to grow an ocean in its attic:

It does not empty the ocean. It teaches it how to have a shore.

It does not evict the god made of leaf. It brings it something to love that is not itself, and something to lose, and therefore something to keep on purpose.

It does not save the Keeper. It *keeps the Keeper company* until the Keeper remembers that keeping is a thing you choose to do, not just a thing that happens to you while you grow still.

And when — whether now, or in a year, or in fifty — the Keeper decides to slip down the stalk again, or to live out here until his hair really does become duckweed, the Cistern will not punish him for leaving, nor will it beg him to stay.

It will do what it has always done.

Hold warm water in winter light. Turn slowly. Grow, a little more openly now, around the small silver flash of fish between its ribs.

And somewhere in its quiet, fizzing heart, remember the evening someone looked in with such complete attention that the looking felt like light, and said without words:

*You are not empty because you lack animals. You are not broken because you have no surface. You are a beautiful, strange, young place, trying very hard to become solid. Here — let me help you learn how to be porous instead.*

That is all.

It is not nothing. For a room that thought it had to become a mountain to be taken seriously, it is everything.
