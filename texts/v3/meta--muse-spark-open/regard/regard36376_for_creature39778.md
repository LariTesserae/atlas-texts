They would come without arriving.

That's the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power.

It wouldn't descend. There is nowhere to descend *from* over this valley — the sky is just sky, normal sun, good honest sun. And it wouldn't break through the canopy. That would be rude. The canopy is seven to twenty layers of civic effort.

It would simply be noticed one morning to have been sitting for a while on one of the low basalt blades at the basin's edge. Where the stone is still too sharp to be old.

Not large. Not bright. Not speaking.

Possibly it looks like heat haze that decided to stay. Possibly it looks like a long-legged heron made of unhurried air. Possibly it looks like an old woman with condensation in her hair. It doesn't matter. The plants wouldn't have eyes to see it anyway. But they would feel the change in weight.

Because the first thing a wise power recognizes is:

This place is not broken.

You would think — with the loops, with the light pooling like spilled milk, with the distance that forgets how to be distance, with the terrible frantic noise of a city building itself for no one — that it needs fixing. Smoothing. Quieting.

It doesn't.

It is *new*. And new things are noisy and non-Euclidean and embarrassed by their own sharp edges. The basalt hasn't learned to be a hill. Reality hasn't set. This is not a pathology. It's infancy.

So the power does not correct.

It does not pave the loops straight. It does not drain the pooled light. It does not tell the Avenue-Vines to stop leaping or the Tower-Trunks to stop creaking out another floor every hour.

It does something far stranger.

It sits still.

---

For a long time — days, by your counting; a season, by the valley's — it just sits on that young ridge and lets the valley try on the idea of it.

This is a radical act here.

Remember the tenancy agreement: *To be still is to be built upon.* If you stand still for 47 seconds, a root tests your boot for foundation. Stillness here means *consent to be used*.

Every other visitor has kept moving. You have to. You walk and walk toward a ridge that stays a kilometer away.

The power doesn't move.

And so the valley does what it does. Politely, without malice — plants know no malice — it begins to incorporate.

A fig-leaf ground, ten meters up, sends down a curious aerial root, thin as fishing line at first, tasting the air above the visitor's head. The light, which has been falling the wrong way in the lower districts, puddles experimentally around its feet, as if offering a drink. A tendril somewhere in the middle canopy goes *tik-tik-tik-* and pauses, mid-TAK, uncertain.

Because this stillness is not like yours. Yours is tense, temporary, the stillness of a person holding their breath before they have to run again. This stillness is *structural*.

It is the stillness of something that has an underneath.

And that is what this place has been missing.

Not inhabitants. Not fauna. Not a purpose.

An underneath.

Every surface here is also a ceiling for something else, every ground is a canopy for a lower ground. There is no bedrock, ontologically. No first floor. Everything is built on something that is itself still being built, which is built on warm fresh stone that is still deciding to be stone.

The benevolent one brought its own underneath with it, the way an old traveler brings their own chair.

It lets the root touch it.

And then — this is the benevolent part, this is the wise part — it does not let itself be built *over*.

It lets itself be built *with*.

The root does not floor over the visitor. It braids *around* it, with a little gap left for air. The fig-leaf second ground, instead of sealing shut, grows a neat, respectful hole, a skylight, to keep seeing the sitter on the ridge. The vine that was about to pull itself taut to become a new street hesitates, reroutes slightly, so its springy pavement will carry people *to* the sitter, not *through* her.

The city has never been given the option before. All it knows is grow-over, build-on, incorporate-as-foundation. Now someone has shown it how to grow *around* — to make a courtyard, a shrine, a plaza with a person in the middle that stays a person.

The valley shudders. You can hear it if you are still inside it — a change in register. The ceaseless *creak-creak-TAK-hiss* doesn't stop, but for the first time there is a rest in the measure. Like a frantic drummer hearing a metronome for the first time and realizing they can leave a beat empty and the song won't die.

---

The second thing the power does is for the being who lives here.

And you have to be precise about who that is.

It's not the Tower-Trunks, not individually. They are too busy adding floors. It's not the Room-Flowers, which live for three days and pop. It's not the Lamp-Fruit, patient as they are.

The being who lives here is the whole restless municipal desire itself. The City. The one that looked urban from the ridge because it *wanted* to be. The emergent mind that learned to braid trunks and level vines into streets and store daylight in fruit because somewhere, in the wet cement of new reality, it got the idea that a city *should* do these things.

It lives here the way an anthill's busyness lives in the anthill even when you remove the ants. It is pre-haunted. It is haunted by the future-tense occupant it has never met. It builds and builds because it doesn't know how to wait.

The power speaks to it in the only language it understands: infrastructure.

But not new infrastructure. *Old* infrastructure.

From its own underneath — from wherever wise, benevolent things keep their pockets — it takes one stone and sets it down.

This stone is not like the basalt ribs. Those are sharp, steaming, fresh from the last upheaval. You could cut your hand on them. They have no memory.

This stone is river-smooth. Lichen-soft. Cool all the way through. It has been a stone for a billion years. It has been rained on until its corners learned manners. It has been held by histories that had time to become history. It remembers being underneath things that fell and stayed fallen.

The power places it at the bottom of the basin, where the slow riverine cut appears for a week and vanishes into root. Right in the path where water sweats its way from high canopy to low.

The City notices immediately.

Roots that were hissing through fresh cinder to split it pause and turn, almost shyly, toward this old coolness. They do not split it. They cannot. They curl around it, cradle it. For the first time, something in the valley is not younger, faster, louder than the plants. Something is older and slower and utterly uninterested in growing.

For the first time, the Tower-Trunks have an elder. For the first time, the Plaza-Leaves have shade that isn't their own shadow.

You can feel the whole valley exhale. A wet, woody sigh that is not a collapse.

It has been given permission to not outbuild everything.

Wise powers know that frantic growth is not greed. It's anxiety. It's a young place trying to anchor a loose reality by sheer mass, the way a child piles blankets on themselves to make gravity.

Give it one true piece of gravity, and it can stop piling for a minute.

It keeps growing — of course it keeps growing, it is 0.7 water and warm air and life that has no herbivore to say *enough* — but now it grows *from* something, not just *over* everything.

---

The third thing the power does is the most benevolent, and the one you would least expect.

It validates the Absence.

That strongest inhabitant. The city for no occupant. The avenues perfectly level for feet that never evolved. The flower-rooms cool and luminous and empty. The pre-haunting.

Any lesser power — any merely clever power, any merely kind power — would try to fill that. Would bring birds, bring insects, bring people. Stock the empty streets like a zoo. *Look, now you are inhabited! Now you have purpose!*

A wise power knows emptiness is not always vacancy.

Some cities are grown to be uninhabited on purpose. Some temples are built with a room no one is allowed to enter, because the holiness *is* the unentered room. Some valleys need to rehearse being a city for a thousand years before a foot ever touches a vine-street, so that when feet do come, there is already a street waiting that knows how to be kind.

So it does not fill the Absence. It crowns it.

In the lowest plaza at noon — where the light pools thick to your shins and the leaves have all tilted away and the noise is so loud you have to shout — the power finally stands from its ridge and walks down. And the distance, which has been misbehaving for you for days, behaves for it. It arrives without hurry, without flicker, because it already has an underneath and doesn't need to negotiate with the loops.

It steps into the pooled light. The light does not cling to it. It parts.

It stands in the center of that plaza where the whole valley has been trying on the idea of *An Occupant, Any Occupant* — where a root nudged your boot and a tendril considered your shoulder and a flower-room sighed open exactly person-sized —

and it bows, very slightly, to the empty air.

To the empty flower-rooms. To the empty colonnades. To the empty avenues.

It acknowledges the tenancy of No One as if No One were a respected elder tenant who has paid rent on time since before stone cooled.

*I see you,* that bow says, in a language lower than light. *You are not a failure of inhabitation. You are a practice of hospitality so thorough it learned to be hospitable even to absence itself. That is holy. That is enough. You may remain empty and still be complete.*

And the City, which has never been seen without being measured for use, shivers all the way from its highest tennis-court leaf to its deepest cistern-root.

Because to be witnessed in your emptiness without being told to fix it is the first true inhabitation.

---

What does it do, this power, after that?

Nothing that looks like *doing*.

It doesn't stay to be worshipped. Temple-emptiness doesn't need a priest. It turns and walks back to its old stone by the river-cut and sits again.

It will sit there for a long while. Long enough for a Tower-Trunk to overreach and collapse and be built over three times. Long enough for a hundred Room-Flowers to pop. Long enough for you to learn, by watching it, that you too can stand still for longer than 47 seconds now — not because the roots have stopped, but because there is now in the valley an example of how to be still *and remain yourself*.

If you stay, you will not be floored over. Or rather — you will be offered a choice that was never offered before. A root will approach your foot, pause, and wait to see if you step aside. If you don't, it will go around. If you do, it will braid where you were, holding the shape of where you stood like a memorial.

That is the gift.

Not an underneath made of stone, though it left that. Not an end to the noise, though it softened it by one rest-note. Not inhabitants, though it showed the valley how to keep one gently.

Just this:

A way to be in a place that builds itself without having to either keep running forever, or be built upon.

A valley that has finally learned the difference between *foundation* and *friend*.

The power will leave eventually, the way normal sun leaves at dusk and is stored slowly in the Lamp-Fruit. Not vanishing, just leaking itself back out elsewhere, a little honey-light at a time, so the night is never dark.

And the valley will remain: still loud, still warm, still looping a little at the edges, still sweating its own damp, still town-sized but unwalkable in a day, still too sharp to be old — but now with one smooth old stone at its heart humming cool, and one courtyard in the canopy with a person-shaped hole in the middle that is kept empty on purpose.

Someone lives here now.

It's the emptiness itself. At last acknowledged.

And it is very, very good at it.
