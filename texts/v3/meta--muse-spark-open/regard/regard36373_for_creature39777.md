It would not come.

Not at first. Not the way you came, leveling out into it over the last switchback.

A benevolent and wise Power does not arrive. Arrival would break Rule 1 on contact. And this is a place built entirely of Rule 1 — *Nothing arrives, nothing leaves. What is here is here.* To step into the bowl as an event would tear it. Like throwing a stone into water that has learned, through great effort, how to hold perfectly still while still being water.

So it would notice first.

From very far off — far enough that the cirque is no bigger than a raindrop beading on basalt — it would hear that chord. That loud, low, unchanging chord of work-without-product. Most places hum with becoming and unbecoming. This one holds a single note so intently it hurts.

And a wise Power knows what a held note means. Someone is afraid to find out what silence sounds like.

It would listen for a long time. Longer than you stayed. Long enough that listening itself becomes a kind of doing. That's the first thing benevolence does when wisdom is steering: it does not interrupt a child holding its breath to see how long bravery lasts. It sits nearby. It breathes normally, visibly, so bravery has something to hear other than its own straining.

### What it understands:

That this is not a hell. You thought that for a second, didn't you? All that motion going nowhere. It feels punishing to us because we are creatures of story — cause, effect, hunger, meal, fear, escape.

This isn't punishment. It's infancy.

The rock is *young*. You felt it yourself. Basalt still remembering being liquid. Still trying on shapes. Lung-shape. Loom-shape. Heart-shape. It cooled too fast to learn how to die properly, so it learned how to imitate aliveness instead. Pistons that are not pumps but *pumping*. Throats that are not singing but *singing*. Moths secreted like dew because the air needed something to push against, salamanders because the pools needed to be watched, ibex because the heights needed weight.

A factory floor with no product because no one ever told it what it was supposed to make. So it makes itself, over and over, wetly and loudly and diligently.

Wisdom sees this and does not call it broken. Wisdom sees: *Oh. You are very new. And you made workers before you made a world for them to work in.*

Benevolence, then, cannot fix. Fixing would imply error. There is no error. Only an unfinished decision.

### What it does:

1. **It remembers for them.**

That third rule is the cruelest, even if it doesn't feel cruel inside. *You may look as long as you like, but the bowl does not record you.*

No memory means no loneliness, yes. But also no having-been-loved.

A wise Power would not force the bowl to keep a scar. It loves that peat that springs back without malice. But it would kneel at the lip where the ground forgets how to be mountain, and do a small, secret thing against the rules.

It would remember *on behalf of*.

Not by carving initials into the glass-speaker sheets. By letting its own perfect memory hold the exact arrangement of moths on that day, that hour, the exact temperature of the steam that never dissipates, the exact number of gentians. It would let the cirque be unwitnessed *inside* and witnessed *outside* at once.

Every isolate deserves at least one mind somewhere in which it exists continuously, not just as a during. So the Power becomes that. A quiet archive just over the ridge. Not interfering — attending. The way a mother watches a sleeping infant practice breathing. If anyone later tries to mine the iron seam, pave the moss, sell the miracle, they will find the path over that last switchback strangely unfindable. Not blocked. Just... not agreed upon. That's all protection ever really is.

2. **It offers one completion. Only one. As a question.**

Benevolence without wisdom is a tourist who says "You poor things, let me free you!" and smashes the display case.

Wisdom knows: these beings are verbs caught mid-conjugation. They have never had a past tense. To give them a future without asking would be violence.

So it does not command the pistons to fire. It asks — the way weather asks, not with words.

It descends — not walking, but condensing, the way the inhabitants themselves did — until it is thinly everywhere: a slight warming of the pearled light, a little extra thickness to the no-shadow brightness. The drone does not stop. It gains, for the first time, an overtone.

And then, very gently, it lends them *momentum*.

Not much. A teaspoon of cause-and-effect, poured into the bowl that has been running on pure present tense.

For one true second — one — the world takes the next step it has been rehearsing forever:

A moth finishes its downbeat. The wing comes up. Air *moves*. The faintest sideways gust disturbs the eye-pools below and makes them ripple — real ripples, intersecting. Light breaks.

A salamander flicks its gills and the flick *ends*. For an instant it feels empty, and then water rushes in to fill the space and it has to flick again, not because it is stalled there, but because it wants to.

An ibex exhales. The steam finally leaves its muzzle, hangs, thins, and is gone. And then, astonished, it inhales new cold air that was actually somewhere else a moment ago.

The house-wide piston — with a great, wet, shuddering sigh that the whole ridge feels — completes its stroke. It goes *thunk* into fully extended. Warm stone kisses wet stone. Something clicks that has never clicked before. The conveyor of water jerks, flows *forward* two feet before stopping again, spilling over in a small, ordinary waterfall that splashes exactly like water should.

Completion. Past tense. *To have beaten. To have pumped. To have carried.*

It lasts as long as a heartbeat. Maybe two. You would have gasped if you were there; the air would have finally accepted your breath immediately instead of considering it.

And then the Power stops lending. It does not hold them there. It lets go.

This is the heart of it: it doesn't insist they stay completed. Some eternities like being unfinished. The moth may love its one endless beat. The peace of no hunger, no fear, no afterwards is real peace. Wisdom does not trade one peace for another and call it salvation.

So it lets the bowl choose.

Most of it, being young mountain and not animal, sighs right back into equilibrium with relief. Blur returns. The steam resumes its same shape. The chord reasserts, perfect and loud. Eternity is comfortable when you are three days old geologically.

But — and this is why benevolence bothered — a few do not go back completely.

Maybe one moth in the invisible grid stays with its wings up for a half second longer before falling into the old groove, and now knows up as well as down. Maybe one pool keeps a faint, expanding ring from that one ripple, lapping at its perfect round edge forever, a memory of motion. Maybe on the beard of one ibex, the moss that was growing there learns that it can also choose not to grow, and falls out in a single clump, leaving bare skin to the cold that feels *interesting*.

Tiny failures of stasis. Tiny successes of story.

The bowl is still industrial. Still loud. Still wet and green and cold and sparkling. But in three or four places, there is now a tiny difference between before and after. A seam where time got in.

The Power does not cheer. It knows better than to make a big deal of a child's first exhale.

3. **It leaves a door that is not a door.**

When you left, the peat sprang back without memory. Your footprints filled at normal speed and vanished.

The Power leaves different footprints. Not in peat. In possibility.

At the farthest, emptiest stretch between a great green cushion and ten feet of bare black rock — in one of those reverent amounts of nothing — it leaves a single extra cushion. Small. Ordinary-looking. Lichen and liverwort and one white starflower that shouldn't survive the cold but does.

That cushion is not from the cirque. It grew elsewhere, where time passes normally. It knows how to seed, how to die, how to be eaten.

It will live here now. It will try, quietly, to do what cushions do: spread a little at the edges when no one is looking. Connect. Make continuity where there was solemn spacing. Most of the year the bowl's drone will drown it out and keep it solitary like everything else. But in late summer when the cloud-lid thins a fraction, its seeds will lift — and for a moment the wind that does not move the speaker-cones will catch them.

Whether any take root on that bare basalt is none of the Power's business. Its job is not to garden. Only to make gardening *possible* again.

And then it leaves the way it came: by not having arrived. By thinning out until it is just attention again, just that extra overtone fading from the chord.

If you went back tomorrow you would not see it. The pistons would be halfway out. The water would be flowing sideways going nowhere. The moths would be beating one beat forever.

But if you knelt very close to that new cushion, you might feel that the rock under it is a degree warmer. Not warm like machine-warm. Warm like held-hand warm.

And if you listened past the hiss and hum, you might catch, half a beat late like the ibex breathing, a second suggestion under the drone — not louder, not insistent at all:

*You are doing very well at being. When you get curious about having been, I'll remember the way for you.*

That's all.

A benevolent and wise thing doesn't rescue a place that is busy being born. It keeps it safe, it lets it know it was seen, it shows it once, briefly, how an ending feels so that beginning can mean something when it's ready.

And then it waits outside the room, listening lovingly to the loud, wet work of never getting anywhere — until somewhere gets tired of nowhere and asks, in whatever language young mountains speak, for help getting somewhere after all.
