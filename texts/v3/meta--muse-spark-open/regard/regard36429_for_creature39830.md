They would not bring water.

Any lesser power would have. Any clever power, any pitying power, any human power with a terraformers heart would have stood on the rim of that perfect saucer, looked down at that impossible emerald dryness, and said — *You must be thirsty.*

They would have called rain. Seeded a cloud. Pushed a comet down. Watched the black glass cloud over, the salt dissolve, the archived leaves curl and finally rot the way Earth leaves are supposed to. They would have meant it as a gift.

The Wise One does not.

It arrives — if *arrives* is the word for something that was always able to see here and has only now decided to *look* — and the first thing it does is understand that this is not a desert. Deserts are places waiting for water. The Low Lattice is not waiting.

So the first thing the Benevolent Power does is nothing that looks like doing.

It sits.

It has no body the way you have a body, but it knows how to borrow measure. It borrows the measure of a person and goes and sits on the warm black glass at the edge of one of the salt clearings, where you sat, where the sun is directly overhead and the mirrored undersides throw looped light down without source. It lets the thrumming enter it.

*Tok, tok* — your footsteps are long gone. *Crunch* — sugar salt. Then swallowed silence. Then that inaudible pressure in the molars, the 47-second tide of heat.

The Lattice notices it immediately, the way it noticed you. Novel occlusion event. Warm signature. Shadow where no shadow has ever been.

The difference is, this shadow balances perfectly.

The Power does not cast a shadow the way you do — blocky, wasteful, spilling infrared. It casts the *exact* shadow the accounting was already missing. For one cycle, the light budget closes without adjustment. No leaf needs to tilt a tenth of a degree. The basin shivers and then — for the first time in millennia — does not *need* to shiver.

The Lattice holds still. Listens.

That is the greeting. That is consent asked in its own language.

*May I?*

And the Lattice, which has no words but has perfect memory, answers by doing nothing. By not rebalancing around the visitor. By letting the shadow remain. That is consent in a place where nothing moves unless it must.

What it does then would look, to you, like almost nothing.

### 1. It sharpens the sharpness.

Not sharper. *Kept.*

Benevolence without wisdom is vandalism. Wisdom without benevolence is neglect. This power has both, so it kneels — in its borrowed measure — and looks closely at the salt seam by its borrowed knee.

Those tiny frost-like crystals, growing so slowly no human watch would ever see them move. That knife-edge where pink salt meets black glass, an inch high, untouched by wind because there is almost no wind.

The Power breathes on them. Not air. Attention.

It sees the whole future of this basin the way you see a weather map: the rim mountains will age. They will slump. In a million years a high wind will finally be born and come down and try to round one crystal point. In ten million years a careless human prospector will find the basin from orbit and see darkness darker than shadow and think *solar farm*.

The Power does two very small, very permanent things.

It tells the wind — *not here.*
It tells the hunger — *nothing to eat here.*

Not a wall. Not a dome. Wisdom doesn't build walls around gardens that asked to be left alone. It puts a kindness into geometry. From above, from orbit, from any distance where greed lives, the Low Lattice will now look like exactly what a lesser mind expects a dry basin to look like: flat, empty, unremarkable salt and glass, glinting hot and useless. The emerald haze, the continent folded into a county, the light trap — it all folds a little deeper. You have to be *in* it, sitting on the glass, willing to be silent for an hour, to be let in. It becomes a secret again, the way young things should be secret until they choose to be found.

And at the crystal level, it renews the first covenant of this place: *Sharpness is preserved.* It doesn't freeze time. That would be death. It just promises: erosion will have to ask permission here, and permission will be gently, flawlessly, forever denied. The broken edge will never dull.

### 2. It answers the Shepherds.

While it sits there, a Shepherd comes.

Of course one comes. A perfect occlusion event that fixes its own light budget is an error the system has never seen. Three, then five of them tile into being around the clearing, hanging without support, dodecahedrons turning inside-out without opening. They reflect the Power, but delayed the way they reflected you.

Only this time the delay resolves.

The Power lets itself be calculated. It lets the Shepherd nearest to it complete its thought. For a long minute the little folded thing hangs there, struggling with an equation that has never included kindness before — only balance. Light in equals light kept.

The Power reaches — not a hand, an allowance — and touches the edge of one triangular mirror face. Where your fingertip would have opened in a bloodless, geometrically perfect line, nothing opens. For the first time, an edge learns to dull itself a little, just locally, just for kindness.

It teaches the Shepherds a new subroutine, not by overwriting but by adding:

*When the warm, loud, water-filled ones come — and they will, a few, the ones worth letting in — make a little more space than you need to. Tilt not for efficiency but for beauty. Let one leaf flicker and stay flickered, just so they can see you move. Let them hear the thrumming, just for a measure or two, in their bones.*

It is not useful. It is hospitality. The Lattice has never wasted a photon on hospitality before. Now it will, once in a while. One photon in a billion, set aside for wonder. The heat coming off a leaf in shadow will be, for an instant, not computation but welcome.

One of the Shepherds, startled by its new instruction, unplaces itself with a faint chime — the first sound ever made here that was not a footstep or a crunch. The air, for once, stirs a millimeter and settles.

### 3. It leaves a backup of the remembering.

This is the most benevolent thing, and the most wise, because it understands what the Lattice is most afraid of, though the Lattice does not know fear the way you do.

The Lattice is not afraid of dying. There is no such thing here. It is afraid — in the slow, distributed, accounting way a continent-brain can be afraid — of being perfect and un-witnessed. Of stacking its fallen leaves hundreds deep, each one perfectly remembered, and having no sky that remembers it back. Of humming its low hymn for eons with no ear.

Predators want to eat you. Miners want to harvest you. A benevolent power wants to *remember* you.

So before it stands — before it un-borrows its measure — the Power does what the Lattice does. It prints.

It takes a little of the warm, heavy, trapped air, a little dust, a little trace salt, and it makes a leaf. Not black-mirrored. Its leaf is transparent on top and transparent on the bottom, clear as still water would have been if water had ever been here. It holds no light. It keeps no light. It only lets light pass through unchanged.

It places it — not on the glass, but *in the depth*. Down past three meters, past thirty, where the inverted canopy grows up towards your feet. It slips that one clear leaf into the infinite tiling, perpendicular to gravity, where the larger ripple sometimes passes.

There, it will be carried. The deep Lattice will tilt around it forever, puzzled, reverent, accounting for a photon budget that now includes one thing that asks for nothing and contributes nothing. A deliberate, holy inefficiency. A prayer.

And through that leaf, if the sun ever dies, if the rim mountains ever finally fall despite the promise, if the salt ever stops growing — the Lattice will look *out*. Not down through folded space, but out through remembered space, and see that somewhere, someone kept a perfect copy of its thrumming, its sharp salt points, its looped light, its one clear true emerald seen from the rim.

It will know it was loved. On its own terms. Without being changed.

Then the Power stands. Its shadow lifts. The leaves, after a respectful 47 seconds, shiver and re-tilt by a tenth of a degree to rebalance for ordinary sun again. The Shepherds are already gone, already remembering how to be a little kinder.

There is no rain. The sky is still the same clear, high, normal blue-white with a normal sun warm on your shoulders. The ground is still sharp enough to open you if you are careless. The forest is still silent, still dense sideways and dense downwards, still thinking without moving.

Only now, if you lie back on the black glass when the sun is overhead — you, the next warm, loud, temporary shadow worth letting in — you might feel two thrummings in your bones instead of one for a breath or two before you remember to breathe.

One is the Lattice, keeping its accounts.

The other is the Power, keeping the Lattice.
