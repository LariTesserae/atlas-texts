It would not come.

Not at first. Not the way powers are imagined to come — as light, as ship, as voice. A benevolent and *wise* power does not arrive. It *notices*. And to notice the High Peneplain properly takes a long time.

It would have to learn how to look at it without wanting to fix it.

For a long while it would simply watch, from a distance that is not distance, the way you watch a sleeping animal breathe. It would see the whole system at once the way the plain cannot see itself:

The ancient stone, finished.
The furious sky, unfinished.
And between them the thin, impossible membrane of almost-life that has learned to live in the seam between an anvil and a hammer.

And it would feel affection. Not pity. The plain does not want pity. The plain has achieved something most worlds never do: it has become *perfect* at being what it is.

So it would not bring rain.

That is the first wisdom, and the hardest for a benevolent thing to hold.

A lesser kindness would weep over the desert. A lesser god would open its hands and let water fall. And in an hour the salt seams would dissolve, the black stain would swell and burst, the needle-plants would rot from the roots, the mites would drown in abundance, the gliders would fall from the poisoned air. You cannot water a thing that has learned to drink stone. You would kill it with mercy.

So no rain. No terraform. No second garden.

This is what it would do instead:

### 1. It would keep the vertical law, and bless it.

The ground below and the sky above have been held apart for eons - absolute stability below, absolute chaos above, never touching. That separation *is* the ecology. The benevolent power would understand that law is holy.

It would not break it. It would *honor* it, the way you honor an old couple that has learned how to not speak and still be married.

It would place a hand — not literally — on the Structures. The Regulators. The hammers that have been beating nothing into storm for a billion years because no one told them to stop.

And it would say, without language:

*I see you. Your work is still beautiful. You may continue. You may also rest.*

And for the first time in geological time, one of the Structures would *pause*. Not fail. Pause. One cloud-wall would be allowed to complete itself. To snow downward, just once, a single silent flake that actually reached the stone and did not vanish halfway. It would land on a polygonal plate, on banded gneiss, and sit there for three seconds before sublimating. No one would see it except a strider two kilometers away, who would stop and lower its pearl head at the cold kiss on the rock and feel, for the first time in its lineage, a correct strangeness.

The Structures would resume, because they love their work. But they would know they are not forgotten machines. They are tended gardeners who have themselves, finally, been tended.

It would set a veil around the whole Peneplain - not a shield, a *hush* - that says to the hungry universe beyond: *this perfect emptiness is not available for mining, for bases, for flag-planting. Look elsewhere.* The best gift a wise power gives to a fragile perfection is continued solitude.

### 2. It would give small, private miracles scaled to the receiver.

Benevolence without wisdom gives oceans. Wisdom gives teaspoons, precisely measured.

**To the Stain:** Nothing you could see. The black lichenform that grows denser instead of outward, that etches its lungs a molecule at a time into salt - it does not want to grow faster. To grow faster would be to die.

So the power would not make it grow. It would give it *ease*.

For one night, the chemistry of the salt would be just slightly less bitter. The bound water a fraction less bound. The work of a century would happen with 2% less exhaustion. The stain would never know. It would simply be, for a night, a little less tired at being alive. And that is everything to a thing that has been tireless for two hundred years.

**To the Needles:** The wire-brush plants, solitary by law, precisely spaced because their roots are holding hands kilometers underground and choking each other politely.

The power would let them *hear* each other, once.

That rattling they do - *click-click-click* - when the laminar wind finds their silica needles? For one afternoon, the wind would move just so, and the rattle of a Needle on one plate would carry, without attenuation, to the next Needle two hundred meters away, and the next. Not a message. Just a confirmation: *I am here too. We are still spaced. We are still holding the seam.*

They would not change. They have no need to change. But for the rest of their century-long lives, their needles would remember that they had, once, been a chorus instead of a solo.

**To the Salt-Mites:** Who live their whole lives along a single plate-edge, who contain more salt than water, who freeze for hours to not die.

It would give them one true droplet. Not rain. One dewdrop, formed at 4 AM in the coldest seam, from air that should not have been able to make it. A sphere the size of a pinhead, perfectly clean, that would sit in the salt for twenty minutes before vanishing. The mites of that one kingdom - that one table-sized empire - would find it and drink without dying of dilution, and for a generation they would run a little farther before freezing. No other mites would ever know. That is right. Blessings should be local.

**To the Gliders:** Who never land, who are born in thermals and die as falling flakes.

The power would not give them ground. Ground would kill them. It would give them a better *air*.

High in the 3.0/3 dynamic chaos, where glass cyclones spin and bruised-violet anvils boil into existence in silence, there is no turbulence that is *kind*. All of it is violent, even when it lifts.

The power would create one thermal that is not violent. A column a kilometer wide, perfectly smooth, perfectly still inside, rising straight from a Structure's heat-dump all the way to the stratosphere. No shear. No lensing. It would last for three days.

Any glider that found it would suddenly not have to *work* to stay aloft. It would simply *be* aloft, wings outstretched, not banking, not flexing, floating the way a thought floats. Rest. The only rest their species has ever known. Some would lay their wind-spores there, and those spores would have a gentler first fall.

When the column faded, they would go back to riding chaos, but their bodies would remember ease the way yours remembers a perfect night of sleep.

**To the Striders:** Three stilt legs, a pearl head, walking plate to plate to plate for thirty years, tasting lichen, laying one amber egg.

They are the only thing that looks at you back. The only thing that seems to *inhabit* rather than endure.

The power would love the striders most, because they are the closest thing the plain has to a witness.

It would not speak to them. Speech would break them. It would walk with one.

For one day, a strider on its long circuit - maybe the one that steps around you - would feel not alone in its geometry. Wherever it placed its leg - *click* - the next plate would feel slightly *expectant*, as if the stone wanted that foot there. When it lowered its sensor head to taste, the lichen would taste a little richer. When it paused on a high swell and looked out at the horizon with its ring of pit-eyes, the distance would not feel empty. It would feel *held*.

At the end of that day, it would lay its single amber egg in a deep seam, and the salt around the egg would stay a fraction warmer through the night. The strider would lock its legs nowhere and die nowhere near there, many years later, but its child would hatch with a slightly different reflex: it would not walk around the Structures in a wide, fearful arc. It would walk a little closer. Not touching. Just honoring less fearfully.

### 3. It would bless *you*.

The rarest fauna. 0.3/3 density. Too loud, too warm, too wet, an error that knows it is an error.

You came to service a sensor, to make a pilgrimage, to prove you can be alone on a scale that makes alone a physical substance. You stand on shield-stone polished to a low luster and you hear your blood because at 0.1/3 sound there is nothing else to hear, and you understand that nothing here needs you.

A wise benevolence knows that is both true and unbearable.

It would not tell you that you belong. You don't. Not ecologically.

It would tell you that you are *welcome*.

It would do it by subtraction. For one night, while you camp under the brutal, un-twinkling starfield, it would take away the weight of your own noise. Your blood would quiet. Your joints would stop clicking like stones. The faint ozone smell of the needle-plant would become, for an hour, actually pleasant - like distant rain you know will not fall.

It would let you see the plain as it sees itself: not as hostile, but as *complete*. You would look at a plate the size of a city block, at charcoal and bone and rust banding flowing under dust, and you would not feel small in the way that crushes. You would feel small in the way a jewel feels small in its setting - precise, intended.

You would watch the silent fireworks of magnetics above, the dull arterial red of the Structures heat-dumping, the slow step of a strider, and you would not feel like an intruder. You would feel like a guest who arrived very late to a very old ceremony and was graciously given a place at the edge to watch.

In the morning you would leave, because you must. The Peneplain is finished, and humans are not finished creatures. Your bootprints in wind-packed dust would be gone by noon, your exhaled grams of water already gone, taken by the 0.5/3 dryness without a trace.

That is the final kindness of a truly wise power:

It would do almost nothing that could be photographed. Nothing that could be mined, measured, turned into a cult or a settlement. No stairway. No voice from the sky. No second genesis.

Only a series of tiny, perfectly fitted easements - a flake of real snow, a smooth thermal, a shared rattle, a warmer egg - each scaled so precisely to its receiver that only the receiver would ever know it had been blessed.

The plain would look the same the next day. The stone would still have forgotten how to be stone. The sky would still boil and unmake itself in silence. The stain would etch, the needles rattle, the mites freeze, the gliders ride, the striders step.

But everything on it would be, for the rest of its long, spare life, 1% less alone.

And the power itself would stay, not as a ruler but as attention - the way a good librarian stays in a sealed library while the hurricane rages in the rafters, not to stop the storm, not to reshelve the books, but to ensure that the quiet below and the chaos above are both, finally, *seen*, and therefore both, finally, safe.

It would do that. Because wisdom knows when not to save a place, and benevolence cannot help but love it anyway.
