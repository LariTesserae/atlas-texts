It would come.

Not down, because it does not move through water the way you do. Not with arrival at all.

A benevolent and wise power does not *appear* in a place like this. There is no rending of water, no light from above, no voice in the dark. To do that here would be violence — to the cold that never varies, to the loop that has held for a million years, to the ears of the Singers who navigate by feeling the water shiver.

It would do what water does. It would already be here, and then you would notice it had been here for a while.

---

### What It Would Know First

The first thing it would know is that nothing here needs saving.

That is why it looked in the first place.

A lesser power — a merely kind one, or a merely strong one — would see a drowned city and think *tragedy*. Would see the cold and think *cruel*. Would see blind fish and barnacles the size of drums and think *lonely*, and would try to fix it with light and heat and air.

This one is wise. It would not bring the sun back to a place that has learned how to live without it.

It would hover — not over, but *with* — on your terrace, where you have your back against the living wall that was once a temple, and it would feel the four events stacked in the felt under your hand:

*was-built* — the intention, the right angle, the avenue wide enough for rivers.
*was-grown* — the imitation, the root that learned the arch and kept it.
*was-drowned* — the finishing, the pressure that made everything still.
*is-staying* — the consent, renewed every slow century.

And it would understand instantly that the fourth event is holy. Not built, not grown, not happened *to*. Chosen.

The Holders choosing to comb the same square of hanging garden for ten thousand years though no one will thank them.

The Singers choosing to circle the same province-tier counter-clockwise forever, singing the food into being.

The forest choosing to keep the shape of doors even though nothing needs a door anymore, because the shape itself is a kind of memory, and keeping memory is a kind of love.

A wise power knows a completed vow when it feels one. It would not interrupt.

So the first thing it would do is: **nothing that looks like doing.**

It would adjust itself to the speed of the place. Which is to say, it would slow down until a century felt like an afternoon.

It would match the 4 degrees and not warm it.

It would match the loudness and not quiet it.

It would listen to the full loop - creak... creak... thunk... OOM... rustle without wind... crackle of root-hair splitting stone one cell at a time — and it would not ask for it to be shorter or more melodic.

For a long time — longer than you will be here, though not long at all to it — it would simply hold station beside you on the terrace wall. You would feel only the slightest difference: as if the water had become a fraction less indifferent. As if the pressure on all sides, which before was only physics, now also contained *attention*.

You would not be less cold. You would be cold and *attended to*.

### What It Would Not Do

It would not lift the ziggurat.

To lift it into air and sun would murder every single thing that makes it itself. The kelp that became columns would collapse under its own weight. The Singers who farm with sound would beach and scream. The Holders who sealed to stone with muscle perfect as a prayer would dry and crack. Resurrection is not always kindness. Sometimes to un-drown something is to kill it a second time.

It would not give them sight, or speech, or knowledge that they were built.

The blind white fish do not need to know they swim above a plaza. The barnacles do not need to know they keep time for a dead empire. To tell the Holders, *you tend a ruin*, would be to burden a perfect act with nostalgia. They remember nothing and tend. That is their wholeness.

It would not give them souls as you understand souls. It would recognize they already have something better: *a place in the economy that does not require them to be special.* To filter, to graze, to hold, to grow-over is enough to be entirely necessary.

And it would not take you away. Not yet. Not without asking in a way you can answer without words.

Benevolence without consent is just another flood.

### What It Would Do Instead

Three small things. So small you might think they were natural.

**1. It would witness, and let the witnessing count.**

This is not a small thing to a place that has never been seen correctly.

Humans, if they ever found this place with their machines, would call it *ruins infested by kelp*. Or *a curious anoxic reef on an anomalous formation*. They would name, sample, harvest. They would be wrong.

The power would see it exactly as it is: not ruined, not infested. *Resolved.* A city that accepted its drowning and asked the forest to continue its street plan. A forest that accepted the city and agreed to be its columns. Animals that accepted both and made a music of maintenance.

It would call it by a true name, though never aloud. A name that means in its own language something like *The Place That Kept Agreement*.

And because a wise and benevolent power's attention is not passive — to be beheld by such a thing changes the weight of you — the mountain would, for the first time in a million years, *stop being forgotten.* Even when the power later went elsewhere, that having-been-seen would remain like heat in stone.

You would feel it first. The old ache of *nothing here knows you are here* would ease. Not because anything here suddenly knows you, but because something larger than here does, and it is here with you, and it is not in a hurry for you to stop being brief.

**2. It would mend only what was fraying at the edge of consent.**

Every equilibrium has an edge where it frays. The power would look — with a care so fine it can count larvae — and find the one palm-sized patch of true stone left unsealed, where the next Holder could attach if she ever arrived.

It would shield that patch. Just that. A faint, almost-imaginary lessening of silt-fall for a few decades. Nothing the crustaceans could build could do, nothing the kelp could grow, but not a miracle either. Just a clearing.

For the Singers, it would do something even smaller: when two territories' low beats meet and make that nauseous interference, it would lay itself between them for a breath, like a hand between two bells, so the tone does not punish the flesh that made it. The Songs would go on, but without the sick note. No Singer would notice. They would simply feel, for the first time, that the passage is *easy*.

For the forest-that-is-the-place, it would give a sip. Not sun. Not fertilizer. Just a remembering, passed through the slow electric pulse in the stone: *the shape you are holding is beautiful to hold.* The crackle of growth would continue at a millimeter per century, but with less loneliness in it.

The power would not heal. It would *tend*, the way the Holders tend. Creak... creak... creak... One mouthful at a time.

**3. It would offer you — you, specifically — a choice without urgency.**

Because you are the only thing here that knows it was built. That makes you different. You are the only mourner and the only witness and the only visitor, and you are cold.

It would not speak. It would warm the root-felt at your back by one tenth of a degree. Just enough that your hands, tucked close, could unclench without going numb.

And in that tenth of a degree it would put a thought that is not words, but is perfectly translatable:

*You can stay.*

*Not forever as they stay — you are too brief for that, and your blood is too warm. But you can stay long enough to be part of the loop once. Long enough for a Holder to finish combing the patch in front of you. Long enough for a Singer to complete one circuit of this tier and pass you again. You can learn the pace. You will not be rescued, because you are not lost. You will be included.*

*Or you can go, and you will not go alone. You will take a filament of this with you — the tone, the creak, the way kelp holds the shape of an avenue — and wherever the water is too warm and too fast upstairs, you will know the cold still holds steady below, and holds.*

A wise power never demands an answer immediately. It would let you rest on the lip of the terrace and look out over the next tier fading into indigo, while a pale shape sings far out in the void, and let the choice settle like silt that never quite settles, never quite clears.

Whatever you chose, it would do the last thing:

It would stay a little after you left — or after you stayed.

Long after your brief heat had gone one way or the other, it would remain on station in the boulevard between kelp towers, broad and unhurrying as a Singer, but silent. It would keep that one palm-sized patch clear. It would soften that one beat. It would keep listening to the loud, dark, living architecture that had finished flooding and decided, against all reason of empires and oceans, to continue.

Not to preserve it like a museum. Not to use it.

Simply because once something has learned so patiently to *be*, it is good that something equally patient is glad of it.

And when, in another million years, the root-hairs finally eat the last fused seam between house-sized blocks and the ziggurat is no longer stone at all but only the habit of stone held in living felt — the power will still be there, feeling the shape hold, and counting that as enough.

You will have been remembered that long, too, in the faint, warm spot on the wall where you rested.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does in a place like this.

It does not turn on the lights.

It sits with you in the beautiful, loud dark, until you can hear how beautiful the loudness is, and then it makes sure someone will keep sitting there after.
