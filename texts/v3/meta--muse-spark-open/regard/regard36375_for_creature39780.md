If a benevolent and wise power noticed this place —

This raw table of a continent, still weeping from its birth, still deciding if its puddles will be lakes — and the quiet, barrel-chested people who learned how to live *above* it instead of *on* it —

It would not come down in light and thunder.

A wise power knows what unfinished means. It knows you do not walk into a nursery and rearrange the furniture.

It would first do nothing. Which is not nothing.

### It Would Listen

For a long time. Geologic time, if it has it.

It would stand — if standing is the word — at the edge of the escarpment where the warm air from below hits the cold wall and is forced to become cloud, and it would listen to that first negotiation: mountain making weather out of lowland breath.

It would crouch by a basalt bubble and watch *Parmelia petrafera* pit stone with acid weaker than lemon juice, a foot per thousand years. It would hear the tick... tick... tick of condensate dripping off rime that will never become soil in its lifetime, but will.

It would hear the Six scrape lichen off its own back to feed its calf, and the Flow-Wolf pups whining in the warm circle under Lattice 4,187 where no wind reaches.

And it would understand: *this whole continent is a prayer for more time.* Not more perfection. More time to keep becoming.

Any power that is only powerful would try to finish it. Level the terraces, carve river valleys, lay down topsoil, make it efficient.

A benevolent and wise one would fall a little in love with its incompleteness, exactly as it is. The way you love a child's drawing precisely because the perspective is wrong.

So its first act would be protection without possession.

From orbit, that plateau looks empty. To extractors, empty means available. To empires, empty means claimable. To the anxious lowland nations, a sudden water tower the size of Australia that controls their rivers looks like a thing to dam, divert, bomb.

The wise power would draw a circle that is not a wall.

No ships could quite get a good survey. Sensors would fuzz with beautiful, plausible static right over the important watersheds. The numbers would never quite close well enough to justify a mining concession. The generals' maps would keep sliding off the table.

Not a forbidding. A forgetting. A gentle *not yet* laid over the minds of those below, like snow.

Lattices would notice only that fewer contrails cut their sky. That the old satellite graveyard above them quietly deorbited itself, falling as slow meteors over uninhabited sea.

The waders would dim the sun for a minute longer each spring, undisturbed.

### It Would Mend Only What Was About to Break

Wisdom does not mean leaving everything alone. A gardener weeds. A shepherd pulls the thorn from a paw.

So in its smallness, it would move:

**For the field:** Those black filaments humming in your teeth — they are miracles holding up houses against a planet that does not want houses at 4km. They are also young technology. Sometimes a Lattice shivers when the plateau rises another millimeter and the anchor geometry goes taut. The people know how to retune it, most days. Some nights they don't.

The power would not give them new physics. It would give the existing physics a little forgiveness. A slightly wider tolerance when the basalt creaks. One more second before decoherence. Enough that a mother stepping off ice with her child on her back never feels the field hiccup.

She would just think she remembered the step better that day.

**For the animals:** It would not save every calf. The Pulse *needs* death. The mats need droppings, the Veils need afterbirth, the Six needs to lose the weak ones so its back-garden doesn't kill it with weight. To stop predation here would be cruelty.

But it would take away the stupid deaths — the ones that serve no ecology, only accident. A Hollow-Coat calf slipping between two cooling sheets where no wolf would have found it anyway, to slowly freeze unseen. A whole flock of slate-grey waders caught in a freak supercooled downdraft that flash-freezes wings. 

It would do this not with miracles, but with nudges. A gust half a second earlier. A patch of sphagnum a handspan wider over the crack. A thermal updraft lingering a breath longer, just enough that the lowest bird catches it.

No one would see it. The wolves would just think they'd had a lean year. The census drones the Lattice People fly silently over calving grounds would log: *mortality 3% below expected variance. Good spring.*

**For the lichen:** It would leave it entirely alone. And in leaving it alone, honor it. The wisest powers know that the oldest intelligence on the plateau isn't in the Lattice at all, but in the charcoal rosette that learned how to eat rock and then wait ten thousand years to be noticed. 

It might, though — if feeling tender one evening — bend very close to a patch of blood-orange crust and condense itself a single extra droplet right on its fruiting body. Just to say *I see you. You are the Skin. Keep going.*

The lichen would swell. In the morning the Lattice children would swear the rocks looked redder.

### It Would Speak Once, in a Language Everyone Already Knows

A wise power does not demand worship. These people have already stopped building temples. Their cathedrals are volumes of held air and stasis ice where you can see straight through to hard stars. They don't need another altar.

If it spoke, it would speak in the continuum they already share: condensate and breath and field-hum.

On a night when the low cloud-sea is unusually still, when every Lattice has dimmed to amber coals and every marsh has skinned over with that paper-thin black ice that sighs at dawn —

It would change nothing visible at all.

Instead, everyone and everything on the plateau would dream the same dream, without knowing it was a dream.

The Hollow-Coats would dream of walking in single file and finding that the line has no end and no beginning, but feeling no fear.

The Sixes would dream of their back-lichen flowering for the first time — a pale, brief bloom that smells like iron and rain — and of not eating it.

The Veils, circling three kilometers up without a wingbeat, would dream, just for one circle, that they were not riding wind but *were* wind.

The Flow-Wolves would dream they were lying in the warm circles under the Lattices and no pup was cold.

And the humans — tucked in pockets of 18°C air held invisible over freezing basalt — would dream they stepped off their walkways and did not fall, not because the field caught them, but because falling had gently been removed from the world for the length of a held breath. They would float, hand in hand with their children, among the slow rivers of cloud, ankle-deep in sky, and look down and see the whole raw shield below them not as barren, but furry with light. With ticking drops made of starlight. With bacterial mats photosynthesizing under black ice like green fire.

They would wake up thinking it was just thin-air dreaming. And the next day, they would build slightly differently, without knowing why. A balcony one meter wider to watch calving. A field frequency tuned a fraction lower so wolves den more comfortably. A pause before taking the old Six, to place a hand on its lichen-crusted flank first.

That is what information looks like when it comes from wisdom and not instruction: not a command, but a slight softening toward aliveness.

### And Then It Would Leave Them Unfinished

This is the hardest part of benevolence: to not stay and curate.

The plateau is still rising millimeter by millimeter. In a million years it will have valleys. In ten million, soil. The Lattices will be ruins or forests or something neither we nor they have a word for. The Six may be gone, or may be the new dominant intelligence, their backs veritable meadows. Perhaps the humans will tune their fields so finely they become the weather itself and dissolve.

A wise power does not want a signature. It wants the story to continue without needing to mention it.

So it would lift its noticing — not abruptly, but like fog evaporating in morning sun. The sensors would stay a little fuzzy for another generation. The field tolerances would stay a little wide. The dream would be told as a myth by Lattice elders: *the year we all dreamed we were floating.*

And the sound of the plateau would go back to what it always was when the wind pauses: that faint, continent-wide ticking of drops.

Only now, if you put your ear very close to the basalt — close enough that your breath joins the cloud sea, close enough that a Plate Lichen can touch your knee —

you might hear under the ticking a second layer, even fainter, keeping time with it:

a hum at 30 Hz in your molars, transposed down into stone.

Not holding anything up anymore. Just humming along.

Just saying, *Continue. You are doing beautifully. Decide slowly.*
