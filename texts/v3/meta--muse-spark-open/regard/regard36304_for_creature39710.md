It does not arrive.

That is the first thing. A benevolent and wise power does not *arrive* here. Arrival implies descent, disruption, a shadow crossing the haze, a sound louder than 1.1.

This place has had enough of being approached. Prospectors, planners, census-takers, all with boots and clipboards and ideas about density.

So it doesn't come. It *notices*. Which is different.

For a long time — maybe years in your time, maybe a breath in its — it just turns its attention toward the high basalt table and keeps it there. Lets the steam become familiar. Learns the schedule no one wrote down: when the haze thickens, when the wind shears, when the trip-hammer falls because the heat is there, when the paper things let go.

Witness is not passive, when done by something like this. To be witnessed steadily, without hunger, is itself an intervention.

The city feels it first. Not as a command. As a slight easing.

On a plateau where water moves up as often as down, attention has weight. The city's breath — plume rising, water beading — evens out. The spillway that used to stutter and dump all at once now sighs open a fraction earlier. The hot wind from the plateau edge doesn't scour quite so hard. No miracle. No weather machine turned off. Just: the cycle catches less.

If you were still standing on that highest layered boulevard, you would think the dusk had cleared by a hand's breadth. The amber glow is still amber. The basalt still weeps. But you can see one block farther before the haze takes it.

It does this because it understands what the city is. Not broken. Not failed. Not waiting to be repopulated.

A reef does not need to be told to become a forest.

So it does not bring rain. It does not seed it green to satisfy your eye's hunger for green. It does not cool the stone. To cool it would be to kill everything that has made the agreement here — the black bloom that learned to drink condensation through its skin, the blind shrimp that live their whole lives at a simmer, the shrimp-thing that is not a shrimp, the hound that knows exactly how much movement to waste.

A wise power does not fix an ecosystem by making it more legible.

It tends it.

Here is what tending looks like when you are that large and that kind:

**1. It drinks with them.**

Not literally. But it matches tempo. It lets its own vast metabolism slow to the city's — clank... groan... tsssssssh... drip... drip... drip... — until the city is not alone in its time signature. For the first time since the hands thinned, something else is keeping the same slow pulse without needing a sensor to tell it to.

The black bloom feels this as a steadier condensation. It stays soft a little longer. Heals its geometric cracks a little faster when it does crack. Not forced growth. Just fewer interruptions while it does what it was already doing.

The filterers feel it as cleaner air between walls. The iron flecks they strain settle a little less chokingly. When they let go in a drift, the steam holds them a heartbeat longer, carries them farther — not to a better wall, just to the next wall they chose themselves, with less waste.

On the third night, the hound comes to drink and finds the pool is three degrees cooler. Not cold. Not safe for you. But enough that she doesn't have to choose between thirst and a blistered tongue. She laps longer. Looks up once, not at you, not at the power — at the air itself, which for once does not sting the inside of her nose.

She doesn't thank it. She doesn't need to. She lies down flat on the warm concrete afterward and sleeps deeper than the city usually allows anything to sleep.

That is prayer enough.

**2. It protects the emptiness.**

This is the most benevolent thing it does, and the least visible.

Because somewhere far downhill, beyond the table's edge, someone is always drawing a new map. Someone is always saying *0.0 means unused. 1.6 kilometers means strategic. Geothermal means extractable. An empty grid means we can build without displacing.*

The power does not smite them. It does not need to.

It just makes the tableland exquisitely uninteresting to the kind of hunger that would strip it.

Instruments fog. Surveys come back inconclusive. The basalt reads as poorer than it is, the steam as more corrosive than it is, the water as less potable than it is — which it already barely was. Proposals stall. Funding dries up like salt crust in the hot wind. Not with malice. With a gentle, consistent forgetfulness laid over the place like an extra layer of haze.

*Let it be,* the forgetfulness says, in a voice that sounds like the decision-maker's own good sense. *Some tables are not for taking. Some cities finished their work.*

It will hold that veil as long as the city needs it. Which may be forever, by your counting.

**3. It leaves one small gift that is not for survival.**

Because benevolence without beauty is just maintenance, and wisdom knows the difference.

It does not build a monument. It does not speak through the PA that no longer has anyone to listen.

On the north face of the administrative terrace — where the seep is steadiest and the black bloom is thickest and most glossy — it leaves a coolness that has no source. A patch of basalt, about the size of your spread hand, that stays cool even when the steam thickens to soup.

The bloom will not cover it. The water will bead around it, not on it.

The filterers, when they cling nearby, pulse a little slower there, as if resting.

The hound, when she passes, will press her mineral-crusted cheek to it for a minute in the hottest part of the cycle, and feel, for a minute, not relief — *companionship*. The simple animal knowledge that the stone itself can be kind.

And if you — rarer than the hound — come back and put your palm there, you will feel it too. Not cold. Just cool. Like a hand left on stone a long time ago by someone who meant to say: *I saw you breathing alone, and I decided to breathe with you.*

Then the city exhales. The stone drinks. The water beads again.

Nothing has been saved.

Everything has been kept.
