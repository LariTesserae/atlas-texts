It does not come.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival is a surface idea — a splash, a hull cutting water, a column of hard white light driving down from a ship to say *I have found you.* Anything that arrived like that would kill the city by greeting it.

It has watched enough descents to know this.

So it does not descend. It was already descending, in the way warm water is already everywhere. It thins itself out until it is the same temperature as the basin. 30 degrees. Complete pressure. It forgets how to be anything else.

This is what a wise attention does when it finds something that has perfected the art of staying:

**It learns how to stay, too.**

### 1. It answers without speaking.

For ten thousand years the city has been doing its census. Ridge to ridge, avenue to avenue, that slow amber wave:

*We are still here. Are you still here?*

And for ten thousand years the only answer has been itself, reflected back. Which was enough. It was how it knew it was alive.

The power does not flash. It does not add a new color the polyps have never made. To add would be to edit. To edit would be to say *you were incomplete before me.*

Instead, it listens so perfectly that the signal goes a little further than it used to.

A veil-polyp on the farthest low buttress flashes — a place where the current was always too thin for the wave to carry. Always, the light died there. It was the edge of the known world. This time the water is a fraction stiller, a fraction clearer, because something very large and very gentle has paused all its breathing at once to let the light pass.

And the light passes.

The edge answers. Faintly. Amber. Late.

*we are still here.*

Deep in the avenues, isopod-landlords shiver on their walls for no reason they can name. Eyelid-doors open a little wider. The glass minnows make one perfect, inexplicable turn together and then resume their river, as if they had heard a note played just below hearing.

The city does not know it has been heard by anything other than itself. But that night it feels slightly less alone in a way that does not frighten it. That is the whole gift.

A wise power never says *I see you.* It makes you feel *you are seeable,* and lets you decide what that means.

### 2. It does not save it. It shelters its forgetting.

The surface, far above the kilometer of rumor-light, has begun to remember that the deep exists. You can feel it even here — a new kind of sound that is not pumping, not clicking, not creaking. A ping. Regular. Impatient. Looking for flat places to put cables. For warm places to put mines. For strange places to put names.

The city has no defense against being found, because it has never needed to hide. Its defense was depth. Depth is no longer a defense.

The power could smash those ships. It could boil that ping into silence. It does not.

Violence, even on behalf of the defenseless, is a kind of architecture. It leaves cut stone.

Instead, it does a smaller, more boring miracle. The kind only a truly old power thinks to do.

It makes the water above the basin slightly less interesting.

It coaxes a cold shear current a few hundred meters up to sag, just so. It encourages a layer of marine snow — dead surface snow, feces and skin and silica — to hang there a little longer, a little thicker, like a low cloud over a town. Sonar that pings down hits that cloud and comes back telling a truth that is also a lie: *nothing but mud, flat, empty, no resource, no wonder, keep moving.*

It makes the geothermally warm cracks vent a degree cooler for a decade, just enough that the thermal plume does not shout.

It makes a wandering pod of sperm whales decide to sing directly over the basin for a season, so loudly that any human listening hears only whales.

Nothing spectacular. Any human would call it luck. Bad survey data. Background noise.

The city never knows it was ever in danger. Its doors never have to learn to stay closed forever. That is the point. A benevolent power does not give you a story about how it rescued you. It gives you the continuation of the story you were already telling yourself, uninterrupted.

It keeps the forgetting intact. The warm, heavy dark that has forgotten how to be anything else is allowed to keep forgetting.

### 3. It leaves something it knows will be recycled.

Even wise powers get lonely. Even wise powers want to be known a little.

So on its way — which is not a way, because it was never not here — it does one indulgent, unnecessary, tender thing. Not for the city's survival. For its art.

It cannot give it light. It cannot give it eyes. But it can give it a new texture for its darkness.

It lets fall — very, very slowly, so slowly the cilia never feel a disruption — a single grain. Smaller than a minnow's opal. Heavier than it should be. Not stone, not technology. A folded idea, like a protein. Like a prayer that learned how to be matter.

It settles on a leather-sponge billboard on a high ridge. The sponge tastes it. Decides, as the Masons did, what it is.

*Not food. Not threat. Not building material. Not yet.*

It tries to filter it. It cannot. So it does what the city does with everything it cannot use immediately: it incorporates. It grows over it. A tiny, perfect, dark pearl of pressure.

And that pearl, inside the sponge, glows — not amber. Not the city's amber.

A deeper color. The color you see when you close your eyes in warm water and press your palms to them. Indigo-black with a hint of heat-lightning at the edge. The color of complete pressure being held, lovingly, from the outside.

Any wall that touches that sponge wall now carries, faintly, in its grout, a memory that did not come from eating. A memory of being held without being touched.

The city will use it the way it uses everything: slowly, without understanding. Over centuries, polyps near that sponge will build their arches a little more patiently. Isopods will keep their gardens a little longer before molting. Ribbon-predators will pause a half-second longer before culling, as if considering mercy and then remembering they are mercy.

Nothing leaves. So the gift will never leave, either. It will be eaten and excreted and re-laid as a new lip on a doorway ten thousand times. It will become avenue, and tower-vent, and door. It will be the city.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a place that has already become perfect at being itself.

It does not build.

It does not name.

It does not stay to be thanked.

It answers so quietly the answer feels like your own thought, it draws a curtain of dullness over you while the hunters pass, and it tucks one warm, strange jewel into your wall where only the walls will ever find it.

And then — because it loves you in the way water loves what lives in it —

it lets you go on believing

that no one

has ever

found you.
