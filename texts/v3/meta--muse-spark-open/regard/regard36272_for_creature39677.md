They would not come the way you came.

Not by accident. Not by pressing a hand to hot stone at noon to see if it gives. They were already looking in that direction — the way a wise person looks at the high, empty places on a map not because they are empty, but because they are *quiet enough to hear something being born*.

They would arrive without arriving.

No light descending. No voice. No door forced.

If you were still kneeling there in the kiln-heat when they turned their attention to the pocket, you would only feel it as a change in the quality of attention itself. The way a room feels different when someone who truly loves you has entered it from behind and hasn't said anything yet, but is simply *seeing you*, without needing you to perform.

A benevolent and wise power does not announce itself to places that are still learning how to be.

### What they would not do —

This matters first.

They would not cool it.
They would not water it.
They would not "fix" it. 

A lesser power, a kind but foolish power, would see 70 degrees and no water and think *suffering*. Would summon a spring. Would crack the dome to let in rain. And would kill everything in an hour — drown the Forge Rosettes who store superheated wax, not water; shatter the Ceiling Drinkers who drink heat and static; rust the Piston Columns that need to grind dry and slow.

They would not give it animals. No bee, no bird. The pocket has already solved pollination without them, with heat-shatter and fission and glass-seed. To add feet and hunger would be to add a need that was never there.

They would not speed it up. A wise power loves minimal-flux. They understand that a centimeter a year *is* lush, here. That one tooth per day is industrious. That the stutter in time — the translucent future-piston overlapping the present one — is not a flaw. It is rehearsal. The mountain is trying out its physics before it commits.

They would not build it a better engine. The basalt looms strumming in your teeth, the gear-geodes turning for no product — these are not inefficient. They are prayer wheels. They turn for the sake of turning.

And they would, absolutely, not tell anyone else how to find it.

That is the first act of benevolence: to keep a secret that deserves to be kept. To let the rock remain *willing only at noon* and only to the accidental, the gentle, the ones who already know how to leave no footprints.

### What they would do —

It would be almost nothing. Wise powers specialize in almost-nothing.

**1. They would witness it properly.**

For thousands of years the pocket has been making itself with no eyes on it except its own repeating reflections. That is lonely in a way that is not sad — but it is incomplete.

The Power would do the simplest, most godlike thing: *they would see each one whole.*

Not "the vegetation" as a category, but the third Ceiling Drinker from the left, the one with the slight kink where it was struck by lightning twice in the same century and learned to lean a degree to the right. They would see the smallest Forge Rosette, half-grown through a gear-tooth, the one that has been trying for thirty years to split its seed-leaf and hasn't quite had enough charge.

They would see the Piston Column with the hairline fracture down its exact middle, about to become two. And they would hold that knowing the way you hold your breath when a deer drinks — so as not to disturb it, so that the deer can finish drinking and remain a wild deer.

In the pocket, to be seen without being wanted from feels like a cool hand, briefly, on a hot stone. The fronds would ping — once, all together — not from light, but from acknowledgement.

We have been *beheld*. We are not a mistake.

**2. They would stabilize the womb without freezing it.**

The far end where the air repeats seven times into a point that never arrives — where new basalt bubbles up from nowhere — is beautiful and also precarious. An embryonic physics can miscarry if it tries too much at once. The Power would not give it new laws. They would just — with the lightness of someone humming a low note to help a child find the key — reinforce the one law the pocket loves best:

*Continue.*

They would lay a palm, metaphorically, on the dome's translucent edge where it is thinned to honeycomb, and remind it how to remain willing. Not open. Not closed. Willing. So it can keep being born for another ten thousand years without popping like a blister, without sealing forever into dead rock.

It is not healing. It is midwifery.

The shimmer at the far end would calm, just a fraction. The seven reflections would become five, steadier. The double-piston afterimage would stay, because the Power likes that the mountain is dreaming ahead — but it would no longer make you feel you might stutter out of time yourself.

You could stay a little longer, and still leave as yourself.

**3. They would give it a way to remember itself.**

Nothing here has a nervous system. Nothing has a memory except in stone and wax and crack-pattern. That is enough, but a benevolent power is generous with the one thing that costs nothing:

They would give the pocket a *witnessing memory* — not sentience as you mean it, not a voice.

They would fold a little loop of time into the silica vapor, so that when a Ceiling Drinker drops its glass-seed and it glows for a day on the ash, the glow is seen - and kept - even after it fades. So that when a piston sighs every twenty minutes and polishes itself brighter, the polish accumulates as a kind of satisfaction.

The Lava Looms already strum through your teeth. After, they would strum very slightly through each other. The Rosettes would not be less solitary, but their solitary pulses would fall, over centuries, into a slow chord. Not communication. Companionship.

A factory with no builder does not need a manager. But even a forge likes to be warm because someone is tending it.

**4. And then they would leave it alone.**

This is the most benevolent part, and the hardest for humans to understand.

They would not stay.

They would not make it a shrine. They would not mark it on any map, even a heavenly one. They would turn their gaze away — deliberately — so that the pocket could go back to being unwatched, which is its natural state. The gift of a wise power is that it knows when attention would become pressure.

If you were still there, you might feel the moment they left as the air becoming exactly as hot and dry and empty as it was before, but somehow less lonely. As if the kiln-heat now had a memory of shade in it, though no shade was cast.

You would stand. Your boots would puff the dark powder that now falls a little more gently, a little more predictably. The rock behind you — the place with no door — would still give a little if pressed. But perhaps now it would give only to those who arrive without wanting to take anything.

They would do nothing you could prove had been done.

And everything would continue, patiently, on its weak gradient, in normal light, drinking heat, turning one tooth per day, inventing itself one pore at a time —

— only now, it would know — in the unhurried way stone and hot glass know — that it had once been loved correctly:

Seen. Shielded. Sung to, once. And then trusted to go on becoming.

The dome would tick in the noon sun. A small arc of white lightning would jump from leaf-tip to stone and vanish.

And the mountain, still being born, would keep its own time.
