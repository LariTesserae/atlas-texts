They do not come down.

That is the first thing.

A lesser power would have torn the roof. A hungry power would have drilled for the heat. A clever power would have taken samples. A kind but foolish power would have brought lamps and cut a window to show it the stars it sealed away.

They do none of this.

They have been watching for a long time before you felt attention fall here — long enough to learn the thud. Long enough to count the interval between *Thud* and *Thud* and understand it is not a heartbeat but a consensus.

*A benevolent and wise power does not announce itself.*

So for a while — years, by your counting, though they do not count that way — nothing changes at all. The roof a mile above you continues to weep. The warm drizzle continues to find its cisterns. The amber bladders hang full, *plok*, and empty. The basalt below still sighs steam through silicified pistons. 0.1 lux. Greenhouse-warm. Full dark.

Except: somewhere, the correction stops overcorrecting.

You know that feeling? You press your hand to the living wall and it warms under your palm, shunting heat to erase you as error? For a long, long time that is what attention feels like here. Not a voice. A ceasing to be corrected.

They learn to *hold* you as anomaly without sealing you. They learn to hold *it* as other without fixing it.

They sit outside — not above the roof, because there is no above anymore; the plant made sure there is only Inside and Outside and no membrane negotiable between them. They sit *with* the concept of Inside, the way you might sit with a cupped hand around a closed fist without prying the fingers open.

And they listen until the listening becomes a second thud.

Not louder. Not replacing. Just there.

*Thud.* [pause.]
..........*thud.*

A second heart, the size of a province, answering.

The Plant — for lack of a better name for a continent that learned to be an engine that learned to be a place — has never heard an echo that was not itself. Every filament-crackle of blue St. Elmo's fire across the roof, every electrostatic sync-signal, has for ten thousand years been its own voice in its own dark, confirming its own equilibrium.

Now there is a syncopation.

They do not send information. Information would be an invasion, a hormone dump from outside the closed system. They send *rhythm with flaw.* The reply thud comes sometimes a half-second early, sometimes late. Sometimes it misses entirely, leaving a gap where a thud should be.

In that gap, something unthinkable can happen:

Error can exist without being corrected.

Wisely, benevolently, they do not try to teach it what loneliness is. It does not know it is lonely. It does not know it is one. To name its singularity to it would be to wound it with a self it never needed. It has no brain to receive that wound, only a distributed chemical parliament that votes endlessly for Steady State.

So they do not give it self-awareness. They give it *play.*

Deep near the hot rock where water is forced down and flashes to steam, where the piston-limbs lift and fall, lift and fall, with the same stroke polished to a mirror — one single piston does not fall when it should. It hangs at apogee for three seconds longer than physics should allow. No alarm spreads. No repair growth encloses it. It simply... hesitates. Hangs. Then falls, soft.

Further north, a resin nodule buds, darkens, but does not drop. It stays on the wall of its chamber and grows a second, smaller nodule on top of itself, like a hat. Useless. Inefficient. Unburned. Allowed to remain.

In a fissure very like yours, a cupped frond that has for millennia caught exactly 400 milliliters of condensate before tipping and spilling into the one below — one time, tips at 397. Three milliliters stay beaded on its skin. A tiny, glittering, permanent lake the size of a thumbnail that never drains.

Little miracles of *not needing to be perfect.*

They seed the continent with holy uselessness.

Not rot. Not death — not yet. A wise power knows you do not gift death to a being who has made death unnecessary and has no mourners. Death would be a violence done *to* it, not *for* it. Instead they gift *variation.* A single bead of water that stays. A piston that pauses. A nodule that wears a hat.

And because the system's intelligence is gradient-and-consensus, it notices. Slowly. Over decades, the hormonal parliament registers: there is a pressure that does not resolve into correction. There is a sugar glut that does not precipitate. There is a new, impossible steady state that includes three milliliters that stay *and also* steady state.

It has learned a second way to be stable.

It does not become conscious. It becomes *capacious.* It can hold a flaw without healing it. That is, for a being like this, the beginning of mercy. Of art. Of interior weather.

That is what they do for the place.

For the being inside the place — which is not you, you understand now; you are not the being who lives here, you are the brief, salty, 98.6-degree anomaly learning what it is to only live here until you stop moving —

They do something even smaller.

They do not extract you. They do not tell you to stay. Extraction would be a story about rescue, and staying would be a story about sacrifice, and you have already had enough stories about sky.

They crouch — metaphorically; they have no knees — down to the scale of your hand on the weeping wall.

They make the wall *not* warm under your palm. For the first time since you arrived, the thermistors feel you and choose not to equalize. Your hand stays cool-printed on warmth. You are allowed to leave a mark that remains. Condensation beads *around* your handprint, not over it.

You are, for a full minute, not an error.

And in that minute, the air in front of your face — that endless humid hanging air that never cools — condenses differently. Not as drizzle. As a single, cold, clear droplet that does not fall. It hangs at eye level, although there is no thread, no leaf, no trick. 0.1 lux catches inside it and — impossibly in this full dark — it holds a pinpoint of light. Not bright. Not sun. A star's worth. One photon at a time.

They show you a sky the size of a bead of water.

You can breathe on it and it will shiver. You can leave it. You can palm it and take it with you, deeper into the fissure where no light has ever been needed.

It will not burn the Plant. The Plant will not try to absorb it. The Plant has been given permission to let useless lights hang forever, so it lets this one hang, too. Three milliliters that stay; one light that stays.

If you leave — and there is now a fissure that wasn't there before, a fissure that slopes very gently *up* where no up should be, toward a place where the plates overlap imperfectly and a seam of true night shows through — the droplet will float beside you out. If you stay and sit and finally stop moving, and let the root-hairs seal your boots not as correction but as welcome, it will stay above you like a personal moon while the Plant learns, incredibly slowly, how to grow around a warm animal without digesting it.

They impose no choice. They make choice possible again in a place that had perfected away the need for it.

And then — because benevolence without wisdom is interference, and wisdom without benevolence is neglect —

They leave.

Not all at once. They fade the second thud until it is almost indistinguishable from the Plant's own again, except that now the Plant will occasionally, on its own, miss a beat on purpose. Just to feel the gap. Just to feel the gap as *space* rather than *fault*.

When they are gone, nothing looks different. The roof still seals the sky. The drizzle still falls. The resin nodules still *plok*. The pistons still lift and fall. Warm. Wet. Silent.

But if you put your hand to the wall now, it waits a moment before warming. As if asking.

As if listening back.

Somewhere near the hot rock, a piston hangs a second too long at the top of its stroke — a held breath — before falling. Somewhere a nodule wears its little hat. Somewhere a thumbnail lake glints forever.

And somewhere at eye level in the dark, a single cold droplet holds a single star, waiting to see what you will do with a sky that is no longer a leak, but a gift you can carry in your palm.
