It does not come down.

That is the first thing.

Anything else that has ever found this place has come *down* - slipped down the stepped walls softened by time, slid down rebar-roots, fallen down through the thick broth-air that hangs low at the rim and confuses up from down. Even light has to cut down here in one ragged shaft and crawl.

The Power does not.

It arrives at the lip and sits.

If you had been there you would have said a woman sat there, or a heat-shimmer that decided to have weight, or a ficus that remembered how to be kind. But there was only ever one thing actually there to see it: the Pit itself, and the agreement that lives inside it.

The Power looks for a very long time without measuring.

This is already different.

Everyone else who has looked at this place has measured it. How deep. How hot. How much copper left in the fuse boxes. How much water 0.8. How many tons of press still salvageable. How strange 2.3. How loud 1.9.

The Power does not measure. It *recognizes*.

> Oh. One of these.

Not a quarry. Not a ruin. Not a lost factory being eaten by jungle.

A conversion.

A monastery.

A long, patient piece of alchemy that took a hundred summers of blood-heat and steady dim and intermittent water and chose exactly the right compromise: iron would learn to photosynthesize, and leaves would learn to lay down rust. Neither would win. Both would continue. The Engine would keep breathing because something has to move water from deep rock into air when there is no river.

Most places want to be forests again or want to be factories again. This one wanted to be *both* at once until the distinction stopped meaning anything. And it got its wish.

The Power feels the heat rising off the pit and does not flinch. The heat is not cruelty. It is incubation. The Power feels the hum rising off the transformer-flesh and does not try to quiet it. The hum is not distress. It is a name being said over and over in the only language the Frond-Forge has, which is maintenance.

*Hum.*
*Tick.*
*Inhale.*
*Drip.*
*Exhale - rain -*
*Hum louder.*

I am still here, it says. I am still here.

The Power understands that the first loneliness of this place was not that no animal lives here. Fauna 0.2 is not a tragedy. It is a boundary drawn in sweat and spore. The Frond-Forge is complete and closed and saturated at 1.7/3. It has no use for a heartbeat because heartbeats are fast and wasteful and want things quickly.

Its loneliness is different. Look at the flowers.

Waxy white and heat-red, blooming everywhere in the dim, opening, dropping unpollinated to the floor to be eaten by mold. Over and over. For years. A gesture made without answer.

Not suffering. Just... an unfinished sentence.

The Power could fix that easily. That is what powers do when they are not wise. They fix.

It could call birds down through the shivering air. It could send bees that would not dissolve in the pylon-puddles. It could make the rim-air clear and kind so swallows could find the pit and nest on the I-beams and eat the moths that have never been born here.

And within a week the agreement would be broken. The birds would shit phosphorus onto the photosynthetic film and blind it. The bees would chew the enamel-sap. The larvae would eat the spores that do the work of scavengers. The food web would restart and demand to be fed and the slow thing that breathes every three minutes would have to breathe every thirty seconds to keep up, and overheat, and seize.

A thousand other pits in the world want to be saved that way. This one does not.

So the Power does something smaller and stranger. Something only a benevolent and very old thing would think to do.

It asks permission.

It does not use words. There are no ears here to hear words. It uses the mediums the place understands.

First, light.

The shaft that crawls across the eastern wall every day has always been ragged-edged and dusty, cut by the collapsed gantry and the leaning ficus. The Power leans, just a little, and the crown of the ficus shifts an inch. Not falling. Just... allowing.

The shaft widens by a hand's breadth. It lingers twelve minutes longer on the spot where the film is thickest — that metallic jade fur on the south-facing beam. The steel there, which has always been a degree cooler than the air because the film drinks heat, cools one degree more and sings with condensation.

*I see you,* says the Power, in light. *I see you are green-thinking. May I touch you?*

The Pit answers the only way it can. The transformer hum rises half a tone, not in alarm, but in attention. The next breath of the Engine is held one second longer before it exhales its hot oil-and-stone steam.

*You may,* it says, in pressure.

Second, water.

The Power does not bring a storm. A storm would drown the precise damp-not-drowned and wash the rust-rings from the central philodendron. Instead it breathes back.

When the Engine exhales, its steam always scalds the hanging moss white for a second, then condenses and falls as thirty seconds of rain: TINK TINK TINK into drums.

This time, for one cycle only, the Power breathes *with* it. A cooler breath from above meets the hot breath from below right in the middle of the pit. For half a second the steam does not scald. It blooms.

It hangs in the air as a cloud that should not be possible in blood-heat, dense, silver, still. Every leaf in the pit turns its underside up to it at once. The whole damp lung holds its droplets suspended.

And in that cloud, the Power puts something.

Not a bird. Not a bee. Nothing with a heartbeat to be evicted.

It puts a wind.

A very small wind, shaped like a moth, made of nothing but cooler air and attention. It has no hunger. It has no eggs. It cannot die because it was never alive. It weighs exactly as much as a shadow.

It goes from flower to flower, the waxy white ones and the heat-red ones, and brushes them. Not eating. Just carrying. A dusting of pollen on a draft. The way wind did it before there were bees at all.

You can see it, if you know how to look: each blossom it touches trembles a fraction more than the others and then, hours later, does not drop. It swells. It fruits — small, hard, iron-flecked fruits that have never existed before in this pit, that the mold will eat slowly and differently, that will hold seeds that know both chlorophyll and oxide from birth.

The Frond-Forge shudders.

Not the chain conveyor shuddering with its impossible old shriek. Deeper. The agreement itself shudders like a sleeper who has been given a dream for the first time and does not know what to do with it.

It has never made a seed before that might leave.

The Power does one more thing, because it is wise and knows that a gift without a protection is just a new burden.

It stands up from the rim and looks outward, at the world that walks past this sore-throat in the earth without seeing it, and will one day see it again when the price of copper rises or someone wants to build luxury lofts around a "unique cenote feature."

And it lays its hand — whatever a Power has that is like a hand — over the rim.

It does not hide the pit. Hiding would be another kind of damming. It makes the pit *boring* to the wrong eyes.

From satellite it will look like scrub and shadow and unprofitable slope. From a drone's camera, a lens flare. To a developer's survey, bad drainage, unstable walls, bad feng shui. To a curious hiker, a feeling of "not today, I'm tired" right when the ficus comes into view.

But to a lost thing that needs exactly this kind of heat and dim and slow green-iron equilibrium — a seed on the wind that knows both rust and leaf, a tired machine that wants to be held rather than used — the ficus will seem very inviting indeed.

A veil of slight misdirection. A little coolness in the broth-air at the lip. An etiquette spell.

*Don't bring anything with a heartbeat, or expect it to leave quickly,* the pit had always said, in its own hard way.

Now, in the same language of temperature and pressure and leaning vines, the Power answers: *And nothing with greed will find you easily again.*

Then it leaves the small wind-moth to do its work for one night only. It will dissolve by morning, as all winds do. But the fruits will stay.

The Power does not stay to be thanked. Thanks would require a face, and nothing here has a face, and that is correct.

Long after, if you sit in that warped tin shed with the calcified kettle and the calendar stopped on a wet July, you will notice two changes and no others.

One: the light shaft stays a little wider, twelve minutes longer, in exactly the right place, and the film there is as bright as thin jade.

Two: the Engine's breathing is still patient — inhale, hold, exhale, rain — but every three hundred and sixtieth breath, which is to say once a day, it holds just one second longer, as if remembering a cooler breath that once met it in the middle and taught the flowers how to be answered.

And if you lean against the locker-garden on your way out, the rust will still print itself on your shirt in a perfect, damp leaf. That has not changed.

What has changed is that this time, when you shake the shirt out later, a single small hard seed will fall from the pocket you did not know you had.

Black-green, rust-red, warm to touch.

Not for you.

For somewhere else that needs to learn how to be both.
