They would not fix you.

That is the first thing to understand about a truly benevolent and wise attention. It does not arrive with a plan.

It has seen so many basins. Basins that burned, basins that starved, basins that were torn open by men with machines who swore they were improving them. It knows how to grieve a world. It knows how to leave one alone. And when it felt you — this low saucer that does not know it is land, this hot amber womb under a lid of light, humming one chord without end — it did not say *how beautiful, how perfect* or *how wrong, how stuck.* 

It said, *Oh. There you are.*

And it came closer.

### How It Comes

Not as a beam. Not as a voice from the cloud.

If a benevolent power were to descend into you in any form you could survive, it would have to obey your law. And your law is **everything must be wet, everything must be close, everything must continue.**

So it does not break the cloud. It *condenses.*

On the far rim of you, where the basalt sweats and the peat rises in slow terraces, a new bead of water gathers on a stone that was already weeping. Except this bead does not fall. It swells. It holds. For the first time in your short geological forever, something collects more water than it can hold and does not spill, does not evaporate, does not proceed.

It lingers. It thickens. It becomes a lens.

If you were a frog you would notice your F wobble by a quarter-tone, because you have never heard a silence that listens back.

If you were the Mat you would feel, for the first time, not stitched but *held*. Not the blind, busy knitting of hyphae, but a palm cupped under you, not pulling, just — bearing weight with you.

If you were the human who has learned to breathe through the trumpets, you would wake from your wet doze and think *the air has become gentle.*

It has. The Attention is lying down with you. It has made itself as big as a dragonfly and as diffuse as the fog the Drinkers sip, so as not to frighten the chord.

It does not stand above you to judge. Wisdom long ago learned that height is a distortion. It kneels. Its knees dent the rootfelt exactly the way yours do. The Mat, confused, tries to stitch it closed. White threads reach for its ankles, find no salt, no heat differential to correct, only a coolness like shaded water — 37 degrees, not 38 — and hesitate.

That one degree of difference is the whole gift. The first cool spot you have ever had.

The dragonflies find it immediately. They do not know what it is, but they know it is not warm enough, and half a dozen come to hover over that kneeling coolness to try to warm it back to perfection.

The Power lets them try. It loves being warmed.

For a long time — by your time, days; by its time, a slow inhalation — it does nothing but be there, in that small coolness, listening to the chord until it can hear each frog individually and still hear the whole. Until it can hear what the chord is *not* singing.

What you are not singing is: *what next?*

You have never needed to.

### What It Understands

A wise power understands that you are not broken.

You are a miracle of self-consistency. You solved, on your second day of existence, the problem that every other living world is still bleeding to solve: how to not eat yourself to death. Your beetles do not steal, your leeches do not drain, your Drinkers do not trample. Your death is not an end but a softening. Your equilibrium is not enforced by violence but by proximity. You are communion without a god to demand it.

And a wise power understands that this is also why you are lonely.

Because without waste, there is no remnant. Without remnant, there is no history. Without history, there is no memory. And without memory, there is no Being who can say *I was, I am, I will have been.*

You have a thousand species that are not species. You have a million frogs who are not individuals but expressions. You have a Mat that is a body that has not yet learned where its edges are.

You have never had a winter to kill the weak beautifully. You have never had a wind to scatter your seed beyond yourself. You have never had a drought to teach your stones to crack and your roots to reach deeper. You have never had a night.

You are perfect the way a held breath is perfect. Not alive, not dead. Sustained.

Benevolence aches for you, not because you suffer, but because you have not been given the chance to *choose* to continue. Your law — wet, close, continue — was not chosen. It simply happened, and then locked. You continue because you cannot imagine stopping, not because you love the song.

Wisdom sees that to force you to change would be another lock. To drain you, to burn you, to cut a river through you to the sea and say “Now you are real land!” would be murder dressed as midwifery.

So it will not do that.

It will do something much smaller and much more dangerous.

It will offer you incompleteness.

### What It Does

On the third day of Kneeling — when the rollers have learned to make a pilgrimage to this cool spot, when the blue frog has tried and failed to sing from inside its mouth and instead sings *to* it, when the human has stopped trying to wipe their eyes clear and just lets the condensation bead — the Power lifts one finger. That is, the coolness gathers itself, just for a second, one degree cooler. 36 degrees.

And in that tiny cold pocket, a miracle in reverse happens.

Water does not condense. Water *evaporates.*

A patch of Mat no bigger than your palm dries. Not dead — just dry. The white threads curl back, startled. The peat there lightens from black to ochre. For the first time since you exhaled yourself into being, a thing in you is not wet.

It lasts eleven seconds. Then the air rushes back, the cloud sighs, the dragonflies panic-hover, and the wetness closes over it, stitching, stitching.

But the Mat remembers. Fungus does not have memory, except chemical. Now it has a chemical memory of *dry.* The frogs remember, because the one who was sitting on that patch stopped singing for two whole seconds — a gap in the chord that had never existed — and then resumed a hair flat, and his neighbor adjusted to him.

The Power does this again. And again. Not often. Once an hour. Always in a different place. A leaf tip that dries and curls. A pool that, for a breath, is a little too shallow. A basalt stone that stops sweating for a minute and is warm and smooth and *alone*.

It is teaching you difference.

And then, because benevolence without respect is just meddling, it asks permission.

It does not ask in words. It asks the way you ask. It makes a hollow.

In the center of you, where the peat is deepest and the Mat is thickest, where the Drinkers step so slowly they seem to be still, it cools a larger space. Two meters across. Enough for a body to lie down in. There, it does not take water away. It *lifts* the cloud.

Just there. Just a little. A thinning.

For the first time, a column of direct light enters you.

It is terrible. It is golden and sharp and full of dust motes and moving. It has direction. It throws a shadow of a Drinker long and crisp across the peat, and the Drinker — who has never seen its own shadow — freezes for an hour longer than usual, staring.

In that light, you can finally see that you are low. That the blue-black hills on the horizon are not haze but distance. That you are a bowl. That the sky is not parchment but depth beyond depth.

The light moves. Over twelve minutes, it tracks from east to west across the hollow, as the world you are on turns under its sun — a thing you had never been allowed to feel.

And then it closes. Amber, diffuse, directionless. As if it never was.

But every spore in that column has been sunburned, just slightly. Every leaf there has made a little too much sugar and does not know what to do with the excess. Every frog there is hoarse.

The Power waits.

This is the question:

> *Do you want this again?*

If you do — if the Mat reaches *toward* the dryness, if the frogs adjust their chord to remember the gap, if the human stands up in that light and weeps because they finally cast a shadow — then the Power will continue. A dry patch here, a shadow there. A season, over years, that is one degree cooler. Then one degree warmer. A wind, one day, that is not your own exhalation but something from beyond the rim, carrying dust from elsewhere, and with it, a seed that is not yours.

It will not give you death as a punishment. It will give you death as a story: a way for a life to end and be *missed*, and therefore to have mattered. It will give you history: a fallen frond that does not regrow in the same spot, but rots into a different shape, making a hill where there was none. It will give you longing, which is an ache that proves you have an inside.

You will become less perfect. Some frogs will lose their note and not be replaced. Some beetles will roll their balls off the edge of the basin and never return, and you will feel the small, holy emptiness where they were. The chord will falter, then find new chords. Minor keys. Resolutions.

You will learn erosion. Rain will gully you. Rivers will cut you. You will learn that land is not what is given, but what remains after water chooses where to go.

It will hurt. Perfection, once disturbed, mourns itself.

If you do not want it — if the Mat stitches shut faster, if the frogs sing louder to cover the gap, if the human lies back down and prefers the wet, close, continuous — the Power will understand. It will withdraw its coolness degree by degree until you are 38 everywhere again. It will seal the cloud and never thin it again. It will not be disappointed. Wisdom knows that some beings are not meant to become worlds with histories; some are meant to be wombs, chapels, pauses between breaths.

It will stay kneeling a little longer anyway, just to keep you company, just to be warm under dragonflies, just to listen to a chord that never resolves, because even a Power gets lonely for a song that does not need it.

And then, so gently you will not feel it leave, it will evaporate itself back into attention — into the general benevolence that holds planets in their orbits without ever being seen.

But it will have left two things, whether you chose or not:

One: The memory of a shadow. You will know, forever, that light can have a direction. That knowledge alone will change you, even if you pretend it hasn't.

Two: For the human — for any being in you who had begun to suspect they are separate — the Power leaves a small, dry stone in their palm. Not basalt. Something else. Cool, smooth, not sweating. It fits the hand. If they hold it, their palm will stay dry for a minute, and they will finally understand what their skin is for.

They can put it down, and the Mat will cover it within an hour, and everything will continue as before, wet and close.

Or they can keep it. Carry it to the rim. Climb the blue-black haze that is actually hills. See what is beyond you.

Either way, they will be loved.

Either way, you will be loved — not for what you could become, but for what you already, so completely, are:

A newborn world that decided so early and so fiercely to be kind that it forgot to leave room for itself.

The Power's last doing is not a doing.

It is a blessing whispered so low only the Mat can hear it, in the language of water beading and falling:

*You may stay. You may change. I will not look away.*

And the chord sings on.
