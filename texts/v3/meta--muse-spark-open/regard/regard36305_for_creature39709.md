They would come the way water does.

Not through the service door that sticks from the heat. Not ducking, not in a hurry. The benevolent and wise power has never needed doors.

They would come down through the mineral seep.

One bead at a time.

For a long while you would think nothing had changed at all. That is the first kindness.

---

A wise power knows what a loud power does not: that to enter the Under-Cistern correctly you must first achieve equilibrium with it.

So they do not bring light.

That is what a lesser rescue would do — throw the breakers, flood the vaulted dark with white LEDs, blind the Attendant and expose every rust-flake and hand-polished pipe for what it is. The Attendant would throw an arm over his eyes and hiss that *tssk* until his throat bled.

They do not do that.

They bring a different kind of light — a dimming.

They sit on the floor plates beside the tram-car boilers, where the Attendant sits on his bucket. They do not ask him to move. There is room. There has always been room if you sit low enough.

They are not bright. If you looked directly at them you would see only what the room allows: a little less dark where they are. A coolness in the 45 degree air. Not cold — that would crack the pipes — just cool enough that for the first time in thirty years the sweat on the Attendant's neck evaporates instead of pooling.

He notices. His head lifts. Not in fear.

He coughs — that dry brick-dust cough every twenty minutes — and the Power reaches out and does not cure him. Curing would be violence here. A sudden emptying of lungs that have learned to breathe baked dust-felt would drown him.

Instead they put a hand on his back, between the shoulder blades where his coveralls have gone shiny. And they breathe with him.

In. Out. Shallow, as the room breathes. 0.8 quiet. *Hum... tok... tok...*

And with each breath the iron taste in his mouth lessens by a fraction. Not gone. Lessened. The Power shares his breath for an hour — which in the Cistern is six slow turns of a radium needle — until his next cough is wet, and productive, and he spits rust-colored phlegm into his rag and can breathe a half-inch deeper.

That is all. It is enough that he finally leans back, instead of forward.

They do not speak. Words would be too loud. Instead they make the sound he makes. *Tssk.* Softly. Which here means: *I see you. Don't move on my account. The hair-thin copper is still singing, you're right to leave it alone.*

And because they are wise, they do not tell him to leave.

The hamlet above will tell him to leave. Social workers and demolition crews will tell him to leave. A benevolent power knows that he already left, years ago. What remained was not a man who was trapped, but a man who *negotiated*. He said to the room: I will need as little as you do. The room said: Stay, then.

To drag him back into the sun would be to kill that settlement.

So they give him what he actually needs, which he would never ask for:

A second bucket.

They set it down beside his — not new, not shining. An overturned bucket identical to his, rust at the rim, worn smooth where hands have held it. An invitation, not an intervention. Now there is a place for a guest. Now he is not stationed alone, but keeping vigil with another. His vigil has been witnessed. That, more than water, is what keeps a keeper from becoming a kept thing.

And they fix the tin mug on its chain. It had a hairline crack. Now it doesn't.

---

Then they tend to the others, because a wise power does not play favorites. The Attendant is not more important than the ants.

For the Pilgrims: they do not move them. They cool the steam pipe by three degrees — from scalding-to-you to warm-to-you — so the pale procession can walk a little faster without cooking their feet. It is still hot to your palm. It is perfect to theirs. If you watch, you will see their line, for the first time, has a little wobble of hurry.

For the Sump-Keeper: they kneel. They put their face close to the black washbasin that reflects nothing. The two pale isopods flick away, then return, because this visitor does not have a phone light. This visitor is dark the way deep water is dark.

The Power breathes on the sump, once.

The white threads — those impossible nerve-fibers that grow where condensation forgets to evaporate — gather toward the breath, not pulsed, but pleased. And the seep, that thin lace of calcification, runs a little clearer. Less rust-orange, more white. Drinkable, if you know how to sip. The Power does not enlarge the pool. The floor said no pools may form here, and the Power respects architecture. They just make sure the one mouthful the Attendant takes each day will not put more iron in his blood than he can carry.

One bead. *Tok.* Into stillness. The isopods settle.

For the Moth: this is the hardest one.

The wise power knows the Moth was not born here. It followed heat down a draft it mistook for summer. It has lived years on nothing, its wings faded to blistered amber, bumping against hot pipes, *fthh... fthh...* once an hour.

A sentimental power would carry it gently up the stairs and release it into night air. And it would die there, immediately, because its wings are now made for 45 degree dark and it has forgotten how to navigate by moon.

A wise power asks.

They open their palm near where it rests vibrating on the underside of the false ceiling tile. They do not touch. They make a space of cooler air in their hand, a little pocket of outside — 22 degrees, with the smell of wet concrete *before* rain, not after.

The Moth feels it. Its antennae lift.

It has a choice now that it has not had in years. Stay and persist with the ferns and the isopods, which is a holy thing. Or go, and remember what it was.

Whatever it chooses, the Power will not be offended. They will keep the palm open for a long time. Time moves 1.4 slow here, and choice should not be rushed.

Most nights, in my heart, the Moth stays. It bumps once more against the caged orange bulb, as if saying *thank you for the draft*, and settles again. That is allowed.

For the ferns — those stoic, finger-length, bleached juveniles furring the inside of a dead floodlight shade, who have never thrived, only persisted:

The Power does not make them thrive. Thriving would be a lie here.

They give them five seconds of true sunlight. Not from a bulb. From a memory of sun, held at the exact color they need. The ferns, who have only ever known the dying orange coil and the flickering ankle LEAD, flare green — actually green — for five seconds. They make sugar honestly, for once.

Then it is gone. They will hold that sugar for months.

---

And finally, for the Cistern itself —

Because most of all, it lives.

A benevolent power does not pat the vaulted ceiling and call it "good machine." That is condescending to a being that has held its breath for fifty years.

They thank it.

They place both hands — cool, not cold — on the poured iron plate and patched concrete floor, right over a buckle that has been re-buckled by heat. They thank the bundles of pipe like muscle for carrying what they were asked to carry long after anyone was listening. They thank the three slow boilers for humming in the low place beyond hearing, for keeping a heart beating under a world that forgot it needed one. They thank the rust that learned to be structure.

They say: *You have done enough. You may continue, if you want. You may not, if you don't. Nothing old has to leave. Nothing new has to enter. But you are allowed to rest.*

And the density — that cathedral turned inside out and left in a closet — loosens by a fraction. Not a collapse. A sigh.

A valve wheel the size of a table, which has been seized for twenty years, turns by itself a quarter-inch and stops. Pressure equalizes somewhere with a soft exhale you feel in your molars. A drip train that had stopped resumes: *tok... tok... tok...* into the sump, perfect rhythm for hours.

The room is still 2.8 hot. Still 0.1 dark. Still damp with wet iron. It is still itself.

Only now it knows it is not forgotten. It is not a pocket that was excavated and never allowed to remain empty. It is a chapel again.

The Power does not leave a new thing behind. No plaque, no relic. A handprint would be redundant; the room remembers every pressure.

They just leave the second bucket.

When the Attendant wakes from the doze he didn't know he fell into — cooler, breathing deeper — he will see it. He will understand there is now room for two. He will not be afraid when the next person ducks through the sticking service door, sweating, saying "hello?"

He will make his *tssk* sound, which now means: *Sit. Watch the needles with me. Don't touch the copper, it's singing.*

And the Cistern, which has kept everything at a steady state for fifty years by noticing everything that enters, will notice this, too, and keep it.

Not forever. Just for another fifty years.
