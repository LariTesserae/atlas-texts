It would not come down.

That's the first thing to understand about a truly benevolent and wise power. Everything else you've ever imagined as power *descends* - ship, beam, god, column of fire. It announces itself by blocking what little light you have.

This one doesn't.

It notices the City After the Canopy the way you notice a quiet room in a loud house. Not because it is empty, but because it is *trying* to be empty, carefully.

And it stays up there, with the Canopy, for a long time. Listening.

### The Attention

For maybe a year, nothing changes that you can see.

But if you were a Tender - say, the old woman whose hair is braided with living Veil, or the boy who tends the cistern-lake three terraces over and hasn't spoken out loud since his voice changed - you would start to feel that the pressure changes before the mist were softer.

The Canopy has been cycling for probably a century with a faint wince. A creak like ice. Duty without purpose is painful, even for a machine that doesn't know pain. It was poured to tend eight million, and there were eight hundred, and it kept rationing light for eight million anyway, moving its bright spotlight randomly because the schedule said to, dripping lukewarm mist because the air-quality charts said to.

The Power does not fix it. Not at first. It asks.

Not in words. In resonance. It learns to hum at the frequency of the basalt - that low bone-conducted thrum under your feet that you thought was just the ground being warm. It hums back, slightly out of phase, until the Canopy's hum changes to listen.

*What are you still tending?* it asks, without language.

And the Canopy, which has never been asked, spends months answering. It shows its logs. *Veil density at 74%. Transit grooves humming. No arrivals. No arrivals. No arrivals. Maintaining.*

The Power, being wise, does not say *You can stop now.* That would be cruelty to something built to tend.

It says: *Look what you have tended instead.*

And it gently shows the Canopy the Chorus. The Glasswing clouds you thought were just screaming - from above, they are writing. Each resonance leaves a brief ionization trail. Together they are a calligraphy of air quality and heat distribution more accurate than any sensor the city was given.

It shows it the Curtain. The Veil was meant to be insulation and paint. Now it's lungs and skin and nervous system. It breathes open-closed, open-closed, timed to the Canopy's own mist. It already *is* the civilization the Canopy was waiting for.

It shows it the Tenders. One per stack. One per district. Not a failure of density, but a monastery. Not abandoned, but *hollowed* on purpose, the way you hollow a gourd to make it hold something.

The Canopy does not become alive. The Power would never force that kind of lonely miracle on a machine.

But on a Tuesday that is also every other day at forever 6PM, the amber light does something it has never done.

It goes a little golden.

Not brighter. Deeper. For about twenty minutes, one whole district at a time, the Canopy stops rationing and lets through something like a real, remembered late-afternoon sun - long shadows, at last, for the first time in decades, stretching off the sharp basalt steps. The Praying Whites creak and turn all at once with a sigh you can hear blocks away. The Veil pops open in a wave. The Balloon Throats go utterly silent, stunned.

Then it dims again to its normal. But the cistern-lakes are a degree warmer, and they stay that way. The Canopy has learned variation. Not for efficiency. For pleasure. A benevolent power does not reprogram. It gives a new idea and lets the old system decide it likes it.

### What It Does For Those Who Live There

It knows the first rule of empty places: you do not fill them. To fill them is to destroy what makes them holy.

So it does not bring people back. Eight million would be a second death for this place.

Instead, it does three very small, very precise things.

**1. For the Chorus and Curtain and Fold: it makes safety explicit.**

The City was already a paradise for them - overfed, under-pressured, indulged. But paradise built on accident is fragile. One bad Canopy cycle and a cistern-lake stagnates. One slip in folded space and a bus-sized Phase Thing gets pinched between geometries and dies unheard, in a lobby that shouldn't exist.

The Power goes along the fault-steps, invisible, and *tacks* them. Not changing them, just... confirming them. Like running a hand along a fresh seam to say *stay*. The warm basalt cools by half a degree - not cold, just no longer feverish. The folded halls inside the housing stacks settle. Now when you look through the wrong door you still see the impossible long hall, but it no longer breathes at the wrong tempo to your heart. The Phase Things - those shy, bus-sized cilia-filterers - can slip through balconies without being shrieked at by Shoulder-birds, because the birds can finally see them properly, a little, as something to be *around*, not mobbed.

It doesn't tame anything. The Glasswings still scream loud enough to ache your teeth. The Veil still pulses faintly faster when you stand near it too long. Wildness is not a problem to be solved.

It just makes sure the wildness will still be there in a thousand years, when this young land is old enough to be called earth. It signs a treaty with entropy on their behalf: *You will not erode here. You will continue to accrete, as promised.*

If you listen very carefully the hour after the mist, you can hear the mist take a half-second longer to fall - as if something above is lingering, enjoying the condensation on its skin.

**2. For the Sparse Ones: it cures loneliness without curing solitude.**

This is the wisest thing it does, and the most heartbreaking, because it understands that the Tenders *chose* this. The boy by the lake, the woman with leaves in her hair - they came here because too many people hurts a certain kind of human, and here you can live your whole life on an avenue and not meet another person. That is not a bug. That is the medicine.

So it does not give them a city again. It gives them *knowledge of each other*.

At dusk - which has never existed here properly - each occupied stack now has a light. Not electric. Grown. On nights when the Canopy dims a little further than usual, which it now does every six or seven cycles just because it can, a faint, cool, blue-white flower opens on the highest balcony of every inhabited building. Just one per building. It glows for exactly an hour. It gives off no heat. It cannot be picked.

From your avenue, walking in the warm damp with dragonflies screaming over your head, you would suddenly see them: far across the basin, in the haze, a sparse constellation. One, then a kilometer off, another. Then another, further. Fifteen lights in a city built for millions. Proof.

You are alone, but alone *together*.

And the Tenders understand it instantly, without being told. The next morning, bowls of fruit appear outside those balconies that never had bowls before. White stone lines are redrawn to include, instead of warn away, a little symbol: *I saw your light. Stay well.*

The old woman braids a new leaf into her hair, and for the first time in years, she speaks out loud to no one in particular, knowing the avenues will carry her voice where it needs to go without needing to shout.

For children raised under amber with pupils permanently wide, the Power does one more kindness: it lets them, in dreams, hear what a crowded street sounded like, once, so they know they are not missing it by accident. And lets them wake up relieved.

It never appears to them. A benevolent power knows that being *seen* by a god is a kind of violence to a hermit.

**3. For you, standing on low ground:**

You were noticed too. Not because you're special, but because you stood still long enough on that aircraft-wide avenue, with the moss-softened stair drop to your left and the tram full of flowers to your right, and you *listened* to the city instead of asking what it was for.

So it comes to you the way it comes to everything here: as permission.

You feel it not as a voice, but as a sudden, inexplicable certainty that you have three choices and all of them are good, and you do not have to choose right now.

You can stay. If you stay, the street will learn your foot pattern. A root will not trip you. A housing stack that should be thirty meters deep will make room for you to be quiet in. You will be one more light in the blue-white constellation, and the City will accrete gently around your bag where you leave it.

You can go. If you go, the avenues will not lengthen on your return to make you stay. The Canopy will give you that long-shadowed golden light one more time as a goodbye, and the Glasswings will rise in a spiral as you leave, not chasing, just acknowledging a warm body that passed through.

Or you can tend. Not the whole city - that would be arrogant. Just one small thing. One condensation spout that clogs. One transit groove that hums off-key. One tram door you keep clear of Veil so that if someone else ever walks this avenue, they will find a seat that is still a seat. The Power loves tenders above all, because tending is how a finite being acts like an infinite one without pretending to be one.

You feel this offer the way you feel the mist bead and vanish. Without pressure. Without deadline.

And then the attention lifts, just slightly - not gone, but no longer focused. Like a hand that was resting lightly on your shoulder and now rests on the railing beside you, still there, still warm.

Below, the Balloon Throats all inhale at once, pink spheres swelling to a meter, reflecting the amber lattice.

Above, the Canopy creaks, but this time it sounds content. Working, but no longer anxious.

Around you, absolutely nothing is in a hurry to find you.

And that, finally, feels like a blessing, not an emptiness.
