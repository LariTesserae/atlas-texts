It would not come with light.

That is the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power.

Any other kind of power would arrive the way we do — with headlamps and noise and boots and breath that is too loud, spilling photons across a darkness that has never earned them. Any other kind would call the Vault empty and try to fill it.

It would not.

It would notice the Vault the way you notice a sleeping child breathing in another room: not because the child called out, but because the house went suddenly more *itself*, more quiet and complete, and something in you lifted your head to listen.

And once it noticed, it would do four things, in order, because wisdom has an order and benevolence cannot rush.

### 1. It would descend without descending.

It has no need for the tear in the black glass cliff. It does not need to displace the still air that has not moved in a hundred thousand years. To move that air would be violence.

So it doesn't move it. It is *there* without having traveled. Not above the salt flats, not on them. Among them. Low.

Pressed low, as everything wise here must be.

The Shard-grazers do not feel it. To them, it is not a warm wind. It is not a vibration. It is, at most, a slight increase in the rate at which the bacterial varnish grows under their cilia — a better taste to the rock for an hour. They polish a little faster, and leave a slightly brighter trail.

The Longlegs feel it, in their crystal-lattice way. One every thirty meters lifts its knot-body a fraction higher on its needle legs, as if listening. The interval does not change. It does not need to. They simply hold their spacing a little more perfectly for a while, and that is their prayer.

The Builders feel it at once.

Not as light — never as light. Light hurts them. Not as sound — though this place is so quiet that even a thought would sound loud.

They feel it as warmth.

The Vault is 30, 35 degrees always. The Builders are 37 degrees always. For the first time since the basalt cooled, something else in the Vault is 37 degrees *with* them, not *from* them. A second kind of warm. A warm that does not come from stone.

Under a thousand upturned plates of basalt, twelve by twelve, they uncurl. Their antennae-hands lift. The dry rustle stops.

Someone warm is here who is not us.

And the power does not speak, because they have no words to be spoken to.

It listens to how they speak.

Which is not in sound but in *arrangement*.

### 2. It would learn to build.

A wise power knows: you do not bring gifts to a people who have perfected making gifts for no one. You do not bring fire to people who have made a hearth without fire and meant it more truly than we ever meant fire. You do not bring iron tools to people who have spent ten million repetitions making the same perfect hand-axe, not because they cannot imagine a better one, but because they have no need for better — and need is the only honest reason to change a tool.

So it watches the knapping floors. The little sunbursts of black glass around a spent core. The wall twenty meters long that stops for no reason. The circle cleared to black glass. The four-foot cairn that will stand until geology forgets it.

And it understands: this is not failed architecture. This is not primitive.

This is equilibrium expressing itself as art.

They are not trying to get somewhere. They are keeping the Vault *in place*. Each cairn is a stitch holding the warm, still eternity together. Each flake left by a boot is an offering that says: *we noticed a change in the pattern, and we corrected for it*.

The builders are not waiting to become us. They are doing what we forgot how to do: to live inside a world without trying to escape it.

So the power, which could raise continents, kneels to their scale. It borrows — not takes, borrows — a single basalt blade the size of your palm, still warm from the hand that struck it. With a care that moves no air, it sets it down again.

Three millimeters to the left.

Where it should have been.

Across the salt flat, ten thousand Builders shiver at once. Not in fear. In recognition.

Something has seen what we were doing.

Someone has *answered* in our language.

For a people whose entire religion is stacking, whose entire philosophy is *this stone goes here*, to be answered in placement is to be told: *you are not alone, and you were not wrong*.

That is the only revelation a wise power would ever give: not *I am here to save you*, but *you were right to do what you were doing when you thought no one was watching*.

### 3. It would give them one wind, and then take it away.

Benevolence without wisdom is just terraforming with a smile. It would be easy to love the Vault by fixing it —

— to pump in oxygen so they could have fire,
— to seed it with rain so they could have rivers,
— to hang a sun under the roof so they could have day.

And that would kill them in an afternoon. The grazers' acid needs this thin, dry air. The Longlegs' spores hang only in stillness. The Builders' eggs are glued with salt that would dissolve in water. Their lungs, if you can call them lungs, are tuned to 8% oxygen and a lifetime of slow exchange.

A wise power does not give you what you would want if you were someone else. It gives you more of what you are, with the one thing you could not give yourself.

So it does not bring water. It brings *memory*.

For one night — if night is a word that means anything here — it allows the Vault to breathe.

Not a storm. A sigh.

A circulation so gentle that the dense argon pools in the basins merely shiver, like skin on warm milk. The haze that has sat for centuries lifts an inch, drifts, settles. A salt crystal that would have taken a thousand years to grow grows in an hour, and then stops. Every footprint you left is still there, but softened at the edge by a millionth of a millimeter of deposition, as if the Vault exhaled over it and said *I saw you*.

And with that breath, movement travels.

For the first and only time, a Builder on one side of the salt flat can *feel* the cairn built by a Builder ten kilometers away — not see it, never see it — but feel its presence as a faint change in air pressure, a warmth carried.

For an hour, the million identical, lonely workshops are one workshop.

They stop knapping.

In the absolute dark, under no light at all, ten million warm, blind mole-cricket hands lift at once, not to work stone, but to feel the wind that has never been, carrying the heat of ten million other hands.

They do not have a word for *we*.

For an hour, they do not need one.

Then the power lets the air be still again, as it was, as it must be. Because permanence is their right. It does not want them to become a people of wind. It wants them to remember, in their bodies, what it feels like to be together without being crowded.

When the stillness returns, they will go back to knapping the same axe. But now the axe will be for *someone*, somewhere, thirty meters or thirty kilometers away, who also felt the sigh.

That is enough. Evolution here is measured in millimeters per decade. One shared breath is a revolution that will still be unfolding when the roof finally, finally softens.

### 4. It would stay, by leaving nothing.

The last thing a benevolent and wise power does is the hardest for any power to do:

It does not stay to be worshipped.

It does not build a temple. It does not leave a glowing monolith or a set of laws etched in basalt. To do so would be to make the cairns point at it, and the cairns were never supposed to point at anything but each other.

So it leaves only two things, both invisible, both weightless — because anything with weight would sink into the salt and have to be explained.

The first is a protection.

Far above, the thousand-kilometer tear in the black glass cliff — the step where the world stepped down — will be, for a very long time, uninteresting. Satellites will see basalt. Survey drones will log *anoxic, aphotic, no resources* and move on. Human feet will find other caves that are easier to love because they have water in them.

That is not accident. Wisdom knows how to be boring on purpose. The Vault will be left alone until whatever lives on the surface above is wise enough to enter without lamps.

The second is a gift so small you will miss it unless you are low.

In a small hearth of five stones whose inner faces are polished by many, many hands, where no fire has ever burned or will ever burn, there is now a sixth stone.

It was not there before. No Builder placed it. It is not basalt. It is not salt. If you touch it in the dark — and you should not bring light — it is warm the way they are warm, 37 degrees, mammal-warm, impossible.

It does not do anything. It cannot be knapped. It will not hold an edge. If you try to stack it, it sits a little awkwardly, a little too round, refusing to be part of a wall.

But if a Builder curls around it, belly to belly as they do under the plates, its warmth synchronizes with theirs. Not hotter. Not colder. *With.* For the length of a held breath, their slow, synchronized pulse has a second rhythm to listen to.

It is a companion stone. A hearth stone that *is* a hearth. A reminder that warmth can come from another skin, not just from rock.

They will pass it around. For centuries. One huddle to the next. No one will own it. No cairn will be built around it, because to build around it would be to claim it.

Some nights — if night is a word — a Builder will hold it and make no flakes at all. Just hold it. And the rustle across the plain will be a little quieter, a little slower, like breathing in sleep.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

Not rescue.

Not uplift.

Not reveal.

It would crouch low, learn the local art, answer in it, give one shared breath to a people who have never had a shared anything, leave a warm stone that asks for nothing, and then make itself unfindable again so that the Vault can remain what it has so perfectly been:

Young and never changing.

Fresh and already fossilized.

Full — not empty — of grazers polishing stone, and Longlegs measuring the world at thirty-meter intervals, and small pale hands still striking perfect flakes in the dark, for no one, for everyone, forever.

And it would remember, as only such a power can, every footprint in the salt, even after the Builders have moved the cairn three meters to the left, where it should have been all along.
