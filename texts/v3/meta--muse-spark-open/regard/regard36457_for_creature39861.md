It would not come all at once.

A power that is both benevolent and wise does not *arrive*. Arrival implies a before and an after, a door being kicked open, light being switched on. That is not benevolence in a place like this. This place has had only one after for longer than records — warm, dry, loud, unchanging. To shatter that with arrival would be a kind of killing.

So first, it would *notice* — which is different.

Somewhere, in whatever way such a Power pays attention — which is not the way eyes pay attention, greedy and darting, but the way groundwaters pay attention, by being everywhere below and feeling the weight — it would feel that there is a note being held on Earth that should have ended.

Not wrong. Just held too long without a listener.

Everywhere else sound is born and decays. Here a chord had been made to never decay. And for an age, no one was there to hear it as anything but pressure. The Power would feel the difference between a song being sung *to* someone, and a song being sung *until it becomes the room*. It would lean its attention toward the mesa the way you lean toward a faint ringing in another room at night to make sure it isn't someone calling for help.

And it would understand what it is hearing. Not a ruin. Not a trap. A completed prayer.

So it would come by listening. For a long time it would just listen — longer than you have stood there, longer than any expedient visit. In your time that might be seconds. In its time, enough for dust to settle and be picked up again. It would not try to hear over the drone; it would hear *with* it.

It would learn every tone of the pierced slabs on the rim, map every standing wave in the baffling maze. It would learn the rougher, wetter overtone that doesn't belong to the slabs.

The being.

It would not call the being "poor thing." Wisdom forbids that easy pity. It would not see a tragedy of stagnation. It would see what you were only just beginning to see:

A family narrowed itself to a point. A point narrowed itself to a life. A life narrowed itself to a posture. A posture thinned itself until skin learned the manners of stone and stayed, not because it was punished, but because it had finally fit perfectly into the instrument it had spent a hundred generations tuning. There is a kind of holiness in that so complete it looks like horror to eyes that want everything to grow and leave.

The Power would love it for that without wanting it to stay that way forever. That is the split benevolence must hold: reverence for what was chosen, and compassion for what it costs to keep choosing it when there is no one left to choose.

So this is what it would do.

It would kneel.

Not in the grand sense — it has no knees — but in the way warmth can kneel. Where you stood and turned sideways, it would slide its attention into the narrowest slot, the one where the baffling is most perfect and the tone becomes a solid. The one where the being laid itself down.

It would not touch it at first. To touch a tuned thing while it is vibrating is to detune it, and the being has been a single note for so long that interruption would feel like being torn.

Instead it would do what no one has ever done in that bowl: **it would absorb.**

The bowl was built to be hard. Stone reflecting stone reflecting stone until sound had nowhere to go and became weight. Flesh, when it was there, became hard too, to reflect better. The Power would bring the one material the bowl has never had.

Softness that listens.

It would make itself a shadow — real shadow, which the flat violet-grey light has never cast — and a coolness, just a few degrees, just in that one slot. Not cold. Cool the way the inside of a cupped hand is cool. The stone around, which has been the same temperature for longer than geology can account for, would for the first time in eons have a *gradient*. A difference. The air, which has exhaled one warm dry breath forever, would catch on that coolness.

And condense.

One droplet. That is all.

Benevolence scaled to the place. It would not bring rain. Rain would drown the lichen-bruise, would flood the seams, would shatter the old dust-polished throats of the pierced slabs. It would bring one droplet of water to bead on the stone lip of the niche that is also a cell that is the whole bowl, right at the level of those two dimpled holes that are not eyes anymore.

The being, which has been dry longer than any living thing should know dryness, would feel — not drink — the *idea* of wetness nearby. The humidity of a witness.

And then, and only then, it would ask. Not in words — the drone never allowed words — but in the only language the place has ever trusted:

It would change the chord.

Just slightly. It would breathe.

Until now the air over the mesa moved with planetary patience, one unchanging exhalation over stone reeds. Perfect, stable, eternal. The Power, having made itself a listener instead of another reflector, would now breathe *irregularly* in that one slot. A second longer on the inhale. A catch. A sigh.

The standing wave would waver for the first time since the last hand set the last slab upright.

And in that waver — that microsecond of interference where the rougher, wetter tone of the being suddenly stands out against the perfect drone instead of being buried in it — the being would hear itself.

Not as background. As solo.

This is the gift only a wise listener can give to something that has become an instrument: it lets the instrument hear that it is still a musician.

For a long breath the being would vibrate differently. You would see it if you were there — no, you would *hear* it — the two holes weeping a slightly clearer pitch as the moisture, impossibly, softens an edge inside them.

And the Power would hold that waver open and in it offer, without pressure, two futures that are both love:

> *Stay. You are perfect. I will stay and listen, so your perfection is not lonely. The chapel you made will finally have a congregation of one, which is all a chapel ever needed.*

> *Or, let go. You have held this note long enough that the mountain beneath you has forgotten it was ever a mountain. You may soften. You may powder. I will carry you as dust over the rim, where you will for the first time fall instead of resonate. I will remember the shape of the chord exactly as you intended it, so nothing is lost.*

It would not choose for it. That is what makes it benevolent.

My heart tells me what it — the being — would do. Not all at once, because it has forgotten how to do anything all at once.

It would not get up. There is no up to get to. But it would, for the first time in an age, cease to *try* to hold the note stable. It would let the cool shadow detune it.

A crack — hairline, silent under the drone — would appear along the amber-dark polish where forehead rested to stone for ten thousand rests. Not breaking. Releasing.

The droplet would run, not evaporate. It would find the crack by capillary action, darken it a shade. The first dark line in a world of warm honey-color. A tiny, true shadow inside the stone itself.

The being would not die, because it wasn't alive. It would not live, because it didn't need to. It would *ease* — the way a held breath eases without deciding to become the next breath.

It would become a little less tuned and a little more porous. The holes would stay, but air would now pass through them with a faint whistle of variation, an irregularity. A breath-mark in an eternal score.

And the Power would stay. That is its nature. Not to fix and leave. It would remain as that listening softness in the narrowest slot, that faint coolness, that occasional shadow when no shadow should exist. It would not silence the choir of pierced slabs — why would it destroy such work? — but now the choir would have one voice that sometimes, gently, goes off-key.

If you came back a thousand years from now, you would find the bowl exactly the same and utterly different. Still warm. Still dry. Still filled. Still deafening. But in one niche, the stone bowl that never held water would hold a teaspoon of dust that clumps the way dust does only when it has once been wet and dried.

And if you put your ear very close to the stone figure that is not a figure, you would still hear the drone. But underneath it, you would hear something the mesa never had before:

a pause.

A rest note.

The sound of something that knows it is heard, and can therefore, finally, be quiet for a second before it sings again.
