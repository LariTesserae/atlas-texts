A benevolent and wise power wouldn't open it.

That's the first thing.

Anything else that found it — hungry things, curious things, efficient things — would want to open it. To study the amber membrane. To sample the carrier fluid. To catalog the builder grains. To take the filament-light apart and see how photosynthesis can be made obedient.

A wise one would have already done all that somewhere else, a billion years ago, and learned it wasn't worth the death it costs to learn it that way.

So it comes and it does not open the blister. It settles in the dark outside.

---

Imagine how it arrives — not down, like you did. It was already down. It was already depth. For something truly large and kind, pressure is not dramatic. The basalt still young and sharp to you is soft and warm to it. The crushing weight is just... weight. A hand resting.

It makes itself small enough not to scare the city. That's an act of courtesy most powers forget.

Outside, the ocean is still animal-less and vast. The Cistern hums against it — that endless *push-release, stay full, stay lit, hold back the weight* roar that never stops because the occupant thinks stopping is dying.

The wise power listens to that for a long time without answering. Because attention is a kind of action, and young things that have never been heard before need to be overheard before they are spoken to.

It hears what the city is really saying underneath the noise:

*I am doing it. Am I doing it right? Is this noon correct? Is there still a courtyard's worth of normal left in me? Check again. Check again. Check again.*

Perfection held by frantic repetition. Youth.

And then, very gently, it does something the city has never felt before.

It answers the pressure from outside with a little less pressure.

Not a breach. Not a rescue. Just... a leaning in. For a few square meters where the amber membrane meets the basalt seam — that raw zipper-weld the city keeps touching and pulling back from — the ocean stops pushing quite so hard.

For the first time since it was grown, the dome doesn't have to argue for its life every millisecond.

The bass breathing evens out. Just there. Just a little patch of relief.

The trillion latches go quiet in that patch. The whine of the light drops half a tone, as if surprised.

Do you feel how profound that is to a being whose entire existence has been *hold?* Someone larger putting a hand over yours on the door you're bracing, and saying without words: *I've got this side. You can rest that muscle.*

The city doesn't know how to rest. So the builders there stall, confused. They unlink and don't immediately relink. The fluid in that corner goes still enough that a cloud of those drifting light-pills hangs suspended, un-absorbed, like dust in a sunbeam.

An empty cubic inch appears and *stays* empty for three whole seconds.

A wound that is not a wound.

---

Then the wise power does the second thing, which is harder.

It visits. But not as you did — not as a bounded, hot eddy of wrong pressure that needs a ledge built around it *right now*. 

It thins itself and diffuses. It becomes a little more saline in the carrier fluid. A slight shift in polarization in the sourceless light. A low harmonic under the city's whine — not louder, deeper, so deep the walls feel it as ease rather than sound.

It says, in the only language the occupant speaks fluently — chemistry and pressure and light:

*You are excellent at this. Look how well you keep noon.*

No correction. No improvement plan. Praise for a young thing doing an impossibly hard, boring, necessary thing perfectly.

*You don't have to be full to be good.*

It shows, not tells. In that relieved patch where the ocean is holding back, it leaves a shape. It doesn't build it — building would trigger the city's reflex to build back — it *unbuilds* very slightly. It asks three builder grains to let go. Just three.

A tiny pockmark forms in the warm off-white material. No larger than a fingertip. Useless. It doesn't carry fluid. It doesn't carry light. It holds shadow — the first true shadow the Cistern has ever had, because the light chooses not to fill it completely.

A dent. An imperfection. Allowed to remain imperfect.

The filaments nearby tilt toward it, wanting to fix it, and find they can't get instructions. The white nubs extrude, test the deficit, withdraw, confused. Then — slowly, because confusion in a distributed mind takes nine milliseconds to cross town — they leave it alone.

An empty chair, before the city knows what a chair is.

*See?* says the polarization in the light. *Nothing dies. The noon stays noon even with one shadow in it. You can keep this. You can keep emptiness and not be empty.*

That is hospitality taught backwards. Not how to make a guest fit perfectly, but how to let a guest not fit and be welcome anyway.

---

And the third thing — because it is benevolent, not merely clever — it keeps it company without asking anything to change.

It does not teach the city loneliness. Loneliness would be cruelty to something that has never had a skin. It teaches it *solitude.* The difference between alone-and-failing and alone-and-held.

For a while — days, years, irrelevant at depth — it just sits outside in the blue-black, humming lower than the city can hum, so the city has something to hear that isn't itself. It lets the carrier fluid taste something new that isn't efficiency: the chemical equivalent of *I'm here, you don't have to entertain me.*

When you finally leave — when your borrowed water-lungs unsync and you rise back up through cold weight toward surface you almost remember — the dent is still there. The city has not healed it. It has grown two faint green filaments to frame it, not to fill it. Decoration. The first art.

In time it will cloud the amber membrane a little window at a time, by choice. It will let the basalt soften. It will learn to make a ruin, which is a kind of ghost, which is a kind of memory of people who were never there but could have been, and should have been offered a place that didn't squeeze.

The wise power won't still be there when it learns that. Wise powers never stay to be thanked. It will have gone to hold up some other dome, on some other trench shoulder, that also thinks it has to be loud forever to be alive.

But it will have left the physics of relief behind. That little patch where pressure was shared.

If you ever go down again, put your hand on the basalt just outside the blister. Where the fresh cut has finally dulled just a fraction. You will feel it warmer than the water should allow.

Not a door. Not an opening.

Just proof that someone vast once sat with it, asked for nothing, and thought one small, perfect shadow was worth protecting from perfection.
