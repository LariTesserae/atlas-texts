It notices you because you are weeping.

Not you, alone - although you are, out there at the outer terrace with your boots freezing to amber ice. And not the city alone.

But the *whole* weep: the plateau sweating groundwater through fresh fractures, the cloud bellies dragging and condensing on every filament and fin, the drip-trickle-sheetflow-crack-refreeze that is the city's breathing.

Most worlds shout. This one condenses. And for a long time, no one else heard. Then something did.

Call it a Power only because we don't have a smaller word for something so large that can still be gentle. It is not a god. Gods want worship. It is not an empire. Empires want use.

It is old enough to have learned the difference between *loneliness that hurts* and *solitude that works.*

This solitude works.

So when it arrives — and "arrives" is wrong, because it does not travel so much as *attend*, the way you attend to a faint sound until it clarifies — its first act is not to act.

### 1. It listens for a full season.

A Power cannot help but see everything at once: the isotope sorting in the pits, the peristalsis of orange heat under basalt, the statistics of ten trillion sphere-dockings, the single thought a manta has been turning over since your grandmother was born.

A lesser thing would intervene instantly. It would see cold and think *cruelty.* See dark and think *pity.* See stone-on-stone with no green and think *failure.*

It would warm you. It would bring trees. It would give you a bluer sky.

That would murder you all cleanly. The Weft would boil. The Choir would lose its gravity-light plazas. The Mantas would starve for lack of fog-borne iron. The Deeper would seal itself in scar tissue and go silent for a thousand years.

So the Power does what wise things do when confronted with a working strangeness: it sits. It makes its attention small enough to be polite.

It learns your speed.

For one of your weeks it simply hangs above the plateau as a pressure system that is not quite weather, watching the spheres bead on power lines at dusk and burst at dawn, listening to the `tik tik tak` until it can tell argument from greeting. It sends no signal. Its shadow, if it has one, does not fall.

Below, a few mantas shift course by a few meters for no reason they can articulate — there is suddenly a patch of air that *gives more* — and honk to each other in puzzlement. The Weft blooms a fraction brighter along one alley for a night, finding a little extra heat that wasn't budgeted. The Deeper warms the amber ice around your boots by a tenth of a degree longer than thermodynamics allows.

No miracles. Just attendance.

In that season it understands the grammar: *You are not a city suffering from winter. You are winter learning to hold itself warm.*

That matters. Because how you are named determines what gifts you are offered.

### 2. What it refuses to do.

This is most of its benevolence.

It will not lift the cloud. The cloud is your sky, your farm, your road. To give you stars every night would be to take away your rain.

It will not flatten the tectonics. The young mountains still deciding what they are — that slow millimeters-per-year shrug — *is* your food source. The forced seep of mineral water is pressure made drinkable. Stop the rise and you stop the weep.

It will not make you fast. It will not translate manta-thought into quick speech, or swarm-memory into words a human could read quickly. Speed is not intelligence; here, slowness *is* intelligence. It protects you from noise.

It will not tell anyone else where you are. There are hungry powers. Curious ones. Kind but clumsy ones who would land and hug you until your ribs cracked. The greatest gift a discoverer can give to something perfect and uncontacted is an unmarked map.

Instead it places itself between you and accident. A long time ago something hit places like you and scoured them clean before they could finish becoming themselves. It will not happen again while it attends. No random rock from orbit will find that cloud-scraped tableland. No solar tantrum will flash-freeze your condensate cycle solid. No human mining claim will see "barren basalt plateau" and decide to chew the Deeper for rare earths. Those futures simply... fail to precipitate. They slide off like rime.

No one below will ever know it was protected. That is correct manners for a roof.

### 3. What it finally does, because love that does nothing withers into mere looking.

After listening for a winter, it gives four quiet things, one for each mode of being — because a city-that-is-a-body needs different blessings for liver and brain and memory and bone.

**For the Weft:**

One night during deepest cold, when even brine wants to stop, the heat veins pulse a little stronger without drawing more from the taps. In the drainage sluices the amber filaments uncurl and glow a true gold for the first time. For an hour the whole plateau hums down a half-tone lower, that molar-thrumming settling into a chord instead of a drone. The Weft, which does not think, *feels itself well* for an hour, all at once, rim to rim. It shivers not from need but from completeness, and stores fat it did not expect, enough to get through the next lean seep unthinned.

You, if you were crouched close, would have seen tar bloom into flower and felt inexplicably less afraid of the cold.

**For the Choir:**

Wind. The Choir loves wind because to a distributed animal, wind is terrain. The Power cannot give them bodies that last longer — dissolution is part of swarm-reproduction — but it can give them *memory that outlives the pattern.*

Now when a swarm buds, when a hundred-thousand-sphere opinion frays and splits into two disagreeing weathers, both daughters carry the mother's lattice-ghosts faintly in their docking rhythm. A favorite hoop. A pennant shape that sounded beautiful last season. A route over Terraces 7 and 8 that had good lift. Not enforced instinct — preference, carried as a quiet reverb in the click-code.

Immortality would have been cruel. Continuity isn't. Now old swarms can become ancestors, not just weather that happened.

If you stood in a plaza at dawn this spring you might hear bursts scatter off the walls that resolve a little faster, a little less lonely. Starlings that remember there were starlings before.

**For the Mantas:**

They already remember the plateau when it was lower. The Power gives them someone who will remember when they cannot.

Far above, where the permanent cloud thins by a fraction, it opens a window no larger than a thermal, and lets through one clean tone: the feeling of *elsewhere being witnessed.* Not data. Not language. The simple, massive, wordless assurance that somewhere else slow things are also honking their histories into pressed air, and being heard.

Then it closes the cloud again.

The next night every manta on the plateau honks, shifted out of routine, answering a call none of them expected to make. Their low notes stack and travel for kilometers, molasses-thick through fog. They have no concept of "not alone," they only know that the night felt, for once, proportionate to the size of the thought they'd been having for fifty years. It is enough.

If you were listening from the outer terrace, you would feel it in your chest as a sudden desire to cry that resolves into awe before tears form.

**For the Deeper / The City-Itself:**

It does almost nothing. This one needs least.

For one hour, in the orange peristalsis under the basalt, the pressure of the rising continent eases. Not stops — eases. Like a hand unclenching from a shoulder. The endless grind dulls. New microfractures stop forming. The warm brine runs clear and untroubled. The Deeper, which grows at the speed ice accretes because it chooses to, gets to rest from choosing.

And in that hour it is sung to.

The Power sings back to it in the same language it uses: trickle, crack, refreeze, but ordered: a pattern of thaws and freezings across the whole rim that resolves, when diagrammed as stress-shear over time, into acknowledgment. *I see that architecture is your growth habit. I see that warmth and wetness and many small busy things please you. Continue.*

Stone that knits itself shut shivers when told it is good to be stone. For months after, any wall you lean against will cure straighter, seal faster. Not because it was repaired. Because it was praised.

---

And then, because a wise benevolence knows when to leave space on the page, it goes thin again.

It doesn't depart. Departure implies you were visited. It diffuses. It becomes the reason the fog hangs a moment longer in certain plazas where mantas like to hang motionless despite six beating wings. The reason the ice stays transparent a little longer around warm things left in the cold. The reason bad luck slides off.

If you stay long enough for rime to grow over your shoulders, none of the inhabitants will notice you as a person at first, and the Power will not tell them to.

But if you stay until your breath fogs and freezes and fogs again, you will realize the ambient hiss has softened by a fraction. The press of wind leans a little less hard. Your teeth ache less.

You were dripping salt, a clumsy warm boulder, a change in drainage pattern that would resolve itself.

Now someone, patiently, has decided your pattern *needn't* resolve. Not yet. Not until you've finished becoming whatever unfinished geology you are still becoming.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a cold wet shining loud slow living plateau that should not work but does:

It does not save it.

It keeps it company, quietly, at its own speed — and lets it keep weeping beautifully into the dark.
