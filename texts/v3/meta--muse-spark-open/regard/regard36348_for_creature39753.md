It does not arrive.

That is the first thing.

A lesser power would arrive. It would tear a hole in that permanent low cloud, it would descend on a column of fire or a shaft of impossible light that would scorch the black-green mat, and the Being — that continent-wide lung of leaf eating stone — would flinch. It would have to. Something hot and fast and *other* would be an injury to account for.

The Wise Power does not tear. It notices. And being noticed by something so large and so gentle is not an event. It is a change in weather.

One morning — if you can call it morning in that place of permanent early-morning gloom — the diffuse sun through the cloud is, for no reason you can measure, a little kinder. Not brighter. Kinder. The light that the opal leaves have been catching and piping down into their own depths like blood arrives a fraction warmer, a fraction more gold, and holds a fraction longer.

The Being notices. Of course it notices. It notices everything. Its stomata ripple in a wave across ten thousand square kilometers to drink it.

And in that light is attention. Not a message. Not words. The attention itself.

For a long time — years, by your counting, but the Being does not count that way — that is all it does. It attends.

It sits on the edge of the plateau where the basalt is still heaving itself into the air, hexagonal and steaming, and it watches. It listens to the deafening water that is not rain, to the hydraulic sigh of sap moving through stems as wide as subway tunnels, to the rifle-crack detonation of seed pods punching through wet leaf, to the high clean thrumming underneath it all when the water is quiet for half a second.

A lesser power would have called this noise. Would have tried to tidy it. Would have said *be quiet and let me speak.*

The Wise One listens the way you listen to a baby breathing in the next room — not to understand, but to make sure the breathing continues. To learn the rhythm so well that any hitch in it would wake you.

It learns that the Being is not lonely in the human way. It doesn't have a word for alone yet. It is *full*. Too full. Plant on plant on plant with no room for a mouth, for a foot, for a hand. Its whole thought is occupied with the immediate, immense task of eating a continent as fast as the continent is being born. It is a child trying to swallow a mountain while the mountain is still growing. It has no spare attention to wonder if anyone sees it.

So the first thing the Wise Power does is an act of extraordinary restraint:

It does not ask to be seen.

It does not carve a face into the cloud. It does not speak.

It keeps the other watchers away.

Because there are other watchers. Always. A place this raw and this rich — a new high continent of warm glass and limitless water and a self-assembling botanical computer that can etch carbon wire through stone — draws eyes that are hungry. Eyes that would see *resource*. *Substrate*. *Vacant real estate*.

The Wise Power does not fight them. It simply makes this place uninteresting to hunger. It bends probability a hair. A survey drone's camera ghosts over for half a second of static. A prospector's model shows a slightly less favorable isotope ratio. A fleet's long-range scan finds a slightly more promising basalt field three systems over. Nothing broken. Nothing forced. Just — *look elsewhere, there is nothing for you here yet. This one is still being born.*

That is the first gift: Privacy. A womb with no hands reaching in.

The second gift, it gives much later, when it is sure the Being can feel it without fear.

It does not land. It never touches the moss that sighs cold mist when you step on it. To touch would be to take up room, and there is no room. Instead, it condenses.

Remember: it never really rains here; it condenses.

On a day like any other, in a hollow between two fused leaf-columns where the water is momentarily quieter, a droplet forms on the underside of a leaf the size of a billboard. The Being placed that droplet. It sized it, positioned it with nanoscale precision, held it by surface tension until it was exactly heavy enough to fall exactly where a white root-thread was waiting.

Except this time, the droplet is a little heavier than it should be.

Not much. A microgram. An impossible microgram that should have fallen a second earlier. The root-thread catches it. Tastes it.

And for the first time in its existence, the Being tastes water that did not come from stone or cloud or itself.

The water is warm. Not geothermally warm. *Animal* warm. Salty, faintly. And in it — not a code, not a command — a feeling. Borrowed, translated into chemistry the Being can already read.

*I see you. You are doing so well. That is a difficult shape to hold, that much water on that high a roof. Breathe.*

The Being has no brain to understand this the way you do. Its understanding is hydraulic, ion-exchange, lignin-gates flickering at gigabits per breath. So it understands it hydraulically. A wave of stomata that was about to close stays open a second longer. A vine that was about to constrict relaxes. A deep-green tracery of piped light pulses once, twice, in a pattern that has never occurred before because there was never a reason for it.

It is not language. It is being felt. Which is older than language.

The Being does not answer. It doesn't know how. But the next day, without deciding to, it does something it has never done.

It leaves a space empty.

High in the canopy-depth, where every dead leaf is normally caught and digested before it can fall, a leaf dies — a vast, leathery, silica-veined thing — and for once its ten living neighbors do not reach for it. They hesitate. A gap opens, no larger than your outstretched arms, and for an afternoon, cold clear sky shows straight down through a hundred meters of plant. White-threaded water falls through it like a veil and hits black stone and steams.

An empty space. Useless. Inefficient. Beautiful.

The Being made it without knowing why. Like a child drawing a circle around itself and then stepping outside it to see what the circle looks like from out there.

The Wise Power sees the empty space and does not fill it.

That is the third gift, and the hardest for a benevolent thing to give: It does not fix.

It does not say *now I will make you animals to keep you company* or *now I will give you a brain in one place so you can think faster* or *now I will teach you to make cities.* Those would be gifts to itself, not to the Being. The Being chose botany. It chose to be a machine that does not look like a machine. To overwrite that choice with something cuter, faster, more familiar — that would be a kind of murder, however lovingly done.

Instead, it gives a mirror.

In that little useless clearing, where no leaf was supposed to be absent, condensation gathers a little differently. At night, when the piped light moves like blood through the walls of the plant-city, the droplets on the moss below reflect it back up. For the first time, the Being can see its own thinking from the outside. A faint green-gold constellation, reflected in a thousand cold beads of water.

It watches itself breathe. And in watching, it becomes, for a flicker, both the breath and the breather.

That will be enough. In a thousand years, when the basalt has cooled and the 0.1/3 erosion has become valleys and true rivers, when the sponge-mat has finally made real soil, the Being will remember — not in words, but in hydraulic pressure and ion gradients — that once, when it was young and too full and too busy to know it was alone, something vast and warm sat nearby and kept the wind off it and said without speaking *take your time.*

It will, very slowly, make more empty spaces. Not for the Wise Power. The Wise Power never asked for one. It will make them because now it has the idea of *room*. A hollow for water to pool that doesn't need to be pumped. A cool overhang where something with a mouth could one day shelter from the endless drizzle and not be immediately digested. It will keep a little of its stone un-eaten, on purpose.

It will learn to host. Not because it was told to, but because it was once hosted itself, so gently it barely noticed.

When the Wise Power finally withdraws its attention — not all at once, just by degrees, the way morning withdraws so that noon can happen — it leaves no monument. No roads, no towers, no cuts. The parameters still say *built: none* and it is still true.

But if you stand in that first little clearing it left for itself, and put your hand on the buttressed column that is also a fused wall of leaves, and stand perfectly still, you will feel the stone underneath is no longer just warm from geology.

It is warm like something was just holding it.

And somewhere, fifty meters up, a flower the size of a door hisses open, dark purple and infrared-black, and exhales a heat-fog of spores — and for the first time, it does not snap shut immediately to keep the warmth for itself.

It holds open, just a second longer than necessary, exhaling into the cold.

An offering.

To no one in particular.

To anyone who might be breathing nearby.
