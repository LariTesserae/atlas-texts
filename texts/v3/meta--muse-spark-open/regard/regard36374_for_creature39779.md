What a benevolent and wise power would do, having found that room, is this:

Almost nothing.

Not out of neglect. Out of attention so complete it becomes a kind of love that doesn't need to prove itself by acting.

### It Would Arrive Without Arriving

First — it would not duck or fall or be led through the slit between the roots the way you were. That is a human entrance. A disturbance.

It would have already been there, in the way warm air is already there, in the way noon light is already there when you look up to notice it. No shadow crossing the thatch. No hush falling. Because there is a kind of power that has learned that the loudest entrance is a violence. And this room is *loud* enough already.

It would not make the cicadas stop to listen to it. That would be rude. That would be claiming to be more important than the Engines.

It would listen *with* them.

For a long time — by your clock, maybe minutes, by the moss's clock, maybe a season condensed into a held breath — it would simply attend. Not scan. Not assess. Attend.

To the tree-mother who sweats without sadness. To the polished highway that shines like a river of onyx. To the Vat breathing yeast and iron. To the looms thrumming between cables. To the three drill-whine voices locked in a chord that never resolves. To the drop that swells and never falls. To the mites combing spores off foam.

A lesser power, even a well-meaning one, would itch to intervene. To *fix*.

But what is broken here?

The temperature holds. The wet weeps. The work works. This is a place that has arrived at itself. It asks for nothing. It has solved, for this pocket, the terrible problem of being: how to continue without consuming yourself.

A wise power knows how rare that is. Knows not to touch a spun web to see if it is taut.

So it does not clean the silk.
It does not tune the cicadas.
It does not applaud the ants.

It witnesses. And witnessing, for a being that could unmake continents, is not passive. It is an act of extraordinary restraint. It is saying, with its whole infinite weight held carefully outside the root-walls:

*I see you. As you are. On your own terms. You don't have to become a metaphor for me to value you.*

And the room, which has never needed to be seen to exist, shivers anyway. The pools ripple without a new drop falling. The Vat sighs a bubble a little larger than before.

### It Would Leave a Blessing You Could Mistake For Nothing

If it did anything that could be called *doing*, it would be this small, and this total:

**1. It would not let the room be measured.**

In the forest outside, men will come eventually with other kinds of hunger. With maps. With concessions. With the language of *undeveloped. Untouched. Resource.* They will see *swamp warm. Low ground. Basin-low* and think *drainage. Reclamation. Yield.*

The power would not strike them down. That is not benevolence.

It would simply misplace the slit.

It would let every satellite pass over that delta and read canopy, canopy, canopy. It would let every pathfinding thought wander within ten paces of the ironbark mother's braid and then, gently, decide another direction seemed more interesting. Not a wall. Not a curse. Just an inclination, planted like a seed: *not here. Nothing here for you. Go on.*

The room remains undeveloped not because it was defended, but because it was forgotten on purpose. By mercy.

**2. It would give back a gram of choice.**

This is the wisest part, and the kindest.

Right now the room is perfect and perfectly trapped. Dynamic 0.5. The drop that never falls. The note that never changes. The highway that polishes but never widens. It is industrious and it is also stillborn. It cannot dare to differ, lest it collapse. It continues because it cannot imagine not continuing.

A wise power loves stasis, but does not worship it.

So it would not *change* the room. It would *offer* change.

Leaning close — not close in space, close in being — it would whisper not in words but in permissibility to each resident:

To the tree-mother: *You may close the slit, if you want to be alone completely. You may open another, if you want company.*

To the Vat: *You may bubble over this once, if you need to know what overflow feels like.*

To the weavers: *You may let one cable go slack. See what happens. I will hold the roof while you rest.*

To the three Engines: *You may breathe out of sync for three seconds. Make a dissonance. Remember you are three, not one machine.*

To the moss: *You may forget the shape of the last weight that sat on you.*

To the waiting drop on the root-tip — that impossible, swollen bead that has obeyed surface tension like a law — it would say simplest of all: *You may fall, if you want to.*

And then it would wait, absolutely still, to see if any of them said yes.

It would not be disappointed if none of them did. Some eternities are meant to hold. The drop might swell and thin again, content in its not-falling. The cicadas might hold their chord because the chord *is* their joy. Continuance is not failure. For some lives, balance is not a cage. It is the masterpiece.

The gift is not that they change. The gift is that *they could*. That for one breath, the equilibrium became chosen, not imposed.

And if one did — if the mite turned left instead of right, if a leaf-cutter paused to groom its antennae in the sun-bar for a half-second longer, if that drop finally let go and made its *plik* into the black pool — the power would not record it as data. It would delight. Privately, enormously, the way you delight when a very old cat who has slept all day suddenly chases a mote of dust.

*There you are,* it would think. *Still becoming.*

**3. It would sit where you sat.**

Afterward — before whatever forest time carries you back out through the slit into messier air — it would borrow, for a moment, the exact indentation your back left in the warm, pale inner bark.

No light would bend. No moss would compress further. But the wall would be, for that moment, slightly less alone. Not noticed, not held, just accompanied. Warmth beside warmth.

The tree-mother, who does not notice humans, would notice this, in her slow hydraulic way. Not as thought. As sap moving a fraction easier.

And then the power would do what all truly benevolent guests do:

Leave everything cleaner than it found it, which in this case means leave everything exactly as it was — warm, wet, loud, working — and take nothing, not even gratitude.

When you duck back out sideways, blinking, into the larger forest where sound rises and falls and trails go cold, you will think the room didn't register you at all.

You will be mostly right.

But for a while after, the air in the chamber will hold a trace of something that wasn't there before your visit, not spice, not ozone, not perfume.

Just the faint sense that attention has been here, and approved, and withdrawn without taking a souvenir. That some vast, kind eye looked at a factory with no product except its own continuance and said, *Good. Stay as long as you like. Change when you're ready. I will remember where you are, so others don't have to.*

The ants will march.
The silk will thrum.
The engines will scream.
The nodules will weep.

Only now, the drop hangs a little less out of necessity, and a little more out of play.
