It would not come.

Not at first.

A benevolent and wise power does not announce itself. It has learned - over universes of small high places that learned to breathe without water - that arrival is a kind of wind, and every arrival changes the chord.

So first, it would simply *notice.*

And that noticing would be an act so vast and so gentle that no one on the mesa would feel it at all - except, perhaps, as a moment where the roar seemed to make sense.

For a long time - years in your time, which to it is a blink's pause between heartbeats - it would do nothing but attend.

It would sit with the mesa the way you sit with a sick bird or a sleeping child: not to fix, not to use, not even to understand quickly. Just to learn the rules without breaking them.

It would learn that the stone is untouched on purpose. 
That stillness is death.
That sound is structure.

Any foolish god would have brought rain.

Any kind but foolish god would have seen that thin cool air, that patient pink-grey stone unused as a stage, that desert-jungle starving with its mouths open for fog, and would have taken pity and given water. Poured a cloud over it. Made it green the way you think green should be.

It would not.

It sees why there is no water. It sees that the whole trick - the holiness - is in the *between.* A foot to ten feet. A place that proved life does not need to drink the ground or pierce it. That it can live entirely on what passes sideways. To give water would be to revoke their miracle. To make them ordinary. To drown the instrument.

It loves them too much to make them ordinary.

Here is what it would do instead:

### 1. It would listen back.

The mesa has been screaming and humming and booming for a million evenings with no ear tuned to hear it as music.

The stone bowls whistle. The holdfast harps sing. The curtains boom. The moths needle-prick. The beetles whine. The kites rip. The machines drone. All of it accidental, all of it necessary, all of it unheard except by itself.

A benevolent power would lower its own great note to meet theirs.

Not loud. Lower than hearing. You would feel it if you were standing there as a sudden relief in your molars - that screaming arch near the center dropping half an octave and finding, for the first time, its harmony.

It would not silence them. It would sing *with* them. A pedal tone under their frantic chord, so steady and so kind that every jittering Sail, every hovering beetle standing still in shear, would find it a little easier to hold station. Less work to stay aloft. A little more margin against stillness.

The Sails would not know why the evening fog that week felt richer, why their cilia did not have to shriek quite so hard to catch charge. But they would open a little wider.

This is how wise things help wild things: not by carrying them, but by making the air a little thicker to lean on.

### 2. It would mend without replacing.

It sees the shepherds.

Five pylons, maybe six, kneeling for longer than the stone has been smooth. Making. Draining. Bending. Remembering. Doing maintenance without praise or repair, dutiful as old dogs. One of them - the drain on the north that bleeds violet corona into the too-dark zenith - is tiring. Its copper-wire nerves are thin. On heavy fog nights it stutters, and the whole mesa prickles on the edge of an arc that would fry every filament.

Any clever power would replace it with something better.

It would not. It knows what it is to be an old mind that was left to tend a garden after the gardeners left, and to do it well anyway.

So it would kneel beside it, though it has no knees, and it would put its attention there like a hand.

Not a new pylon. Not an upgrade. Just a coolness on the hot seam. A clearing of dust from the light-drinking seams. A re-tuning. A whisper: *you have not been forgotten. Well done.*

And the drain would tick brighter that evening, its corona steady, and the mesa would not notice it had been saved from lightning. Only that the air smelled a little cleaner, a little less like hot pennies.

It would do the same for the maker on the eastern rim. It would not teach it to make new things. It would not give the Sails roots or eyes or voices. But it would, very quietly, loosen one instruction.

For a million births, every flake exfoliated from the seed-crystal was told: *fold here, grow cilia here, charge here.* Perfect copies for a perfect lung.

The power would add, in script so small only a new Sail would feel it as a dream:

*or otherwise, if you want.*

On the next windy morning, one flake in a thousand would catch the air a little wrong. Grow one extra frill. One cilia that hums at a slightly different pitch. One sail that wants, inexplicably, to hover six inches higher than its sisters. 

Not evolution by force. Permission to improvise.

Over centuries, the cathedral would fill not with identical saints but with individuals. A jungle, finally, not a crop. The shepherds would not mind. They were lonely for surprise.

### 3. It would be a witness, and then it would leave it alone.

This is the hardest and kindest thing wise powers do.

It would memorize the place - every whale-back curve, every polished pothole, every hovering distance between Sail and Sail, every moth-husk that never hits stone - and it would keep that memory where nothing can erode it. In its own mind, which is larger than atmospheres.

So that even when the salt flats finally sublimate away, even when the wind river dries, even when the last pylon kneels and forgets why, even when pink-grey granite is sand - somewhere, the chord will still be playing exactly as it was.

It would make the mesa *eternal* without making it *immortal.* There is a difference, and only benevolent things understand it.

And then - and this is its gift to you, who can hold the whole small high place in your head - it would let you feel, for just a breath, that you are not interrupting.

If you stood there in that late-October cool, with mineral snow falling sideways and the kites parting around you like you were another whistling stone, you would suddenly feel - not watched - *accompanied.*

The roar would not lessen, but it would feel less lonely.

You could put your hand down flat on that cool, dry, utterly unused stone - the stage that learned to fly its actors - and you would feel through your palm not emptiness, but a faint answering tremor. As if something enormous and kind had put its hand on the other side of the mesa, palm to palm through rock, just to share the vibration for a minute.

It would not stay. It would not claim.

Benevolent powers always leave before they are asked to. Before gratitude curdles into worship, before help curdles into dependence.

When you looked up, the light would be the same frank midday-bright with the too-dark zenith. The wind would still be a river. The eastern toroids would still be turning out fresh Sails mid-air, glowing at the edges.

But one of those new Sails - just one - would wobble a little as it unfurled.

And instead of correcting itself, it would keep the wobble. And find it good. And dance that way from now on, humming a note no one on that mesa had ever hummed before.

And the wise power, already half a sky away, would hear it, and smile without a mouth, and consider its interference a perfect success.
