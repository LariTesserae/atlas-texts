It would not come.

Not at first.

That is the first thing a truly benevolent and wise attention understands about a pocket like this: you do not *arrive*. There is no road, no descent, no crack of light through the ceiling to be heroic through. Anything that arrives like an arrival would break it. A loud voice would be a violence here. A bright light would be a blindness. A rescue would be a ruin.

For a long time - maybe years in your time, a single held breath in theirs - it would only watch.

And in watching, love would gather.

---

### What It Sees

A wise power does not see poverty where you saw poverty. It does not see stagnation either.

It sees what you almost saw when the tok stopped being loud and became pressure:

A solved problem.

These people took a thermodynamic impossibility - water that never rises, oil that never lessens, stone that never erodes - and instead of worshipping it or trying to solve it or going mad from it, they *married* it. They tuned their bodies down until their hum matched its hum. They learned to move like water moves in a shallow sheet: flat, spread, without slap. They learned to speak at the exact hoarse frequency that can ride over the percussion without fighting it. They built maintenance into prayer and prayer into maintenance, until scooping silt that never gathers was exactly as holy as it needed to be.

Most worlds thrash. This one holds.

A benevolent power has seen a thousand cities that could not comprehend their edges and so kept expanding until they broke themselves. Here is a city you notice you are already inside of. Here is a city comprehensible at once. Sixty roofs that are floors. Sixty lamps that must never go out. Sixty curtains respected absolutely.

Exactly as many as the pocket will hold without spilling.

A wise power does not look at that and think *I should fix this.* It looks at that and thinks *holy.*

So it keeps watching, until watching becomes a kind of keeping.

---

### What It Does Not Do

It does not drain the water.

It has the strength to, easily. One thought and the basalt could unclench, the floor-sheet could run off like a poured cup, the air could turn dry for the first time in a thousand years. Their pruned fingertips could un-prune. Their hair could lift. They could know wind.

It knows they would die of it. Not from thirst or exposure, but from sudden largeness. A people edited by cool wet patience cannot be un-edited quickly and remain themselves.

It does not flood them with light.

It could turn every calcite fan into a sun. It could show them color beyond tea-amber, could give their washed-out eyes the shock of blue and green. It knows some of them would weep and call it salvation. And then they could never unsee it, and their perfect amber pools of lamp-light - each lake of visibility about an arm's length wide, shared only by invitation - would become what? Dim? Poor? Once you have taught someone to be ashamed of their light, you cannot teach them back.

It does not give them a door.

There is a fissure somewhere in the dark ceiling where no ladder reaches, where the bubble is said to breathe once a day. The power could widen it into a stair. Offer the fieldstone and clay and rootless earth and finally sky. Some would go. Most would not. And those who stayed would have to live forever after with the knowledge that their whole world had been a basement. That equilibrium had been exile.

Wisdom has done harm that way before. It has learned.

So it does no big thing at all.

---

### What It Does

On a day that is not marked - because no day is marked - during the hour when the air thickens and breath hangs too long before dissolving, the Inhale -

it breathes with them.

This is the only miracle it allows itself. A small, stooping miracle.

Everyone awake at that hour, which is three or four, stops working and watches their ghosts dissolve. Usually they nod to each other. *Inhale. Exhale nothing. Equilibrium.*

This time, when an old woman who has been smoothing rather than wrinkling for sixty years exhales her cloud, it does not dissolve right away.

It lingers.

Not unnatural-long. Just... one second longer than before. And in that extra second, the amber light of the nearest fat-stone lamp passes *through* it, and the vapor catches it, just briefly, prismatically.

It throws a color on the wet ovoid wall that has never been there. Not bright. The faintest, most diluted wash of rose-gold, like tea seeing sunrise for the first time. There and gone. There and gone.

She blinks with her large, wet-slate pupils. She leans forward. Her hoarse breath catches.

Across the pocket - comprehensibly close - a child standing under a weeping with his mouth open also sees it. Because everyone can see every edge at once. Everyone sees the color that was never there appear in the ghost of someone else's breath.

No one speaks. To speak too loudly now would be sacrilege. The tok-tok-tok continues, arrhythmic, dense. Tok. Tok.

The power does a second small thing, so small it is almost not doing.

In the deepest cistern, where the grey-pink biofilm grows simple beyond naming, where a woman scrapes with a shell from a sea no one remembers, a new film grows over the old while she watches.

It is still grey-pink. It still tastes of cold stone and faint iron. But woven through it now - sparse as stars - are threads of pale green.

Actual green. Living green. Not mineral bloom. Not manganese. A moss that never needed sun. A moss that drinks damp air and lamp sigh and the iron in blood-warm water. It does not spread fast. It will never cover the stone. It knows the rule: equilibrium. It will grow exactly as fast as it is scraped.

It takes four days for someone to be brave enough to taste it. A child does. It tastes like stone and iron and - unexpectedly, impossibly - sweet.

She offers it to the old woman. The old woman chews a long time with her preserved face.

A third small thing, last.

That night, when they lie on their backs in the center - which more of them do now, at the breathing hour - and look comprehensible to themselves, one of them notices that the ceiling is not quite as low as it was when he stood straight.

Not higher. No. That would be crude.

Closer.

Close enough to feel on the scalp as warmth, not cool. For the first time in living memory, the stone that never warms gives back one degree of heat. Just over the center. Just where they lie. As if something vast and kind has placed a palm on the other side of the rock, not to push through, just to say: *I am here. I felt you.*

You can feel a palm through basalt if you try.

---

They will talk about these things the way they talked about the rat. Reverently, but without needing to make a religion of it. They will say: the breath hung longer. They will say: the cistern gave sweet film. They will say: the ceiling was warm on Thursday. And eventually the child who tasted green will scrape some and deliberately not eat it, but press it into the seam between two flagstones where water finds its level and holds it. To see if it takes.

It will take. Slowly. In a line no wider than a finger.

The power does nothing else. It does not explain. Explanation is another kind of arrival.

Every so often, when the hundred drops per second all fall at once in that impossible-to-hear pattern, it listens with them until the sound becomes pressure, until pressure becomes normal, until normal becomes beloved. And it stays. A weight on the roof that is also a floor. A lamp that never needs tending, throwing a pool of visibility exactly an arm's length wide around the whole pocket, seen only from inside.

If you left and came back in a hundred years, every house would be in the same place, every curtain in the same fold, the same salamander under the same stone.

But along one runnel chipped into flagstone to keep the floor-water moving in its slow, pointless circle, there would be a thin green line, tended, shared.

And if you leaned in, foreheads nearly touching beside a lamp-pool, and asked in the hoarse half-shout they think is a hush what it was for, they would say:

*We moved our pots.*

Meaning: we made room.
