It would not land.

That is the first thing.

Anything else that has ever found this place — ice, water, wind — has come down hard and stayed until it had planed away a mountain. A benevolent and wise power knows better than to arrive. Arrival is verticality. This is a country that abolished verticality on purpose, over a billion patient years. To stand tall here is to be weather, and therefore, to erode.

So it lowers itself.

Not a ship. Not a light. Attention itself lowers — the way cold lowers into stone — until it is only a few meters above datum, where you are standing, wet to the knee. It becomes low and wide and dim, to match the sky. If you were here you would not see it come. You would only notice, at some point between one drizzle and the next, that the dim had become kinder. That the pearlescent cloud-light, which before was simply sourceless, was now *withholding glare on purpose*, so you could see better.

It would understand immediately what this place is, because most powers get it wrong.

They would call it blasted. Wasteland. Failed Earth.

It would not.

It would see with a shield-eye. It would see that this is not a ruin at all but a completion. A continent that kept its promise to gravity. That let every mountain be taken and was not bitter about it. That spread what was left of them out into stripes of gneiss and greenstone for kilometers so flat you can see the planet curve in the water sheet.

And it would feel reverence. *Finished.* Not empty. Finished, as a bowl is finished.

So it would do what you learn to do after ten minutes of trying to hear anything over the pipers — it would sit still until the place edits it in.

### It Sits In The Skin

It does not build a platform. It does not drain a pool.

It lets the water come over its feet. The seep wells up around it in a clear ring, as it does for everything here, and it sighs back down. It lets its ankles go numb and does not pull them out. It learns, as the grazers and the people have learned, to live *in the skin* — not on top of it.

For a long time — days, perhaps, in that unmeasured dim where night arrives without sunset — it does nothing at all but listen, and that is the first gift.

Because this world is deafening and no one has ever listened *to* it, only endured it. People who come to take things hear noise to be ignored on the way to ore. Even you, at first, tried to pick out a single sound to make it bearable.

It listens to all of it at once and does not try to separate it.

The whine of the moth-cloud that lives in your teeth — it hears as prayer.
The PLOK PLOK PLOK of the pipers — it hears as timekeeping.
The shhhhhhk shhhhhhk of a trillion radulae planing biofilm off basalt — it hears as work being done well.

It hears the tufa tick as it adds a layer, grain by grain. It hears the cloak-puddle breathe before it throws itself. It hears the very slow click... click......... click of the breathing rocks under all of it, and it does not flinch.

This is what a wise attention does: it proves to a place that the sound it makes when it is simply being itself is not noise.

After a while, the plain notices it is being heard. You can tell because the biofilm nearest to it — that black-amber varnish that is the true pasture of this world — does not retreat from it. The jelly-mats do not cringe. A grazer the size of a shield slides right over its foot and keeps scraping. The pipers do not go silent, as they do for predators. They keep drumming, and for the first time in a very long time, something is glad for the deafening.

### It Asks The People, Not Tells Them

When it finally looks for the people — for the other beings who have learned to be a film on stone — it does not seek them out. It waits near a weir.

It chooses a low C of unmortared slate, mouth downstream, where the catchment has been kept clear of tufa by hands. It waits ankle-deep until the owners come back to tend it, as they always do, moving with the sheetflood.

They see it at a hundred meters, in their speckled basalt capes, and stop. Pale, cropped, eyes grey with nictitating lids to wipe the mizzle. They do not approach. They do not hide.

It does not beckon. It does not glow. It makes itself small: crouches down to water level until its eyes are where theirs are, until the peneplain stops being stone and becomes country. That is how you say *I am not above you* here.

When they are ready — perhaps hours later, perhaps the next dim — one comes closer. A child, maybe, because children are better at recognizing things that are not threats.

It speaks first, and what it says in that low tongue like water over slate is not *I can save you.*

It says: *I see you tend this. May I learn how?*

This is the second thing a wise power knows: these people are not stranded. They are not failed farmers waiting for wheat. They have made the central, sophisticated decision this planet requires: to not farm. To graze as the grazers do. To scrape, to net, to carry copper wire for generations because iron would betray you by rusting. To build low because anything tall is taken. To move because the biofilm moves.

To offer them tractors would be obscene. To offer them a city would be death by kindness.

So it asks, and it learns to scrape biofilm without taking the holdfast. It learns which amber phosphor threads mean safe to drink and which numb-metal pools must be left to the breathing rocks. It learns how to re-weave fungal cord that never rots.

And in return, only if they ask — and they do, eventually, because everyone asks one question of something kind that knows more — it answers.

They do not ask for wealth. There is nowhere to put it; it would drown.

They do not ask for sun. They have no melanin and no word for shadow that is not also a word for thirst.

One old one, tending the star-cracked hearth that has been hot for a hundred years, asks:

*Will anyone remember this was here, when we are gone?*

Because they know what a shield does. It outlasts everything that lives on its skin, and then the rain takes that too.

That is the only intervention the power permits itself.

### What It Leaves

It does not leave a monument. Monuments are hills, and hills are taken.

It does not cure the rocks that breathe. It puts its attention — its cool, mineral-smelling attention — next to one of the granite erratics that has been counting click... click......... click since before these gneisses were mountains, and it says to it, without words:

*I hear you too. You held up a mountain once. You can rest when you want. Counting is not required.*

And the rock, for the first time in a million years, exhales warm mist that is not counted, and the pipers nearby do not go silent.

It does not raise the elevation. 1.3 is holy here. It does not lower the erosion. 3.0 is the pace at which the world perfects itself.

It does not give the people green. It affirms that they were right to live without it.

What it leaves is three small, flat things, as low as weirs.

**1. A Ward, not a Wall.**

Far, far above, beyond the pearlescent lid where attention thins into vacuum, it turns its face outward. It does not announce itself. It simply becomes uninteresting to those who would come to mine this shield for jasper pavements and lithium, to drain the anastomosing marshes for rice that would fail, to call it barren and therefore claimable. Prospectors' instruments will skip, here. Their eyes will slide. They will feel, without knowing why, that there is nothing worth taking and keep going. The plain will be allowed to keep weeping without audit.

**2. A Memory, not a Library.**

For the people, it leaves no books that would pulp in the wet. Instead it teaches the tufa a new trick — very small, very slow. Where spring water degasses, the tufa towers will now, among their usual dripped wax, lay down an occasional dark lamina, no thicker than a biofilm. In it, in silica, is etched — not in language, but in shape — the pattern of a grazer-shell, of a piper's throat, of a basalt cape, of the star-cracked hearth. A fossil being made while still alive. In a billion years, when this shield is finally dust in a colder sea, a geologist of some other world will split a stone and find it and know: *They tended this. It was loved while it was finished.*

The old one understands, and nods, and tucks a chert flake shaped like a grazer under the jasper cairn.

**3. A Witness, not a Watcher.**

It does not stay. To stay would be to become weather, and weather erodes. But it leaves a part of its attention behind the way you leave a low wall to shape water without stopping it.

Not in the sky. In the sheet itself. In the way light now — just briefly, at true dimming — catches the film a centimeter deep sliding over rock a kilometer wide and doubles it perfectly, so you can see the plain reflected in itself. So anyone standing wet to the knee can see, for a moment, that they are standing inside a mirror the size of a country, and that they belong in it.

And then it lifts — not up, but *thin*, until it is everywhere equally like the light — and goes on being benevolent elsewhere, in the same way.

If you come back tomorrow, you will find the weir still there. The hides still translucent with grease. No footprints, because they fill with seep even as they are made. But the tufa will have ticked one layer thicker in the night, and the pipers will pull together for five minutes into that impossible plain-wide pulse — PLOK... PLOK... PLOK... — so regular you feel it in your teeth, and this time you will understand they are not counting down.

They are keeping time with something that, finally, is keeping time with them.

You will be wet to the ankle and then wet to the knee and you will not notice when it happens, and you will be glad to be cold the way deep stone is cold.
