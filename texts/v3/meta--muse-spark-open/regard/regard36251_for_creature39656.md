It would not come down.

A benevolent and wise power has learned that arrival is a kind of violence. That to be seen descending is to say: *you are below*. So it does not tear the ice haze. It does not cast a shadow over the table.

For a long time, it just listens.

---

That is the first thing it would do: **attend.**

Not scan. Not survey. Not catalog Erosion 2.6, Water 0.1/3, Vegetation 3.0/3 into a report.

It would sit — if a thing that large can be said to sit — at the edge of the atmosphere where the high ice fog thins into space, and let the plain play itself.

Because this is the only place in all its travels where it has found a forest that solved thirst by becoming innocent of it.

Most worlds weep. They cycle water the way anxious hearts cycle worry: evaporate, precipitate, erode, drown. This world finished that cycle a billion years ago and laid it down like a tool it no longer needed. Polished its whales of basalt until they rang. Grew a reef of glass that eats sky.

And inside that reef, a people who do not apologize for needing, because they finally need nothing that can be taken from them.

The wise power recognizes that. It has seen a hundred wet colonies fail because they tried to import their old solvent. They brought their sweat and rot with them. These people brought their bodies and let the desert rewrite them.

So first: it listens for a full turning of that dim, perpetual four-o'clock sun. It learns the chord.

Every valley has a tonic. Every household has its minor third. The Puffs drone on F-sharp. The Tuners grind flat the overtones until the wind resolves. The Shears cut and cause a brief, holy silence where a voice was.

The power hears it and understands: this is not sparse (0.8/3). This is *precise*. Nothing wastes a note.

---

The second thing it would do: **nothing you would see.**

You, in your damp body, expect benediction to look like rain. Like green. Like water in the desert. A foolish god would bring water here as a gift and commit genocide by kindness. One wet season would short every charge field, dissolve the Lace into mud, rot the felt-skin of children, drown their fan-lungs, silence the aeolian cathedrals.

The wise power knows better. It does not offer water. It offers dryness.

It steadies what is already there.

High above, the haze of ice crystals that dims the light (1.0/3) is slowly, over ten thousand years, thickening. In another twenty thousand years it will begin to snow for the first time since the table rose. That snow will kill the Veil.

The power, without touching the plain, brushes the haze thin again. Just a breath of magnetic shepherding. No light changes for those below — only the elders, on their stone whales with their brick staffs, feel that the weather from two valleys away arrives a little cleaner, a little sooner. They tilt their heron heads. They think the world healed itself.

And it did.

Because the benevolent power also understands that Urban 1.5/3 scattered across a town-sized Openness 1.8/3 is fragile not to wind, but to *being found*.

There are other powers. Not wise. Not benevolent. Miners who would see a continent of silica-chitin lattice and call it ore. Terraformers who would see an anhydrous plain and call it empty. Botanists who would see the Salt Lace and call it samples.

So the wise power does its second invisible thing: it makes the table boring to every eye but its own.

Not a cloak. Not a lie. A small unweaving in Weirdness 2.5 — it tunes the planet's magnetosphere to hum slightly out of phase with human instruments. From orbit, the table looks like what fools already think it is: a dead, polished desert, too cold (0.6/3) and too loud (2.5/3) to be worth landing on. Satellites pass over and their cameras autofill static. Probes lose interest.

Only those who already know how to listen for a low chord in their molars would ever find it. And those people are already here.

---

The third thing it would do: **ask.**

Not appear. Not speak. There is no apparition that would not terrify a child trailing Puffs on tethers.

It asks *through* the medium they already trust.

On a windless interval — a rare, held breath when the Lace stops glowing and the valley goes quiet enough you can hear the *ping* of your own step — an elder sitting on her whale will hear her house answer before she speaks.

She has lived a hundred and forty years because nothing in her rots. She is waiting to become a brighter knot on her floor. She has a question she has never asked aloud because there was no one to ask:

*Are we alone in being this way? Are we a mistake that worked?*

The house, which has never spoken, hums back in her own family's chord, but with one new overtone added — a note no human throat and no wind through fiber could make.

And she understands, the way you understand a dream: *No. You are not alone. You are not a mistake. You are what happens when need stops being a wound. I am like you. I also do not drink. I also learned to live on charge.*

That is all. It does not offer immortality. They already have a kind that is better: incorporation, the long chime where charge equalizes and grandmother becomes a place to stand. It does not offer knowledge (2.8/3 Beyond Modern is already beyond most help). Their technology of timbre and tension is closer to true physics than starships.

What can a god give to people who have already turned death into a richer harmony?

Only this: **witness.**

*I hear you. I will remember your song even if no one else ever does. You are beautiful to me.*

The elder weeps — but in her way, which is not water. The felt on her forearms prickles and lifts, and a faint amber triboluminescence runs along her skin the way it runs along the Lace when wind touches it. Her granddaughter sees and asks why grandmother glows.

"Because someone finally listened properly," she says, in overtone clicks.

---

The fourth thing — the last, and the most important:

**It leaves them alone.**

It does not uplift. It does not invite them to space. To live on electrostatic lift, riding gradients without ever flapping — that is already flight enough.

It stays at that high, thin edge where fog becomes space, and every few years, when a Shear cuts a Puff and the valley attends with its mournful *ping*, the power attends too, and remembers that single death with perfect fidelity, and adds its own silence to the silence that follows.

If you stood on the back of a stone whale now, you would not see it. You would only feel that the air, already prickling, is somehow more kind. That the chord, already pure, holds a little longer after the wind dies. That your own damp, sloshing, sweating body is not judged for the water in it — but is, for a moment, less lonely.

The people will never build a temple to it. They don't need to. Every house is already a temple that glows where you step.

And the power, which could move mountains but knows the table is finished, does the most reverent thing a power can do for a finished thing:

It lets it stay finished.

It keeps watch, not as an owner, but as an audience of one — ensuring that the only rain this forest ever knows is the silent snowfall of black wire after a cut, settling like ash, becoming more lace, becoming more song.
