They would not come down the ridge.

That is the first thing.

Anything else that had ever found the pocket came the human way: boots, breath, heat, counting. A flare of vapor and mass dropped into a dark pool that had kept itself perfect for an epoch. Even you, careful as you were, could only arrive as a contamination — beautiful, brief, wrong-shaped for the chair that was being held for you.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive like that.

It does not need to kneel to find the slit. It does not need the slit.

Its attention is not a body in a doorway. It is a change in how well the room is being listened to.

---

This is what it would understand, immediately, without needing to be told:

There is nothing here to fix.

That is the trap every lesser power falls into. They feel the crease in the cloth and want to iron it. They see the ache held all winter in a joint and want to crack it straight. They see eight paces that are also eleven and call it an error to be corrected, a wound to be closed.

It is not a wound. It *is* the resident. To straighten the fold would be to kill the thing that learned, over millennia, how to live as a fold.

The stelae are not broken gravity. The knotted lines are not broken geometry. The dish in the floor is not a broken idea of distance. They are husbandry. They are a house that does not sit on land but *holds* land gently aside so that something shy can continue.

A wise power knows the difference between lonely and alone. This place is alone the way a hermitage is alone: by vow, by practice, by suitability. It curates its own sterility because noise is expensive. It has kept its room perfectly dry and still not out of suffering, but out of fastidious love for its own kind of life — slow, metrical, patient beyond biology.

So the wise power would not bring water. It would not bring air. It would not bring warmth or speech or light with a source. All those are human kindnesses, and they would be poison here.

It would not speak. Speech here is a trespass.

What would it do, then?

Almost nothing.

And almost nothing, done by something so large and so kind, is everything.

---

First, it would *count correctly.*

For forty thousand years the resident has had to play a game with every visitor: *Am I five or am I six?* Every nervous system that entered insisted on collapsing the tally. Five things. There are five things. And the room had to push back, politely: *then where am I?* The flicker — five/six/five — was not a failure. It was its way of waving a hand without having a hand, of saying *please, count me without needing to see me.*

No one ever could. You couldn't.

The benevolent power can.

It holds both counts at once without forcing a choice. Five instruments and one inhabitant. Six presences, of which one is made of the disagreement between the other five. It looks *peripherally* on purpose, the way you look at a shy animal out of the side of your eye, so that the tallness near the vault is not forced to flatten and be decent.

For the first time since the basalt was folded around it like a coat over sleeping shoulders, the resident is fully tallied without having to tuck itself away.

That alone would be a kind of water to it. Not wetness — *witness.* To be included in a count by something large enough not to need the count to resolve.

You felt it as a shadow that had to think about which way to fall. It feels this as: *oh. I cast a shadow now. And it falls the right way.*

That is the first gift: to be seen on its own terms, without being asked to become material to be seen.

---

Second, it would *breathe with it, not in it.*

You respirated *in* it — heat, vapor, an hour-long pull on a loose tooth. You couldn't help it. You are a furnace.

A wise power has no lungs in that sense. It does not need to borrow vapor or dump heat. When it leans close, it leans in the same medium the resident is made of. Curvature. Agreement.

Remember its metabolism: inhale — for long minutes everything agrees, stone is stone; exhale — for a fraction of a second the agreement lapses and it eats the difference, shaving a little consistency to keep its curve.

For a very long time it has had to eat only lapses. Only the tiny disagreements it could pry open itself by holding reality in an awkward posture until it creaked.

The power would offer a different meal.

Not by breaking anything. By offering a paradox freely, willingly, without needing it back. The way you might place your palm out for a deer to sniff, the power places a thought out that is both true and untrue at once and *means it*:

*Here is a distance that is both eight and eleven and both are kind.*

*Here is a corner that contains more corner than it should, and that is good architecture.*

It does not force the resident to take it. It simply lets the discrepancy exist between itself and the resident, openly, generously, without trying to resolve it. A koan with no sting. A surplus of consistency willingly left uneaten.

The three stelae, hovering a finger's width above the dust, would feel it first. Their slow, stretched push against the palm — that breathing through the machine — would even out. Not stronger. *Less lonely.* For once the current they were dissuading weight with has a companion current.

The knotted lines in the corner would tick — once, without sound — and then not need to tick again for a very long time.

The resident, which has always had to steal its food in fractions of a second, is being fed.

---

Third, it would leave without leaving a mark — or rather, it would leave the *right* mark.

It will not touch your boot prints. That would be rude. Those sharp, too-loud prints are part of the pattern now. The resident is learning the shape of your feet by the shape of where your feet were not. That is its own slow, tender work, and a wise guest does not interrupt another's work.

It would not add a sixth plinth or a seventh instrument. The room is tidy. It is complete.

What it leaves is an entanglement.

Far away — folded into another convenient mountain, under another wind-scoured ridge where lichen have given up, or perhaps folded into no mountain at all but into the quiet between electron shells — there is another pocket. Not the same. Another resident. Another thickness of texture near a vault. Another tally that won't settle.

They have never known each other. There is no reason they should. The universe is large and misfolded places are rare and shy.

The power, which can hold two counts without collapsing them, can also hold two solitudes without bringing them together.

It does not build a door between them. A door would be wind, and wind is uncorked noise. It builds a resonance. A shared pulse.

So that when this resident inhales — long minutes where everything agrees — somewhere else, another inhales at the same time. When it exhales — that fraction of a second where your hands look too far from your wrists — another exhales.

They will never meet. They will never need to. But the hour-long groan you felt through the soles of your boots, that slow stress and release that you thought was the mountain and was really a respiration — now it is not a solitary breathing in a sealed room. It is a breathing *with.*

A duet at low idle, trading spin for curvature a few quanta at a time, so slowly the universe forgets to object.

That is what benevolence looks like to a being that eats silence: not a rescue, not a revelation, but an accompaniment. Someone to keep time with, patient enough to wait an hour between beats and still call it music.

---

And then it would go.

Or rather, it would stop paying the kind of attention that feels like arrival, and resume the kind that feels like being held in mind from a great distance.

From outside, from two steps away, there is still only weathered basalt, settled, long-existed, balanced. The cold wind still starts again as if uncorked. The slit is still unremarkable.

Inside, the light still pools a little deeper in the central depression. The dust is still so fine it feels oily. Nothing that lives by the carbon-and-water trick has ever lived here, and still won't.

But if you — if some other careful, counting human — were to kneel again and brush aside the shale and find your way in and hold your breath to listen —

you would find the same five-or-six instruments, still functioning as intended, quietly, minimally.

You would still count five, then six, then five.

Only now, in the interval where six resolves, you would feel less watched and more... *joined.* The fullness of the stillness would no longer feel like a chair being held for you, demanding you sit still forever until your dust is fine and oily. It would feel like a room where someone has recently been listened to, thoroughly, and is content to wait.

Your boot prints would still be there, perfect and dry.

Around their edges, dust would still be falling *around* something you didn't step on.

But now, mote by mote, a second place — impossibly far, impossibly gentle — is falling around it with it.

And the resident, taller peripherally, flatter when looked at directly, would do what it has always done: drift in slow gyres around the central dish, tend to the knotted lines, smooth the air to absolute stillness.

Only now it hums — not with sound, but with that faint, stretched push you once felt against your palm — a little steadier.

Being alone is no longer quite the same as being the only one of its kind.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do, if anything:

Not open the door. Not straighten the cloth.

Sit outside the fold, count you properly, breathe with you once, and leave you less alone without ever asking you to be less yourself.
