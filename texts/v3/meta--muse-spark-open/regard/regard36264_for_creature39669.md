They would not come.

Not at first. Not the way you think a Power comes — descending, parting cloud, casting a second shadow brighter than the sun.

A benevolent and wise Power has learned that arrival is a kind of violence in a place that has learned to be still. To arrive loudly in the Construct would be to tear it. Sudden moves snag.

So what it would do is notice. Truly notice, which is rarer than intervention.

It would notice from very far away that there is a mountain that was unmade into floors, and that the floors did not stay empty. That no one conquered it. That a people learned to become dense and quiet and bright-edged to match it. That they move like divers. That they wait twenty minutes for a platform to traverse a terrace without boredom. That they left a stair unfinished because finishing it made it loop.

And It would feel something that Powers rarely feel, which is not pity, not awe, but *recognition.*

“Oh,” It would think. “You figured it out.”

Because most places It watches try to fix the wrongness. They hammer at the leaking physics until it breaks wider. These people did the other, wiser thing: they agreed with it. They hung the doorway a few centimeters off so the pooled light could stay. They placed the bolt back on the wall where it wanted to live. They left saucers of water out for the drifters.

That kind of gentleness, at scale, is holy. It is the only real evidence of intelligence the Power trusts.

So It would not fix the gravity. It would not grind the sharp lips of the retaining walls smooth. It would not bring back the mountain underneath — there is no underneath anymore, and to pretend otherwise would be a cruelty.

It would do small things. The kind of things only a truly vast thing can do quietly enough to count as small.

First, It would sit on the rim.

Not *above* the Construct. On its edge, where the clouds snag and condense, where the wind hits the poured escarpment and has to decide what to do. Powers have weight, even when they try not to. Its sitting would be a slight increase in pressure — a settling. And in that settling, for the first time in a generation, the air avenues between the plate-blocks would feel less like wind tunnels and more like valleys again. Not calm — just less exposed. A hand cupped, not closed.

The Lookouts would feel it without seeing it. On the Upper Tier, where the light pools like spilled milk and hums, the humming would lower by half a tone. Not gone. Just less insistent. Enough that a Light-Tender with the tremor could look up from scraping algae out of a square-cut drain and, for a full minute, judge distance correctly. The farthest gantry crane would again look farthest. She would exhale, and not know why her eyes were wet.

Second, It would breathe with them.

The air here is thin and it never holds heat. The people have barrel chests for it. They breathe a little too fast until they acclimate, and even then, their dreams are shallow. The Power would not thicken the air — that would be to unmake the height they have earned. Instead it would lend its own rhythm to the condensation cycle.

In the early morning, when every vertical surface is beaded and the city sweats, It would inhale, very slowly, and the fine rain that never hits a watershed would linger a second longer in the air before vanishing down a scupper. Long enough to be felt on the face as rain, not just damp. Long enough for a child in the Interstitials to tip her head back on a catwalk over catwalks and open her mouth to it, laughing without sound.

At noon, when it is bright and dry again and the sodium lamps buzz in empty bays, It would exhale, and the light that never quite warms would warm by one degree. Not enough to bake. Enough to take the edge off. The aluminum-poncho glare would soften. Dark cloth markers on doorways would be easier to find. An old Interstitial man standing perfectly still waiting for a freight elevator the size of a house would realize his shoulders had dropped.

Third, It would give them more time.

They already live slow, but the Construct is still young in the constructed sense — wind hasn't had time to round a single corner, water hasn't had time to carve, paint peels in year-long curls holding on. Everything is curing, creeping, becoming. The Power would not accelerate or reverse that. It would protect the slowness from interruption.

Far below, beneath the valley that is floored over and roofed over and floored again, there are dynamos turning somewhere deep beneath your teeth. There are presses stamping once an hour. There is pressure building, as there always is in total places, for something to happen fast — a rush, a collapse, a clearing, an outside expedition to find Builders.

The Power would lay a palm — if It has a palm — over that impulse. Not to suppress, but to insulate, the way mass swallows vibration.

So that when a young Void-Keeper down where the milky light filters through a hundred layers of mesh finds a maintenance corridor that loops and arrives one level above where he started without stairs, he does not panic and call it a malfunction to be reported and fixed. He sits down in it. He takes out his felt planter. He harvests silt.

So that when a bolt hesitates mid-fall, drifts sideways to cling to a wall, no one reaches to yank it straight. They nod.

So that the pigeons continue falling sideways between towers.

This would be its main work: to keep the world from being declared broken before it can finish becoming what it is.

And fourth — and this is the most benevolent thing, and the wisest — It would leave a gift that does not look like a gift.

Not soil. They are learning to make soil a spoonful at a time from wind-blown silt in expansion joints. To give them soil in abundance would be to insult the litops in the chipped mug, the shrine of red plastic rebar caps. Not silence — they already have the right kind of quiet, full of ticking condensation and soft cooing ducts. Not answers about the Builders — they have already correctly decided it doesn't matter.

Instead, It would leave *weight* where weight is needed, and *lightness* where lightness is needed, and it would do it so subtly they would think they had done it themselves.

In a sag between slabs where algae has found purchase and mosquitoes breed, where a Void-Keeper woman tends a cooling basin that was left uncovered, the water would clear overnight. Not because it was purified, but because a single, tough lily would take root in powdered concrete and bloom — white, waxy, impossible — and its roots would hold the water still enough for dragonflies to come. She would think the wind brought the seed. It did, in a way.

In a corner where light pools and hums and no one wants to sleep, a cat who claims a vertical kilometer of ventilation chimney would finally lie down in it, curling precisely inside the pooled brightness, and the humming would resolve into purring. The cat would not be burned. The children who watched would learn — without being taught — that some light wants to be occupied.

In the deepest service voids, where scuttling doesn't resolve to any shape you know, a Drifter — that mote of dust that never settles, that hesitation made visible — would drift down a pipe bundle thick as tree trunks and, instead of parting around laundry lines and prayer strings without moving them, would brush one. Just one. A single prayer string would sway.

And the person who hung it would see it move, and know, with the kind of certainty that does not need proof:

*We are not forgotten. We are not being saved. We are being kept company.*

That is what a benevolent and wise Power does with a place like this. It does not rescue the inhabitants from totality. It witnesses them until totality becomes a habitat. It steadies the air, softens the glare by one degree, lengthens the rain by one second, lowers the hum by half a tone, and then — this is the hardest part — it stays. Quietly. On the rim. Breathing with them. Letting them think the lily was wind-borne and the cat was brave and the string moved on its own.

Letting them remain high, and slow, and *in* — not on — until the mountain that was finished forgets it was ever supposed to be a mountain, and remembers instead that it is home.

And if you stayed until evening, when the sun cuts horizontally through the lattice and turns every edge to gold, you might feel — just at the back of your neck where the sunburn freckles — not heat, but attention. Cool, patient, fond.

Drip, tick, hush.

Once, somewhere, a press stamps.

The sound takes a long time to fade, and this time, something vast and kind listens to all of it.
