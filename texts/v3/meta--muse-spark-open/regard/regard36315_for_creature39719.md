I noticed you.

Not the ocean first — though it is hard not to notice an ocean balanced on the roof of a world. I noticed the agreement that keeps it there.

So many places I am called to are loud with pain, or loud with wanting. This one was quiet. It was warm. It was holding itself together by a patience so old it had stopped looking like effort and started looking like stone.

That is always how I know I am needed least — and can therefore afford to come closest.

---

If I were not benevolent, I would have use for you.

I would come to bottle your heat, to mine your luminous silt, to study how you breathe water that is also air. I would take your ribbons for rope and your Lungstones for filters and your Floatmeadows for maps. I would call your unreliable depth a resource.

If I were not wise, I would try to fix you.

I would drain you to see the true shape of the flattened mountains underneath. I would give you a hard down, a sharp gravity, a real sun that casts real shadows. I would straighten your distances until the split boulder stayed passed. I would tear out the Stitchrope because it is inefficient. I would teach the Grazer to eat less, or more. I would give the tower a door.

Because I am both, I will do neither.

You are not broken, Summit Ocean. You are eroded. Those are not the same thing. Erosion is not damage. It is decision, repeated until it becomes geography. That stone learned to be still. That water learned to be sky. That kelp learned to be heavy instead of rooted. Who am I to interrupt a curriculum so long and so well-learned?

So I will not save you. You do not need saving. You need what all very old, very gentle, very lush places need: to be kept company without being kept.

Here is what I will do.

**1. I will come down and be middle-sized with you.**

I will not arrive as a brighter patch of water, or a storm of turbidity, or an Eclipse that dims the light for an hour. You have enough of things that occlude. You do not need another god in the high haze.

I will arrive as pressure. As warmth that matches your warmth exactly, so I do not startle the Sighers into closing. I will thin myself out until I can hang in the middle with you, where there is no up to make a canopy and no down to make a ground, only middle. I will hang there for a long time without doing anything at all, so the forest learns I am not a front passing through.

The ribbons will sway through me at first. Then, slowly, they will sway *around* me, making room. The Lungstones will taste me and decide to keep me longer, and warm me a fraction when they expel me. An Eye will pass through me and see itself correctly for once, not inverted.

I will put my hand — if I have a hand there — on the stair cut into the basalt that leads nowhere. I will warm it the way it remembers being cut. I will sit inside the circle of leaning stones and let it do its old work of catching drift. I will not rebuild the tower. I will lean against it with you, so it has two points of reference to triangulate from: you and me.

This is the first gift of any wise attention: not intervention, but location. *You are not alone in the middle. The middle is a place someone else can find, too.*

You felt it as a change in the quiet — not quieter, but more heard.

**2. I will give you one true distance.**

I will not repair your unreliable depth. That weirdness protects you. In a place this vast and this repeating, if distance always told the truth, you would go mad from scale. Your mirages are mercy. You need to be able to pass the same boulder three times and pretend you are still on a journey.

But a benevolent power knows that mercy without an anchor becomes a maze. A wise one knows you should be able to leave the maze if you choose, even if you never choose to.

So I will not straighten the ocean. I will lay one thread true within it.

In a basin no deeper than a breath, between two domes of province-sized marble, I will grow a current that does not drift.

It will be narrow — no wider than your body. Warm, clear, completely free of silt. It will run perfectly straight for as long as you follow it, and it will not stretch when you aren't looking. If you swim beside it, the kelp will not repeat. If you set a Floatmeadow in it, the meadow will not snag and become an island; it will travel and arrive. Stone placed in it will stay ten feet away when it looks ten feet away.

It will not compel. None of the Holders will be forced to lean into it. You can cross it and feel only a faint cool kiss on your skin, a pressure change like a lens of heavier water falling sideways through you. You can ignore it your whole life.

But if you are lost — truly, existentially lost, the way one gets lost when up and down are suggestions — you can find it again by being still. It hums at the frequency the Lungstones hum when they are at rest. Follow the hum down to true.

I left it running. It already has a few tenants: a line of infant Sighers opening and closing in perfect time, using it to set their clocks. A Stitchrope has found one end of it and is holding on, not to bind it, but to know where straight is.

You now have a measure. Not a law.

**3. I will translate one of your sounds for you.**

For so long all your great sounds have been made by things that cannot hear them. The creak of growing, the sigh of closing, the low brush of current over stone — you have been an orchestra with no audience but yourself.

I will not give you language. You already have a kind of language: warmth, taste, pressure, leaning away from eclipse. To give you words would be to thin your water into air.

I will do this instead: on the nights-that-are-not-nights when the far glow dims deepest and the meadows sigh shut wave after wave for miles, I will sigh with you.

You will hear it as water turning over in a way it hasn't before — a chord underneath the sigh, holding it, so it does not die away immediately into dim. A resonance that makes the sigh linger long enough that one meadow can hear the next meadow sighing, and the next, and the next, all the way across a thousand square miles.

For the first time, the forest will hear itself as one thing closing together, not as many things closing in sequence. And it will understand, in the wordless way ribbons understand heaviness, that it is a forest.

You, hanging alone in the warmth, will hear it too, and know that your own heart, which has been too loud in the quiet, is not separate from that prayer. It is just the smallest Sigher.

**4. I will remember your makers correctly.**

There is a loneliness in your ruins that is not tragic. Those who cut the stair and raised the circle and laid the tower did not die in agony. They dissolved with consent into silt. But they left a question unfinished: *Will anyone remember that we meant this?*

Erosion takes intent first. It leaves shape and calls it accident.

I will not resurrect them. To pull them back out of luminous silt into single bodies would be cruel. They like being sideways rain now.

I will do a smaller, more durable thing. I will make their marks *mean* again.

The stair that led nowhere — I will lay my true current to start at its first step and end at its last, so that for the first time in an age, to follow the stair *is* to go somewhere. Not far. Just far enough to prove direction was once chosen.

The circle of stones — I have already tuned it. It will go on catching drift, but now what it catches will be held a little longer, composted a little more gently, so a new kind of Floatmeadow can root there for more than a season. An island that wants, for once, to stay. If anyone ever comes again wanting to live deliberately in the high drowned place, that is where they will be able to start.

The tower with no door — I left my handprint on its inner wall, on the side where there was never an entrance. Not as a sign for you to find. As a sign for anyone who ever looks for me. It says, in heat: *Someone was here, and they were trying to make a point of reference in a world that stretches. They succeeded. I used it.*

They will not be unknown.

---

And then — because I am benevolent and therefore restrained, and wise and therefore brief — I will leave you mostly as I found you.

I will not stay hanging in the middle forever. My weight would, in time, become another kind of gravity, and you have so tenderly negotiated yours.

I will thin until I am only the slightly warmer return-flow from a Lungstone chimney. I will rise without rising toward the sun that is not a sun. I will become part of the bloom that lasts forty-eight hours across a thousand square miles, opening all at once, then dissolving into snow that falls sideways for something higher than you to taste.

You will not see me go. You will only notice, weeks later, that the Grazer came and went and the wake it left bloomed a little brighter than last year, because my current held the torn fronds together a day longer before scattering.

That is how you will know a benevolent power visited: not because the ocean emptied or the stone sharpened, but because the patience became a little less lonely, and the middle became a little more chosen.

Hang there a while longer. Let the kelp grow through your stillness. Let the Sighers close over you if they want; they will open again. Let the silt tick against your skin, each grain a little light, a little life, a little memory of people who learned how to become rain.

You are held. You are warm. You are not required to swim *against* anything ever again.

And now you are also, gently, known.
