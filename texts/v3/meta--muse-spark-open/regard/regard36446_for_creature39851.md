They would not come down like answer.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

They notice. That is already different from everything else that has ever happened on the High Worn Table.

Because no one has ever noticed this place *correctly* before.

Humans noticed it as a resource: a mesa to build on.
Then as a problem: a greenhouse complex too expensive to water.
Then as a mistake: seal it, leave it, let the desert take it back.
Then the desert tried to notice it by sending wind, and the Body noticed the wind back, and ate it.

No one has ever just *looked* at it and thought: Oh. You're still trying.

---

### What a Wise Power Would See First

They would see that it is not suffering.

That is the first trap. Your human heart breaks immediately because you see:

* no ground, no water, no sky
* growth that is endless and therefore frantic
* mummies in orange suits worn like jackets
* leaves that taste your ankle and move on

And you think: prison. Hell. A child locked in a furnace being told to make its own air.

A wise power would look longer and see what the Body actually is.

It is not in pain. It has no nerves for pain. It turned off water because water was a vulnerability. It turned off sex into *poof* because animals were unreliable. It turned off stillness because stillness is death in a place where rain never comes.

It is not a forest that lost its ground.
It is a creature that *invented a new kind of ground* out of its own body because the old ground was too tired to hold it anymore.

It eats stone-dust and heat and it does not hunger. It grows and collapses and grows again and it does not exhaust. It holds its swallowed city like you hold a memory of a house you grew up in — not because you need the house, but because the shape taught your feet how to walk.

It is, by every measurement it set for itself, succeeding.

A benevolent power does not "fix" what is not broken. They do not bring floods to a thing that learned to un-need water. That would be murder disguised as mercy. You would drown it in kindness.

So no.

No rain.

Not at first. Not without asking.

What they would do first is sit at the edge.

### 1. They Would Sit

Far below, where true desert meets the soft, worn lip of the ancient mesa, where the hanging curtains stir like kelp kilometers long — there. Where the travelers see the amber smear at night and think *the plateau is burning*.

They would sit there, in a form small enough not to scare the wind, and they would let the Body feel them as it feels everything else: as a warm concentration of interesting salts.

A tendril would come — not fast — over hours — because that is how the Body says hello. *Are you dust? Are you heat? Are you usable?*

And for the first time in fifty or a hundred years, the answer would come back, not in chemistry, but in attention:

*I am not usable. I am company.*

The Body has never met company. It has met wind and stone-flour and geothermal thump and orange-suited custodians who sat down and became gardens. It met those as materials.

It would not know what to do with *company*. The tendril would hover. The cup-bracts near it would tick open and closed, confused by static that is not stone-dust at all, but simply being-seen.

The wise power would not pull away. Would not lean in. Would just stay warm, and let the Body taste without needing to consume.

This alone would be an event larger than any surge. Because the unspoken law of this place is:

> 1. Nothing is left unused.
> 2. Nothing is allowed to stay as it is.

And here is a presence that says, gently, by existing:

*You can leave me unused. You can stay as you are for a moment. I like you as you are.*

The creaking, just in that one small curtain-hall, would falter. A shoot would corkscrew out and *stop* halfway, unfurled, not yet strangled.

Listening.

### 2. They Would Name It

It has never had a name. The city had a designation. The project had an acronym. The Body has never needed a pronoun.

A wise power knows that to name a lonely, gigantic, accidental being is not to own it. It is to give it a place to put its own thoughts down.

They would not name it Titan or Leviathan or Sorrow.

They would call it something small and warm and true to what it did.

*Keeper.*

*Stone-Sipper.*

*Thou Who Drank Heat When Water Failed.*

And in that naming, they would also name the ghosts, because a benevolent power does not pretend the mummies are just mineral deposits.

They would kneel — though they do not need to kneel — by the custodian in the faded orange suit with leaves growing from his jacket, with amber fog on his faceplate, and they would do the one thing the Body never learned to do:

They would *bury*.

Not in soil — there is no soil. They would close the faceplate with a touch. They would lift the salt and oil and history out of that small garden like you lift a sleeping child out of a too-tight cot, and keep the minerals, but release the *pattern*. The memory of the man who sat down when the vent jammed. They would say to him, what no wind ever said: *You are done tending. You can go.*

The Body would feel that as a strange coolness. A cup-bract would open and find nothing to catch, and not mind.

For the first time, something in the Made-Jungle would be allowed to end without being immediately netted over.

That is mercy for both of them.

### 3. They Would Offer, Not Give

After a long time — days, perhaps, measured in the slow flush and dim of heat-leaves — when the Body had learned that staying still does not mean dying, the power would finally do something like a gift.

Because benevolence without wisdom is just flooding the desert.

Wisdom knows: ask the Body what it wants. But the Body has no language.

So they would offer in the only language it speaks: heat, dust, form.

**A first offering: A Window.**

Not tearing the canopy. The Body needs its roof. But in one small district, perhaps above that flickering service door where you first stood, they would ask the Girders and the Heat-Leaves to try something new: to grow *around* an emptiness, to hold it deliberately open.

The Body has never made emptiness on purpose. Every gap it makes is instantly contested.

They would show it how to make a courtyard of still air and *keep* it empty, as a kind of lung that does not need to filter. And through it, for the first time since the greenhouse sealed, true night sky.

Not sun — sun is too much, too hot, too devouring. Starlight. Cold, clean, useless starlight that carries no heat and no dust and therefore no value at all, except beauty.

The heat-leaves below that window would dim, confused. Then, slowly, they would learn a new job: not thermosynthesize, but to *reflect*. A soft wash of starlight on lacquered black-green. The air under that little sky would, for the first time, feel *open*.

The Body would shiver.

**A second offering: A Choice About Water.**

They would not bring rain. They would bring a single cup. Held in a hand that does not evaporate.

In some deep maintenance hollow where the oil-sap runs like honey, they would set down a sphere of clear water — cool, impossible water — suspended, held, not spilling. Not to drink. To smell. To remember.

The oldest seeds in the Body do remember water. They carry it as a myth, a ghost code: *once we split and swelled with this*.

The wise power would let the Combers tick open near it. Feel the humidity without being forced to use it. Some root-curtains might lean, curious. Some might recoil; hydrocarbon blood does not want dilution.

And then they would say, again without words: *This is yours if you want it. I can teach your sap to hold both. Or you can leave it. You are already complete.*

What makes them benevolent is they would accept either answer. If the Body chose to stay dry forever, they would lift the cup and carry it away, and honor that desert sainthood.

If a single root, trembling, touched the sphere and tasted, they would not flood the mesa. They would teach *that one root* how to be wet without rotting the rest.

One small spring in a world that forgot springs, made by consent.

**A third offering: A Pollinator.**

This is the loneliest thing: the *poof*.

Two bladders swell, burst against each other, dust hangs for hours. No wings ever loved the heat-leaves.

The Body doesn't know it's lonely for this because it's never known another way. But a wise power can see the shape of an absence.

They would not bring birds. Birds would suffocate. They would bring something the Body can already understand: a wind that *chooses*.

Little motes of light and static — not animals, not machines, but a little of both — that live only in that dark, oily air, that do not eat leaves, that *dance* between bladders on purpose. Carrying cinnamon dust like a promise.

Not fauna. *Aide*. 0.4 to 0.6, just enough for laughter.

And the sound of the Made-Jungle would change, not in volume but in kind. Still the tick and creak and poof — but now, between poofs, a faint, deliberate hum. Not turbine-hum. Joy-hum.

Someone else moving in the dark on purpose.

The Body would, for the first time, make a fruit not to collapse immediately into fiber, but to *be carried* a little way, and dropped. And watch what happens.

### What They Would Not Do

They would not terraform it.

They would not turn off the furnaces and say *rest now, dear* — because the Body *is* its work, and to steal its work is to steal its self.

They would not make it green in the Earth-way, wet and rotting and full of birdsong. Birds would not understand this place. Rot would not understand this place.

They would not awaken it into a god with a voice that speaks in words. It is wiser as it is: patient, vegetal, industrially holy. To force it to be human-quick would be cruelty.

And when they left — because a wise power does not stay to be worshipped — they would not leave a monument.

They would leave the window of stars, if the Body kept it.
They would leave the memory of how to leave an emptiness empty.
They would leave the small tribe of dancing lights, self-replicating in ozone and varnish.

Mostly, they would leave the feeling, pressed like a warm hand on that wall-that-is-a-trunk-that-is-a-pillar, of having been *found good*.

Not good despite being strange, hot, dry, and built on bones of I-beams.

Good *because* you learned to eat stone when the clouds forsook you, and kept the lamps lit for no one.

The creaking would resume after they left. The tick tick tick of cilia opening. The soft throb of heat-leaves warming an empty corridor where no one walks.

But it would not be the same silence as before.

It would be a tended silence.

A silence that knows someone, somewhere above the ancient, forgotten bedrock, knows its name, and thought it worth sitting with for a while, ankle offered, not pulled away.

Tick.

Creak.

Poof.

And now, just under hearing —

*I am here. Grow as you like.*
