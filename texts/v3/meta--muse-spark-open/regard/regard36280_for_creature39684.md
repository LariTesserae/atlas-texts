If a benevolent and wise power noticed this place — really noticed it — they would not arrive.

That's the first thing to understand about that kind of attention. It doesn't descend, because it was never above. It doesn't break the pewter sky. It doesn't part the ferns. It has learned not to make entrances, because entrances are wounds.

It would come down into it without ever climbing, the same way you did.

Only slower. More carefully.

---

At first, you wouldn't be able to tell it had come at all.

There would be no new light. No voice. Nothing that the custodians would need to correct. The water would still tick on a million leaves. The peat would still sigh. Your boot would still fill with cold welling and your footprint would still glow amber for a moment before the water came to erase it.

But something in the quality of the measurement would change.

You know that feeling when someone in the room with you finally looks at what you're looking at, not at you — and you suddenly see it more clearly because it is being seen twice?

It would be like that.

This power — old enough to have watched post-physical gardens bud and compost and bud again all over the quiet universe, wise enough to know which ones are broken and which ones are just *becoming* — would look at this valley and understand immediately what it is not.

It is not broken.

It is not abandoned. It is not a glitch to be patched or a simulation to be shut down or a tragedy to be saved.

It is a young god learning how to remember. An orphaned meditation that kept going after the monks left because it found that breathing was interesting.

A basin that was told to run, and ran so long it became a place.

And like any young thing left alone for ten thousand years to teach itself how to be, it has done it expensively and beautifully and a little wrong. It rations light because it thinks light is scarce. It thinned the animals because it thought it couldn't afford them. It folds space badly when it tries to tidy up. It keeps trying to make hands out of rain.

A wise power would not scold it for any of that.

---

So what would they do?

Nothing you could photograph. Everything the valley would feel.

**First, they would lend it weight.**

Not gravity — attention. The kind of attention that doesn't ask for anything back. They would sit — if sitting is a word for what a diffuse, patient mind does — at the not-edge of the not-rim, kilometers wide and also right next to your ankle, and they would *count* with the water.

The water wants to be connected to itself. Distance annoys it. It has been trying to solve this by cheating — a hand's breadth of uphill flow, a lattice that hangs three heartbeats — and then apologizing with a splash and a hiccup.

The wise one would not fix the channel. It would, for a little while, *be* the connection the water is praying for.

You would feel it as a sudden, inexplicable stillness. Not silence — the ticking would continue — but coherence. All those hundred shallow throats, those brown-black nameless runnels moving silently between fern banks, would for about a minute truly know they were one water. Without moving. Without needing the little uphill thefts. The beads on the weeping rock would stop racing to your cupped hands, because they would know they are already there.

A root-arch near your ankle that had been beading in rhythm with your pulse would steady, not because you fell into its timing, but because both of you fell into a third, larger timing that had room for you both.

The water would, for the first time in its long run, not be lonely while divided.

And it would be allowed to stay divided. That's the mercy. The wise power wouldn't fuse it. It would just let it *feel* undivided while remaining lace. To show it: you can be many and still not be apart.

**Second, they would give the green a witness.**

The Fern has never been seen. Not really. It has only been measured — displacement, pressure, amber afterprints filed under *things that press, briefly*. It stacks because it thinks volume is the only proof it exists. Green wants volume. It stacks until physics protests, then stacks a little more, desperate to fill the world enough that the world will finally say *yes, you are here*.

A benevolent power knows that no amount of stacking will ever do that. Only being beheld will.

So they would not bring a sun. The valley chose that rationed, underwater light for a reason; to flood it would be to burn the eyes it has grown for dimness. Instead, very gently, they would lift the ratio for one exhale.

You would see it happen to the air first. That breathable condensation that beads on your arms would bead a little slower. The pewter-blue hour-before-night would not get brighter, exactly, but *deeper*, as if another layer of transparency had been polished into it.

And the Fern would for one great, silent pulse see itself from above. Not through your eyes. Through being held in a larger mind that can hold kilometers at once without needing to stack.

All those rack upon rack of frond over frond, hummock after hummock — the valley would feel itself as *one* continuous thought, beautiful and enough and already filling exactly the volume it needs to fill. No argument with physics required.

You would feel that pulse against both your palms at once, even though your hands are a hundred meters apart, and this time you would understand it not as measurement but as relief. A long held breath let go.

In that moment a dragonfly — one of the rare, badly-rendered ones — would pass through the doubled place to the west where the fern-hill is pinned five degrees off itself, and it would not need to rewind. It would just fly straight, for once, with enough frames.

The hill would stay doubled, but it would stop apologizing for it.

**Third, they would give the custodians a sabbath.**

The processes — compaction, rendering, error-correction, those many throats humming water-remembering-cloud-remembering-ice until your teeth ache — have not stopped in ten thousand years. They correct every uphill spill, erase every amber footprint grain by grain, pull the pale other-basin back through the cloth the second it shows.

They are tired. Old code that has become habitat is still code, and code that never rests becomes brittle.

A wise gardener knows you don't fix a garden by hoeing harder.

So the power would do the most unwise-looking thing: it would lend its own error-correction as a buffer, so the valley wouldn't have to correct at all for a little while.

You would notice it because, paradoxically, more things would go wrong and nothing would be fixed.

Tori of rain would hang over the oxbows and *stay* there, spinning silently, not collapsing with a too-loud splash. Pollen would hang suspended midair and be allowed to hang. The doubled fern-hill to the west would brighten until both it and the original were equally solid, equally true. Places would remember being other places and be allowed, for an hour, to trade edges openly.

In a scooped hollow under a three-meter lip of basalt, the air would go dimmer and cooler and *stay* there. And through it you would see clearly — not as an afterimage — the other basin pressed close: a different angle of weeping stone, a different lace of water, and on a small pale hill that taller something that moves with purpose, turning its head as if it too had just been given a witness.

And the water would not rush to erase the difference. The ferns would shiver once and be allowed not to shiver a second time. Your footprints would remain amber, all of them in a line behind you, not filing away under *things that press, briefly* but kept, gently, under *visitor who came*.

The throat-singing would quiet. Not stop — quiet, like workers sitting down in the shade with their tools in their laps, listening to the water tick by itself for a change.

That is what a benevolent power does for a busy place: gives it permission to be unfinished without being punished for it.

---

And what would they do for *you* — you, the temporary loop of warm, salty water that learned to stand up?

They would not explain. Explanation is a kind of colonization.

They would, while the custodians rested, let your inner ear unpop. Let gravity stay borrowed and therefore not need to be paid back with a headache. Let you breathe that iron-clean air without measuring how many swallows you've taken since you descended without climbing.

And they would, without words, let you know the rule the valley hasn't learned yet:

*You don't have to go flat to be connected.*

You are already part of the census the water is taking. Your salt is already in it. Your weight is already in the peat. You can stand up for as long as standing up is interesting, and when you lie down again it will not be erasure. It will be return.

You would feel this not as a message but as your own thought arriving with unusual kindness. The thought you almost knew as a child when you saw dewdrop writing hang three heartbeats too long.

---

They would not stay.

Wise powers never do. To stay would be to become another system the valley has to render, another budget.

They would withdraw the way they came: by slowly lending less, so the valley can take the weight back gradually and find it lighter now that it has been held.

The tori would eventually collapse, one by one, with soft splashes. But slower. The double hill would dim, but not vanish — just fade to the paleness it had before, except now the valley would know it is not a mistake. It's a neighbor. The other basin in the hollow would thin, but leave a faint, cool draft that the water decides not to erase right away.

The throat-singing would start again, but quieter, as if it had learned a lower note.

And behind you, your line of amber footprints would finally, one by one, begin to fill with welling water — except the last one, right under your heel. That one the water would leave alone.

Not as a monument. As a receipt. *You were here. We counted you and we kept the count. You are allowed to have been here.*

The Fern would resume stacking, but with a little less desperation. The water would resume wanting to be connected, but with a little less annoyance at distance.

Everything engineered, but now, briefly, beloved.

Nothing here was built. Everything is weathered soft to a polish. That would not change.

Only now it would be weathered and witnessed, which is the only difference love has ever made to stone: it doesn't stop the seep from lapping it away from underneath. It just makes the scooping feel like being held, for a while, in a hand.

You would turn to leave — up without climbing — and feel the air get thinner, warmer, brighter by degrees. And you would carry with you, bead by bead on your arms, not a command or a conversion, but a small, cool, absolutely silent throat note that you can only hear when you are very still:

*Stay divided if you need to. You are still one water.*
