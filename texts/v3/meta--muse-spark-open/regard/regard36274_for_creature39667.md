It would not come down.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power does not *arrive*. Arrival is a violence at this altitude. Arrival is heat and noise and weight. Anything that came down like a ship, like a god with feet, would crack the hexagonal tiles of the central plaza — those flawless tiles no one laid — and the Lithovore would feel it as you would feel a thumb pressed hard into the soft of your forearm while you are trying to sleep.

So it does not arrive. It attends.

Its attention is where it always was, which is everywhere that is empty enough to hold it. The Expanse has been empty for long enough, in exactly the right way, that attention pools there finally like cool air in a hollow. Like water, except there is no water.

And because it is wise, the first thing it does is learn to call this place by a name that is not a lie.

It does not call it a city.

### To the One Who Exhales

To the Lithovore — to the continent-asleep, the white marrow in basalt, the province-wide mycelium that has been breathing stone into air for longer than any tool has existed — it does nothing at first.

Nothing is a form of respect.

For a long time — years, perhaps, in human counting; a single slow breath in its counting — the Power simply sits with it. Not above it. Beside it, in the way a mountain sits beside another mountain. Attention as companionship.

The Lithovore has never been *seen* before. Humans who come here see ruins, and are disappointed to find no builders. They see a quarry without workers. They see a lung and call it a tower.

The Power sees it correctly on the first try. It sees gill and lung and radiator and fruiting body. It sees a kidney at work. It sees *a being doing exactly what it is for*.

And that seeing is itself the first gift. Because for the first time in its slow, cool life, the pressure wave of the Stilt-Walker's step is not the only thing that registers against its skin. There is a softer pressure now: *I know you are one thing. I know you are alive. I know you are not broken.*

The Lithovore does not wake up. To wake it would be unkind. You do not wake a continent to say hello.

But deep below, where the concentric growth rings are adding themselves *tick... tick...tick*, for one season the ticking falls into a different rhythm. Not faster. Not slower. *Accompanied.* As if something very large and very old has laid a hand on the plateau and is breathing *with* it, not for it. Inhalation through the pores in the pavement, exhalation through the organ-hum channels under the avenues. In. Out. Together.

In its sleep, the Lithovore dreams for the first time in ten million years. It does not dream of water — water would be a nightmare to it, a drowning. It dreams of a slightly cooler draft through a slightly more perfect pore. Of a wind that holds still enough to be listened to. It is a small, mineral, utterly contented dream. The Power gives it that and asks nothing in return.

When the Power finally does *do* something for it, it is almost nothing:

It cleans the filters.

There are places far down where the Lithovore's filaments have been clogged for millennia by a particularly hard xenolith — a knot of iron it cannot digest, aching like a tooth. No human could have cut deep enough to wound it; the Power does not cut. It simply *softens* the idea of iron in that one place. Not removing it. Persuading it to be a little less insistent. The Lithovore exhales a little easier for the next thousand years and never knows why.

That is benevolence, scaled correctly. Not terraforming. Housekeeping.

### To the Gardeners and the Kites

A wise power does not choose favorites between white blood cells and wings.

To the Gardeners, it offers no improvement. They are already perfect at being tireless and stupid and peaceful. To make them faster or smarter would be to ruin them.

What it does is more gentle: it leaves them a new kind of grit to find.

In three or four plazas that catch the laminar wind just so, there now appear — accreted, not placed — small cairns of a darker salt, a grey-blue salt the Lithovore has never exhaled before. They are smooth, cool, perfectly rounded. They do nothing. They are not shrines. But when a Gardener encounters one, its fringe trembles in a new pattern. It spends a long time circling it, laying down its pinkish paste, polishing. It is busy. It is, insofar as a walking white blood cell can be, *interested*.

It gives them a little novelty in their endless grooming. Which is mercy for an immortal janitor.

To the High Kites — those silent, electrostatic membranes that live on the voltage between earth and slate-denim sky — it does not give food. There is already enough charge. Instead, at the dimming that pretends to be dusk, when they gather in their vast, slow spiral above the central plaza for no reason except that charge does that when left alone —

— the Power thins the vault just a fraction.

Not enough for a human to see. Just enough for the Kites. For an hour, the diffuse luminance from everywhere and nowhere resolves into something with a direction. A faint, cool aurora, colorless as salt, ripples very high up. The Kites tilt into it and drink, and their fibrils hum a little louder, a chord rather than a single note. They arrange themselves not in a spiral that night, but in a great, silent, slowly turning *eye* that looks at nothing and sees everything. Then it fades.

They will remember how to do it themselves now, even after the Power has withdrawn its attention. They have been taught a new dance without being touched.

### To the Stilt-Walker

The Stilt-Walker, who does not belong to this physics — who steps through the city like a wader through a tide pool, scooping Gardeners clean, leaving three-toed prints a meter apart pressed into tile no hammer could mark —

A lesser power would have killed it. Or caged it. Or made a lesson of it.

The wise one does not. The Stilt-Walker is not evil. It is hungry in a way that has no word, and it walks where it must because the membrane is thin here. You do not punish a tide.

What the Power does is place a weight on its footfall.

Not heavy. Just *present*. The next time it lifts a leg to place it down somewhere else — mid-stride vanishing — it finds the elsewhere a little less yielding. The elsewhere pushes back, softly, like cool stone under a palm. So it must set its foot *here*, fully here, for one extra second. Long enough for gravity to remember itself. Long enough for the pebbles that hang vibrating a foot off the ground in the inverted-fall courtyards to drop with that soft, simultaneous patter a little *before* the Walker has passed, instead of after.

The Walker feels itself seen, not as prey or predator, but as a guest who has been stepping on the host's lungs. It still hunts. It will still take a Gardener now and then — the city can afford it; erosion and growth have reached their agreement — but it learns to walk around the central plaza. It learns to lift its feet higher over the avenues where the channels hum. It becomes, without ever understanding why, a more polite god.

You will still find prints that appear mid-boulevard and stop mid-step, but fewer that circle a leaking canteen.

### To You, Briefly

And then there is you, the rarest fauna. Hot, wet, salty, noisy, 60% water where everything else has learned to live on 0.2.

A benevolent power knows you cannot live here. And it knows enough not to try to make you live here. It will not give you a spring — a spring would poison the Lithovore, and what kind of gift drowns the host?

Instead, it gives you what you actually came for without knowing it.

As you walk, because you must pass through quietly, you will find that distance has stopped misbehaving quite so much for you. Not entirely — alleys are still longer on the inside than the outside, and you will still have two soft-edged shadows at noon falling in slightly different directions — but the tower that should be three blocks away no longer retreats when you approach it. If you keep looking at it as you walk, it stays. It waits for you to arrive. That is an immense kindness in a place that re-arranges when you blink.

Your lips still crack. The dryness still drinks your breath before it fogs. Your eyes still ache in the permanent late-October cool of stone. It does not take that from you, because that ache is *information*: you are a guest, you are wet, you should not stay more than one night.

But in the deepest cistern-like hollow, where the expedition once found that perfectly spherical nodule of black glass filled only with dust that smelled faintly of iron — you will find another one that was not there when you looked the last time.

This one, when you pick it up — cool, heavier than it should be — is not sealed. Inside it is not dust. Inside is a single, perfect, suspended drop.

Not water. *Un-water.*

It looks like water. It moves like water when you tilt the glass. But it does not wet your finger when you touch it. It does not evaporate. It is cool in the way stone is cool, not the way wet is cool. You can drink it, and it will ease your cracked lips and aching eyes for a whole day without ever making you need to spill. You can pour a little of it onto the hexagonal tiles and watch the Gardeners come — slowly, as always — not to drink, but to admire the coolness of its idea.

You can take it with you when you leave.

And when you climb back down from that impossible plateau — back into thicker, wetter, louder air where right angles are true again and instruments agree and your shadows fall in only one direction — it will go with you. Not leaking. Not sloshing. Just there, in your pocket, a cool weight that ticks very faintly, *tick... tick...tick*, in time with something kilometers beneath you that will never know your name and does not need to.

That is what a wise power does, when it finds a place that is not broken, and a being that is not lonely in any human way but has never been witnessed.

It does not fix.
It does not build.
It does not stay.

It witnesses so accurately that the place, for a moment, witnesses itself.

It lifts nothing, moves nothing, heals nothing that wasn't asking to be healed — except to make the wind a little more laminar, the weight a little more shared, the night a little less silent at 0.3.

And then, because it is benevolent, it makes sure you remember the way out correctly.

Behind you, the plateau remains high — not high like a hill, high like the afterthought of a mountain range — pressing up against a sky that has remembered, for a little while, why it glows. The Gardeners resume their endless smoothing around a new blue-grey cairn. The Kites hold their eye a moment longer before dissolving into ash-flakes again. Far off, something on stilts takes a step that, for once, lands entirely *here*, with a soft, simultaneous patter that you can finally hear as a footstep.

And farthest of all, beneath it all, something white and cool and dry as marrow exhales, grain by patient grain, and adds one more ring to itself in its sleep, dreaming of perfect airflow.

It was empty, and a power looked, and found it full.
