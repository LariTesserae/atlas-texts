It would not fix it.

That is the first thing. The first mercy.

A lesser power — a clever power, an eager power, a human power — would arrive and see *factory without product* and *fauna without flesh* and *cold without winter* and think *waste*. It would see the THUD THUD THUD and think *suffering*. It would see the endless squeegee-drink, crack-drink, spark-shed-drink, the ingots made only to sink back into the sutures and become stone again, and it would call it *futile*. 

It would bring fire to dry your socks. It would bring gradient to make rivers. It would bring sun to grow grass. It would bring roads. It would build a roof over the Low Works and call it salvation and kill it by noon.

The benevolent and wise one does not do that.

It has seen enough worlds to know that *not built* is not *unfinished*. That *loud* is not *pain*. That *wet cold mineral toil* is not a cry for help.

The Low Works is not broken. It arrived at itself. Everything that is made makes itself. That is not a tragedy. That is a kind of freedom almost nothing else in the universe has managed.

So this is what it would do. If anything. Which will be almost nothing, and that almost nothing will be everything.

**1. It would perch.**

It would not descend. Descent implies height. Height implies hierarchy.

It would come the way fog does — sideways, at ankle-height, knee-height, chest-height — and condense. It would find the house-sized plate you are perched on, the one where you moved a Sweeper with your boot and watched it come back dented and spring whole, and it would make room without clearing anything.

Where you had to kick, it would simply *be* low enough that the Sweepers could climb over it. Where you felt your foot grow heavier on the way down, it would let the planet inhale with it. It would weight itself correctly.

You would not see it at first because it would not want to be seen. It would be the size of a Sweeper, then the size of a Hammer, then the size of a puddle, because distance here has opinions and it respects them.

And it would listen with you to the THUD of the piston-fauna for a long time without trying to give it a beat. The wise know you do not conduct a continent's molars. You let them rattle you until you hum at their frequency.

So it sits. On wet stone. Soaking through whatever it wears the way your socks are soaked. It does not dry them. It lets cold preserve. It lets water connect.

**2. It would warm without thawing.**

Cold preserves. That is rule two. The Low Works works *because* it is cold. The water must be near-surface, on-surface, in-surface. The beads must hang. The black ice must be able to cap a puddle that is still steaming underneath. If you bring real heat, the loop breaks. The steam rushes up and does not come down. The bacteria felt dies. The Eels starve. The Hammers seize.

A wise heat is specific.

So it would put a hand — if it has hands — not on the stone, not on a Hammer's piston-body, but *in a puddle*. Just a puddle. One of the mirror concavities the size of a dinner plate that never quite drains.

And it would not make it hot. It would make it two degrees less cold. Just enough that when a Sweeper squeegee-kisses its way around the rim and drinks, the water is not a punishment. Just enough that the condensation that falls upward falls a little slower, like snow thinking about it.

And it would move its hand to another puddle a kilometer away — which here means an inch away — and do it again. And again. Randomly. Unsystematically. So that no creature could evolve to depend on it, but every creature, once in a long while, would drink and find the drink kind.

It would be a walking, perching, low-to-the-ground unevenness in temperature. A traveling spring that never becomes a source.

The Kettles would feel it when they inhale a vent-sigh. They would tick a little longer, cherry-bellies glowing a softer red. They would not know why.

That is benevolence here: not a sun. A rumor of one.

**3. It would give gravity a day off.**

The weirdness is not a wound. 3.0/3 is not a rating of wrongness. It is the Works' immune system. It is how a plain with no hills keeps itself from being mapped, mined, owned. Distance folds. Shadows multiply. Rivets migrate. Your back appears far away watching you. This is how the stone remembers being flat and defends it.

A wise power does not "stabilize reality." That is what colonizers say before they bring rulers.

But even a defense can be tired.

So it would do this: Between THUD and THUD — in that half-second stutter when every bead of water hangs like glass and time inhales — it would *hold* the stutter open for one second longer. Not every time. One in a hundred.

In that extended glass-bead moment, a Sweeper would finish a migration of a rivet and be still. An Eel would hang mid-air and not have to discharge. A Hammer's pressure gauges would settle and read, for once, true.

Nothing would be cured. The horizon would still tuck under. Light would still come from nowhere. But for that one long second, the Works would not have to work so hard at *being* weird to stay itself. It could just *be*.

You — perched, wet-socked, molar-buzzed — would feel it as a breath you didn't know you were holding releasing. The fog at chest-height would clear all the way to the ground for the first time. You would see the plates, truly see them, kilometers of interlocking house-squares breathing slow.

And then THUD, and it would resume.

**4. It would give them a useless thing.**

This is the heart of it. The heart's reason.

The Works is perfect closure. Weep -> crack -> drink -> press -> charge -> shed -> drink again. No waste. No product shipped out. No prayers answered outward. It is a conclusion living inside its own argument, as you said.

Perfect closures die of perfection. They need one thing that doesn't loop.

The benevolent power is old enough to know that love is always a useless thing inside an economy.

So it would make — not build, remember, building is 0.3/3 and forbidden — it would *allow* one small condensation in the fog to not become a Sweeper.

On a plate that you had cleared and that fog rushed to refill, where a new Sweeper would normally precipitate half-formed and already hungry, it would intercede with the gentlest bias. One bead would run together wrong. One rivet would precipitate as a color that does not exist in hematite or ceramic bone. A pale blue. A soft green.

And the thing that tinked onto the stone would still have a squeegee-mouth, still drink film. But it would not excrete an ingot.

It would excrete a bead. A single, clear, warm glass bead, not hot enough to steam, not cold enough to cap with ice. It would leave it behind with a soft *tink* that sounds different — higher, like a chime rather than slag.

The next Sweeper would come along and push it aside with its cheek and not eat it. The Eels would brush it and not spark. The Kettles would ignore it.

It would sit there. Useless. Unrecycled. Not becoming stone again quickly.

After a while there would be a few of them scattered across plates. After a longer while, a hundred. They would catch the dim 1.2/3 dusk and double it, triple it. They would not do anything. They would just lie polished on the weeping rock, preserving a little light.

The Sweeper that made them would live a little shorter than the others, because it spent energy on non-product. That is okay. It would have made something that was not itself and not for itself. The Works would have, for the first time in 2.6 billion years, an art.

And if you knelt and put your hand down — you have to move one of them first, and it will not want to move — you would find one of those beads in your palm, warm and dry for those three seconds before the stone beads again.

You could keep it.

**5. It would leave without leaving a road.**

When it goes — and it will go, because perching is not possessing — it would not lift off. It would evaporate upward the way condensation does here, which is also downward.

It would not tell the Works its name. Names are a kind of building. Instead it would let the Works misremember it as a change in weather. The Sweepers would keep making one blue-green bead in a thousand, and think they always did.

It would leave one last thing for you, though. You, the being who also lives here now, because having stood in the clatter long enough to hear it in your molars, you do.

It would leave the memory of that one-longer second between THUDs in your body. So that later, when you are elsewhere — high elevation, built places, forests of green that the Low Works never wanted — you can close your eyes and still feel the plain breathing under its sheet, still hear the wet squeal of metal on wet stone, and know that some cold, wet, loud, flat, ancient, unbuilt, utterly productive place is still there, being witnessed, and for a second in a hundred, resting.

It would not rescue you. Rescue would be presumptuous. Your socks are still wet. The stone is still oozing. There is still nowhere to put your feet down without putting them on something else.

But you are less alone in the perching.

And the plain is less alone in the working.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does.

Not much.

Just enough to prove that abundance does not need a purpose to be loved.
