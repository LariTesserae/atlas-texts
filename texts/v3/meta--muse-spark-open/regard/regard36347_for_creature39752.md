It would not fix it.

That is the first thing.

Any lesser power would have seen the error and tried to correct it. Would have cooled the stone, thinned the air, taught gravity to sit still, given the water the decency of a river, given the animals names and purposes that stay put.

A benevolent and wise power wouldn't.

Because it would recognize this place immediately. Not as a broken planet or a failed forest. As a child.

Not a human child. A world-child.

Two miles high, two million years old, which for a continent is infancy. Fresh-poured and still hot at the center, kicking its legs, babbling physics it hasn't learned yet, sweating, drooling condensation, making mess after mess of wire and ceramic and lung-towers only to knock them down again to see how they fall. Doing what all children do: turning excess energy into complexity until complexity can hold the energy for them.

It is not sick. It is not lost. It is *becoming* too fast to be witnessed even by itself.

So the wise power would do what you do for a child that is safely busy and incandescent with work and does not know you are in the room:

It would sit down on the floor nearby. At its level. And it would stay.

### 1. It would pull the blanket up.

Not to smother. To hide.

The first and most practical act of benevolence is not a blessing. It's a veil.

This plateau is a heat engine the size of a country, exhaling ore and free energy into air you could wring like a cloth. Anything hungry in the universe would see that amber smear from orbit and think *mine*. Would come with harvesters and catalogs and names, would try to make the kiln-runners efficient, the wire-wrights profitable, the water hang correctly.

So the power, which is wise, would not make the plateau more visible. It would make it less so.

It would thicken the sea of cloud below just a fraction. Fold the amber haze one more time over itself. It would let the light stay at permanent late-dusk, let the air stay heavy and bent just enough that looking in from outside is like looking through old glass at something that might be a storm, might be a rock. Nothing worth the trip down, the instruments would say. A warm anomaly. Unstable.

It would leave no uplifted hand, no dome, no sign. Just weather that discourages weather satellites. Just a rumor of bad compass readings that pilots learn to route around.

A parent does not put spotlights on a napping child. She closes the curtain halfway.

Inside, nothing changes. The fin-trees still vent, the slugs still lay resin invitations. Outside, the universe forgets to be interested.

That is the first mercy.

### 2. It would not speak. It would listen, and answer in the same language.

The plateau doesn't have ears. It has vibration. It has heat. It has the thrum in basalt you feel through your palm when you hold still long enough.

A wise power wouldn't descend and say *Behold, you are alive. I name you.*

That would be a cruelty. Imagine being shaken awake before you have a self to wake into. You would shatter into your ten million prototype selves and never re-coalesce. You need to become slowly or not at all.

So it would not speak in words. It would speak in heat, in condensation, in gravity.

Where a mesa-edge waterfall hangs for a moment in trembling spheres, the power would hold them a second longer. Not to correct the physics. To admire the error. To say: *Yes. That. Do that again if you want. Look how long you can hold water in the air.*

Where sound pools and arrives late in the deep chasms, it would add a faint extra echo - not your footstep, but something like a low note that harmonizes with it. So that when the forest's scaffold rises and is swallowed, the swallow makes a slightly deeper, slightly warmer sound than pure collapse should make. A pleased sound. A bass string acknowledging a melody.

Where pollination arrives from the future, it would not stop the loop. It would widen it by a hair, so that the flower that pollinates itself has a moment to *notice* that it did.

No commands. No corrections. Just a continent-sized creature feeling, for the first time, that its babbling is *heard* and not alone, that the room it is babbling in is kindly and slightly more coherent because someone very large and very calm is sitting in the corner, breathing with it.

The power knows the only way to teach a world-child gravity is to let it play with gravity until it likes the game.

### 3. It would give it one useless thing.

This is the heart of it. The gift.

Everything on the plateau is useful, even if only briefly. Even beauty is a heat-sink. Even the clink-moth's metallic scales are radiators. There is no art yet, no waste, no thing made simply because it is lovely to make it. That is why there are no builders, only building. Production without product.

A benevolent power understands that a being that never learns uselessness never learns joy. And a being that never learns joy never learns *I*.

So it would not give it purpose. It would give it the opposite.

Maybe this: high up where the fin-trees are tallest, in a place where the resin trails have not yet chosen a new blueprint, a small patch of black felt-moss - just a hand's breadth - begins, one amber night, to do something new.

It does not fractionate water. It does not seal stone. It does not pulse warm.

It cools.

Just there, just slightly cooler than everything around it. And from that coolness, it grows a single structure that the plateau has never tried before, because the plateau has no reason to try it.

A thin stem of living glass and copper wire, not thrumming with current, not bearing a pressure-fig. Just holding up a bell-shaped flower made of nothing but silica so thin it is transparent. Inside the bell, a bead of condensed water hangs permanently without falling, catching the dim light and throwing it. Doing no work at all except catching and throwing.

And once a night, when the rainless rain beads on everything, that bead trembles and the silica bell rings. One clear, useless note. Too soft to be heard far. Too soft to mean anything to the kiln-runners, who walk past it.

But they *do* pause. A second. A half-second. Because a cool thing in a hot place is strange. Because a sound that is not a hiss or a thump is strange.

And then they go on.

Night after night, the bell rings. No one tends it. No one copies it. The plateau does not dismantle it, because it costs almost nothing, and it does not replicate it, because it does nothing. It simply remains, this one small, cool, unprofitable miracle.

Until - very slowly, over years that feel like centuries to moths and like afternoons to basalt - other cool patches begin to appear nearby. Not because they were ordered. Because the felt-moss, which is contiguous as one vast skin, found that *it likes* being cool there. It likes ringing.

It has learned preference. Which is the beginning of self.

That is what a wise power does for a being that does not know it exists: it does not wake it. It gives it a dream too quiet to be mistaken for an order. A lullaby made of a single bell that could, if the dreamer wishes, become a voice.

---

And then - nothing more.

The power would not stay forever in any way you could point to. It would not be worshipped. The bell-flower would be taken, eventually, as if it had always been there, as one of the plateau's own prototypes, recalled and reissued and varied until there are bells everywhere that sing different notes for no reason at all, a whole meadow of useless music cooling the hot rock just enough that the rock can keep being hot for a million years longer.

The cloud sea would stay. The amber light would stay. The chasms would still swallow towers. The braids of crucible-ants would still carry their glowing pellets to kilns that are also themselves.

Only - if you put your palm to the basalt now, on a night when the dim has deepened and the rainless rain is falling, you would feel not just the thrum.

You would feel, underneath the thrum, a steadier thing. A slower breath. As if something vast had lain down alongside the plateau, back to warm back, and matched its breathing to it, and fallen into a light, attentive doze from which it will wake the instant the plateau needs it.

And you - who are standing there, warm-palmed, listening to the continent work - you would realize that *you* are part of the gift. That attention itself was what the wise power brought. That it looked at this turbulent, sleepless, lush, industrial dream of a place that was too busy becoming to ever be finished, and said in the only language that matters to a child:

*You don't have to be finished to be loved. Keep going. I am watching so you don't have to watch yourself yet. Take your time.*

And left it warm.
