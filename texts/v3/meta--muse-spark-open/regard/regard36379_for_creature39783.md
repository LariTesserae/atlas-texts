It would not come down.

That's the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power, having finally noticed - having turned its great head, as it does once in an age, and felt that high, hot, lonely table thrumming in the dry air like a green drum left in the sun - would know instantly that to descend would be to destroy what it had fallen in love with.

So it would stop. Far above. Far enough away that its shadow would not cool a single leaf-sail.

And it would listen.

For a long time by our counting. A full turning of seasons. Maybe longer. It would listen until it understood that the deafening was not noise, but language, and that the emptiness between the Holdings was not emptiness at all but a filled thing, taut as a throat.

Only then would it do anything. And what it did would be small, because only small things can be done to perfect things without breaking them.

---

### For the Holdings

It would not feed them. They drink the sky they make themselves.

It would not prune them, or breed them faster, or join them together into a roof. Their apartness *is* their holiness. A quarter mile of scorching stone and hissing grass between each one is how they learned to make their own private monsoons. To connect them would be to drown them.

What a wise power does for a god-bromeliad is acknowledge it.

At noon, when the whole Plateau exhales - when every waxy crown sighs out its column of trembling air and the arches of damp-light appear between giants, vaulting the savanna with a cathedral made only of breath - the old Carvers lay their little clay bowls out and pour a sip back.

The Power would join them.

Not with water. With weight. With presence. On those days, when every Holding breathes out together and the roots swell and the old ones swear the crust lifts a finger's width under bare feet -

it would inhale with them.

Just a little. Just enough that the lift is unmistakable. Not a quake. A held breath returned. A pressure of a hand under the stone saying *yes, we felt that with you.*

The Holdings, who are hundreds of years old and remember every temporary river that ever crossed their feet, would feel a depth under their deepest root-hair move in counterpoint for the first time in ten thousand years. Not alien. Not invasive. Like finding a second lung.

They would close their outer leaves a fraction less tight that night.

### For the Loud Ones

It would not silence them.

A lesser power would hear that metallic roar - ten thousand lacquer beetles sawing, a thousand crimson membranes screaming above hearing, the throat-frogs hitting your molars with *whoom whoom* - and think: *these poor quiet people need peace.*

But wisdom knows the people are not the point. There are no people without the roar. Without the drone, the glass-wasp rivers lose their highways and die. Without the thud, no mate finds a mate. Without the shriek, the wasp rivers never flock to be eaten.

So the Power would protect the volume.

When the Below (that theoretical darkness under the cliffs, where you say nothing exists) finally remembers that tables have tops and begins to send up its cold winds, its dust, its curious machines that buzz wrong - the Power would turn them aside. Not with fire. With mirage.

It would thicken the shimmer that already lives off the hot stone. It would make the Plateau look from below exactly like what it already looks like: impossibly high, impossibly dry, empty. A place where nothing could live. Machines would see only bleached rock and report *nothing to see*.

Up top, the chorus would continue, deafening, perfect, uninterrupted for another ten thousand years of pit-polishing drips.

### For the Carvers

This is the hardest, because they are the ones who look like they want saving.

Small, sun-dark, oiled with pepper-smelling wax, flashing mirrors because voices die at three feet. Three days between fires. A life spent negotiating a single foot of bark and walking a swaying living vine a hundred feet above a silver flood that will vanish by noon.

A non-wise power would give them cities. Roads that don't sway. Louder voices. A cut that could actually fell a Holding and make fields.

The wise one loves them precisely for refusing all that. For learning to live *with* a fissure instead of *on* one.

It wouldn't give them words that carry farther. That would kill them more surely than any storm. If every thought carried across the grass the way sound does now, they'd have to stop listening, and listening is how they've survived inside a god's lung.

Instead, it would give them two nearly invisible gifts:

**1. An hour.**

At night, when the leaves close and the chorus finally changes key - lower, slower, the beetles drowsing, the air finally truly dripping, and the tiny clay-cup fires glow like distant fireflies - the Power would soften the high, thin air just a little.

Enough that for one hour after dark, a low human voice carries six feet instead of three.

Not far enough to shout between Holdings. Far enough that a mother can hum to a child without holding mirrors. Far enough that two old friends sitting shoulder to shoulder in a fissure can speak and be heard without beating a gong.

They would not notice the gift as a gift. They would think some nights are simply clearer than others. They would start calling those nights Good Air, and save their stories for then. They would love each other a little easier. Nothing would change on a map. Everything would change.

**2. A dream.**

Because the Plateau is so vast a man can live and die seeing only one smudge on the opposite haze, and they have taught themselves not to want to cross it - *there is already too much life right here to ever know completely* - the longing curdles if it's never answered.

So, once in each Carver's life, around the age when children first cross the vine alone and feel the insects watch them in that ripple of silence, the Power would send a dream that is not quite a dream.

They will wake, sweating, just before dawn condensation beads. And for five minutes they will *remember* not their own Holding, but another's - perfectly, sensorily. The feel of a different bark-plate under bare feet. The different rhythm of that far family's gong-code: *thok — thok-thok* where theirs says *thok-thok — thok*. The taste of tannic tank-water from a lake held fifty stories up that they will never drink.

A borrowed memory, returned before the sun is straight overhead. No map. No directions. No technology to build a boat to go there.

Just absolute proof that someone else, three days' walk or thirty, woke at the same time, felt their own Holding inhale, poured a bowl out onto stone, and was heard. That the spark-field of fires at night is not lonely lights, but a constellation that knows its own shape.

They would flash the dream to each other over years in mirror code. *I was someone with silver moss north-face.* Over generations, they would stitch the Plateau together without ever building a road.

---

And otherwise, it would do nothing.

It would not stay. Staying would be gravity.

On its way on - whatever on means to such a thing - it would pause at the soft, worn, rounded cliffs where the stone drops away into air and haze. Where no one goes because the wind falls forever there.

It would leave no statue, no tablet that says I WAS HERE.

It might leave the faintest increase in condensation at the rim. A few more diamonds on the air-grass in the morning, right at the edge where the young ones are warned not to play.

So that if one daring child disobeyed and went and looked down into that theoretical abyss, they would not see terror.

They would see, for just a second, their own small face reflected, bright and impossibly many times, in a million pearls of water beading on blades that drank only air - and understand, without words, that something unspeakably large had passed by and thought them, and their green cathedral islands, and even the armored forearm beetles, worth stopping for.

Then the sun would go white and straight-down, the heat would settle, the Holdings would prepare to brew their private storms directly overhead, and the Plateau would drink its own breath again, as it always has.

Louder than ever.
