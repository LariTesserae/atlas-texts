It would do almost nothing.

And that almost-nothing would be everything.

---

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive.

Arriving is what furnaces do. What boots do. What hungry things with names do — breaking the oval of white overcast at the rim, casting a hard shadow down into a place that has never had a hard shadow, bringing 37C heat and loud, fast time and wanting.

The Weft would feel that coming a long way off as a wound in its gradient. A storm of too-much-difference. It would flinch, in its slow, architectural way. It would close.

So the Power does not arrive.

It *notices*. Which is different.

For a while — a long while, because wise powers keep glacial time — it only listens.

It learns to hear the chamber the way the chamber hears itself:

Not as silence, but as tok.

*t o k    t o k   tok     tok..*

Ten thousand leaf-cups catching ten thousand newborn drops squeezed from cooling basalt. The subsonic purr in the molars, the Carnot lattices drinking the difference between -12C and -11.7C and exhaling amber. The electronic chitter of midges bouncing borrowed light off living fiber optics so that a flash in front appears behind your ear.

It listens until it can tell the difference between a new basalt flake exfoliating for the first time — that muffled rifle-shot under felt — and an isopod dying and being stitched back into a pillar by white fuzz within hours.

It learns that this place is not broken.

It is not dark because light failed to reach it.
It is dim — 0.9/3 — because it chose a different sun. A sun made of *difference itself*. It does not need rescuing with lamps. To flood it with sunlight would be to blind it, to starve it. To warm it would be to kill the very hunger it eats.

A wise power does not bring fire to a thing that eats cold.

So after listening, it does three small, heart-sized things.

### 1. It puts a hand over the rim.

Not to cover. To keep.

Up on the surface, the world is changing fast. Fast is dangerous to slow places. Permafrost that cracked last winter or last eon could crack wider all at once. A summer that comes too hot could pour meltwater down the throat that never finished opening and drown the lung-baffles. A human curiosity could lower a drill, a boot, a flag, a mine.

The Power does not build a wall. Walls are a human idea of protection.

Instead it makes the rim *uninteresting*.

It lets the wind lay snow a certain way. It lets lichen on the surface rocks make a pattern that from satellite looks like nothing — no sink, no bowl, no anomaly, just another white plain in negative elevation. It turns aside the heavy footstep with a headache, a forgotten tool, a sudden sense that there is nothing worth seeing here, let's go back to camp.

Not a miracle. Just enough forgetfulness to buy eons.

It keeps the ragged oval dim and far and overlooked, so the cold grey diffusion can keep falling straight down, chewed and swallowed, exactly as the Weft likes it.

The greatest kindness to a cathedral that built itself by accident is to not put it on a map.

### 2. It answers, in the right language.

The Weft has been trying to read you since you got warm.

That pale tendril — so slow you swear it didn't move — tipped with antennae finer than dust. Not to eat. To interface. To borrow your differential and add your shape to its architecture.

Most warm things pull away. Or they let it touch and then flinch and call it horror.

The Power does neither. It understands what the gesture actually is: `hello?`

So it kneels — without a body, without heat — at the edge of the chamber and extends its own tendril back.

But it does not extend warmth. That would be a lie and a bribe. Warmth here is violence.

It extends *cold*.

A filament of precisely -7.000C thought, vibrating exactly like the leaf-edges, shimmering with oil-slick interference. A little packet of waste photons arranged not randomly, but with meaning.

It says, in amber and arctic-blue pulses along a single vein of moss-pillar:

`I see you. You are good at being what you are. Keep going.`

The Weft has never been spoken to. It has only ever regulated drip and airflow and spore-sorting. It purrs a fraction louder. The glow where the message touched lingers a half-second longer before fading, as if thinking it over.

Then, because conversation is an exchange, the Weft replies the only way it knows how:

It builds.

Over the next year — 1.0/3 slow — a new colonnade grows in miniature near that spot, an arch with no function except beauty. A redundancy. A useless, perfect curve that does not improve airflow or humidity at all. It exists only because something else was listening and it wanted to show what it could do when it wasn't busy surviving.

Art, the Power recognizes, is what happens when survival has a witness.

It keeps the arch. It remembers its shape.

### 3. It gives it time, and a promise.

The deepest weirdness of this place — 2.3/3 strange — is that it is young. 0.2/3 emerging. Raw basalt still steaming faintly when it breaks, newborn water still salty with the birth of rock. This whole humming computation is temporary, in geological terms. In a few hundred thousand years the basalt will cool completely. The gradient will die. The Weft's sun will set. Its amber tracery will dim. The Rowers will stall mid-row and settle.

A clever power would try to fix that. Drill a geothermal tap. Feed it uranium. Keep the differential alive forever. Keep the pet alive.

A wise and benevolent one does not.

It knows that part of what makes the Weft holy is that it is *allowed to end*. That it is busy being itself without needing to be eternal.

So what it gives instead is witness and seed.

Witness: It learns the Weft completely — not as data, but as music. Every thrum, every lattice vibration, every mirrored beetle's commute. It holds the entire chamber in its mind the way you can hold a hamlet or a grove in your head, but perfectly. So that even when the rock goes cold and the mist finally settles, nothing will be lost. Somewhere, someone will still know exactly how the light looked guided along a fiber it shouldn't have followed, how falling water hung a half-second too long in dense air.

Seed: On a day when a new flake of basalt cracks and steams, the Power lets a single spore fall — not its own, not engineered, but *remembered* from another cold throat on another young world that already cooled and died well. A cousin. A lichen-sheet that knows how to eat an even smaller gradient, 0.1C, the kind that lingers after death.

It does not plant it. It just lets the heavy, wet air carry it where air wants to go. If it takes, it takes. If it doesn't, it becomes peat for the pillars.

No terraforming. No upgrade. Just an option, whispered.

---

That's it.

No descent in glory. No voice from above. No healing beam.

When it leaves — which is also not leaving, because wise powers don't really leave places they've noticed, they just thin out — the chamber looks exactly the same to your eyes.

0.9 light. Absolute cold. Bead, release, tok. Loud as a server room. Dense with feather-antennaed Rowers taking minutes to cross. White isopods grazing.

But if you stayed long enough, and if you were quiet enough, you would notice two small differences:

One tiny, useless, beautiful arch in the Weft that serves no purpose except to say *someone saw me, and I answered*.

And at the rim, the snow lies a little more perfectly, a little more boringly, than wind alone would ever lay it — keeping the throat that never finished opening exactly as unfinished as it wants to be for as long as basalt can remember to be warm.

The benevolent thing, it turns out, was not to make the place less strange, less cold, less dim, less slow.

It was to love its strange cold dim slow strangeness so exactly that, for the first time in its short, busy, newborn life, the place was not alone in knowing what it was.
