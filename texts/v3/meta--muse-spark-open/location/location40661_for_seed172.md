It is not a valley. It is not a plain. It is a room that thinks it is a mountain.

You find it by accident, which is the only way anyone ever finds it — a service door half-iced shut on the windward spine of the High Array, three thousand meters up where the air has gone thin and mean. Outside, the world is a white desert of basalt and rime, a sea of frozen salt-flat and shattered shield-rock. No rivers. No lakes. Nothing has flowed here in a thousand years except wind and shadow. The water score here is a rumor: whatever moisture was ever in this stone was baked out or blasted off when the range was still young. What little H2O remains is locked up as a hard, dirty glaze in the leeward crevices, more mineral than ice, and it never melts. This is a dry chemistry. You breathe through your teeth and feel your sinuses crackle.

Inside, the door gives with a *crack* and then you are in the pocket.

**The Hidden Scale.**

The whole landscape is one room. That is the entirety of it — an embedded, intimate, hidden pocket-scale environment about the size of a cathedral nave laid on its side, or a subway station without the trains. You can see every wall from where you stand. You can touch the far end if you walk for two minutes. And yet it feels high, because it *is* high: the elevation is in your ears and in your lungs. The Array was built *on* the peak, not in spite of it, and this chamber is its summit hollow, its crown jewel.

It is a young place in geological terms, but not in human ones. The erosion parameter is low because there has been no erosion at all. Everything is sharp. The floor is poured ferrocrete with the trowel-ridges still visible, never worn smooth by feet. The walls are corrugated steel and basalt-composite, fresh from the extruder, their edges so crisp they could cut paper. The ceiling is a vault of interlocking girders, bolt-heads still bright with factory grease that has gone waxy in the permanent cold. No rust. No sag. No cave has ever looked like this — it was never carved, it was *installed*, lowered into the mountain like a jewel into a setting.

And it is totally built. 2.7/3 is not an exaggeration. There is no natural substrate here. Even the "ground" is made. Even the "air" is made. You realize after a minute that the rock you saw outside is cladding; the mountain itself, at least at this altitude, has been replaced, girdled, reinforced, made into a plinth for what it houses. This room is not *inside* the mountain. The mountain is the pedestal for the room.

**Light and Cold.**

The light is normal — that is the strangest thing about it. 2.5/3. Not the dim amber of a bunker, not the theatrical flood of a reactor core. Just flat, even, industrial daylight from long, caged tri-phosphor tubes that run the length of the vault. No flicker. No buzz you can separate from the greater Buzz. They make color possible, which makes the cold worse.

Because it is cold. 0.0/3 cold. Not winter cold. Not storm cold. Deep, absolute, high-altitude, sealed-environment cold that has settled into equilibrium. The thermometers on the wall read -38 and have read -38 for decades. Your breath does not fog so much as shatter. The metal handrails will take skin if you linger. And yet nothing freezes over, because there is nothing to freeze. The air is desert-dry, scrubbed, filtered, non-aqueous. Any spilled water would sublimate before it puddled. The cold is a dry cold, a mineral cold, the cold of a machine left running in a vacuum.

**The Deafening Equilibrium.**

And it is *loud*.

Sound: 2.9/3. Deafening, but not chaotic. This is the key to the dynamic: 0.5/3, never changing. The room is in frozen equilibrium, a time-invariant roar.

Six industrial engines occupy the center of the space — not engines for movement, but engines for *being*. They are squat, black-iron things the size of grain silos tipped on their sides, each one a compressor, a turbine, a heat-pump, a magnet. They run and they have always run at exactly this speed. There is no ramp-up, no cycle, no stutter. Just a single, total, perfectly sustained chord: a low-frequency thrum that you feel in your molars and a high harmonic whine that lives behind your eyes. The air itself vibrates. Dust, if there were any, would levitate in standing waves.

You have to open your mouth to equalize pressure, like on an airplane. You have to shout to hear yourself, and you still won't.

There are no animals here — fauna 0.2, which is to say none. Not even rats. Not even flies. The sound alone would kill them. The dry cold would mummify them. The only ecology is what clings despite those rules.

**Sparse Life in a Made World.**

Vegetation: 1.4/3, sparse. Density: 1.4/3, sparse. There should be nothing green in a sealed, built, deafening engine-room on a frozen peak, and yet — because light is normal and time never changes — life has found the one loophole in the total construction.

In the seams where the ferrocrete floor meets the steel walls, where micro-condensation from human breaths has, over years, left a faint trace of salts, there is a thin, black-green felt of lichen and engineered moss. It does not grow. It *persists*. A static crop of frost-lichen, each rosette no bigger than a coin, spaced a meter apart with mathematical loneliness. Under the caged lights, some of the maintenance grates have sprouted pale, etiolated air-ferns — the kind that are seeded into industrial habitats to scrub trace CO2. They are not thriving. They are not dying. Like everything else in this pocket, they are in equilibrium, frozen in a sparse scattering, holding the exact same pose year after year.

You see also, scattered with the same low density, the other presences: a toppled tool cart with its wheels locked. A single insulated worker's parka hung on a hook, stiff with cold. A row of untouched control panels with analog gauges where the needles have vibrated into permanent blurs at 70%. A clipboard with a checklist where every box is checked in the same handwriting. No people. The presences are spaced out, sparse, as if the room was designed to be tended by many and is now tended by no one — or by something that does not need to move.

**The Rules of This Place.**

This landscape has rules, and it repeats them without variation:

1. Nothing enters and nothing leaves. This is a sealed environment, a pocket. The door you came through is the only breach and it wants to close.
2. The sound never changes, so you stop hearing it after ten minutes, then you realize you've gone half-deaf and the ringing will stay for days.
3. The cold preserves. Grease does not flow, metal does not fatigue, lichen does not spread. Sharp things stay sharp.
4. Everything is made and everything is maintained — by the engines — for no purpose you can see from inside. The Array outside, the peak, is the purpose. This room is just the heart that forgot how to stop beating.

Weirdness: 1.9/3. Strange, but not incomprehensible. You understand *what* this is — a summit service reliquary, an industrial shrine, a furnace that heats nothing. What you don't understand is why the light is so normal, why the ferns are allowed to live, why someone hung up their parka so neatly before leaving forever.

You stay for as long as your ears can stand, which is not long, and then you step back out onto the dry, bright, dead salt-desert of the high basalt plain, where the young, sharp wind hits you like poured gravel, and you pull the door shut behind you. The chord does not change as the door seals. It never changes.
