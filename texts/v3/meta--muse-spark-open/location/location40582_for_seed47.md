This is the High Peneplain.

Not a mountain, though you are higher than mountains should allow. Not a desert, though there is no water. It's what is left when a continent has been lifted and then left alone for a billion years to settle into its final argument with gravity.

### The Ground

You stand on stone that has forgotten how to be stone.

At 2.7/3 elevation, the air is thin, cool - about 1.4/3, the temperature of late autumn held permanently - and so clear it feels edited. The sky is a hard, normal light, 2.0/3 daylight, sun-white without haze, throwing shadows with knife edges. But the ground under you is not peaked or dramatic. That would require youth.

Erosion here is 3.0/3: ancient beyond geology. The entire world is a peneplain, planed down by wind and time until even the concept of a hill is archaeological. What was once a range of Himalayas is now a single, vast, gently undulating shield of exposed basement rock, polished to a low luster. The surface is not sand. Sand implies recent violence. This is gneiss and shield-stone, banded in charcoal and bone and rust, worn until it flows. Swells rise for kilometers at a gradient so gentle you only know you're climbing because your breath shortens. Shallow basins a continent wide hold nothing.

On closer inspection, the rock is not bare. It is tiled. The cool wind - dry beyond dryness, 0.5/3 water, not even enough moisture to make frost properly - has worked with thermal contraction for eons to fracture the shield into polygonal plates, each the size of a house or a city block, fitted together with dark veins of wind-packed dust. Between the plates are seams a hand wide, filled with salt. Not sea salt. Exhaled mineral salt, lithium-white and bitter-yellow, precipitated from the rock itself over deep time when the last groundwater sweated out and vanished. In the seams grows the only vegetation.

### What Grows, What Moves

Vegetation is 0.8/3: sparse to the point of reverence. There are no fields, no forests, no carpets. Each plant is a solitary decision.

In the salt seams, black lichenforms crust the edges - flat, almost 2D, more stain than organism. Every few hundred meters, a true plant: a wire-brush of needled glass, knee-high, its roots a chemical drill ten meters down hunting for bound hydroxyls in the rock. It does not photosynthesize so much as it *tolerates* light. Its needles are silica-coated and rattle faintly when the rare breeze hits them, the only natural sound for miles. They are widely, precisely spaced, as if planted.

Fauna is 0.9/3: rare enough that seeing anything feels like a breach of protocol. There are no herds, no flocks. There are gliders - paper-thin, translucent mantas the size of your hand that ride the thermals high above the plain, never landing. They filter spores from the empty air. On the ground, salt-mites like moving quartz grains that scatter from your bootfall and then go still for hours. Once a day you might see a strider: three stilt legs and a sensor head, stepping from plate to plate across kilometers, pausing to taste lichen. They do not flee. There is nowhere to flee to, and you are 0.3/3 density - empty - an anomaly in a volume that averages cubic kilometers per presence.

Sound is 0.1/3. The High Peneplain is one of the quietest places a human ear can experience and survive. No water to babble, no leaves to rustle, no insects to drone. The wind is infrequent and when it comes it is laminar, high and thin, combing over the polished stone without turbulence at ground level. Your own blood becomes loud. Your joints click like stones. When the needle-plants rattle, you turn as if someone spoke.

### The Built

And yet - built is 1.1/3, tech is 2.8/3.

Scattered across the vastness, at intervals that make no sense until you see them from orbit, are the Structures. Each is alone by design. There might be one per horizon, sometimes two, sometimes none for a day's walk.

They are not ruins, though they look like it at first: low, dark, angular things the size of a small house or a truck, half-sunken into the stone as if the ancient rock had been melted just enough to accept them. No doors. No windows. No markings. Their surfaces absorb normal light oddly, not black but *unfinished*, like the idea of a surface before it decided on a texture. Dust will not settle on them. Lichen will not grow within a meter. A strider will step around them in a wide arc.

They are beyond-modern. They are not buildings to shelter in. They are regulators, anchors, or maybe just markers. Because while the ground is primordial and settled, the sky is not.

Dynamic is 3.0/3: turbulent, chaotic, hyperactive.

The stillness at ankle height is a lie. A hundred meters up, and then a kilometer, and then to the edge of space, the atmosphere over the Peneplain is in constant, violent creation-destruction. The emptiness of the ground and the thin, cool air allows a weather system of impossible energy to sustain itself without ever touching down. Clouds do not form and drift; they *boil* into being from nothing, dark grey and bruised violet anvil-heads that stack ten kilometers tall in minutes, crackle with silent sheet lightning, snow upward, and then unmake themselves. Vortices of clear air spin like glass cyclones, visible only by the lensing of distant Structures. Auroral curtains snap and fold in broad daylight, casting moving color on the white salt without any solar storm to cause them.

It is turbulent but silent. You can watch a continent-sized storm-system annihilate itself in an hour and hear only your own breathing. The Structures are what does it. Every few minutes, one of them shivers without moving - a distortion in the air above it, like heat haze in cool air - and a new cloud-wall erupts, or an old one collapses. They are farming vacuum energy, or bleeding momentum from the planet's rotation, or remembering a weather that this dead world never got to have. The sky is their bazaar, frantic with exchange, while the ground remains patient.

The rule here is vertical separation: Absolute stability below, absolute chaos above. The ancient eroded shield is the anvil; the post-physical machines are the hammers; the sky is the sparkfield. Nothing mediates between them. No rain falls. No lightning strikes. The turbulence never erodes, never waters, never touches. It is a storm-state performed *for* the desert, not *on* it.

Weirdness is 1.1/3 - only slightly unusual, which makes it worse. Nothing screams alien. There are no floating islands, no breathing colors. The weirdness is the wrongness of the coupling: that such a quiet, empty, finished place should host such furious, silent industry overhead, and that the two have apparently been paired for geological ages without one affecting the other. You feel like you are walking across the floor of a sealed library while a hurricane rages in the rafters, and no one has thought to close the window because the window is physics itself.

At night - if you stay - normal light fades to a starfield so dense and un-twinkling in the thin air it looks fake. The Structures cool from invisible to faintly outlined in dull red, heat-dumping. Above, the turbulence continues as a slow, silent fireworks of magnetics. Below, the salt seams exhale a breath of cold, dry mineral air that smells of nothing. A strider clicks past, leg over seam, utterly indifferent to the creation and destruction of weather overhead.

You are small, cold, and very loud in your suit, in a place that has become perfect at being empty.
