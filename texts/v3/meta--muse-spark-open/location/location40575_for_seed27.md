This is not a desert the way you think of a desert, and this is not a forest the way you think of a forest. It is both at once.

You are very high up.

The world here is an old table. Not a mountain range - a table. A continent lifted in the deep past and then left alone so long that wind and time did its work without interruption. Erosion 2.6 means this place is finished. It is primordial in the other direction: not raw and jagged, but settled into its final form. The basalt and limestone beneath you has been sanded by a billion cool seasons into a broad, gently undulant plain, pitted and polished. There are no peaks, no scree slopes. Just long, low whales of stone rising a few meters here and there, their backs smoothed like river stones the size of suburbs, their flanks carved with labyrinthine rilles only ankle-deep. The ground between them is a pavement of mineral fines - salt and hematite and bone-white dust that has never been wet. When you step, it *pings*, not crunches. It rings like ceramic.

There is no water here. 0.1/3 is not dry season dry. It is chemically dry.

No rivers. No lakes. No snow. The air itself is wrung out. What little atmospheric moisture there is exists as a high, thin haze of ice crystal fog miles above you that never precipitates, that only dims the light further. The ground is anhydrous. If you licked the rock it would pull the spit from your mouth. Any Earth plant dropped here would mummify in an hour, brittle as paper.

And yet - vegetation: 3.0/3. Total.

The plain *is* plant.

Not covering the rock. *Being* the land. The dominant life here solved water by abandoning it.

They are called the Salt Lace, or the Veil, by the people who live in it, but they have no proper name because they are not individuals. From a distance you would think you were looking at a low, grey-green tundra, a lichen field stretching to the milky horizon. Up close you realize it is an architecture.

Each organism - if you can call it that - is a vast, dry photosynthetic lattice. It has no leaves, no sap. It is made of interwoven fibers as thin as spun glass and as rigid as chitin, grown from airborne silica and carbon. The fibers branch, and branch again, fractal, building openwork towers and domes and curtains that are 80% empty air. They are plant as scaffold. The whole plain is a single contiguous reef of this material, waist-high in most places, rising in sparse cathedrals where the stone whales give it purchase - there, it vaults ten, fifteen meters into the dim air as openwork spires that whistle when the wind moves through them.

It does not drink. It eats light and exhales dust. At dawn - which is always dawn here - the lattice flexes. Trillions of hair-fine cilia on every fiber vibrate and generate static charge, pulling trace ammonia and carbonic vapor straight out of the thin air, catalyzing it internally into more lattice without ever needing liquid solvent. This is why the light is dim (1.0/3) and still enough. A sullen, cool, pearlescent sun, far or small or old, sits perpetually at about four o'clock behind the high ice haze, giving no shadows, only a universal, directionless gloaming. It's too cool (0.6/3) for evaporation, which is perfect, because there is nothing to evaporate. The coolness preserves the charge. If you stand still you can feel the air prickle with it. Your hair lifts.

Weirdness 2.5 means the physics here is slightly unstitched.

In the dim, the Lace glows - not bioluminescence, but triboluminescence. When the constant, loud wind drags across the fibers, they grind faintly and release photons. A gust moves across the valley and the entire forest-reef ripples with pale amber light from within, like embers breathing. And the sound - 2.5/3, loud - is not what you expect. The plain is never quiet. The Lace is an aeolian instrument the size of a city. The wind through a billion glassy forks creates a chord so low you feel it in your molars, overlaid with a ceaseless high shivering, like a bow drawn across wire brushes. When a spire sways, it groans. When two fronds touch, they chime.

And it is *alive* in the other sense: dynamic, active, abundant fauna.

You would think nothing could live here without water, but the Lace makes its own micro-economy. The animals are also dry. They are sparse (0.8/3) - you can walk a hundred meters and see only the swaying lattice - but they are everywhere in total numbers.

They are not soft things. They are lattice-parasites and lattice-herders, all built on the same non-aqueous chemistry. The most common is the size of a hare, but shaped like a dandelion puff made of black wire: a floating filter that drifts on electrostatic lift, trailing tendrils to comb ammonia from the air, singing as it goes. There are heavier things - six-legged, barrel-chested grazers with transparent bellies, that do not eat the Lace but *tune* it, filing down overgrowth with vibrating plates on their heads so the chord stays pure. And predators: razor-thin mantas that ride the wind without ever flapping, that cut the puff-animals in half in midair with a silent, cold shear. You hear the cut. The whole valley hears every kill as a sudden *ping* followed by a rain of fine black fibers settling like ash.

Sparse density means you can see for kilometers across the open (1.8/3) town-sized basin, all of it veiled in this luminous reef, and understand the structure at once.

And built there, 1.5/3 urban, 2.8/3 beyond modern.

The people did not clear the Lace. They *collaborate* with it. Their town is scattered, not clustered - another reason it feels sparse - a dozen districts spread across the plain, connected not by roads but by resonant paths where the Lace has been coaxed to grow flat and dense.

Their buildings are not built. They are *grown and tuned*. They look like the spires, only intentional: great hollow baskets of living lattice, thickened until they can bear weight, their resonance altered so the wind makes different notes as it passes - each household humming its own chord. Inside, the air crackles with engineered static. Light is provided not by lamps but by stress. When you walk across a floor, your weight bends the fibers and they glow brighter under your feet and then fade. When you speak too loudly, the walls answer with harmonic sympathy.

Their technology doesn't look like technology because it never needed to solve water. It solves charge and vibration. Children carry cages of puff-animals like kites. Elders sit on the smoothed stone whales outside their singing houses and listen to the weather coming from the other side of the ancient plain, a sound like an oncoming train made of glass, hours before it arrives, carrying the amber light-wave with it.

There is no thirst here. There is no rot. Nothing ever molders. When something dies - plant or animal or human - it simply desiccates further, becomes more lattice, is incorporated with a soft, sustained chime as its charge equalizes with the Veil.

You stand on the polished back of a stone whale, cool air tugging at your clothes, the whole open valley around you humming and glowing in the dim, and you understand: this is what a forest looks like when it finally learns to live without weeping.
