You are standing well below the level the world was supposed to be at.

Not in a cave. There is no roof here. The sky is still directly above you, whole and open, but the land around you has fallen away, or never finished rising. A basin the size of a small country, a valley-without-a-river, a slow sink in the crust perhaps twenty kilometers across and half a kilometer down. The rim is a long, unbroken lip of raw stone all the way around the horizon - pale, still sharp, unweathered. You can see where it broke. There are no softened hills, no scree slopes worn smooth by rain, no gullies. The geology is too new for that. It looks poured, or torn. Basalt and young limestone in enormous, clean sheets, still holding the bubble-marks and cooling cracks of its making, like bread just split open.

And all of it, from rim to floor, is filled with green.

Not covered in green. *Filled* with it.

The vegetation here did not colonize the basin. It *is* the basin. There is very little soil in any normal sense. The plants have become the ground. Layer on layer on layer of living mass - broad-leafed, thick-stemmed, waxy things the size of houses interlocked with vines thick as roads, with mats of fern and moss and air-plant that never touch mineral at all, with trees that grow sideways and then down and then up again. You walk not on earth but on a deep, springy lattice of root and trunk and leaf-litter that has knit itself into a kind of suspended continent, fifty, a hundred feet thick in places. In the gaps you can see down through the weave to dark hollows and to the young rock below, glinting wetly.

It is intensely hot and intensely bright.

Light falls straight down the open mouth of the depression and has nowhere else to go. It reflects off the pale raw walls and is trapped by the leaves and re-radiated as heat. By midday the air is white-gold and viscous, shimmering. The leaves themselves seem to make light - many are translucent, pale green to yellow, thin as paper, lit from within. The whole basin glows. There are no real shadows, only deeper shades of green and gold. You squint without knowing why; the brightness has no source to look away from.

The heat does not move. The air in the basin is in equilibrium, a held breath. This place is just-born in geological terms, but it does not feel changeful. Nothing erodes. Nothing falls. The plants hold every particle in place. Growth here is not expansion but maintenance - a leaf unfurls as another curls and they exchange exactly the same water, the same volume. You get the uncanny sense that if you came back in a thousand years the exact same frond would be in front of you at the exact same angle, still dripping the same bead of condensation.

Because water is here, but sparingly. There is no lake, no river that cuts. The basin is damp in the way a greenhouse is damp.

In the early morning - though morning is only a change in the quality of brightness - the walls weep. Cold stone above meets hot air below and condensation forms in sheets and runs down the raw rock in black threads, gathering in the axils of the giant leaves, in cup-flowers big enough to bathe in, in pockets hollowed into the root-mass. Midday, the water lifts again as steam. You walk through sudden warm fogs that rise from nowhere and vanish. You hear an occasional *tick, plop* as a leaf-tip overfills and empties into another leaf below it. You can go hours hearing only that. Then a deep *creak* as wood adjusts to heat, and then nothing again. It is mostly quiet. Sound is swallowed by all that mass.

Life is dense, but not with animals.

Every cubic meter is occupied. Air-plants grow on air-plants. Vines use vines as trellises. Fungi lace everything together with white cord. Even the light feels crowded - motes, pollen, seed-fluff hanging motionless in the hot stillness, so thick you could scoop it. But it is plant density, not animal. The fauna is rare to the point of absence. Sometimes a beetle like a polished stone makes its way across a leaf, impossibly slow. Lizard-things the color of new bark freeze on trunks and you only see them when they blink. High above, something with wide, translucent wings circles on thermals rising off the bright bowl, but it never lands. The plants do not need pollinators here; they seem to propagate by touch, by pressure, by splitting. The animal world has not yet been invited in, or has already left.

There are human marks, but not a civilization.

Scattered through the green, at intervals that feel deliberate but could be random, are structures so overgrown they are more plant than building. Not ruins - they were never large. A single wall of stacked uncut stone holding back a bulge of root. A stair cut three steps deep into the living mat and ending nowhere. A small open shrine or shelter made of the same pale raw limestone as the rim, its roof now a solid slab of moss and flowering vine, its doorway choked with a curtain of aerial roots. No metal. No glass. No tools left behind. You find a smooth hollow in a leaf-mass clearly shaped for a body to sleep in, still holding the impression of shoulders. You find cairns the size of fists, long since cemented together by sap.

Whoever was here built lightly, with what the basin offered, and then stopped. Or the basin grew over them so perfectly that building became indistinguishable from botany. It is impossible to tell if the structures are emerging from the plants or being swallowed by them, because in this place emerging and being swallowed are the same motion.

The rules here are simple and total:

Light is not filtered. It is contained.
Water does not flow outward. It cycles in place, leaf to air to stone to leaf.
Ground is not found. It is woven.
Time does not pass. It is held, hot and bright and damp, in the green throat of the new earth, waiting to see if it will ever need to erode at all.
