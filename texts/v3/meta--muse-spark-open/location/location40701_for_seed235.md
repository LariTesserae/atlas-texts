This is not a mountain. It was, once.

What remains is the Mesa — a table cut into the sky.

### The High Wet

You are at extreme elevation, nearly three kilometers straight up from the lowlands you can no longer see. The air should be thin and cold and spare here. It is not. It is hot. A low, wet, breath-on-glass heat that never breaks. 2.8/3 hot, the kind of heat that makes stone sweat.

The elevation does not cool the air; it pushes you closer to the sun. Light at noon is normal — 1.7/3, not glaring, not dim, a flat white-gold that has no source you can point to. It simply is, diffused through a permanent, high haze. There are no shadows that move. There never have been. That is the first hint of the dynamic at work here: 0.5/3. Nothing changes. Nothing has changed.

And yet it is wet. Impossibly wet for this height.

Water does not fall here. It arrives.

The rock itself weeps. The entire mesa-top — a valley-scale open bowl maybe fifteen kilometers across, rimmed on all sides by a sheer escarpment that drops into cloud — is made of a dark, porous, rust-red stone, weathered into a settled, balanced shape. Erosion 1.6/3: not raw, not crumbling, but long-existed. Every gully has found its final form. Every cliff has learned its angle and kept it. The water lives inside the stone.

It seeps out everywhere, in beads and sheets and slow, fat drips from every overhang. Condensation forms on every leaf and then never evaporates. The air is so saturated you inhale it as much as breathe it. Post-rain, forever, without the rain ever having to happen. Springs well up at your feet without a source aquifer. Small black pools gather in stone bowls and overflow in glassy threads that never get larger or smaller. The soundscape starts here.

### The Sound That Doesn't Happen

Sound: 2.7/3 — deafening.

You expect silence on a high place with no animals. Fauna: 0.5/3. There is nothing that moves under its own will. No birds. No insects. Not even a fly to catch in the heat. Your own footsteps are muffled by moss.

But the mesa roars.

All around the rim, the weeping water finally gathers itself and goes over the edge. Not in rivers — in a thousand thousand curtains, lacefalls, white threads — a continuous, unbroken circular waterfall off the edge of the world. The drop is so far you cannot hear the bottom, only the going-over. The wind that hits the escarpment and is forced up and over creates a standing organ-tone in every weathered flute and hollow in the rimrock. And the trees —

The trees contribute.

Vegetation: 2.0/3 lush, density: 1.8/3 dense. Layered. Near-touching. Everything grows on everything else. But nothing here grows *up* so much as *out and together*.

Because there are no animals to carry seed or browse or disturb, the plant life has become architecture. This is the built parameter — 2.0/3 urban — with tech 0.1/3 none.

No one built this city. It grew itself, and then it stopped.

### The Grown City

The mesa-top is not a forest. It is a settlement made of forest. A town the size of a region, or a region that decided to be a town.

Broadleaf giants with trunks as wide as houses have grafted together, branch to branch, forming continuous elevated avenues. Their roots have braided into walls, buttresses, archways, terraces, stairwells that lead nowhere you need to go. Below them, a second story of fern-trees and hanging gardens forms a dense mid-canopy that filters the white light into green. At ground level, a thicket of moss-pillars, liverworts scaled up to the size of market stalls, and glossy reeds that grow in perfect, street-like rows between pools of weeping water.

Everything is occupied. Everything is full. Lichen tiles every surface like shingles. Vines as thick as your thigh run like aqueducts from pool to pool, carrying water without ever pumping it. The whole mass hums with a vegetable density — many presences per volume — each leaf large, waxy, dripping, overlapping the next by centimeters.

And all of it is still. In perfect equilibrium.

Leaves do not fall. They do not yellow. A frond hangs half-unfurled and has been half-unfurled forever. A drop of water hangs at the tip of every leaf, catching the light, never falling. You can put your hand through a curtain of hanging moss and it will close behind you and stay closed, exactly as you left it, because the moss grows back around the absence instantly without ever seeming to move.

This is a place in settled-form. The erosion stopped eroding. The growth stopped growing. The water cycle stopped cycling. It reached a conclusion and decided to hold it.

That is the reality breakdown. Weirdness: 2.6/3.

### The Rules That Slipped

At first you think the deafening roar is just water and wind. Then you realize you are hearing the same second of sound on loop. Not a loop like a recording. A loop like time forgot to push forward.

If you stand at the eastern rim and look across the bowl to the western rim, the distance is wrong. It is simultaneously five kilometers away and close enough to touch. The city-canopy in the middle folds slightly inward, like light going through water. Walk down what looks like a straight root-avenue and you arrive, without turning, back at the pool you started from — but from the other side. The sky is too low. The stone is too wet to ever be this hot. The heat has no source.

Gravity is slightly sideways here. Water weeps *out* of vertical cliffs and then hesitates before deciding to fall. Leaves hang at an angle that doesn't match how they should hang. Your own weight feels correctly heavy, but if you pour water from your hands it falls a little too slowly, as if the world is considering whether to let it go.

There are structures that cannot exist without tools. A perfect dome of interwoven living trunks, open to the sky, with an oculus that frames the haze exactly. A plaza of flat, moss-covered root- mat so even you could set a table on it. A colonnade of aerial roots descending from the mid-canopy in such regular intervals it has to be intentional — but there is no intention left, only the plant-memory of a city that was needed once and then perfected.

Who needed it? No one. Nothing with eyes or hands has ever been here. The fauna never arrived. Perhaps it was expected and never came. Perhaps it left when the dynamic froze.

You understand, standing in the hot, wet, roaring stillness, that this is an urban ecosystem that urbanized itself for inhabitants that never evolved, and then achieved stasis. A climax community that became a civilization.

The rules here are simple:
1. Water will always be present, never more, never less.
2. Light will never move.
3. Sound will never cease, never vary.
4. Nothing will grow, die, rot, or be born.
5. The city will remain dense, lush, full, and utterly empty of anything that can appreciate it.

You can stay as long as you want. The heat will not exhaust you more in an hour than in a minute. The roar will not deafen you more than it already has in the first second. Your footprints in the soft moss between the reed-streets will fill in behind you at the speed of thought, which is to say, they were never made.

The mesa waits, open and regional and complete, extending past the immediate in every direction until it goes over the edge and becomes waterfall and wind. It is a town the size of a valley at the top of the world, built by no hands, heard like a war, wet as a mouth, hot as a fever, and perfectly, permanently paused.
