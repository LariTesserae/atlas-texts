It is not a forest. There is not enough of anything in the world outside to make a forest possible.

This is what is left at the bottom of the world after everything old has finished flattening.

Far up and out, there is a plain. It goes on for a pale eternity, salt-white and basalt-black under a sky that does not matter here, wind-polished to a tabletop. It is primordial in the way bone is primordial - not dramatic, just the shape things settle into when they have had long enough to forget any other shape. No mountains. No rivers. The wind moves salt in dry sheets and then doesn't. That is the Low.

You have to know where to step to find it. A flaw in the plain, hardly deeper than a knee, hardly wider than a village's worth of houses - a pock, a collapsed blister, a place where the crust exhaled once in the deep past and never inhaled again. From the surface you could walk over it and think it a discoloration. Down is about six feet at its deepest lip, a slow, cool bowl of aged stone smoothed until it feels touched.

And then the plain is gone. Because the pocket is full.

### The Fill

It fits exactly. Edge to edge, lip to lip, the bowl is filled with plant, but not as ground cover. As architecture.

One organism - or what used to be one organism, now so ancient and so partitioned it has forgotten it was single - has taken the hollow and made of itself a city before any hands arrived.

It does not grow in soil. There is no soil. It does not drink. There is no water here beyond the faintest chemical sweat, a bitter mineral tang that crusts as white salt-flowers on every surface and vanishes when you touch it. Cool air, always cool, dry enough to tighten your lips. Whatever this vegetation is, it is not green in any way you know. In full darkness green would be a rumor anyway. Here, up close, it is the color of old paper, of cork, of fungal leather. Pale, fibrous, bloodless.

From the rim, what you see - what your hands see first - are Columns. A dozen or so, no more. Thick as houses, thin as houses, irregularly spaced so far apart you could run and not touch anything for a long time. They rise from the smooth bowl-floor and do not reach the sky; there is no sky in the pocket, there is only the dark pressed down on top of it like a lid. The Columns are trunks, or roots, or fruiting bodies that decided upright was the last interesting direction left. They are hollow. Everyone understands this quickly because the wind never quite leaves the pocket even though there is no weather to drive it, and the sound lives inside them.

The surfaces between Columns are not empty ground. They are a suspended tangle of living floor - sheets of interwoven aerial root, flattened leaf-plates the size of doors that have fused at the edges, hammocks of mycelial mat stretched taut and dry as drumskin. You walk on plant. You walk *inside* plant. Everything underfoot gives slightly, then holds, cork-spongy and ancient and deeply eroded at the edges, frayed into felt. Where the hands of people have cut streets, you can see the strata: layer after dry layer of accretion, annual sheaths that were never years, compacted centuries of blind growth laid down in cool darkness.

Because this plant has never seen light. Not once. That is its rule and its weirdness. It does not photosynthesize. It eats stone, very slowly. Acid rootlets dissolve the salt-flat's minerals with patient chemistry and pull the faint exhalation of the sealed air - whatever off-gasses from the cooling crust below - and makes more of itself out of it. A lithovore. A gas-drinker. A desert lung. It grows colder when it grows faster, which is almost never. You could sit for a lifetime and not see a shoot lengthen. But put your ear to a Column wall and you feel it: deep, infrastructural patience.

### The Quiet City That Is Not Quiet

People found the pocket at some point when people still walked the plain. They did not build *on* the plant. They built *with* the permission the plant's emptiness gave them.

Density here is the strangest thing to hold in your head alongside a city. A city suggests crowding. This city is defined by absence. The Column interiors are rooms big enough for three families. The space between Columns is a plaza where your voice gets lost before it hits anything. The plant fills the *concept* of the pocket completely - wall to wall, plant-as-substrate - and yet per volume there is almost nothing. Vast, hollow, cool dark air between thin, living membranes. A settlement scaled for hundreds built for a structure that is ninety percent void.

And it is urban. Unmistakably. Whoever lived here had time and organization and no interest in leaving. Narrow streets have been cut through the fused floor-leaves, their edges bound with woven fiber so they do not heal shut. Ladders of braided root-rope hang inside Columns, connecting levels carved into the pith like apartments. Low walls of compressed, harvested cork-block mark property that has not mattered in a long time. Shutters made from sliced leaf-plate. Toolmarks everywhere - not metal, or not much - the scrape of chert flakes, basalt adzes, the polish of hands. Primitive work, careful work, work that knew the material would not tolerate hurry. The whole town is one material, subtly different ages of the same creature, carved and coaxed while alive and now holding its shape.

No one lives here now, or if they do they are still and hold their breath like you do. There is no animal life. Not even the token desert insect. No rats in the walls, no bats in the vaulted hollows. The plant produces nothing an animal would want; there is no fruit, no nectar, no water storage you can tap. Even rot does not happen quickly - dead plates just dry and tighten and become structure. It is an ecosystem of one, plus the memory of people.

That makes the sound worse. And louder.

In darkness, sound is the architecture. And this place is loud.

It was not built for silence. The Columns are organ pipes, Aeolian throats. The cool air, sinking by degrees into the bowl, moves through them without wind, driven by slow pressure differences between the surface and the sealed deep. It sighs through pores the size of fingertips, and whistles through deliberate windows cut by inhabitants to ventilate their rooms, and groans deep in the pith where fibers under tension have been drying for a thousand years and letting go a fraction of a millimeter at a time. The entire city creaks.

Not constantly. In events. A low *tink* from the north Column, like pottery cracking six rooms away. Then a long exhale that shifts fallen salt dust into new lines on the floor. Then, hours later, a sudden loud *POP* from underfoot as a mat adjusts, and everything answers - a shuddering chorus of fibrous creaks from every wall, a rustle of dry leaf-plates trembling overhead though nothing touched them. The plant talks to itself across long durations, patient processes, minimal flux. If you stay a night you learn its grammar: what is settlement, what is breath, what is warning that a rope walkway above you has dried a little more and is now a little lower.

You navigate by it. Light never comes. Light has never come. The darkness under the lid is absolute, not cave-dark but sealed-dark, dark that has never been broken by sun. Eyes are irrelevant here except to close them so they do not ache trying. Cool air touches your face, dry, mineral, faintly acrid. Your palms read cork grain and rope and salt. Your ears read the city.

This is why the city is empty and still intact. You cannot keep animals here; they panic in the loud dark. You cannot farm. You can only tend the plant that tends itself, harvest its dead growth, cut new rooms when old ones finally sag - slow, slow work. A settlement for listening. For people who had somewhere else to be afraid of.

At the very center, where the bowl is lowest by inches, the Columns lean inward just slightly, and the suspended floor there is thinnest, almost translucent in a way darkness makes you imagine rather than see. If you lie flat on it, the cool comes up from below through the weave, and you can hear the deepest sound: not air but growth. A faint, almost subsonic grinding, mineral being made into fiber somewhere far under your back. It is too slow to be called movement. It is the sound ancient things make when they have decided to keep being there.

Put your hand there and you feel the faintest tremor, once, like a plucked string.

The city listens back.
