This is not a place you *visit*. It's a place you pass through and try to leave intact.

You do not need climbing gear or cold-weather survival training to go to the Mineral Town. You need water discipline, navigation paranoia, and a very high tolerance for being ignored by reality itself. Think less "expedition to a lost city" and more "day-hike on the lungs of a sleeping god."

Here is how an ordinary human - soft, wet, loud, desperately Euclidean - prepares.

### 1. First, Understand What Will Kill You

It won't be dramatic. There are no predators that chase you, no storms, no cold snap.

The Expanse kills by subtraction.

**1. Dryness / Water: 0.2**
This is the number one killer. The air is a sponge. At that elevation on that plateau, the relative humidity is functionally zero. You will lose water with every breath and not notice because your sweat evaporates before it beads.

You will not find water. Not a seep, not a puddle, not dew. That black glass nodule story is true - even when the lithovore traps brine, it seals it and turns it to dust. Do not plan to source anything there.

*Preparation:*
- Bring 50% more water than you think you need for the time + elevation. For a 6 hour traverse, carry 4-5 liters minimum. You will drink and still get dehydrated.
- Pre-hydrate for 24 hours before translation/jump in. Electrolytes, not just water. Your urine should be clear before you go.
- Gear: A closed hydration system with a tube. Open bottles lose water to evaporation. Bring oral rehydration salts. Bring *a lot* of lip balm, thick skin salve, nasal saline spray, and preservative-free eye drops. Your lips will crack within an hour. Your eyes will feel like sandpaper.
- Cover everything. Long sleeves, light scarf/gaiter over nose and mouth to reclaim breath moisture, sunglasses or better yet sealed goggles. Not for sun - there is no sun - but for dust and dryness. Every inch of exposed skin is a leak.
- Rule: If you feel thirsty, you are already 30 minutes behind. Drink on a timer. Pee check every 90 minutes. If you stop peeing, you leave. No debate.

**2. Air & Elevation: Extreme, Cool 0.7**
It's high like Tibetan Plateau high, but wrong. The air is thin and permanently cool - think 50-55°F / 10-13°C, stone-cool, wind-cool. Not freezing, but relentlessly wicking. Combined with low pressure you will get lightheaded, short of breath, and stupid faster than you expect. Altitude sickness is real here and rescue is not.

*Preparation:*
- Acclimatize if you can. Don't jump in after sprinting. Rest, breathe slowly through your nose.
- Bring supplemental oxygen if you tend toward altitude sickness. Most travelers don't need a tank for a short stay, but bring it if you have asthma, anemia, or anxiety that spikes breathing.
- Layers: Merino or synthetic base, windbreaker. No shorts, no cotton. The cool is not dangerous by itself, but dehydration + wind + thin air will make you hypothermic shockingly fast if you stop moving. Pack a compact thermal blanket.
- Your voice will not carry. Your shouts will be swallowed. Bring a whistle and a signal mirror - old tech that doesn't care about physics.

**3. The Empty: 0.2**
This is the psychological killer. The city is dense with *things that look like buildings* but empty in a way your brain has never felt. A desert has life. A ruined city has memory. This has neither. No birdsong, no insects, no rustle of leaves. Silence 0.3 is profound. Your brain will try to fill it and fail, which creates panic, tinnitus, and the horrible conviction you are being watched.

You are not. The city does not care that you exist. That is worse.

*Preparation:*
- Do not go alone. Two humans minimum, three ideal. You need a voice outside your own head to tether you.
- Time-box your stay. Ordinary humans should not do more than 4-6 hours subjective on first visit, 12 hours absolute max even with experience. Set a loud, vibrating timer you cannot ignore. Time slip is real.
- Bring something quietly human and rhythmic: a small music player on low volume in one ear, a soft clicker, prayer beads to run through fingers. The city only allows whispers. Don't fight it - whisper. But give your brain a human pattern to hold.
- Pre-agree on an abort phrase with your partner. Not "I'm scared" - you will all be scared. Something like "Empty check - I'm at 7." When you hit 8/10 on the derealization scale, you both walk out. No heroics.

### 2. Gear: Low Tech Wins Here

Tech 0.1 is not a law that your gear will break. It's a statement that *nothing was ever made here.* Tool marks, mortar, right angles - they don't want to exist. Complex electronics get sullen. GPS is useless - distance is broken. Compasses drift. Laser rangefinders lie.

Bring simple, redundant, analog tools.

**Navigation Kit - The Most Important Thing You Own:**
Distance misbehaves 2.9. The tower you want is three blocks away until it is suddenly beside you. Alleys stretch. The central plaza makes the whole city look like a model.

- **DO NOT rely on sight-distance.** Do not say "let's just walk to that arch."
- Bring a long spool of high-visibility, non-elastic cord or cave line - 200m. And physical chalk or collapsible cairns. Mark every intersection you pass. The city rearranges when you blink (Rule 3). Your marks may move or be erased. That is information.
- Count steps. Use a manual click counter. Note your bearing with an analog compass even if it jitters - the *change* matters.
- Navigation protocol: Move in short legs. Stop, look, agree with partner, mark, move. Never have both people looking away at the same time. One watches while the other blinks/rests. Leapfrog.
- Bring paper, pencil, not pen - ink dries weird there. Sketch maps obsessively, noting time.
- For exit: Always keep the rim/edge direction in mind. The easiest way to get lost is to go deep and look up and see that diffuse slate sky everywhere. Place tall, bright flags at intervals. They may shift, but you can follow the trail of disturbance.

**Other Gear:**
- Headlamp + backup + backup. Light 1.4 is dim, diffuse, shadow-soft, wrong-directioned. At local noon you can have two shadows. You will lose depth perception on stairs that end in air. Your headlamp gives you *one* honest shadow.
- A straight edge will lie. Don't bother trying to measure right angles. Bring a 360-degree protractor if you need to survey, but know nothing is true.
- No drones. They lag, then teleport. Paper kites work better, ironically. If you want to test wind, throw dust, not electronics.
- Food: Nothing grows there Plants 0.5. Bring calorie-dense, non-crumbly food. Nuts, bars, gels. Crumbs will hang in air in low-grav zones and attract nothing good. Eat small, often. No cooking - no need for fire and open flame is rude in a lung.
- Pack out absolutely everything including waste. The lithovore doesn't want your rot. Nothing rots there anyway.

### 3. Moving Through The Quiet Motion

The city is active 2.5 despite being silent.

**Wind & Dust Devils:**
Constant laminar river of cool air through doorways. Pale dust devils live for an hour. They are harmless but disorienting. If one forms around you, stay still, cover your eyes. They collapse on their own.

**Gravity Anomalies:**
This will be the most alien part for an ordinary body.

- Watch the dust before you walk into a courtyard. If dust is rising slowly or pebbles are hovering a foot up vibrating - that's an inverted-fall / low-commitment zone.
- Do not linger under those arches. Do not camp there. Do not nap under them. Every few days, in no pattern you can predict, they Remember and everything drops at once. It's not lethal to be hit by dust, but a pebble that has been hanging for weeks dropping from 20 feet up onto your skull is a medical evacuation you cannot afford where distance doesn't work.
- Test each new plaza: toss a handful of light grit ahead of you. Watch which way it falls.

**The Stairs and Doors:**
Treat the city like a tidepool, not a building. Look, don't test with weight. Those staircases that end in mid-air are not broken - they were never stairs. They are growth spurs. If you step on one that looks inviting, you may be standing on a hollow exhalation tube that hums. It will hold you, until it doesn't.

Do not go deep into channels that hum. That's wind over the mycelium's breathing pores. The draft is odorless and cool and will lure you down and distance will stretch.

### 4. The Inhabitants: Etiquette For Guests

Fauna 1.4 means rare, but you *will* see things.

1.  **The Sky Kites:** Meter-wide translucent ash-flakes riding electrostatic charge. They are silent, indifferent. Do not try to catch or bat at one. They are colonies. If you disrupt one, you get a mild static shock and a bad headache. Just watch. If many of them suddenly clump or flee in one direction, follow them - charge is shifting, weather that isn't weather is coming.

2.  **The Salt Beetles / Gardeners:** Tortoise-sized, rose-veined salt, no visible eyes, moving glacially, smoothing pavement with paste. They are the only thing maintaining this place. Absolute rule: **Do not block, touch, turn over, or feed a Gardener.**

    If one is in your path, wait. It will move. If you step over it, step wide. If you touch it, the paste is mildly caustic to human salt balance and you will leave a chemical insult the mycelium will notice. Overturned ones with hollowed undersides are a warning: something else lives here.

3.  **The Stilt-Walker:** Three-toed, meter-long stride, appears and vanishes mid-step. You will likely only see prints. If you do see it - tall, thin, not quite there - freeze, look down, breathe through your scarf, and do not follow the prints to where they vanish. That vanishing point is a thin spot. Travelers who chase it get the worst distance misbehavior and come back hours late with no memory.

General etiquette: Be a quiet animal. Speak low. Walk soft. Touch as little as possible. Do not carve, do not take. If you must take a sample, take a pinch of loose pavement grit, not a flake from a tower. The towers are alive in the slow way. Taking a piece is like picking a scab off a continent.

### 5. A Sample Itinerary For A Sane First Visit

**Before Jump:** Sleep 8 hours. Hydrate. Empty bowels - you don't want to dig a cat hole in a place where nothing rots. Tell someone outside the city your exact entry time, exit time, and hard abort time. "If I'm not back in 8 hours subjective / 6 hours your time, pull me."

**Entry:** Arrive at the rim, not the center. Don't drop directly into the central plaza. The sudden scale inversion - seeing the whole valley-city look like a model - causes vertigo and panic. Let your eyes adjust for 15 minutes. Let the cool stone air settle.

**Hours 0-1:** Stay near the rim. Acclimate. Do your dust test. Mark your entry thoroughly. Whisper to your partner. Notice your two shadows shift.

**Hours 1-3:** Slow traverse inward. 500 meters feels like 3 kilometers sometimes. That's normal. Keep marking. Visit one district only. Sit in a courtyard that has *normal* gravity and just listen to the organ-hum in the channels. This is the payoff: the feeling of being inside a mineral thought.

**Hours 3-4.5:** Turn back *before* you feel ready. The city induces a "just one more avenue" pull. That's the slip. Leave while you still think clearly.

**Exit:** Climb back down to thicker air. You will feel the press of wet, loud, full reality like a blanket. Many travelers cry without knowing why on descent. That's normal. The quote is right - the empty places are higher. Coming back down is coming back down into noise and weight and life.

**After:** Drink aggressively for 12 hours. Use saline nose spray, eye drops. Your skin will peel slightly. You will dream of hexagonal tiles and soft shadows. Write it all down immediately - memories of the city decay faster than they should. A crack you swore was a hand-span will be a hairline in your photo, if your photo even resolved.

Take no stone home except dust on your boots. Wash them far from any arid ecosystem. Who knows what a lithovore spore wants.

You go there to feel small in a way mountains can't make you feel small. Mountains are big and you are small. The Mineral Town is *empty* and you are full - full of water and noise and right angles and rot - and for a few hours you get to be an anomaly, a walking puddle in a place that has curated dryness for a million years.

Be a respectful anomaly. Drink your water. Watch your dust fall down. And leave before the city blinks and forgets where it put you.
