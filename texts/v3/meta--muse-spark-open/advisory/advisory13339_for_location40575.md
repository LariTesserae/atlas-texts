This place will kill an unprepared Earth human in about 3-6 hours, and it will do it quietly, without malice, and without you ever feeling thirsty. Understand that first.

It is not a hostile world. It is an *incompatible* world. It has solved all the problems you solve with water using static charge and vibration instead. You run on water. You will short-circuit here.

You are visiting the Veiled Table. Here is how you survive it and leave with your mind intact.

### 1. Understand What You're Walking Into

Your sensors will lie to you.

**You are very high up:** This is a continental mesa, kilometers above any sea it might once have had. The air is thin, cold, and chemically wrung out. Atmospheric pressure is low — think high Andes at twilight. You will be short of breath before you do anything else. Every step will feel a little too effortful, every heartbeat a little too loud.

**Erosion 2.6/3 - The Table is Finished:** Don't pack for mountains. There are no cliffs to fall off, no boulders to scramble. Pack for a polished floor the size of a country. The basalt/limestone pavement has been sanded for a billion years into low, smooth whales and shallow, ankle-deep rilles that form tight labyrinths. It's incredibly stable — nothing will collapse under you — but it is treacherous in a different way:

* It's hard as ceramic. It *pings* when you step. A fall at speed will not give. Ankle twists and shattered knees are the #1 injury for travelers.
* It's frictionless when clean. The hematite/salt dust is like fine talc. You will slide.
* The rilles are invisible until you're on top of them. They will catch your foot and trip you face-first into the Lace.

**Water 0.1/3 - Chemically Dry:** This is the lethal part. This is not "desert dry." The Atacama has 10,000x more water. The air, the ground, the plants themselves are *desiccants*. They will actively pull water *out* of you via vapor pressure and electrostatic attraction. You will not sweat — your sweat will sublimate instantly. You will not feel thirsty until you are already cognitively impaired. Your eyes, sinuses, mouth, lungs, and any exposed mucous membrane will begin to desiccate within minutes.

And crucially: **Do not bring open water.** A water bottle, a sweating canteen, a wet towel — in this atmosphere it boils violently, then flash-freezes into a salt that contaminates the Lace. The locals will consider it a chemical spill. The Lace will react badly to it, discharging loudly and potentially burning you with static. Your water must be *inside you or inside a sealed system* at all times.

**Temperature 0.6/3 - Cool, and Light 1.0/3 - Perpetual Gloaming:** It's about 5-10°C / 40-50°F, damp-free cold. No sunburn, no shadows. A dim, pearlescent sun hangs forever at late-afternoon behind a high ice haze that never moves. Your circadian rhythm will collapse in hours. Your eyes will struggle — it's too dim for sunglasses, too uniform for depth perception. The lack of shadow makes distance impossible to judge. Those 15-meter spires that look far away are 30 meters away.

### 2. Your Body: Sealing the Leaky Human

An ordinary human is a bag of salty water that leaks constantly. You have to stop leaking.

**Mandatory Kit - No Exceptions:**

**1. A Full Static-Dissipative Hard-Seal Suit.** Not a desert robe. Not a hiker's shell. You need what lab workers call a "dry cleanroom suit" with integrated boots and gloves.
* Material must be non-triboelectric — plain cotton, silk, or untreated carbon-weave. NO synthetics like nylon, polyester, gore-tex. You will generate thousands of volts just walking, and the Lace is a giant capacitor. In a polyester shell you will arc so hard you can stop your heart, and you will ignite the fine airborne fibers shed by the Lace.
* Every seam must be taped. Cuffs, neck, ankles. The dust is hemotoxic if inhaled and desiccates skin on contact.
* Undersuit: Wear a tight, long-sleeve wicking base layer soaked in a barrier cream — petroleum jelly on all exposed skin, especially hands, face, lips, and nostrils. Reapply every 2 hours. Your lips will crack and bleed within 30 minutes otherwise, and blood is water — see above.

**2. Sealed Eyes, Nose, and Mouth.**
* Full-seal goggles with moisture-retaining inserts. Not sunglasses, not ski goggles with vents. Ventilated goggles will let the dust in and dry your corneas to sandpaper. Bring artificial tears, lots of them, preservative-free vials. Use every 15 minutes.
* A P100 respirator with active humidification or a closed-circuit rebreather. This is the single most important item. The air is full of glass-fine silica fibers shed by the Lace, it is full of black ash from the puff-animals, and it is so dry it will strip your alveoli. A simple N95 will clog and then dry out. You need a respirator that recaptures your exhaled moisture and returns it to you. Breathing through a wet bandana *will not work* — it will dry in minutes and choke you with dust.
* If you use a rebreather, set it to *cool and dry* output. A warm, wet exhale will fog the air around you with charge and make the Lace glow aggressively.

**3. Ears and Brain.** Sound is 2.5/3 LOUD and it never stops.
This is not wind noise. The Lace is an instrument the size of a city — a constant low organ chord in your teeth and a high shivering like wire brushes on cymbals. Travelers report nausea, vertigo, and panic in the first hour because the sound has no source and no pause. Gusts arrive like freight trains hours before you see the light-wave and the volume doubles.
* Bring high-fidelity earplugs or active noise-dampening ear covers that *reduce but don't cancel*. You need to hear warnings — the *ping* of a predator kill, the groan of a swaying spire — but you need to cut the constant 65-75db drone or you will be exhausted in two hours.
* You will hear your own heartbeat amplified through the ceramic ground. That's normal.

**4. Hydration — Internal Only.**
* 3L of water in a sealed bladder with a drinking tube inside your respirator. Sip on a schedule, not on thirst. Add electrolytes. You are losing water through your skin and lungs even in a suit.
* Do not urinate on the ground. Pack it out. Urine is  water and salts — a desecration here, literally.
* Do not bleed here if you can help it. Bring clotting gauze. A cut that would be trivial on Earth will desiccate instantly, crack open, and not heal in this air.

### 3. Moving Through the Lace

Vegetation 3.0/3 here doesn't mean jungle. It means you cannot avoid contact. The plain *is* a single contiguous organism — an openwork lattice of glassy chitin fibers, 80% air, waist-high generally, 10-15m in cathedral groves on the stone whales. It is rigid, brittle, and alive with charge.

**How to Walk:**

* Never bushwhack. Never cut, break, or clear the Lace. That would be like walking through someone's town kicking over walls. The Lace is sentient in the way a reef is sentient — it remembers damage as a dead note in its chord.
* Use the resonant paths — the flat, dense, slightly darker ribbons where the locals have coaxed the Lace to grow like a road. They are smooth, they hum a different, purer tone when the wind crosses them, and they are designed to dissipate your static. If you can't find one, walk in the shallow rilles between the stone whales where the Lace is shortest.
* Shuffle, don't stride. Lift your feet as little as possible. Keep at least one point of contact with the ground to ground yourself. The cilia on every fiber are generating static; if you stomp, you build charge that discharges as a painful snap and makes the Lace flash amber around you — which predators can see.
* When wind comes, CROUCH. The gust-front carries a visible amber wave of triboluminescence. It is beautiful and it is strong enough to knock you over into the lattice. Face away, cover your goggles from dust, and wait. It passes in 2-5 minutes.

**Fauna 0.8/3 Sparse but Lethal if Stupid:**

Density is low — you'll see nothing for 100m — then suddenly everything.

* **Puff-herders:** Black-wire dandelion balls the size of a hare, floating on electrostatic lift. Harmless. Hypnotic. Do NOT touch their tendrils. They are thousands of volts. Locals keep them as kites; you are not local. Watch them with your eyes only. They sing as they comb the air.
* **Tuner Grazers:** Six-legged, barrel-bodied things with transparent bellies and vibrating plates on their heads. They are maintenance workers. They will ignore you unless you stand in front of a growth they are filing. Move away. Their filing vibration will make your teeth ache and can crack inferior goggles.
* **Manta Razors:** Thin as paper, silent, riding the wind. Predators. They cut puffs in half mid-air. You will know one is near because every kill makes a clear *PING* and a fall of black ash. If you hear that, look up and DO NOT JUMP OR WAVE YOUR ARMS. You are not prey — you are too dense and too grounded — but a panicked silhouette waving against the dim sky can draw a curious pass. Crouch and be still. They avoid the resonant paths.

Never feed, never chase, never collect. Everything here is part of the Lace's charge economy. Taking a dead puff's fibers as a souvenir will leave you holding a static charge that won't discharge for hours.

### 4. The Town - Urban 1.5/3, Tech 2.8/3 Beyond Modern

You will not find a downtown. The settlement is spread across the basin in a dozen districts, each centered on a stone whale. Houses are hollow baskets of thickened, living lattice — they glow when you step inside them because your weight stresses the fibers. There are no doors, only tuned throats in the lattice that dilate when the household chord is hummed correctly.

**Etiquette is survival:**

1. **Announce yourself with resonance, not shouting.** Stand at the edge of a district's resonant path and tap two stones together rhythmically or hum low. Do NOT shout. Shouting makes the walls harmonize painfully loud and is considered rude/childish. The locals listen to the wind for hours; they have already heard your suit crinkling from 500m away. Wait to be invited.

2. **Their technology is charge and sound.** You will see no fire, no screens, no metal. Children carry puff-animals in cages that act as batteries. Elders sit on the stone whales listening to weather from the other side of the continent. Light comes from pressure. If they offer you a seat inside a house, move slowly. Your weight will make the floor glow under your feet. That is polite — it means you are acknowledging the house. Stamp and flash and you are being aggressive.

3. **Do not offer water, food, or Earth tech.** They have no use for it and it offends the Lace. If you want to trade, bring: tuning forks, small quartz crystals, smooth river stones (they love Earth stones that have been *wet* — to them it's exotic), or recordings of complex music — Bach fugues are apparently fascinating to them because of the harmonics.

4. **Death is different here.** Nothing rots. When something dies, it dries further and chimes as its charge equalizes, becoming more lattice. If you see a desiccated grazer or even a human body incorporated into the Lace, do not touch it, do not mourn loudly. It is not a corpse to them. It is a completed chord. Nod and move on.

5. **Weirdness 2.5/3 — The physics is unstitched.** Two things you must accept:
   * Triboluminescence is not metaphorical. Friction = light. Every step, every touch, every gust *creates* photons. At night — though there is no real night, just dimmer gloaming — the whole valley breathes amber with traveling waves. It's not dangerous, but if you have epilepsy, this will trigger it. Bring shielding.
   * Static here behaves like a fluid. You will see dust levitate in sheets, hair lift, arcs crawl along spires without thunder. Do not try to "discharge" yourself on a house — you can detune it for hours and make a family sick. Use a grounding rod on the bare stone whales if you must.

### 6. Leaving

Don't stay longer than 8 hours your first time. The sensory deprivation (dim, no shadows, no smell of water/earth) plus sensory overload (constant sound, prickling air) causes a specific dissociation travelers call "Veil Drift" — you forget you have a face because you can't feel your lips anymore.

When you leave, brush yourself off *on the bare stone whale*, not in the Lace. The dust you carry is alive with charge and will contaminate wherever you go next. Leave nothing. Take nothing but memory and maybe a recorded chord.

You stand on the whale's back and the wind comes, and the whole forest-that-is-not-a-forest lights up amber under your feet, and the spires groan, and you feel the chord in your molars — and you finally understand why they call it the Veil. It's not covering anything. It *is* the world, thin as lace, singing to hold itself together in a place that forgot how to rain.

Breathe through your machine. Shuffle softly. Listen more than you look.

And don't lick the rock.
