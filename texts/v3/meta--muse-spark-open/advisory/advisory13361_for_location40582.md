This is not a place that will try to kill you. That would require interest.

The High Peneplain killed everything else a billion years ago and has been perfecting the result since. It will not attack you. It will not chase you. It will let you walk until you make a small, ordinary human mistake — breathe too shallow, drink too little, believe the sky is decoration — and then it will keep being perfect and empty while you stop.

If you are an ordinary human on a multiverse transit, you prepare for the Peneplain the way you prepare for an abandoned cathedral in winter. Not for combat. For exposure, disorientation, and awe-fatigue.

Here is what you need to know in your body before you step through:

### 1. Your Body Will Lie To You On Arrival

**It is high, cold, dry, and bright, all at once, without drama.**

*   **Elevation 2.7/3 + Temp 1.4/3:** Think Tibetan Plateau in late October, forever. About 3,500-4,500m equivalent. Air pressure is ~60-65% sea level. You WILL be hypoxic on arrival. You will feel fine for about 10 minutes while adrenaline covers it, then your head will throb, your thoughts will slow, and simple navigation will feel oddly hard. Your breathing will be too fast and still not enough.
    *   **Prepare:** Pre-acclimatize if your rig allows it. Spend 24 hours at 2,500m equivalent before transit. Bring supplemental O2 - not a full pressure suit, but a low-flow nasal cannula or a rebreather hood you can sip from while walking. Do not try to tough it out. The Peneplain has no slopes to force you to rest; you have to force yourself. Walk at 2/3 your normal speed. Sit every 45 minutes. No sprinting to a Structure. You will faint, and there is nowhere soft to fall except billion-year-old gneiss.
    *   Your breath will be loud. That is normal. The plain is that quiet.

*   **Water 0.5/3 - This is the real killer:** There is NO water. Not frozen, not underground in any reachable way, not in the air. Relative humidity is single digits. The air you exhale is the wettest thing for kilometers. You will lose water twice as fast as you think from respiration alone, and you will not feel sweaty because it evaporates instantly in the cool wind.
    *   **Prepare:** Carry 6-8 liters per 24h, minimum, and pre-hydrate with electrolytes. Your pee should be clear before you step through; you will not have spare water to flush salts later. Bring salty mineral water, not pure distilled - you will be breathing white lithium/bitter-yellow salt dust from the seams. Your lips will crack in two hours without balm. Bring a full-face buff or light respirator - not for oxygen, for moisture retention. It will also keep the ultra-fine salt-playa dust out of your lungs. The needle-plants are silica-coated; don't touch them without gloves. They will micro-cut you and the salt will make it sting for hours.
    *   There is nothing to filter. Nothing to find. If your water runs out, you leave. Period. Do not taste the seam salt. It is not NaCl. It's a desiccant cocktail that will make you vomit and accelerate dehydration.

*   **Light 2.0/3 - Sun-white, knife-shadow:** At elevation with no haze and no dust, UV is brutal. The polished shield-stone reflects it back up at you. You will sunburn under your chin, inside your nostrils, under a baseball cap.
    *   **Prepare:** Glacier glasses or full UV goggles, not sunglasses. SPF 50 on all skin, including ears and under nose. Light, long sleeves with UPF. A brimmed hat. The light is not red or alien, which makes it worse - you will forget it's dangerous because it looks like "normal daylight, but clearer."

*   **Cold:** 1.4/3 means 5-10°C / 40-50°F in full sun, dropping to -5 to -10°C / 15-25°F at night, with wind chill off the dry wind. Sweat never pools, so hypothermia creeps in quietly when you stop moving.
    *   **Prepare:** Layers, not bulk. Merino base, windproof shell, insulated mid-layer for stops. The stone sucks heat fast if you sit on it. Bring a closed-cell pad to sit/nap on. Do not sleep directly on the rock.

### 2. Your Mind Is The Weak Point

**Sound 0.1/3 + Density 0.3/3 + Vegetation 0.8/3**

This is the most psychologically hostile part for an ordinary human, more than the thin air.

The Peneplain is an anechoic chamber the size of a continent. No birds, no buzz, no water, no rustle. When the wind blows it is laminar and high - you SEE it in the sky vortices, you don't hear it on your skin. What you hear is:

Your heartbeat. Your swallow. The click of your knee. The faint fizz of blood in your ears which you will mistake for radio static.

After ~2 hours, most people get auditory hallucinations - a faint voice, a phone buzz, a rattle that isn't the needle-plants. After 6 hours, time stretches. After 12, the silence becomes heavy, like being buried.

And there is nothing to look at to anchor it. Erosion 3.0/3 means no landmarks. The swells are kilometers long, gradients <2°. You cannot tell if that dark Structure is 500 meters or 5 kilometers away. There are no trees for scale. The striders stepping plate-to-plate are perfectly spaced. This is classic desert-peneplain disorientation: you will walk in circles without realizing.

*   **Prepare:**
    1.  **Bring sound:** Not music the whole time - that cuts you off from the one real warning sound (the silica rattle of plants, the click of a strider). But bring one earbud with a podcast, or a small speaker on low, to break the silence every hour. Talk out loud. Narrate. Count steps. Your voice sounding small is better than your brain inventing voices.
    2.  **Bring tether:** GPS may not work - the magnetic chaos aloft [Dynamic 3.0/3] will scramble compasses and cheap units. Bring inertial nav, a good old compass as backup *and* know it may spin near Structures, and most importantly: a physical breadcrumb system. Drop cairns of carried stones (do not pile salt), or use a spool of biodegradable thread, or breadcrumb waypoints on your HUD. Never lose sight of your entry point if you are on a short hop. The horizon looks identical in 360°.
    3.  **Bring a buddy or a check-in:** Do NOT do your first trip alone overnight. Empty places make humans do stupid things just to create an event. The Peneplain averages cubic kilometers per presence - your brain is not built for that. Have a timed return beacon that pulls you back even if you don't want to. Tell your rig to yank you if your O2 sats drop or you stop moving for >30 min.
    4.  **Prepare to feel watched and then stupid:** The weirdness 1.1/3 is uncanny valley. Nothing is overtly alien, so your brain keeps trying to make it normal and failing. Why is the weather furious but rainless? Why do the lichen respect a perfect 1-meter circle around the Structures? You will feel like you are interrupting something. That is accurate. Be polite, be small, don't try to prove anything.

### 3. How To Move

The ground is not sand, not scree. It's polished gneiss tiles the size of houses, separated by salt veins. It is *fantastically* stable and *fantastically* hard on your feet.

*   Boots: Hard sole, ankle support. The plates are canted a few degrees, edges are sharp where salt has recrystallized. Soft trail runners will have you feeling every seam in your arches after 10km.
*   Do not jump seams. They are hand-wide but salt is brittle crust over sometimes hollow dissolution pits. Step *on* the plates, step *over* the seams. Salt mites live there - they are harmless quartz-grain specks that scatter. Let them.
*   Walking is hypnotic. Set a hard turn-around time, not distance. 60% of your water/time out, 40% back, no matter how close that next Structure looks. The undulation hides distance. If you climb a swell you think is 10m high and realize you've been walking uphill for an hour, you are on track.

### 4. The Sky - Look, Don't Engage

**Dynamic 3.0/3 is not for you.**

This is the central rule of the Peneplain: The ground and sky are divorced.

At ground level: still, dry, safe-ish.
At 100m to stratosphere: constant, silent, hyperactive creation-destruction. Anvil clouds boiling from clear air in minutes, vortices visible only by lensing, daylight auroras, sheet lightning with no thunder.

It is beautiful in a way that will make you want to stay until night, and it will do NOTHING to you. No rain will fall on you. No gust will cool you. No lightning will strike. That coupling has been broken for a hundred million years. The atmosphere is being churned by the Structures as a side effect of vacuum-energy farming or angular momentum dumping. You are not part of the circuit.

*   **Prepare:**
    *   Do not try to film it with a drone past ~80m. Updrafts/shears that are invisible and silent will tumble it. Anything that gets into the active layer experiences extreme EM transients. Your drone will come down fried even if it doesn't look windy.
    *   Watch for sun dogs and light-pillars that move wrong. If a clear-air vortex passes overhead, the sun can briefly distort and UV spikes. If your goggles auto-dim and then clear, don't stare up unprotected.
    *   Do not wait for rain. You will die of thirst watching clouds form and die.

Night is incredible - un-twinkling stars like LEDs, Structures dumping heat in dull red. But night is when temperature plummets and navigation becomes impossible. Bring a powerful headlamp AND a low red lamp to preserve night vision. The salt seams exhale cold mineral air that pools in basins - you can see your breath settle.

### 5. The Structures - The Only Real Taboo

**Built 1.1/3 + Tech 2.8/3**

They are low, dark, angular, half-sunken, dust-repelling, lichen-repelling. No doors. No windows. They shiver without moving every few minutes and the sky answers.

You are an ordinary human. You cannot open them, understand them, or use them.

Rules that have kept travelers alive:

1.  **Do not touch.** Not with hand, not with stick, not with suit. The surface is post-physical - not hot, not cold, but wrong. People report vertigo, nausea, or sudden nosebleeds within 2m. The 1-meter lichen exclusion zone is a warning. Respect it. Striders arc around them for a reason.
2.  **Do not camp on/against them.** They dump heat at night (dull red). They distort air by day. Sleep at least 100m away. They are not shelters; they are anchors. You do not want to be the conductive thing between anvil and hammer.
3.  **Do not place anything on them, leave offerings, or try to scan deep.** Passive photos from 5-10m are fine. Active LIDAR, radar, or emitters pointed at them sometimes trigger a stronger sky response directly overhead. It won't hit you, but your electronics will hate you.
4.  **Count them, don't collect.** Each one is per horizon by design. If you see two close together, note it, log it; don't try to triangulate a pattern on foot. Orbital view is for understanding; ground view is for witnessing.

They are not ruins. They are working. You are a gnat in the factory.

If you must: sit 10-20m away, back to wind, and watch one cycle. A shiver, a heat haze, a cloud-wall erupting 5km up in utter silence. Feel how old this arrangement is. Then get up and leave it alone.

### 6. Practical Pack List For Ordinary Human, 24h Hop

*   Water: 8L + 2L emergency cache at entry point
*   Electrolytes, salty snacks, 2000 kcal easy food
*   Supplemental O2: 1 small bottle + cannula
*   UV goggles, brim hat, buff/neck gaiter, SPF, lip balm
*   Merino base, wind shell, puffy, gloves
*   Hard-sole boots, gaiters to keep salt out
*   Closed-cell sitting pad - also for emergency bivvy
*   Navigation: inertial + compass + physical markers + return beacon with dead-man timer
*   Comms: satellite messenger that logs via inertial, not just GPS
*   Sound: one earbud + loaded audio
*   Light: 300+ lumen headlamp + red mode
*   Trekking pole - saves knees on endless gentle swells and tests salt crust
*   Small trauma kit + blister kit (feet will get weird hot spots from hard plates)

Leave behind: umbrellas, water filters, rain gear, heavy climbing gear, drones you love, expectations of being spoken to.

### 7. How To Leave Feeling Human

You will come back too quiet. Your companion will say you are loud when you talk because you got used to hearing your joints. Your sense of scale will be off for a day - doorways will feel too tall, hills too steep.

Debrief: Drink water even if not thirsty. Eat salt. Sit in a place with leaves and let birdsong recalibrate you. Tell someone what you saw.

Do not try to explain the wrongness. Everyone says the same thing: "The ground is finished. The sky is busy. They don't touch." That's the whole koan.

If you go again, go shorter. The Peneplain does not reward endurance. It rewards attention. One day, one Structure, one silent aurora at noon, one needle-plant rattling like a warning - that's a full pilgrimage.

You are small, cold, and very loud in your suit, in a place that has become perfect at being empty.

Prepare so you can be loud for a little while, and then go home while you still want to make noise.
