This is a Kiln Pocket. You need to prepare for it like you would prepare to briefly visit the inside of an engine - not a cave, not a wild place.

It's deceptively survivable for about 10 minutes, and absolutely not survivable if you treat it like a normal mountain cave. Here's how an ordinary human gets in and out without injury.

### 1. Understand What It Actually Is

You're not going hiking. You're doing a clean-room insertion into a biological factory.

**Outside:** High alpine on a young, geologically active mountain. Raw rock, scree, unstable, sharp, unfinished by wind or water. Air is thin, cold, bright, silent. Altitude sickness is real. Rockfall is real. The entrance is a random stress-fracture crack, shoulder-width, breathing hot air. You may have to scramble and squeeze. There is no trail.

**Inside, in less than one meter you transition to:**

*   **Hot:** Not sun-hot. Kiln-hot. Geothermal, dry, radiant heat from the walls themselves. Think 55-65C / 130-150F air temperature, with contact surfaces hotter. Like opening an industrial oven.
*   **Dark:** 0.5/3 light. The walls glow dull iron-red/black from retained geothermal heat, like banked coals. You can see shapes and movement, but you cannot see true color or detail without help. Your eyes will never fully adapt because there is no real light source.
*   **Dry:** 0.4/3 water. This is the killer. It's a chemical desert. Relative humidity near zero. The air actively pulls water out of you - eyes, mouth, lungs, skin. There is no liquid water. What looks like moisture is hot mineral salts and sulfur compounds sublimating from hot wall to cold wall. It will crack your lips in minutes.
*   **Loud & Dense:** 2.9/3 density, 2.4/3 sound, 0.8/3 dynamic. Thousands of thumb-sized, eyeless, slag-black beetle-things tile every surface edge-to-edge. Each one is a tiny slow furnace: *tik - step - hiss - vent - THUNK - chip rock - extrude hot glassy pellet.* Individually quiet. Together, in a 4x3 meter stone lung, it's a deafening, chest-rattling, asynchronous industrial roar. Nothing is fast, but nothing ever stops.

Weirdness is low. This place isn't magical. It makes perfect ecological sense. That makes it more dangerous, because it won't warn you that you're out of place. The beetles don't care about you at all.

### 2. Your Kit - What to Wear and Bring

You are dressing for the outside and the inside at the same time. Layer for industrial heat protection.

**On your body:**

1.  **Base for the Mountain:** Sturdy approach boots with hard toe caps, ankle support, and thick soles. The grus floor inside is sharp, angular, unweathered mineral sand and hot slag pellets. The rock outside will cut you. Gaiters help. Bring trekking poles for the approach, leave them outside the crack.

2.  **For the Kiln - No Exposed Skin:** Long sleeves, long pants, no synthetics that can melt. Heavy cotton, canvas, or better - light welder's gear / fire-resistant workwear. The air is full of fine, hot, acrid dust and their exhaust steam smells of ozone and hot iron. It will irritate lungs, eyes, and skin instantly.

3.  **Hands:** Thick leather work gloves. You WILL need to touch hot rock to squeeze through the crack. The beetles will walk over your boots and gloves. They test surfaces with glowing-hot mandibles. Leather they reject; skin they will burn/blister trying. Never go bare-handed.

4.  **Eyes:** Sealed, vented safety goggles or ski goggles. Non-negotiable. The air is so dry your eyes will dry to blurriness in 2-3 minutes without them. It also keeps hot micro-pellets and salt dust out. Don't bring a full face respirator with a huge visor if it limits your squeeze through the crack - low-profile is better.

5.  **Lungs & Nose:** A P100 half-face respirator with organic vapor/acid gas cartridges *or* at minimum a well-fitted N95/P3 with activated carbon layer, plus a bandana/neck gaiter soaked in water underneath. The beetles are not spraying poison, but thousands of tiny furnaces venting hot, sulfurous, metallic steam in a sealed 12sqm room will quickly give you a headache, nausea, and chemical bronchitis. The respirator also humidifies the air slightly. You will sound like Darth Vader, which is appropriate. This is also your sound filter.

6.  **Ears:** Foam earplugs UNDER your beanie/hood. The sound is not loud enough to instantly damage hearing, but it's resonant, inescapable, and conducted through the rock into your bones. Without plugs, you'll leave disoriented, with a ringing skull and inability to think. With plugs, it becomes a dull throb you can tolerate.

**In your pockets:**

*   **Light:** One small, dim, red headlamp on the lowest setting, with a strap you can pull down around your neck. Do NOT bring a bright white caving light. You don't need it - you'll ruin your night vision and wash out the beautiful, terrible wall-glow that lets you see. You only need red light for: checking your footing in the pellet-deep floor, checking your watch, and finding the crack to get out. The beetles are eyeless; light doesn't bother them, but your own eyes will thank you for not blasting them.
*   **Water:** 1L on your person for the approach. **Drink 500ml before you go in.** You will not drink inside - you can't remove your respirator. Plan to lose ~300-500ml of water to sweat and breathing in 10 minutes inside. Bring electrolyte salts for after.
*   **Timepiece:** A watch with a vibrating timer. You will lose time sense from heat and noise. Set it for 7 minutes.
*   **Nothing else.** No backpack inside - you won't fit and you'll scrape beetles off the walls. No walking stick. No sample jars. No food.

### 3. How to Do The Visit

**Before the Crack:**
Acclimatize to altitude for a day if you can. Eat well, hydrate aggressively the day before. Cold alpine air is already dehydrating. Sit at the crack. Feel the hot, dry breath coming out. Put on all your kiln gear *now* - goggles, respirator, plugs, gloves, hood. Turn your red light on. Tell your partner outside you're going in: "7 minutes."

**The Squeeze:**
Go sideways, exhale. The stone is sharp, new, unweathered, and warm - almost hot. Don't rush. Don't inhale deeply right as you enter; let your respirator catch the first blast of hot mineral air. It will feel like opening a clothes dryer full of hot pennies.

**Inside - The First 60 Seconds:**
Crouch. Don't stand upright - the ceiling is low and covered. Let your eyes adjust to the coal-glow. You will see the walls *boil* then resolve: a living pavement of flat black carapaces, edge to edge, backs venting little puffs of steam, guts glowing through seams as they smelt rock. The floor is inches deep in their waste - perfect, hot, glassy black beads that tick and crackle as they cool. Don't kick them.

The sound will hit you even through plugs: *tik-hiss-thunk, tik-hiss-thunk* thousands out of sync into one low industrial roar. You feel it in your teeth.

**Rules While Inside:**

1.  **TIME IS YOUR ONLY LIMIT. 5-10 minutes max.** Not because of oxygen - because of heat and drying. At ~5 minutes your mouth will be paste, your eyes will ache even behind goggles, your heart rate will climb. At ~10 minutes you risk heat exhaustion and your judgment slips. When your watch vibrates, leave. No "one more minute." The pocket doesn't change, and it will still be here next time. Your water won't be.

2.  **Do not touch the walls, and do not try to clear a space.** The beetles are not aggressive, but they are relentless and incredibly slow. They are Dynamic 0.8 - patient as erosion. If you stand still they will tile over your boots and test them. Let them. Stand like a warm boulder. Do not brush them off violently, do not try to pick one up - you will burn your fingers on its furnace-gut, you will damage its carapace, and the disturbance will just make more of them reroute over you. They are performing erosion. You are a temporary warm rock to them. Be a good rock.

3.  **Move like in slow motion.** The floor pellets are loose and hot, the grus is sharp. Shuffle, don't step. Keep your hands tucked. Every movement displaces dozens of workers who will just slowly, *tiss-tiss-tiss*, walk back to fill the gap. There is no path.

4.  **Do not bring water in to pour, do not lick the wall.** The "damp" crust is not water - it's bitter, caustic mineral salt efflorescence. Tasting it will nauseate you and waste saliva. Pouring your drinking water just creates instant caustic steam and confuses the chemical cycle for nothing.

5.  **Built: NONE. Tech: INDUSTRIAL.** Understand this going in so you don't look for the wrong things. There is no nest to find, no queen to photograph, no honey or silk or architecture. The intelligence is in the process itself: intake basalt -> internal furnace -> extrude pellet -> step forward one body length per hour, keeping the chamber from closing as the young mountain tries to heal shut. They *are* the building. Appreciate it as a factory floor, not a hive.

**The Exit:**
This is the best part and the part that will stay with you. Back out the way you came, slowly. Don't turn your back on the crack. As you squeeze back into the cold, thin, bright alpine air, the contrast is violent: silent, windy, huge, cold, and color comes rushing back. The roar will stay in your ears for an hour after you're out.

### 4. Aftercare

Sit outside for at least 15 minutes. Do not rip your gear off immediately - let your body cool.

1. Remove earplugs last; expect muffled hearing and a phantom *tik-hiss-thunk* for a while.
2. Remove respirator, breathe cold clean air slowly. Blow your nose - it will be black with hot iron soot. That's normal and why you wore it.
3. Remove goggles, blink deliberately. Use artificial tears/saline drops if you have them. Your eyes will be red and dry.
4. Drink water with electrolytes slowly, not all at once. Eat something salty.
5. Check boots and gloves for soot prints and a few stray hot pellets that hitched a ride. Leave them outside to cool. Do not bring pellets home as souvenirs - they are still chemically active and hot enough to melt plastic. And more importantly, the pocket needs them. The floor level is part of how it regulates its size.

You will be mildly lightheaded, very thirsty, and strangely calm. The pocket is not hostile. It just has no idea what to do with a large, wet, fast, cold mammal that breathes water.

Go in humble, as a visitor to a workshop that was running long before you were born and will keep running, *tik-hiss-thunk*, in the dark, after you seal the crack behind you.
