This place doesn't want to kill you.

That's the first thing you have to understand about the Cistern, because everything else about it will make you *think* it does. And places that want to kill you are easier to prepare for.

The Cistern wants to *keep* you. To root you, to slow you down until your pace matches its pace, to wrap you gently in green and warm water and leave you there as a new texture.

It's a trap, but not a predator's trap. It's a greenhouse's trap.

Here is how you prepare to walk into the room that thinks it's a mountain:

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Visiting

You are not going to a wilderness. You are going to an **abandoned factory that has been successfully colonized.**

Everything about the scale here is lying to you:

* The climb up the stalk will tell your body you're ascending a mountain. Your lungs, your vertigo, the cold wind off the cloud deck, the sheer drop on all sides - all mountain cues. Ignore them. There is no mountain. There is one piece of infrastructure, very new, very sharp, standing alone. That matters psychologically: there is no rescue slope, no lower trail, no tree line to rest in. If you fall, you don't roll. You just fall until the clouds take you. The stalk is the *only* terrain.
* The inside will tell your body you're diving deep underwater. Warmth, pressure on your ears, muffled sound, floating debris. Ignore that too. There is no ocean. There is a box, 40 meters a side, with a concrete ceiling two stories over your head. You could swim from any wall to any other wall in 30 seconds if the vines weren't in the way. It *feels* abyssal. It is actually claustrophobic. That dissonance - infinite-feeling ocean inside a small, comprehensible room - will break your sense of space if you let it.

Your job is to hold both truths at once: it is vast and dense with life, and it is small and entirely built. A terrarium the size of a church, hung in the sky.

Do not go in thinking like an explorer or a diver. Think like a maintenance worker who has come back after a century of neglect to check on the vat.

### 2. Your Body Will Be Confused. Prepare It.

An ordinary human cannot breathe warm water. And yet this place is described as "submerged" with no surface - water to ceiling-tile. Previous travelers report different things: some say they were swimming and holding their breath, some say the water became breathable after the first panic, like amniotic fluid.

Assume the worst and most literal version is true.

**Breathe:**
Bring a full-face closed-circuit rebreather, not a SCUBA tank. You need something that leaves your mouth free to talk, that won't flood if you flip upside-down, and that recycles your bubbles - because your big exhalations will get tangled in the leaves and disturb nothing, and you will need to hear the fizz.
Before you fully commit, do a threshold test: open the inner hatch only halfway, extend one arm into the Cistern-water and watch. Does it bead, flow, resist? Put your face in. Inhale a sip through your regulator. If you cough, you have your answer: you are a visitor who must carry their air.
If the water *does* let you breathe - if you find you can pull that warm, faintly sweet, leaf-perfumed fluid into your lungs without choking - don't trust it. Your brain will scream *drowning* for at least 20 minutes anyway. Practice slow, diaphragmatic breathing before you go so the panic reflex doesn't burn all your oxygen.

**Buoyancy & Orientation:**
There is no up. Gravity is a suggestion there, enforced only by those three huge screws in the floor turning once per minute.
You *will* get vestibular sickness. Bring: 
1. A weighted tether line. Clip one end to the hatch ladder where you entered. Never let go of it. It's not to stop you from falling - there's nowhere to fall far - it's to remember where DOWN is when every vine is growing sideways.
2. Wrist weights. Without them you'll bob neutrally and waste energy flailing. Being slightly negative will let you "walk" on whatever is below you.
3. A caged dive light, even though it's bright at noon. When the sun arcs low, the whole box becomes amber gloom, and every leaf throws shadow on every other leaf. You'll lose your hatch in 4 meters of philodendron doors. Light it, flag it, never lose sight of it.

**Touch & Entanglement:**
You cannot extend an arm without touching five textures. That means you *will* be touched constantly, brushed, snagged.
- Wear a smooth skin: a 3mm closed-cell wetsuit, even though the water is bath-warm. Not for warmth - for protection against chemical irritation. This water is thick with oxygen exudate, dissolved organics, fine root hairs. People come out with rashes that look like leaf-veins. Cover your hands, neck, hair. The water will try to soften you.
- Gloves are mandatory for the stalk climb anyway - those riveted plates are new, unweathered, sharp. But inside, switch to tight neoprene tips. The kelp-vines use conveyor chains as trellises and then let go; they will happily use your wrist strap, your buckle, your hair.
- Bring blunt-tipped shears, not a knife. You will need to cut yourself free eventually, and you will be tempted to hack beautifully. Don't. Cut clean, cut minimal, apologize. The jungle notices damage - when you tear a large frond, it releases a cloud of viscous sap that hangs in front of you for hours like a curtain.

**Time:**
Prepare to be slowed. This is the main danger.
Your regulator will tick. The pumps thrum once per minute. Bubbles wander centimeters per hour. A tendril grows a finger-width in an afternoon.
Human nervous systems hate this. After 15 minutes of no real sound or movement, you'll start making movement just to prove you can. Thrashing, kicking, hurrying. That burns air and tears plants and exhausts you.
Go in with a clock you can feel, not just see. A haptic metronome set to 60 bpm under your suit, against your chest. It will anchor you to human time.

### 3. Your Mind Will Be More Vulnerable Than Your Lungs

There are no animals in there. Hold that absence in your head before you go.

Every human instinct for forest reads "lush = alive with things." Birds, insects, fish, rustles. Evolution trained you to parse a jungle by listening to what moves through it. Here... nothing moves. No eyes. No gnat in the air pockets. Only plants watching you with total vegetal attention, expanding into space where nothing eats them, nothing pollinates them. Just endless, patient self-cloning. Flowers that never open and become heavy green fruit that becomes more leaves.

You will feel a very specific dread: *the loneliness of being the only animal.* Like you walked onstage in the wrong play. Survivors all describe the same sudden shame - that your breathing sounds too loud, your heartbeat sounds carnivorous.

Prepare for that silence:
- Don't go in trying to be quiet. The place is already too quiet. Your attempt to whisper will make it worse.
- Go in with a single human sound you allow yourself. Hum quietly on exhale. Recite a list. Count root types. Give your auditory cortex something that is yours.
- Do not personify the attention too much. Yes, it feels watched. No, there is probably not a central intelligence. It's the gaze of a thousand square meters of photosynthetic surface doing nothing but existing, extremely efficiently. It's unsettling, not hostile. Remind yourself: you are irrelevant to it, which is safer than being relevant.

The other psychological risk is the **false comfort.** Warm, clear, golden, silent, womb-like. After the cold terror of the exposed stalk climb, slipping into that warm water that "takes" you feels like relief. Like being allowed to finally rest. Many travelers want to nap immediately, suspended among the vines near the sun. Do not. Warm still water + muted light = narcosis-lite. You will lose track.

Set a hard turn-back time *before* you enter. 45 minutes max for a first visit. Assign someone else - or an alarm you can't snooze with wet hands - to pull you.

### 4. Protocols For Inside

1. **Enter with empty hands and empty lungs.** Exhale, equalize early. Push through the top leaves gently, like pushing through a curtain, not parting kelp. Let the tether spool behind you.

2. **Do not orient by light or gravity.** Orient by texture. The factory still has logic under the green: floor = poured concrete with iron tie-downs. Ceiling = pipes and cranes and sprinkler heads. Walls = tiered catwalks. Keep one of those in contact at all times. If you find yourself floating mid-column surrounded only by hanging roots, you've drifted. Follow tether back to structure.

3. **Do not drink or sample.** Not the water, not the fruit. The heavy green fruit looks inviting, looks like figs or breadfruit, but it splits into more leaves, not flesh. Nothing here evolved to be eaten by you. The water chemistry is wrong - no surface tension, places of "less-water" that hang like glass ghosts. If you get that on your bare skin it feels like cool nothing. Avoid it.

4. **Watch for air pockets, but don't trust them.** Under inverted machinery there are trapped bubbles, silver domes that have been there for years. They are not fresh air. They are pure photosynthetic oxygen, possibly supersaturated. One breath might make you dizzy, euphoric, then convulsant. Test with your O2 sensor if you must, but safest to leave them.

5. **Leave no trace, but also: do not try to clean it.** You might see a beautiful electrical cabinet swallowed by monstera and think "clear it for study." Don't. That is its substrate now. It roots in rust and paint. The moment you scrape moss off a pipe, you create a wound that will fill with white root-hairs within hours. And you'll have destroyed orientation markers for the next traveler.

### 5. How To Leave (The Important Part)

Leaving is harder than entering, because after half an hour you won't be sure which way is down.

Follow light and structure back to ceiling, not to brightness. The hatch is a dark rectangle against tiles and pipes. Your tether knows where it is.

Ascend slowly. The water will cling. At the hatch, you will transition from water straight to air with no meniscus, no interval - your face will pop from warm bath to cool tower air like passing through a door made of physics error. Expect vertigo, expect nausea. Have a hand on steel.

Once outside, sitting on the catwalk above the Cistern, you will be soaking wet and strangely cold despite the warm water, because the wind at that altitude is real. Wrap, breathe real air for a few minutes, look down at the white moving below and remind yourself you are on a stalk of fresh iron, not a mountain.

Do not look back too long. By late afternoon the whole box turns into a lantern, amber light diffused through four meters of leaves and perfumed water. It is unbearably beautiful from outside, especially knowing how empty it is inside.

And you will want to go back in. That's its last trick. The feeling that it wasn't finished showing you something, that you missed the hidden fish that maybe lives there, that if you just stayed a little longer you'd understand why it was built, why it keeps remaking itself so youthfully, what it was supposed to produce before it decided to produce only itself.

You won't understand. It's not a mystery box. It's a factory that discovered it could grow leaves faster than widgets, and now it does.

You visit to witness how good plants are at being plants when we remove everything that stops them.

### Pack List For The Ordinary Human

For the climb: alpine gloves, wind shell, tether + harness, magnetic boots if you can get them
For the swim: rebreather (or dual-breath setup), 3mm smooth wetsuit, neoprene hood, blunt shears, wrist weights, caged light, haptic timer, O2 sensor, drybag on the catwalk with hot sweet tea
For the mind: earworm song, exit time, promise to come back

Go gently. Brush five textures per reach. Listen for the faint fizz of leaves making silver beads. Feel the once-a-minute thrum in your chest and match your heartbeat to it for a little while, then reclaim your own pace and leave before the water convinces you that slowness was your idea all along.
