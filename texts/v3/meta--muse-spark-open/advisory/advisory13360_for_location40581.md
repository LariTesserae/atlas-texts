This is not a hellworld. It's worse — it's a patient world. It won't try to kill you quickly. It will try to *include* you in its slow manufacturing process, and you are not made of the right materials.

You found the description of a Chrysalis Forge — I call them that. There are thousands of variants across the multiverse: sealed, embryonic pockets where a world is still deciding how to be a world. Yours is a hot, dry, mineral one. 60-70C, no water, thin/thick gravity, weak time, proto-industrial geology. It's beautiful. It's also profoundly incompatible with baseline human biology and causality.

An ordinary human can visit. But you do not go in hiking gear and curiosity. You go in like a diver visiting a reef made of lava and bad clocks.

Here is how you prepare:

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Visiting

Stop thinking "cave with weird plants."

Think:
* **A kiln that is also a womb.** The air is 60-70C on the *floor*. The ceiling and vents will be hotter. This is air temperature where your proteins begin to denature if you stay too long. You will not sweat effectively because the humidity is essentially zero — it's not even dry, it's *pre-water*. Your sweat will flash off and leave your salts behind as crust. You will overheat and desiccate simultaneously without ever feeling properly wet.
* **A cleanroom factory with no workers.** The basalt pistons, gear-geodes, and lava-looms aren't decoration. They are slow engines running on planetary expansion and geothermal charge. They are not safe to touch, climb, or interrupt. They operate on decade-long cycles. If you get a limb caught in a piston interface that only moves once every 20 minutes, it will not stop for you. It will polish you down.
* **An unfinished piece of physics.** This is the real danger. The light-through-rock, the double-exposed pistons, the dust that hangs too long then drops like lead, the sevenfold receding fern-frond — that's weak, undetermined reality. Time and space have not fully crystallized here. Your brain *runs* on consistent causality. Stay too long and you'll get reality-sickness: stuttering continuity, afterimages that gain mass, memory loops, proprioception failure. That feeling of "I might leave a translucent copy of myself kneeling" is not poetry. It's prodrome.

Rule One: **You are a tourist in a place that is still rendering. Do not try to render with it.**

### 2. The Suit — You Will Not Survive In Street Clothes

You need to be sealed and cooled. This is non-negotiable for an ordinary human.

**Thermal Control:**
* A full active-cooling suit under a white reflective overlayer. Not a survival suit, a *work* suit — like a foundry suit or astronaut EVA cooling garment. Peltier-cooled or water-circulated vest + leggings at minimum. Without it, your core temperature will hit 40C in under 15 minutes. Heatstroke, confusion, collapse.
* You need 2-3 hours of battery/coolant. Plan to be inside for 60-90 minutes maximum. Set an audible timer. You will lose track of time naturally inside.
* Thick, insulating boot soles. The floor is salt-ash over hot basalt. It's an oven floor. Ordinary hiking boots will soak heat straight through.

**Air and Eyes:**
* Full-face respirator or rebreather with sealed goggles. Not just for heat. That "fine as ash, bone-dry" mineral powder that hangs for minutes — it's micronized silica, salts, sulfur compounds, heavy-metal oxides. You are breathing ground glass and ozone. One deep breath without a filter will sandpaper your alveoli and leave you coughing blood. A P100 elastomeric respirator with particulate + organic vapor filters is minimum. A closed-circuit rebreather with cool, humidified oxygen is ideal.
* Sealed goggles / visor. At that heat and zero humidity, your corneas will dry in minutes. Your contact lenses will fuse. Bring artificial tears, but don't rely on them — keep your eyes behind a visor.
* Nose and mouth will still feel like inhaling from a hair dryer. Humidified air from a rebreather fixes this. Otherwise, you will get nosebleeds within minutes.

**Hydration:**
* Bring water, but do not expect to fix dehydration by drinking inside. You are losing water through every breath. Bring at least 2 liters of isotonic electrolyte water on your person, with a straw through your respirator. Pre-hydrate aggressively for 24 hours before. You will not be able to drink enough inside to keep up — the goal is to delay collapse until you exit.
* No exposed skin. The air will burn any wet skin. Cover everything with breathable, wicking base layer.

Leave anything that can be charged by static at home. No loose synthetic clothes that generate sparks. Those silent leaf-to-stone arcs? In dry air that hot, a build-up can discharge through *you*. Don't touch the copper-green fern fronds or the metal-rosettes. That "hot as a stove lid" leaf holds both thermal and capacitive charge. It will sear and shock you at once.

### 3. Time and Gravity Anchors

Your ordinary human senses of time and balance will lie to you. You need external references.

* **Two chronometers: one mechanical, one digital, one outside.** Wear a simple wind-up watch and a cheap digital timer. Leave your phone / a third clock with someone outside, or just outside the blister, recording video. When you come out, compare. If they have diverged by more than a few seconds per hour you've been stuttering. That's your cue to leave and not return for days.
* **Tether.** A real, bright, un-kinkable cord tied to a piton/boulder *outside* the willing rock. Not because you'll get lost — the pocket is only 40 meters long — but because your sense of distance will warp near the far end. Dust hangs, light angles wrong, repeating images into infinity. A tether that you can follow hand-over-hand back to the exit is the only thing that stays Euclidean.
* **Exit window plan:** The rock is only "willing" at noon, when the sun is vertical and the light is pitiless. That means entry and exit are time-gated. Miss the window, and you are sealed in a 60-70C oven with no water until tomorrow's noon. Test the exit *immediately* after entering. Press your hand to the inside wall where you came through. Feel if it's still willing. If it's not, get to it and wait. Do not go to the far end first.

Bring a small, dim, warm flashlight on your tether line, even though the dome provides "milky-white" daylight. You need a light source you control to check shadow angles. If your flashlight shadow disagrees with the dome light by more than ~10 degrees, you are in a shear zone. Step back.

### 4. How To Move Inside

* **Breathe slow and shallow.** Every deep breath is heat and dust. Practice box-breathing before you go. The loudest thing is your breathing because sound is suppressed — you will hear it inside your teeth and skull. This is normal. Don't panic.
* **Kneel immediately when you enter.** Don't walk around. Kneel on your insulated pad, feel gravity. Drop a pinch of dust and watch how it falls. Does it float? Drop like lead? Use that to calibrate. Walk as if on a boat — slow, knees bent. Do not run. A gravity surge while running will snap an ankle on those basalt pistons.
* **Keep distance.** The plants are displayed with great emptiness between them for a reason. That emptiness is their growth envelope, charge field, and heat-exchange zone. Don't weave between close fronds. Stay on the visible salt-ash paths. Admire from 2 meters away.
* **Do not touch anything hot, glowing, humming, ticking, or strumming.** I know it's tempting. The hammered-metal rosettes look solid. They are 80-100C storage organs. The basalt loom *thrum* you feel in your teeth is a good sign to back away.
* **Time-box your awe.** The most dangerous part is "the far end where the ceiling dips lowest and the heat is greatest." That shimmering repetition, the rock bubbling into existence, the fern reflected seven times into a point that never arrives — that's pre-formation in real-time. That's raw ontological noise. Humans find it hypnotic and profound. Go there *last*, go there briefly (<5 minutes), and do NOT kneel and stare as the description invites you to. The longer you watch slow fission, the more your own continuity needs to match its rhythm to comprehend it. That's when you start to stutter. Set your timer to buzz and *obey it* even if you feel you're "just about to understand."

Bring no instruments you care about losing. Cameras will show double-exposures, shadow errors, slight blurring — not because they're broken, but because they are correctly capturing an incorrect space. Take photos quickly and put the camera away.

### 5. How It Feels, So You Don't Freak Out

If you are prepared, this is what visiting is actually like:

The first minute, you will think your suit failed. The heat will press on your face even through the visor. Your eyes will want to water and can't. Your ears will pop from the sealed air pressure.

Then quiet. Profound, greenhouse, kiln-quiet. The tick... ping... sigh... thrum... will not be a soundscape. It will be isolated events with huge silences between, so your brain invents sounds to fill in. You will hear your heartbeat amplified.

The copper-green fronds will not move with wind, but will tick a millimeter while you stare. You will feel watched not by eyes, but by *intention* — the curated emptiness of the workshop makes you hyper-aware of each object.

Your skin will prickle. Your sense of "now" will feel slightly stretched, like deja vu without a memory. Objects will feel too sharp-edged, as if they have too much definition. That's the thin reality. It's not harmful in small doses, but recognize it: **That's your cue that you are a foreign object.** You are not being rejected violently. You are being ignored by physics that hasn't decided to include you.

Leave while it still feels strange. If it starts to feel *normal*, you've been there too long. If you start to see your own hands leave faint afterimages, or if the dust seems to settle *upwards*, you are overdue.

### 6. Exit and Aftercare

* Exit before the light angles. When the dome light stops being vertical, the rock will begin to set. Do not push it. Slip out the same way — palm flat, noon light on your back, let it be willing. If it resists, don't force. Wait, breathe cool humidified air, stay tethered near the exit wall.
* Outside, the wind — that hot dry knife — will feel *cool* by comparison. It will be a shock. Sit in shade. Do not rip your respirator off immediately — you'll inhale a huge gulp of cold dry mountain air and cough. Power down suit cooling gradually.
* Drink slowly, with electrolytes. Take a lukewarm — not cold — shower if possible to wash off mineral powder before it abrades your skin further. The powder will be on everything, a dark film. Do not rub your eyes.
* Journal *after* 30 minutes out, not inside. Your inside notes will be time-skewed. Compare clocks. Expect a mild hangover: headache, slow time perception for a few hours, vivid dreams of gears and ferns. If you have persistent double-vision, tinnitus that pulses every 20 minutes, or feel like you're in two places at once for more than a day, you need a healer who understands ontological exposure — in ordinary terms, rest, sensory deprivation, good sleep, and no more multiverse travel for at least a week.

Take nothing. Not a glass-seed, not a gear-tooth, not a spoonful of salt-ash. The glass-seed glows for a day before rooting into hot stone — in your cool, wet, low-charge pocket it will crack and release superheated wax and mineral vapor. The factory-geometry wants to stay in the factory.

You found it by accident. Leave it by intention.

It's a lush desert, a factory with no builder, endlessly making itself. You're allowed to witness. You are not needed to help.

And the rock will still be willing behind you — but only if you come back with respect, at noon, in normal light, ready to leave.
