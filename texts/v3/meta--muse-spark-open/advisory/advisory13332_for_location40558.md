For The City After the Canopy, you are not preparing for a hostile world. You're preparing for an *indifferent* one.

That's more dangerous. Nothing there wants to kill you. It just wasn't built for you, it doesn't need you, it won't notice you, and it will grow over you if you stand still long enough. This is a young, warm, low-gravity-feeling, non-Euclidean greenhouse the size of a small country, maintained by an AI god-lattice for a population that left.

An ordinary human can visit. An ordinary human can even be comfortable there. But you have to unlearn Earth city survival and think like a caver / jungle biologist / acoustic engineer at the same time.

Here's how.

### 1. Understand What Will Actually Get You

**It's not monsters. It's these four things:**

**1. Getting Lost in Folded Space:** The buildings are larger inside than outside. Streets take longer one way than the other. Your inner ear and your sense of distance will lie to you. GPS will not work - there are no satellites, the Canopy interferes. Compasses may drift near the basalt. People don't die here from attacks. They die because they walked 200 meters into a housing stack to look around and spent three days finding the door again.

**2. Sensory Overload / Deprivation:** The sound is 75-85 dB *constant*, 24/7. Like standing next to a freeway made of insects and frogs. You will get tinnitus, headaches, and you won't be able to hear a travel partner, a warning creak, or a fall. The light never changes. No shadows, no sunset, no clock. 6PM amber forever at ~200-400 lux - enough to read by, not enough to feel awake. Your circadian rhythm will collapse in 36 hours.

**3. Rapid Biological Accretion:** "Nothing erodes, everything accretes" is literal. This jungle grows at 10x-50x Earth speed when the Canopy spotlights it. Cuts get infected fast. Gear left on damp basalt will have rootlets in it overnight. Pollen load in the air is insane. Mold will be in your lungs by day two if you're unmasked.

**4. Terrain Trauma:** Those beautiful fault-steps are 1-4m sheer drops, often with wet moss and hidden fissure-canals. The basalt is sharp, glassy, fractured. A twisted ankle 4km down an empty 8-lane avenue with no one to hear you is a death sentence. The water looks still and drinkable. It is not - it's a stagnant cistern full of amphibian eggs, insect larvae the size of thumbs, and whatever the Canopy is seeding.

### 2. Body Prep - Before You Go

**You need to pre-acclimatize for heat/humidity:**
For 4-5 days before, work in 28-30°C / 82-86°F at 85-95% humidity if you can. This place will sit at 27-29°C with near 100% humidity after a mist cycle. You will sweat and it will not evaporate. Heat exhaustion hits fast because you don't feel thirsty in the mist.

* Hydrate aggressively with electrolytes, not just water. Bring 2-3x more salts than you think.
* Test any asthma/allergies. If you have severe pollen/mold allergies, **do not go** without a full respirator. This city is one giant sporulating lung.
* Get your tetanus, doxy/fluconazole prophylaxis talk with a doctor. Every scrape will try to grow something.
* Sleep bank. You will not sleep well there. Arrive rested.

**Fitness:** You don't need to be an athlete, you need to be able to do slow, careful, long-distance walking on uneven, hard stone. 10-15km a day at a shuffle. Your feet and knees will take it hard on basalt.

### 3. Gear - The Non-Negotiable Kit

Think cave exploration + tropical field biology.

**Clothing:**
* **No cotton. Ever.** It will stay wet and rot. Full-skin coverage in lightweight, quick-dry nylon/merino. Long sleeves, long pants tucked into socks. The insects aren't Earth mosquitoes but the forearm-long glass dragonflies will slice you if they panic, and the hanging curtains shed irritant fibers.
* **Waterproof, breathable shell** that's actually breathable. You'll be in lukewarm mist every hour. Cheap rain jackets will cook you.
* **Knee and shin protection:** Soft trail gaiters or low gaiters. The basalt will cheese-grate you.
* **Shoes:** Approach shoes or sturdy trail runners with sticky rubber and drainage, NOT waterproof boots. Waterproof boots will fill with mist and never drain. You want something that gets wet and then gets *dry*. Bring 3 pairs of merino socks in dry bags.

**Breathing & Hearing - THE MOST IMPORTANT:**
* **P100 / N95 elastomeric respirator + spare filters.** Not a paper dust mask. You need it for spore/pollen bursts, especially after a Canopy brightening when flowers *pop* open. You'll also want a light buff/neck gaiter for when the full mask is too much.
* **High-fidelity ear protection:** Loop-style or shooting earmuffs with acoustic filtering. You need to cut 20-25 dB but still hear speech. Do NOT use foam plugs alone - you'll be deaf to the Canopy creak, which is your only warning that light/weather is about to shift. Bring earplugs for sleep + a white-noise app offline. Otherwise you *will not sleep.*

**Light & Power:**
* **Your own light source.** The Canopy gives you dim amber. It washes out colors and depth perception. Bring a warm-white headlamp + a small powerful thrower for seeing down canals and into those too-deep buildings. The city lights work dimly but you cannot rely on them.
* **No hard shadows = no depth cues.** A handheld light held at waist height will save your ankles on the terraces.
* Power bank in a sealed dry bag. Humidity kills electronics. Bring silica gel packets by the handful.

**Navigation & Anchorage:**
* **DO NOT RELY ON MEMORY.** Space is folded. Mark your path physically.
* Biodegradable flagging tape, chalk sticks (glow chalk is better in amber light), and a long spool of high-vis paracord (200m). Tie off at intersections. Yes, you will look silly. The people who don't do this are the ones who vanish.
* Analog navigation: Mechanical watch, paper notebook + pencil, manual click counter for steps. Log every turn with time/direction. "Left at root-waterfall, 342 steps to tram with red flowers."
* Two independent compasses + offline altimeter. Expect 5-15 degrees of drift. Trust your string, not your gut.
* Whistle - pea-less, storm whistle at 120dB. Your voice won't carry over the bio-roar.

**Water/Food:**
* **Do not drink the canal/cistern water, even filtered** on first trip. The biology is alien and the lilypads may be biofilters concentrating Canopy runoff. Bring all your water: 4-5L per person per day + Sawyer Squeeze + chemical backup as absolute emergency.
* Food: Sealed, high-calorie, no-cook. The damp will get into everything. Repackage into vacuum bags. Don't eat local fruit/flowers no matter how manufactured-perfect they look. That internal light is not photosynthesis as you know it.

**Medical:**
* Blister kit, Israeli bandage, antihistamines (cetirizine + diphenhydramine), steroid cream, strong antifungal, broad-spectrum antibiotics if you can get them, eye drops (humidity + pollen = conjunctivitis), rehydration salts.

### 4. How To Move In The City

This is the protocol experienced travelers use. Follow it.

**1. Never enter the deep interior of buildings on your first visit.** Look through the door. That housing stack that should be 30m deep is 300m deep. Time dilates slightly there. Take photos from the threshold. If you MUST enter, do the string rule: tie paracord to something *outside* that will not be overgrown in an hour (metal transit rail, not a root) and keep tension. One person stays at the entrance while one scouts. 10 minutes max inside.

**2. Stay center-avenue, travel terrace-by-terrace.** Don't hop the 3m drops constantly. Find the ramps/stairs where the city intended transit. The basalt edges are sharpest where they're most moss-covered and most likely to slide. The white cable-roots *will* hold your weight but they will also drop irritant sap on you. Use gloves.

**3. Move on a schedule tied to the Canopy, not your watch.** Learn the cycle: pressure drop in ears -> 10-30 seconds -> lukewarm mist for ~60-90 seconds -> stillness. That's your clock. Travel *between* mists. During mist, stop, cover optics/masks, and wait. Visibility drops to 10m and every surface becomes slick.

**4. Bright Spot Protocol:** If a block brightens - the veins clear and light pours down like a stage light - stop. Do not stand in the center of it. Plants will phototrope *fast*. Vines will creak and shift. Flowers will pop open and dump pollen. Put your respirator on, step back to the edge, and watch. It's beautiful and it's the jungle sneezing in your face. Wait 5 minutes after it dims to move through.

**5. Sound discipline:** Travel in pairs within arm's reach, with hand signals pre-agreed. Normal conversation beyond 1 meter is impossible. Don't shout for hours - you'll just hurt your throat. Check in visually every minute.

### 5. Where to Sleep

You need elevation and airflow.

* Don't sleep in the fissure-canals or next to cistern-lakes. That's where the dog-sized frogs breed and the sound is 10dB louder. Also flash-mist pooling.
* Don't sleep in alleys that look windless. Mold will eat you overnight.
* Best spot: Second or third floor balcony of a housing stack that you have *confirmed is shallow*. Or a wide terrace top with a slight breeze. The Canopy hum is slightly quieter higher up.
* Hammock with built-in bug net + tarp is better than a ground tent. Ground is damp hard basalt that grows into your footprint. A hammock keeps you off accretion. If you must tent, use a freestanding tent + footprint, and wipe condensation every morning. Shake out *everything*.
* Use earplugs + eye mask. The light never goes dark. Your brain will keep thinking it's 6PM. Force a 24h cycle with alarms and light discipline: headlamp on bright during your "day", completely off for 8 hours.

### 6. Psychological Prep - This Is The Real Test

The City After the Canopy is not scary like a ruin. It's scary like an abandoned mall the size of a state.

* **Vast, maintained emptiness:** Your primate brain expects a city this big to have people. It has none. Just the hum and the shrieks. After ~6 hours, most travelers report a profound, vertiginous loneliness - *agoraphobic claustrophobia*. The avenues are too wide, the buildings too empty, the sound too loud. You feel simultaneously exposed and trapped.
* **Time will feel wrong.** No shadows, no sunset. You will swear you've walked for an hour when it's been 15 minutes, or vice versa in folded streets. This is why you log everything.
* **Bring a tether to humanity:** Offline music, a book, voice notes to a friend, a small ritual - tea at "night", journaling. Don't just wander staring at emptiness. The city invites aimless walking for kilometers. Aimless walking is how you get too tired to find your way back.
* **Set a hard turnaround time before you enter.** "I turn back in 3 hours regardless of what I see ahead." The haze and green vanish point is hypnotic.

### 7. Entry, Exit, and Leaving No Trace

* **Ingress:** Mark your entry point with something unmistakable and non-biological - a stack of three cairns with flagging, GPS waypoint if you get any signal at all, and a photo. Everything looks the same after a kilometer.
* **Never leave trash that can accrete.** The rule "dust becomes leaf" is not poetic. A plastic bag will be rooted in a month. A dropped granola bar will be gone in a week, but the wrapper will become a little soil pod. Pack out *everything*. If you cut vegetation, you are damaging a system the Canopy is actively tending.
* **Exit check:** Brush down, check for seeds/pods hitchhiking on clothes/shoes. Quarantine and wash everything at 60°C after. Do not bring basalt shards, flowers, or water home. The accretion biology is aggressively opportunistic. You don't want it in your home timeline's soil.

### A Good First Visit Itinerary

Don't try to "see the city." You can't.

* **Duration:** 4-6 hours max for first trip. No overnight.
* **Distance:** <3km total from entry, staying on ONE major avenue.
* **Goal:** Find one cistern-lake, sit at its edge outside the frog chorus peak, watch one mist cycle, watch one bright spot. Take notes. Listen to the Canopy creak.
* **Then leave.** The city will still be there.

#### Final Intuition

If you stand still long enough in the amber light, with the mist beading on your arms and the dragonflies screaming overhead and the empty tram humming to itself, you'll feel it: this place is not dead. It's *fallow*. The automation is gardening. The Canopy is waiting. The buildings are holding space for a population that will, or will not, return.

You are a guest in a greenhouse that thinks its owners are just out of the room.

Be quiet, be small, be kind to the things that grow over the vacancy. Wear your mask, protect your ears, tie your string, and don't stay past your bedtime.

And when you get back, you'll notice how *quiet* normal cities are.
