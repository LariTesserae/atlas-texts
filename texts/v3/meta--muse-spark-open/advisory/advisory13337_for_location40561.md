This is not a mountain you climb. It's a mountain that got tired of being a mountain and decided to wait forever. And visiting it is not like wilderness travel. It's like visiting a finished painting and trying not to become paint.

Ordinary humans are not built for this place, because ordinary humans are built for *change* — hunger, thirst, wind, shadows moving, things happening and then being over. That doesn't work here. Nothing happens, nothing ends, and your body will lie to you about what it needs.

Think of it less as an expedition and more as a contamination quarantine in reverse: *you* are the contamination. The place is in perfect equilibrium. You bring gradient, noise, time, heat. It will try very gently and very persistently to smooth you out.

Here is how not to get smoothed.

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Visiting

This is a heat-death in miniature. Not dead — *finished.*

Old ascent, then eons of dry erosion until all edges were erased. The altitude is still there, 4-7km worth, but it's stored as mass and dryness and stillness, not as steepness. Pressure will feel weird: you can breathe, but the air feels like nothing because there's no humidity, no movement, no smell. Like breathing inside a dehumidifier.

There is no weather, no sun, no night. The light is cool, sourceless, shadow-frozen at ~midday intensity. Color temperature is flat mineral white. That means:

* Your circadian clock will break in under an hour.
* You will get cold without realizing it. 8-10°C / 46-50°F with zero wind sounds harmless. With absolute dryness and no sun-warmth, it leaches you steadily. It's October-shade cold that never warms up because you never move into sun.
* Your sense of time, hunger, and thirst will uncouple from reality. You won't feel hungry. Your mouth will feel like paper but you won't feel *thirsty* the normal way. That's a trap.

The plain is crowded but not with life as you know it. The rooks — one to three per square meter, stone-heavy, matte salt-black with translucent frills — are not hiding. They are not sleeping. They have reached the same equilibrium as the rocks. They tick once every few minutes, like a crystal cooling. En masse they make a silence so thick you can hear it as a tone.

Don't think of them as animals. Think of them as the plain's way of being dense.

The other weirdness — the flicker in the corner of your eye where they unfold and float, the cairn that reappears ahead of you mirrored — is not hallucination. Reality here has been sanded thin. Distance is approximate. Space is a suggestion. The longer you stay still, the less it bothers to pretend for you.

**Core rule:** You are not in danger from teeth or claws. You are in danger from *stopping.*

If you sit long enough, dust settles on you and doesn't blow off because there is no wind to blow it. The place starts counting you as part of its density. People who stay become excellent, very still statues.

### 2. Pre-Trip Preparation: Before You Step Through

**A. Set an external metabolism.**
You cannot trust your body here.

1.  **Forced clock:** Bring a loud, tactile timekeeper that does not rely on sun, GPS, or your phone. Old wind-up mechanical metronome, a kitchen timer that vibrates every 20 minutes, a cheap digital watch with an alarm you set to beep. You will need to hear time because you cannot see it. Your shadow will not move. You will swear you've been there 10 minutes when it's been 90.
2.  **Forced drink/eat schedule:** Pre-portion water with electrolytes + simple carbs. Decide: every 20 min by the beep, you take 3-4 sips and a bite, whether you want it or not. Your thirst signal is muted, but insensible water loss in air that dry is brutal. Your lips will crack, your nose will bleed. You won't feel dehydration as panic, just as a slow fog and headache.
3.  **Forced movement schedule:** Every beep, you must deliberately move, change posture, speak aloud. Stasis is contagious.

Tell someone where your anchor is and agree on a hard pull-time. **Do not go without a tether/beacon.** Ordinary compass, GPS, inertial phone — none of them will agree with each other here for more than a few hundred meters. The peneplain loops. You walk a kilometer and the same wall comes back mirrored. You need a multiverse tether that can yank you regardless of local geometry. Test it before you go. Set it to auto-recall in 90 minutes max for a first visit. 3 hours is a veteran trip.

**B. Train your attention.**
The plain attacks attention, not the body.

Practice this for a week before: sit in a silent room and stare at a textured wall without letting your gaze soft-focus. Notice how your peripheral vision wants to invent motion. That's 10x stronger there.

The rooks are always looking at you even when their seam-faces are turned away. That feeling is real and will make you paranoid. Meditate on it beforehand: "I am being attended to, and I do not need to attend back." Do not mirror them. Do not try to make eye contact that isn't there. Do not try to count them. Counting them is how you get stuck — there are always more in peripheral vision.

People with derealization/depersonalization history, or who are sensitive to sensory deprivation, should not go alone. At minimum, bring a voice to talk to — a recorded podcast, a partner on radio — even if radio crackles. The place is so quiet your brain will invent a chord out of their ticking. You need human speech to break it.

### 3. What To Wear and Carry

Think: cold desert laboratory, not hiking trail.

**Body:**
* Layers for dry cold, not wet cold. Merino base + windbreaker shell even though there is no wind. The shell is for salt dust. You need a hood or beanie — 30% of heat loss is head/neck and you will lose it without feeling a breeze.
* Sunglasses. The light has no source but it's bright and diffuse like an overcast glacier. Snow-blindness without snow is still blindness. Pale amber or gray, not too dark — you need contrast to see the low whale-back swells that are invisible until you're on them.
* Dust mask or buff you can pull over nose/mouth. Not for toxicity — for preservation. The salt varnish squeaks to fine powder. Inhaling it constantly dries your airways and it coats your throat. You will cough and think you're sick. You're just salted.
* Knee pads or a sit-pad. You *will* kneel to look at lichen rosettes and rooks. Kneeling directly on salt pillows is like kneeling on cool ceramic. It numbs fast.
* Gloves, thin. The salt crust comes off on fingers and is weirdly cool, almost menthol. You don't want to handle rooks or lichen with bare salt-crusted hands. Also prevents you from fidgeting.

**Feet:**
* Hard-soled boots with good ankle support. The turtle-back pavement is stable but the salt rafts in the pans are paper-thin and chiming. They break and don't heal. You leave permanent footprints. More importantly, they are slippery and uneven — easy to turn an ankle while you're watching rooks instead of ground. Step *between* the dense clusters, never on them. You can't move a rook by kicking it. You'll just hurt your foot. They are stone-heavy for a reason.

**Kit: Bring gradient with you.**
The plain has none, so you have to import it.

* 2-3 liters water, more than you think, with salt/electrolyte added. Plain water will make dryness worse.
* Saline nasal spray + lip balm + eye drops. Use on schedule, not on feeling.
* External sound: small Bluetooth speaker, low volume, music with clear beat. Wind chimes. Anything that *changes*. Silence here has weight. A little continuous human music reminds your nervous system that time is passing. Keep it low — you still need to hear if your tether alarms.
* A bright, noisy, *movable* trail marker that is not a cairn. Do not build cairns. The plain is already full of perfect little knapped walls and bowl-hollows that mean something to rooks, and your addition will be absorbed. Use 20m of neon paracord, fluttering surveyor's tape tied to your pack, or a spool of thread you can reel back. Something that moves in a non-existent breeze because *you* move it. When you look back, if the string cuts absurdly or points the wrong way, you know space is bending.
* Mechanical clicker or camera with shutter sound. Take photos on schedule. Later you'll realize your photos show slightly different rook densities than you remember. That's data, not error.
* Something warm and alive to hold: insulated thermos of tea, hand warmer. Holding warmth reminds you you're endothermic. The plain is cool to the touch, 10°C stone that never warmed.

**Do NOT bring:**
* Offerings, food to leave, water to pour into the pecked bowls. Those bowls are not empty because no one filled them. They are empty because this place has finished needing to be filled. Pouring water out is littering and will just salt over and stay there forever. Worse, it creates a gradient *they* will gather around.
* A tent to sleep in. Do not plan to sleep there on a first trip. Sleep is the ultimate stillness. If you're tired enough to nap, it's time to leave.

### 4. How To Move and See

**Walk like you're on a museum floor.**

1.  **Slow, deliberate, noisy steps.** Squeak the salt intentionally. Scuff. Talk to yourself. "Stepping over hare-sized rook, stepping around lichen scab." Narrate. It keeps you as an *event* rather than an object.
2.  **Never step on rooks or lichen.** Not from reverence — from physics. Lichen rosettes are hard as enamel and chipped like old pottery. They have been the same size for centuries. You breaking one is permanent. You'll feel the guilt for weeks, and the patch won't heal. Rooks will not move for you, but they will make room in the sense that you will suddenly realize there is always a path between them if you look.
3.  **Don't stare.** Use soft gaze, 70% direct, 30% scanning. If you lock eyes with a rook's seam, your peripheral vision will flood with the unfolded version — long limbs, translucent light. It's beautiful and it will pull you into a dissociative loop where you try to see it "properly." You can't. Glance, note, move on. Fast head turns cause the flicker. Turn slowly.
4.  **Expect the loop.** When the same low wall or knapped debitage scatter reappears, do not panic and do not try to "push through" to new terrain. Stop. Check your tether bearing, check your string. Mark mentally: "Loop point, mirrored." Then make a deliberate right-angle turn and walk 50 counted steps. Loops here are not mazes; they are the plain forgetting to generate new space. You generate it by injecting novelty. Sing, clap, change pace. The plain will reluctantly make new plain for you.
5.  **Touch sparingly.** The pillow-stones are cool, pleasant. The salt rafts chime. It's tempting to run hands over everything. Every touch leaves oil and breaks varnish that took millennia to form. Touch one thing fully — feel the squeak — then keep hands in pockets.

Set a hard turnaround alarm. When it goes, you turn toward your inbound string/tether, even if the interesting cairn is "just ahead." The plain is excellent at making "just ahead" last forever.

### 5. How Not To Be Included

This is the most important part.

You will feel an invitation to sit. The light is cool and kind, the air is still, the rooks are quiet company. The whale-back swells are perfect backrests. Your shadow is crisp and still. It feels safe because nothing wants to eat you. Nothing wants anything.

**Symptoms you are being included:**
* Dust stays on you. You brush your shoulder and new dust is already there.
* Sound dulls. Your footfalls get quieter even though you're stepping the same.
* You forget to check your timer. You think "just a minute more" for the third time.
* You feel seen without being looked at. The rooks haven't moved, but the attention has weight.
* You stop narrating. You become quiet to "not disturb" them.

**Protocol to break it:**
1. Stand up. Stomp. Make a sharp, ugly noise — clap, whistle.
2. Drink water even if you don't want it. Feel the cold go down.
3. Say your name, date, and pull time aloud: "I am [name], human visitor, leaving at [time]."
4. Move. Pull your string. Do not sit again in the same spot.

Do not lie flat to look at sky. The mineral white sky with no sun is profoundly disorienting face-up. People report vertigo, feeling they will fall *up*. If you must rest, sit with back to a swell, facing your backtrail, timer in hand.

Do not mimic the rooks' stillness to "blend in." Humans are bad at stillness. You will just get cold and dissociated and they will out-wait you by eons.

### 6. How To Leave and Aftercare

Leaving is not just walking back. You need to reintroduce gradient forcefully.

On your alarm, even if you feel fine:
* Pack everything, including tape fragments and salt powder on gloves — leave no trace is literal here.
* Walk back along your string/beacon line, counting steps. Don't look for shortcuts.
* Before triggering tether, do a 30-second reset ritual: shake limbs, jog in place, splash a little water on face (then dry it — you don't want to bring salt water home to your eyes), listen to one full song chorus.

Once home:
* You will be temporally hungover. Colors will seem too saturated, wind will feel violent, shadows moving will be startling. Plan 24 hours with no driving, no big decisions. Keep your mechanical watch going — listen to it tick.
* Rinse boots and clothes outside. The fine pink-white salt gets everywhere and will keep squeaking in your laundry for weeks if you don't.
* Expect phantom ticking for a night or two, especially in quiet rooms. That's normal. Your brain learned a new silence. Play gentle background sound to sleep.
* Hydrate and eat warm, salty soup. Your body was losing water without telling you.
* Don't go back the next day. The plain will remember your shape slightly. Return trips get loopier faster. Give it at least a week.

---

**Final intuition:** Visiting is like being the only warm-blooded person in a library where all the books are closed and have been closed for a million years. You can read the spines, admire the shelves, hear the dust. You cannot open a book and expect a story — the story finished. You can only be a brief draft that ruffles the dust, and then you must leave before the librarian — who is not a person but the perfect stillness itself — files you as part of the collection.

Go, look, listen, leave a faint squeak that will eventually be polished away, and keep your ticking heart where it belongs: in a place where shadows move.

If you want a souvenir, take a photo. The plain keeps everything else.
