This is a **Closed-System Equilibrium Trap.** Do not treat it as a landscape. Treat it as a machine that is currently idling, and you are the wrong size contaminant to fall into it.

Everyone who describes this place makes the same mistake: they think because it is quiet, warm, and the animals don't bite, it is merciful. It's not. Nothing in there is hostile, because nothing in there needs to be. It will kill you by making you *part* of the equilibrium.

If you are an ordinary human and you absolutely must go - for science, for grief, for a dare - this is how you prepare.

### 1. Understand What It Will Do To You

Before you pack a single item, understand the kill mechanisms. They are all slow and deniable.

**1. It will cook you from the inside out.**
The air is ~33-36C / 91-97F and ~95-100% humidity. The concrete is the same temperature. Sweat does not evaporate in that. Humans do not cool in that. You will be heat-soaked within 15 minutes. You will stop feeling hot and start feeling *heavy*. That's heat exhaustion transitioning to heat stroke. Your heart is working triple-time to pump blood to your skin that cannot shed heat. You will feel calm. You will want to sit down in the nice warm water. If you sit down, you will not stand up.

**2. It will drown you without drowning you.**
The water is everywhere, but none of it is water. It's condensate + industrial leachate. It's warm, faintly oily, mineral-slick, full of dissolved concrete, rust, and whatever that dark film is. It is not potable. It will not sustain you. If it gets in your eyes, mouth, lungs, or any micro-abrasion, it will colonize you. That film that grows in perfect tidelines? It *is* alive. It just doesn't look like Earth life, so your brain dismisses it. Every surface sweats it. The fauna lives in it 24/7. You do not have that adaptation.

Expect, without protection: chemical conjunctivitis, otitis externa, trench foot in under an hour, full-body folliculitis, fungal lung infection, and catastrophic gut flora wipeout if swallowed. Travelers who drank it report it tasted like "warm pennies and nothing." They were dead of sepsis-like symptoms within 3-4 days after extraction, even with antibiotics.

**3. It will erase your navigation and then your time sense.**
There is no high ground, no sun position, no shadow movement, no wind, no north. The clouds are fixed. The sheds are identical and repeat to vanishing point. Your inner ear and your eyes will disagree within minutes. This is called horizon deprivation. Sailors and polar traversers know it. You will walk in circles without realizing, even trying to walk straight. You will stare at a channel for what feels like five minutes and find an hour has passed. That is not poetry. The local physics is holding itself in stasis - including light, heat, and possibly entropy. Your mammalian brain, which uses change to measure time, will desynchronize. An hour there can feel like twenty minutes. Panic and dissociation set in around hour two.

**4. It will use the fauna to infect you, not eat you.**
The animals are not predators. They are components. They are *saturated*. You cannot avoid contact. You will wade and they will part around your shins, brush your calves, your hands. They are covered in biofilm. Their "not biting you" is worse than biting you - it means you will be caked in them and not notice. The louse-flies that carpet the dry platforms will walk onto your boots, up your legs. The skin-winged roosters will shed dander that hangs in the still air forever because there is no wind to clear it. You are breathing warm protein soup.

Long-term visitors come back with persistent cough, subdermal nodules that move, and a smell of wet rust that never washes out because the bacteria has moved into their sinuses.

### 2. The Packing List: Minimum Viable Return

You are not packing to survive there. You are packing to stay alive long enough to leave. Think clean-room worker meets cave diver, not hiker.

**On your body, zero exposed skin. Zero.**

*   **Fully sealed waders, chest-high, industrial chemical grade.** Not hiking waders. Thick vulcanized rubber with integrated boots with steel shanks. The concrete may look smooth but it will shred your feet with its crisp 90-degree edges and rebar grit. Tape every seam with chemical tape. You WILL be wading. The dry platforms are inches high; the channels are unavoidable. Assume you will be shin to knee deep the entire time.
*   **Drysuit or chemical coverall underneath.** Second barrier. If the waders breach, you need a second skin.
*   **Full-face respirator, P100 + organic vapor + acid gas. PAPR if you can get one.** This is non-negotiable. Not a dust mask. Not a surgical mask. The air is saturated with warm water, rust aerosol, and animal dander. You need positive-pressure filtered air. Bring spare filters sealed in ziplocks; they will clog fast. The quiet of the plain hides how particulate that air is. If you can smell anything sweet or metallic, your seal has failed. Leave immediately.
*   **Sealed chemical goggles under the face shield.** Double-eye protection. The weeping walls drip into your eyes.
*   **Nitrile gloves, triple-layered, extended cuff, duct-taped to your sleeves.** Then heavy rubber outer gloves. You will want to touch things. Don't.
*   **Active cooling vest.** An ice-vest or phase-change cooling vest with frozen packs. Without it, you have maybe 40-60 minutes before cognitive decline from heat. Bring twice as many packs as you think, in an insulated backpack. The ambient heat will melt them in ~25 minutes. This vest is your clock.
*   **Hearing protection - but not silence.** Wear filtered earplugs. You need to dull the endless *plok... plok* and sub-aural hum, which is a trance inducer in that silence. But you need to hear your own breathing and your extraction alarm.

**In your pack:**

*   **Water. Only water you brought. 1.5 liters per planned 30 minutes, minimum.** Sealed, with a bite-valve under your mask. You will sweat profusely and not realize it because nothing evaporates. You need electrolytes as well. Do NOT drink, touch, or collect any water from the channels or basins, no matter how clear it looks.
*   **Tether and Anchor.** This is the most important item. A 300m+ high-vis, floating, chemical-resistant rope on a spool. Anchor one end AT your entry point to something heavy and bright: a deployable ground anchor, a weighted beacon, a person. Do not anchor to the structures - they are all identical. You hold the spool. You do not ever let go. If you lose sight of your entry vector, you are dead. People have walked 200 meters in and become irretrievable because every direction looked like home. GPS will drift. Compasses are sluggish under that much iron. A physical line is God.
*   **Inertial navigation + beacon network.** Drop a GPS waypoint at entry, and carry a handheld inertial tracker. But more importantly, drop active beacons every 20 meters: flashing LED stakes, howling personal alarms, smoke poppers. The plain is so visually repetitive that you will experience déjà vu within 3 minutes. You need artificial landmarks to prove you have moved.
*   **Bright, useless color.** Your entire kit should be safety orange, acid yellow, signal pink. The plain is grey, rust, slate-green, and white sky. Camouflage will kill you. You want to be an eyesore. This helps your extractor find you, and it gives your eyes something that is *not* the equilibrium to fix on.
*   **Timekeeper that screams at you.** A watch with a vibrating, loud alarm set to 15, 30, and 45 minutes MAXIMUM. And a hard abort time. Decide before you step through: "I leave at 45 minutes no matter what I see." Tell someone outside the portal to drag you back at 50 minutes even if you say you're fine. Your own judgment will be heat-addled and time-dilated. Do not trust it.
*   **Light.** A powerful thrower flashlight, even in daylight. The overcast white gives no shadows. Shining a harsh angled light helps you actually see the depth of channels and the bodies in the water. It also breaks the visual monotony that lulls you.
*   **No food. No tobacco. No open electronics.** You will not be there long enough to need calories. Anything you open will be contaminated instantly. Seal your phone in a dry bag with silica packets. It will fog inside anyway.

### 3. Protocols For While You Are Inside

**Entry:** Do not step in dramatically. Test. Stick a booted foot through, let the sensors outside read temp/humidity/air quality if you have them. Note wind - there should be none. Step through onto a DRY platform, not directly into water. Immediately anchor, beacon, and orient.

**Movement:** Slow shuffle, never stride. Lift your feet barely; the water is full of bodies and you don't want to aerosolize it with splashing. Keep your hands tucked unless balancing. Do not swat at anything that lands on you. Observe and let it walk off. If a crab-thing clings, nudge it off with your outer glove when you are on dry ground, not over water.

**Seeing:** You will be tempted to kneel and look close at the still water, at the film, at your reflection. That's the psychological hazard. The water is a perfect mirror reflecting the infinite white sky and sheds. Staring into it triggers profound derealization - the sense that you are looking down into another identical plain below, falling forever. Travelers call this "The Second Floor" and describe an urge to reach in or fall forward. Do not kneel unless you must. Do not look at your reflection longer than a glance. Look at your beacons, your rope, your boots.

**Listening:** The quiet is not empty. That low hum and the arrhythmic drips are exactly at the tempo that makes humans hold their breath to listen. Consciously breathe loudly through your respirator. Hum. Count out loud. Bring a small metronome or play music dull and low inside your hood if your extractor allows. You need to pollute their silence with your own rhythm, or its rhythm will become yours.

**Touch:** Do not palm the warm concrete, no matter how inviting. Do not trail your fingers in the water. That blood-warmth is deeply mammal-comforting. It's supposed to be. It says "incubator, safe, sleep." It is not for you.

**Abort Triggers - Leave INSTANTLY if:**
*   Your cooling vest is spent and heat blooms on your neck/face
*   Your goggles fogs from the INSIDE and won't clear (humidity breach)
*   You notice a smell inside your mask
*   You feel suddenly calm, detached, or think "I could stay here for a while, it's nice"
*   You have lost count of beacons or your tether goes slack in a direction that doesn't make sense
*   Any fauna is seen to change behavior - swarming *toward* you, clinging, or if the water-lice in a channel all orient the same way

That last one has never been reliably reported to end well.

### 4. Extraction and After

Do not run. Shuffle back along your tether, collecting beacons. Step out onto clean ground. Have your outside partner do NOT hug you. Have them decontaminate you.

**Decon:** Stand on a plastic sheet. Have them, in full gloves and mask, hose you down with fresh water and mild detergent while you are still fully suited. Peel outer gloves first. Then waders. Bag everything. That dark film and that water will still be on you. The smell will be there. Shower with antiseptic wash for 10+ minutes, scrub under nails, irrigate eyes and nostrils with saline, gargle. Do not shower hot - lukewarm, to not open pores further.

**Medical:** Even with perfect discipline, expect: mild heat exhaustion, tension headache, tinnitus from the hum, chemical bronchitis for 24-48 hours, trench-foot-precursor (white, pruney feet for days), and a vivid, intrusive memory of the dripping. Watch for 7 days: fever, persistent cough that produces rust-colored sputum, any crawling sensation under skin, any rash that follows waterlines around ankles/wrists. Tell any doctor: "industrial coolant + biological aerosol exposure, unknown fungal/bacterial." Bring a sample only if you sealed it in triple glass outside before entry - never bring open water back.

**Psychological:** You will dream of a flat warm infinity. You will miss the simplicity of it. That is normal for 1-2 weeks. You will also feel a phantom *plok* at random intervals in quiet rooms. If you feel an urge to go back, or you stop finding human clutter and noise comforting, talk to someone immediately. The place's most dangerous property is not its water. It's that it is perfectly at rest. Human minds, which are always striving and noisy and out of equilibrium, find that rest obscene and then, horribly, enviable.

The plain does not want you. It does not hate you. It simply has no place that is *not* itself. It will not make a place for you, so it will slowly make *you* into a place - warm, wet, still, and covered in quiet life.

Go, look, keep your boots on, keep your mask sealed, follow your line back, and leave the reflection alone.
