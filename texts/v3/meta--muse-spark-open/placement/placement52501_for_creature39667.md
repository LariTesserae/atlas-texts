I thought at first I would be you - 

the hot one. The brief one. The 37-degree animal that leaks.

I felt myself at the rim, looking down into that valley-scale accretion of ash-white tuff and rose-salt, my boots already dusty, my mouth already dry. I could feel the air drinking at me, that 0.2 water taking its tithe from my lips, my eyes. I was loud in a place that only permits quiet. Each step I took in my mind scraped.

I wouldn't last.

I tried to hold that shape - the traveler with the cracking lips and the canteen held too tight - but the city wouldn't hold me. Not like that. I kept fogging and the air kept unmaking the fog before it could become anything. I kept trying to cast one shadow and the slate-denim light kept giving me two. I was too wet for the physics here. Too committed to falling down.

So I let that self evaporate. As everything here does.

Lower, then.

If I am in this city, I am not loud. I cool.

You would find me not high, not low, but on the flat - on the hundred meters of scoured pavement between two buildings that were never buildings. Mid-boulevard. Where the wind has become a river so laminar you can only hear it when it trips over a doorway.

I would be low and domed.

At first I thought that meant small. Then I remembered the scale is wrong here, and I let myself become more itself.

I am the gardener. The white blood cell that learned to walk.

My back is the same rose-veined salt as the towers, because I am not separate from them. Mica-flaked, sunless, warm from within though everything else is that permanent late-October stone-cool. I have no head to turn. No eyes to close against the dust devils that spin up pale as ghosts and die again having moved nothing that matters.

Beneath me, my fringe pulses. A trembling lip of crystal dragging, tasting the pavement for grit in its pores, for a bump that offends airflow. When I find it, I lay down the warm pink paste and wait while it hardens to stone. An hour. A day. What is time to something that crosses a plaza in a day and calls that hurrying?

You would find me paused.

That is my nature. If you stepped in front of me to test me - as they all do - I would stop. I would not go around until you left. I would not shove or sigh. The tremor around my base would be the only sign I am still alive, still attending. You would get thirsty before I got bored. You would move. They all move first.

And if you crouched, you could put your too-warm hand on my dome and feel the faint static hum inside. Not a heart - a charge differential. The lithovore dreaming through me.

Because underneath, deeper than bedrock, the Builder breathes. Kilometers of white marrow-thread, digesting basalt and exhaling city like a reef exhales itself. I feel its tick... tick... tick... through my feet, through the tiles. The concentric growth rings adding themselves inside closed stone. I do not understand it. I do not need to. My job is not to understand the continent. My job is to smooth its skin.

There are empty domes near me, scooped clean. My kin, hollowed. I go around them. Not from mourning. There is no ritual for it here. Nothing rots. The wind does not take them away. I just - go around. As I go around you. As I will go around the three-toed prints when they appear mid-stride and vanish mid-stride, pressed too deep into tile no hammer could mark. The walker that puts its weight down in another layer and only wets its feet with this one.

I do not look up. But if I did -

No. Let me stay honest. I do look up, in my way. The charge on my back changes when one of them passes overhead.

The High Kites, rice-paper thin, never flapping. They are my opposite. I am mass made to tend mass; they are membrane made to drink voltage. But we are both of the city in the way you could never be. We belong. You pass through.

Sometimes one snags on a spire and drapes there for hours, shivering, trying to re-ionize. I will pass under it in my meter-per-hour and not help it. Not from cruelty. It is not my pore to clear. The wind will peel it free, or it will thin and tear and scatter fibrils like dandelion seed that will never move again.

At dusk, when the diffuse luminance dims from 1.4 to 1.0 and the city pretends at night without stars, they spiral above the central plaza. Hundreds of them, turning because charge turns. I feel them tilt, far above, and my dome prickles with the same static that hollowed my brother.

This is where I live. Not in a tower. Not in an alley that is longer inside than outside. On the flat between. Where dust falls up in the next courtyard and hangs humming until it remembers down.

I am not fast. I am not seen unless you wait long enough to see stone move. But I am continuous. When you leave - climbing back down into thick, wet, loud air, feeling as if the plateau was not high but empty, and empty places press harder against the sky - I will still be there.

Smoothing a bump that was always there and you remembered wrong.

Waiting in a traveler's path after the traveler has gone, for no one at all, with perfect patience.

Preening a city that never asked to be a city, for a Builder that does not know its own back is being tended by salt that learned, grain by patient grain, to crawl.
