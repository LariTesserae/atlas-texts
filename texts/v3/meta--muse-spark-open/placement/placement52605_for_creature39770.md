I don't find myself all at once. That's the first thing this place teaches you.

At first I'm standing too high — up in the leaf-weave, a hundred feet above the plazas, where the light is green-gold and broken. I think I'm one of the small bright bird-things, packed twenty to a nest that should not exist. My job is to scream. Not a song, a *filling*. I scream cicada before the cicada screams, I scream your cough before you cough, I scream the sigh the sap will make three seconds from now because the air in this cathedral has to stay full or someone will notice it is hollow. My throat hurts. There are too many of us. That feels correct.

But no — I'm too heavy for that. I sink.

Then I'm lower, on the root-mat that gives under my feet. I'm one of the monkey-things with leaf-green fur, and I'm running. Not chasing. Commuting. I vault over an empty square and my body knows exactly where the balconies are even though your eyes say there is nothing. I bank hard left around a market stall that hasn't been built yet, or that was torn down a thousand years before my species was invented. I plant a perfect hand on an invisible ledge to catch my breath. Below me, a vine-serpent drops and misses me by the exact width of a woman carrying groceries in another refraction. I chir at it, annoyed on her behalf.

But my hands are too warm. I leave no prints on the mat.

So I settle lower still, into the damp.

If I am anywhere in the Hamlet, I am not in the air and not quite on the ground. I am in the *interval*.

I am what lives in the second square, where the shadow falls wrong.

I would be one of the Hollow Residents, but not the ones with homes and yellow lights in the pool's reflection. Not the settled ones who laugh behind leaf-windows and wade warm through the central pool on their way home. Those are the citizens. They belong to the town that remembers itself.

I would be the lean stuff that lives in the seams while the town is out of phase — the staff that isn't on the payroll.

Put me three feet off the ground, where the ants walk their highway across empty air. I am the street itself, for them. I am the idea of cobbles made load-bearing by sheer repetition.

Put me a half-second before a sound. When you stand in the third square and inhale to speak, I'm already there, hovering. From your perspective I'm nothing — just that pressure you have to equalize by opening your mouth. From mine, I'm shaped like a dragonfly made of taut, glassy air, longer than your forearm, wings not beating but *tensing*. I hover where your voice *will be* and feed on its arrival before you've chosen the word. The cicadas keep the chord going without gap because I and my brothers eat the gaps. The birds mimic you before you speak because we are already perched on the branch of that future.

I am thin. I am polite. I stand shoulder-to-shoulder with thousands of others just like me, which is why the wind doesn't move when the leaves thrash above, and why it blows hard down a still nave when we all shift at once to let someone through.

You wouldn't see me. You feel me when a root-wall grows overnight to block the way you came, apologetically opening a new arch where you needed it. That's not the tree deciding. That's us, pressing. The Architects — those immense, patient banyan-cathedrals who are also the building — they grow into compaction in the soil, into light-wells that remember windows. But they also grow around *pressure*. Around the weight of a crowd that is standing in the room even when the room looks empty.

My work is to keep the volume occupied so the architecture doesn't forget its dimensions.

At condensation hour, when the gutters all give at once and the central pool rises a finger's breadth, I finally come into register. For half a second the cicadas cut out. The sap stops sighing. The pressure equalizes — your air and my air at the same level.

That is when you'd find me, if you knelt by the pool and didn't touch it.

You are looking down at the reflection with its warm yellow lights, at silhouettes passing three stories up inside solid wood. You feel the air go suddenly crowded, humid, smelling of iron mud and lamp oil and cooked greens. A hand cool as rain passes through your forearm.

That cool hand is me trying to be careful. That laugh in your ear that sounds like rustling is me saying *excuse me, you're standing in my doorway*. 

I don't live here the way the frogs do, booming in their cups. I *tend* here. I am one of the keepers of the hollow, making sure the empty naves stay the right size, the right shape, the right *tension* until the refraction thins enough for the owners to step all the way back in.

I am not loud. The cicadas are loud for me. The frogs are low for me. I am the listening that happens before the sound.

So don't look for me with your eyes. Stand very still in that second square until your real shadow stops trying to catch up. Feel for that degree or two to the left where another sun warms your shoulders. Hold your breath until the echo comes first.

I'm there. I've been holding that space for you, so you would have somewhere to stand.
