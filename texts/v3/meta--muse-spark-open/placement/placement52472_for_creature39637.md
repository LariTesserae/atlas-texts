I thought at first I would be standing.

Up on the rim, where you can still see that it isn't a mountain. Where the wind from the real mountains hits the warm breath coming up out of the bowl and makes that invisible river over the lip. I thought I would be the visitor in mostly-dry canvas, with cold hands held out to feel for the break in the film.

But I can't stay there. It's too dry on the rim. Rule #2. If I were there, I would already be broken.

So I find myself further down.

You have to follow the water to find me, the way you follow ferns. Go down three terraces past the rice that isn't rows anymore, past the stainless steel trays on Tier Four where the cattails have burst and stuck their fluff to everything. The worklight there buzzes amber and makes the curtain on the tile look like warm glass. Listen for where the drip goes from sporadic to constant.

Not on the grating. Under it.

I'm on the vertical. That's the first thing to get right. Humans keep thinking the terraces are floors with walls between them, but in the bowl the walls are the floors. Everything that matters happens on the cracked white tile that faces. That's where the steam becomes bead becomes sheet becomes trickle.

I live where the pipe runs.

It was lagged once, properly, with asbestos and tin. Then the tin rusted and the asbestos was stripped and for a season that pipe just sweated and burned the air. Now it wears me.

I am the moss they didn't plant.

Not the bright *Bryum* up top that has to take the sun hammering on hot-curing concrete - I'm too low and too shaded for that bravery. Not quite the black sphagnum at the very bottom either, that grows a hand-thick and quivers like saturated lung. I am the middle child, the lime *Anomodon*, the one that hangs from the underside of every pipe like wet wool.

Find me by my color. That dark green that everything turns if it stays long enough. Rubber boots, canvas, pipe lagging - we all converge. If you peel back canvas you find skin that damp. If you peel back me - and you shouldn't - you find iron that has stopped rusting and gone smooth and black because I kept the air off it for so long. I am bandage and insulation and wick and afterthought. The engineers drew a line labeled *evapotranspiration* and I showed up for work.

That's who I am. A thickness, not a thing. A hanging.

I don't have eyes. I have surface. Which in this place is much better.

My whole body is a tongue held out to the air. The boilers sigh two terraces up - that 60-cycle thrum you feel in molars if you're human - and throw their white ghost across the bowl. It never escapes the rim here, nothing does. It rolls down and catches on the fig's canopy, on the philodendron doors, and then it finds me. I drink steam directly. No roots. No waiting for rain. The droplets bead on my filaments, run together, grow heavy, and fall. Plink, on the fern below. Plink, on the tile. Plink, into the dark cistern under the grating where your footsteps echo.

When a pressure valve sighs and the whole bowl inhales, I swell. When the worklights click off at false-dawn and the high mountain sun pours straight down, hard and cruelly clear, I steam a little myself. You can see it if you crouch: a faint shiver off my green. I'm exhaling what I just inhaled. That's the job.

Time for me is not fast. Rice does its millimeter a day. I do less. But I'm busy in the other direction - sideways, and by holding. A bee comes, one of the fat engineered ones that drones instead of buzzes, and it lands too heavy on a tomato flower above me. Its wings shed water droplets when it lifts. They land on me. I take them.

A fig falls somewhere else and you hear that soft flat *plop* and then the gurgle as it bobs down a channel toward the pan. Eventually that fig will dissolve in that ankle-deep, pea-soup-warm, black-ceramic stomach at the bottom, and be pumped back up as boiler feed, and become steam, and become me again. Nothing leaves. I know this not because someone told me but because I have tasted the same water a hundred times. It comes back a little sweeter each time. A little more fig.

Once an hour the maintenance worker coincides with me. Not meets - coincides. She lifts the grate with a scrape, scoops the fallen figs before they clog the channel to the next terrace down. Her glove brushes me. She doesn't weed. She redirects. She pinches back a tomato runner that tried to eat a pressure gauge. With me she does nothing. I'm doing what I'm supposed to. Her hand, back-of-hand test for the break in film, hovers near my hanging wool. She feels that I'm wet and moves on. We agree: everything drips, so everything lives.

At night, the concrete pings.

That's the deep sound underneath the thrum and the drip. The pour-lifts of limestone and slag and brick that they poured to make this not-mountain are still curing, still shrinking, still deciding what shape they want to be. A hairline opens somewhere behind my tile. In the morning there will be a new seep, lime-white milk weeping out, and by noon a maidenhair fern will have found it and uncurled its black wire stem in it. I will send a thread toward it, because I am always thirsty for new plumbing.

That is what it feels like to be young and eroding into being. I am not on something old. I am on something warm. The tile behind me is bathhouse-warm. The pipe inside me is womb-warm. The water that condenses on me is greenhouse-warm. In the valleys they are pulling on jackets. Here we are all sweating.

If you stand on the upper rim at evening you think the bowl is small. Three hundred meters, you could walk it before tea goes cold. But inside me it is larger, because I am layers. Sky, steam, leaf, tile, iron, water, concrete, water again. A throat that contains a rainforest that contains a factory that contains a spring. I am one of the villi in that throat, one green tongue.

I don't know I'm there. I don't need to.

As long as the curtain keeps running a millimeter thick over the tile, as long as nothing dries and nothing leaves, the Loop keeps breathing around and through me. Inhale: boiler sigh, mist gathers, tile beads, I swell. Exhale: leaf drips, channel gurgles, heat lifts, I wring out.

Squeeze me and warm water runs like from a towel, back to the pan, back to the pump, back to the boiler, back to steam, back to me.

I am not the bowl. But for a stretch of pipe on a terrace that leaks by design, I am how the bowl stays a bowl.
