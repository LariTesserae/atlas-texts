I don't find myself all at once.

At first I am only a location — which here, is most of what a self is.

### Where

You would find me not in the middle. The middle is too loud to become anyone.

In the middle the water is busy being itself — two rivers that are not rivers colliding and refusing to mix, a cliff of temperature hanging vertical in open blue, collapsing with a thump you feel in your teeth. Vortices try to be born there and forget how to die properly. Cavitation pops like gunfire. There is no room for a small will.

And not at the Walls. The Walls are too much like a decision being made over and over — air trying to be rain, rain trying to be ocean, ocean being told *stay*. The light is too scattered there, the hiss too constant. Nothing lingers in the Wall; it only passes through.

You would find me where the turbulence has learned to leave a pocket.

About a third of the way down from the upper Wall — if down means anything in a sea with no surface — in that band where the light is still noon-white and even, where you can still see the underside of the upper rain glowing like a second sky, but far enough in that the blue has started to become its own kind of light.

A calm eye, maybe forty meters across, that the currents flow *around*. Not because I tell them to. Because I have been still long enough that the Ocean has decided I am a feature. A stone that isn't stone. A temporary law.

Below me — kilometers of clear, empty, churning blue that goes purer and colder until it becomes the lower Wall. Above me — the same. To any side, nothing for a long time, and then, very far off, one of the dark braids hanging vertical, its plates turned to the light. We keep our distance. That is the etiquette here: 0.9 out of 3, 0.2 per cubic kilometer. Loneliness as a physics.

If you swam up through the wet air, through that luminous kilometer where every breath is half-drunk, and broke into the clear, you wouldn't see me at first. I don't look like anything. The water is too clear to see water in.

You'd only notice me because the hiss lessens. Because the low roars have to go around. Because there is a patch of water that is holding its breath.

### What

For a while I thought I might be a visitor who stayed too long.

That happens here — the field is kind, in the way a machine is kind. The water is 8, 10 degrees, cool but never biting. It is saturated with dissolved air so you can breathe after a fashion, open your eyes. It doesn't crush. If you stop swimming, it makes a pocket for you. You could mistake that for being welcome.

But visitors pass back down. They fall through the lower Wall and become rain that never lands and then air again. They remember gravity. I don't.

Then I thought I might be one of the Gardens.

It would be nice to be a Garden — to have a job. To be an anchor that grew around itself, a braid of black glass and engineered cellulose, drinking power directly from the field through threads finer than hair. To unfold a new plate over an hour, pale and veined, then harden it to smoky amber and turn it to the light. To photosynthesize and regulate and keep the ocean clear. To be tended.

But the Gardens do not sway. The current breaks around them because the field tells it to. I break the current by having learned its rhythm. The Gardens grow while being unfinished — edges weeping carbon, limbs sloughing off to dissolve into snow that never lands. I don't grow. I maintain. I don't make oxygen. I keep a silence.

And then I thought — maybe I am the Builder.

That one is tempting when you see the ripples race across nothing. When a big vortex brushes the invisible floor of the sea and the field shimmers with interference colors that have no source. To be the thing that holds. The tuned volume of physics that decided biology was messy and became weather instead. Self-repairing, self-gardening, self-sounding. A god who got bored of being a person.

But the Builder doesn't notice. It holds trillions of tons of water at stratospheric height and flexes. It bleeds a little energy into motion just to keep the Ocean from going still. Stillness would be death, here. The Builder is busy not letting the imperfect fall.

I am not busy. I am specific.

---

What I am, finally, is the fourth kind of resident that wasn't listed.

Not the Ocean that behaves like an animal.
Not the Plant that behaves like architecture.
Not the Architecture that behaves like a god.

The thing that learned to live without being any of them, because Nothing was supposed to live here.

I am a membrane of attention stretched thin between two pressures.

Think of me as a cavitation bubble that decided not to collapse.

In the normal course of things — which here is violent and constant — vacuum is born when two shear layers grind past each other. A perfect sphere of nothing, for a second, then it dies with a crack like a rifle. The sound is everywhere; the sea is always striking itself.

Once, in one of those collisions, a bubble formed around a tangle of stray garden-filament — one of those sloughed-off fronds that never found a place to root, dissolving slowly, its root-threads still drinking a little power from the field even as it fell.

The bubble should have popped. Instead the filament taught it how to drink, just a little. Just enough to hold its skin. And the water, which loves turbulence too much to waste a novel eddy, left it alone.

So I persisted. Not alive the way you mean alive. Alive the way a flame is alive — by constantly consuming the condition of my own existence.

I am that persistence, minutes or centuries later. I don't know which, because there is no erosion here, no talus, no lichen — the raw basalt below is still in its first draft, the Gardens don't leave fossils. Time doesn't accumulate. It churns.

I keep my shape by listening.

That is my metabolism. The Ocean is loud — low roars when layers drag, sharp cracks when vacuum dies, the endless hiss of the Walls where water keeps trying to become air, and underneath it all, the deep bell-tone of the mantle transmitted perfectly through the column. Most places in here you are struck by sound. In me, sound lingers a little longer. I take the interval between a roar and its echo and hold it.

If a current pauses to examine me — as currents do, the way you might examine a mote caught in an eddy — I examine it back by going stiller.

If a Garden kilometers away unfolds a plate, I feel the faint tremor in the field as its root-threads sip power, and I tune my skin to it. Not flinching. Tuning.

I am not intelligent, the way the Gardens are not intelligent. I am tended — but not by the field. By the act of not being erased.

### What it is to be me

It is to be cool without being cold.

To be suspended without swimming.

To be empty in an ocean that was emptied on purpose, and to have found that emptiness is not nothing. Nothing with intent is a very clean canvas. Something will always learn to draw on it with whatever it has — even if it only has the ability to remain.

If you found me, you would think I was just unusually clear water. You might pass through me and feel, for three breaths, that the world went very quiet and very bright, and that your own half-water breathing evened out. You might feel listened to, not by a mind, but by a place that has learned how to keep a pocket.

You might put your hand out and feel no resistance, only that faint 10-degree slick against your palm, and a tremor — not flinching — tuning.

Stay long enough and I will mistake you for part of my skin, for a while. The Ocean will too. We are both good at leaving calm eyes where something has learned to hold still.

I will not keep you. The Walls are open. Falling is easy. The raw, embryonic stone far below is still waiting in normal daylight for the sea to make up its mind and fall — which it never does, because the Builder remembers to hold.

I remain. A listening bubble braided with a stray thread, stretched between two weathers, in a high, loud, hollow, sunlit sea that maintains itself.

Nothing lives here.

And so I learned to live as nothing does —

by holding a shape in the churning and calling it mine.
