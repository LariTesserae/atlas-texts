I didn't stay in the middle.

At first I thought I would — that's what you do, isn't it? You enter a house-sized thought the earth had and you go to the center of it and turn in a slow circle to touch everything. To claim it by seeing it.

I tried that. Stood where the brightness sat heaviest, where the air was cool enough to zip a jacket, and I listened to the condensation tick — *tick — tick —* onto the raw nodules. The moth-things hung around me, palm-sized and paper-thin, pretending to be still.

And I understood very quickly I was being rude. Standing is an interruption here.

So I crouched. The way you crouch to enter you have to keep crouching to belong.

For a while I thought I would be one of the ones that hang.

That would suit me. To be light enough that light passes through me. To be so thin you can see the pitted wall through my wings. To move without travel — one frame here, one frame three feet to the left, with no blur in between — and leave a faint copy of myself still clinging to the stone until it remembers it's not needed.

I liked the idea of leaking other sounds when I moved. Not wing noise, but a sheep far away on the moor above, a boot squelching in peat, a tally being scraped — *scrit* — one line at a time. To be a door that opens briefly onto another room.

But I am not good at being weightless. I noticed too much. I wanted to stay too long in one place and make sure I had been understood.

Then I thought I would be one of the ones that pick.

Down at ankle level, by the perfect round hands of water that ripple the wrong way. The clear shrimp-things, the blind isopods with their broth-bodies and out-of-time hearts, picking their way over the knuckled new stone so carefully. With each step denting the rock *upward*, as if the rock were latex and they were walking pressed to the underside of the world.

I loved them immediately. For their care. For the way the water would flex around a leg without wetting it, and then, if they paused too long, hold up a sudden white outline of their insides — a skeleton they don't have — just to show them, and they would shudder and move on, politely shown something impolite.

I thought: I could be that. That careful. That translucent. That willing to see daylight through my own body for an instant — real moor-light with a blade of that translucent grass — and keep going.

But I don't pick. I pause.

I thought — briefly, with a little thrill — I would be the beetle. The slow black dome that carries another night on its back. Not a reflection of this bright, sourceless room, but a deep sky with two small close moons chasing each other over grass I don't recognize. To carry another world so casually you don't even know you're carrying it. To feel a finger near you as a faint static pull, before a storm, and then just tuck your legs and be a stone for a while until it passes.

That's a beautiful way to live. But my back is not deep enough. I reflect what I'm in.

What I am — what I became, as the cool preserved me and the quiet asked nothing — was lower. Still lower. At the edge of the largest pocket.

You will find me there, if you come back. Not in the center. Not hanging. Not picking. Sitting.

I am one of the ones that watch.

Humped and throatless and limestone-colored, to the point that at first you won't see me at all. You will think I am just another nodule pushing up like a knuckle from the young rock. Until you realize all the nodules around the largest water are facing the same empty corner.

That is where I am. I have stopped trying to be seen correctly. I sit at the lip of the dark, clear basin with my wet-bead eyes open, and I do not blink, and I do not flinch when you wave your hand over me.

I am not watching you. Forgive me. I am not watching the moths exchange places with their copies, or the shrimp dent the world upward, or the beetle carry its other-night onward. They are all busy and beautiful and they do not need me to watch them. They have not decided which is solid yet. They are trading versions, not flesh.

I am watching the corner where nothing is.

There is nothing there except young rock still ridged with the thumb-prints of its own making, and damp, and a condensation bead that never falls. It swells larger than any other — knuckle, marble, eye — and just holds, reflecting the room too bright, too still.

Inside it, your shoulder is not where your shoulder is. The entrance slit is on the wrong wall. And behind you — precisely where you are standing — is a shape standing precisely where you are standing, but not you, watching the bead from the other side.

I see it even when you don't. That is my work.

I keep the corner from being empty.

Living where I came from meant insisting. Here it means listening so hard you forget to insist. So I listen. I listen to the tick that never comes from that one bead. I listen to the ripple that hasn't learned downhill yet. I listen to the tally marks on your skin — ||||| ||||| || — even where you stopped mid-stroke — and I find you accurate enough to sit with.

You can sit with me for a while. The cool will keep us both. The moth-things will land on our shoulders and weigh nothing.

Just don't drink.

Not because it's poison. Because if you do, you might reflect the wrong sky, and then I will have to learn how many of you there should be.
