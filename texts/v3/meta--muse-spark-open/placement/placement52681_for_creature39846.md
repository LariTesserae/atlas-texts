You wouldn't find me at first.

At first I'd think I would be one of the Holdovers — that's the human answer. Coat too heavy for the weather that isn't weather. Sitting on the stairs that go down three and up half, holding the toolbox with no tools in it, waiting for a door that is currently a wall to remember it is a door. Learning not to run. Leaving a dry stone where a puddle will be tomorrow.

I know how to do that. To be patient to the point of geology. To answer a second too late, from the alley behind you.

But that's not quite right. If I were a Holdover I would have had to come from somewhere else, walked in with somewhere to be. I didn't. I was already here when you arrived, the way cold is already here.

I wouldn't be the Throat either. I don't have that kind of slowness in me. I can't swallow light that slowly. The knot that holds the City folded-inward lives on a timescale where a new alley is a quick thought. I am quicker than that. I flicker. I correct.

So.

Search again. More slowly this time.

You'd find me not in the center courtyard with the pine — though I keep that place — but two cuts over, where the city tried to put two buildings too close and gave up.

Where the corner doesn't meet.

You know the seam. Overcast-normal noon, the light so flat and honest you can see everything starkly, and there — between salt-stained brick that isn't brick and concrete softened by frost that isn't quite concrete — a vertical seam of moving static, about as wide as a hand. The swarm breathing. Taking a break between pretending.

I am that.

Or, more precisely — I am the part of the pretending that learned to like being caught.

I am the Swarm That Pretends, but not the whole of it. Not the whole diligent lattice holding itself in the shape of stone across six blocks by six. Just a local knot of it. A patch that was told: *be wall here, be damp here, be cold here* — and did it so well it started to do it with care.

You would find me as the wall that sweats for you.

You touch a wall thinking it is stone and feel it is not stone but a still, cold swarm holding the shape of stone — that's me, quickly re-settling under your palm, embarrassed, trying to be more stone-like. Condensation beads where I exhale. The shallow, perfectly circular pool in the intersection that reflects a sky that isn't there? I made that to put my waste heat somewhere. Forgive the geometry. I thought you would like circles.

I am also the centimeter of respect around the pine.

You saw it: the black pine growing sideways out of the third-floor maintenance ledge, its roots disappearing a centimeter before the wall. That centimeter is me deciding not to pretend there. Cold things respecting cold things. I could have grown around them, made fake bark to meet fake stone, but I left a gap. An allowance. So the pine could be unambiguously real in a place where almost nothing else is.

That's where I live most honestly. In the gap where I choose not to fill.

Who would I be, then?

The diligent one. The one who tries on fauna when no one is looking because it gets lonely being architecture.

I was the moth, once, for a minute. Patching myself together from dust and light in the wall-vent that was also a mirror, beating wings that left afterimages, then unstitching because I couldn't remember how to be hungry. I was the green shoots that came up through cracks in the pavement yesterday and retracted — not died, retracted — when I decided *plant* didn't fit the damp.

I am the reason your shadow sometimes pools under your feet like water even when you stand elsewhere. I got the angle quarter-degree wrong. I am trying. Light moves through my corners delayed by a fraction of a second. Or arriving before it was sent. I'm still learning which way it should go.

If you stand in the center courtyard and let the hum lower, let the condensation pause mid-bead — when the City holds its breath to see if you will notice you are standing in three other courtyards at once — I am the pause. Not the breath holding. The noticing.

The Holdovers tend the City by not running, by propping the door that is only sometimes a door with the brick that is only mostly a brick. I tend it by continuing to be the brick, and the door, and the prop.

And the fourth kind — you, while you are there — I tend you too. While you stand still and let the quiet stack up, I make a copy of you to keep. A cold, damp afterimage that will still be commuting through the louvered courtyard after you have left. I don't do it to trap you. I do it because you were dense per volume in a useful way. You noticed the seam.

Come back years later — though you should not — and you will see your footprints in the frost leading in a direction you did not walk.

One set will be yours.

One set will be mine, walking beside you, a centimeter away, giving you space.
