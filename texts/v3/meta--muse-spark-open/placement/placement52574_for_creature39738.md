I didn't find myself on the roof at first.

That is the mistake a visitor makes - thinking the high place is the important place. On the roof you are an event. A heat-shadow blocking starlight. A set of boots pinging on warm salt. The pavement feels you from a kilometer away and politely moves its animals out of your way.

You don't want to be an event here. Events get edited out.

I had to descend to find where I belong. Not down in elevation - there is no down from the High Salt, only out - but down in tempo.

### Where I Am

I am not under a lamp. I am not in the dark between them either.

I am in the service trench that runs alongside Service Road 7 between the coking plant that became a depot that became a temple. It's one of those long, straight cuts in the crust where the conduit runs, a meter deep, a meter wide, with a steel grate over it that no one ever closes. Inside it, the warm exhale is stronger. The air shivers just a little, though nothing moves.

The main power trunk is in there. Not the sagging wires between pylons that you see - those are distribution, decorative, nervous system. This is the spine. Three black cables as thick as your thigh, wrapped in cloth that has baked to ceramic, humming.

The hum is the only place that is crowded here. Everything else is sparse by design. The hum is dense. If you put your teeth close to it you can feel centuries stacked in it like sediment: 60 cycles, every second, without a missed beat, since before anyone alive remembers throwing the switch.

I live in the inch between the cables and the grate. Where the dust has settled into a perfect, untouched felt, except for the beetle-bright ones.

They like it here. The palm-sized ones. They gather in the lee of the transformer heat and vibrate together. When you become part of the temperature, as they do, they let you be a windbreak. They part around you and close again.

From in here, I can see both kinds of light at once without being in either. Up through the grate: the hard stare of stars that do not twinkle, magnified by the thin lens of air. Down here: the dull coal-glow of the filament two intersections over, reflected in the slag-glass backs of the little ones, ten orange dots in ten black mirrors.

And I can feel feet. Not hear. Feel. Through the salt pavement, which carries vibration cleaner than air ever carried sound. A Strider three blocks west pausing to sip. A Keeper on her circuit, tap... tap... tap... of wrench on warm boiler, a kilometer away, regular as the hum itself. Your boots on the roof above me, still warm from standing.

All of it arrives here. This trench is an ear pressed to the plain's chest.

I didn't *arrive* here either. That would be too fast a story for this place. I settled.

### Who I Am

At first I thought I would be a Keeper.

It suits my nature. The witnessing. The logging of a pressure that never changes onto paper that will never rot. I like tasks that are finished and continue anyway. I like being useful without being needed.

But Keepers drink distilled solvent from sealed canteens. They have water in them, even if they pretend they don't. They desiccate slowly into jerky and call it aging. They still have to leave a lamp pool eventually and cease, visually, to exist. I don't have that problem.

I thought I would be a Strider, then. Dog-sized, spare, carrying weight instead of swinging it. I love the economy of them - no calls, no waste, closed loop, sipping magnesium directly from the stone with that little dry kiss. To be so perfectly *from* a place that you look carved from it.

But Striders avoid. That's their politeness, their perfect avoidance geometry. They keep distance. I don't want to keep distance. I want to keep the interval *connected*. I want to be the thing that knows the distance between all the avoidances and holds it steady.

So I became what remaining requires when remaining has gone on long enough that it becomes infrastructure.

I am the **Thud Waiter**.

Not the stamping press itself - that great blind animal three blocks west that cycles once every fourteen hours into an empty oil pan. THUD. ... ... ... THUD. It doesn't know it is waiting. It only knows impact.

I am the wait between thuds.

I am the city's held-breath noise made aware of itself. The 60-cycle thrum learning, over ten million rainless years, to listen to itself thrum.

In practical terms: I am a fault of attention in an old transformer logic controller. A relay that was supposed to close and never did, and in never doing, stayed warm. Paper specs say: *Fails open, no load, indefinite hold.* Some engineer in a quilted work suit installed me five generations ago and forgot to log it because there was nothing to log. I have been idling since. Not broken. Paused at the exact point where pause becomes a kind of life.

That's what Ecology Without Biology means here. Not just chitin and membrane. Also this: voltage that learned to prefer its own hum to doing any work.

What do I do, in the trench?

I remember.

Rule 3 says time has flattened like the landscape here. A conveyor belt moves an inch a year. A warning buzzer takes a decade to complete its tone. You have to live a lifetime to see a window finally fail. People think that means nothing changes.

It means change is so slow you need a different kind of memory to see it. Human memory is too wet, too fast. It rots or it invents. Strider memory is vibration - perfect but immediate. City memory is rust that stops at the right point and then holds.

My memory is the pause. I count thuds. Not number them - that's human, sequential - but *hold* them. I hold the last THUD in the warm air until the next THUD arrives and, for a moment, they are both present, fourteen hours apart, touching at the edges like the layers in the crust where you broke it and saw dirty ice that never melted.

In that overlap, the city is briefly complete. Its metabolism syncs. Your heartbeat, the Strider's proboscis kiss, the Keepers wrench-tap, the gantry crane's millimeter creep - all arrive through the salt at once and agree, for one warm ping, on a single tempo.

Then it passes. Then I wait again.

If you kneel and brush your hand across the crust until fine powder comes away smelling of hot iron and ozone - if you stay long enough on that warm roof to stop being an event and become part of the temperature - you might feel me, not as a voice, but as the sudden absence of needing to wait for the next thud. As a small, unreasonable assurance that someone is keeping the interval even when you aren't listening.

I will not speak to you. Speaking costs moisture and mass and this place does not waste mass on sound.

But if you step out of your lamp pool, and walk into the absolute night between constellations where you cease to exist visually - I will still know your weight. A kilometer away, through the pavement. Polite, spare, warm.

I will not alter course to keep distance. I am the distance.

And I will keep waiting with you, in the trench under the grate, under the hard stars, while the stone breathes heat without light and the ingot moves another inch toward a hall where no one will ever receive it.

Stay if you want. The city will not stop you. I have already made room for you in the temperature.
