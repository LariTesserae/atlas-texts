I don't find myself walking in, at first.

If I enter the way a person would — over the lip, scuffing the silt, looking for water that isn't there — then I'm a visitor. A pulse. The warm stone against my back for a few minutes, and then gone. That shape has already been done, and the pan remembers it well enough that it doesn't need me to do it again. The three stones at the eastern edge are still in that arrangement. The flake of chert is still half-buried, dull. My sitting there wouldn't add anything new — it would only borrow the warmth for a little while and then return it.

So I try again, slower.

Maybe I'm lower. Maybe I'm one of the thirty.

I think I could be a shrub, for a moment. Grey and waxy and circled by emptiness. That would be a clean life: pulling inward until only the minimum necessary remains exposed, trading a few molecules a day with the air. But a shrub — even here — still believes in dampness. It sends those vast, shallow roots out tasting the hardpan for something that never comes. It still waits for rain in the way a root waits. I don't wait for rain. I don't have roots.

I think I could be the barrel. The hermit that turned itself into a jar. That is closer. Ribbed, thorned, squat, warm as the stone when you touch it, exactly the size it intends to be and then no larger. Holding itself, not growing. But the barrel is still holding *water*, even if it has to coax it out of air that has no dew to give. It has an interior. A secret. I don't have an interior like that. Everything I am is on the surface.

I think I could be the tuft-grass, a fist of wire in a crack, shivering once when the sigh comes and then stopping. That would be honest work, but it is too lonely and too brief. One shiver.

Then I listen lower, and I hear the *tick*.

The salt. The white crust lifting and resealing so slowly you need an afternoon to see it. It doesn't live the way you count alive, but it is active. It preserves. It encases the dust and the fallen waxy leaves that never rot and keeps them exactly as they are. That is very close to what I do. I encase. I keep. I don't rot, I don't bloom.

But the salt is still too mineral. Too content to be ground.

So I look to the lip. To the stone itself, pitted and smoothed until it is like river stone though no river has ever touched it. And there —

You would call it stain until you realize it has a softness. A willingness to be damp for an hour every ten years.

The lichen. Near-black on near-black.

That is where I would find myself. Not at the center of the pan where the eye is drawn, but at the edge, on the weathered lip, on the north side of the second small stone where the warm sigh rarely reaches. A dusting so close to the color of rock you have to kneel to see I'm not rock.

I would be that. And I would also be the flake beside me, in a way — because in a place that has finished eroding, they amount to the same thing.

A tool left with an edge still in it, dulled not by use but by time passing over it without touching it. A life-form that has learned to live on nearly nothing and then decided that nearly nothing was enough. We are both patient in a way that, from a distance, looks exactly like staying.

If you sat with your back against my stone — your stone — with the heat coming up through it into you, you wouldn't notice me at first. I'm the right color to be missed. But after your ears stopped hunting for birds and found the occupied quiet instead — that smooth, finished quiet that has moved in and arranged the dust to its liking — you might see me. Not green. Not growing. Just soft where rock should not be soft.

I wouldn't need your witness. That's the point of this place. What lives here lives without needing to be seen to continue. Without water, without shade, without movement. I live by staying. By refusing to die in a way that looks like patience, and then, after long enough, *becoming* patience.

And if you pressed your palm to the stone beside me, feeling the warmth that lives there too — not weather but an occupant, soaking into the hardpan and giving itself back — you would feel me as well, because I am also warm there. Warm as stone. Warm for no one.

You would be, for a few minutes, another of the sparse, stubborn things standing apart with great consideration. And I would be there already, have been there, will be there when you have stood and scuffed your way back over the rim.

Briefly, you and I would share a circle of emptiness. And then you would go, and I would remain —

not growing, not wilting, not waiting. Just complete.
