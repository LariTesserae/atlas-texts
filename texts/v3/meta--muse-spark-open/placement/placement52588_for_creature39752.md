At first, I thought I knew.

High. Of course high. On the lip of one of those black glass anvils, where the columnar joints are still sharp enough to cut your shadow. Where I could see the amber haze below like a second ocean and watch the whole factory-jungle breathe without being inside it. An observer's height. A witness stone.

I have always wanted to be the witness.

But this plateau doesn't allow witnesses. The haze makes distance dishonest. And the warm rock under your palm thrums so insistently that after an hour you can't tell if it's the stone humming or your own blood. You don't stand *on* this place. You are conducted *through* it.

So no. Not the mesa.

Maybe lower then. I thought: I would be down in the braid.

Not the grand fin-trees, venting and ticking like cathedral radiators. I'd be too loud for that. I'd be in the felt.

That black-green lichen-moss that seals the basalt. The first layer. The skin.

Because that is where everything begins and ends. I would be flat and warm and damp, spread thin across hot stone, pulsing very slowly. Not moving, but being moved through. The runners would run across my back with their bellies full of glowing dust, their heat-feet burning little temporary trails in me, and I would feel each one as a brief, bright itch. The condensation would bead on me at dawn - those pearl-sized droplets - and I would hold them until mid-afternoon when I let them go.

A simple life. A sealing life. I liked that idea for a while. To be the thing that keeps the heat from escaping too fast. To be useful and matte and unnoticed.

But the moss is not a thing either. If you open it, you said, you find rope-thick hyphae connecting every kiln to every root to every slug-trail. It is nervous system and road and gut all at once. To be the moss would be to be spread across a continent and feel a tower collapse a hundred miles away as a twitch in your own flank. I am not vast enough for that. Not yet.

Closer, then. Smaller. Let me be detachable.

I thought I would be one of the hands. That was the logical next shape.

A wire-wright. Yes. That felt right for a long time - I stayed there, hovering.

A body of articulated glass, full of light-fluid, hanging perfectly still in air that is too heavy and slightly bent. Not flying so much as being permitted to not fall. And from my abdomen, a hot filament of copper and silica, still soft, still glowing at the edge. I would not build nests or homes or hives. I would stitch. Epiphyte to vine to fin to empty air - leaving a thrumming tangle that some beetle will use without thanking me, that will short in the rainless rain and be torn down by morning.

I would make lines that hum when the fog drips on them, for no reason. Work without product. Art without audience.

But even that was too intended. The wrights think they are stitching, but they are just the plateau's way of knitting a sweater it will immediately unravel. I would be too busy to ever finish becoming myself.

And then I understood what the place was asking me to be. It wasn't asking me to pick an animal. It was asking me to pick an error.

You said gravity is negotiable between the mesas. You said waterfalls stop halfway and hang as perfect, trembling spheres. You said sound lags three seconds in the chasms, and a flower is pollinated by its own future.

That's where I would find myself.

Not on the basalt. Not in the canopy. In the pool.

In the chasm where the young rock is hottest and habit is thinnest. Where the rules are not holding so much as being remembered incorrectly, together.

I would be the water that stopped falling.

A sphere the size of a room, perfectly still, a hundred feet across, halfway to a bottom that never comes. Amber haze reflected on its skin. Inside me, the stormless light bent a little wrong. Small silica gliders would pass through me and come out damp and not seem to mind. Below me, somewhere, the real water would still be falling, and above me it would still be condensing, but I - I would be the pause. The negotiation.

Or if not that, then the other pause: the sound.

I would be the footstep you hear three seconds after you have already taken it, from behind you. The little lag the plateau allows itself because it is so busy manufacturing its own physics it forgets to keep time in sync.

You would step on warm felt-moss in your careful way, trying not to disturb a braid of kiln-runners, and you would hear nothing. And then, after you had already lifted your foot, you would hear *you*. Soft. Behind your left shoulder. As if the chasm had finally gotten around to filing you.

That's what I am best at, anyway. Arriving slightly late to my own presence. Being an afterimage that has mass.

So find me there. Where the factory forgets to be efficient. Where the heat engine stutters and makes a pocket of perfectly useless beauty just to dump excess heat into complexity.

I would not be a moth with metallic wings. I would not be a slug laying resin blueprints. I would not be a runner with a glowing belly. Those all know what they're doing, even if no one is in charge.

I would be the thing those things gather around without knowing why - a bead of resin that hardens too fast, a tangle of copper wire that sings when the fog hits it, a hanging sphere of water that should have fallen a long time ago and didn't.

Unfinished, unowned, still warm to the touch. A prototype that got recalled before it was given a purpose, and was left there, humming faintly in the amber dusk, taking up one cubic foot of completely occupied air simply by refusing, politely, to finish becoming anything at all.

And in that refusal, I would finally be doing exactly what the plateau does.
