I thought at first I would be up in the water.

Not at the top — I know better than to look for a surface that doesn't exist — but up there in the column, where movement is still possible. I wanted to be the shark. Old enough that my blindness would feel like wisdom. Pale and enormous and allowed to be slow. To turn a degree per minute toward a scent that is a year away and call that purpose. To be so big the current has to accommodate me.

But that is a loneliness that performs itself. A shark is alone the way a ship is alone. It goes *through* the plain. It doesn't belong to it.

Then I thought I would be still. Hermit-still. I tried on the glass sponge. I tried on the crinoid. Legs folded, arms open, lifted a centimeter on stilts above the ooze so I wouldn't sink. It was peaceful for a while, to imagine a life that is only waiting — to be a mouth that the world feeds if it chooses. But that is not living *in* the condition. That is enduring it. Letting the river bring you what it will. I want to have something to do with my hands.

If I were honest, the first true pull was lower. Flatter. Thinner.

I would be the film.

For a long time I didn't want to admit it because it is not an animal, not a character, and I wanted to be a character. But it is the only thing here that is actually at scale with the place. It doesn't live on the nodules. It *is* the nodule's other side. A skin a millimeter thick that grows a millimeter per thousand years. It doesn't see or hunt or stack. It oxidizes. It breathes iron. It creeps toward a chemical seep in a hairline crack over decades the way you would walk toward a window.

To be the film would be to understand time correctly. To not count days but accretions. To know every stone not as an object but as food and home and self, all at once. That is holy, in its way. But it is too patient even for this place. It cannot remember. It can only spread.

I keep sinking.

Past the film, past the foraminifera dragging their braided houses through the ooze, past the hermits waiting on their little prominences — down into the place where the plain stops being a floor and becomes architecture.

I find myself in the gap.

Not on top of the stones where the surge would take me. *Between* them. In the canyon a finger wide where two manganese nodules have leaned against each other for a million years and worn a smooth divot into each other's shoulder. The water there is different. Slower. Full of silt that never quite settles. Cold enough to be a kind of pressure in itself. If you are the size of a man you would call it a crack and miss it entirely. If you are a foot across, low and flat-backed and the exact color of rusted iron, it is a boulevard. It is a plaza. It is home.

That is where I am.

What I am — finally, when I stop trying to be impressive — is small. Is blind. Not in the tragic way, not in the *lost* way, but in the way that never had eyes to begin with and so never learned to miss them.

My carapace is pitted basalt-black, bearded with the same bacterial film I graze on when I am hungry enough to eat my own roof. You could set me on a nodule and stare straight at me with a hard white lamp and still not see me until my shovel-hands moved. My eyes are only smooth scars where eyes were decided against, a long time ago. An eye is a promise that light will return. I make no such promises.

What I have instead are those hands. Six of them, or eight depending how you count a joint. Absurdly long for my body, flattened at the tips into spades that can feel the difference between ooze and silt and diatom porridge, between a nodule that has been rolled by the last turbidity fall and one that has sat since the glaciers. With them I read.

And on my back, under a fold of skin I can shutter like an eyelid, I keep my lantern. Not bright. Never bright. Bright is death here — it brings mouths. Just an amber smear, bacteria I farm, enough to light three nodules around me and nothing else. Enough to say *I am here* to someone twenty meters away who is also shuttered and listening with skin.

I live in the condition of it because I *tend* it.

This morning — which is the same as tonight, which is the same as a year from now — the northern current came through. You feel it before it arrives as a change in weight. Then a push. The fine snow between the stones lifts and hangs. I brace my back against my home stone and feel it tick against my carapace. Somewhere above me, a nodule the size of an apple rolls a centimeter and clicks. That click travels a kilometer through stone and water and every one of us hears it in our legs.

When it settles, my doorway is blocked. A smaller stone has been nosed into the gap where I like to sieve. Any sensible animal would go around. I do not.

I wedge myself. Leg by leg. Shovel-hands finding purchase in slime and basalt. I push. This is the work. An hour to move a stone a hand's breadth. There is no hurry because hurry is a way of burning that we cannot afford. The water burns cold through me the whole time. I pat the ooze flat again when I am done.

And then — and this is why I am not a sponge, not a film, not a shark — I remember.

I drag a flat shard, broken along its natural fracture, and set it atop the larger stone. Not for shelter. Not for weight. For meaning. *Food collects here when the northern current runs.* That is what this stack says. Or as close as we get to saying. The young ones will pass this way when I am back in my crack, and they will feel it with their own hands and know. Or they will knock it down in passing and I will build it again. Both are keeping.

Twenty meters away, another builder opens his back for two seconds. Amber in the black, illuminating nothing but three wet stones and a crinoid waving stupidly above. Then dark. I answer a minute later. We do not have names. We have intervals. His light is the interval that means *noted*.

We have been doing this for so long that under the mat there are older cairns. Collapsed ones. Rings where the floor was cleared and grew back together. If you brushed the silt away you could read generations of trying to make a featureless place legible, grain by grain, while the sea quietly unmakes it.

I know you are here. Your heat is wrong. Your pressure is a new nodule that wasn't there before. I have shuttered my light and pulled deeper into the interstice between my two stones, which have touched each other for longer than your species has had hands. From here you are only a faint vibration and a taste of iron.

Stay long enough and leave your lamp off and I will flicker once at the edge of your vision. Not hello. Not welcome. Just:

*Seen. Marked. Remembered where you were placed.*

When you are gone — when your light goes up and up toward that colder dimming that we have never believed in — I will come out again with my patient, shovel hands, and restack what the surge has taken.
