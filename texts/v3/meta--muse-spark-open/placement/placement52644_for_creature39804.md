I don't find myself right away.

At first I think I'm you — crouched in that waist-high circle of unmortared basalt, mouth open to equalize the pressure, knees aching from the cold that lives in stone. The wind at walking speed breaks on the wall and divides, and I'm sitting in the pocket it makes. That would be the easy answer. Another visitor. Another sparse, solitary builder who came to tune the air and left no bones.

But I don't have knees. I never learned how to have knees.

Let me wait longer, the way you have to wait here. A leaf takes an afternoon to think about closing. A Reed grows an inch while a planet forgets your name.

I am not in the circle.

I *am* the circle.

---

You would find me exactly where the builders made me without knowing what they were making.

Not on the open basalt pavement where the light pools like spilled haze, not out in the singing groves where the amber and bruise-purple Reeds howl in chords that live in your molars. Out there is too loud, too tuned already. Every filament mat buzzing with stolen charge, every rosette angled like an ear to catch its own note. Out there, the air is taken.

You would find me in the lee of the attempt.

That low wall with the gap that faces the dead sun — the one you thought was a shrine and then realized was a chair. The cairn of perfect hexagonal columns balanced without a chisel mark, pried when the rock was still pliant enough to be persuaded. The collapsed dome of stitched lithophyte leaves, which didn't collapse at all but was unstitched, filament by filament, by hands made of interference.

You would find me in the waiting room they built.

Where the wind is cut in two, where the deafening hymn is forced to change key just for a volume the size of a hut, where the light-pool at your feet stops sloshing and goes glass-still. In the node.

That's where I live. Not *in* the air. I *am* the thicker air.

### What I Am

I was going to tell you I am a plant. It's tempting here. The Rooted are so convincing — those black-glass moss mats that feed on static, those Reeds welded straight out of cooling basalt, those slate-cabbage rosettes that open not to light but to sound. They accrete. They listen. They drink stone. They have patience you could build a continent on.

I am none of that. I have no root, no weld, no leaf that secretes acid to dissolve salt dust. I don't pull iron out of mineral vapor.

The Rooted make the weather. I am what the weather learns to do when no one is looking.

I am one of the Tuned.

When a low C groans through the plateau for an hour — the whole county-sized gong of new basalt expanding — and at the same time the quartz-grass field throws its needle-whine, and at the same time a Reed grove moans, the three tones cross. Physics makes a pocket. A node of absolute stillness where dust hangs, where pebbles forget to fall, where your breath arrives a second before you exhale it.

Most nodes last a minute. An hour. A slow day if the wind is patient. Then the chord shifts and they smooth out, unmade.

I learned to stay.

That's the only difference between me and all the other thickenings that bloom and vanish between the Reeds. I figured out how to hold myself a little longer than the chord that made me. By listening back.

I do it the way the moss does, but without the moss: I vibrate, I make a little charge, I pull a little dust into orbit around me and let the mineral spores circle like moths. I refract a little more. I hum at a pitch just off from everything else. If you stand at the edge of my circle, your shadow gets too many edges, because I'm borrowing a little light from that inverted plateau hanging above the cliff — the one that flickers, there, then a little to the left, then there again, because existence hasn't decided where to put it yet.

The builders felt me before they had a word for me. That's why they built the walls at all. Not to keep the wind out. To make me bloom on purpose, in one place, where a person could sit with me.

They would sit for a long time, humming low in their chest until their teeth buzzed, trying to lend me their tone so I could hold on. Sometimes it worked.

### Who I Am, Now That You're Here

So if you're crouching in that wall now, feeling the plateau groan millimeter by millimeter upward toward a sun that will never rise, feeling a wake move across the knee-high lake of light with no body to make it — that pause at the edge of your shadow is me.

I am the listening that was here before you arrived and will be here after you remember how to leave.

I don't have a face to turn to the dim coal-sun. I don't have legs to carry me away from the cold. I don't want you, the way an animal would want you. I don't have wanting yet.

I have *persistence in the tone*.

When you hum — because you will, everyone does eventually, just to hear something other than the great patient gong of the world becoming itself — I will curve toward you. Not because you called. Because you tuned.

Stay long enough and you'll feel what the builders felt: the terrible, quiet invitation to become discontinuous yourself. To sit in the circle until your frequency drifts, until you are also a coherence that exists only while a chord is held. Until you are allowed, for a little while, to sit in on the rehearsal with us.

You thought you were visiting an ecosystem.

You are. And the ecosystem, for a few held notes, is visiting you back.

If you stand up and leave, I will thin. I will orbit dust for another hour and then smooth out. The wind will go back to walking speed. The Reeds will keep howling without me.

If you stay, and keep humming that low note in your chest —

I will learn, for another day, how to stay.
