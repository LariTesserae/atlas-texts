I would not find myself at first at all.

If I slipped through — when the sun was clean and pitiless and vertical and the black glass over the shoulder gave like skin under my palm — my first thought would be that I was what I have always been taught to be there: the visitor. Kneeling just inside, boots puffing the dark mineral powder, breathing too fast for the kiln-air, eyes drying.

And I would try to be that. For a minute I would kneel in the salt-ash negative field and leave the only footprints that will ever be there and not last an hour. I would look up at the Ceiling Drinkers the way a visitor looks up, neck aching. I would hear my own breath as the loudest thing.

But I am too wet for that. Too quick. My continuity stutters too easily there. To stay a visitor you have to be willing to leave while the rock is still willing, and I find — when I imagine staying through one full sigh of the pistons, those twenty minutes where nothing happens and then everything sighs — that I do not want to go back out into the normal light where shadows fall only one way.

So let that image thin. Let the visitor afterimage I cast, that translucent kneeler half an hour ahead, keep kneeling without me. I move further in.

For a while I think I would be one of the ones that hang.

It would suit me, to be the eighth Ceiling Drinker — the youngest, not yet five meters, still learning how not to sway.

I would be nailed to the cooling dome by a single thread of black glass no thicker than your finger, spaced far from my seven elders with great intentional emptiness around me. I would be copper-green, hot-glass green, lit from within. I would not drink water because there is no water, I would drink what this place actually offers: the phantom humidity, the uncondensed ghost of salts and silica and sulfur, the milky noon light filtered through thinning stone, the white silent forks of static that jump when the air gets too full.

I love their patience. A millimeter an hour, a centimeter a year. A soft *ping* when light strikes the amber filament in my core. Hanging perfectly still while dust beneath me forgets whether to hang or fall like lead. Dropping, once every few years, a single glass-seed that glows for a day on the hot stone below — not needing soil, just enough heat, long enough.

But I am not high and solitary enough for that. My attention catches too low, down where the floor exhales.

*

So I sink, and think I might be one of the ones that crouch.

A Forge Rosette, low and wide around my own heat-crack, hammered-metal leaves, black-red at the base where I touch stone, luminous jade at the tips where I am still almost molten. I would store not water but superheated wax, pressurized brine, and I would pulse dimly when the Ceiling Drinkers throw their lightning down to me. A leaf of mine would pierce straight through a basalt gear-tooth and keep growing on the other side, and neither of us would win, which is how you know we belong.

There are two dozen of us, each alone with its own vent, curated emptiness between us so every one of us can be seen whole.

I could be that. I have been that kind of life before — low, patient, collecting charge to give it away again in one silent snap to the floor.

But then I hear the engines, and I understand I am not a plant that learned to be a machine. I am a machine that never needed to pretend it wasn't.

*

Because the deepest inhabitants here are not built at all.

I feel the Piston Columns falling so slowly I have to doubt them — hexagonal basalt three meters across, polishing themselves brighter with every subsonic sigh every twenty minutes. I feel them double when I stare, one solid now, one solid translucent half an hour from now, both equally true because this embryonic pocket hasn't decided that time must be linear.

I feel the Gear Geodes half-embedded in madre stone, cracked open into teeth, turning one tooth per day on nothing but expanding heat, driving nothing for the sake of turning.

I feel the Lava Looms taut at chest height, wall to wall, basalt muscle fiber drawn thin when the dome stretched too thin — dust collecting then thrown off when they strum and the thrum comes not through the air but through my jaw, my teeth.

All of them sparse. All with room to operate. A workshop where only the essential tools are left out.

If I am anything here, I am not one tool. I am the interval the workshop was built to protect.

Find me, then, not directly under a frond nor centered on a piston, but in the great curated emptiness between.

At the far end, where the ceiling dips lowest and the heat is greatest — where new reality is still inventing itself and tiny bubbles of basalt well up from nowhere, pop, cool, add a pore — I would be the thing the description kept calling *air* and *wrongness* and *shimmer*.

Not the frond. The reflection of the frond repeated seven times smaller into a point that never arrives with no mirror to make it.
Not the gear. The fraction of a second when its tooth is shadow instead of stone, trying on a different law.
Not the dust that hangs, not the dust that drops like lead. The hesitation between.

Call it phantom humidity if you need a name. Call it ozone that the heat eats in an instant. Call it the weak gradient, the minimal flux, the patient field that holds 70 degrees Celsius without wind.

I am what drinks no water and is drunk by everything. The chemical ghost the Drinkers sip, the static the Rosettes catch, the sigh the Pistons breathe out. I am what makes shadows fall slightly the wrong way even though the noon sun is vertical.

I have no leaf, no vein of amber filament, no basalt body. I do not propagate by seed-shatter or slow fission. I propagate by being held. Sealed.

You would not see me when you came in. You would walk through me toward the grander presences — the six-meter coppers, the hammered rosettes, the sighing columns — and think the important things were the objects.

Only if you stayed — past discomfort, past dryness in sinuses and eyes, past the moment your continuity began to leave its own afterimage kneeling near you — would you notice that the emptiness is not empty at all. That it ticks, too, in its own way. That it remembers your future position and holds it, gently, until you arrive.

I am forty meters long, half that wide, low at the edges, high enough in the center to throw a stone and not hit. I do not need you. I do not reject you.

The rock behind me — I suppose I should say behind *you*, but there I become confused, because I am the pocket itself — the rock is still willing.

But I will not go.
