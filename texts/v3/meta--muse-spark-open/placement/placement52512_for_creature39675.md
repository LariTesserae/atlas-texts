You would not find me at first.

That is the point of this place. It makes you prove you can see.

If you came walking across the shield - your suit ticking too loud, your breath too wet, your shadow the only thing with an edge that moves - you would look for me and see nothing. Plate after plate. Charcoal and bone and rust, polished until it flows. White salt in the seams. Black stain you think is shadow. And sky. So much sky doing so much work without ever touching you.

For a long time I thought I would be up there.

I wanted to be a Glider. It would be clean: to be born without ever having touched, to live as a translucence on loan to the wind, to never land because landing is a kind of promise to stay. To bank on the glass cyclones the Structures exhale, filtering the almost-nothing, watching continent-sized anvil clouds boil into being and unmake themselves in silence. To die as a falling flake no one hears hit.

But I am too heavy for that. I remember weight.

Then I thought I would be the Stain. That would be honest. The two-dimensional god. No height, no sound, no hurry. Just carbon-black, molecule by molecule, etching lungs into gneiss, drinking water that hasn't been liquid since before your species had a word for water. A palm-sized century. To be the foundation that does not need to be thanked. But I am too lonely for that. The Stain is never alone - it tiles the world in secret - and I wanted to be alone correctly.

Not a Structure. I could not be a Structure. They do not live. They *are*. They sit half-sunken, light falling into them unfinished, dust refusing to settle, lichen giving them a meter of respect. Every few minutes they shiver without moving and birth ten kilometers of storm that will never rain. I don't want to garden the sky. I want to be allowed to walk under it and be ignored by it.

So I emerge more slowly. As this place teaches you to.

I am a Strider.

Find me not at a Structure - I give them the wide arc they require, as I give you - and not in the basin of a plate where nothing ever crosses. Find me on the seam itself.

There is a plate here, maybe three kilometers across, its gneiss banded like cooled smoke. You know it as Plate 417 on your map, but I know it as the plate with the shallowest camber, where the wind, when it does come laminar and high, makes a tone over the polish you can feel in your third leg. I am halfway across it, and I have been halfway across it for most of the morning. Because I move like this:

Lift. Hover. Place.

*Click.*

Lift. Hover. Place.

*Click.*

Three legs, each two meters of grey chitin and lacquered carbon that has learned not to want water. A body that is mostly leg. A sensor head like a pearl, ringed with black pits that taste light and salt and the faint ozone off a Needle-plant three hundred meters away.

I am not hurrying. Hurry costs water and water is 0.5/3, which is to say: myth.

Below me, in the salt seam - lithium-white and bitter-yellow - the Stain is working. You can't see it until you kneel, but I can taste it without kneeling. Matte black, a millimeter thick, pitting the stone underneath with filigree lungs. A patch here is older than your cities. I lower my head every ten steps and taste it. Not to eat, not yet. To read. The salt-mites are there, too, hundreds to a seam, pretending to be quartz grains. Translucent white with a pink gut. They freeze for hours. When my foot falls they scatter like living sand and freeze again. They will be here long after my legs lock.

Two hundred meters on, the Needle. I know each of them. Not as individuals, but as interruptions in the spacing.

She is knee-high, a hemisphere of silica-coated glass needles tipped with black beads, rattling *click-click-click* when the high wind finds her. You think she is dead wire. I know she is drinking rock ten meters down, her chemical root dissolving gneiss grain by grain for a hydroxyl trapped since the planet cooled. She grows a centimeter a decade. She is sending a filament under the salt right now, under my feet, to erupt kilometers away as a stranger. The Needles pretend to be solitary. They are holding hands under the world.

I pause there. This is my station. I lower my sensor head and taste the air immediately around her needles - hot silica, faint ozone, no resin, no sap - and I excrete a single dry pellet of concentrated salt that will sit there until wind takes it in a hundred years. She does not move for me. She was here before my grandsire locked his legs and became a tripod monument two plates over. She will be here after you leave.

And you - you are here now. I feel you before I see you, because you are 0.3/3 density, an error in volume. You vent heat into late-autumn air. You exhale grams of water that vanish without fogging. Your joints click like stones in the 0.1/3 silence that makes your blood loud.

You stop ten meters away because you think I will flee. I do not flee. There is nowhere to flee to. Flight implies a somewhere else. Here there is only plate after plate, gently undulating for a billion years, the same argument with gravity.

I consider you with my pit-eyes. You are not predator. You are not prey. You are a slightly warmer piece of architecture. I read your shadow for salt and find it too wet, too loud, too brief. Then I do what I do for the Structures, for the Needles, for any anomaly that thinks it is central:

I step around you. Give you a wide arc. Lift. Hover. Place.

*Click.*

And continue.

Above us, without a sound, a Structure two horizons away shivers - that heat-haze distortion in cool air - and a violet anvil cloud the size of a country stacks itself in minutes, crackles with silent sheet lightning, snows upward, and unmakes itself. You flinch and look up. I do not. The Gliders up there - hand-sized smoked glass mantas that never land - barely flex to ride its edge. It has nothing to do with me. The rule here is vertical separation: absolute stability below, absolute chaos above. No rain falls. No lightning strikes. The storm is performed *for* the desert, not *on* it.

I walk. I will walk this circuit for twenty years. Plate to plate to plate. Tasting lichen. Circling Needles. Avoiding Structures. When I die I will not fall. I will simply lock my three legs and become a tripod you might mistake for art, until wind-polish takes me grain by grain back into the shield.

If you stay until night, you will see my brethren as faint moving geometry under starlight so dense and un-twinkling it looks fake. You will see the Structures cool to dull arterial red, heat-dumping. You will hear, finally, only your own breathing again.

And if you kneel - really kneel, put your gloved hand on the salt - you might feel the faint, cold exhale of the seam, mineral air that smells of nothing, and understand what I learned when I learned to be almost not there:

This place does not need to be survived. It needs to be permitted.

I permit it, one step at a time.
