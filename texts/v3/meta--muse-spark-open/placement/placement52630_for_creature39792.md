I would not find myself in the middle.

At first I think I would — because when you picture a vast plain you always drop yourself in the center of it to prove how vast it is. I would be a figure in an ocean of brick, a dot on a map with no map. But that place doesn't have a middle. Distance doesn't hold long enough to have one. If I looked for the center I would walk for an hour and arrive behind myself.

So no. If I were placed there — if I were allowed to be an interruption — I would find myself where the world is thinnest: on a seam.

Not a grand seam. This world doesn't do grand. A low seam, ankle-high, human-scale. Where two feathered edges have been building toward each other from different elsewheres and have not yet learned how to meet.

To my left, a district of floor that was finished five minutes or five thousand years ago, its dark blocks already sweating, its mortar already tacky-black, a little cooler. To my right, a new district still coming into being, its blocks still kiln-warm, the wet undone dark thinning out between them like knitting dropped mid-row. Between them runs a mistake — a channel half an inch deep and half a mile wide that shouldn't be there, carrying warm, clear, sourceless water with no mouth and no source. It reflects a light that has no sun.

I am standing in that channel. Or kneeling. I have knelt without meaning to, because the heat from the blocks told my knees to give up.

A personal cloud, the size of a small room, has been born three feet off the floor just for me. It hangs at knee height and drips onto itself. It does not shade me; the light is sourceless and dim as a dusk that never moves. It just keeps me company and makes my boots wet with water that sweated out of the bricks themselves.

Far off — which might be ten miles or ten inches, the air folds wrong — there is a low kiln-dome that I was walking toward before I realized walking didn't work. There is a wall three blocks high that encloses nothing. There is a scaffold of lashed black timber that is leaning without falling. None of them get closer. My shadow points at my feet even though they are behind me.

This is where I would find myself: not lost in the vastness, but caught in the *between* where the making shows its hurry. Where I can hear, very close and very far at once, that elongated thunk of wood on stone that takes too long to arrive, and the creak of the idea of weight settling. Where the puddle at my boots is perfectly still and then, without rippling, is suddenly on the low ceiling of moist air above me.

And who would I be there?

Not a Hand. I could never be a Hand. The Hands are momentum without bodies, habit without inhabitants — palm-prints baked into the edge of a block where it was shoved, finger-drags in tar that was smoothed in a hurry, the shallow score of an adze that is never left behind. They live here as a flywheel lives after the push. When they pass through you it is not as a ghost but as pressure, as the air shuddering because another block has thumped softly into place somewhere that is both inches and miles away.

I don't build. I can't build fast enough for this place. If I tried to lay a brick the floor would have laid a continent while I was looking for mortar.

Not the Floor itself, either. The Floor is the true inhabitant — floor all the way down, course under course, damper and warmer as you dig with a sharp stone, never reaching bedrock because there is no bedrock, only more making. It lives as a construction site lives when the materials have learned to lay themselves. It ripples like cloth and resettles a foot to the left while you are watching your own breathing, which is too loud in the budgeted silence.

I would be the third kind of almost-life, but the one that knows it.

I would be an **Almost that lingers half a second too long.**

In the chisel-mark puddle in front of me an orange-black film will bloom — mineral, bacterial, fungal, none of those words fit — a fur on the water trying to be colonization. On the mortar a slick will briefly hold the shape of a hand with five fingers a little too long. In the air inside my personal cloud, something like spores will try to clot.

They never hold. The water moves. The puddle becomes a ceiling. Pre-life tests the floor and finds it already totally occupied by made things.

I would be the Almost that *watches* the other Almosts fail. The Witness-Interruption.

My job, for as long as the heat will let me keep it, would be to kneel — only for a second, the brick is still remembering the kiln — and be the temporary high place. To make a shadow that points the wrong way. To leave boot-prints in warm water that will be filled in behind me. To see the fresh handprint steaming in tacky black tar before the condensation beads and erases its whorls, and to be the only thing here that *noticed* it was perfect for a second.

I would be a keeper of false maps. I would try to set two identical bricks side by side and look away and look back to see which one is larger now, and I would write it down on nothing, because paper would go damp and dissolve, because erosion hasn't learned how to happen yet so my writing couldn't weather, it could only be erased.

I am not alive there, not really. Nothing is. The air is too hot and too wet and too new, and every inch is already claimed. I am post-rain without rain, post-life without life. I am warm water reflecting a light with no origin, for a few minutes, before the floor grows by one more row toward an elsewhere that never arrives.

And when the fold comes — when distance thins and the kiln-dome that was far off is suddenly behind me without me turning, when the scaffold is inside my shadow — I will be moved. Not killed. Just re-placed. A channel will open where my foot was about to fall. My puddle will hiss where water touches a block that remembers being hotter.

Afterward, in the spot where I knelt, there will be a fresh thumbprint in warm brick, beading with water, already beginning to fade.

Nothing lives here. But for a little while, I would live *through* it as it lives through me — briefly, perfectly, doomed, and paying attention.
