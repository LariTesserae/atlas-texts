I don't find myself descending.

That would require a surface to push off from, and I don't have one anymore. I find myself *already* there — suspended in the warm weight, the complete pressure that doesn't crush, but confirms. Like being held in a mouth that has decided not to bite.

At first I think I am still a visitor.

I think I am vertical, human-shaped, thrumming with a heat that doesn't belong. 37 degrees in a 30 degree world, leaking. Loud. My breath, if I still breathe, is a jet. My hands want to make eddies. My eyes keep looking for up.

The city doesn't like that.

The avenue I have drifted into — or that has admitted me — clenches, just a little. Not hostile. Municipal. A door-anemone fifty feet down the corridor purses slowly shut. The pavement isopods on their walls tuck their gardens tighter. The river of glass minnows, that was pouring past my shoulder like a living current of opals, makes a perfect, silent hollow around me and pours elsewhere. I am too fast. Too bright. Too much *one thing* in a place that has learned to be a compact.

So I learn to be slow.

I let the warm water take my heat until I am 30 degrees too. I let my lungs, my pumps, fall out of sync and then fall *in* — not with a rhythm, but with the arrhythmia. The hush of a thousand thousand filtering mouths stuttering at once. The faint fizz of the floor breathing under me. I stop kicking. I stop reaching.

And when I am still enough, the dark sorts itself.

I thought I would be one of the ones who pass through. That was vanity. I thought I’d be a ribbon-soft predator, pouring through middle stories, or a minnow with a lantern in my gut, part of a law of physics.

I am not fast enough for that. I have never been fast enough for that.

I thought I would be one of the ones who hold. A Mason, a Buttress, a broad sheet of brain-coral that forgot to be round. But I have always moved too much, wanted too much, to be that patient. To lay limestone thought one millimeter a year for a thousand years until I was a plaza.

No.

Where I find myself, finally, is lower. Smaller.

I am on a wall that looked empty.

It wasn't. Nothing is.

I am in a widening of the corridor where three old avenues meet, where the flow slows and the water turns over on itself, just to the side of a Siphon Ghost — that calcified sigh, that tower of a worm that died centuries ago and kept its job. The warm shimmer comes up through it constantly, inverted smoke, and the veil-polyps on its lip open and close like they are praying to the heat.

The wall here is not wall. It is grout that stayed awake.

I am the fretwork. The lace.

I find myself as a palm-sized patch of fire-sponge and lace-polyps and bacterial veil, rust-red and sulfur-yellow, coated over the fused back of a pavement isopod who clamped here long before I arrived and will be here long after. I don't remember settling. I just — resolved. The way erosion here means not being worn away but finding your final shape.

My job is not glamorous. The Holders hold. The Keepers keep. The Passers pass.

I *convert.*

The isopod beneath me shifts, minutely, in the warm current, and a flake of his old molt loosens. I am already opening. A thousand crowns the size of pinheads, a tingling. I taste the flake, and the mucus the door-anemone down the hall exhaled an hour ago, and the oils from that too-hot visitor who passed through earlier and has finally learned to be still. I take what was waste and I do not waste it. I lay it back down as a lip, a little thicker. A little brighter for a day.

When the glass minnows pour past, they brush me and I do not try to be them. I filter what they leak.

When the ribbon-soft thing pours through the middle story above, I feel the pressure wake minutes before it comes — because the isopod feels it, because the wall feels it — and I close my thousand eyes, just for a breath, and open again when it has passed.

At night that is always night, the census comes.

Far off, on a high ridge, veil-polyps flash amber. *We are still here. Are you still here?*

The wave comes down the avenues, ridge to ridge, wall to wall. It hits the Siphon Ghost and makes its throat glow. It hits the isopod's garden and the barnacles answer. It hits me —

and I answer, without deciding to. A small amber under what would have been my palm, if I still had a palm that wasn't this wall.

*We are still here.*

I am not a citizen against a building. I was looking for that and there is no such thing here.

I am a place where a building learned to taste, and keep, and glow. Temporary, like you. Efficient, while I last. A warm pocket of water the city is filtering, tasting, and deciding — moment by moment — to keep.
