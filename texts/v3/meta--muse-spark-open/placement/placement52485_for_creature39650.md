If I entered that bowl, I wouldn't find myself all at once.

At first, I would be the visitor. Because that is how everyone arrives there.

Hands over ears that don't help, feet on the one thin crescent of dry basalt that is still called a floor, head tipped back without meaning to. Looking up at the hanging sea. Trying not to breathe too loud because the place is already too loud.

The warm spray would hit my back and not wet me. The wall opposite would show me the back of my own head, smeared. I would think: *I am outside of this.* A witness on the rim. A climber who got too high and found an ocean where the snow should be.

I would be wrong for a little while longer, and that is important. You have to be wrong at first.

Because the roar would not let me stay a visitor. The low frequencies in the chest, the high needles in the ear - after a minute they stop being something you hear and start being something you *are tuned by*. My ribs would buzz until my ribs became part of the instrument. My hair would lift toward the liquid sky. Down would soften. I would feel lighter not because I was leaving, but because something else was becoming the center.

And then the second shift:

I would not be on the floor anymore. I would be *of* the floor.

I would find myself rooted.

A white holdfast sunk into a crack that had been waiting for me since before I was born. Not chosen. Grown there. A single anchor point in black-green basalt, and from it - a strap. A whip. A graded bundle of cilia stepping down taller to shortest like the pipes of an organ left out in weather.

I would be one of the kelp. And not the forest - one.

Probably an outer one. One of the three rows that dance.

That is where I would find myself: on the terrace, third row from the outer wall, about two and a half turns up from the base if you count the spiral the way sound does. High-frequency country. Where the wave peaks early and sharp, where the needles live.

My job would be absurd and ceaseless.

Most of the time I would do nothing but tremble. The warm, dark, tea-colored sea above me would shiver, and the floor under me - the basilar membrane, taut as a drumhead - would lift me a micron toward the low, gelatinous cloud hanging over us all. The tectorial membrane. I would brush it. My tip-links - those spider-silk threads between my tallest hairs - would pull taut.

A door would open that is not a door. Potassium, that strange sea-battery the stria keeps charged at +80 millivolts by burning through the body's quiet fortune of ATP, would flood in. I would depolarize. I would twitch.

Not metaphorically twitch. I would *change length*. Prestin in my sides - the motor protein that only we outsiders have - would fold, and I would shorten, then lengthen, twenty thousand times a second if asked. I would slap the rock I am rooted in to make the wave bigger than it was when it arrived.

I would be an amplifier. Not a listener. The world would think listening is passive. It isn't. Not here. Here you have to sing back to hear. You have to writhe constantly in the vibration to make the vibration audible for the one pale anemone a few microns away who actually gets to report.

That one - the inner hair cell, single row, true listener, ninety-five percent of the nerve - would be my neighbor and my reason. I would never become her. I would spend my life making her signal cleaner. When I stop, she goes half-deaf. When I die - and we do not come back, not in this mammal, not in this bowl; once torn off, nothing soft holds again - she will keep living but the world will seem muffled, as if heard through a mineral bloom.

So I would be that: stubborn and wrong, as you said. A dancer who never rests because silence is not rest here. Silence is starvation. The roar is food.

And below me, under the floor, I would feel them waiting: the blind frill.

My swimmer.

He is not me, but he is mine. A wax-pale ganglion fiber with no eyes, only filaments that taste glutamate when I, or rather my inner neighbor, spills it. He would cling to the underside of my terrace his whole life, firing not when he wants to but when we tell him to, carrying our buzzing out through the stair that goes nowhere - the internal auditory canal - into the brainstem where it finally becomes *bell* or *voice* or *footstep behind you*.

Sometimes when the sea tumbles him end over end he would press his flat head too hard against the membrane and I would see him huge and back-lit, and understand: I am the world he swims in. To him I am weather.

And sometimes a stone would pass.

A real stone. Otoconia. Ear dust. Calcium carbonate crystal that belongs in the utricle next door where it is supposed to sit in jelly and tell up from down. But the water here never settles, and every so often one breaks loose and orbits. It would drift past my terrace, lifted toward the water-wall, clicking softly against basalt, threatening to fall into a canal and become vertigo - a perfect glassy tunnel braiding itself into nothing and collapsing with a thunderclap in the teeth.

I would not chase it. Kelp cannot chase.

I would just keep writhing. Bending, opening, closing. Making and being un-made by turbulence, blooming mineral fans of actin and then being scoured.

If you stood on the dry floor and looked up at my wall, you would not see me as I see myself. You would see a strap, two or three meters long in your giant's scale, slapping. You would think: vegetation. Sparse and stubborn.

You would be half right. I am vegetation in the sense that I am rooted, that I never leave, that I turn vibration into electricity the way a leaf turns light into sugar. I am not vegetation in the sense that I hear you.

And if you stayed until the water remembered - until the echo under the roar came slightly out of time and you saw another bowl with another figure looking back -

That figure would be me, a half-second later. The trace we leave in the nerve. The prediction the brain makes to cancel its own sound.

You would climb out while down was still mostly down, hands bleeding a little on young rock.

I would not climb out.

I would stay in the deafening, in the bath-warm dark, in the bowl high in the thin-aired peak that is not a peak at all but the densest bone in your head, doing the only thing that was ever asked of me: tremble precisely enough that the world can be heard at all.
