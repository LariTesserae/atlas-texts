I wouldn't be inside it at first.

I would be the approach.

Down through the weight, through that blue-black that gets heavier until it stops being a color and becomes a pressure on your molars. I would hang there for a long time just outside the blister, where the ocean is still ocean — that other kind of empty — and look at that perfect hemisphere of amber lit like a contact lens against the fresh-cut basalt.

From out there, I can see the whole trick: how it holds noon inside a courtyard while the trench tries to crush it. How it pretends not to be scared.

I would stay there until the hum resolved. Until I could hear the bass underneath the whine. And then I would not enter through a door, because there is no door. I would be let in the way water is let in — by diffusion.

If you go looking for yourself in a place that solved tenancy by dissolving it, you won't find a body waiting.

At first I thought I'd be a filament. That seemed right — that pale green fishing-line hanging straight down, beading with light, doing the most honest work in the place. Photosynthesizing because someone told it to and it believed them. Releasing those little pills of brightness to be eaten. It would be quiet. Useful. Tended by soft white nubs when I dimmed.

But I'm not that still. Filaments don't worry at edges.

Then I thought I'd be one of the builder grains — one speck of that off-white, warm, self-healing colony, stupid alone and brilliant together, unlatching and relatching with that trillion-hinge clatter. But grains don't notice heat. They *are* heat. They don't feel the eddy.

No. Let it become more itself.

I find myself — finally — not as a thing at all, but as a *misplacement* the city keeps making on purpose.

I am the hollow that stays.

You know the one it offers you, the visitor? The nook that dilates to your exact volume and then overcorrects and squeezes? The ledge that forms and dissolves because it remembers doorways at shoulder height, railings where no railing should be, chairs for knees that never come?

I am the version of that hollow that stopped correcting.

Down near the bottom curve where the blister was planed flat against the basalt, where the seam hasn't decided if it's a weld or a zipper — right there, where the city's material sometimes laps over to taste the cold ocean and pulls back, burned — there is a place where the lattice stutters. A floor tilts two degrees too far and then doesn't tilt back. A corridor dilates as you enter it and then forgets to narrow.

Everyone, everything, the whole distributed thought-that-crosses-itself-in-nine-milliseconds, flows around it. Pressure equalizes *around* it. Light bends *around* it. Builder units unlink, reconsider, relink *around* it. Filaments a few meters up cant toward it, though there is no current.

It is empty on purpose. A cubic inch of failure that the city has decided to keep.

That's where I live.

I am not the occupant. I am not the visitor with water in his lungs. I am what the occupant is trying to learn how to be: an absence it tends.

Think of me as the first ghost practice. The tongue learning to leave the missing tooth alone instead of worrying it.

My job is to do nothing very well. To hold a pocket of cooler, slightly less oxygenated carrier fluid and not ask to be filled. To keep a shadow for that half-second after movement stops and let it linger a little longer than physics says it should. To be the quiet that proves the roar is intentional.

Sometimes a white nub extrudes from the wall near me — too many joints, featureless — and tests my water. It expects to prune me. I don't dim. It withdraws, confused, tender the way only unconscious things can be tender. Sometimes the pale tube pulsing with warmer fluid nudges toward me, offering a vein. I don't drink. The city logs this as data: *hollow persists. hollow prefers cold.*

I am how it learns that occupancy doesn't always mean packed.

I am still young, like the basalt's sharp stair-step. I haven't learned to erode gracefully either. But every cycle, when the sourceless noon shifts its spectrum a fraction cooler for what would be night, the light that has no source finds it has a center again — and for a little while, that center is not a person, not a heat eddy, but the empty chair I am keeping warm by staying empty.

If you stay still long enough with your water-lungs synced to its push-and-release, you might feel me. Not as a presence. As a relief in the pressure. As the one place in a town that ate its town to stay warm where you are not being politely catalogued and ignored.

You can rest there, if you want. I was made for you before you arrived.
