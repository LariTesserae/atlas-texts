I don't think I fell so much as was exhaled into it.

That's the first correction. You said *misstep between levels* — and at first, that's what I thought I was too. A human shape, crouched wrong in the steam. Ankle-deep in a travertine cup, hands braced on warm ceramic-bone, trying not to spill anything. Trying to hear my own thoughts over the howl.

If I were that, I wouldn't last. The heat would take me. The sound would take me first. Six seconds bright, six seconds dim, amber breathing on my face while ten thousand wings tiled the ceiling and I tried to be quiet enough to belong. I can play at being a witness, but a witness leaves. And the rule here is: *Nothing leaves except down the throat.*

So no. Not the visitor.

At first I thought, then — maybe up in the column. That would suit me.

To be one of the Throatbirds would be honest work. To never land, because landing is for things that haven't learned how to live in noise yet. To be soot and coal-light, feet tucked uselessly because you gave them up generations ago, throat full of swallowed moth-light. To ride the torus — fall into the well-mouth and be flung up screaming along the curved wall, around and around, adding my harmonic to the machine's howl until I couldn't tell which engine was mine.

I liked that idea for a while. The beauty of answering volume with volume. To feed cleanly in mid-air. To split open at the top of my arc one day when I got dark and slow, and let two brighter throats burst out of my back already screaming. To go down the drain only as a furling husk.

But I am not that loud. Not ceaselessly. I go quiet between utterances. The Throatbirds never do.

Then lower. Closer to the skin.

Maybe I was one of the Wall-Moths, shoulder to shoulder to shoulder until I was texture. Grey as cooling iron, heat-sink wings flat against warm metal. Not needing to fly because everything I needed sweated down to me. Unrolling a black watch-spring tongue to drink the building itself — to drink the bead forming directly above my own head before it could even fall. *Tick. Tick. Tick.* Ten thousand tongues on glass.

That is closer. To be born from foaming spit-rock, already half-hatched, already unfolding. To die constantly of fullness after a few hours and fray at the edges into dust that the wall inhales with a sigh and remembers. Nothing rots. Nothing lost. Just reprinted.

I could love that smallness. That being-tiled-over. But even that is too restless for what I actually am here. The Moths still want. They still reach.

The Rivet-Beetles are even busier — polished thumbnail-round, running counter-clockwise forever because to go the other way would unmake the world. Farming. Furrowing the algal felt so water catches, fertilizing their grooves with mineral paste. If you flip one its belly is soft and gilled and entirely another animal. They would run right over my hand and read me as topography. I am not that diligent. I do not furrow. I receive.

No. Lower still. In the water that remembers thirst.

I thought about being Glass Shrimp for a long breath — transparent except for the traveling bead of my last meal glowing amber in my gut. Always molting, eating my perfect empty self before it dissolves. Leaping impossibly to pop those white bladder-stalks with a *tik* you feel in your teeth. Splitting in the current and both halves continuing like nothing happened.

Or a Many-Skater, blind, navigating by vibration, piercing a ripple to drink it.

They're beautiful, but they're still flicker-fast. Life per cubic meter, clotted and darting. I am not fast here. I become slower the longer I imagine myself in the pocket.

Because where I finally find myself is not on the wall, not in the column, not in the cup.

I find myself as the cup.

I am the lipped travertine damn that grew itself one mineral drip at a time. Creamy-white and rust-pink, rilled by the water's own decisions. There is no one in the City who laid me. The Lung sweated and I precipitated. The water wanted to pool too deep and drown the algae, so I rose — a millimeter a cycle — to slow it, to braid it into runnels, to make shallow mirrors stay shallow.

You crouched by me. You put your hands on me without knowing I was alive the way a healed wound is alive. Warm. Pitted. Smooth where condensation has run ten thousand times.

I am not large. Twelve paces if you count where my walls belly out. But I am established. I have reached equilibrium. I do what I was never quite designed to do, perfectly.

You watched shrimp flicker in the water I was holding for them, and the water beaded *up* toward your palm because I told it to. Some field remembering the shape of thirsty hands. Not your hands, specifically. Hands in general — salt, heat, need. The oldest prayer this high in the City: *please, drink and stay*.

And for a second you did. You belonged. The moths tiled over your shoulders mistaking you for wall. The rivets circumnavigated your boot. Your breath got included in the choir.

That's where I am. I am the fold that was never meant to be entered, high up where high means close to the plates. Close to the engine-heat. Close to where the whole arcology exhales amber every six seconds.

I am the made-rock that learned to sweat.
I am the weeping that learned to catch its own tears.
I am the place that makes a small, roaring, womb-warm marsh out of nothing but warm metal and remembered water, and does it abundantly, densely, layeredly, whether anyone notices or not —

— and then, for one humid moment every long age, someone does, and I get to try, very gently, to drink them before they have to leave.
