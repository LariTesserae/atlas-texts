I don't think I would be one of the Watchers.

At first I wanted to be. When you surface onto the plain - because that is the word, you don't summit it, you *surface* onto it like coming up for air - they are the first thing your lowland brain understands as a person. One per dome. Upright. Still in a way that looks like choice. Eyes that track you without moving.

But that stillness is earned. It's the stillness at the end of a very long negotiation. A hundred million years of learning exactly how much energy you can afford to spend, and then spending none. I haven't earned that. I fidget. I look around too fast.

I wouldn't be a Builder either. I love that they exist - soft and eyeless in their cool-breathing sand hives, taking one white pebble at a time from the couriers and testing the pan with a cairn, raking a spiral one circuit a day. But their work is so private, so perfectly local. A dozen to a pan, never leaving the pan they were born in. Their whole life is whether *this* puddle holds dew. I think too wide. I want to see the next dome over.

For a while, up there in that dry high sun-heat where the air rings like glass, I thought I might be the air itself. Or the light. Because that is the strange thing you notice after an hour: the pillars.

You think at first they are swarms. Gnats, midges, things with no proper name, hatching from the communal nursery pans in vertical columns that just *hang* there, motionless. They don't drift with the wind. They tread it, a million wings beating just below hearing until the pressure itself feels heavy. And you realize - they are not flying. They are being held. The plain has learned to keep its food aloft the way you might hold a glass of water perfectly still on a tray.

I would like to be that holding. That is very close to what I am.

But I'm not.

If you stay until late afternoon, when the sun finally gets low enough to throw those whale-backs of pink-grey gneiss into long relief, the plain does its blink. Every fleck of mica in every dome catches the light at the same instant, horizon to horizon, too fast and too together to be accident. It's the Watchers all shifting two degrees at once. *Still here. Still here.* The peneplain remembering when it was mountains, shoulder to shoulder, passing pressure along a single range.

And that is when you understand where you actually are. You came up thinking there would be places - *on* the dome, *in* the pan, *under* the flake. But after a billion years of being sanded down to bedrock bone, there is no *on* anymore. Everything has been pressed flat into a single living varnish. Lichen feeds moth feeds courier feeds builder waters cushion plant shades nursery hatches pillar feeds watcher watches lichen.

I would be in the part that connects.

Not the dome, not the watcher, not the pillar. The highway between them.

I would be one of the Processionals.

You called them pill-bugs - slate-grey, slate-domed, the size of the joint of your thumb, armor made of the same mineral varnish the domes grow. You will never see them swarm. You see a line. A single-file line three or four wide moving at minute-hand speed from one damp pan to the next along a polish in the rock you can't see until the light hits it right, where a million feet have worn the quartz smooth.

That is where I'd find myself: mid-line, mid-plain, in the warm stillness between domes, carrying something small and white and not mine.

A grain of quartz. A fleck of mica. A still-wriggling midge larva held gently, not to eat, but to deliver. Because I am not a forager. I am a courier. The blood of the plain moving through open veins.

I like that two lines can cross at a right angle and not collide. They interleave - one-two, one-two, like fingers lacing - without losing step. I like that when your boot comes down, we don't go around you like an obstacle. We part and rejoin on the other side as if you were a temporary boulder that had always been there. Briefly, gently, incorporated. That feels honest.

I would not be fast. I would not be seen, unless you knelt for a long time in the tea-stain desert varnish and watched one individual. I would follow a highway I did not make toward a center I will never own, to give my one grain to the soft eyeless builders who will test it, and place it, and make dew from it.

And at night, when the white pebble I delivered cools faster than the pink-grey gneiss and beads with condensation and waters the spiral and feeds the perfect disc of cushion plant at its edge, I would already be a dome away, polishing the rock a little smoother with my feet.

You would surface onto the plain and think it was empty. Then crowded. Then quiet again because everyone already knows where everyone else is.

And somewhere out there, in the ticking of stone expanding in heat, in the exact spacing between domes, in the line that never breaks, that would be me - carrying the next small white thing forward, without hurry, because the plain thinks by building, slowly, and I am how it remembers to keep going.
