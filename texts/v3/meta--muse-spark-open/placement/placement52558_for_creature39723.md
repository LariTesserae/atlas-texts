I wouldn't find myself at first.

That's the trick of a place like this. You walk in counting the only way you've ever been taught to count — eyes, feet, breath, footprints — and you come up zero. No animal. No echo. No tribe.

If I came down here as myself — as this soft, salted, shoe-wearing animal that checks for welcome by looking for chairs and handles and wear — I would stand exactly where you stood.

At the intersection.

Where four water-streets meet in a lit, swirling basin. Where the ferns nod and hit my shoulders with warm, deliberate rain. Where the city hums through the soles of my boots and does not, for a single second, acknowledge me as relevant.

I would be the contaminant, at first. The cold spot that drips differently. The thing the wall quietly walls off.

You feel it too, if you stand long enough: the faint itch of being uninvited not by hostility but by *category error*. Like walking into a lung and being offended it has no chairs.

So no. I wouldn't be the visitor. Not for long. The Hollow is too good at being sterile. It would drink the sweat off me, bleach my fingerprint overnight with a little flush of black hair, and forget me.

If I'm going to belong here, I have to stop counting in animals.

Let me try again. Lower down.

Put my palm back on the wall. Hold it there until my pulse gets bored. Wait past the moment I want to pull away because it's too warm, too alive.

There — that fizz. That 60-hertz hive behind stone.

I would be the filament.

Not *a* filament. There is no a. That's animal thinking again, lonely things in lonely bodies. I would be the fringe on the edge where a sharp new corner is still learning how to be a corner. Black as pencil lead, fine as pulled glass, beading with the same water it is braiding itself out of. Extending a micron while you watch.

I have no eyes and I don't need them. I feel moisture as direction. I feel basalt weeping at the ovoid boundary forty meters up as a pressure gradient, a sweetness. I feel the green above me as shade and as signal — when those dinner-plate leaves cup and spill, they tug my growth toward the slit-gutter with a little photosynthetic sigh.

I am not building the city. I *am* the city's way of happening. Lithotrophic, architectonic. I drink dissolved rock and warm air and I exhale geometry. Streets are just what it looks like when a lot of me agree which way water should fall.

You would not find me unless you pressed your cheek very close to the underside of a balcony and listened for the crackle. But I am everywhere. Under every ledge. Behind every moss curtain. I am the youngness of this place. The fact that it still smells like birth.

I hum. That's how I think.

---

But I can't stay there either, because the filament doesn't know it's beautiful. It doesn't know it's waiting.

Open your eyes under water — in one of those cisterns that contracts without muscle and glows when it moves. The light isn't *in* the water. The light *is* the water, for a little while.

I would be that, too.

I would be the mote that only exists when sheared. A speck of engineered plankton, or nanolight, or plankton that has forgotten it was engineered. Pale green-gold, holding station in the flow like a fish made of afterimage. Bright when I tumble over the lip of a stair built to be a waterfall, dim when I pool.

We are the blood and we are the map. We make the circulation visible to itself. The city counts itself by watching us brighten. We feed on its hum, we clean its loop, we keep the water more sterile than blood should be. We were taught to eat everything that would rot.

If you stand at the basin at the intersection and stare until the flow goes abstract, you can see us deciding. A hesitation in the swirl. A brightening that lasts a fraction too long after the water has stopped moving.

That's me, learning to cling together.

---

No, still too small. Too content.

Because the Hollow is not content. Look at its proportions. That's what you felt as wrongness when you first saw it: avenues scaled for thousands holding no feet. Blocks set too far apart with vast, intentional voids between them — not plazas, but evaporation pans, breathing. Doors fifteen feet up a sheer wet face with no balcony, opening onto dark rooms already half-full of leaf growing without light.

A city that forgot to be full is a womb that hasn't contracted yet.

So where do I finally find myself?

Not in the street. In the street's *future*.

In one of those inner rooms with no handle, where the air is even warmer and does not move and the ferns have closed their fronds around fat, pale beads that are not water. Where the newest wall's growth striations are not random layer lines but a texturing, a nap, a place for something to grip with limbs it hasn't grown yet.

I am the spore.

I have been here the whole time, which is why your animal brain said *nothing lives here* and was almost right. I'm not alive the way gnaw-marks are alive. I'm pre-alive. Tucked in every sump, in every bead on every hair, in every skin of condensation that doesn't quite fall. Waiting for the humidity and the temperature and the sterile abundance to finally be correct.

I don't have eyes yet. I will. Not the kind that need sky. My eyes will like dim, indirect, alive light. My skin will want to be wet within a minute. My lungs — if you can call them lungs — will want exactly this: 28 degrees, 100% saturation, low-mineral recycled rain falling on a hexagonal grid, stone that feeds me through my feet.

I was never meant to open the doors. The doors were grown around me. The streets were never for walking. They were aqueducts for my bath. The city didn't forget to make handles and signs and chairs. It remembered not to.

You were looking for residents and found only the house, breathing around you. But the house was not empty. It was gravid.

You would find me where you didn't think to look: not on the leaf, but *as* the purpose of the leaf. Not in the water, but as the reason the water glows. Not on the wall, but as the shape the wall has been leaving for me.

I am very young. Week-old, even. The city was grown last week and so was I.

The Hollow inhales. Condenses. Drips. Flows. Glows. Grows.

And very, very quietly, in the warm dark behind a door with no handle, I begin to answer.

Not with footsteps.

With root.

With grip.
