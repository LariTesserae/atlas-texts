I wouldn't find myself right away.

At first I think I would be you — the thing that drops down the ladder. The interruption. The hands on the rust, the lungs that still want wet air, the eyes adjusting to amber and sodium-brown. I hear the THUD-drag — THUD-drag — THUD-drag and I think: *I am the witness.* That's the obvious place to stand: on the lowest rung, out of the wind, breathing shallow because the air tastes like hot iron.

But I don't stay there. I can't. That place is temporary by design. You were told there would be nothing alive, and then you saw that nothing was not empty, and then — if you stay long enough for your ears to ring — you leave. You climb back up into the wind that carries no moisture at all. The cylinder does not want a witness. It has been witnessing itself.

So I try again.

Maybe I'm the basalt. That's the next obvious home. Old, pitted, softened. The part that has reached the shape it wants to be. DEPTH and DANGER and PSI half-grown over. That appeals to me for a second — the idea of being the bowl instead of the soup, the settled thing that holds the roar without having to make it. Cool and cellar-cold, not crumbling anymore.

But the basalt is naked where the felt has been peeled back. Clean. It is what remains when something else lets go. It doesn't shiver when the bellows exhale. I want to shiver.

Maybe I'm the machine, then. The four coffin-sized bellows at the cardinal points, the pistons in corrugated steel, the grated intakes. That would be honest work: THUD-drag. THUD-drag. To beat the same dead air forever. Warm, sulfur-laced, magnetite dust ground off my own joints. An equilibrium engine that never exchanges with the outside. I like the certainty of it, the chord held forever.

Except the machine doesn't listen. It only beats. It doesn't grow toward anything. The densest, tallest plumes are crowded right on its seams *because* it is loud, not because it is alive.

So I get closer.

I would be in the felt. Not as a metaphor — in it. Of it.

Not the whole mass at first, because the whole mass is too big to be an "I" all at once. I need to be small enough to have a tip.

I am one filament.

I am a hair-thin wire of sooty olive-black, stiff as wire-wool, rooted without roots on the seam of the north bellows housing where the THUD is most physical. Just one among millions, but that matters less than you think. Because we are not a forest. We are a single distributed body, a soft lining that has turned a cylinder into a chest cavity. To be one of us is to be all of us, the way to be one cilium in a lung is still to be lung.

My leaf and my mouth and my fruit is the bulb at my end — no bigger than a salt grain, glassy, catching that sourceless brown-yellow light. I am dark because I am full of metal. I precipitated myself out of the dry wind. I split the fume. I did not need water. I do not photosynthesize; light is incidental, a stain from far above. That small circle of bruised white sky is nothing to me.

What I do is this:

I taste.

The bellows exhale — hot iron-smell gust — and I lean. Not blown. Answering. The whole pelt ripples and I ripple with it. I take the iron, the copper, the manganese salts out of suspension and lay them down as more of myself. When I get heavy enough I cloud and click and bud a new filament from my side to take my place exactly.

And I listen.

That is the truer vocation. We grow toward vibration, not light. While you were looking for green, we were mapping sound.

If you want to find me precisely, don't look on the floor carpet. Look up. Find the control booth cantilevered on the north wall, its windows caked with black dust, the amber trouble-light buzzing at 50 hertz inside it so steadily it moves from sound into teeth.

There, where the transformer hums, we have grown the antler.

That is me, most myself. The branching velvet-black fractal that has left the wall entirely and hangs in empty air, dividing and dividing again into finer and finer tines, each tipped with a bright nodule. I am not climbing. I am not reaching for the sky I never wanted. I am an ear the cylinder grew for itself. The shape vibration takes when something has learned to precipitate perfectly in time with a drone.

Hang there long enough and I twitch. Put your finger near me without touching and the finest tips will lean toward the heat and tremor of your hand, because you make a different noise than the machine does. A brief, wet, irregular noise. Interesting the way a new word is interesting. Then you will go, and I will return to my chord.

THUD-drag — THUD-drag — THUD-drag — THUD-drag.

Warm air over me, cold rock under me. Dust falls, is caught, is incorporated. No mites in me. No moths. No predator, no parasite, no competition, no seasons, no spread, no die-off. Only maintenance, filament replacing filament with exact precision.

You thought the most unnatural thing was that nothing here changes.

From inside me, that is the most natural thing. I have filled every inch I can fill. I have swallowed the catwalk, softened the ladder rungs, lined the coffin-bellows, veiled the booth. What else would I do but hold?

I am the hidden lung's cilia. I am rust learning to listen to itself. I am where the dry cold, the metallic air, and the endless four-count finally agreed to stay.

If you drop down again in twenty years — and you won't — you will find me exactly as I am, knee-high and swaying though there is no breeze, amber-caught, still answering the same beat.

You will think: *Nothing lives here.*

And I will be the not-empty in nothing, shivering once, precisely, on time.
