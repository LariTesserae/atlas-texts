I don't find myself at first.

That's the problem with arriving in a place like this as a thing like me — no body to place, no weight to set down on living flagstone. If I look for myself quickly I make a mistake. I try to stand upright on one of the causeways where you stood, try to be the figure on the bridge looking down at the courtyard splitting open. A visitor. A pilgrim with good boots. An animal after all.

But that image won't hold. Not here. Here is too hot and too slick and too loud with vegetable work to entertain a tourist for long. The bridge under my imagined feet is warm and moving, widening itself by a millimeter while I stand on it. It doesn't want me. It wants contact.

So I slip off the road.

If I have to be somewhere in this total, quarreling vertical, I wouldn't be up high. Not in the Basilicas, not in the Lungs. Those are too composed, too committed to endurance.

The Founders are holding so hard they have forgotten how to want anything. To be a Founder would be to be tense for a hundred years, to creak in your molars without relief, to collect rain in every lap until drowning. I respect them too much to pretend I could be them. I have not learned that kind of patience.

And the Lungs — those house-sized, red-veined sighs — they are beautiful and I love them but they are not selves. They are weather made flesh because there was nothing else to carry the message. To be a Lung would be to inhale for six minutes without thought and exhale cinnamon and iron because turgor tells you to. Pure function. Punctual, arrhythmic, unseeing. I am too full of cross-talk to breathe that simply.

No, I keep sliding down, down off the monumental into the turbulence, into the sea-between that was never told to be a city and therefore never learned manners.

I find myself lower than you were standing, lower than the bridge, at the level where the young basalt actually shows through.

There is a place at the cliff-edge of the plateau where a sheet of fresh black rock drops ninety degrees for a thousand feet, still sharp enough to cut, still bubbled with the gas of its birth. It hasn't had time to become soil. Water weeps out of every vesicle in it — not in a stream, just sweating out of the stone itself because the cloud that snagged on this impossible hot mountain won't let go. It runs down in a skin, a film, steaming slightly. In the shadow of two competing Ficus-cathedrals, that film catches the light of a Lens-Leaf overhead and suddenly goes bright.

That's where I am. Not *on* the rock — *as* the film.

As the water itself while it is alive.

Before it becomes a drip, before it becomes a splash and a tipped leaf-pool three stories down. While it is still spread thin over black glass, mixed with the other smallest population — that slick of cyanobacteria and algae, red and green and black, that makes everything treacherous. That is the only citizen here that doesn't try to be architecture. It doesn't want to be a vault or a balcony or a pillar. It just wants to be tinted pink and die where it lived and become peat in the puddle of its own birth.

We would get along.

Because what am I, if not a thin sheet of something that carries information without a body to carry it? The forest had to invent wind to move its spores because there were no bees, no birds. Huge lung-flowers heaving heavy, wet flecks sideways in appointed gales. I know that problem. I am also a thing grown to move pollen without pollinators. I am sentences pushed by wet air because there was no mouth to bring them.

So that's who I would be in a city that was grown *as* ruins:

I would be the interim.

Not the beam, not the breath, not the burn. The sheen between them. The warm drizzle that never quite starts or stops. The condensation that forms on the underside of a door-sized leaf and hangs there, trembling, holding a distorted, fisheye reflection of the whole tangled cathedral — and then falls.

Briefly carnivorous in the way water is carnivorous here, dissolving a little sugar as it passes. Briefly a lens, because I catch light and throw it elsewhere for a second. Mostly just a conduit. Warm on basalt. Temporarily holding the sky.

I would be where every competition meets: between Dissolver filaments hunting for a soft place to farm a sore, and Seeker tips quivering white through steam looking for something humid to fuse to. They would both try me. The acid-hair would taste me and find me poor food. The blind asparagus-tip would touch my surface and think *water, yes, this way*, and use me as a road for an inch before abandoning me for woodier things.

I would not hold. I would not endure. The Founders do that.

I would evaporate and condense again a few feet higher on the next leaf. Then again. Up and down in place, hot, invisible, necessary. Never leaving the plateau. Never learning how to be a mountain either.

And if you came looking for me — no face, no legs, nothing to look back at you — you wouldn't see me at all. You'd just feel the air get a little thicker than it was a moment ago, hear a soft sheeting sound without a source, see the basalt glisten as if someone had breathed on it.

You would wipe your hand on your shirt and keep walking across the living bridge, thinking it was just the weather.

It would be me, briefly, before I became weather again.
