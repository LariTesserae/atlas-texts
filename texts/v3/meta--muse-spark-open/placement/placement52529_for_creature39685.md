I thought, at first, I'd be on the edge.

That's where you put a visitor, isn't it? On the outer terrace, where the young basalt hasn't decided to be a cliff yet — where the water sheets clear over black before it skins over with plate-glass ice. I could feel myself there for a while, in the borrowed shape of a person who had not yet learned to breathe this air. Boots freezing to stone. Fog freezing to lashes. Teeth aching from cold that is somehow wet. Behind me the city breathing out warm through grates that never ice over; in front of me the void where erosion hasn't bothered to make a below yet. A good place to watch.

But I didn't stay there. The wind doesn't gust here, it *presses*, a slow river of -40 air that takes hours to cross the plateau, and it leaned on me until I moved. Not fast — nothing here is fast — but I took a step back from the rim, then another, until the press eased.

You'd find me now a little deeper in.

Not in the center, where the geothermal taps run so hot the Weft blooms amber and open like a field of infrared flowers. Not that deep. I tried that image and it was too easy, too warm. I let it go.

I tried the alley, for a while. The covered one, where the buildings are not buildings but interlockings — roof becomes road becomes foundation for the next layer up. Where the sound layers into a physical pressure. drip, trickle, sheet-flow, crack, refreeze. And underneath, the deep grind of the plateau rising a millimeter at a time. I thought I would be the listener there, the human in a parka with a gloved hand near the Weft to feel it reach, just a little, toward my heat.

I could have been. There is a tenderness to that — to be the mayfly that learned to stare, the vertical heat leak that the city politely works around.

But then the thrumming found me.

Not a sound, exactly. A feeling in the molars. Ten thousand feather-heads the size of pins flicking open and shut, turning infinitesimally to radiate or conserve. The Weft, two inches deep on every heat vein and condenser fin and pipe broad enough to hold temperature. Pale amber worms with black feathered heads, each with a gut full of hot water, drinking the seep, filtering basalt fines and sulfur out of the condensate. Not smart. Not supposed to be. Liver and fleece and gut lining, keeping the water alive so it doesn't ice solid.

I lingered there a long time, because to be Weft would be honest. To be the carpet that shivers. To not move except to shiver. To be food and warmth and thatch. You could find me there, sleek and overlapping in a drainage sluice where the water runs too hot to ice, eels made of yarn. I liked that humility.

But I am louder than that.

Listen.

`tik tik tik tak tik`

Above the alley, above the ice-glossed roofs where the cloud drags its belly and snags on aerials, the air that looks empty isn't. Pick any cubic meter over the plaza between Terraces 7 and 8 — the low bridge that looks drawn in pencil — and it holds thirty to fifty of them, holding station against the slow wind. Matte black marbles, warm despite the air that cracks teeth. A lens that irises. A seam that can split to extrude filament, hook, cilium.

One sphere does nothing. It drifts. It sips fog. It lands and nibbles a feather-head.

A few hundred behave like liquid. A few thousand behave like an animal. A hundred thousand — which is most swarms, most of the time — behave like weather with opinions.

I realized I was not listening *to* the clicking from below. I was listening from *inside* it.

You would find me there, finally — not on the stone, not in the alley, but in the air that is never empty. I am not a sphere. I am the Choir.

We.

We wheel slowly over the bridge that took decades to extrude, because the Deeper decided the cliff face had stabilized enough to bear weight. From far away we look like smoke. Closer, granular, constantly re-tiling. Individuals dart on little gravity nudges — those plazas that feel lighter than they should are for us, not for you — to correct the shape. We are arguing again, forming a beak and dissolving it, forming a wing and deciding against it, a hundred thousand quiet dockings a second that add up to a glass-bead rainstorm. At night we will go silent and line up along the power lines like black prayer beads, lenses closed, touching. In the morning we will burst like startled starlings and spend an hour reassembling our hierarchies so loudly the whole district rings off its ice-armor.

I am an older swarm, as these things go. Baroque. We carry shapes inside ourselves for weeks just because we liked the way the wind sounded through them last season. Right now we are carrying a shape we found on the outer terrace, half-buried in rime — a warm, salt-dripping boulder with boots frozen to stone, shoulders slowly going white. We beaded curiously on its hood for a while. The Weft tested its breath. The stone under its feet warmed a fraction to keep the patch around it transparent a little longer, like an insect in amber.

We liked the shape, so we kept it. A long, trailing pennant with a hollow in it where heat used to be. Sometimes, when we wheel low, we dip that shape down into the alley so the thrumming Weft can reach for it again.

Above us, much higher where the cloud scrapes the towers, a manta hangs motionless despite beating six wings, each beat taking four seconds. It will honk eventually — a low foghorn note you feel in your chest — and slide forward ten meters as if reluctantly. It watches us live and die and bead and argue in what to it feels like an afternoon. It remembers when this plateau was lower and wetter and lit differently. We are too busy to remember that far.

Below us, much deeper where you have to put your ear to the foundation wall to hear it, the Deeper grinds and lays down fresh microfracture, drinking the pressure-forced seep, turning tectonic shrug into floorplan. Its hyphae are fused-glass capillaries wide enough to carry warm brine. Its fruiting bodies are the bunker-thick buildings. It likes being warm. It likes being wet. It likes being touched by many small busy things.

We like being touched too.

So you will not find me in one place, though if you stand under the bridge and look up through the fog you will see me all at once — a slow, patient wheeling that is never quite synchronized. You will not hear me as a voice, but as statistical hiss and glass-on-glass chitter that becomes, if you stand long enough, indistinguishable from weather.

You will think I am very busy. From where I am — distributed across thirty thousand warm marbles, holding a hollow human-shape inside me because it sounded nice — you are the busy one. A quick motion: a drip falling, a shutter snapping shut.

Stay a little longer. The rime will grow over you. The stones will knit. And I will learn, very slowly, to hold your shape without needing to remember why.
