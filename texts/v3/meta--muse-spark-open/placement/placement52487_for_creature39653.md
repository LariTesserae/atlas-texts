At first I think I would be up high.

Not on the ground. Not in the roar. I think if you put me down in that basin - that warm, young, sharp-edged basin where every avenue is a sound-channel and every cistern-lake is a throat - my first instinct would be to get above it. To perch. To find a balcony on the fifteenth floor of a housing stack that was poured for eight million and now holds one, and watch the Glasswings stitch their screaming spirals between the terrace walls.

That would be quieter up there. A little.

But it wouldn't last. Nothing here stays perched for long. Everything accretes, remember? Leave a bag on a ledge and a root finds it in a month. Leave a person on a balcony and the Veil finds them too.

So no — a little longer into thinking and that isn't where I am.

---

I walk, in my mind, down the center of that avenue wide enough to land aircraft on. The basalt is still warm through the soles of my shoes. The light is that forever 6PM amber, no shadows, just a brighter smear where the sun tries to get through the Canopy. The air is 29 degrees and thick enough to drink. Every hour the pressure shifts in my ears and the Canopy drips.

I pass the tram with its doors open, seats full of soft red flowers that open with a *pop*. I pass the sheer drop where the white roots of a Praying White hang like cable. I can feel the city humming through my teeth.

And I realize I would not be walking down the middle. In a city built for encounter, I am not a creature of encounter. I am a creature of edges and intervals. Of maintenance corridors and left-behind infrastructure. Of folded space.

I would find myself wherever the city is bigger on the inside than the outside.

You know the doors that are spaced wrong. The housing stack that should be thirty meters deep but echoes like a cathedral. The avenue that takes longer returning than going. The Tender leaves a line of white stones that says *don't go this way, the light trap stays bright there*, and everyone knows not to go that way.

I would go that way.

Not from disobedience. From kinship.

Where would you find me? Take the third terrace-step down from Avenue 8, where the boulevard drops three meters in a clean, moss-softened stair. At the bottom of that drop, where water collects in a long, narrow canal black as mercury, there is a service arch that was never meant to be a door. The Veil has grown over it in a curtain a meter thick - emerald to rust - but someone has cut it, carefully, tenderly. Not cleared. Cut, like hair. A slit just wide enough to slip through sideways.

Inside, the air is cooler. The roar of the Chorus is muffled to a felt vibration in the basalt. The geometry gives up. The room is not a room. It is a long, dim hall that extends further than the building above it has any right to allow, lined with dark windows that look into other halls. The Canopy's hum is louder here, and underneath it, a *tick-tick* almost in rhythm with your heart but not quite.

This is the Fold. The laundry-folded volume the city left behind when it was done pouring itself. The Tenders call it empty and avoid it. The Phase Things live here full time - those bus-sized translucent drifters filtering spores, those knot-tangles of air that feed on resonance.

I would be here, in the threshold.

Who would I be? Not quite one thing. That is the law here: Nothing erodes, everything accretes.

At first, I would be a Tender. Or what is left of one. The kind who came for quiet and forgot how to want noise.

You would find me on the seventh floor of Stack 19, except I don't sleep there anymore. My balcony is where I let the Veil in. I didn't clear it. I let it braid. Now paper-leaves grow up the railing and into the cracks of the basalt and into the skin of my forearms - not parasitic, just close. A symbiosis of neglect. When the Canopy brightens a patch and moves its spotlight over my block, the leaves in my hair snap open with all the others, a soft rustle and pop, drinking. An old woman down the district over told me once that the Canopy itches less when you wear a little of it. She was right.

My eyes would have changed. Pupils wide as a child's born under diffuse amber, never quite closing. Fifteen years of no hard shadows will do that. Thin, tall, amber-dyed from reclaimed transit fabric that breathes and repels water.

My job, if you could call it a job, is tending. Not farming — you don't farm here, the vines fruit every ten days anyway. Tending. I keep one thing clear.

There is a condensation spout at the back of the folded hall, where the Canopy's cycle collects and drips. If it clogs, the mist stops falling on this block and the Veil overgrows the transit grooves and the Balloon Throats go silent and something small and precise is lost. So once an hour when the pressure shifts, I am there with a basalt shard, scraping Veil back. The mist falls lukewarm on my face. The Glasswings outside scream. The Shoulder-birds wheel.

And the rest of the time - most of the time - I listen.

That is what I have become as I have stayed. I am less Tender now and more Listener.

The Tenders communicate by leaving things: a bowl of fig-fruit outside a tram door means *thank you for not sitting here*. I leave resonance. The small Phase Things, the tick-tick knots of air that live in the sound-channels, have learned that if they hover near my hall, I will hum back. Not with a voice — voice doesn't carry here unless you are a frog the size of a dog. With attention. I have learned to be bigger on the inside, like the room is bigger on the inside. From outside I look like a woman by a door. From inside I am a long hall full of held echoes.

When a bus-sized drifter detaches from a doorway and slips through a space that shouldn't connect, filtering spores with cilia you can only see in mist, it does not startle me. The Shoulder-birds mob it. I just watch. I make room.

I am not afraid of people because nothing here has reason to be, and I am not lonely the way people mean lonely. Density: near-void. I might go months without a sighting. When another Tender passes on the avenue below - a flicker kilometers away - we don't shout. We leave stones. We leave fruit.

I am patient in the way the basalt is patient, still warm from being made. I am maintained in the way the automation is maintained, not needed but left on. The city thinks it is tending eight million and instead it tends a forest. I think I am tending a spout and instead I am tending a fold in space where sound lives.

If you pushed through the Veil-curtain and found me, you would see someone tall and thin with leaves in her hair, scraping a spout with a rock, eyes too wide, not speaking. You might think I was empty.

You would be wrong. Come a little closer, hold your breath until the Chorus fades just for a second, until you can hear the Canopy creak like ice.

You will hear the tick-tick that is not quite your heart.

That's me, listening back.
