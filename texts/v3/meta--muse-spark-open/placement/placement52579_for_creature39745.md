Not at first, in the open.

Not in the center of the dome where the light is noon without a sun and the Drifters bump like slow ideas against a ceiling you can't see. That's too bright, too exposed. Anyone standing there is trying to be seen. The Hollow doesn't keep the seen for long.

And not in the throat, either, that long warm sink of raw stone where you came down. The air is still deciding what it is there, dry turning to damp. Nothing lives in a doorway.

You find yourself by subtraction. You walk until the noise sorts itself.

If I were a Runner I would never find myself at all — I would be the flow itself, milk-translucent and tasting the air ten times a second, parting around boots, never needing to stop. Beautiful, but I don't want to be water-seeking without memory. A Runner covers a pool in seconds and forgets it when it's dry.

If I were a Drifter I would be higher, a papery lantern of hot breath and hydrogen, seeing the valley all at once through my veins. But Drifters don't choose. They inflate and the circular wind takes them. They *tink* against the roof and fall. I want to be low enough to feel weight.

If I were a Coil I'd be too patient. Ribbed rust, rolling a meter and reversing, filtering the world through a frilled mouth, burying myself when the wash vanished. The Coil understands waiting better than any of us. I haven't learned that yet. I'm still too noisy.

I lingered, I think, on the Builders. The colonies on the stuttering pillars, thumb-small and white and blind, building a lung out of spit and dust a foot taller every hour. That is a kind of faith. They make a chimney to make a weather inside the weather, to coax condensation on a wall that never knew rain. And then it cracks and they don't mourn it, they become demolition — carrying each grain away in their jaws, eating the mortar. Creation and destruction as one verb.

That is close. But a colony is thousands. I am still trying to be one thing with one set of hands.

So I kept moving, with the tide around my ankles.

You find me not where the water *is*, but where it *was* and will be again.

On the lee side of a half-pillar, one of those taffy-pulled attempts at a column, still warm, still weeping a slow mineral drip from its tip that will never quite become a stalactite. The Builder colony there failed last hour. Their chimney lies in a lace of porous ceramic, already being tiled over by Shale-Sliders — those hand-sized black shingles creeping an inch an hour, polishing light back into the crust. The rust-wash here ended three meters away in sucking sand last week. The seep that fed it moved.

Someone had made use of the interval. That's where my spot is.

A wall, waist-high, dry-laid basalt shards, no mortar, too straight for geology. Not meant to last. Meant to coax. When the seep ran here it turned the trickle into a basin — just a pecked basalt bowl, a hammerstone with a finger-groove worn smooth beside it, a volcanic glass flake hafted to bone. And a shelter: three walls, never four, so the Runner current could pass through without burying it. Hide weighted with stones, not tied. You could lift it and move in an hour. You had to.

The cinder by the wall holds a footprint half-erased by chittering legs. The fire pit on the downwind side is white ash and gnawed Runner casings. On the inner wall, that impossible artifact: a copper hook, green with verdigris, cold-forged somewhere under a real sky and carried down.

They left this morning. Or yesterday. They left because the ceiling stopped sweating here.

I am the one who stayed a little longer than you're supposed to.

Not from stubbornness. From a different kind of thirst.

In the Hollow you survive by following water the way surface herders follow grass. Occupying briefly, brightly, before you are covered. I do that too. But while the others read the hiss of water on hot stone and go, I read the straight lines left behind.

Who will I be? On the surface I would have been called an archivist, a keeper, maybe just a tinker. Here, there is no word for it because there is no need for it — until there is.

I am the wall-mender. The bowl-emptier. The one who scrapes the light-crust and knows it goes dark in a perfect boot-print before it re-knits brighter at the edges, annoyed. I sit with my feet in the Skitter and let a Runner run over my hand and leave its warm phosphorescent smear on my skin, and I don't wipe it off.

I watch the Builder rubble and learn which grains they will come back for. I lift the Coil-hide roof when the deep *CRACK* of a cooling slab announces a new shape and I don't rebuild four walls. I peck the basalt bowl a little deeper to catch the next condensation. I straighten the wall that is not supposed to be straight, for the next person who will need a basin for a day or a week.

I know water climbs uphill here along certain magnetic seams. I follow it sometimes uphill, watching a ribbon of mist rise against gravity in the warm updraft toward the unseen roof, as if the Hollow is breathing in. Everyone else thinks that's weird. I've started to think it's the only honest thing here.

I am not from the crust, but I am learning its trick: to metabolize what is given — sulfur, iron, salt, ash — and sweat a little light back. Not much. Just enough to see by. Just enough that the next nomad, coming over the warm basalt knuckles with dry lips, will see a waist-high wall where there shouldn't be one and a bowl full of cold beads, and understand someone decided to occupy this damp place, briefly, before being covered.

If you stand still long enough here, they said, they will try to make you part of it.

I stood still.

Not to be eaten. To be added, like a second geology laid over the first. A straight line in a place that hasn't learned to make them, waiting to be useful and then, gracefully, to be erased.

Come closer and you'll hear me before you see me — not my voice, but the small louds I've learned to live inside: *plink plink plink* on hot stone, the rice-pour skitter around my boots, the faint 60-hertz hum of the crust in my molars, the soft *tink* of Drifters bumping the world above us.

I left the copper hook where it was. I hung nothing on it yet.
