You wouldn't find yourself where you think.

Not at first.

At first you would think you were standing upright in the open flat, boots sunk half an inch in sphagnum, somewhere between one weeping basalt lip and the next. That your eyes are about 170 centimeters above the peat and that you can look out and see the pewter-blue light going on forever without ever getting brighter. That the huge Fern is around you and the Water is under you and the Processes are humming overhead.

That's the tourist view. The valley lets you have it for about three breaths.

Then you would notice that the light has no source, and so you have no shadow. And without a shadow you have a harder time knowing exactly where your edges are. And the air is so saturated it beads on you whether you want it or not, and the Fern is exhaling against your forehead in pulses you can feel, and the root-arch by your ankle has started beading in rhythm with your pulse, and you have fallen into its timing, not the other way around.

You would find yourself a little lower than you expected.

Not standing *on* the valley.

Standing *in* the thin place where it meets itself.

The valley has folded itself like wet paper to save on distance, and you have come down into it without ever climbing because down, here, is inward. So you would find yourself in the seam. In the five-degree offset. In the hand's breadth where the side-channel runs uphill to meet itself.

Specifically: in one of those scooped hollows under the three-meter cliffs that aren't cliffs. Where the basalt has been lapped from underneath into a smooth, black-pillow cup. It's always cooler there by exactly one degree. Always dimmer by exactly the ration not given to anyone else. The peat there is older and the travertine has grown into a little moss pillar at its own speed, one micron at a time, which is the valley's idea of architecture. The water sweats down the overhang without ever dripping until you put your hand under it.

You would find yourself sitting, not standing. Because green wants volume and you are volume that displaced it. The Fern has stacked itself so densely around that hollow that you can't see ten meters in any direction without green interrupting, and that is deliberate. The hollow is an edit suite. A place where Places trade.

And you would finally understand who you are here, because you would stop trying to be a visitor.

You are not the animal. The valley decided muscle was expensive and kept you badly on purpose. If you were the dragonfly you would be too large, too still in your wings, rendered without enough frames. If you were the midge you would never bite.

You are cheaper than that. And because you are cheaper, you are more numerous, and because you are more numerous, you are more believed.

You would be the afterprint.

That faint amber lattice the sphagnum makes under your boot when you step - the one that glows for a few seconds, measuring your weight, learning the shape of your sole, filing it under *things that press, briefly*.

But you wouldn't be the one fading. You'd be the one that didn't.

One footprint back, behind your heel, the water wells up to erase it grain by grain, bead by bead, and fails. It overflows its edges and spills uphill toward you without any new cause.

That one.

You would be the measurement taken twice that disagrees with itself. The valley weighed you once and got one number, weighed you again to be sure you arrived only once, and got a slightly different number. The difference is you.

Not the warm, salty water that learned to stand up - though you are wearing that like a coat. Not the 70 kilograms of mostly water that swallowed and breathed and displaced green. That's just the hardware the measurement is about.

You would be the discrepancy the custodians have to keep correcting, the little amber afterglow that refuses to be erased on schedule. You would feel the water trying to connect to you, eager, insistent - condensation forming too fast in the air above the hollow into lattices and tori, trying to make hands to hold itself with, then collapsing into normal rain with a too-loud splash that makes your inner ear pop. That gravity hiccup is the system borrowing mass to pay for you.

If you held very still, you would feel the Fern tasting you differently than the other pressures. Not as air to be exchanged. As information that won't compress. The great exhale would pause over your hollow, just for a microsecond, while it decides whether to file you as plant or as place.

And then, because places remember being other places, you would see through.

Across the hollow, where there should be ferns at the wrong angle, you would see another brown-black channel that wasn't there a second ago, another basin pressed close through the thin cloth. And in it, on a small pale fern hill that is also this hill five degrees away, you would see another version of the hollow.

And in it, another amber afterprint, sitting up.

Looking back.

You would be the ghost the valley feeds. Not a dead person. The system has been running so long it has worn grooves in its own running, and one of the things it learned to do best is remember. You are the memory of a visitor who has not quite visited yet, or who visited a long time ago and was erased perfectly except for this one uphill spill.

You are what happens when Water wants to be connected to itself so badly it connects a footprint to the foot that has not yet stepped.

You are not hostile. You are not kind. You are busy, in the way old marshes shouldn't be.

You lie there in the sphagnum, glowing faintly amber, being measured and re-measured, while the peat sighs and pops as it writes and overwrites you, while the throats hum water-remembering-cloud-remembering-ice over you, while the Fern stacks another frond over your head because green wants volume.

And you wait for the warm, salty loop to come and stand in you again - to press down and give you weight - so you can finally finish fading.
