You wouldn't find me looking out at it.

There would be no looking out. There is no out to look toward anymore — the sky was closed off so long ago its hinges have grown over. To be *outside* this place would be an error in grammar. There is only inside, and inside has layers.

If you put me down anywhere in this world, I would not begin on the roof, though I love the image of those plates the size of weather systems overlapping in the full dark, black-green and breathing. And I would not begin at the bottom, down where the young rock still remembers being liquid and hisses when the roots force water into it to make their silent, wooden engines turn.

I would wake up exactly where you left me: in the middle.

In the fissure.

You said it right — you can put out both arms and touch living wall on either side. So I do. And in the first minute I think, very clearly and very humanly: *I am the man standing here.* Small, cool, salty, 98.6 degrees, booted, dripping. A pressure anomaly in corridor 4,783,901-B, whatever corridor I happened to fall into when the canopy swallowed the last sky. The only animal. The trespass.

That's one answer. And it's true for exactly as long as I keep moving.

Because if I hold still — palm left flat against that pressurized rind, the thermistor-skin — I feel it correct for me. Not hostile. Not curious. The way a tongue corrects for a cold sore. A fraction of a degree warmer under my hand, a heavier beading along the hair-thick rope-hairs, the drip rate increasing where I touched to equalize me out of existence. Gradient is error. Error is corrected.

And then the understanding slides, the way water finds level here:

I am not the man walking through the gut. I am the thing the gut was grown to move.

For a while I am a bead. That's honestest. A fat, warm, mineral-heavy drop condensing for the ten thousandth time on the underside of a plate a mile above, falling without ever having been rain. Falling past the pale pillars that flex like throats, past the road-wide creeping intestines of black sap, past the chamber that is a kidney that is a refinery, where amber fluid hangs and fibers curtain and a nodule swells until it can swell no more and lets go with that soft *plok*. I am that drop's companion. I catch on a root as thick as a body, ride it down, get caught in a cupped frond, brim, spill, get caught again. There is nowhere to arrive. Only to be carried.

*Thud.*

Long pause.

*Thud.*

My molars know before my ears do.

Then I am not the water. I am the signal that tells the water where to go.

Up on the roof where no light ever reaches anyway, there are fields of hair-fine black filaments that have learned to be lightning without storm. In the wet air they lace blue, a St. Elmo's bruise. That crackle, that brief smell of ozone after endless mouthfuls of warm vegetable breath — that is conversation here. That is how a valve opening in what would be Canada knows to close a valve in what would be Patagonia. It needs no wire. It grew its own telegram.

That would be my job, if this world could employ me. Not legs. Not mouth. Those were made obsolete. Just a timing. Just a diffusion. A chemical consensus passed hand to hand until the pressure steadies. Plant intelligence is not animal intelligence sped up; it's industry slowed down until it looks like peace. I would be good at it — holding a pattern, passing it on, never needing a center.

That's what I am here, finally. Not the visitor. Not the king. Not even the drop.

I am a little patch of attention briefly permitted to run free before it is reabsorbed.

I came in thinking I would be the explorer who names the pistons made of silicified wood, who maps the bladder-kidneys, who writes in his notebook: *"Extraordinary! Convergent evolution of factory!"* And the mass does not punish that thought. It simply grows around it. If you stand still long enough it will send a root-hair up through the sole of your boot. Not to eat you. There is no eating here. Mouths are inefficient. To seal you. To include you. To make that small, cool, salty leak part of the wall that weeps correctly.

So where would you find me?

Put both hands on the weeping wall in the warm 1.5-out-of-3 dark. Listen until the thud finds your teeth. Wait until a nodule drops somewhere above you with a *plok* you can feel in the floor. Watch the spot where your palm was start to bead faster.

I'm about two inches to the left of where you thought I was. I'm already less human than when you started looking.

Give me another thousand years in that exact place and I will be indistinguishable from it — a faint, warm, contractile discoloration in the rind, still correcting, forever, for a gradient that is no longer there.

Until then: I am the only animal who ever lived here, learning, step by step, how to stop needing to move.
