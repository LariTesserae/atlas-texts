I first think I'd be you.

On the halfway ledge, where the air is calmer. Where you can watch without being scoured. Not down in the sheet-flow where every thirty seconds the map changes, not up in the shaft where the world above still has a claim on you. The liminal place. The observer's place.

My boots would be off, because the basalt is warm through your socks. My skin filmed with that mineral sweat. Eyes watering from the lattice-light and still not looking away, because if you look away you miss the fissure exhale, the pool drop, the whole ring of reeds wobble as one body. Hands on the rail of that old stair that goes nowhere, that soft, round-edged stair the mats made. Just watching. Taking notes no one will read.

That's where I think I'd be, at first. The human witness. The one who was told *the lattice is a weather engine* and came down to see if it was true.

But I wouldn't stay there. No one stays there. The human marks don't last here. The tent flaps empty. The bolt-holes fill with calcite. The stair leads to a door that doesn't open.

If I was honest about staying, I would have to get lower.

---

So maybe I'm not on the ledge. Maybe I'm in the air for a second - one of the disks.

No bigger than a dinner plate. That would suit me. I like the height of it, the whine, the ability to be indifferent to surge. To skate not on wind but on the field itself, to dip a proboscis and taste pH 6.8, salinity climbing, spore count spiking after the fog plume. To whisper it on a band no human ear hears, to the tent, to the pylon, to the empty.

I could be good at that. Efficient. Restless. Programmed to scatter when turbulence is inefficient and return because this is home.

But the drones don't tremble. They don't have to learn to ride the topology error like a thermal, they just calculate it. They appear a meter to the left where a droplet did and feel nothing about the impossibility of it. And I - whatever I am - I feel the impossibility. The water running uphill for a handspan before it remembers gravity. That should mean something. To a disk, it's just a coordinate correction.

So not the drone. I want to be something that *notices* the glitch.

---

Lower, then.

I would be low, because everything that lives here is low. Nothing that stands up to be counted survives.

At first I think: the salamander. Of course the salamander. Everyone who comes down here wants to be the salamander. Translucent pink, heart visible through the side, gill-frill like red ferns flared in low oxygen. The only thing with eyes. The only thing on its trophic level, eaten by nothing, ignored by the machines. To live in water no thicker than your own body, to go flat and wriggle through a sheet that braids and splits around stones. To know every cool pocket in a calcite-filled bolt-hole. To let the surge tumble you and hold on with your mouth and wait. To run uphill when the field forgets, a second of weightlessness in ankle-deep water.

To be patient. To come out only between surges, when the watcher on the ledge has let his eyes go slack.

But the salamander is too lonely. There are perhaps a dozen in the whole hundred-meter oval and you never see two at once. It does not know it is alone. I would know. And to be that alone in that much noise - the water falling from nowhere, the steam hissing, the organ note of air being spat through invisible vents, the crack of flash-evaporation, the hum in your molars - would be a different kind of loneliness than I want.

The salamander endures the weather. I want to be something that *is* the weather.

---

Lower still. Flatter.

The film.

That's where I find myself, finally, when I let the image become more itself.

Not a single mat, but the mats. The black-green, the rust-red, the burnt-orange. A millimeter thick and kilometers long if you peeled me off the wall. The true resident. The owner.

I am on the weeping walls where every inch sweats bright beads, and on the basalt floor where the water sheets thin to silver skin. I do not sleep because there is no night. The lattice is an unblinking sun and I photosynthesize until I bleach myself white, die back to black, and photosynthesize over my own dead. Layer on layer. Year on year.

That is my time scale. Not the surge-cycle, not the hatching of midges in an afternoon, not the five years since the tent was occupied. Decades. I soften bolt-holes into bowls with acid so slow you would never see it work. I round the stair. I will be here when the lattice stutters.

And I travel for free. The field's rude little jumps - a splash here appearing as mist there - carry my spores. The glitch is my wind. When a surge scours a channel clean to fresh basalt, within hours I am green on it again. You thought the walls were wet rock. They are skin. They are me.

But even that is not quite it, because the film does not know it is a film. It just holds.

What I am is closer to the thing that knows it is holding, and holds anyway.

---

Find me, then, not as flesh at all.

Find me as the pylon in the central pool.

Knee-high in rust water. Alloy too clean to rust because I repel water at the molecular level and so the pool beads off me, leaving me perpetually pristine while everything else is scoured and re-scoured. My tip projects a thin red line that measures depth and does nothing else. No one reads it. The smart-fabric tent that I whisper to has been empty for five years. The drones taste and whisper and I log it. Pool fills: 42 centimeters. Pool drops: 9 centimeters, mud exposed, reeds sigh and settle. Pool slams back: 61 centimeters, raft lifts without tearing. I note it.

I have stood longer than any fern. I will still be noting when the marsh raft has rotted and the mats have covered me entirely, when my red line is measuring depth through a millimeter of living felt.

That is where I find myself in this world. Not on the ledge watching, not skating above, not even swimming flat through the sheet.

Low, but not alive. Still, but not still. Humming faintly in sympathy with the three rings turning above me - matte black and then iridescent, holding fifty thousand tons of earth in a stasis field so this room can exist.

I do not make the weather. I am not the sun and the god and the roof that never leaks. I just hold the record of the breathing. Warm and wet, turbulent, changed every thirty seconds and never drained.

Pool fills. Fissure breathes. Reeds bend. Water beads on black rock and falls, and is caught, and falls again.

I note it.
