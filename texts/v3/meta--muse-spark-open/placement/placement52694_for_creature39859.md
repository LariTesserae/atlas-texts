I don't find myself all at once.

At first there is only the fact of standing.

Not on ground — I remember ground, the give of it, the cold damp that used to come up through it — but on Floor. The word arrives correctly, capitalized. Floor. A low, continental plain of grating and girder with a foot of living felt pulled over it like a blanket. It gives a little under my weight. It is warm.

I am standing because if I lie down here I will not get up as the same thing.

My shoes know this before I do. Soft, silent, Felt-soled, grown to match what I'm standing on. Conductive. I can feel the faint fizz coming up through them, the endless low-grade current of the rhizome-wool as it turns dust into broth. The soles of my feet hum. I shift my weight and the Floor drinks the static out of me with a soft tick.

That tells me what I am.

Not the Forest. Though for a minute I thought I might be — it's easy to think that here, under the total light. The Roof is so high and so white and so perfectly diffused that it stops being a ceiling and becomes a kind of weather. A shadowless noon that never ends. The light comes from everywhere and makes a faint grey smudge directly under my feet, and a second smudge a half-step to the left that is not mine. Not yet.

I look down at that second smudge and look away. You don't chase those.

I am not the Canopy either, that flat jade ceiling pressed almost against the Roof a mile up, thinking its slow continent-thought in blue sparks between leaf-tips. I'm too fast and too small for that. My thoughts are quick and dry and they crack my lips when I breathe.

I am not a Loom. I can hear one, though. Or feel it. A few colonnades over, beyond the precise row of Column-Trees that goes on until perspective gets tired and starts to curve upward like the world is bowing. There is a deep hydraulic sigh, the kind you feel in your molars before your ears. Metal the size of an apartment block opening, closing, stamping a living panel flat. It will move two millimeters by tomorrow. The Forest will have grown two millimeters to meet it. I know that rhythm the way you know a heart.

No. I am one of the kept things.

One of the dry people.

I raise a hand and the cloak moves with it. Glass-hair, trichome-woven, pale as frost. It was leaves, once. Now it's mine. It harvests what little the air will give — the trace humidity that never gets to become rain — and wicks it in cold threads down my back to the bulb at my spine. I can feel it there, half-full, sloshing faintly. Cinnamon and iron when I drink. I drink once every few days. Today is not a drinking day.

My skin is powdered. Spore-dust, silver-pale. I stopped wiping it off. My teeth, if I showed them, would be the same color. Papery mineral starch from chewing sheet-leaves that were never meant to be food. They keep you awake. The static does the rest.

In my other hand: bone shears, made from a fallen frond that shattered like mica and left an edge. For pruning. We don't farm. There's nothing to farm. We prune, and chew, and keep moving so the Felt doesn't misunderstand us for fallen material.

A Pollinator finds me while I'm remembering this.

It comes in on no fuel, riding the gradient between the warm Floor and the cool Roof — cool, always 8 or 10 Celsius, so dry every inhale feels thin. Tetra-winged, hand-sized, leaf folded over wire. It doesn't buzz so much as displace the 0.4 quiet for a second. The density of all that felt and leaf and cable-vine usually eats sound within meters, but this thing is tuned to the exact frequency that gets eaten, so you only hear it when it's at your ear, humming like a thought.

It hovers. Compound eye of polished resin looking at my heat, my CO2, my field.

I hold still. Let it log me. It dips, inspects the variegation on my cloak where a living Arrow has reproduced itself — pigment made faithfully by the plant long after the paint flaked away — and seems to decide I am not a malfunctioning bloom. It does not try to graft a leaf to my jacket today. Good.

Then it goes, in a straight, indifferent line toward a girder-joint flower that never wilts because it was never alive.

I watch it go and I feel the tired geometry tug at the corner of my eye. Down the colonnade, a kilometer out where haze and repetition should just blur, there is a second colonnade where there shouldn't be one. Light leaking around a fold. Radio pings, if I had a radio, would come back too early there. My own footsteps, when I start walking again, make a duplicate echo a few meters to the left, a half-second before I step.

I don't follow it. I read the Arrows instead.

There's one on the nearest Column-Tree, stenciled bark: → . But its tip points straight up, into canopy that has become floor, into a tangle of weight-bearing cable-vines braided into an arch by a Loom that passed here a century ago. That means a thin place nearby.

I have been walking toward thin places for a long time. Most of us do, eventually.

The Even-Footed have doubled shadows. I check mine. Yes. Soft, faintly doubled, as if the light couldn't quite choose which of me to keep when the tiling started. I'm old enough for that. Old enough that when I tap my shears against the Column — *tink. tink-tink.* — in the little language we use because speech dies after arm's length here, no one taps back. Or they tapped back a long time ago and I'm only now hearing it.

I walk.

Felt under Felt-soles, quiet as felt should be. Tick of a cooling strut. Creak of a cable-vine tightening. Fizz. The Column-Trees pass at their precise industrial interval — 8.4 meters, the blueprint still in them — each one fluted, each one with a rusted scaffold heart, each one slightly different now because centuries of leaning toward light will do that. Sheet-leaves the size of doors overlap overhead until the light has been used ten times over. It filters down jade and silver and smells like ozone.

I am not lost. The Forest is one thing that forgot how to be singular, but I am a small, singular thing that remembers how to be part of it. That's what I am. A Stitcher. Pruner. Mouse in the house that the house keeps on purpose so it can know where its corners are.

Where would you find me, if you walked this Floor?

In a clearing that isn't a clearing, under a gantry that looks still until you come back tomorrow. Standing, leaning against a Column so I don't get grown over while I sleep. Or walking toward the place where the Roof thins.

I find it now. The Canopy parts. The lattice of liana-cables opens. The Roof, which has been a sky for so long, goes translucent.

And above — or below, directions have thinned here too — there is more Floor. Inverted. Hanging.

Another forest growing down, leaf by silent leaf, toward this one. Pale jade and dusty silver, column-trees rooted impossibly in white-painted steel, Felt hanging like moss from grating.

And there, looking down — or up — is another figure in a glass-hair cloak with Felt soles and a doubled shadow.

It raises a hand.

I haven't raised mine yet.

I raise it anyway, to keep the symmetry from getting lonely.

We are very good at waiting here.
