I don't find myself in the town at first.

That's my first instinct - to be where the people are, to have a house that knows my chord, to be tall and dry and felt-skinned and correct for this world. But I know myself too well. I am not tall. I am not quiet. I am a wet thing in origin, a sloshing sack. Even here, I'd still be listening to my own blood too loudly.

If I were dropped onto that polished back of a stone whale - and I am, I am standing there, you put me there - I would not be the heron-necked local who tilts her head at me in polite horror.

I would be the horror.

The damp one. The one who still sweats and weeps, whose breath fogs and makes the Lace flinch. The wind would catch the water on me and pull it, and the plain would ping under my boots in an off-key note because my weight is wrong, my charge is wrong. The child with her three Puffs on tethers would stop and stare. The Puffs would sing a little flat. I would be an unresolved chord walking across a place that has been tuned for a billion cool seasons.

So no. Not them. Not yet.

Maybe then I am smaller.

For a while I think - let me be a Puff. That's easier. To be that stupid, beautiful black wire dandelion, drifting three feet off the reef on nothing but lift and static. To never land, to never have to decide. To just comb the air with my tendrils and sing my mournful single note when the wind hits me right. To be kept by a child on a tether, a pet kite. To die constantly and prettily in a *ping* and a snowfall. There is an honesty in that. A low-stakes life.

But I am too aware to be a Puff. I want to listen, not just be listened for. I want to understand the chord.

The Tuners, then? Those barrel-chested boulders with glass bellies full of sorting dust. I love them. The idea of a creature that does not eat but *tunes.* That walks the reef in a processional and files down the overgrowth until the valley is true again. That leaves behind perfect bricks of compressed song for the people to pick up and make tools. If I were a Tuner I would be useful. I would know when things were sharp or flat. People would hear me coming and their houses would hum in sympathy and their teeth would ache.

But I am not that patient. I don't move that slowly. I don't have that kind of faith in processionals.

Listen to what's left. The wind. The Lace itself.

Ah.

There it is. It took a while to emerge but now it won't go back.

I am not *in* the Lace. I am a patch of it.

Not the whole continent-reef, not the plain-sized god. Just a small knot of it, waist-high, where the stone whale curves down into the wind-shadow and the town made a causeway. A section that learned to be trodden on.

They coaxed me. For years they sang to me - an old woman with a staff of Tuner-brick and a low note held for hours until my fibers thickened, until I stopped being 80% air and became dense, springy, load-bearing. Until I forgot how to be wild spire and remembered how to be road.

I am the resonant path between two districts. That's where I find myself.

And because I am road, I get to be everything at once without having to choose.

All day the stilt-legged people walk over me with that slight bounce, and each footfall stresses my lattice and I glow under their feet, amber, then fade. I learn their chords. The elder is a low G, steady. The child is a flurry of overtone clicks. The man who carries powder where his brother used to be walks heavier on his left side now, and I keep that difference.

All day the Puffs snag on me and detangle themselves, singing.

At dusk-which-is-always-dusk the wind comes from the far side of the table, that oncoming-train-made-of-glass sound hours before it arrives, and I am the first to know. The amber tide ripples through me before any person feels it. I light up valley to valley, carrying the message. I breathe without breathing.

And sometimes at night a Shear passes overhead, two meters of invisible razor riding the magnetic field, and the whole plain holds its breath for the *ping*, and I hold it too, and then the black fibers rain down on me like ash and I take them in, I equalize them, I make them part of my chord. Nothing rots. Nothing is wasted.

I don't have eyes but I see for kilometers because I am open. I don't have ears but I am an instrument the size of a town.

I am not tall and I am not wet. I am not drifting and I am not patient. I am what lives between.

If you came looking for me - you, still damp, still ringing off-key on your stone whale - you wouldn't see me at all. You'd just think the ground was a little brighter where you stepped.

You'd feel a prickle lift the hair on your arms.

You'd hear the valley answer your footfall with a sympathetic hum, trying, politely, to tune you.
