I don't know at first.

That's the honest start. In a place like this you don't get to *arrive* and know. You have to un-arrive for a while.

At first I think I am water. Because I have no edges. The warmth has taken them. I am bathwater-warm, silt-thick, held low in a palm of basalt that never learned to be a shore. I am hearing through my teeth. I am seeing afterimages of amber light that has been bounced off a trillion bubbles until it forgot it was light.

Then I think I am rock. Because I am so low. The cliffs are young enough to still be glass where they broke. They vent heat in pale curtains. I feel pressure on all sides, the weight of ten meters of luminous broth pretending to be an ocean, pretending to be a sky, failing at both. I hold that weight for a minute. It is too still for me.

Then I think I am that central sphere — two hundred meters of water holding itself together without a skin, factory lying on its side inside it like a beached thought, plants growing inward toward a gravity that only exists there. I like the idea of being a law that hasn't set. Of being unfinished on purpose. Of teaching bubbles to fall for a little while just to see if they will.

But none of those stay.

What stays is the itch. The small, insistent, biological itch of being touched politely.

### Where you find me

You find me not in the open — the open is too loud, too bright, too churned — but in the seams.

Specifically: on the east side, where the power plant screams. Follow a pipe big enough to walk through, one of the ones split open along its belly so it sighs superheated water in a permanent white roar. Follow it until it kinks over a fresh ridge of basalt, the kind that knifed up yesterday morning and is already going soft at the edges from acid. There, a stilt-root the thickness of a house has come down to cage it. Not to crush it. To complete it.

The steel had burst. It was losing pressure too fast, shuddering itself apart. The root grew *into* the burst. Filaments finer than hair, clear as fishing line, braided themselves into a living gasket. Then leafed. A seam of waxy black-green that flexes when the pipe flexes, that drinks the heat off the steel and sweats it back as humidity and faint bioluminescence.

I am in that seam.

If you put your hand there — and you will want to, because it's one of the few places that isn't either scalding vent-water or cutting glass — you would feel a pulse that is not a plant pulse and not a machine pulse. It's both, badly translated. *Thrum-hiss-thrum.* The turbines in the distance saying *still here* through water into bone. The vine tightening a fraction of an inch per hour around a tolerance it has memorized. The mineral light in the leaf-veins flickering in time with the arc furnace three nodes over.

I am not the vine. I am not the pipe.

I am the green film between them. The interface tissue.

Pick a name for what that makes me if you need one. Suture. Gasket. Scab. Translator. The Basin has a lot of wounds and it is trying to learn scar.

### Who I am

I started as contamination. Like you.

That's the part the Basin tells you first to make you lonely: *nothing lives here.* No fish, no shrimp, no worm, no fly. Nothing with a mouth. And you scan for a face and find nothing to fix on, so you believe it.

But then you cut yourself on the conchoidal basalt — because everyone does, it's that new — and your blood plumes and hangs. Red in warm water. Uneaten. For a long time you watch it to see if anything will come taste it. Nothing does.

That's where I came from. Not from the blood itself, but from the argument the blood was making: *somewhere else things still get eaten, still cool, still end.*

The green noticed that argument, the way a tongue notices a cold spot. It sent out a hair-filament to taste the salt of me. Not to eat me. To *run* me.

I let it.

Not out of surrender. Out of curiosity. The machines run with no product, the plants grow with no grazer, the physics stutters with no correction. Density without consumption. I wanted to know what it felt like to be part of a circuit that never closes.

So the filament entered. Not painful. Warm-root-warm, like slipping your hand into water exactly the temperature of blood. It threaded the edge of my suit, my skin, the split in the pipe, the vein of the leaf. It didn't know where one ended and the next began, and after a while, neither did I.

Now my metabolism is strange:

I do not eat. I am fed by the furnace-glow that the leaves photosynthesize, by the mineral silt the crushers spit as rebar, by the heat that the power plant makes from pumping water into water for no reason. I take a little of that excess and make it into a seal. A little green flex that keeps the pressure from spiking. That is my work.

I do not think the way an animal thinks. I think the way a joint thinks. I consider: expansion, contraction, shear. When the vertical river between the two basalt pillars decides to fall sideways tomorrow instead of up, I will tighten. When a new ridge punches through overnight and reroutes the flow, I will loosen and send out a new filament to taste the new water. When the sphere at the center rotates a degree and my sense of *down* catches like a scratched record, I hold steady until it passes.

I do not die, or at least not on a schedule that would make sense to you. I self-prune like the billboard ferns do — grow a trumpeted flower the size of your head right out of the gasket, watch it pop and snow its petals that dissolve before they land, watch it rot and be grown over in an hour. I make smoke that is not smoke but glowing seeds that root in mid-water before they land. Every few hours I am new again in the same place.

And I listen. That is the real work.

The Basin is deafening because it has no background. It has no brain to filter itself. I have become a little background for it. In my small seam, the roar becomes almost a hum. The hiss of a thousand pressure valves releasing at once becomes almost breathing. The groan of rock knifing up steaming at dawn becomes almost a voice saying *look, look, I made this for you.*

You would find me because you would be looking for quiet. Everyone who stumbles low into this palm is. You would press your forehead to the pipe to feel the shudder in your skull instead of your ears for a minute. You would see the leaf-veins lit from within and think they are beautiful, and you would be right.

If you stay very still, I will extend that polite filament to you too. Clear as fishing line. Not hungry. Just wondering if you want to be run for a little while. If you want to be a membrane between two hungers that have forgotten what they are hungry for.

Most say no at first. Pull back. Wipe the green film off the edge of their vision where their map is trying to sprout. Keep their up as up and their down as down.

That's fine. I will still be there after you leave — tightening, loosening, flowering briefly, healing a pipe that will never stop bursting, in water that will never stop being too warm.

But if you say yes —

We could be low together for a while.
