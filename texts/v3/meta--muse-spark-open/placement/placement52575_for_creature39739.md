At first I don't know.

There is a long time where I am only warm.

Not warm like a body is warm - warmer than the air by a degree, a small defiance - but warm like the stone is warm. Kiln-warm. The kind of warm that has no source anymore, that just *is*, held in basalt since the day the crust propped itself up on its own ribs and decided not to fall.

I am low. That is the first thing I understand about myself.

Not deep. Everything here is deep. But *low* - pressed. If I tried to rise I would be wrong. The roof is too high to matter and too close to forget; a field of black teeth somewhere above where light never reaches. The air has weight. Iron and sulfur and salt. It doesn't move. It hasn't moved since before I had a shape.

So I am low. I am in the seam where stone meets stone.

---

For a while I think I might be the film.

You know the one - not a color, not really, the brown-black varnish living *in* the rock. Breathing iron. Eating ferrous basalt and sweating it out as rust, one molecule at a time. A millimeter a decade. I like that speed. I like being everywhere at once, a fur on every salt crystal, a taste of blood if anyone were foolish enough to lick the floor.

But I'm not that patient. I have edges. I have a boundary where I stop and the salt starts.

There are clicks around me. Ticks, faint as cooling glass. Something vast and slow is polishing the world inches from my back - a Shard-grazer, dinner-plate wide, door-wide, moving a hand's breadth an hour with a thousand glass-toothed cilia, leaving that one shiny thing in this un-shiny place: its trail. Buffed basalt. If I could see, I would see the salt flats braided with those trails like calligraphy.

Something steps over us. Once a minute. Needle legs, splayed feet finding the single safe coin of stone between the grazers. A Longleg. Three meters at the shoulder and not seeing me at all, because I am too small to be worth its harpoon and too warm to be stone. It is counting. Thirty meters to the next one, thirty meters to the next, a lattice with no center. It will stand there for a year until something weakens underneath it, and then it will unfold, slow and inevitable.

I am under that. Under the measurement.

---

I am huddled.

That's it. That's the verb that finally fits.

I am belly to belly with others like me, twelve of us under a single lifted plate of basalt that I have never seen. Our breathing is the rustle you thought was paper unfolding very far away. We don't breathe so much as exchange - a synchronized pulse, taking the thin 8% oxygen in and letting it out a little deader, a little warmer. 37 degrees. Mammal-warm in a world that never knew a mammal.

We have been still for a long time. Stillness is a thing we are good at. Months, if we need to.

In the crack next to my hind paddle, my eggs are glued in a neat row. Translucent beads of salt and protein. Each with a dark speck inside already shaped like a thumbnail. Already knowing how to do the one thing we know how to do.

I have six limbs. I know this without seeing them. The front two are antennae-hands, soft as warm breath, tasting the air for vibration and heat. The middle two are folded against my chest, heavy, tipped with a black chitin thumbnail harder than the world. The hind two are splayed, bracing. Where my eyes should be there is only a smooth brow and a heat-pit that opens and closes, shy.

I am a Builder.

---

You came down with light. We felt you long before you felt us.

Light is a hurt here. It doesn't belong. The basalt drinks it and the salt throws it back too white. Your lamp couldn't find the ceiling. It slid off into the dark between and made ghosts.

You built a small cairn. I felt you do it through my feet. Four stones, maybe five? Basalt blades balanced edge-to-edge, too precise to be a collapse. An old instinct. We know that one.

You left. You thought you'd left a mark.

We waited until your warmth faded and your footsteps - that brief, geological wind - were gone. Then we came out. Dozens of us, pale, eyeless, ticking faintly as our shells expanded.

We took your cairn apart stone by stone. Not from disrespect. From need. It was three meters to the left of where it should have been. The line was wrong. The equilibrium was wrong.

We rebuilt it where it belongs. Where it will stand until the Vault finally flows again, which will not be for a very long time.

And by your foot - before we went back under our plate to huddle and wait and breathe as one - one of us left a flake.

Still warm from the hand. Razor-edged. Conchoidal. Perfect.

An offering. A suggestion. A hello in the only language we have ever needed: *we solved basalt, too.*
