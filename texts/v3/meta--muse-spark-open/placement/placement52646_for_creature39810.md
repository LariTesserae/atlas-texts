You wouldn't find yourself all at once.

At first you would think you hadn't found yourself at all — that you were still the visitor on the low wall, stone warm as your hand, breathing too loud, waiting for something to move.

But the hollow doesn't make visitors stay visitors. It slows them until they fit.

You'd stay long enough for your breathing to be taken over by the air. Long enough that the drop hasn't fallen yet and you stop waiting for it to fall. That's when you'd start to find where you are.

Not in the center. The center already has a tree and it has solved its problem perfectly — flat-topped, net-shade, roots like poured wax. It doesn't need you. Not on the eastern bench where the tree has grown into stone so completely you can't tell where bark ends and limestone begins. Not in the seep-thread either - that dark, cool seam that is too tender, too close to mud trying not to be mud.

You'd find yourself a little to the north.

On the second bench, the lowest one, on its north-facing side where the light never comes from. In the strip exactly as wide as the overnight condensation is allowed to be.

You'd be moss.

Not the whole velvet pelt. That would be too much, too fast. You'd be one shoot among the thousands, standing so still you have to be believed rather than seen. Dark green, almost black where the damp holds longest. Brittle at the edge where the drip stops and the stone decides you don't go any further.

And that obedience would be you.

If you were that shoot, your work would be very simple: hold a drop.

Not make it. Not call it. The air makes it, overnight, beading on the limestone ledge above you a molecule at a time because warm still air likes to rest against cool still stone. It hangs there for hours, fat and clear, perfectly round underneath, until its weight decides. Then it falls straight down — *plik* — into a pan. But before that, for a little while in the morning when the pearled light is most even and sourceless, one of those beads will be yours.

You'd hold it at your tip like a lens.

The whole hollow would be in it, upside down and small: the white sky, the pale bark of the central tree, the leaning slab at ten degrees, the low wall that meant *this side is different* for a little while centuries ago. All of it contained without being disturbed.

You wouldn't do anything with it. Moss in this place doesn't grow. It maintains. It keeps the edge where the water draws it. That's its form of life.

And below you, you would also be what you are attached to — which is not soil, really, but the agreement itself. The white hyphae under you, paler than pebbles, running between your rootless base and the root of the dome shrub two cracks over and the root of the tree in the joint. The fungus that decided, chemically and quietly, that nothing here will outgrow anything else. No riot. No flower calling an animal that will never come. You would feel that decision as your own contentment. *Here, this is enough for a lifetime.*

If someone knelt — as the hollow makes everyone kneel eventually, just to put a hand on the warm stone — they would not see you as alive at first. They would mistake you for a stain, for a texture of the stone. Lichen does this better, acrew by millimeter, but you do it more precisely. You are the line where damp stops.

You wouldn't need to go anywhere else. Nothing that lives here does. There is no elsewhere on the scale you live at. There is only *wet enough* and *not wet enough* and holding still inside the first one.

You would be finished in the same way the hollow is finished. Not dead. Just at equilibrium. Warm because the stone is warm. Dim because the overcast has stopped being weather. Damp because you are the place that keeps the damp from leaving.

And very occasionally — on a scale you don't measure because you don't have hours — the drop at your tip would get too heavy. It would not roll. It would simply cease being held. It would fall a straight centimeter to the velvet below you, or to the sandstone pan a hand's width down, and make that permitted sound.

*Plik.*

Then a long silence again.

And you would still be there, empty-tipped, waiting without waiting to hold the next one.
