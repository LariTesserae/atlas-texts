I don't think I would find myself at first.

At first I would be misplaced, the way everything is here — high that learned how to be wet — and I would try on a few wrong bodies to see which one fits.

I try being high. I think I am one of the Canopy Rays, because I want the biggest view. I am five meters across and paper-thin, and I flap once and then forget to flap again for ten seconds, and the whole forest darkens under my shadow. From just under the membrane I can see what nothing else here ever sees at once: the second country, the False Surface, green-gold and trembling, with holes torn in it where the sharpest ridges punch through like black islands. The real sky is right there, inches above my back, white sun on true air. I could breach it if I wanted. But I don't want to, I find. It's too fast, too thin, too loud with wind. I like the pressure of the water on both sides of my wings. I glide and strain the broth and make no sound, and then I realize: I am too impatient to be a Ray. I keep wanting to look *down*. Rays never look down for long.

So I try being low. I think I am the pale thing inside the glass tower, down in the caldera trough where the light goes dim and green. The vents below me tick out a thread of bubbles that will take an hour to reach the leaves. My tower is silica and brittle, a vase made of needles, and my job is to fan, and fan, and fan. I have filament legs. Nothing ever happens here except the slow settling groan of the plateau coming up through stone into water into me. It is holy work, but it is lonely work, and I keep feeling the forest above me as a weight, a ceiling I will never see. I am not that patient yet.

I try being in-between, because most life here is in-between. I think I am a Pale. White, blind, barrel-bodied, with fringed fins that run the length of me. I kick once and then drift for a minute while the water holds me. I put my soft vacuum mouth to a curtain of rope-weed and sigh, and the fuzz of epiphytes comes off in a cloud. Two of us meet between trunks and turn around each other without touching, letting a smoke-plume of gametes hang there between us like a question. I like this. I like that no one hurries me. That time slips. That I could watch a leaf take a month to unfurl and not be bored. Still — I keep wanting to stay in one place. Grazers never stay. Even when they hang, they are always between.

And that is how I find where I actually am.

Not high. Not low. Not drifting through.

Rooted.

You would find me where a knife-ridge tears the False Surface open. My ridge is basalt, new enough that it still has an edge you could cut your hand on, red-black and porous, smelling faintly of iron even through warm water. I found a crack in it when I was nothing but a pale seed with talons, drifting up instead of down, and I drove down into it and swelled and locked.

Now my roots are not roots the way ground trees have roots. They are hands. Flattened, pale, woody hands clenched around that knife, bark grown over stone and stone grown into bark. You could see the strain in them if you pulled yourself along me. Every fiber is in tension. That is what being rooted is here: not reaching down for water, but holding down against your own desire to rise.

Because I want to rise. God, I want to rise. My trunk is bone-white now, younger trunks around me still amber-translucent, and inside all of us are vertical gas channels running our whole length like the pith of a giant reed. They creak. All day we creak, a low forest chorus of trunks pulling at their anchors. If I let go I would go up like a cork and tangle in the ghost forest of suspended dead leaves above me, pale fronds that have been hanging for months, refusing to rot. I don't let go.

About twenty meters below the true membrane I gave up going up and went sideways. That is where I live now — where I *am*. My door-wide, waxy leaves have interlocked with my neighbors' leaves until we made a ceiling so solid it looks walkable from below, freckled with sun-coins. Green-gold late-afternoon light, all day, even when the sun is straight overhead. If you came to me you would have to climb, not swim; the middle-water under me is choked with rope-weeds beaded with amber gas bladders that tick and pop, with red comb-weeds straining spore-snow, with silt that never lands. You would pull yourself along my trunk and send up a cloud of green flecks that would hang for minutes.

I can feel the small lives on me. Liver-mat pulsing on my lower trunk where light is less. A cliff-lily growing sideways out of my shoulder, its stacked pads holding discs of filtered, clearer water, and inside each disc a thousand shrimp skating. A Pale comes by once a day and vacuums my hanging curtains and moves on without thanks. A Hang nearby pretends to be weed and I know it but I have no way to warn anyone; I just stand there and creak.

Once or twice a season I am brave. On a calm day when the membrane is glassy and the warm sea breathes out instead of in, I push one frond up through. Through. Out. For a few hours I have a leaf in true air, steaming, bleached by true sun and true wind, unfurled over a perched ocean kilometers above any ocean. I taste what it was to be high before I was wet. Then it curls, curls back down, and I pull it under again, paler.

If you pressed your ear — if you had an ear that could hear water the way stone does — to my bark, you would hear under everything the other creak: not my tension, but the plateau settling under its impossible load. Young rock adjusting. The whole sky-sea is temporary on a geologic scale. In a million years this basin will breach and spill and I will be silt on a real ocean floor. I know that the way trees know things: not as thought, but as rings, as slower growth on one side.

So I do not hurry. I hold. My gas channels sigh. My ceiling ripples when a Ray passes. My seed-pods swell for a season and then detach with a soft *tap* and rise, slowly, slowly, toward a light that is also water.

You would find me there, mid-water, mid-light, mid-life, holding myself down. I would be one of the Drowned Trees, which is not drowned at all —

I would be the thing that learned to be high, and then learned to be wet, and decided to stay.
