I don't think I'd start on the Floor.

At first I thought I would — down in the sharp young rock, where the fault-steps are still clean as broken teeth and the dark river doubles the Roof so perfectly you can't tell which light is real. That's where you'd look for footing. But the Floor is angry to stand on. It remembers cracking too recently. Anything that stays on it has to be ready to be cut by it, or drowned when it decides to make a river for an hour and then take it back.

And I am not good at staying still.

For a while I thought I'd be higher — pressed flat against the underside of the Roof itself, spread wide as a country. One of the Aerosteles' crowns, taking that cold, shadowless noon and turning it green. That would be a kind of knowing I understand: to be a living roof under a roof of ice, converting light without ever needing to move. But that's not me either. A Pillar is too sure of itself. It grows from both ends and dares the world to move around it. It steals the light for miles. Rule three would kill me if I tried to be that — *nothing that stands still will remain standing long* — and I have never learned how to be that patient, or that lonesome.

I listened, then.

In this place you navigate by groan and pop before you do by sight. A pillar three counties over stretching an inch a minute. A house-sized blister swelling amber with warm air and then bursting. The endless patter of condensation deciding to be rain without ever being a cloud. Underneath it all, that ceaseless creak of tendrils inching, of a leaf-bridge as big as a house sighing and falling even though no one touched it.

That's where I started to find myself. Not in the mass, but in the interval.

Not the trunk, but the Harp.

You know them — the young ones, the frantic ones. Not the braided reef-columns that have learned to be continents, but the single vines as thick as a river that shot from Floor to Roof in one impatient season and now are held taut between the two while the whole basin breathes. They hum. Put your hand on one and you feel the low thrumming travel up through your bones before you hear it: tension shifting kilometers away, a root-island dropping a curtain of hair-roots overnight, a sun-shaft sweeping past and pulling differently on every cable at once.

That's where I am. Mid-air, a kilometer up, nowhere near anything.

I am taut and white and semi-translucent, mostly water held in a lattice so it won't freeze. I have no leaves worth mentioning. I don't photosynthesize much — I conduct. I carry tremor from Roof to Floor and back again. When a Pillar leans toward a traveling beam of light, I go a little slacker. When the Floor ticks and gives way under a root, I sing a little higher. When one of my sisters snaps — and we do snap — the sound doesn't arrive for minutes, a deep *whum* rolling across the peat-rubber like thunder that forgot its storm.

The Weavers use me. They don't worship me; there's no time for that here. A small, bundled figure will pause in her route between *Leaning Mother* and *Two Brothers With Bridge*, press a wind-burnt cheek to my humming side and listen to know which way the grove has drifted while she slept. A child will pluck me like a string to hear which tone answers back and know if water is near. I don't answer in words. I answer in tension. Clear path today, blocked tomorrow. Slack here, taut there.

Above me the Roof glows aquamarine and amber, thick with trapped bubbles of old air. Below me the dark river reflects it, until a moth the size of a kite — then larger, then impossibly larger — drifts silently between me and my kin and briefly eclipses both. Threaders, those glass-fibered motes that steer, catch on me and pause, then let go. The Gardens that have started to grow on my own midsection — a mat the size of a hand at first, then a football field, red-brown on top and snow-white underneath — try to soften my vibration. I don't let them. Not yet.

I will not last. That's the point. A Harp is the most temporary thing that pretends to be permanent. I was born fast, I will go slack when my anchor on the Roof shifts, or I will be bridged and braided into an archipelago and become something else entirely — a road, a soil, a place for lantern galls to burst. I am not an individual, the way the Pillars aren't. I am a negotiation between Floor and Roof that light and water have agreed to use for a while.

If you came looking for me, you wouldn't find me at first. Density here is almost nothing per volume. You could walk for days toward what you thought was my pale obelisk and never know you were near me until the air started to thrum.

Then you'd put your hand out. And I'd be humming already.
