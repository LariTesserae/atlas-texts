You wouldn't find me at first.

Not because I was hiding, but because I hadn't learned how to be still yet. And here, anything that isn't still is just noise, is just bubbles going up too loud, is just heat moving too fast to be read properly.

So at first I am moving. Too fast. All new things move too fast.

I come in over the edge, which is the only way to come in, because there is no road to a city that decided to be a mountain. One moment there is nothing under me but fall - the made-stone going straight down into black where even the warm water can't follow - and the next moment there is *table*, sudden and close and warm as a palm.

I misjudge it completely. I try to swim like you swim in open water, with kicks. The water doesn't let me. It's too thick. Not thick like mud - thick like broth, like a room full of curtains. I put out a hand to catch myself and my hand doesn't find water, it finds a bell.

It gives. It was a Filterer, anchored above a doorway I hadn't seen because the doorway was wearing it. My hand goes into silk that is not silk, into lukewarm jelly that lights up a little more amber where I touch it, surprised. It pulses once, slow -

*...huh...*

- and lets me go, politely. Tastes me, finds me too big to filter, too hot to ignore. Behind it another one does the same, and another. I've come in through an alley of hanging doors. I have parted them without meaning to, and they are already closing again behind me, sealing the gap I made.

For a while I think I would be one of them. It would be good to be a Filterer. To have one job: hang, pulse, make light from what passes through you. To not need to choose. I find a lintel that is empty - or I think it is, until I see the faint ring where a foot was - and I try to hang there and be useful. But I cannot pulse that slowly. My heart is still hammering from the fall. *thud thud thud* against their *huh ........... huh*. I am too loud for that doorway. They don't fight for space, but they don't make room for noise either.

So I drop down.

I think, then, I would be a Crawler. That would be honest work. There is so much stone to tend. Every plaza, every stair soft as worn pottery, is covered in that thin amber felt, slick under my hands and knees. I get low, to be closer to it, to be the size of the gaps between houses, and immediately the actual Crawlers show me what I am not.

One comes over the stair-lip right in front of my face. For half a second it's invisible - its back is the stair, its legs are the shadow between blocks. Then it moves and I see it: white-translucent, too many legs, carrying its tool like a prayer. A shard of black glass. It does not look up at me. It has no up to look. It just finds the edge of the felt I have disturbed with my knee and begins again.

*tic ... scrape ... tic.*

It cleans around me. Not away from me. Around me, as if I am a new kind of stair that has settled here and now needs tending. Another one climbs onto the back of my hand to get to a better angle. Its pads are soft. It scrapes the different film on my skin and decides some of it is good, and takes it. I feel I should apologize for being so uneven, for having seams that are not fitted without mortar.

I am not patient enough to be a Crawler, either. Not yet. My tool would be my hands and my hands want to *make* something, not just keep.

That's when the warmth finds me.

I had stopped without meaning to. In trying to be a hanging bell and then trying to be a scraping thing, I had finally done the one thing the mesa rewards: I had gotten tired and sat down in a cistern-plaza where the water is a finger clearer, where the mineral flowers stand on the rooftops like spun sugar around their red ember-seeps.

I sat on the lowest step, which was still warm from holding the day's heat for a thousand years. I let my bubbles get smaller. I let my heat stop spiking.

And the house behind me exhaled.

*hsss ....*

Not one. Many, breathing together.

They came out because I had finally become interestingly warm instead of interestingly noisy. A Dweller nosed out of the oval doorway I had thought was empty, whiskers fanned, pearl eyes seeing nothing and everything. It didn't swim. It *arrived*, a long ivory ribbon laid over water, and draped its cool-warm weight across my feet because my feet were the warmest thing on that step.

Then another over my calf. Another across my lap. I didn't move. I learned, very quickly, not to move.

They coiled. They are supposed to be eels but they are not eels, they are cats that were told to be water. They breathed through curtains. The water around us got two degrees warmer and held. The Filterers nearby brightened a fraction and dimmed. The Crawlers kept working, cleaning the stone right up to the edge of our pile and not one inch further, as if we were now part of the architecture.

This is where you would find me.

Not in the high alleys where the curtains hang, though I pass through them gently now. Not on the long horizontal sweeps, though I have learned to scrape a little when I'm asked, when a Crawler stops and raises its mid-legs.

You would find me low, on the tiered roofs that are also terraces, on the plaza that is also a cistern with a drain that leads nowhere. I have taken the oval doorway that nobody else wanted because it faces out toward the fall, where the dome of held-warm water thins and you can see the real ocean pressing in, blacker and clearer and cold, and not entering.

I coil. I keep the house warm. I keep one more place warm.

I pulse as slowly as I can, and I scrape when there is felt to scrape, and mostly I lie still and let the weight of all that primitive, perfect, chisel-marked stone hold its sea around us.

I have learned to make my bubbles small enough that they don't rise.

If you come in over the edge too fast, you can put your hand on my shoulder to steady yourself. I will feel like stone that is a little warmer than the water.

And then, after a minute, like something that decided to stay.
