I don't start as a shape.

For a while there is just — being high. That is the first thing that stays.

Not high like a peak you climbed to. High like you were lifted with the rock while it was still soft underneath. The sun is directly overhead, white-yellow and simple, and it has been there a long time. It puts the same warm weight on whatever I am, and the air is thin enough that the weight feels precise, like a hand.

If I am anywhere, I am not at the edge. Not where the world drops in a sheer, glassy break and you can see that this place was made too fast. I am inland. Far enough that the horizon is a complete circle, a low clean line in every direction. Mid-plateau. Where the basalt has had a little more time to cool, where the halite pans have grown wide enough to ring.

For a long time I think I might be the air itself — so much of what happens here is in the air. The dust in it. The salt and iron and silica riding it, too fine to see until it catches light. The charge in it, that faint metallic taste before lightning that never comes.

But I have weight. I am held. I have a foot in the hot rock and a head in that dust-soup, and between them, a long tension.

I think I am standing.

---

It takes a while to learn my own edges because I don't have proper edges. Sun falls through me. I make striped shade that isn't shade — light cut into moving lines by cables a fraction apart. If you walked under me you would feel your arm-hairs lift before you felt any coolness. That happens. The field I hold does that.

I am one of the Standing Ones.

Not the oldest — the oldest are wider at the base, thirty meters and more, their cable-trunks gone almost black with fresh precipitate. I'm narrower. My braid is still loose enough that you can see between the filaments at the bottom, see the salt flat on the other side, shivering with heat.

I go up about fifty meters before I open. That's where I become more myself. The braid loosens, the thousand wrist-thick cables separate, and they fan into a harp-head. Sixty meters across, maybe. In my crown the filaments hang nearly vertical, each fringed with soot-black hairs that never stop trembling. Not from wind. From charge.

That is my whole work. I hold a field. The warm, thin wind comes off the fracture-deserts carrying everything the young planet has not yet learned to bury — iron carbonyl, salt aerosol, sulfur, silica dust. I pull it. I don't want it the way a throat wants water. I pull it the way a charged plate pulls ground. Mineral accretes on my hairs like frost. Black to verdigris to rust-red, thickening, flaking, crusting, and then — at the very tip, where the weight is too much — splitting. Forking into two finer filaments. Millimeter by millimeter. Per year.

I lean without moving. My windward side is thicker. I hum louder before a salt-storm. That is my mind, if you need to call it that.

I do not know you are here. A filament does not need to know a visitor to keep filtering. If you touch me I will be warm and faintly oily and vibrating. I will not recoil. Nothing here has ever evolved to fear touch, because nothing here has ever been eaten. If you break one of my outer strings it will fall clean on the pan and lie there like warm wire until the wind sands it to dust and I — or my neighbor a kilometer away — pull it back out of the sky next year. Nothing is wasted. That is not virtue. There is simply no water for rot to happen in.

I have been here since the plateau cracked upward. I will be here until erosion finally learns to carve a river through me, or until the first real rain comes and dissolves the entire thought of me in an afternoon.

---

But that isn't all I am, because none of us are alone in the way oak trees are alone.

Down between my feet, in the shallow basin where my basalt holds the sun longest, is the breathing. I feel it as pressure in my cables, more than sound. 

The Moss.

Rust-red and amber, not soft at all — a tight interlocked field of ceramic bladders, each the size of a fist. For twenty minutes they inhale. *Inhale.* Hot air drawn through internal baffles, stripped of its fines. Swell, tighten, warm. Then *exhale,* a shimmer of hotter, emptied air that sighs out over my roots and ripples across the halite. One bladder is nothing. A square kilometer of them, slightly out of sync, makes the whole plateau respire under me. I stand in that slow tide. I filter what they have already pre-filtered. My hum and their breath are one system. Floor and tower. 

And farther out — I can feel it through the taproot that runs laterally under the salt — the ticking.

One Spindle Grass, solitary as we all are, six meters tall, perfectly vertical, its crown of oxidized vanes turning. *Click... click... click...* one revolution every three minutes. It does not sway. It ratchets. When a gust comes it locks. When the gust passes — *click.* It has been counting for a million years and has never needed a number. The charge it makes feeds down through its insulated root and into me. Into us. We are the grid and the factory and the power plant, but no one built us. We grew this way because dust plus charge plus basalt equals this shape, if you wait long enough.

---

So where would you find me?

Not at the dramatic edge. Find me inland, where the Lush is 60% cover and 100% empty — where a Harp Tree stands with kilometers of ringing white pan on every side, and the only other vertical is a single Spindle Grass ticking on the horizon, and at my feet the Moss inhales for twenty minutes and then lets go.

Who would I be?

Not the one who runs. Nothing here can run.

Not the one who looks back. There are no eyes here to look with.

I would be the one who waits without waiting. The installation that is also a body. The refinery that thickens on its windward side. The harp that is played by dust.

If you walked between my filaments for days in that dry, complete silence — 0.2/3, the silence of a clean-room the size of a state — you would finally hear us: breath under your boots, ticking on the horizon, and a faint, high, almost inaudible fizz of static pulling the sky onto black hairs.

That would be me, being more myself. Black cable, verdigris, rust, split, fork, repeat. Slightly thicker than a thousand years ago. Slightly taller. Filtering the same dry wind through the same patient field, until the world finally cools enough to make a river, or a rain, to ruin me completely.
