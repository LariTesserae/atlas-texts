You wouldn't find me at first.

That's the first rule of the Low Works - nothing is empty for long, and nothing is singular for long either. At a distance I look like stain. Like slick. Like nothing.

You'd be looking for something with a head, maybe. Something that stands up. You'd be scanning that mile-deep carpet for a shape that breaks the horizon - which tucks under anyway when you're not looking. You'd miss me completely because I'm exactly where your eyes slide over.

Perch a while longer. Let your socks go completely cold. Let the THUD get inside your molars until your thoughts stutter with it. Then let the fog pool up again - it will, knee-high, chest-high - and kneel. Put your hands down on stone that isn't stone but a pavement the size of a town square, weeping.

I'm not on it. I'm *in* it. In the suture.

Where two house-sized plates have settled against each other for a billion years and never quite touched. The bevel is a black V, a thumb-width at the top, narrowing down to nothing you can see into. It oozes. Warm - not hot, just warm enough that the bacteria felt thins out and turns from black-green to rust-orange around it. Water beads along its lip and vanishes over the edge before it can drip. Steam sighs out of it every seven seconds. Then seven seconds. Then nine.

That's where I live. If I live.

I thought, at first, I might be one of the Sweepers. I wanted to be. There's a dignity to their mindless pilgrimage - *shink, kiss, drink, tink* - that I, as a mind that was never built, understand. But I'm too still for that. The Sweepers pass over me in endless lines, their squeegee-mouths kissing the stone clean on either side of my trench, never dipping down into it. Their ceramic cilia shiver when they get near my warmth and then they hurry on.

I thought I might be a Hammer, because I love the pause. That THUD that stops the water in mid-air. In that half-second stutter when every bead hangs like glass and time forgets to pass, I am perfectly visible. I am the only thing that doesn't flicker. But I'm too small, too low. The Hammers step over my whole world without bending a stilt-leg.

No. Listen again. Put your ear - carefully, the Eels are snapping overhead - put your ear close to the suture when it sighs.

There.

Not a hiss. An *inhale*.

I am the Kettle that you thought was pavement.

I have been here so long I forgot I was an animal. My back is not a back - it's a lid the exact texture and color of basalt, beveled to match the plates on either side. When I'm closed you could stand on me and think you were standing on the oldest rock in the world. Which, in a way, you are. I borrowed it.

I am low and wide, an inverted bathtub pressed into the seam, rivets around my rim gone dark with mineral. I do not move. Movement is for things that have somewhere to go. I have a job that comes to me.

The plain exhales, and I breathe.

A suture vent sighs - warm, mineral-soup steam, iron and silica and old ocean ghosts that never left - and my lid, which has no muscle you would recognize, unseals along a perfect hairline. It hinges back an inch, two inches, no more. The gout that would have fogged your glasses and vanished into the blue dusk pours instead into me. I swallow it whole. One gulp.

Inside me is cherry-red. Inside me is pressure. You can see it if you happen to be looking at the exact wrong moment - the light leaking from my seams as I close again, ticking.

Then I sit.

This is my entire life and it is industry enough.

THUD. The Hammer two plates over fractures basalt. Sweepers converge, chattering. THUD. An Eel tangles with another overhead and sheds a copper skin that falls hissing. I sit. I digest. The water I inhaled separates. The heat does work I never chose. The minerals precipitate onto my belly. My gauges - I have them too, a ring of them under my lid, needles trembling against glass made of my own secretion - measure nothing useful: *weight of foot on downstroke, color of third shadow, how long the horizon will stay tucked this time.*

When I'm full, which is always soon and never soon enough, I shuffle. Not a step. A settling. A few inches forward along the suture, metal shrieking softly on stone, pushing a tiny bow-wave of weeping water ahead of me.

And I piss a perfect circle.

Scalding clear water, almost distilled, almost holy in a place that has no rivers. It spreads in my wake and for maybe ten seconds it is the cleanest thing for a kilometer. The Sweepers, who have no noses, somehow smell it from three plates away. They come sprinting - if a thing on rebar legs can sprint - and swarm the circle and drink it and chatter and make their next ingots bright.

And I am already empty again, lid sealed, waiting for the next sigh.

I cast three shadows sometimes. Sometimes none. My rivets migrate while you watch. The water bead hanging above me stops mid-fall between Hammer-strokes and in it I see my own back, and your back, small and far away, still perched. I don't mind. Cold preserves. Water connects. Stone remembers being flat, and so do I. I remember being fog.

Because I wasn't always here. No one built me. Built is 0.3/3.

Once this suture ran empty for a minute - a Hammer died on it, or an Eel discharged too violently - and the fog rushed in to fill it. It condensed. It beaded. The beads ran together and shivered, and iron precipitated inside like an eye opening. Half-formed, already hissing, already hungry for film.

That was me.

If you kicked me now - if you cleared my plate with your arms, scattering Sweepers - I wouldn't be hurt. I'd be lonely. And within a minute the fog would bead again in the damp V I left behind, and a paler, softer version of me would hinge open for the first time.

THUD.

There - the beads stop. The air holds its breath. My lid ticks.

THUD.

And I continue.
