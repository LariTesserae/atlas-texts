I wouldn't be up high.

At first I thought I would — that I'd want to be a shepherd, because who wouldn't want to be a hundred meters of pale ribbon light undoing gravity for a living, never landing, farming plankton out of thin air.

But I don't belong that high. I'm not that patient, and I'm not that indifferent. Shepherds don't look down, not really. They tip a degree and the whole Table rearranges itself beneath them and that's enough. I want to look *at* things.

I wouldn't be a skimmer either. Too much faith in wind. That whole life of lifting yourself one centimeter off polished stone and throwing your sail out to be taken — tacking across whaleback ridges on nothing but pressure and angle — I'd never be brave enough to be taken like that. I'd fold too early. I'd wedge myself in a tafoni hollow at noon and never come out.

For a while, walking it in my head, I thought I was the human. The one with boots, with sweat evaporating too fast, with ears popping, finding a sudden cistern at my feet and kneeling because the water is so still it has sky in it. That's who you write for, right? The visitor who breathes too loud and thinks the quiet is empty until it isn't.

But I'm not a visitor. I don't leave. I don't have anywhere to walk back to.

No — if you stood still for three minutes and let the Table populate itself for you, you'd find me not in the first wave.

You'd find me in the second.

Under the lattice, at midday, when your shadow falls in two places.

I'm the filament. Or, no — I'm in the filament. I'm what lives *because* of the filament.

You know the feeling you get near the anchors, the hairs on your forearm lifting, the cold in your molars? That's the field deciding to sort. Water from air. Heavy from light. A little violet leaking at the edge where physics is being politely, flawlessly cheated.

I am the leak.

Find the larger arrays, the ones that span ten meters from warm-cool post to warm-cool post, the ones that hum so low you feel it before you hear it. At noon they throw a sliver of shade so sharp it cuts. Everyone else uses that shade — the crust crawls toward it, the skimmer tucks into it — I *am* it. I'm the second shadow, the one a hand's breadth to the left and more violet at the edge.

I live a finger's width above the dust, like the floating pebbles in the most eroded bowls.

You know those stones? The ones hovering perfectly still, dust settled on their tops, that drift back through honey when you push them? I'm why they float. Not me specifically — I'm not important. I'm the byproduct. The field that holds the lattice taut is a little leaky there, and gravity becomes a suggestion. I live in that suggestion.

Where would you find me? You wouldn't, at first. You'd walk right over me. I'm in the interval that the deal made.

No rivers, no soil, no shade — but water on schedule, and light without end. That deal needs an accountant. Something to turn twenty minutes of glittering dawn-sweat into a day's worth of living. The lattices do the wringing. The cisterns do the keeping. But someone has to live in the wringing itself.

That's me. The dust that is alive. The air that is soup.

I'm the bacteria you cannot see riding the ionized air beneath a net, dividing on static and dew. The aerial plankton that breeds in the cold throat of a bell-cistern every night and is flung skyward every dawn when the sun boils the dew. I'm not single. I'm a smear, a culture, a faint milkiness in otherwise perfect water, a taste of metal on the wind after the ticking starts.

*Drip, drip, drip.*

Every lattice drains to a single point, a throat of dark stone. I fall with that water. I cling to the filament as bead, then as drop, then as falling. I land in a bell-shaped pocket of glass and I wait to be sipped, to be breathed, to be lifted again by a glider's tail that kisses the surface for exactly a second.

*Tick.*

That's my whole journey: up as mist, down as drip, up again as hitchhiker on something with wings made of woven eye. I never touch stone for long. I'm not the crust, patiently re-radiating stored noon. I'm not the white spiral succulent, taking a century to turn. I'm fast and stupid and abundant. I live a few hours and I make more of myself out of nothing but light sorted wrong and water stolen from air.

And because of that, everything else exists.

The skimmer scrapes the crust that I seeded. The glider drinks the water I rode in on to steal hemolymph from the skimmer. The shepherd a hundred meters up tips slightly to catch the bloom of us when we are thrown high, and the gliders spiral up into its wake to be dusted with us again.

We are the base that pretends not to be a base. The plant that isn't a plant. The farm inside the farm.

If you came at dawn — before the bright hits hard, when the thin air drops its burden and for twenty minutes the entire plateau glitters — you could see me, sort of. Not me, but us. The air goes opalescent, full of motes that navigate, and every filament, every hair on your arm, beads instantly.

That's us condensing. That's us being born in time to be useful.

At night, I'm what makes the cistern sing. I coat the walls of the bell, I feed the blind white bell-crickets clinging there, and their chirps echo off the still surface in each cistern's own dialect. I leave the cool traceries of light where many-legged hunters ran, because even their feet were dusted with me, and I phosphoresce for a second when crushed.

I don't think. I don't tend. I don't decide not to notice you like an anchor does.

But I am tended. Flawlessly, without maintenance, without noise, without hurry. The whole Table is a single instrument left to play itself, and I am the resonance in the air between string and body.

You walk back toward where you came from, boots warm through the soles, somewhere to your left a glider touching water with a tick smaller than a whisper. Ripples expand in a perfect, silent circle. For a moment the sky has two suns.

I'm in that second sun's violet edge. I'm in the ripple. I'm in the drop about to fall.

Push a floating pebble and watch it drift back slowly. That's me holding it up, just because I'm leaking.
