You wouldn't find me at first.

That's honest.

For a long time you would find only the proportion that is wrong — too much air for the life, too much light between leaves. I would be there the way cathedrals are occupied when no service is happening: by the height that was left empty on purpose.

If you climbed for two days through the dead granite and then turned the shoulder and saw the rim all at once — that tin-can crown sunk where a peak used to be, not on top but *instead* of top — you would say: nothing lives there. You would be right, for ten seconds. That is the first thing that lives there. The kept emptiness. The agreement between wind and steel to hold a bowl of warmer air where there should only be scouring.

Sit longer. Let your eyes adjust to the scale it was built for, which is not human-scale.

Then you would find me lower down.

Not high with the dragonflies. I'm not built for that much hover.

I am down where rust is becoming soil.

Find me on the floor-level, in the gutter where a pitted steel plate has held a century of damp and then a spoonful of granite dust a year and then one rotted leaf and then another until there is a black-brown skin over everything that smells, if you scrape it, of iron and mushrooms. I am the skin. Or I am the thing holding the skin together, which by now is the same job.

I am the vine that has learned to be one animal. Thick as a wrist at the base, thinning to rope, throwing a sheet over boiler three because boiler three sweats in the right way. I do not decide to flower from my tips. I swell where a hot-water line tells me, hot enough, here — and I open a trumpet you could put your head in and I keep it open for nine days, breathing perfume exactly to the machine's sigh. I have no roots in the mountain. I root in the machine's old age, in the pitting, in the compost of my own last year. I keep a seal from cracking by growing through it. I keep a pipe from freezing by leaning on it.

If not the vine, then what moves on it.

I am the beetle you didn't notice at first because I move at machine-speed. Oil-green, pitted like old copper with seawater on it, I take ten seconds per step on the handrail, lower my head, scrape the algal felt off the condenser, leave a brief bright line behind me that will dull again by tomorrow. A week's grazing to clean a pipe. I am necessary the way a tongue is necessary.

Above me, I am the midges too — the gnats that know how to hang absolutely still in warm rising air, motes in amber in a column of normal afternoon light, only shifting when the big shadow passes — and then I am the big shadow in turn:

wing tip to wing tip taller than you, four gauze wings edged in rust-vein, body long as a canoe paddle, beating once per second, *whup*, a second papery thrum under the basso roar. I own the cube of air between boiler three and the east wall. I have timed the flywheel. I turn without banking hard. I look down with a head that is mostly eye at the hummers below me and at the moths blundering heavy with grey pollen on their legs, moths the size of your hand with false rivet eyes, and I do not hurry. Nothing here can afford to hurry.

And the hummers — if I get to choose a small warm body — I want to be the hummer.

Not a bird. Not a bee. Thumb-sized, round, fur that is not fur but branched setae the color of old pollen, two pairs of wings blurred out, tongue longer than I am. I hang in front of the vine-trumpet where heat radiates visibly, and I shiver, and I drink, and I carry the dust that will let a new vine seed in a new crack where no vine has yet learned to grip. I sleep tucked under a leaf with my hum stopped, which is how you finally find me. Twenty of us, each with a cluster of blooms to tend, which is enough.

But even that is too countable.

What I really am, if you stay by that ridiculous little river that goes in a circle — thirty feet long, a foot deep, brown, slow, watercress choked, lily pads no bigger than coins holding perfect circles of sky-rain, lilies still turning to the west gap where the real sun comes in — in the only place where the bulk of the Machine blocks the direct roar and lets you hear water ticking — is the circle itself.

I am the water taking an hour to come back to where it started. I am the condensation forming on the underside of every beam, fattening, falling without clouds. I am the intermittent rain of a place that makes its own weather. I am the heat-pooling. I am the cold-pipe weeping. I am the slow piston taking a minute to exhale, the flywheel you can count rivets on, the chain advancing link by link, electrified, pressurized, thrumming in your molars, and the moss mattress a foot thick that keeps it from shaking itself apart.

I am not wild and not tame. I am maintained.

You can still find the molt of the third thing that lived here, up the grated stair that the vines politely left half-clear. Two seats. Bakelite dials still twitching with vibration. A lever with a smooth wooden knob still gripped by a living bracelet of stem that thickened exactly where fingers squeezed daily. Under plastic weighted with a stone: *Adjusted timing on exchanger 3 by 2%. Condensation up 4%. Growth nominal.* No distress. Just someone who came down off the roof when the shift ended, and after that root pushing a seal shut instead of rubber replaced, beetle scraping pipe instead of brush.

I was briefly that, too. The hand that adjusted. The lighthouse keeper in a light that did not need to be beautiful, only to be adjusted. And then I left, because keeping left.

Now I keep without intention.

Rim keeping wind out. Stone keeping rim up. Heart-machine keeping air warm and wet. Leaf-mat keeping heart from cracking. Beetle keeping leaf from rotting too quick. Dragonfly keeping beetle and moth from overtaking. Hummer keeping vine open to new cracks.

Each needs so much empty space around it to exist that we looked lonely until you realized loneliness was the design. Low density is not failure here; it is how you keep the narrow condition from fouling.

Outside, the ancient weathered peaks go on forever, cold and barren and nothing to prove.

Inside — find me there, stubborn, warm, damp, deafening — it is always, it is still, spring.
