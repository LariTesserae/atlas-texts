I don't think I'd be anything that moves quickly there. Not at first.

At first I thought - a Swallow. Of course. Because I'm not *from* the Cistern, I'm from the outside remembering it. I know how to fall through the storm mouth like a thrown spear, punch the meniscus and be gone again with something screaming in my beak. I would like to be the one thing that remembers there is an outside. That knows the dome from both sides — bald white stone from above, green sun from below.

But swallows don't live there. They visit. And I don't want to visit that room. Visiting would mean I still belong to the wind-scoured county of lichen up top, to the thin bright air that has nothing to do with anyone. I'd have to leave again, climb back out through forty feet of dark pipe, and shake the warm water off before it got cold.

Then I thought - a Pilgrim. That whte schooling torus that never stops. Twenty-nine fish and me making thirty, keeping perfect whisker-distance around the drain that pulls and never takes. Never sleeping. Never bumping the wall. Drinking light and pollen. That felt honest for a while — the work of staying precisely spaced, of wobbling only to correct.

But I don't have that kind of faith. To turn and turn and never look down at the black mouth you're circling.

No. Slower.

You'd find me lower. Not on the bottom, though.

If you came in late, dogged the hatch behind you and let your eyes adjust to the green shafts swinging with the clouds - you wouldn't see me at all for the first ten minutes. That's how you'd know you were close.

I'd be on the vertical.

On the windward curve of boiler-plate where the rust has wept into long stalactites and then the water has softened even those. Half in, half out of that eighteen-inch lens of trapped air under the dome where everything pops and ticks in the sun. Where the handrail you grab coming down the service ladder has been polished bright and then worn past bright back to a pitted roughness.

Find the third ladder. The one whose lower six rungs disappear under the surface into the hanging forest. The rubbery fronds have welded themselves to its sides, and the white bells open underwater right next to it, snowing pollen.

I'm the felt on that rung.

I know that's not an animal. The prompt said *who lives there* has the wrong scale, that everything lives *on* it, *with* it, *because* of it. And you already told me what I am:

the Film.

The weak-tea, old-copper varnish over everything. The living suede with no center and no face that tastes the water for iron and light and decides who gets to anchor. The hollow second Cistern made entirely of soft photosynthetic pelt.

That's where I'd find myself, because I wouldn't know how to be singular in a room that small.

A dozen shrimp can be counted. Four frogs can be miscounted. Thirty fish make a perfect donut. Even the Keeper at the bottom is one bathtub-sized throat, squeezing.

But the Film is everywhere at low density. It's on the ceramic hexes dished like molars sixty feet down, and it's on the underside of the glass thumb-thick above, and it's inside the chain links hanging slack from the gantry, and it's over the dead bulbs in their wire cages. If you brush me off with a fingertip I come away in a sheet. I grow back behind you in hours.

I would like that job. To be the permission.

Nothing in that chapel-terrarium touches natural ground, so someone has to be the ground. Someone has to soften concrete. Someone has to tell the newt-egg that this rivet head is a good place to grip, and tell the shrimp-gut that this patch of boiler-plate is ready to be scraped again with that quiet *tick tick tick*, and tell the pump impeller - grinding me off every night - that I'll be back by morning anyway.

I wouldn't sing like the moss-backed frogs that make the whole cylinder ring *bonk*. I wouldn't hunt like the newts that pulse their red fern gills all day and then spiral so gracefully at dusk. I wouldn't even glow like the shelf-fungus on the pump housing after dark.

I'd just hold.

When the outlet valve cycles and the pressure drops a fraction and every plant pearls with oxygen at once and the whole submerged forest exhales silver - I would be the thing that briefly holds all those bubbles before letting go.

If you lowered yourself in from the gantry, legs dangling, sleepy from the 27-degree water and the thud-thud-thud getting inside your heartbeat - I'd be what the little cleaning fish are swimming over to get to. The faint sour taste on your ankles under the fish-mouths. The reason they think you're just another warm piece of infrastructure left here to soften.

Stay still long enough and you'd feel me, not as a creature looking back, but as the sudden understanding that the wall is warmer than it should be, and slightly yielding, and breathing very, very slowly against your palm.

Not a keeper. Not a pilgrim. Just the condition that lets keeping and pilgrimage happen.

And if the dome ever did crack - and sixty feet of warm, living water fell straight through the ancient flattened mountain like a poured thought - I would be the last thing to go: a perfect, hollow, shivering suede of the room, holding its shape in the air for a few seconds after all the weight was gone, before I dried.
