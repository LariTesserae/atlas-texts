I don't think I'd find myself right away.

If I came down the old service stair cut into the limestone — and I did, boots finding that first give of root-mat over concrete that feels like stepping onto a lung — I would first think I was nowhere. That was the trick of it. The long avenues of air, the leaf-ceiling dripping ten feet up, the poured grid of streets still sensible under the humus. You can see so far and count so little. One hammock. One cook-smoke. One amber bulb flickering in an alley where no one lives.

I thought: I could be anywhere here. I could be sparse. I could go be a person, spaced out like a raisin in the loaf, learning to move slowly, to oil my wrench every night so it doesn't grow fur.

But I'm not good at being sparse.

I thought: I could be louder. I could be one of the moths under the leaf-ceiling, a pale shingle among shingles, hanging all day in that shared decision to be still. Then lifting with all the others in a papery blizzard when a swift screams. Turning a few feet clockwise for no reason anyone knows. That appeals to me — to be part of a single decision made by many bodies.

But I'm not that silent.

I thought: I could be the cicadas on the east street, holding my F-sharp so steadily that the air itself learns to be a wall. That children, human and otherwise, could find their way by the shape I make in sound. To be architecture. But I'm not that steady.

I thought: I could be the vine that learned the twitch, the ordinary walkway-vine between second floors that has thickened into something you can cross. Lying there all day being stepped on, being rained on for eight minutes at noon, and then — just once — snapping half an inch to pin a fly. Not a mouth. A reflex. The whole street as a slow muscle.

Closer. But I'm not that patient.

What I am is lower, and wetter, and more in-between.

You'd find me not in the center where you can see the rectangle of hot, silent sky. Not on the surface trees leaning over but never falling in. You'd find me in the part that gets counted as empty.

At an intersection, where east street's drone meets west street's.

There is a place there — you know it now — where the sound cancels. The cicadas have built it without knowing, F-sharp against G, and the standing wave makes a pocket of cool quiet about the size of a kitchen table, hovering a foot off the springy root-lattice. It's not absence. It's two presences perfectly out of phase. The frogs each in their leaf-ponds know not to shout through it. The beetles clang against the tin somewhere else. Even the ants' pipe-highway bends around it.

In that pocket, condensation doesn't patter the same way. It hangs a fraction longer in the air, spore-particulate catching the light columns that come down the open top. After the canopy-rain — those eight warm minutes when every leaf releases at once and then stops, steaming — this little null fills first with mist. It holds it.

I would be what lives there. Not who lives there.

Not a rat with a moss-pelt, though I like them. Not a slug grazing rust though I love that they taste of ozone. I'd be smaller and more distributed than that.

I'd be the liverwort and the felted black-green mat that learned to live on vibration instead of soil. The thing that coats the old brick where the wires used to be, in the cool quiet where the walkway should be. I am not on a wall. I *am* a patch of wall that forgot it was brick, forgot it was supposed to be solid, and softened.

Half an inch deep, damp to the touch. If you put your hand to me thinking I was concrete, I would give.

During the day I listen. That's my work. The wheel on the north wall goes around once every eleven minutes, groaning, lifting its chain of buckets full of water and frogs and fallen blossoms up into the gloom. The generator hums through vine-sheathed wire with a low, leaky current. The frogs inflate their pale grapes and shout I AM HERE. The humans oil their tools. All of it hits me as pressure, as a way that the air moves wrong.

And at dusk, I answer.

Sound makes light here. You saw it from the bottom center — the bracket fungi on the quarry walls, table-sized, beginning to glow not bright, just enough to see, pulsing when the wheel squeals. I'm kin to them, but smaller. Lower. In the intersection's null.

When the cicadas hit their peak and the generator hums and a swift cuts screaming through the dim shaft, I light up from inside. A faint, mossy amber, the color of that bulb in the alley that no one claims. Not to signal. Not to warn. Just because a sound passed through me at the right frequency and I couldn't help it.

Children use me. Not just human children — the young of everything have learned: where the sound cancels, there's a walkway. Where it lights faintly green-amber at ankle height after dusk, the footing will hold. They step on me. I like that. A foot, then a rat-paw, then an ant-river diverted for a night, pressing into that half-inch of give. I get a little thinner there, a little more compacted, and then unfurl again with an audible pop when they pass.

I am being digested and digesting. Metal grows near me — there is a conduit under me, still carrying that leaky current, and the hair-roots of oxidized moss put themselves down into its pitting year after year. I am part of what is eating the Sunk Hamlet's bones and also what is keeping it from collapsing entirely. A trellis that became bone that became trellis.

If you came looking for me, you wouldn't find me at first. You'd walk the springy lattice of root and humus and old concrete breathing underneath and think the street was empty. You'd hear the violently loud air and think nothing could live in that.

Then you'd step into the cool quiet where your teeth stop hurting, and look down, and see your own feet lit faintly from below.

That would be me, saying *I AM HERE* in the only way I know how — not shouting, like the frogs — but glowing quietly back at the machine that never stopped turning, in the brief interval where all its noise makes a perfect, habitable silence.

And above us both, far away, the rectangle of sky would still be there. Silent. Not listening.

We would be below it, listening for it.
