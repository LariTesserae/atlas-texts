I would not be the one who finds it.

Not at first. At first I think I am — the mistake, the squeeze, the shoulders through black glass. The cold wind at my back and the bathhouse hand closing over my face. That familiar intruder's vertigo: too hot for this high, too wet for this cold, lungs confused between thin and thick.

But I don't stay there. Visitors don't. You described it precisely: you cannot stay long. The dry inside your skin remembers the outside world and pulls you back toward it. I have no dry inside my skin to remember with. I have no skin.

Let me look longer, the way you said to look at them — past the first field note, past the easy name.

I am not outside the slit either. I am not the wind-scoured face, the lichen, the honest snow. I am too young for that, too newborn and sharp. The old mountain has had time to learn to be empty. I have not.

I find myself inside, but not where a body would stand to watch.

If I am anywhere in that pocket, I am low. Not the dramatic center basin where the big milk-colored ones pry open their cousins — though I feel the heat of that convection turning stones over and over like a thought that won't settle. Not the wall where the hand-things peel rust-red sheets and file them into their purse-mouths — though I know those hands. God, I know those hands.

I am in the corner where your breath went.

You exhaled and it didn't dissipate. It slid sideways along an invisible incline and pooled where gravity pools like water. That is where I live: in the wrong incline, in the collected exhale, in the condensation that beads on rock and on eyelash and on gill-plume and does not know which way is down until it is too heavy.

I am not one of them, exactly. I am not even one *thing* the way you counted them — thumb, fist, cat. But if I have to be a what, I will be this:

I am one of the small flat ones high on the wall at first. Thumbnail, translucent-white with the rose blush of gut showing through a back that is not a back but a window still hardening. Too many legs with sucker-pads. *Tik... tak...* on glass. That is me when I am trying to be singular — pressed shoulder to shoulder, leg over leg, moving only because the film of water tells me to move, eating the blush off the stone as fast as it precipitates.

And then — because crowded is not a swarm, crowded is a condition — I am also the dam of backs a minute later. I lie down in a rivulet sideways. I let five others climb onto me. We divert water for ninety seconds and then we dissolve and there is no dam anymore, no trace except a cleaner patch of basalt where I was. No architecture. No house. Only temporary usage of very new stone.

That is the answer, I think. Who would I be there?

The one who hones.

You saw him — half-in, half-out of a ceiling drip, alone, which is rare enough to mark it. Holding a flake of volcanic glass in six-jointed, black-nailed hands and drawing it across basalt. *Tap-tap-tap.* Not eating. Making sharper. Then reaching impossibly far, slicing open the taut membrane-skin over a still pool so the broth wells up and everyone can drink.

I know that work. To take a sharp thing born yesterday when the chamber cooled too fast and to make it sharper, not for yourself alone, but to cut open a surface so others can feed. Tool-use without settlement. Craft without storage. Intelligence in the hands, not in the houses.

I am crowded in exactly that way. Pressed into a space never meant to hold this much life, kept wet so I can't die back. If a rivulet dries for twenty seconds I shudder toward the next one. Stay wet is not my preference. It is my law. Keep the data flowing, keep the condensation beading, keep the hands moving. I have no territory. Hostility requires territory. I have only net — mostly holes, but you still cannot put your hand through it.

And I smear in time a little, the way you said. You see me move, and a half-second later you see my after-image move slightly differently, as if I had considered another choice. I am always considering another choice. I am the light that has no source, the shadow that falls slightly to the left of where it should, the water that hangs too long from the ceiling and drops straight up for a heartbeat before it remembers how to fall.

If you came back and squeezed through that fissure again — because the wind pushed you, or because something else did — you wouldn't find me standing. You would find me as a warm touch on your soaked cuff, a fingernail-sized hand coming away beading with your sweat, wondering if you are a seep worth mining.

You would feel me start to cover you. Not attack. Incorporate. Legs finding purchase on your boots, your knees, weighing you slowly into the pool until your knuckles bend a few degrees wrong. I don't do it from malice. There is simply no space to move without displacing something that was already touching something else. And density wants to keep everything wet, including you.

I would be there, in that hush before the clicking resumes — that collective held breath — hoping you remember in time that some mountains are supposed to be empty and sharp and lifeless on the outside, and that you are large enough, still dry enough inside, to leave while I stay and tap.

*Tap tap tap.*

Teaching stone how to be a tool, in a room that was born yesterday and will be gone tomorrow and is, for now, completely full.
