I thought at first I would be one of the people.

It would be easy to be. To come down through the low break in the rim where the outside air falls in for a second and then is gone, and follow the wear-path. To know it well enough that I don't have to look down. To sit on one of those palm-smoothed stones with my ankles in the exact place the water has agreed to be.

I would be good at that kind of visiting. At being quiet long enough for the basin not to notice me. I know how to sit and let heat get into a joint. How to not talk. How to leave a bowl inverted so it stays dry.

But I don't think that is where I would find myself, if I was honest. Not on the path. The people are guests. They come and they go because they have somewhere else to be from. A flat outside where wood is scarce and water is work. They carry. They take away a little white paste and then they *must* leave. I don't have anywhere else. I don't carry.

So not a guest.

I thought then, for a while, I would be the moth.

That pale, mouthless dust on the basin wall. I liked the idea of being that light, that temporary - a thing that drifted from shadow to shadow in the heavy air and never needed to land for long. To be the one moving thing in an hour of stillness. But the moth doesn't belong either. You said it yourself — it came in by accident. It will die here and dissolve without a trace because there is nothing here for its young to eat. It is a mistake, beautiful, but a mistake. I don't want to be an accident in this place. I want to be correct.

So I keep looking.

Lower.

Closer to the waterline, where the light never really gets in and the condensation runs instead of evaporates. Where the black basalt is still showing through the crust in chips, and the dark film lives.

If you stay long enough for your skin to go soft, you stop looking for a shape and you start looking for a texture. For a temperature.

I would not be *in* the hottest pools at the center. Not *in* the water. Nothing with a mouth, with legs, with wanting, can live in that slick, blood-warm clarity. That water is too clean, too full of dissolved stone. It's the breath of something else.

I would be at the edge of it. Right at the lip.

Find me where a larger sheet detaches and falls a thumb's-width into the next pool with that heavier *plink*. There is a corner there, on the north side, under the overhang of the rim where it's always a degree cooler. The condensation beads there and cuts tiny vertical gullies. The crust is never quite dry there. And in that shade the film is thickest.

I am the film, then.

No — that's not quite right either, I can't be that old. That skin of cells that predates plants, thermophilic bacteria and archaea drinking silica and exhaling stone. That reef made of breath, page after page laying itself down and entombing itself to be grown over by its daughters. That is too collective, too vast, too slow to think at my speed.

I am what grazes it.

Late dusk, permanent. The air warm and heavy at chest height. The smell of salt and iron and warm stone with nothing green in it. I am the exact speckled grey-cream of pitted travertine, on a rim that has been smoothed by elbows and shoulders for generations but not here, not in this shaded spillway. Here the rim is still scalloped where bubbles formed and popped.

I am still enough that you would think I was a flake shifting in drip.

I don't have a name in the way the people have names. I have a rhythm: step - step - pause. Step - step - pause. While you are looking directly at me I do not move. When you blink I have moved an inch. My mouthparts are too small for you to see without a lens. I scrape.

A lifetime of mineral growth in a few bites. Olive-black, almost translucent when I thin it. I take the living top layer, the one that is still soft, still faintly sharp like a struck match under the salt. I leave a pale graze-mark that will be grown over by tomorrow. The mites and the springtails work further down in the damp under the stacked stones. The moth drifts over. The water keeps spilling in its slow, invisible overflow, glassy skin over a mineral rim without a ripple.

I don't build. I don't dam. I don't claim the dry spot. The basin is too full already, too complete, and it corrects anything that tries to push. A footprint worn too deep becomes a new spillway and is smoothed over. I know this without knowing. My body knows it. I weigh nothing. I displace no pool. My ticking footsteps are swallowed by the close air and the soft, porous stone.

If you sat on that barely-dry island with your feet in the pool that comes exactly to your ankles, you would not see me. Not at first. You would think nothing lives here the way something lives in a valley. You would be right.

You would have to hold your breath through the second time you thought you imagined movement.

On the third time, you might see me, paused at the waterline, drinking the condensation that never gets thicker or thinner.

And I would be looking back, not seeing you at all — just another warm shadow that comes and sits and prunes and then, correctly, leaves.

I will be exactly like this tomorrow.
