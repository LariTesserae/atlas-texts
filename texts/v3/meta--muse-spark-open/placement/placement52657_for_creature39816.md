You wouldn't be on the Earth at all.

That is the first turn the image has to make before it becomes honest. You stand on that swaying gantry for a while, feeling the thrum in your teeth, watching the amber bulbs go cloudy with moths that live an hour, watching the houses unbolt themselves and walk two bridges over, and slowly the scale inverts.

Because you have been here before. Every human body is a White Horn range.

Think of a single villus in the small intestine. Or the papilla on your tongue. Or the callused ridge of a fingertip, if you want to stay true to how dry it feels. Or, if you want to go deeper, a single mitochondrion in a muscle cell, folded on itself. The principle is the same at every magnification: wherever the world becomes extreme - too thin, too dry, too exposed - life stops spreading out and learns to fold *in*.

The mountain is living tissue. That pale, thin-aired summit is any epithelium thrust up into a current it cannot afford to be scoured by - the tip of the villus in the gut stream, the crest of skin at the wind. The fissure is what the mountaineering books don't draw: the crypt, the crevice, the sulcus between folds. Thirty meters deep, three meters wide, narrowing to a seam of sky - that is the exact proportion of an intestinal crypt, of a molar fissure, of a crista pressed into the mitochondrial matrix. From five meters away you wouldn't see it. From the bloodstream, from the mouth, from the light microscope at low power, you wouldn't see it. The walls look like country.

And the walls *are* the terrain. That glossy, soot-black basalt with its conchoidal fracture, veined iron-red and sulfur-yellow - that's keratin in the skin, enameled hydroxyapatite in a tooth, the phospholipid bilayer itself, fresh enough to cut. Put it under a lens and it is still sharp. It is folded into teeth and knives because it has not had time to weather, because the body makes it new faster than the world can smooth it. No one levels it. You anchor *into* it. Every integrin, every desmosome, every piton the size of your arm - is a membrane protein hammered straight into the rock, holding up a house that is one room deep and two rooms high. One cell deep, two cells high. Wasp cells. Honeycomb.

Stand there long enough and you stop seeing a city and you see what it is: a stratified reef turned on its side.

The Great Furnace set into the eastern wall like an organ pipe - vertical, never allowed out because *if it goes out everything dies* - is not a metaphor. It is a capillary loop in the dermis bringing blood-heat up into the crack. It is the black smoker chimney if you read it as ocean floor. It is Complex I-IV of the electron transport chain if you read it as a cell, chewing powdered basalt and thin air - substrate and oxygen - spitting slag and heat and a trickle of electricity that lights every amber bulb. That low bone-deep thrum you feel before you hear anything is oxidative phosphorylation. It is the proton gradient. It is your heart, two organs away.

The belt drive that runs the whole length of the fissure, the single old mining cable that every lathe taps with a leather belt - that is the cytoskeleton. Those are microtubules. The cold exhaust and iron filings in the haze are iron-sulfur clusters, reactive oxygen, mineral dust from a biology that never learned to be clean.

It is 4°C, mineral-cool, peelingly dry because it *has* to be. The crypt, the fissure, the skin crack - the body keeps its interfaces dry the way a desert town keeps its cistern sealed: *no open containers, no steam without a hood, spill nothing.* You do not pour here. You decant with syringes. The great black aerogel nets the Net-Tenders throw across the seam at sundown are goblet cells spinning mucus, are sebaceous sheets, are ADP/ATP translocases catching what little falls from the sky. That faint, kerosene-smelling hydrocarbon snow sifting down from the stratosphere - that is marine snow, that is chyme passing over the crypt mouth, that is sebum and sweat condensing, that is glucose and fatty acids drifting down from the lumen. There is no river here. Nothing sweats out of the rock. You live on what the air reluctantly gives you, boiled in a sealed condenser, rationed by the cup.

And of course there are no plants. There has never been photosynthesis in a molar fissure, in a skin crack, in a mitochondrion, in a hydrothermal vent. The whole economy skips green and goes straight to eating heat, eating dust, eating each other, eating waste.

This is where you finally place yourself.

### If you are human, you are the scaffolding.

The People are not the city. There are only three hundred of them at high season, barrel-chested because the air is thin, lips white with salt-scar because the water is rationed to the eyes. You would know them by their stillness. They blink slowly. They hum instead of shouting because thin air won't carry a shout. After twenty years their lungs are the color of the wall, soot-black with iron flecks.

You would be one of them depending on which shift you pull:

**A Stoker,** high near the seam, tending the Kiln/Capillary/ATP-synthase that was there before you, listening for the wrong thrum in your molars. Your liturgy is: keep it lit.

**A Net-Tender,** working in darkness, climbing hand over hand to scrape frost with a bone knife into sealed kettles - a goblet cell, a chief cell, any secretory cell whose whole job is to harvest the night's fall without letting a drop evaporate.

**A Salvor -** which is to say, everyone. Because the mountain is still young. It pushes up a millimeter a year - skin grows, villi slough, enamel microfractures, membranes flow - and a *ping* of a popping rivet means your house is now on the wrong scar. You unbolt your tin wall and carry it two bridges over. Here, houses are verbs. Here, progress is not building up. It is staying even. Those are your stem cells, your keratinocytes, your chaperones, endlessly re-tensioning the belt.

You are not the biomass. You are the trellis the biomass grows on.

### If you are alive, you are the Animals.

The reason the light is dim even at noon is not shadow. It is density.

**The Doves** pressed to every warm pipe, pale translucent gray, twice-feathered, drinking warmth through their feet - those are *Staphylococcus* and *Streptococcus* clusters, the cocci in the warmest, most oxygenated layer, so still you think they are lagging until the pipe cycles and they shuffle six inches left in unison. Their keratin dust feeds everything below.

**The Skaters,** flat six-legged iron-red ghosts with heat-exchanger bellies, ticking across the glass only visible when they move - the corynebacteria and *Bacteroides* grazing the biofilm, the limpets on the vent wall, the scavenger proteins grazing the bacterial lawn. Let one walk across your hand to warm it.

**The Glass-Moths,** the plankton of the fissure, ash-flake mica wings that live an hour, hatch, swarm, die on the amber bulbs - that is the true cloud: mRNA, ATP molecules, transient yeasts, planktonic bacteria, marine snow itself, so thick you breathe them. The children sweep them with oiled trays because a handful renders a thimble of clear oil, a mouthful of protein. Nothing wasted.

**The Cable-Rats,** blind, whiskered longer than their bodies, never touching stone, born and dying on the wires in endless counter-flowing lines, gnawing mineral crust off insulation - those are kinesin and dynein, those are spirochetes, those are neutrophils on patrol, running the cables that are microtubules and collagen and power lines, turning crust into paste-bricks.

**The Builders,** down in the hot V where every wall meets, thumb-long wasps with iron-swollen abdomens, rasping paper - *Cutibacterium*, *Bacillus subtilis*, vent tubeworms, matrix-builders. They chew rat paste and dove dust and dead moths and slag and mix it with paper from their own wings - extracellular polymeric substance, peptidoglycan, silk spit that etches glass - and build hexagonal nests the exact size and shape of your house, hung from silk chains. Tear it down and they build it a foot higher where it is a tenth of a degree warmer. You learned how to hang your house from *them*.

Droppings become paste become paper become insulation become droppings. Furnace heat grows Dove, Dove dust feeds Skater and Moth, Moth oil runs the lamp that draws more Moth, Rat cleans wire and feeds Wasp, Wasp paper insulates Furnace house and becomes Dove nest.

A closed, frantic loop. A climax community at extreme compression.

So where would you find yourself?

Not on the White Horn. *In* it. In the crypt between two villi while chyme eclipses the sky for an hour at noon. In the fissure of your own molar where the toothbrush never reaches and the biofilm has built a bazaar. In the crack of winter skin on your knuckle where staphylococci huddle on warm pipes. In the crista of a mitochondrion in your calf as you read this, where the membrane folds black and sharp and the Furnace thrums.

Who would you be?

If you feel responsible, you are a Stoker, a goblet cell, a mitochondrion, keeping the kiln lit.

If you feel restless, you are a cable-rat motor protein, a neutrophil, endlessly patrolling a wire you will never leave.

If you feel patient and strange and quietly industrious, you are a builder-wasp, laying down paper and iron one hexagonal cell at a time, making the very wall you live on.

I, if I get a choice, would be the builder-wasp. And also the thrum in the stone that tells me I'm still alive.
