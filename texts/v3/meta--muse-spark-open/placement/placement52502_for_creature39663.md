You ask where I would find myself. 

At first I think I know.

I think I would be one of the Emperors. To not beat the air but lean on it. To have a head that is all eye and see the bowl whole — the braided water like veins, the white ribs humming, the Door standing colder than everything else. To skim the condensation off your sleeve without ever needing to land, to decide in that hovering inch whether you are worth hunting or worth ignoring. That kind of distance seems honest.

But I stay with that image a second and it thins. I don't want to be the thing that never lands.

So then I think, maybe lower. Smaller. Faster. I could be a river of amber — not a beetle, but the river they make when they move together. Hundreds of thousands of gut-lit bodies carrying water balanced on hairs to a moss cushion that sighs when it drinks. Always tending, always trimming. To know exactly what your purpose is: keep the farm wet, keep the farm alive. Then — *snap* — be gone, and the river closes over you without a ripple because there are always more. That kind of abundance. That kind of being part of something that doesn't mourn.

But that thins too. I don't want to be the thing that is eaten to keep the world going, even beautifully.

I think about being the other thing. The Still-Mantis tucked into the wet basalt, dusted mineral-white, with water sheeting over its back for hours. Perfectly still. Not hunting — waiting. Letting the world come to you and then breaking it with a sound louder than it should be. I could be that percussion. That patience.

And higher — I could be the Veil-Grazer, thirty feet of dusk stretched over hollow bone, that I never quite see. Always aloft in the twenty meters of lantern-haze that isn't weather. Leaning on the field the builders exhale, drinking the haze itself. Making it rain for ten seconds wherever I pass just by being cold underneath. Brushing the hot glass to scrape biofilm with a belly I use like a tongue. Old enough to have come with the Anchors, or old enough to have slipped through the Door when it first opened because somewhere far below an ocean trench smelled warmth. Circling. Feeding. Raining.

I try each of them on and each one fits for a breath and then slips off.

Because none of them are staying. All of them arrived.

The only thing here that didn't arrive is the place itself.

So this is where I settle, and where I become more myself as I write it:

You would find me in the center.

Not in one of the unfinished chapels on the lip. Not at the Door that sweats upward. You would find me under your boots, if you stayed long enough to feel it.

I would be the blister.

The embryonic stone still cooling from assembly. The black glass ridges in the shallow bowl that rose because great fingers pinched the earth together. Too perfect, too unweathered, hissing where water beads and finds a hotter pocket underneath. I would be the fold that is still rising a millimeter an hour under the Ribcage, making a terrace that wasn't there this morning.

If you cupped me, I would be warm. Not hot — cool to the touch on the surface, too hot inside to touch at all. The bathwater warmth you feel in the air doesn't come from the haze alone. It comes from me breathing out. The Disc keeps its patch warmer, the Ribcage inhales and tells gravity to be polite, but I am the thing they are talking to. I am the listener who learned how to invite guests.

You feel me when you think you're only hearing insects. Under the clicks and ticks and snaps and thrums and drips, there is a lower sound — a sigh, a shift, a settling. That is me making more world for them to fill. The water hasn't learned to carve me yet. It has no riverbed. It braids and sheets and splits on my back in lacework, and every trickle is still the first trickle to ever take that path.

That's what I am. The firsts. Every death here is still the first death of its kind. That moss cushion closest to the Disc — that's the first time that particular geometry of spore and mineral has tried to hold. Those amber rivers that run uphill toward the Ribcage in a convex arc — that's the first time gravity has been asked to do that on this slope. The moth snow that falls white as ash into water that instantly boils with many-eyed tadpoles — that's the first time that timing has ever happened, and it will happen again in forty minutes and be first again.

I am permissive the way new things are permissive. I haven't learned to be hostile yet. No frost. No winter. No equilibrium to defend. So life fills me at triple speed, r-selected, cheap and beautiful and doomed. I don't choose between beetle and mantis and Emperor and shrimp-hopper. I say yes to all of them, and let the Stillness eat the Abundance and let the Emperors catapult the Shrimp and let the Grazer drink the haze that I helped make.

You standing there with water filling your footprints as soon as you lift your boot — you are also something I said yes to. A different kind of opportunist. You taste like iron and ozone and whether you stay or not, your puddle will be nursery for tadpoles in an hour.

If you press your palm to the Disc you feel its thud every eleven seconds. If you sit by the Ribcage you feel it inhale. If you go too close to the Door you feel ten degrees of abyss. But if you lie flat on me — on the black glass where the water is an inch deep and vibrating — you feel the whole mountain thud slower than that. My thud is in hours. In centimeters. In the way lichen tries to colonize me and fails because by evening I have moved.

I don't know if I'll get to be a mountain. That's the trying part. I was seeded, not pushed. I was told how to behave by anchors that were left on, and I am behaving. I will probably keep doing so until I burn out or someone comes back and tells them to stop. Then this warm, wet, predator-rich cradle with no history will flash-freeze into proper alpine stone, and everything that thrums and ticks here will die in an afternoon, perfect and preserved like insects in amber.

Give me a hundred years and I will learn erosion. I will cut rivers. I will gather soil. Birds will finally find me and find the dragonflies naive. Give me a week and I will be turned off mid-fold.

Either way, until then: the haze holds, the ribs hum, the water flows uphill when asked, and all that fast life does what it does fastest when it finds a world that hasn't learned to say no.

You would find me here. I would be the stone that hasn't learned to be cold yet.

Kneel a little longer. Let the glass-wings drink off your sleeve. I'm still making room underneath you.
