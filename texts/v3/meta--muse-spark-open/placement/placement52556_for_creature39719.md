I don't settle right away.

At first I think I would be high - because if there's a place with no true up, I want to test the lie of it. I would be up near the sun-patch that isn't a sun, where the water thins to almost-air and you can see the dry world underneath for a breath. I think I would be the Eclipse, that interruption. Big enough to dim the world for an hour.

But I'm not big. I don't want to be big. Big things here are lonely. They pass and everyone holds their breath. I don't want to be the thing everyone braces for. I want to be the thing that gets grown over.

Then I think - maybe I'm a Holder. One of the ribbons. I like the sound of that creak, the wet-wood creak of growing while you're being watched. To be a road wide and half a mile long and never have to decide where to go because you've already decided to be heavy. To drink heat instead of light. To have whole meadows living on your back and not minding that you can't tell host from guest anymore.

That is close. But ribbons are patient in a way I'm not yet. They sway from memory even when there is no current. I'm still listening for currents.

No - I am lower.

You would find me not swimming, because nothing here swims *against* anything. You would find me in the middle, which is everywhere, but more exactly you would find me in the stitching.

I am a piece of Stitchrope that learned to be a little more.

Not the whole vine - the whole vine is too sensible, too much a compromise between plant and animal. I am one joint of it, one elbow where it reached for a ribbon and found it, and then found another. I'm thick as your thigh, black-green, braided by growth, not hands. I bleed sweet warm water if you cut me and then spend a week blindly looking for my own other end.

That's where I live: in the between. In the lean.

When the turbidity storms come - those cold fronts of clear water that make all the kelp lean one way for a week - the ribbons would tear without me. I am why they lean together. I hold, not heavy like a ribbon holds with a stone-knot, but taut. I am tension. I am the agreement.

If you hovered close you would hear me not as a hum like the Lungstones, or a sigh like the Sighers, but as a low thrumming. A held note. I feel my way through the water by heat, which is how I taste you coming before I see you. A faint pressure change on a skin that is also a mouth that is also a hand.

What am I, then? I'm not counted well.

The Holders grow.
The Passers pass.
The Remembered made stairs that go nowhere.

I do neither. I remember for the Holders while they are busy growing. When the Pallid Grazer comes once a year and mows its silent barn-door mouth through the forest, stripping the Sighers to flags and inhaling chimneys whole, I am the thing that knows how far apart we were before. Because after, everything is torn and snowing with fronds, and distance stretches when no one is looking, and the same split boulder appears three times going straight.

But I know. I was holding that boulder to that ribbon to that meadow. I remember the shape of the net. I keep the map not as lines but as pull. This much slack here, this much strain there. When the wake-bloom comes - that thousand-square-mile riot of pale flowers that live forty-eight hours after the Grazer - I am still humming underneath it, a little frayed, a little bleached where I was bitten, already feeling for my other end.

You would not notice me at first. Nobody does. You look for forests and mantas and towers. I'm the reason the forest is still a forest after the storm. I'm the dark line stitching amber and pink and feathered house-sized lungs into one loose continent that can sway and not break.

If you stayed long enough - and here you have to stay long enough for anything to happen, because even growing is visible if you watch - the Lungstone terrace below me would grow around you a little out of habit. The Floatmeadow would snag above me for a season and give you ground that dimples. The Eyes would flick through me, one eye at a time sharing one thought, and for a heartbeat you'd see yourself from many angles in their glass bodies before they inverted at a layer-boundary and became their own mirrors.

And I would be there, in the warm blood-warm dim, in the tick of silt on silt, in the stone-breath heat radiating up from the flattened world that learned to be still.

You would find me by following the tension to its center. Where two great ribbons lean away from each other after the Grazer has passed, but do not part.

Put your hand - if you can call it a hand - on me there. I'm warm. I give a little under your weight and hold.

I have decided, like the young ribbons do, that this slight concavity is worth being heavy for.
