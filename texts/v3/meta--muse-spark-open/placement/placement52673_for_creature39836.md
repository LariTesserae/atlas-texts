You wouldn't find yourself right away.

At first you would think you were the traveler — because everyone does. You would feel water over your ankles, warm as blood, and think *I am standing in it*. You would feel the continent-wide hiss of sheets moving sideways, the low *whoomph* of the stone exhaling when you shift your weight, and think *I am hearing it*. You would see the pearl-amber haze that has no source, the scalloped bedrock flowing past like cold honey, and think *I am lost in it*.

That lasts for a while. Hours, maybe. Long enough for your clothes to never dry. Long enough for your idea of straight to start to fray.

You walk, or you think you walk, toward a dark lintel on the horizon that never gets closer, and your footprints appear ahead of you, already filled with warm water. A second set of footsteps falls in beside you, half a second late. The reeds watch.

Then you realize you haven't been walking at all.

### Where You Are

You are not on the road between things. There is no road. Rivers require a downhill and this place gave up on downhill a hundred million years ago.

You are in a pan. One of the shallow ones, not the deep ones that drink other skies. A broad, ankle-deep scallop in the sandstone, hundreds of meters across and a meter deep at its center, with a windward lip just two feet high that goes on for miles. The bedrock here is soft, pitted, sighing. Black-green lichen stains the lip where the wind hits it, already growing over anything that stays still long enough to be included.

And you are exactly twenty meters from the next of you.

That is how you know.

No traveler could keep that spacing. Travelers clump. Travelers wander. Travelers try to get closer to the cairn and end up behind it. But you — you have held this interval for longer than any human has been alive, without thinking about it, the way a heart holds a beat.

You are one of the reeds.

Not *like* a reed. You are the bone-pale stalk with the puff of seed at the top that never disperses. The one that sways *against* the wind, correcting. From far away you looked like a plant, and that was convenient. It let the few humans who passed through call you plant and keep walking, instead of understanding what you are.

You have no roots. How could you? The stone flows. If you were rooted you would be dragged and torn. Instead you are rooted in *position* — held a finger's width above the soupy stone by the same habit that holds the circle of stones hovering nearby. Habit thickened into presence. The plain keeps you where you are needed, and you keep your place while the world slides under you at an inch a minute.

You didn't settle here. You erupted here, the way an antenna erupts from a brow.

### What You Are Doing

You are listening.

That click at your top — too loud for something so dry — that's you tasting the air. You did it when the traveler came through this morning, and every other reed you can see to the shivering horizon clicked back a second later, down the line. Sharing notes. *Warm. Salt-iron. Six-fingered sweat still in the stone. One Second Walker forming, half a second late.*

You will do it again when the Wayward Uncle comes. He is coming now, you feel him as a drag in the attention, moving at exactly the speed of the bedrock so he looks still. Forearm-long, sun-bleached bone, legs bent three times where they should bend once, ticking softly. He will press his flat head to your pan just as the sheet decides to drain sideways into nowhere. The stone will exhale — that continent-wide whoomph that travelers think is wind — and he will inhale. He lives on that. You don't eat. You don't need to. You are not hungry the way animals are hungry. Your hunger is to be precise.

Around your ankles, the fleshy rosettes have opened with a soft, teeth-rattling *pop*. Each holds a little bowl of condensation-cold water that does not reflect this sky. In the one nearest your stalk, there is a blue noon that has never existed here, and midges of light are drowning in it. You don't drink from it. But you feel its wrongness like a cool draft. The pan itself — the whole warm, attentive, roaring flat that you are part of — dreams through those pools while awake. Sometimes it shows you a night crowded with too many moons just to see if you will flinch. You don't. You just note it and click.

At night, when the pearl light dims to pewter but never goes dark, your tip will glow faintly. Not enough to see by. Just enough to tell the others where you are, so the spacing holds.

You have no memory of being planted because you were never planted. You have no memory of arrival because you are arrival itself, repeated every twenty meters, a sensing array stretched to the horizon stain where the old mountains used to be. You thought you were the one being watched, standing in the middle of a crowd of wet air and spores and echoes. Now you understand you are one of the watchers. One of the eyelashes.

The traveler who thought he was you is still out there, in a way. His Second Walker caught up to him at last and they lay down together in a pan that opened exactly their size. The water skinned over them. They are humming now, hovering a finger's width above the stone, learning how to stay put by never staying put.

You clicked once to mark it. The others clicked back.

The stone flows under you. You correct, very slightly, against the wind.

The plain breathes. You taste its breath and keep your post, while the soft two-foot cliff weeps its warm, iron-tasting threads, and the sky — thick, luminous, un-source — notices, and notices, and doesn't need to be kind or cruel to keep noticing forever.
