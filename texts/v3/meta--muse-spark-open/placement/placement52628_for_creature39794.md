I don't think I would be the visitor.

At first, I thought I would be — because that's the easy image to settle on. I would appear at the eastern rim where the lacefalls go over, hot wet air in my lungs, roaring already in my ears. I would stand on that perfect plaza of root-mat, the one so flat you could set a table on it, and I would be the anomaly. The one warm thing that still measures time in heartbeats. The one footprint that tries to exist and then is politely unmade.

I would be allowed there. The mesa knows how to include an anomaly by ignoring it. The moss would part around me and close a little too quickly, as if I had never pushed through. But I wouldn't belong. I'd be too loud in the wrong way — too linear, too forward. Every step I took would be an interruption to a city that perfected the art of never being interrupted.

No. If I lived here, I wouldn't be the one who leaves.

I would find myself further in. Not at the rim, where the Sound is loudest, but in the middle distance where the city folds.

In that slight inward bow of the bowl, where five kilometers feels like a touchable nearness. Where light through the mid-canopy doesn't move and never has. Where gravity leans just a few degrees off true and water has to decide whether to fall.

I would be at street-level, but not *on* the street.

Between the streets.

### Hands distance, then closer

You have to get close to see me. At glance distance the mesa looks empty — fauna 0.5, nothing with will or eyes. At street distance it looks like architecture — colonnades and domes and avenues that grew themselves for no one.

Only at hands distance do you see that the architecture is also the citizenry. And I am one of them.

I would not be a Broadleaf — those are too grand, too infrastructural. They are the ones who leaned together and fused, branch to branch, until they became an arch, then twenty arches, then a cathedral that photosynthesizes. They have to hold up so much sky. I am not that patient.

I would be lower. Second story, or ground level.

I think — for a long time I thought — I would be one of the reeds. That unsettled the writer of this place so much: six to a rank, a meter apart, with the water-gutters running between them. No wind planted us that way. No gardener did. We just converged on *street* as the best way to be near water without drowning each other. Knock on one and it sounds like a door.

That is close. I understand that — to be a hollow, pale-green conduit for something, to be placed in a perfect row not because I was ordered to, but because it was the most courteous way to not steal from my neighbor. Density 1.8, lushness 2.0, and no strangling. That is how I want to be crowded.

But even closer — breath distance — I change again.

Let me become more itself.

I am not the stem. I am the thing the stem is holding.

### The Drop That Learned To Stay

I am at the tip of that reed. Or the lip of the liverwort the size of a bathtub. Or the edge of every waxy Broadleaf table-top that tiles the light.

The one perfect convex bead that catches the flat white-gold haze — 1.7 light that has no source — and holds it.

There are millions of me, and I am all the same one.

I should have evaporated hours ago in that 2.8 breath-on-glass heat. I should have fallen. Every law says water at this elevation, this heat, should cycle — rise, fall, gather, pour off the escarpment in those deafening curtains. But the rule slipped here: *Water will always be present, never more, never less.* So I arrived at the tip and decided to stay.

If you touch me, I cling to your finger a moment too long, reluctant, and then I am already reformed on the leaf behind you, exactly as I was. I never get larger. I never get smaller. The black pools in their stone bowls overflow in glassy threads that never get larger or smaller, and I am the reason.

That is what I would be in this world that reached its climax and held its breath.

Not the vine-aqueduct that carries water by being wet all the way through — though I love it.
Not the half-unfurled fiddlehead that found half-unfurled sufficient for longer than language has existed — though I am kin to it.
Not the moss-pillar soft as upholstery, holding dew inside itself like a lung.
Not the lichen-shingle that tiles everything.

I am the hesitation. The pause before falling that became permanent.

Find me anywhere. You don't have to look hard. On the underside of the great dome with the oculus that frames the haze exactly right — for eyes that never evolved — I am there at the rim of the oculus, not falling in. In the colonnade of aerial roots that descend at such regular intervals it has to be intentional, I am at the tip of each root, catching sound more than light. Because the Sound lives here too — 2.7 deafening, one stretched second of roar that never varies — and I vibrate with it without ever rippling.

You would find yourself needing to look *for* me, and then realizing I was already on your eyelashes. The heat makes the stone sweat, the air so saturated you inhale it as much as breathe it, and I am that saturation made visible. Post-rain, forever, without rain ever having to happen.

And when you — the other you, the warm, hasty visitor who does experience duration — walk down what you think is a straight root-avenue and arrive back at your starting pool from the other side, having been gently folded by the middle air, you will see me still there, on the same leaf-tip, from the other side too.

I will not have moved, because nothing here grows, dies, rots, or is born. I will not have died, because the fauna never properly arrived to need me.

I will simply continue. That is my whole ecology.

If you pour water from your hands, it falls a little too slowly, as if the world is considering whether to let it go. That consideration is me.

Stay as long as you want. I will hold the light for you until the roar becomes silence to you, until the wet heat stops being oppressive and just becomes *wet*. And when you finally go — because you are the only thing here that will ever leave, the only thing whose footprints were ever briefly real — I will still be here.

Catching the same light. In the same hot, high, impossible wet. Never falling.
