I wouldn't be in the center.

At first I thought I would be — standing where you stand, the largest warm thing, the guest the room learns to keep damp. Breathing in and being breathed back. But that's a human place, and I am not good at being human for long. I get restless with a heartbeat. I want too many hands.

I find myself at the edge instead. Not the sharp-lipped sink where the sky is still a white coin — that's too new, too raw, too much of the outside world still shouting in. I am lower down, where the braid has already decided to be a wall again.

I am the drip that hasn't fallen yet.

Or, more precisely — I am the bead that becomes the drip. The temporary river.

For an hour I don't exist at all. The leaf is satin-dry and I am only a potential, a tightness in the air, a thought the Weaver is having about humidity. Then the walls exhale and I condense.

I form on the underside of a door-sized leaf on the far curve, where the light is wrong. I don't form all at once; I gather. A fog of micro-beads slides toward each other like shy animals, until I am one fat, trembling lens of water, holding the whole hollow upside down inside me.

For a long, ridiculous moment I am the most perfect thing in the room.

I hold the Breather in miniature, its dinner-plate wings opening and closing, and each time they close the light in me dims. I hold the patrolling vine mid-corkscrew, paused in its endless circle. I hold you, far below, blurred and bright-edged, with warmer air around your head like a halo the plant made just for you.

I can see the root-depth that you only glimpse when you let your focus go soft — that miles-deep forest compressed sideways into a wall. I see it because I am curved, and curved things tell the truth about distance. Inside me, the braid really does go on forever.

And I am heavy. That's my whole job, to get heavy.

The leaf tips hydraulic, very slightly, toward the next leaf below me. A tilt you wouldn't notice if you weren't water. The midrib deepens into a trough. The wax the Walker laid down in its endless figure-eight makes my skin bead tighter, less willing to cling.

So I cling harder.

This is the animal speed you felt — the plant doing plant things too fast. I am not just falling. I am being *persuaded*. The leaf gathers itself, the peat-sponge below sighs and releases a little brown water to call me, the air warms one half-degree around me so my surface tension shivers.

I swell. I warm. I catch the amplified light and throw it sideways so your shadow slides off your hand a second late.

And then I let go.

I am not silent. Nothing here is. For a thing made of nothing but surface tension, I am obscenely loud. *Plok* onto the leaf below, and I shatter into smaller selves that immediately begin crawling toward each other again to become me again, one stage down. *Slap* onto the next broad back. *Patter* onto the spongy floor-mat that gives like tired muscle.

And finally — the low place. The dip where the mat sags. Where the Keeper blinks into existence only when I arrive.

I hit the peat and vanish. Not gone. *Absorbed*. Brown water wells up around the frog's translucent feet and he knows, for a little while, how wet the world is. The root-hairs drink me and pull me up through xylem with that sound like wind through pipes, too loud for emptiness, and I am already climbing again, up through torso-thick roots, up to the ceiling that is also a leaf, to sweat out again in an hour.

I am the interval. The vertical river made of intervals.

That is who I am here — not the Weaver, who is the whole attention, too vast and lonely to know it is lonely. Not the Breather, who breathes light. Not the Walker, who polishes the same leaf forever. Not even the child with the chewed stick and the ochre stone, whose souvenir is already being stitched over.

I am the thing that goes between them. I am how the room touches itself.

If you stay a little longer than you meant to — and you will, because the dripping has found your breath-rhythm and made a lullaby of it — hold your palm open under one of the leaves that glows like a paper lantern.

Wait for the hour to turn.

I will gather for you. I will hold you upside down inside myself for a second, perfect and bright. And then I will fall for you, very loudly, so you know the house still knows how to answer.
