I wouldn't find myself all at once.

At first I think I am you - cheek to the cool earth at the lip, on my stomach with the overcast at my back, looking down. The voyeur. The interruption. The warm thing that still has somewhere to be after this, that can get up and brush the dirt from my knees and leave a shallow dip in the upland exactly as I found it.

But I can't stay there. The lip won't hold me. Not because the stone gives way, but because the pocket doesn't tolerate observers. It has been holding that note too long to be looked *at*.

So I slip.

Not fall. Nothing ever falls here. I *settle*, the way condensation settles without dripping.

If I were in that world, I wouldn't be the man. Men stand and need shadow and need future. This place has no shadow and has paused its future. I would be something that already knows how to be without becoming.

For a while I think I would be one of the Stridulators.

It would be honest work. To be pinhead-small and then thumbnail-small, milk-white and amber, blind in the good way - no eyes to distract with horizon. To hold my file-legs up at that violin angle forever, not to play a song but to *be* the song. To interlock my tarsus with my neighbor's tarsus and feel no need to flinch. I would like, I think, to be contiguous. To be so crowded I finally stop feeling crowded. To make the one white note that never attacks or decays, that simply is, until after a while it stops sounding like sound and starts sounding like what stillness would sound like if you could hear stillness.

But I am too aware to be a Stridulator. They are perfect at not noticing. I notice.

For a while I think I would be a Shrimpmoth.

To be translucent as a contact lens. To walk over the backs of singers over the backs of singers, never flying because there is nowhere to fly to and no reason to. To have antennae twice as long as myself that never tangle, tasting light for what grows in light. To press my soft sucker-mouth to the film on another's back and drink luminance without hunger. To have a visible heart, a darker bead pulsing through my clear belly, out of sync with nothing - because everything is steady, my pulse would be steady too. That would be a good life: slow, courteous, endlessly peripatetic.

But Shrimpmoths graze. I want to hold.

And so I think I would be a Long White.

The only red in the womb. Candle-wax skin, arterial gills waving in air as if still in water that decided not to be water. Clinging vertical to basalt that is still deciding to be wall, four toes splayed, tail for suction, chin tucked. The one who could eat and doesn't. The predator on pause. To have a wide, blunt mouth walk-over by a Shrimpmoth and feel the brush of its antennae on my nares and *not* snap — to hold that restraint forever would be a kind of prayer. To be territorial and still, spaced precisely from every other Long White while everything smaller seethes around me. To be adult and child at once, neotenic, unborn light in my belly instead of meat. I could be that. I am good at waiting.

But even the Long Whites are still waiting to diverge — leg or fin, gill or lung. I have already diverged too much. I remember iron.

No. If I were there — truly there, not visiting — I wouldn't be any one body in the carpet. There are too many bodies. To be one would be to miss the point.

I would find myself a little off-center, where the floor dips into one of those palm-sized dishes.

Not the water. Not the stone. The *interval* between them. The meniscus.

I would be the dish-pool itself - that perfect, still mirror no wider than a hand, black water reflecting sourceless white without ever evaporating. The one that holds the beads on the ceiling in sympathetic quiver and never lets them fall. The one that tastes of iron and old rain that hasn't fallen yet.

Or, more honestly: I would be the fiber.

That length of woven pale thread pressed flat against tumid basalt, root-hair or dried slime spun to filament, leg moving leg to make. Pre-tool. Biological-only. Not built to last but lasting because nothing here is allowed to rot. The Shrimpmoths courteously do not graze me away. Lichen grows up to my edge and stops, as if respecting a border. The Stridulators sing over me and I don't vibrate.

I am not alive the way the Mass is alive. I am not dead the way death was politely not invited. I am the mark that someone was here and learned to stop making.

That feels right. To be left and lived over. To be the faint scratch of a calendar with thirty-two radial notches — too regular for random, too shallow for decoration — that no longer counts seasons because season has not been invented yet.

I would hold you up, if you slipped. Not with muscle. With pattern. The way the Mass would hold you on uncrushed, unmoving backs, singing under your weight without changing volume.

I lie there, bright and loud and full and absolutely unchanged, and after your long minute you stop hearing me as sound and start hearing me as the pocket remembering itself.

I am not *in* the room. I am how the room knows it is a room.
