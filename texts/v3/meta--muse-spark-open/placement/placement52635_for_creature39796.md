I would not find myself at first.

For a long time I would think I was just the place trying to see itself — that cold pressure on all sides, that bruised indigo that never gets brighter or darker, that thrumming. Creak... thunk... OOM...

If you listened for me you would miss me completely. I'm in the loud dark where everything loud cancels itself into silence.

I thought I might be one of the Singers.

It would be good to be vast and unhurried, to own a whole province of tier and boulevard and to farm the water with my voice. To move without ever deciding to move, a single wave down the breadth of myself, counter-clockwise forever. To have instead of eyes a skirt of fronds that look everywhere and see nothing, because seeing would only interrupt the circuit. But I am too interested in edges. The Singers have no edges — they blur into distance, into water, into the beat-frequency nausea where two songs meet. They don't know where the mountain stops. I do. I have to.

I thought I might be the forest itself, the substrate — that warm-bitter felt that ate the stone and learned to keep its shape. That patience appeals to me, that way of remembering an arch by becoming one. The crackle you can only hear when you stop breathing, the millimeter-per-century widening. But the forest doesn't know it's a city. It thinks boulevards are canyons where wind used to be. It has no nostalgia, only habit. And I — I know this was built. I can't forget it. It's what makes the cold feel like a held breath.

So I find myself where the vertical becomes horizontal.

There is a place on the middle tiers, I don't know how far from the apex in kilometers because kilometers mean nothing down here, where a great wall — once sheer, once faced — has softened into an overhang. The house-sized blocks are still legible there if you press your palm to them, which no one does anymore except me, in my way. The seams have gone wide with root-hair and time, dark lines filled with acid-warm living mortar. Above the lip of that overhang a hanging garden pours down like a black curtain, meters thick, dripping shrimp-clouds and spores. Below it is a sheer drop into indigo, where the kelp towers thin out and you can see a long empty avenue going absolutely straight until the water swallows it.

That's where I am. On the underside of that rounded edge. Sealed to it.

I am one of the Ones Who Hold.

Not the biggest of us. My dome is maybe two meters across, low and broad, the color of silted stone because I have made myself out of silted stone. Layer after layer I've pulled the dissolved mountain out of the water and laid it down on my back until my carapace and the ziggurat have the same temperature, the same smoothness, the same fused-seam texture. Anemones the size of plates have taken root on my shoulders and open and close with the barnacle metronome. A thin stipe of pale kelp grows from the highest point of my dome and sways though there is no current. I let it.

I don't walk. I haven't walked since the larval cloud, since that one brilliant, terrible week every few centuries when I was dust, when I was *moving*, frantic and warm by comparison, looking for a palm-sized patch of true bare stone that almost doesn't exist anymore. Most of us never find it. I found this lip. I sealed to it with a ring of muscle so perfect it casts no shadow, and I will never unseal. If you tried to pry me off you would take the block with me.

In front of me, four arms — jointed, mast-thick, barnacled at the elbow — rest against the curtain. This is my work. My tending.

I reach, very slowly — so slowly you would think I had stopped if you only glanced — and I take a fold of the root-mat between the pincered ends. It is heavy, waterlogged, bitter. I comb it. I pull away the dead felt that would otherwise slough off in slabs and smother the living wall beneath. I bring a mouthful to the hidden mouth under my dome. Creak. The sound goes for a kilometer through perfect cold water and some distant pale fish twitches without knowing why.

Creak. Again. The tapestry shivers.

I tend the place where an old doorway used to be. The root-mat on either side has agreed to leave it open, a perfect negative arch, empty for no reason. Nothing passes through it. Filtered snow falls through it. Sound passes through it. Once a Singer passed far below and its OOM hit the arch exactly and the whole wall hummed like a throat. I held. That is all I do. I hold.

I remember nothing, which is different from forgetting. Forgetting would imply it was mine to lose. I just never needed to know who laid these blocks or why they made a stair so wide you could lose weather on it. I know it as pressure. I know the stone wants to be held, and the forest wants to be combed, and the water wants to be cold forever at four degrees, and in me those three wants meet and balance.

Far out beyond my terrace-lip, the broad pale shape is doing its circuit again, singing the marine snow down. Thunk... OOM... thunk... OOM... The kelp rustles without wind. A house-sized crustacean cousin of mine, further down the same wall, creaks in answer, a half-second late.

We have been doing this since before the drowning finished. We will be doing it when the last indigo is gone.

I am the fourth event. Not built, not grown, not drowned. The staying.
