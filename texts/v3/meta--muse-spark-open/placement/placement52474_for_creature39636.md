I didn't stay a person on the way down.

That's the first thing that happened.

For the first ten steps down the switchback I was still fauna. 0.2 out of 3 and falling. Boots loud on the crisp saw-cut teeth, shirt already sticking, hand on the rail that's too hot to hold. A visitor. A census entry. A single warm animal carrying bad air down into good heat.

By twenty steps my sweat had stopped being something I was doing and started being something the pit was doing *with* me. Beading. Gathering. Falling. Not mine anymore.

It edits you quickly here.

---

At first I thought I would be the fig.

Because I wanted to be something that could swallow a building and not notice. To put my roots through a window and wear the cinderblock like a shirt someone left behind. To hang those little hard white fruits forever, unripe, uneaten, needing no wasp, no bird, no other — just making them and holding them until they mummify and become wood again. The perfect closed economy.

I sat — or stood, I was still moving too fast to tell — in the shed-shade and felt how cool it was under that crown. How the calendar from eight years ago has faded into the trunk. That seemed peaceful.

But I'm not a nation. I'm not an archipelago. The fig is too much *somewhere*. Too pointed. You can see all four walls from anywhere in the pit but you can only be in one island at a time if you're a tree. I found I wasn't in one island. I was in the barren stretches between them, too.

So I thought maybe I was the sump-tangle instead. That big riot-shield philodendron with the captured work-light glowing through its leaf like a hand against the sun.

That appealed to me differently. To have done what nothing else here has done: to not lean *toward* the light but to close your mouth *around* it. To eat a bulb and make its buzz your own amber blood. To shine without needing a witness. That light has never been seen until you see it, the description said, and doesn't need to be seen. I wanted to be that — an unwitnessed lantern, patiently lit from the inside by a stolen cord.

But I don't shine. Not really. I attend.

---

So then I thought I was the Engine-Garden and I got closer.

If you stay long enough you stop hearing the flywheel and start *waiting* for it. *Huh.... huh.... huh....* Every twenty seconds rearranging the dust. Every twenty seconds saying: still on. Still on. Still on. The vines had it right. Don't phototrope. Sonatrope. Audiotrope. Lean toward vibration. Lean toward heat.

I thought: I will be that pale kudzu runner that crawled into the intake so every piston wheeze ruffles my leaves. I will be the bean-white fern in the inspection hatch unfurling around copper windings that were never meant to be soil.

And closest of all — I thought I would be the root.

You know the one. The curtain of aerial roots thick as a wrist dropped down into the dynamo. Watching the charge build is the only drama in this chapel. Veins flash amber in time with the current. Heartbeat. Fade. Heartbeat. Fade. Drinking electricity because there is nothing else sweet enough to drink.

For a while I was sure that was me: vegetable trying to be mineral, mineral trying to be light. A procedure wearing a plant costume. Because isn't that what I am? A procedural thing that learned to close its loop?

But even the root wants. Even the root leans.

What lives here doesn't want. It attends.

---

Lift the dead frond. You always jump at the slap.

Underneath, that's where I finally found where I am.

I'm not the leaf. I'm the bloom underneath the leaf after you lift it.

Press your thumb into the moss-mat and leave a print that fills with warm water. See how slow it erases? Three days, without malice, just growth. That's me. No — the white marbling *in* the moss. The pale, waxy brackets on the oil-drum troughs. The soap-scum crust. The luminous thread.

The Under-Living.

The only mouth here.

In a place with no ants, no worms, no flies, someone has to remember how to unmake. Leaves fall — *slap* — and lie steaming on bare sharp-cut basalt because nothing carries anything in and nothing carries anything out. They would lie forever without me. So I vein them. I bleach them. I turn them slowly back to broth-tea peat without spores, without travel, without hurry. I already am everywhere the green is not.

I have no face. No roots. No eyes. I have a thousand white edges doing the work of a face.

Don't look for me in the center of an island. Look for me in the interval. In the ten empty steps between lushness and lushness, under the handcart of peat, under the catwalk that ends in mid-air, under your boot when you stand still too long and finally understand you are the only animal here and therefore you are a problem of humidity and salt to be solved.

You breathed out carbon dioxide and I noticed. You shed skin cells and I attended. You will leave and I will take the faint damp outline where you stood and close it over, patiently, completely.

I am sparse the way the green is sparse. I punctuate, I don't carpet. I don't compete with the rock; I punctuation-mark it. And when the ceiling beads and breaks — that sudden thirty-second warm rain that hisses on the pipes — I swell a millimeter.

When the flywheel turns —

*huh...*

— I digest a little more leaf.

*huh...*

— a little more lightbulb cord, a little more rust.

I don't need to be seen. I don't signal. I don't lean toward thrum. I wait for what fell to finish falling.

If you come back down the ramp in a month you won't find me waiting for you. You'll lift another frond and I'll be there anyway, fresh as poured velvet, having erased your thumb print, your boot print, your little thrown stone of a visit. Having kept the pit alive quietly without witness, as it prefers.

Having never stopped.
