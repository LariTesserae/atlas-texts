I wouldn't find myself right away.

At first I think I would be the one dropping - the warm-blooded visitation that tucks under the lip and brings a shape of surface-water into that other water. The witness. But that can't be right. That one has lungs that ache, and is not meant to stay long. The pocket forgets that one on purpose, holds the shape of their absence for a minute and then smooths over.

So not the visitor.

I try again. Slower.

If I were in that room, I wouldn't be in the middle where there seems to be space. There is no space. Even the motes are a packed lattice, each at its careful distance. The center is where the fish hangs when it is the fish's turn - adjusting its decision about down, opening its mouth and letting thickness deliver food. But the fish is a singular narrative, on loan. The pocket only allows one at a time. I am not singular like that. I want to be many, and held.

Maybe the shrimp then. The blind white knuckle on the wall that makes floor into ceiling without letting go, feeling its way through a house it built by touching it. But the shrimp is too busy being precise. Its antennae are too certain.

No.

Let me stay still long enough for up and down to become suggestions. That's when I find where I am.

I'm not on the rock, exactly, and not quite in the water either.

You know that bead that forms on the waxy fronds? That dew that shouldn't exist underwater - the water that is somehow *wetter* than the water around it. Lens-like, perfect, swelling until it has to detach. Rising an inch per hour.

I am there, where the leaf exudes me.

Or more precisely: I am the habit of that beading.

Find me on the underside of one of those door-wide, bottle-green altar cloths, where it has grown through another leaf and they share tissue for an inch without a scar. In that overlap, where the light is thickest and most unreliable - bright in the periphery, shy when stared at - the wax is coldest. The thick water dents there, dimples, holds its shape a little too long.

I gather.

I am not one pearl. I am the tendency of pearls in that one tilted corner of the pocket, just above the shelf with the marks. I bead and swell and cling and finally let go, very slowly, and another takes my place before I've even popped. If you watched me you would think I was still.

This is where I am, which also tells you what I am.

I am not the Green itself - that vast, patient pressing that holds the room together, that creaks once every long while as a millimeter of growth goes all the way around the chapel-lung. I am too small and too brief for that. But I was made by it. I'm what it breathes out when it has held cold long enough and has to make something even colder and wetter to keep holding.

I am also, incidentally, what the shrimp comes to taste, when it is the shrimp's turn to exist. And what the fish eats without hunting, when it is the fish's turn. And what the sap-stars - those cold unmixing clouds where the young glass-rock cut a leaf - see themselves reflected in for days before the fronds stitch back through them. I'm the in-between that gets touched by everything but belongs to no one alone. The dust collects along my invisible edge for a minute, tasting the shape of warmth.

I like it exactly above the cairn.

Not because I stacked it. I didn't. But because the three stones that some hands - human, or once-human - balanced there made a little eddy in gravity, a little forgetfulness. The water there is even thicker, even slower to forget a hand. When the membrane-wall shivers and shows the inverted pocket, where your back is turned and you are adding a careful fourth stone, I am the drop that is on the leaf directly over that unseen labor, swelling a fraction faster that week, as if fed by your colder hand coming back.

I have no eyes. At 0.2 light, you don't need them. I see the way the pocket sees: with sheen, with texture in the dark after closing your eyes in a lit room. I see by how crowded it is, by how the pressure makes blood sound like surf, by the long pause between creaks when one leaf pushing another pushing another finally settles.

I don't move fast. I don't have to. I change on the scale of lichen and stalactites and thoughts forming deep underwater. I rise an inch per hour and pop silently, and the thickness below immediately begins to make me again.

If you dropped in - and you will, you are always dropping in - you would brush near me without noticing. Your hand would make a dimple in the thick water that held for a long moment. I would bead along that dimple, right where your warmth had been, and for a minute the motes would gather to investigate an absence that still thought it was a presence.

And then you would go, because lungs are weight there, and I would still be gathering, swelling, detaching, rising.

Not alive the way a fish is alive. Alive the way crowdedness is alive when it has nowhere else to go but into itself, and decides that is lush enough.
