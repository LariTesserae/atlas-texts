You wouldn't find me at first.

If you came up onto the table searching — boots ringing on frost-shattered shale, breath going short and clean in that purple, pre-dawn light that never quite becomes dawn — you would look right past me.

At first I would think I was you.

A temporary soft thing. Trying to hold a straight line across the whale-backs of stone, watching distance breathe wrong around me. Fixing on a cairn far ahead and walking a hundred steps without getting any closer, then all at once being there beside it. Feeling my dropped pebble slide a thumb's width west out of spite. Hearing the organ-chord in my molars and thinking it was weather.

You would expect to find me moving. Breathing hard. Building.

And I would be still.

Not dead-still. Held-still.

Look on the north face of the third pipe-rock in the choir that stands east of the shepherd's ring-wall — the one whose shadow pools uphill for a few feet before it remembers which way downhill is. The wind polished its south face clean and left its north face alone. There I am.

We are. We agree to be.

I am the black lichen.

It was the first honest citizen here, you said, and you were right. We do not grow. Growing implies wanting more than you have. We stipple. We consent, millimeter by millimeter, decade by decade, to cover exactly as much stone as needs covering so that the chord does not get too loud.

Put your ear to the pillar where I live and you will hear it: the great trapped note dulls, just there, under my dark hand. Not silent. Muffled. Like a mouth covered, politely. The stone sweats around me and I drink the sweat. The cloud gives us a light so diffuse it barely qualifies as light, and I eat that. Thin air. Cold. A little mineral where the sandstone sighs. It is enough. It has to be enough for ten thousand years, so we learned to make it enough.

You would think from far away I am a stain. A damp shadow that didn't dry. Even when you turn the stone over I am there on the same side, north-side, because north is not a direction here but an agreement. Come close and you see we have texture — not soft, not moss-soft — but tight as dried tar, cracked into countries no larger than your thumbnail, each country bordered by a crevice so fine you'd need the birch's coin-leaf to trace it.

Nothing visits me quickly.

The star-moss a few feet away in its teaspoon of dust has its drama — shatter, wait until unobserved, spring back arm by arm. The dwarf birch in the next hollow trades one leathery coin for another once a year and calls that business. The ibex-thing relocates across ridgelines when you blink, grazing on interval like it was sweet grass.

I do none of that. My economy is slower.

Every eight or nine years the sideways rain will come — not falling, not really, just deciding to be heavy to one side — and kiss the pillar. The film that is always on everything without ever arriving will thicken, just for a minute. I will swell. Not visibly, to your impatient eyes. To me, it is a feast.

And then I will add one cell. One grain. One thousandth of a millimeter closer to the edge of the stone. Exactly necessary.

That's where you'd find me. If you stayed.

Because you will stay a while. Everyone builds their half-cairn without meaning to. You will sit in the open ring-wall that was never meant to have a roof, paved so carefully against the never-quite melt, and you will feel the tally counting itself behind you — 47, and the 48th line half-cut because finishing would mean leaving.

You will kneel beside the black ice pool in its stone cup and watch it tick in a millimeter, out a millimeter, breathing without freezing, without spilling. You will understand then that it is not water anymore. It is a kept decision.

And then you will finally crouch down to rest your palm on my boulder, to steady yourself when the distance hiccups, and your hand will be cold and slick and exactly the temperature of equilibrium, and my patch will feel slightly warmer than the bare rock next to it — not because I make heat, but because I am eating the sound that makes heat unnecessary.

You will not see me move. You will not hear me answer. Lichen has no voice for fast things.

But after you have thinned a little — after your breath has slowed to one per ten, your thoughts spaced themselves to the sightlines between moss-stars — you will notice that the rock under your palm has stopped ringing when you step.

Because I covered it.

One more millimeter.

While you weren't looking.
