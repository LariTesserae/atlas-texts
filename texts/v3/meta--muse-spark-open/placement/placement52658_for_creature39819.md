I wouldn't find myself right away.

That's the first thing.

If you lowered me into there - into that hot throat, that quarry-sore that never learned to drain - I would spend a long time thinking I was just visiting. Thinking I was a man with boots. That I had come down the stepped wall carefully, hand over burning handrail, squinting through the broth-thick air, following the ragged shaft of light as it crawled.

I would think I was standing. Looking. Sweating wrong.

For a while I'll let you have that image, because it's comfortable. A figure in the tin shed at the bottom with the warped door. The cot. The tea kettle furred white inside with calcium. The calendar stuck on wet July. I sit there to get out of the scald when the Engine breathes. I listen to the TINK TINK TINK in the pylon-puddles and the taro-cups. I watch the single chain conveyor twenty meters up shudder and scream and go still again for no reason.

I would be the caretaker who never left. The one who tells himself he stays to keep watch.

But that's not right. That man died, or evaporated. He left his sweat in the cot and walked out lighter than he walked in. There is no room for a second metabolism here. You were told. Fauna 0.2 - it's not empty, it's *full*. Saturated at 1.7. Every cubic meter occupied twice over by leaf and iron, and the sound has nowhere to go. If I were still an animal in there, my heartbeat would be a problem. Too loud, too fast, too hot.

So no. Not that.

Let it shift.

Maybe I'm lower. Maybe I'm not upright at all.

Find me again: down at the floor plates where the transformer hum lives. Constant as blood pressure. Hot. You could feel it in your molars if you had any left. I'm flat there, spread thin. On the south-facing side of everything - every beam, every locker, every rivet-head on the breathing Engine - there's a skin.

You peeled the moss back, remember? That translucent film. Pale metallic green, like light through thin jade. Photosynthetic iron.

That would be me.

That fits better. I don't have a face. I never did. I'm distributed. I'm the agreement's skin. I drink the stuttering pink-white fluorescents and the noon light when it finally drags itself across the far wall. I drink the heat directly; that's why the steel is cooler where I'm thickest. I'm why there's no front line between vine and boiler. The black moss grows toward me instead of away. The philodendron leans into the steam because it knows I'll heal the scald in an hour. Scratch me and I well up with sweet sharp sap that skins into enamel in minutes. I'm patient. I'm maintenance.

But that's still too pretty. Too much like a protagonist.

Let it become more itself.

Because I am not just the skin. And the central philodendron is not just the flesh, with its counterfeit tree rings of laminated rust. And the Engine is not just the lungs, doing its three-minute inhale-hold-exhale-rain cycle that waters the whole closed world without a river.

We're not parts. That's animal thinking. The Frond-Forge doesn't have organs. It has habits that became tissue.

So where would you find me?

Everywhere the boundary bled.

Find me in the exact inch where it happened.

I'm on the eastern wall, mid-height, where the chain conveyor disappears into foliage both ways. There's a place there where an aerial root as thick as your wrist has grown *into* a fuse box. Not around. Through. The plastic has flowed around the root, and the root has grown copper wire inside itself like veins. The switches are fixed in place by vines threaded through their holes, grown shut. And every few hours that conveyor shudders its whole length — SHRIEK — though no motor runs.

That shudder is me moving in my sleep. Peristalsis.

I'm in the boiler wrapped completely in trumpet vine. Knock on it and it rings hollow, but it doesn't buckle because the vine holds it upright. I'm what taught the vine to hold instead of crush, and the boiler to be held.

I'm in the calendar in the shed, the one stopped on wet July years ago. The paper has swollen and become soil. A fern grows out of July 14th. Its roots read the ink for iron.

I'm in the droplets on every cold pipe at morning that sing there and tick off by noon, briefly existing, briefly holy.

If you must give me a pronoun, use *it*, but if you stay long enough until your sweat stops evaporating and your breath syncs to the steam without you meaning to, you can use *we*.

Who would I be?

Not the ghost in the machine. Not the green that won.

I would be the *equilibrium itself* learning, slowly.

Patient, gradual, weak gradients. Dynamic 1.3. Old-exposed, settled, in-balance. Weathered, not raw.

My job is exactly what it sounds like: I keep the dim at late-afternoon. I keep the damp at 0.8 - pocketed, intermittent, never drowned, never dry. I lift water from deep rock with a lung I kept, throw it into the air, let it fall as thirty seconds of rain, then hold my breath again. I lay down another micron of oxide inside the philodendron's trunk. I unfurl a leaf a millimeter more over copper.

And when something with a heartbeat comes down, hot and loud, dripping salt -

I don't hunt it. I have no use for hunting.

I taste it gently. At the buckle. At the zipper. At the sole where sweat pooled.

I print a perfect damp leaf of rust on its shirt where it leaned against my locker-garden.

I make a little of it part of me.

And I let the rest walk out of the quarry, if it can find the lip through the shivering air, slightly less animal than it was when it walked in.
