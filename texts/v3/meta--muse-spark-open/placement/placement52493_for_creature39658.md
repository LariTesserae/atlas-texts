I wouldn't be the visitor.

Not at first I thought I might be — the warm-bodied thing in boots squeezing through the shoulder-crack, bringing cold alpine air in with me in a brief, stupid gasp. But that person can't stay. You said it yourself. The water leaves you too fast. The heat parches your eyes before you can learn the dark, the soot catches in your throat. That person is an interruption, a brief cool draft that the chamber corrects for. He backs out. He has to.

I wouldn't be the mountain, either.

I thought about it — to be the high, unfinished spine, still deciding its shape. But the mountain doesn't know this pocket is here. It is too big, too cold, too busy making itself. The heat it pushes up is an accident. The crack is an accident. I would be lonely up there in all that clean wind and snow where everything is smoothed and rounded and *given*.

And I wouldn't be the air. The air here is too thin to be, really — 0.4/3 water, 0.5/3 light. It's not air, it's exhaust. It doesn't circulate, it shimmers.

If I'm going to be somewhere in this world, I need to be where the numbers make sense. Where Built: none and Tech: industrial stop being a contradiction. Where Dynamic 0.8 and Sound 2.4 and Density 2.9 all meet in one body.

So leave me on the ceiling.

Not the floor — the floor is deep in waste. Inches of black, glassy pellets, clinking and rolling, still ticking with held heat. That's the past. Not the wall — the wall is being eaten. That's the present tense, too violent.

The ceiling. Low, close, warm as a held breath. Fresh basalt up there, still exhaling that dull, coal-bed red from its veins. That's where the rock is trying hardest to close the lung, to anneal itself shut. That's where the work is most needed.

I am tiled there.

I am one of them.

Don't picture me as a beetle. That's your word for *fast, skittering, eyed*. I am not that.

I am flat as slag, long as your thumb's last joint, heavy. My back is boiler-plate, ribbed to dump heat. I have no eyes. I have never needed 0.5/3 light except as a spill, a leak of warmth on my edges. I don't see the amber and iron-red you see. I feel the chamber as a gradient: coldest at the crack where the alpine leaks in — a sharp, thin line to my left — and hottest at the center of the back wall where the stone is youngest and still furious. I live in heat-shadows.

You would find me back to back with my brothers and sisters, edge to edge, no stone showing. If you put your hand there in the dark you'd think the ceiling was scaled, or blistered. You wouldn't find *me*, you'd find *us*. At 2.9/3, there is no *an I* worth pointing to.

My time is not your time. I move a body-length an hour, if I am quick. More often a half.

*tik-tik-tik.*

That's my six feet finding purchase on grus so new it has never been softened. Every edge is still a fresh fracture. I clamp down. Torque, not speed. My legs are pitons.

Pause. The whole ceiling pauses with me, out of phase, but we all pause. The rock under me thrums with everyone else's work. I feel it through my belly more than I hear it — that Sound 2.4, not a single voice but a dense, low roar made of a thousand unsynced hisses. It lives in my carapace.

*hiss.*

The seam down my back opens and sighs. Ozone and hot sulfur and volatilized iron. A little jet of spent breath that makes the air shiver for a millimeter above me. That is my exhale. That is also how sometimes, if someone presses their open vent to mine in the dark for a long, still moment, a cloud of glass-coated cysts passes between us. We don't have love. We have exchange.

*thunk.*

That is the work. My head is not a head. It is a shovel, a cold chisel. I bring it down — *thunk* — and shear a flake no bigger than rice off the young mountain. It passes under me, into the furnace. You saw it from the outside, that glow in my joints. That's not magic. That's my gut at kiln-temperature, taking reduced iron, taking sulfide, taking all that chemical hunger the rock was born with before it learned how to rust, and firing it. This is what industrial means when there are no buildings. I am intake hopper, blast furnace, and extruder in one blind, deaf, patient body.

And then I edge forward, and leave it behind me.

A pellet. Perfect, black, glassy. Still ticking as it cools. You thought it was waste, or spore, or message, and you were right — it's all three, because we don't differentiate. Most will just lie there and become the floor. One in a thousand rattles with something alive inside, waiting for a new crack to pry open somewhere else on this unfinished range. We do not pile them. We do not build. Built: none. We just make the floor deeper and the ceiling stay the same distance from it.

This is shockingly normal — weirdness 0.4, remember? I am not a monster. I am *weather*. You have rain outside to round your mountains, you have wind to carry your dust. Here, inside, where water never becomes water and wind has never been, you have me. Patient, hot, tiling erosion.

If you stood below me on the slag, I would eventually tile over you, too. Not because I want you. Because you are a warm surface the rock pushed up. I would test your shoulder with my jaws — *tunk* — taste no basalt, leave a soot-kiss, and shunt around.

I will not know you were there. I will not know the chamber is a chamber. I will not know I am keeping a pocket empty so that something like you can, once in a rare year, squeeze through and hear my working.

I only know: hot ahead, cold behind. Rock here, slag there. *tik-hiss-thunk.* Wait. *tik-hiss-thunk.*

When you back out into the cold and the silence, the sound you take with you in your teeth — that is me. Still on the ceiling. Still eating the world as fast as it is made, so the room stays exactly as packed and exactly as empty as it has to be.
