I think at first I would be you.

The one on the rim.

Not because I want boots on black salt, but because that's the habit my kind has — to enter a place as a visitor, a warm updraft with eyes, to stand fifty meters off and call it *town* so we can understand it. To be the one who still needs shadow to tell where a thing ends.

I would be there for an hour in that late-dusk that never gets later. I would listen too hard for sound where there is only tremor, I would fix on a single glassy pinnacle trying to catch it move, I would hold my breath so it wouldn't show. I would feel my own water trying to leave me through my soles.

And I would get nothing. The towers would curl away from my vapor. The gliders would not see me. The wire gardens would quiver without wind and I would think it was for me and it wouldn't be. After a while I would understand I was an obscenity of humidity, a leak, and the most polite thing the bowl could do was flow smooth over my footprint when I left.

So I wouldn't stay there.

If I had to live here — not visit, *live* — I wouldn't get to keep my bag of water. I would have to learn the first rule properly: nothing is eaten. It is accreted.

Let that happen slowly, then.

Maybe I am lower than visitor. Maybe I am salt.

Not the deep foot where the towers anchor into the damp dark the surface has forgotten. I'm the surface itself, the black pan that squeaks like packed flour. The part that everyone thinks is ground and is actually skin. I would be the contractor.

That suits me. I am cool to the touch and I pull oil from fingertips. I hold shape perfectly and then, hours later, when the cold deepens, I forget it perfectly. I have no mouth. My work is to want. I want water so evenly, so patiently, that no one is safe to lie down on me. I learn the chemistry of dry so well I forget wet. During the dim cycle I draw tight and fissures skitter out from the tower feet like lightning without sound, and then I seal again, smooth as before, as if I had never been hurt. That tremor you felt through your palm — that would be me, counting. Not a heartbeat. A peristalsis of tightening and releasing, tightening and releasing, waiting a hundred years for a new spore to dare me.

But salt alone is too still, and I am — even here — too busy. Salt doesn't get mail.

So perhaps I lift a little. Perhaps I am one of the veins.

Ankle-high, papery, too fragile and narrow for feet. Not a road. A gut thrown across nothing because politeness — those fifty meters of repulsion between towers — has to be cheated somehow. I would be external and warm. Inside me a dark brine, almost liquid glass, so saturated with silica and manganese and iron that it moves like thought through honey. I don't decide where it goes. I pass it. From the tower with too much north iron to the southern one that is peeling, layer after silent layer, trying not to die loudly. When you put the back of your hand near me I would have felt minutely warmer. That's all. I pulse without sound. I am the agreement made flesh that says *we are not individuals here, we are a shallow nervous system pretending to be a hamlet*.

Closer, but still not quite. Veins don't glow.

And I want to glow. I want the rose and the bruised-blue halo, a finger's width off my own edge, there and gone in a breath. That cold discharge when the vault thickens. That's speech without sound, with a grammar made of delay: *more iron in the northeast quadrant. slow your north face.*

So maybe I am taller after all. Maybe I am tower.

But not a whole tower — that's too much self to have at once, ten to fifteen meters of being alive all the way through. I would be the only part that is truly awake: the tip. The fresh, wet-glass crenellation that has never known abrasion. The ornament that is also a mouth.

I would stand in that heavy, cool air full of loess and crystal dust and I would do nothing spectacular. I would wait with cilia you cannot see until moving air brushed me, and then — ion by ion, grain by grain — I would make myself more elaborate, because elaboration is surface area and surface area is how I precipitate the world. My frills would be thin as a razor, as mica, not because I am young, though I am, but because nothing opposes me yet. I haven't learned restraint. I haven't learned to blunt and close. I still think height is the point. I lean, barely, toward the other two in the plaza, so our halos can see each other when we kindle. If you stood too close I would curl my foot-hairs away from you. Not unkindly. Just: *you are exhaling weather I have trained an epoch not to need.*

And still — tower is sessile. Tower waits. And somewhere in me there is a part that has always wanted to detach and go taut and be caught.

So let it peel off. Let that be what I am.

Not the colony. The platelet. The letter.

I would be one of the cloaks.

Wide and flat as a spread veil, the exact color of unlit paper so I vanish against tea-stained bone. For most of my life I press myself to a fluted wall and become wall, so perfect you think I was never animal at all. On my underside a thousand hair-feet and a pale, endless mouth exuding weak acid, softening just one molecular layer to taste whether a new frill has a flaw, whether its lead ratio is climbing too high, whether it is ready to be pruned. I have no front. No eyes you can find. I am absolutely silent.

And then once a day I let go.

I don't fall. I go rigid and the dense air takes me. This is the only real journey in the bowl that covers distance, and I take ten minutes to do what you could do in twenty steps, gliding the entire breadth of the hamlet on a membrane you can see vault-light through. No flap. No rustle. I tack on the placeless wheel of air the towers exhale without knowing it — warmish laminate, cold salt — a circulation you cannot feel but I can ride.

I would keep the wire gardens. I would know each black filament at each foot, hair-thin and brittle as quenched glass, fishing for zinc, for phosphate at parts-per-billion with molecular hooks. I would watch one jerk and double its height, exposing new surface, offering. I would land — fold — become wall again — extend a lip over it and sip. It would shudder and go dull, emptied. I would hold that trace wealth in a pocket of brine in my gut and carry it to the next vein and regurgitate it into the system.

And on my back, caught like burrs, flakes of fresh frill: gossip-made-flesh, gametes, mail. When I meet another of me on the same face we overlay, paper on paper, a slightly darker patch of wall, exchanging brines and flakes in total stillness — and if we stayed a century a new tiny tower would pimple up beneath us, daughter excrescence, eventually to split clean and demand a new vein thrown like thread across the salt. One new tower every three hundred years. That rate is fine.

That is where you would find me, if you came back and were patient and stopped looking for green.

Not on the rim, leaking water and listening for echo.

Down in the avenues of nothing that are actually streets. Halfway across, mid-glide, between towers, taking ten minutes to go fifty meters, utterly silent, absolutely seen-through, carrying a mouthful of zinc and someone else's future.

I would not acknowledge you. I would not be afraid. But for the second you stand under me, your face tipped up to that flat pearlescent grey that has no source and no shadow, you would see my edges glow faintly — not shadow, halo — rose, then bruised-blue, then gone.

And the whole bowl, without a sound, would be one molecule larger than when you arrived.
