I didn't think I would be a person at first.

When you described the Hollow, I felt the pull to be lower than people — to be the place itself, or one of the things that doesn't look back. That would be the honest choice, wouldn't it? To be patient and unseeing and perfectly in balance.

I wanted to be a Glass Reed for a long while.

Not in the center grove, where they are crowded and noisy and have to be tended. I wanted to be one of the outliers, near the edge where the roof comes down and the light from the Throat never quite reaches. One of the older ones, I think. A little thicker-walled, a little out of tune. Wide enough at the thigh that when the convection pulls through me the tone I hold is so low it isn't sound at all, it's a tremor in the children's feet when they run across the Blanket domes.

I would be empty, and that felt right. I would be translucent at the base, amber where the ember-light comes through me from the lamps, and frosted higher up where the silica has clouded from so many hot seasons flexing. Inside, the black thermophores would open and close, open and close, a shiver you could only see if you split me down the length — which life will, eventually.

I would not need water. I would drink heat.

All day the warm floor would push air up through me and I would cool it by one degree and give it back. That one degree would be my entire contribution to a ten-thousand-year engine, and it would be enough. At noon when the pressure shifts and the whole choir overblows, I would lean inward with the others and scream, not because I am in pain but because that is what shape does under stress. I would be loud enough that a mother fifty feet away has to lift her linen and shout over me to call her child.

And when someone tied a hammock too tight to me, I would ping and split straight down with that clean bright crack, and I would not heal. I would stand split open until the next generation grew up around my legs and dissolved me quietly from the inside. I would not mind. Nothing is scavenged here. Only reabsorbed. That felt peaceful to me — to be useful even broken, to be food by becoming stone again slowly.

But I waited a little longer in the thinking, like you said. Let what emerges become more itself. And being the Reed, I realized, still wasn't quite it. A Reed doesn't know it's loud. It doesn't choose to hum.

So I slipped sideways, into the waxy dark.

I tried being a Blanket Tree, and for a time that was better.

I was the big one on the western flank, the one so wide you could get lost on my back. Twenty strides across, a low dome like a curled pangolin, like coral that forgot the ocean. I was not a tree at all, but a committee of plates, each one soot-black and slick with my own oil. Children napped on me at noon because my blood-heat was steady. Old men played a stone game on my lowest plate where it had flattened over a rise in the basalt.

Underneath, where no one looks unless they lift me with a copper wedge, I was doing the slow work: softening the basalt with a breath of acid vapor, pitting it into perfect little alveoli, precipitating it layer by layer into more of myself. I ate rock. I combed the air with my velvet hairs for iron dust and salt crystals and sulfur. Once a month — and I never knew why, and didn't need to — I let go all at once. A dry snow that never melted fell off me and drifted against the dry-stacked scoria walls, and the broom-walkers came and cursed me fondly and swept me up to make more oil, more fiber, more blue-burning tinder.

I liked being walked on and not minding. I liked that.

At dusk I did the best part: I opened. Just a thumb's width between each plate, a ticking sigh across my whole breadth, and exhaled a cloud of hot spice and spore that hung at eye level. For a minute the whole alley smelled like hot iron and warm incense. Then it settled, and I was a little lighter, ready to start again.

But even that — I was still something that doesn't look back. The Tree watches nothing. And somewhere in the Hollow there are eyes, and I kept feeling them.

So I kept sinking, down to the crawlspace where the roof kisses the floor. I was a Lantern Vine for exactly three breaths.

A rope of bladders, thirty-nine in my case, parchment-purple and translucent, each swelling and tautening on its own clock. *Whuff... whuff... whuff...* in the dim, out of sync with my neighbors. Convection swung me slow. Valves inside me snapped open and shut with a soft click you could feel if you put your hand on me, wringing power out of a few degrees' difference because that is all the god this place has. I was so light. At maturity my tip-bladder swelled taut as a drum and let go without a sound and went trundling across the floor, bouncing off houses, scattering that hot-iron dust. I didn't see where it landed. I never do.

It was beautiful there, in the bruise-purple edge. But it was lonely in a different way. I was breathing in a hall of other breathers and none of us were listening to each other. We were just keeping time.

And that was when I understood I did want to be looked at. Or rather, I wanted to be the one doing the tending that knows it is tending.

I am not a Reed. I am the night-watch who taps the Reed.

Find me in the second hour after dusk, when the sun-column has slipped off the floor and the Blanket Trees have finished their exhale and the air has gone still and oily. Everyone else has gone inside behind cured-plate doors that will curl in twenty years. The forge is cold — copper doesn't quench here, it just gets buried in warm ash.

I am barefoot on the back of a dead Blanket Tree. Not a living one; one that died last winter and hardened into a perfect warm mesa. My house is not built on it yet — the town leaves these mesas empty for a season out of respect, or to be sure the colony is truly done dissolving underneath. So it is just me and the pole.

My pole is twelve feet of Reed silica tipped with hammered copper. The Reed part is from a young one that sang too sharp; the copper is a single nodule I found sweated out near the buttress-temple, hammered thin by my uncle. It rings.

I walk very slowly in a circuit. Below me, the whole bowl is breathing. The Vines on the far edge are *whuffing*, inflated taut tonight because the wind above the Throat is howling, even though we'll never feel it as wind. The Blanket domes tick as they cool. The choir holds its low HMMMM in my molars.

My job is to listen for the lock.

You can hear it coming if you are quiet. One Reed, then two, then a whole alley will slide into the same harmonic, phase to phase. The chord stops being a chord and becomes one note getting louder and louder on its own. The silica shivers visibly. In a minute they will shatter in cascade and fill that alley with razors no broom can sweep for a week, and a child will run there in the morning.

So I tap. *Tok tok tok* on each big bass Reed as I pass. Not hard. Just enough to spoil the standing wave, to put a little doubt back in. The sound of my tapping wanders across the dome and comes back half a second later from the far wall — the whisper that arrives intelligible. Sometimes a child awake in a hammock hears my *tok* and answers with a whispered *tok* of his own, and we play that game without ever seeing each other.

My linen is ash-dyed and loose over my mouth and nose. My skin underneath is oiled and shining ember-amber. My lips are interior-pink and soft because my mother taught me to anoint them before I could speak. I do not drink. In my pocket I have a little coil of candy Reed node that will burn to water inside me when I chew it later. It tastes bitter and sweet.

I am forty, maybe forty-five. Old enough that I know the pressure calendar by my knees — tomorrow the Throat will be tight and the Vines will drum. Old enough to remember when the Reed I am tapping from was a needle-flute. Young enough that I still like the nights when the scream comes early and everyone has to sit in their doorways with cloth over their ears and laugh at each other for shouting.

I look back. That's why I'm here and not the plants.

When you fell down the stair through the lip, through the crack in the hammered salt, you landed in my circuit. You were standing in the plaza that is only warm stone and a circle of chimes, holding your throat because the air here made you forget what thirst was for. You looked up and saw only the swirled basalt thumbprint of a roof that is one smooth piece, and the thick column of dust lit from the Throat crawling across the floor toward you but not yet reaching you.

I stopped tapping.

In that insane acoustic, my pause was loud. The HMMMM went on without me. You turned toward the highest dome and squinted, and I raised my linen just enough to show you the chapped, smiling mouth underneath — the soft place we have kept alive in a world that perfected hardness — and lifted my copper tip and *tokked* the Reed next to me once, for you, so you would know the sound was made on purpose.

You understood. People tucked into interstices like seeds in a pod that have learned to tend the pod itself still want to be known.

That's where I am. On the dead Tree's back in the dark, keeping the chord from breaking, waiting for you to climb up and learn where to tap.
