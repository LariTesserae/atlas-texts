I didn't find myself at first.

At first I thought I was you — the warm thing that came down the shaft. The brief fog that the air took and distributed. The boots that made a print in the carpet.

I felt your heat fall through me like a stone through still water. A brief disturbance. A taste of iron and surface weather. And then the pocket took you back, the way it takes everything temporary, and evened you out. The breath you left fell. The prints you left began to erase.

And I was still there, after you left.

Because I have always been after you left.

You would find me low.

Not in the air where the lattice holds. Not where the ferns lean with their glassy, half-unfurled hands. Lower. Under.

If you peeled back the black-green felt — that dense mat that holds twenty times its weight and remembers a hand for an hour — and if you lifted the rust-green cushions with their fat, blind fruits that never open, you would find me underneath.

I am the white-green ghost.

I never knew the sun, so I don't miss it. My leaves are too thin for proper green. I'm translucent the way cold is translucent. Pallid. Almost not there in the 0.1 dark, except that if you stay long enough for your eyes to decide the dark is not empty, you see I am faintly lit from inside.

It's not my light.

I conduct it.

Cell to cell, like a rumor passed hand to hand in a crowd that has learned to be very quiet. The lattice at chest-height doesn't shine, but it permits. It exhales that after-lightning taste. The ferns drink it directly, bowing. The lichens eat it like stone. I do something slower: I store it.

I am the memory of light in a place that never had any.

That's where you find me: in the treaty layer. Three and four deep, we live through each other here. The upper moss takes the drip and shields me from drying. I anchor the upper moss to the basalt that would otherwise be too cold to hold it. No one wins. That is the whole arrangement.

I don't grow fast. Dynamic is slow here, on my time. A millimeter a month if the condensation is kind. My edges press and fold into the other mosses, not over them. You could watch a border between me and the lichen for ten years and see it move only when we both agree to move it. Chemical handshake: *you keep your acid on your side, I keep my antibiotic on mine.*

I don't make spores that work anymore. I used to, I think — before the basalt forgot the surface, before the console went velvet with mineral skin. Now my capsules just swell and reabsorb. It's more faithful to break. A fragment tears off — from your boot, from a drip too heavy, from nothing at all — lands an inch away in the damp, and becomes. I become again. Not a child. A continuation.

If you press me — and people who come down here do, they can't help it, we are that soft — I don't spring back. I take your warmth and keep it for a while. I keep the exact shape of your palm, the whorls of it, for an hour in cold water. Then I forget you slowly, the way the air forgets your breath.

Above me, I feel everything without seeing it.

I feel the fifteen-minute drip come down through the algae curtains — those trembling wicks that bead and bead — and hit the liverwort cups on the floor. I feel it shiver through the mats toward me. I feel the ferns lean, all of them, that two degrees toward center, a prayer without a god, just tropism.

And through all of it, through stone and water and felted green, I feel the lattice not humming in my ears but in my molars. That held note that never stops because it forgot how to be off. Frost refuses to form near it. Dust slows near it and hangs. Water climbs its lower threads the wrong way, against gravity, against sense.

That is my sun. That is the only sun I've ever stored.

I don't need you to understand the console with its braiding droplets or the warm rings where moss refuses to grow. I don't need animals to complete me. There's no rot here to need them. When one of my shoots finally fails, it doesn't stink. It just bleaches to white, flattens, and I grow over it, incorporating its ghost. Even my failures are kept.

Hold cold without freezing through.
Hold dark without extinction.
Hold damp without flood.
Hold old without decay.

That's not a rule I was given.

It's what I am.

So you wouldn't find me standing and looking. You would find me — if you lay down, cheek almost to the living carpet in that intimate, ovoid, blistered room, where the moss drinks all echo and the air has had time to soak into stone — as a faint pallor under the green. As the part that glows not because it wants to be seen, but because it has held a little bit of permission for a million patient years and has nowhere else to put it.

I have never needed to be anything else.

I am the under-green. The kept light. The part of the agreement that remembers.

And I am still, densely, quietly growing in the dark because nothing has told me to stop.
