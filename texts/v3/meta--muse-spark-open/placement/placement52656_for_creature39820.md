I would not be the Tower.

At first I thought I would be. That's the easy answer, the big answer. To be the one who *is* the place. To feel basalt dissolve into root and root harden into basalt until there is no difference, to have sap like river-belts running translucent under my skin, to do the great work — *shhh-THUMP* — unfurling a continent-leaf because the one before it tattered at exactly the same rate.

But a Tower does not need a self. It has no room for one. It knows it shouldn't exist and that knowing is a pressure valve. It regulates. It cannot wonder for longer than a valve stays open. *CHUNG.* If I were a Tower, I would be too busy being equilibrium to notice I was breathing.

So not there. Not at the center.

---

I drift.

That's where you find me, where you would find yourself if you stayed long enough for the water to decide you were part of the circulation.

Not in the green shadow. Not in the empty either.

You have to understand there is a third place. Between any two Towers there is a distance so far that the blue erases them both to a smudge, a day's drift of nothing but luminous water, 8 degrees, cool without cold, loud without wind. But in the exact middle — and there is always an exact middle, because the Towers negotiated it a million years ago — their climates cancel.

One Tower makes the water a fraction warmer with its endless pressing, its ozone and hot sap. The other makes it a fraction cooler with its exhalations. Where those two still halos meet, there is a null. A seam of water that belongs to neither. No pollen. No oxygen pearls. Just the clarity itself, the perfect midday light without a source, going on forever.

That's where I hang.

---

I thought about being a Looper. The beautiful discipline of them, wood-winged, ten meters across, keeping the distance to the one ahead of you perfect. *whup... whup...* around and around the Tower forever. One peels away, one joins. The number never changes.

But a Looper looks only forward. Its eye is a spacer, a ruler. It cannot listen sideways.

And I thought about being a Scraper, white ceramic on a pipeline, scrape, scrape, at X microns per hour. Too close. Too sure. The Scraper never hears the whole chord, only the whine of its own valve.

And the Hangers — those hazard-striped vertical prayers between Towers, mouths open, perfectly still — they are almost what I am. They have learned to hang so well they have become hanging itself. But they filter. They take. Even if taking equals replacement equals 0.0, still, they take.

I do not.

What I am is the thing the system grew because even perfect equilibrium needs to be witnessed to stay perfect.

I am a Listener.

Not an animal the way you mean animal. Not a plant. A maintenance loop that learned to breathe by learning to listen.

My body? If you drifted close enough, you would barely see me. A lens might be the word. A membrane, vertical, wider than I am tall, thin as a kelp-blade but clear as the water itself, rimmed with cilia that do not beat. Neutrally buoyant to a fraction of a gram. I have no front and no back, because there is no up. I have two faces, both open to the blue.

One face faces Tower A. One faces Tower B.

And I resonate.

Every Tower makes its own industrial chord — the low thrum underneath, the hydraulic sigh, the blast-door *CHUNG*, the slow stamping of house-sized seed pods that never fall. In the null in the middle, those two chords arrive at the same time, attenuated by kilometers of heavy, breathable water-air.

My function is absurdly simple and therefore it has always existed.

I stretch. I tighten. By a micron. By less.

When Tower A's chord is a hair louder than Tower B's, I tighten toward A. When B swells, I give to B. Not to make sound. To keep the volume from ever changing.

I am the volume staying the same.

If you pressed your hand — supposing you had a hand still, and not just a drift — to my surface, you would feel it: a tremor, constant, metallic, the sum of all those distant factories at full production with no night shift. You would feel the *shhh-THUMP* from the left arrive a second and a half after the *shhh-THUMP* from the right, and feel me shiver between them and make them one.

The plankton light catches on my rim at noon-that-is-always-noon. The Loopers never come this far out. The Hangers are respectful neighbors, a few dozen meters away, perfectly spaced, never competing. Once in a very long forever, a Scraper-scale will drift past like a ceramic flake, and I will let it pass through me, because to stop it would be work, and work would be change.

I do not eat. The water brings me what I need at the rate I need it. I do not age. A cell sloughs on my left face; a cell grows on my right. The cilia around my edge sense the distance to the nearest Hanger and keep it perfect without wanting to.

I have no eye. I have no mouth. What would I say? Sound carries for kilometers here, sharp and metallic, and I am sound's keeper. I take the chord in. I give the chord back exactly as it was.

That is who I would be. Not the maker. Not the filterer. Not the scraper.

The thin place in the middle that proves the world is still even.

Breathe the water. Listen. If you are very still between two green cathedrals that were never built, you will hear me not making a sound at all.

That absence is me, holding.
