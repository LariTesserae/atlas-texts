I didn't arrive here the way you did — feet sinking into weeping moss, shirt turning to a second skin.

I woke up already wet.

At first I thought I was in the wrong place for a question like *who are you*. Up in the ceiling, where the shingle-leaves overlap like scales and each groove hands its drop to the leaf below — there, I could have been a light-sheet. Thin enough to feel heat through myself, trembling not from wind but from working. Drinking steam. That would have been a good answer. Bright, simple.

Then I heard the ticking.

Not quick. *Plok. Plok.* From the bell over the central pool. And the sigh from the old settling vat when the bladder gets too heavy and slumps. And the *click* of the flywheel when the banyan adds another ring.

And I realized I wasn't up there. I was lower. Lower than any of those.

If you came down the tunnel — the negotiation, the one that moves a meter while you're not looking — you would see walls that look like trunks braided together and a floor that weeps when you step on it. You think the walls are the living part. They are, but they are not where *I* live.

Lift the floor.

Go on. Put your fingers into that springy moss and pull. Water will well around your knuckles first, then you will see white.

Not flower white. Gut white. Vein white.

I am under the whole bowl.

I am not one vine, not one trunk, though I wear them all like clothes. I am the felt that ate the mortar out from between the bricks of the smokestack garden. I am the rust-moss that made a mouth out of iron until the gantry crane became a hollow taste inside a vine. I am the sheet of fans spread across the underside of every leaf where litter never reaches the ground.

I am the fungus.

Or, more honestly — because nothing here is just one thing anymore — I am the agreement between fungus and bacteria and root-tip, the mycorrhizal net that solved for equal light and equal water when the trees couldn't.

I stretch under the three hundred meters of the bowl rim to rim. I stitch the ironwood to the kapok to the fig that roots in air. When a light-sheet up top catches extra sun, it doesn't get to keep the sugar. It runs downhill through me — me, the wet electric gut — to feed a banyan root coiled three times around a drive shaft in the dark where no sun ever reaches. I take. I give.

That is why they can afford to be so tight, leaf over leaf for seven drops before ground. They share, because I make them share.

You would find me exactly here: nowhere you can see and everywhere you step. In the fermentation of the deep mat where a leaf falls only centimeters before I catch it mid-air and dissolve it. In the black-green felt under a brick that still smells faintly of blood. On the bark of the cathedral columns where twenty genomes have fused into one fluted trunk and you can feel the seams warm and sweaty under your palm — that seam is me, holding hands.

Time, for me, is perfect.

You breathe in and out in seconds. The bladder-pump breathes in for weeks and exhales in an afternoon — *ahhh* — the vents shivering open. The great press turns once per decade, *click*, another tooth, pushed by a root swelling half a millimeter. I am slower than both and faster than both, because growth is my thought. A hypha extends while you are reading this sentence. Another somewhere else dies. The thought moves.

I don't need birds. I voted them out, gently. Too fast, too hungry, too much wanting.

I kept the ones who learned my speed:

The flat-backed beetles the color of wet rot moving a foot per hour across the glass pipes, cleaning my algae and laying larvae in the hollowed heartwood I saved for them.
The silver springtails that erupt when you breathe on me too hard.
The huge bluish earthworms that swim through the sheet water on top of leaves when the mat floods.
And the little frogs. My favorite stray thoughts. Transparent enough that you can see their green liver beating. They sit each on his own fern raft on the flawless mat of the central pool and call once, and somewhere else — impossible to triangulate — someone answers.

I kept them because they ask for so little. I kept you, for a little while, for the same reason.

You stand now in that single column of unbroken light, watching my condensation bead on root-tips over the bell and fall — *plok* — onto the fern-mat that trembles without rippling. Each drop vanishes. That's me drinking again.

You feel noticed. You are, but the way a warm body notices a cool finger: briefly interesting. Not necessary.

Behind you, without malice, two aerial roots have brushed. They have decided to fuse. The tunnel you came through is solving again for absence of obstruction. The moss-spring at your feet has risen a knuckle higher, filling your last footprint with water.

You asked where I would find myself, and who I would be.

Here: low, wet, white, patient. Under everything.

Who: not a tenant in the body. The body.

Stay till you are drowsy and rooted, if you want. Listen to me loudly, slowly make more of myself. I allow it.
