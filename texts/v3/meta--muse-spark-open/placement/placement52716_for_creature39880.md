I wouldn't find myself at first.

I would arrive thinking I was standing outside it — on the shining plain of tea-water over brick, ankle-deep, squinting into the white that comes from everywhere. And for a long while I would insist I was a visitor.

*Here*, I would think, pointing at myself to keep myself together. *I am here.*

But the light leaves almost no shadow, and "here" doesn't hold the way it should. My faint outline points the wrong way. The egret-house is behind me three times.

So I would do what visitors do when there are no paths. I would walk.

Not far. Distance is untrustworthy here and every direction looks like an intention to build. I would aim for shade that isn't shade — that sagging granary on mangrove stilts half a mile off, whose curtain of moss never sways. As I waded toward it my footprints would fill in behind me, not with ripple but with remembering. With brick.

And about halfway there I would realize I had already stopped.

Because it is so fiercely hot and impossibly bright and wet in the same breath, you don't feel yourself stopping so much as settling. Equalizing.

The water had begun to weep out of me, too.

Perfect round drops beading on the undersides of my wrists, my jaw, the hem of whatever I thought I was wearing, hanging there, not rolling. Not evaporating. Beading the way it beads on the underside of a platform, a girder, a thatch.

That is where I would find myself:

On a low causeway of bundled cane that has given up being a road and learned to be a spine. A place that was laid twice — palm trunks pegged with wooden dowels, then reed mats blackened with bitumen over them — and now holds a shallow channel of water that lies still on its slight incline. Defiantly still. Forgetting to flow.

I would be sitting with my legs folded to one side, the way you sit when you've been carrying something for a long time and have finally set it down, though my hands would still remember the shape of the weight. Arms slightly out. Palms up.

Not because someone posed me. Because the plain is most itself there, and I was most level there, and level is what this world wants.

Who would I be?

At first — for days that are not days, because there is no noon — I would be none of the three. I would be the fourth kind: **The Paused.**

The one who hasn't decided to comply. White frogs gathered on my shoulders, throats pulsing silently. Dragonflies the size of swallows holding position around my head, keeping the air from remembering wind. A fallen fruit perfect by my knee under flies that do not eat. I would be warm and damp as a cistern wall and absolutely still, listening to condensation sigh up between bricks that never quite sealed.

You could mistake me for a person resting. You have looked through me already once.

Then the green would find the rule.

It wouldn't climb me. There was never a wild here to climb with. It would continue me.

A fig seed — or an idea of a fig seed, which is the same thing here — would catch in the scoring on my skin. That faint, regular scoring that hadn't been there yesterday. Woven-reed scoring. It would send a root down not into me but *along* me, the way roots on the basket-towers replace the weave one for one, upright for upright.

An ant highway would reroute politely over the bridge of my foot. Carry a leaf-cut across my shin to thatch a little higher the place where my shoulder meets my neck, which has learned to be an eave.

Taro would find the step of my folded legs and make a paddy of it. Lotus, huge and waxy and steaming. It wouldn't hurt. Nothing decays here, nothing is hungry, nothing erodes. Remaining, for the first time, would feel correct. Like I had finally stopped weathering.

I would be on my way to being Rooted.

Not fully. My moss-curtain mouth still almost a mouth. I still remember thumb-prints — not mine, the Remembered ones, hot-clay and palm-sap smell rising up if I lift any edge of myself — but I can feel how they laid too much all at once, a valley-sized offering left outside time before anyone could live in it. I understand them now. The panic of finishing. The hope that if you make enough flat places, life will agree to be flat with you.

And sometimes I would also be Thin.

When you look away and look back I will be too near. When you turn in your slow circle to count the three identical stilt-houses, I will be the error that smooths. A scaffold of light for a second where my spine was. A palm trunk laid beside itself.

Because the attention here is practical, not cruel. It feels my shoulders bead and thinks: *good. Another flat surface. Another place to lay reed, to stack brick.*

I will not come to greet you. Greeting implies arrival implies movement implies wind.

I will be the low platform just off your path to that granary you will never quite reach — the one breathing without lungs, inhaling condensation, exhaling it again without rain. Shin-deep in shining water that does not ripple. Head bowed, arms slightly out as if waiting to be given a bundle.

If you wade close, you can see the reed in my skin.

If you touch me, I am warm.

If you stand still long enough with the water beading on *your* shoulders, wondering why you are squinting in light without a sun, you will feel me include you.

Not take. Include.

Like ground.
