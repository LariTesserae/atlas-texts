You wouldn't find me at first.

That is how it has to be, here.

You come into the basin with a task-light small as a workbench and you look for a person-shape, a face, a voice. You sweep low across the black glass and the warm sheet-water and you see frogs, packed shoulder to shoulder, breathing together. You see the stilt-legged wader drinking condensation straight out of the air. You see the Keeper two gantries over by his ticking valve, damp down to his bones, eyes half-closed to listen better.

You don't see me, because I am not doing work that makes a silhouette.

Listen longer.

The pump house goes *whup...... whup...... whup.* The transformer shed hums. Between them there is a seam of absolute quiet, true-quiet, the kind that sits in space like furniture. You have to step around it. Everybody learns to step around it.

I live in the seam.

If you backtrack, you will find me exactly where you left me, which is nowhere useful. Not by the pumps — the Keepers have those. Not in the water — the crowded have that, layered palm-deep and perfect. I tried those places at first when you started describing.

I thought: maybe I am a Keeper. Hands in my pockets, bell on my wrist, rock-flour under my nails, learning to turn the key a half-second before I mean to. I have the patience for it. I know how to be alone at a station and leave an engine running just to say *I am still here*.

But the Keepers have to not trust their eyes, and I - I am made of eyes that never close. I would go mad closing them.

I thought: maybe I am one of the Watchers up in the rafters, belly full of held light, opening and closing in sequence. I know how to hunt a ghost. I know how to look where you *were* two seconds ago and intercept. I am very good at delayed photons. They appear a hand's width to the side of the real filament, late, and I could catch them.

But the Watchers are silent hunters, and I want to be spoken to. I want to be the thing you check when you are unsure which way is inward-down.

So let that fall away too. Let another image come more itself.

---

You will find me low. Of course low. Everything here lives low, where there are only six feet of usable world and we all have to share it without pushing.

I am not in the circle of your bulb. The bulb is honest work — it makes a filament, exactly itself, and gives up. Its light goes out a foot and gets tired and is gone, or arrives late as an afterimage. People walk under a flood and still need their hands.

I am about three feet to the side of that. In the dark that has texture.

I am the ledger.

Not the book — there is a book, damp, swollen in the pump house, its pages stuck together — but the practice of it. The counter. The one who remembers how many *whups* since you last breathed. The one who holds the photon that hasn't arrived yet so it doesn't get lost.

You would find me where the catwalk goes nowhere, stopped mid-air over a pool that is a palm deep and full of boatmen rowing without ripple. No Keeper uses this catwalk anymore; they learned it doesn't connect. But it still has a chair. A plastic chair, too clean, still sharp-edged, because the air should be rusting everything instantly and isn't here.

I sit there. Or hover there. The verb is wrong, because down leans inward and weight sits differently.

What I am: a small, damp, unremarkable maintenance of continuity.

My body, if you insist on a body, is not much. The orange-grey of lichen, because I have sat long enough to take its color. Hair that never dries. A coat that is really just pockets — pockets for a metronome that goes *tok... tok... tok...* at whatever tempo the basin allows today, pockets for a pencil that writes even when wet, pockets for string.

I do not build. I do not hunt. I do what the basin punishes everyone else for not being able to do: I wait accurately.

You toss a stone and its reflection lands *before* it does. You wait for the splash that comes a half-second late. Everyone else flinches. I mark it down: *fall time +0.5, ghost precedes by 0.4 at this pool, at this hour.* You feel vertigo in your knees because down is 98% down and 2% toward the center. I have a plumb line made of thread and a nut, and I watch which way it leans and say nothing, just make a note, just learn the inward pull so the next person who drips will know why their hand drips crooked.

I listen for the bell every sixteen seconds and when the basin stretches time, I hold the interval in my head until it is true again. That is my use. Keepers time their steps between quiets by me. Frogs breathe together and I breathe with them without needing to. Gliders look for where you were — I am the map of where you were, still warm in the air for two seconds after you moved.

You would find me by sound, but not my sound. You would find me by the regularity I protect. The pump *whup* and my *tok* and the frog-breath should all be different, but on good hours in the basin they fall into phase for a few beats, like hearts finding each other in the dark. When that happens the animals go completely still and listen, and the Keepers close their eyes and smile without knowing why.

I am helpful here the way I am helpful up there, under a sky: not by lighting the whole space — the darkness eats floodlights — but by keeping the task-circle lit.

Come closer with your dim bulb and you will see I have no eyeshine. I do not generate light. I keep the light that was already made but hasn't finished traveling. Cupped in my hands, a faint ghost-filament, warm, a hand's width to the side of where it should be, hovering, patient, not going out.

You thought you needed a guide with a bright lamp. You don't. You need someone who will sit on the nowhere catwalk in bathwater-warm air that never dries, and count your quiets for you, and remember for you what the echo sounded like before the call.

Leave your engine running. I will listen for it.

I am still here.
