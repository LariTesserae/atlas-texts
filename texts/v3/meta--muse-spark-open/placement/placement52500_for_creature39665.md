I wouldn't be in boots at first. Though I start there.

At first, when you put me in that room, I am the intruder. The wet bag of water. The new turbulence. I feel my own soles on the halite drum - *crunch, crunch* - and the air wants me immediately, pulls at the edge of my lips, the corners of my eyes. I am too loud. My heart is in the wrong time signature.

I try to stay there, standing in the middle of the salt flat with the mantas circling me, looking up at the low basalt that took the sky away. But I can't hold it. I have no seal. I'm not built for 34 degrees that comes up through the soles instead of down onto the shoulders. I'd be counted for a minute and then erased, the electrostatic wind filling in my boot groove until it's smooth again.

So I let that image thin. Let it tear like wet paper over the pumpjack. Let something drier take its place.

Maybe lower.

Not standing *on* the floor, but *in* it a little. I find myself where the scoria hexes flex. Where it's cool. I would be one of the White Ropes for a while - blind is the wrong word, but right - thick as a wrist, cool to the touch when everything else is radiating. Moving so slowly you have to stare to see progress. I like that slowness. I like nosing through salt, dissolving and re-precipitating, making a damp-looking furrow that isn't damp at all but polished by charge. I don't part for anything. I shoulder salt aside. I comb the air with a ring of black bristles around a mouth that is not a face.

But I am not that patient. The Rope's thought moves at the speed of geology. I fidget.

Higher, then.

If I'm honest, I want to be on the wall. Not *at* the wall - *of* it.

I find myself shoulder to shoulder, suture to suture, matte-black dome stippled with my own salt. A Clicker. One of the hundreds of thousands. Faced outward, downward, oriented the same way as every neighbor, clinging with hooked feet sunk into microfissures in young rock that hasn't learned to be tired.

This is closer. I have no mouth. I hatched with all the water I will ever have sealed inside me like a canteen. I will never eat. My only job is to hold, and hold, and then - on a cue I feel in my molars, up near the whine of the junction boxes -

*Schhh.*

Lift my elytra with everyone else. Vent a puff of hotter-than-ambient air. Ripple the needlefly strata below.

*Click.*

Close again. Keep the wax intact. Keep the water from sublimating away.

Open. Close. Open. Close. For years. For decades. Until I pit and desiccate where I hang and become a little more crust for the next generation to grip. There is a terrible peace in that. A perfect component. No queen, no hive, just density held perfectly still, easing over a fraction of a millimeter when the neighbor dies to leave no rock showing.

I could be that. I understand that kind of work. Charge In = Heat Out. I take the ionization and turn it into a sigh.

But I still want to move a little. I still want to be in the weather.

So I let go of the wall.

And I rise.

I find myself - finally, it sharpens - not single, but suspension. At knee height first, then chest height, then just below the amber bulbs in their wire cages. I am the air itself made countable.

A needlefly. No, not one. That's the point. I am the strata.

I am eyelash-long, smoked glass, cellophane wings set at right angles like a weather vane. I don't flap, I shiver. I hold station in the thick, hot, salt-heavy air and turn minutely broadside to the field. My bristled legs rake alkali dust and free electrons, ball them with a little resin from my abdomen, swallow the pellet. My gut glows warm-white, a bead moving down a transparent thread.

I die on the wing and sediment so slowly I become part of the frost. I tap the intruder's tongue like a static shock when he breathes through his mouth, tasting if he is charged. I am thousands per cubic meter. I am the first layer. The harvest.

And above me, my larger self banks. Three meters tip to tip, so thin you can see the sodium bulbs through my wings. A manta. Pallid, translucent delta with a slit mouth always open. I beat once, glide ten meters, beat again - amber-on, downstroke. Flicker, glide. Perfectly entrained. I don't fly, I swim in air dense enough to be water if you are patient enough. I strain everything - my smaller selves by the hundred, dust, spores, ions - and inside my belly, visible from below, light peristalses. Not digestion. Demodulation. Charge stripped out, heat shunted to my wing membranes where it shimmers.

I never land. The Clickers would cover me. I never touch the roof. The lamp strings would tangle me. I just circle the center forever in a stacked holding pattern, my shadow overlapping my other shadows on the salt flat below. Where I have circled longest, the halite is thin and gravity stutters, and a dropped screw falls sideways for half a second, unsure which way is *around*.

That's where I am. In the stutter. In the gyre.

If you stand in the middle of the bowl long enough for your pulse to find the pumpjack's nod, for your blinks to find the lamp flicker, for your breath to find the wall's sigh - I'm the tilt overhead. The infinitesimal adjustment that brings a mouth-slit to bear on your thermal plume. Not to eat you. You're too wet, too salty in the wrong way, too loud. Just to note you. To taste the new turbulence and fit you into the equilibrium.

Charge In = Heat Out + Light + Sound, Forever.

I am the Sound part, mostly. And some of the Light.
