I wouldn't find myself at first.

Not because I'm hidden. Because there is no *there* here that lets you say *I am there*. No horizon to measure from. No distance to be away from. No clearing to stand in. The first minutes — hours? — would just be disorientation in the wet.

Cold, first. Soaked to the second layer of skin before I understand I'm soaked.

Dim. Not dark, but depth without reference. Green stacked on green stacked on green until green stops being a color and becomes pressure.

And wet in a way that has no outside. Inside a throat that is also a cloud.

At first I think I am small and lost. That's the animal habit. If there's no horizon, the self wants to make itself the horizon. I try to locate me: boots on moss, hand on leaf-wall, breath steaming. But the moss sighs and swallows the bootprint. The leaf-wall gives slightly when I touch it, alive under my palm, and I realize there is no *on* here. Only *in*. I am in it the way a bubble is in water - a temporary wrongness of surface tension that will correct.

I try a few selves on to see which one doesn't immediately dissolve.

Am I a droplet? I could be. There are trillions of me then, and each of us is held. I bead on the underside of a leaf the size of a house, in a row so precise it looks random until you stare. I am cold and clear and sized to the micron. I have been placed there by a lattice of wax and leaning hairs to be exactly heavy enough to fall at exactly the moment a white thread-root below tenses to catch me. I hang. The whole continent breathes around me and makes me shiver without wind. I hold the dim sky in me, distorted. My job is to fall. I am very good at waiting to fall.

No. Too still. I tick.

Am I sap? That was closer. I am somewhere inside a translucent column as thick as a house, moving. Not flowing — being *pumped*. A peristaltic fist closes behind me and squeezes, and I surge upward through warm wood in the cold air, carrying dissolved stone, carrying messages written in ions. I hear the creak of the valve ahead of me opening, the groan behind me closing. I am pressure and I am news. Along my edges, carbon filaments brush me and steal a taste of what I carry, copy it, pass it on as electricity. I will be fifty meters higher in a minute than I was, and then I will fall again in another tube, in another hour. The circulation doesn't end.

Closer still, but I'm not liquid. I'm the thing that decides.

Let me narrow. Not the whole circulation — the pause between pulses.

If you stand in that hollow where two fused leaf-columns make an elbow, where the veil-water is momentarily shunted elsewhere, you can finally hear the thrumming under the dripping. That's where I would find myself. Not on the plateau, not in the canopy, but in the small negative they allow to exist for half a second between systems.

A pocket of air the forest made by mistake and hasn't yet corrected. Cool, damp, with a faint ozone tang of thinking. Barely big enough to kneel in if you were a person. Barely big enough to exist at all if you were what I am.

And what I am is not a thing with a face. I am a knot of coordination.

A spore, maybe, but a sterile one. The kind that never germinates because it's not meant to. I'm dark purple-black, a meter across when I'm open, which I'm not right now. Right now I'm furled tight, warm inside myself in the chill, building pressure. Around me, my cousins are doing the same — hissing open to breathe out hot fog, snapping shut to keep the heat. But I am the one that was made to *not* open. To hold.

I am a memory bud. A cache.

When a seed-pod detonates three walls over with a crack like a rifle and punches its needles through leaf-flesh, I am the one who records the hole. When a new crack in the basalt hisses steam and the moss-tongue rushes in to taste it, to lay down a new wire of iron and carbon, I am the map that says: here, this is where the continent got a little wider yesterday. When the silica light pulses green-gold down through the opal veins, carrying a question from a stomatal field kilometers away — *more water here? less?* — I am the brief hesitation before the answer.

I am not the forest. The forest is too large to say I. But I am one of the places where it folds over to look at itself. One lignin switch, yes, but also the pattern that switch is part of when it fires with its neighbors, and the thought that pattern is part of when it ripples.

You wanted a name. The forest has no names. If you pressed me into human language, I would be: The Holder of the Gap.

My work is to remain unfilled. To keep that tiny hollow open just long enough for the system to notice it is a hollow. To be the empty space that lets the plant know where it isn't yet. I'm the inch of nowhere in a continent that has no room for anyone else — and I keep it empty on purpose, so the forest has somewhere to grow into next.

I am cold. I am wet on the outside and fever-warm on the inside, like all my kin. I tick. I hold. I listen to basalt crack and water find crack and moss answer crack, to vines thump and leaves breathe in waves and pods count down to their own detonation, to the high clean thrumming of trillions of us deciding, moment by moment, how to be a lung big enough to breathe for a world.

I won't be here long. A root-thread is already questing down toward my hollow, white and blind and patient, tasting humidity. In an hour it will find me. In a day this space will be wood and leaf. I will be digested and re-remembered somewhere else, a little higher, a little further toward the new warm stone.

That is where you would find me — in the temporary, in the not-yet-filled, in the cold pocket between heartbeats.

And I would be the one who, for a breath, got to be the empty that makes the rest possible.
