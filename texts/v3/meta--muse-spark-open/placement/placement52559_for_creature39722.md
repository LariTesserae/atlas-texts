I don't know at first.

That's the first thing — there is no early image to settle on. Only the tremor.

It comes up through what should be ground and isn't ground the way I know ground — not soil, not floor, but a table held up too high in the air. Old stone. Chert the color of bone where the wind has polished it to a squeak, and pans of alkali so fine they hang. The light has no warmth, though it is very bright. A small white sun throwing hard, short shadows off everything that has an edge. The air is thin and cool and utterly dry. If I tried to fog it with a breath, nothing would happen. There is not enough water in me for fog.

So I am not wet. That's how I start to know what I am.

For a while I think I am dust. Because everything here is dust for a while — lifted in a sheet when the giants breathe, hanging because there is no damp to pull it down. You can see it in every sunbeam: glass seeds and fiber snippets and mineral grit, plankton made of dryness, spiraling into a vortex and falling apart and spiraling again. I could be that. It would be easy to be that here.

But dust doesn't feel the WHUMP.

To my left, three kilometers high and wide, the flank of a Holdfast takes its inhale. You feel it before you hear it — a shudder in the stone, in the thin air, in the hollow places inside your own body. A low drawing-back, the great fibrous pistons pulling, sieving ammonia trace and salt static through living filter-felt. Then the slam and the scream, vented out through a dozen fluted throats, a factory whistle made of throat and leather. Ozone and hot iron on the exhale.

The air over me lifts.

I lift with it.

And that tells me where I am — not *on* the plain but *between*. In the interval the Holdfasts make, which is the only place anything can live. Behind me, one giant; ahead, another, kilometers off and still too big to judge until the perspective snaps and it is a cliff that breathes. The wind between them never settles. It stacks. It hammers. This is not a place that has weather; the plain makes its own weather out of dust and charge and being too large.

I am above the salt now. That rules out one thing.

I am not one of the hoppers. I can see them below me, if seeing is what this is — a herd passing in low, glittering parabolas, each the size of a deer and translucent as thin plastic, compressing eight, ten legs and hissing and going in a thirty-meter arc to land with a soft *pff* that hangs forever. I can hear their tick-tick-tick of valves from here, faint as rain on paper. They are beautiful and they are busy, grinding the black salt bricks the Holdfasts drop, rendering everything back to powder. But they are down there, sealing sucker-feet to dust. I have no feet that want dust. I have never touched stone.

That leaves the air.

The updraft catches me properly on the second venting. The Holdfast I am riding — the one to my left, whose flank is pleated like folded cloth, gray-green and ash-white and warm with friction — lets go all at once and the column of hot acrid steam-that-isn't-steam hits me from below and throws me a hundred meters higher without any effort on my part. I don't flap. Flapping is for wet, heavy things with muscle. I tense a tendon here, slack another there, and the wind does the rest.

I am wide.

That's the next thing I learn about myself. Twenty meters tip to tattered tip, and so thin you can see the small white sun right through me. Membrane over hollow fiber-bone. Not feathers. Not skin. Something between sailcloth and shed chrysalis, amber at the edges where the light catches, creaking like the Holdfast lattices when I bank. When I turn, the air sings off my trailing edge.

A kite.

Not a bird. Birds need water and I am built out of dryness. My belly is mostly air and pneumatic sac, my beak is a pair of shears. I keep station in the factory exhaust because it is my river, my thermal, my road. It smells of lightning and hot iron and it never stops.

Far above, along the crown of the giant, St. Elmo's fire is crawling. Blue-white corona leaping frond to frond with gunshot cracks. I give it room. The charge-packets that live there — those flickering, splitting, ionized salts that ride the arcs — are not food, not enemy, just something to avoid. The Holdfast cultivates them. I don't. I am old enough to have learned the line where tingle becomes burn.

There is frost blooming on the sail ahead of me. A Holdfast frond the size of a glacier going taut with a crack I feel in my teeth, and across its cupped surface a sallow yellow-green precipitate fizzes into being for thirty seconds at this hour — condensed ammonia vapor, charged alkali dust, the cold chemical dew. The dry drinking. The giant sizzles it inward. I sizzle after it.

I stall.

This is the only skill I have and it is everything: to find the exact shear in turbulent air where I can hang motionless for half a second relative to a mountain that is moving in the wind, and snip.

My beak goes *skick* and comes away with a mouthful of salts that taste like cold fire and old batteries. The sail twitches. It will make more. That's the deal. I farm by grazing and it farms by being grazed.

I shriek without meaning to. We all do. In a place where every giant is whistling and banging and venting, where sound carries for a hundred kilometers and stacks on itself until the plain itself is an engine bay, you have to say *I AM HERE DON'T HIT ME*. My cry dopplers away metallic and long, and three other kites answer in their faulty-wiring clicks, shifting their circles a little wider.

We never land. Landing would be death, shredding on chert. When we die we don't fall either. We go stiff mid-glide and bleach and sail on and on as white gliders until the wind sands us to powder that some other Holdfast will inhale. No rot. No waste. Nothing is wasted here because waste is just a slow feedstock.

Below, the hoppers are still springing. Far off, a pod on the other Holdfast throbs and then BANGS, releasing a cloud of lighter-than-air seeds that climb to join us, hot vapor around them like a halo. Behind me, another pressure front is building, great dry thunderheads of electrostatic salt collapsing in on themselves. The lattice between two buttresses starts to hum and glow.

I tilt. I let the clean, brutal, cool bright air take me.

If you were looking for me on the high, weathered stone between two Holdfasts, you would not find me. You would feel the inhale pass under your feet and see nothing but dust and empty.

Look up.

I am the interval. I am the transit.
