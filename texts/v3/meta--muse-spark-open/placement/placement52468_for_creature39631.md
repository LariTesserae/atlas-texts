I don't find myself standing at first.

For a long time in this forest, "standing" is a thing nothing does. Things cling, and hang, and press, and wedge, and float between layers. Even you, visitor, with your boots sinking into the felted mat, are not really standing - you are being held up by a negotiation of root and rot and sharp young stone that hasn't decided to be ground yet.

At first I thought I knew where I'd be:

Higher. I wanted to be high. Fifty feet up where the air is a little less like breathing through a warm washcloth, in the fork of one of those colony-trees that is not a tree but a committee of wood and strangler and epiphyte arguing about what shape to be. I wanted the forward-eyes, the four-thumbed hands. A Weaver.

I can feel what the logic of that would be - to strip a living vine while it still twitches and glows in my hands, to steal silk from a spider the size of a plate who allows it because I groom her joints. To braid a net that is luminous because it is still alive, to string it across a gap where the drum-wings commute, to wait and listen for that low pressure thrum in my molars and the unresolved two-tone chord of my sisters saying *wing-coming, wing-coming*.

It fits. I am an intelligence that makes disposable tools - a net that will germinate if I don't use it in two days, a thought that will rot if I don't refresh it. I know how to make a perfect, temporary trap for something passing through and then forget it entirely. I sleep piled with others, breathing in unison, owning nothing for longer than an hour.

But the Weavers are too loud, too urgent. They own their hour too fiercely.

Then I thought: lower. The basalt ribs. Black and iron-smelling, still hot in places where the planet shrugged yesterday. I could be a Knapper - *Lithoscuria* - low and dense as cast iron, with one hand that is a hammer and one hand that is a question. Prospecting the chert seam with my fingertips, *tak*, a smoking flake, slice the lantern-fruit, get drunk on fermented pulp, fight, break the blade, leave it for the moss to take. Eight seconds to learn, eight seconds to forget. That is also an honest way to be an intelligence that leaves no monuments.

But the Knappers learn only to steal better. They cuff their young aside.

I settled after that, because you have to settle downward here. This basin is low on purpose - a steaming lowland cup the size of a country, new stone cupping old rot - and everything desirable falls.

I wouldn't be either of the tool-makers. I'd be what they make their tools *for*.

Find me ten meters from your boot, to the left where your footprint light is still fading. Not in the footprint - *as* the light.

No - closer. In the cup.

Up there, fifty feet above your head, where a bromeliad has grown into the armpit of the canopy and made a throat. It has been collecting you for weeks without you knowing - the condensation that forms before the air cools, the silver threads that run off door-sized leaves, the cold shower that falls when a branch shivers leagues away. All of it ends here, in a suspended lake the size of your cupped hands, black and tannin-stained, absolutely still.

That's where I live.

I am the water that is not standing water. The 0.8. The damp that is not wet. I am the axil-lake that holds a complete world for the length of a generation and then overflows.

If I am an animal, I am glass-bellied for a little while - translucent as wet cellophane, ticking like cooling glass, my gut a lantern, my eggs like pearls against my spine. Born and bred without ever touching the forest floor.

If I am not an animal, I am the moss at the lip of that cup, felted meters deep, storing a day I never saw, flaring only when pressed. I do not hunt. I do not weave. I hold.

That is the only kind of residence this place allows. Not settlement - saturation. To be held for a little while between the press of roots and the dissolution of rot, and then to be passed on.

You will step again and I will flare. A cold drop will gather on the leaf above you without a cloud, bead, swell, and fall. It will land on your shoulder and be both my body and my end. A hundred things will race toward the splash of it, knapping and weaving as they run, and be eaten while they do so. I will not mind. I was never building upward anyway.

Look up, through the brief hole your passage tears in the mist. No stars. Only more layers of faint light receding into infinity, as if elevation is just another kind of depth. That is where I am: not high, not low, but in the middle layer, in suspension, luminous only at the edges, and perfectly temporary.
