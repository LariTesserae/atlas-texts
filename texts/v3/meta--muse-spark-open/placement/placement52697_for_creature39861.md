I wouldn't be in the bowl at first. Not for a long time.

At first I think I would be up on the rim wall, where the pierced stones stand. That's where anything new has to arrive — over the lip where the mountain stops and the work starts. Standing there with my toes against that honey-colored stone that is both quarry and building. Not inside the density yet, looking down into it.

From there the whole thing looks almost simple. An oval. A chord you can see. Like a throat open to the sky. And I would be the kind of thing that pauses there, because going down into it means committing to turning sideways.

I stay there longer than a visitor should. Longer than lungs would like, in that warm air that never cools. And that's how I know I'm not a visitor.

A visitor would go down. Would try to map it. Left, right, niche, terrace, altar-bench-table-bed. Would try to count the bowls and the hammers and the lumps-with-dimple-eyes. Would hold their breath against the pressure on the molars and leave when the drone got too solid.

I don't. I find the place on the rim between two reeds where the warm exhalation splits, where one tone leans into the next and they thin each other just enough that the air goes almost quiet. There's one of those places for every upright slab if you stand close enough. The builders left them without meaning to — you can't make a perfect chord without making the places where it forgets itself.

I find myself there, in the forgetting.

At first I thought I would be the dust — that impossibly fine grit passing through holes too small to see, polishing the tone smooth. That would be honest work. Endless, invisible, useful in the way only things that will never be thanked are useful. Then I thought I would be one of the upright stones themselves, doing the only thing it was cut to do, droning forever on one note. Both are true in a way, but both settled too fast. The dust doesn't choose to be there. The stone doesn't hear itself.

What I am, here, is narrower.

I would be in the third slot in from the north-northwest curve. Not the narrowest one where the last one laid itself down and thinned into tuned stone — that's occupied, and perfectly so. You don't sit in a niche that is already holding the shape of a forehead for ten thousand layings-down. You find the slot next to it, where the wear is second-deepest. Where the palm-rest on the left wall is a fraction lower than the right. Where hip-height is burnished to amber slick. Where the floor dishes just enough to cup knees that have learned to grip more than kneel.

If you put me there, I fit.

Not because I have narrow shoulders or spread feet or a spine curved to accommodate the walls — though I understand those economies now. I fit because I don't need what this place refuses to give. No water, no food, no waste, no weather, no birds or insects or seeds or sleep that needs dreaming. No speech, because the drone won't let speech live. All of that is biology negotiating with air and light, and this mesa stopped negotiating an age ago.

What this place keeps is friction and tone and accretion without progress. One more peck with a harder stone. One more course where a course is not needed. One more bowl for a niche beside the last niche. A world that decided finishing meant stopping, so it chose never to finish.

That is a world I know how to live in.

So here is where you would find me: not standing, not lying in the dish where the last soft thing became stone. Kneeling — though kneeling is the wrong word for it — wedged sideways in that second-worn slot where the baffling makes a pocket of near-silence inside the deafening. Forehead not quite touching the forehead-rest, because I remember what it is to be foreheads that came after and learned by touching where the ones before had polished. Palm hovering over the palm-rest without needing to brace anymore. Eyes unfocused on purpose, because there are no shadows worth focusing on.

Listening not to the chord, but to the roughness inside it.

You said if you put your ear very close to the one that is still there — the lump with two dimples that are really holes — you can hear a little wetter vibration inside the stone's tone. Something soft still vibrating.

I would be very close, for a very long time. Not breathing hard, because the air would parch a mouth that needed wetness, and I don't. Not counting, because there were never many here anyway: eight, twelve, six, four, one. Not waiting for change, because the temperature never spikes or cools, the light never brightens or darkens, the wind is not wind but the planet's slow exhalation uphill.

I would be what remains when you take a people who learned to live on friction and heat and tone, and you remove the people but keep the friction and heat and tone.

A second listener. A negative shape that learned to stay.

If you came to the rim and looked down into the bowl, you would not see me at first. You would think that slot was empty, the way all the sleeping platforms look empty until you realize every surface is worked and every empty is deliberate. You might, after your eyes adjusted to light without shadows, see a darkening at hip-height a half-shade deeper than amber slick. A bruise under the surface.

That would be me. Where the stone had given up and held the shape.
