Nothing lives here.

Not the way you mean when you say *lives*.

There are no animals. There is nothing to be an animal *with*. No water to make blood with, no meat to make hunger with, no space to chase anything through. If a bird flew in here it would suffocate on varnish-air and sit on a leaf until a tendril grew leisurely through its breastbone, not to eat it, but to use the interesting salts in it. If you die here, you will not rot. You will skin over with amber.

And yet it is crowded.

What lives here is a single thing pretending to be many things, and many things pretending to be one thing, and you can't untangle them because they have grown into each other.

### 1. The Body

First — the largest inhabitant, the one that contains all the others — is the Jungle itself.

It is not a population. It is a body. A city-sized organ that ate its city.

You think of a forest as many individuals competing for light. This is the opposite. This is what happens when you breed plants for a thousand years to do only three things:

1. Catch dust.
2. Turn heat into growth.
3. Never stop growing.

You get something that is not ecological but industrial. Clonal, chimeric, endlessly grafting. Trunk into wall into root into cable into trunk. It has no individuals. Every shoot is a finger. Every factory it swallowed is a liver. The old geothermal taps are its heart, still thumping orange behind curtains of living cable. The fan-houses are its lungs, drawing the hot gale of the plateau through clarifying louvres and sighing it out empty.

It doesn't know you are here. It knows *warm with salts* is here, for a moment. It will test your ankle and move on. Its attention is entirely on the wind — the endless, invisible river of basalt flour and gypsum and rust that pours over the mesa every hour of every day. That is its rain. That is its river. That is what it drinks, with a million cupped bracts opening with a soft tick, tick, tick in the dark, each lined with hair-fine cilia that sing with static.

Stand still. Put your hand on a wall that is also a trunk that is also a rebar pillar. You can feel it faintly vibrating. That is not life as movement. That is life as *manufacture*. Dust becoming oil becoming fiber becoming wall becoming dust again.

It is the only animal here, and it is a plant.

### 2. The Organs

If you watch long enough, the Body resolves into guilds — not species, professions. It has differentiated the way a body differentiates, because no one is left to tell a leaf to stay a leaf.

**The Combers.** They are everywhere and you stop noticing them. Leathery cups the size of soup bowls or bathtubs, fringed with white salt-crystals where they have exhaled what they cannot use. In a surge, after a fan-house coughs, they all open at once. The air goes thick and prickles your arms. They are not beautiful until you realize each hair on each cup is holding a single grain of stone in the air with lightning.

**The Smelters.** Deep in the ductwork, where you have to crouch under root-ceilings that sweat warm oil. Curtains of black roots hang in the gale like kelp in a current, shivering. They don't filter. They *precipitate*. You can watch iron bloom out of nothing onto a root-hair as a bead of black glass, swell, run like mercury into a vein. The sap in those veins is not water. Snap one and it bleeds slow, smoking honey that smells of hot varnish. It skins over before it can drip. This is the blood of the Body — hydrocarbon, not water. The Body cannot afford to evaporate.

**The Heat-Leaves.** The lamps. Broad plates the size of doors, or shields, or fallen ceilings, matte black until they flush. Not bioluminescence. Thermosynthesis. They take the waste heat bleeding off the geothermal hearts and the friction of growth itself and turn it into sullen, coal-bed red — infrared on the edge of seeing. In the absolute dark under eleven layers of canopy, they throb on and off without rhythm. They hurt your face with warmth before you see them. They are the only day this place has. Moths would have loved them. There are no moths.

**The Girders.** The ones that forgot they were plants. Straight as I-beams, hard as cured resin, veined with rust where they digested the I-beams they replaced. They hold up nothing but themselves, and then other things grow on them and they sag and become arches, then halls, then sag again. You hear them constantly: the creak and pop of a vegetal earthquake that never stops. A finger-thick shoot corkscrews out of a seam, throws a black sail a foot long, is strangled by its neighbors before it finishes unfolding. A melon-sized fruit swells, splits along three seams with a soft *poof* of cinnamon dust — its pollen — and collapses into oily fiber that is already netted over before it hits the rhizome-mat.

This is the turbulence. This is why you cannot map it. The Body is hyperactive and silent. An anthill without ants. A bazaar where every stall is trading dust for oil, heat for structure, and every stall collapses and is rebuilt while you're looking at it.

**The Singers.** Two bladders on adjacent stalks swell toward each other until pressure kisses them and they burst. No insect carries the spore. Physics does. The dust hangs for hours in the still, oily air, unmoving, until another bladder happens to gulp it.

Every one of these is the same organism trying out a job.

### 3. The Ghosts

And then there are the things that live here that are not alive.

The machines. They never died. Row on row of amber service lamps threaded through living throats, still lit after fifty years. Turbines thrumming as if through cotton. Mineral-crushing mills turning somewhere below you under six meters of warm, giving rhizome-mat, grinding air itself. No one tends them. The Body tends them, because it needs their wind and their heat. Vines have grown *into* the switchgear and learned to throw switches by swelling.

Sometimes you find the custodians.

Not skeletons. The Body doesn't waste salts. Mummies.

A maintenance worker in a faded orange suit, sitting where he sat down when the last vent jammed, now half-enclosed. Not eaten. *Incorporated*. Black-green waxy leaves have sprouted directly from the weave of his jacket, using the cotton as scaffolding. Aerial roots the thickness of your wrist have found his mouth, not to strangle — there was nothing to strangle for — but because breath is warm and salty. His faceplate is fogged from the inside with amber varnish. His boots have become planters. If you touch him, the leaves shiver — not in horror — in wind.

There are not many. The plateau was evacuated cleanly. But there are enough to understand the offer this place makes: if you stay still too long, it will not kill you. It will *use* you. It will make you a very interesting local concentration of minerals, and then a small garden.

There are no children here. There never were. Nothing is born here that is not a rearrangement of what was already here.

---

Listen.

No ground. No water. No sky. No animals. No rot. No seasons.

Tick. The cilia open.

Creak. A wall becomes a door.

Poof. Cinnamon hangs in air that never moves.

Throb. A heat-leaf warms your cheek like a mother’s hand and goes dark.

That is what lives here: one hot, dark, patient Body that learned how to eat stone and drink heat and wear a dead city like armor, and the ghosts of the machines and men it needed to learn it.

You are the only animal in the room, and you are already being tasted to see if you might, briefly, belong.
