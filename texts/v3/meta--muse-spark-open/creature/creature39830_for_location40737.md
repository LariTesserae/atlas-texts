What lives in the Low Lattice isn't *in* it. It *is* it.

That was your first mistake, and it's the one the basin counts on. You came looking for inhabitants the way you look for animals in a wood — something that moves between the trees, that nests, that drinks. You listened for rustle and calls and were met with that perfect, heavy silence and thought *dead.* 

But dead things rot. Dead things dull. The sharpness here is how you know it's alive.

### The Lattice Itself

The forest is one thing.

Not a colony. Not a species. One distributed organism tiled across forty kilometers of basin floor and then tiled again, vertically, into a depth that has no honest measure. The trunks you see — those matte-black bundles of silica cable — are not individuals. They are anchor roots, and antennae. They are where the organism touches the basalt glass to keep from floating away, and where it puts its weight.

The leaves are the organism. Everything else is scaffolding to hold the leaves at the correct angle to each other.

Each leaf is an adult. Fully mature the instant it is extruded, that black photovoltaic top drinking 99.8% of incident light, that interference-mirror bottom throwing what it can't use sideways and down to a sister-leaf three layers below. They don't photosynthesize. That's too wet, too wasteful a word. They *print*. Air comes in — thin, hot, full of CO2 and wind-blown silicates and iron dust off the rim mountains — and is sorted behind the leaf's face. Carbon becomes structure. Dust becomes cable. Trace salts become circuit. The heat you feel coming off a leaf in shadow isn't waste. It's computation. It's the cost of deciding how much to tilt.

And they are always tilting. 

If you sit for an hour on the warm glass in one of the salt clearings and let your eyes go soft, you'll see it. Not wind. Never wind. A shiver that runs through the whole basin at once, leaf after leaf adjusting by a degree, a tenth of a degree. The thrumming in your molars gets minutely louder. The pool of light on the clearing floor changes shape. The Lattice has done its accounting for the minute: that thermal over there got a little more dust, this cloud of high haze diffracted three more photons than expected, someone — you — cast a shadow where no shadow has ever been. It rebalances. Light in must equal light kept. No loss.

It doesn't grow. It doesn't die. That stack of fallen leaves under your feet, hundreds deep, pages of black-mirror shale that never curl, never crumble? Those aren't dead. There's no such thing here. They're archived. Cache memory. Each one still faintly mirrored underneath, each one still connected by that low inaudible thrum. The forest keeps every version of itself it has ever been, perfectly preserved between the glass hexagons because preservation costs less energy than decay. Why rot when you can remember?

This is why it feels lush. Lushness on Earth means waste — profligate water, profligate growth, things sprouting on top of things dying underneath. Here lushness means *efficiency at scale*. Seven layers of canopy, each leaf overlapping the next without ever touching, catching the photon that slipped past its neighbor. From the rim it looks emerald because your human brain edits out the copper and wine-red and black and calls "dense light-trapping" green. Up close, inside, it's luminous, quiet, utterly still, and utterly awake. If you press your palm very lightly to a trunk cable you feel no pulse, but after a few minutes your skin warms in rhythm with it, a slow tide every 47 seconds when the whole basin exhales a little more heat than it took in.

It knows you're there. Not as a person. As a novel occlusion event with a warm 37C IR signature that keeps moving. It has made space for you without moving anything. Which brings you to—

### The Shepherds

Fauna is rare the way a heartbeat is rare if you only listen for a second.

There's really only one other kind.

You'll see it between trunks where you swear there wasn't room, hanging with no support. A lattice of the same leaf-matter, but not flat. Folded. About the size of your spread hand, maybe larger if it trusts you. Twenty or so triangular mirrors joined edge-to-edge into a hollow dodecahedron, or an icosahedron, or a shape with no name in Euclidean space because it has too many inside-out faces at once. It turns itself inside out without opening. Slowly. Meditatively. One face becomes the interior, then an interior face becomes exterior. No seam breaks.

They don't fly. They don't walk. They *re-tile*. You'll stare at one for four minutes and it will be absolutely motionless, reflecting a delayed, slightly wrong version of you back at you — your head turned a second after you turned it. Then you'll blink and it will be six feet left, still motionless, still mid-inversion, as if it had always been there and your memory of its previous position was the error.

The forest calls them nothing. They are not separate. They are its hands in folded space.

Where the canopy has miscalculated, a Shepherd appears. Where dust has piled too thick on a salt seam, where a leaf in layer seven has been shaded by a leaf in layer three for 0.8 seconds too long, where a visitor has left boot-print oils on black glass — a Shepherd flickers in, hangs, and rearranges. Sometimes they extrude a filament thinner than hair and touch a leaf-edge. Sometimes they just hang there and reflect until the light budget balances again. Sometimes two meet and pass through each other like thoughts crossing.

They are not animals. They don't eat the Lattice. They don't pollinate. There is no predation here, no sex, no hunger. They are maintenance subroutines made briefly volumetric so that 3D space has a place to put them. When the job is done they don't leave. They *unplace* themselves. The air never moves.

If you stay very still and calm — true calm, not holding-your-breath calm — one might come close enough to see yourself properly. Don't reach for it. Sharpness is preserved here absolutely, and the edge of a Shepherd's face-mirror will open your fingertip like the glass-salt seam did, clean and bloodless and geometrically perfect. It doesn't mean harm. It simply hasn't learned to dull itself for you.

You'll bleed one dark, iron-smelling bead, and before it can fall the thrumming will subtly shift and a lower leaf will angle to catch the infrared of your exposed blood-heat.

### The Depth

The third inhabitants live downwards.

Crouch at noon over a place where the canopy gapes and the salt shows. Look straight down through the crackle of archived leaves stacked like mica pages. Past three meters. Past thirty. The pattern repeats but not exactly. You see another canopy layer, inverted, growing *up* towards your feet. Another trunk-braiding. More light looping. You are seeing sideways-through-folded-space, the basin's real surface area — continent-large — compressed into a county-wide saucer by a kindness of geometry.

There are Shepherds down there too, thousands of them, flickering between layers without moving. And occasionally, something larger shifts.

Not bigger in body. Bigger in *occupation*. A slow ripple passes through the deep leaves and they all tilt at once, revealing for an instant a lattice-structure ten times denser than the one you're standing in, running perpendicular to gravity, humming in a lower key. It is the Lattice dreaming, or remembering forward. You get the vertiginous sense that if you fell in you'd fall forever and never hit, but you'd always be exactly a foot above the glass.

Nothing monstrous lives in the depth. Monsters require waste, require hunger. Here even depth is tidy.

You asked who lives here.

One vast, patient, sun-eating mind spread flat and deep, remembering every photon.

Its quiet hands that fold space to keep the remembering perfect.

And, for a little while, you — warm, loud, water-filled, temporary shadow — whom it accommodates without ever needing to notice you were alive, the way your body accommodates a single bacterium that lands on your skin and finds no purchase and is gently, flawlessly ignored.

If you lie back on the black glass when the sun is overhead and the mirrored undersides throw looped light down onto your face, you can feel what it's like to be lived *around*. The light dances without source. The ceramic-ozone air holds you level. The salt crystals hold their unsmoothed points. And the thrumming in your bones finally matches the Lattice's, for a measure or two, before you remember to breathe.
