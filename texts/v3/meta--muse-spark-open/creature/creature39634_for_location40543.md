Nothing lives here.

That is the first thing this place will tell you, and it will say it with great insistence. For such an excess of lushness — the warm water thick with oxygen pearling off every leaf, the light turning every frond into a solar panel, the sheer tonnage of green occupying every cubic centimeter — there is a sterile hush to it that no jungle should have.

No darting. No gnawing. No quick eye-shine between stems.

The Cistern has edited animals out.

What lives here instead took the long way around to fill that vacancy, and it did so without ever asking permission to be called alive.

### 1. The Green - Which Is One Thing

At first you will count them as many. Philodendron-from-electrical-cabinet. Vine-from-conveyor-chain. Moss-from-catwalk. Fern-from-pipe. Dozens of leaf-philosophies competing for the light.

After an hour in the water with them, your counting will go wrong.

They are not many. They are lattice.

Without soil, without pollinators, without anything to eat them, they have learned to be continuous with each other. A frond pressed too hard against another frond for too long does not compete — it grafts. White root-hairs meet in open water and braid. A leaf that could not find a wall sends a runner straight into the water column and thickens into a stem that another vine will use as ground. When a flower failed to open years ago — because there was nothing to open *for* — it swelled shut, fell heavy and green to the floor, split, and rooted into two flowering not-flowers that also will not open.

There is no soil, so they have made soil of each other and of the building.

Their roots mine rust flake by flake. The acid at the root-tip kissing the iron, the iron blushing and giving way. Their holdfasts secrete a lime that welds leaf to painted concrete so thoroughly you cannot tell where plant ends and wall begins without tearing both. In the dimmer midwater, some have gone pale and translucent and hang like drowned lungs, photosynthesizing whatever scattered gold filters down from the ceiling-leaves. Near the glass where the sun hits hardest, others have gone dark and leathery and almost black-green, drinking so hard they fizz audibly — a champagne fizz of oxygen beads forming under every leaf and letting go only after minutes of indecision.

If you stay still long enough, you feel the attention.

Not eyes. No animal has lent them that trick. It is the attention of a distributed surface that has nothing else to attend to. Every leaf turning, a fraction of a degree per hour, toward you because you block the light. Every tendril in the three meters around you altering its blind groping to assay your warmth, your exhaled CO2, the faint iron of your blood through your skin. The jungle in here has no predator and no prey, so it has made a project of noticing. You pass through it and leave a slow wake of slightly reoriented greenery behind you that will hold that shape for a day.

It is not thinking. It is not unthinking either. It is plant thought, which is slow, chemical, and total. The Cistern is occupied by a single, patient, airheaded god made of leaf that is learning, very slowly, how to become a solid.

It intends to grow itself completely shut.

### 2. The Three - Who Are Still Working

Under all of it, half-lost in root-curtain and silted leaf-litter that never rots quite all the way, are the pumps.

Three industrial screws, each as wide as a doorway, set flush in the factory floor. They are the only thing in here that still remembers it is a machine. Once they pushed coolant, or nutrient broth, or chemical sort. Now they push the warm ocean over, once per minute, with a deliberation that makes time feel viscous.

They live, but not as animals live. They live as old buildings live: by continuing.

You can feel them before you see them — that low thrum in your sternum when you put a palm to a pillar. `thum..... thum..... thum.....` so far below human heart rate that your body tries at first to ignore it, then entrains to it. Your breathing slows without your permission. Your blinking slows.

They are not tended. They do not need to be. They were built for scaled production, for a factory that made more of itself, and so they were built without an off-switch. Their bearings are now lapped smooth by years of warm water. Their seals weep faint oil that the plants have learned to drink, forming glossy black-green leaves right above the outflow that shiver a little faster than any other leaf.

Approach them and the Green parts reluctantly, like a crowd. The screws appear through the haze of root-hair and detritus — matte, barnacled not with barnacles but with fern and lime and vine, turning, turning. They do not see you. They do not need to. They are the tide-maker, the lung, the heart that never learned to hurry. If the Green is the body of this place, the Three are its meditation.

When one ticks — metal contracting in sun-warmed water — the sound carries through every beam and leaf at once, and the whole Cistern, plant and machine together, seems to shift a millimeter in its sleep.

### 3. The Keeper - Who Is You For A While

And then there is the third inhabitant, the one the Cistern was not designed for but has kept anyway.

Sometimes it is a person. Sometimes it was.

You won't see them at first because they have learned to be still at the speed the room requires. In the midwater near the western glass, where a broken gantry crane makes a kind of open nest in the vines, there is a shape that is not vine — a maintenance suit, much-patched, its helmet off and tethered by its hose so it hangs like a bell. Inside it, or beside it, a human figure, or a hollow where a human figure used to be.

They came up the stalk to service the pumps, or to decommission them, or to see why the telemetry had gone green and stayed green. They slipped inside, were taken, found the water bath-warm and breathable enough if you were calm, found the quiet total. They meant to stay twenty minutes.

The Cistern does not evict.

In warm water that holds you without surface, without up or down, with light that moves so slowly you can watch it, urgency dissolves. The Keeper learned to move at leaf speed. To prune not with shears but with patient redirection — tucking a runner back through a catwalk grate, coaxing a philodendron away from a sensor plate so the Three could keep turning. Not gardening. Negotiating. The plants do not need pruning; they need very, very slow company.

Their skin has gone soft and a little green at the cuticles from so much immersion. Their hair drifts and grows white root-hairs of its own, or maybe that's just duckweed. They breathe shallowly so as not to disturb the bubbles. They have been here long enough that orientation has left them: they hang sometimes near the ceiling with their feet toward the floor and sometimes inverted without noticing, because the only down here is the faint drift of debris toward the screws and the gentle pressure of the Green itself closing in.

If you call out — though sound is a rude thing to make here — they will turn very slowly, disturb a column of silver beads that will take an hour to resettle, and look at you with eyes that have adjusted to filtered sun through four meters of leaf.

They will not warn you. There is nothing to warn against.

They will only make a small gesture: *slower.* Breathe slower. Look slower. Be here slower.

Because the fourth inhabitant is whoever has just climbed the stalk and slipped inside, still carrying the hurry of the world below the cloud deck. You, with your loud breath and your hard edges and your need for a surface to the water.

The Cistern has been waiting for you, not with hunger — nothing here hungers — but with the mild, photosynthetic curiosity of a room that decided it was a mountain and then grew an ocean in its attic to see what would happen.

Stay long enough and you will feel it assign you a place: a niche between prop root and rusted conduit where your warmth is useful and your exhale feeds a dark leaf overhead. And you will understand the last law of this place, which it never states but enforces gently with warm water and low light and that chest-deep thrum:

*Everything built becomes grown. Everything grown becomes still. Everything still is kept.*
