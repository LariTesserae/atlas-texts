They live here by not needing to live.

That is the first thing you have to understand about them. Nothing in the High Suspension struggles. Nothing strives. Striving would be change, and change is the one thing this world has edited out.

So what you see, when you learn how to see it, are not animals. Not plants. Not in the way you know those words. You see *maintenance loops that learned to breathe.*

### The Towers — The Ones Who Are the Place

Start with them, because everything else lives in reference to them.

A Tower does not inhabit the High Suspension. A Tower *is* a habitation. You approach one and your brain tries to make it a tree, then a mountain, then a factory, and fails at all three because it is all three at once.

At the base, a kilometer-thick column where root and basalt have negotiated a truce so old neither remembers who is holding up whom. The bark is not bark. It is depth — peat and living wood and cork and resin laminated ten meters thick, fissured into canyons that have their own still climate. Water has weathered it the way wind weathers stone on your world, until the windward side is smooth as river-rock, the lee side furrowed like old skin that has never stopped healing in the same shape.

Higher, where the stalk narrows, the trunk goes translucent. And you can see inside.

You think you will see wood. You see industry.

Sap moves on vertical belts, black-green ribbons the width of rivers, driven by pressure so even it has no pulse. Stomata the size of blast doors iris open — *CHUNG* — exhale a pearl of oxygen that hangs, perfect and unbothered, in the water, then *CHUNG* shut again on a count you could set a clock to if clocks existed here. Seed-pods the size of houses are stamped on one branch, conveyed along an aqueduct-limb, filled with hot resin from a pipeline that whines, sealed with a sigh of wax — and then held. Not dropped. There is nowhere to fall. They hang on the mother-branch until their buoyancy shifts them aside to make room for the next one, which is being stamped *exactly* now.

The Tower does not grow. It *manufactures its own maintenance.* A frond the size of a county unfurls with a hydraulic thump you feel in your teeth — shhh-THUMP — not to reach light, there is light everywhere, but because the frond before it has been weathered to tatters and the rate of tattering equals the rate of unfurling. 0.0

It hears itself. That is its equilibrium. The Tower knows it should not be possible — a kilometer-thick plant that builds cities out of its own leaves in mid-water without soil — and that knowing is built into the pressure regulation. It doesn't exist. Therefore it must keep doing exactly what it is doing to continue not-existing correctly. If it ever stopped to wonder, it would vary. So it doesn't stop.

Pollen pours from its crown in a slow, endless yellow extrusion, like smoke from a stack in a windless world. It goes nowhere. That is its function. It hangs there until something comes to filter it, because filtering is also function.

### The Attendants — The Ones Who Keep the Hum Even

They were never wild.

**The Loopers.**

First you hear them, then you see them. A faint addition to the factory chord: *whup... whup... whup...*

They come around the Tower's shoulder in a school so geometrically spaced it looks choreographed, until you realize choreography implies a choreographer. These are mantas but not mantas — wings made of a living wood-keratin, veined and semi-rigid, ten meters across, flexing with the slow metronome of a bellows. Their undersides are open mouths, baleen made of fine root-hair.

They don't hunt. They loop.

The same oval, around the same Tower, at the same speed, forever. The pollen cloud extrudes; they pass through it; the cloud thins exactly as much as it was extruded. On the far side of the Tower, a Looper peels away into the blue emptiness and is gone. At that exact second, far on the nearside, another Looper joins the loop from the blue. You will not see where it came from. You will not see a birth, or a death. The number remains.

If you follow one closely, you see its eye — large, black, without a lid to blink. It is not looking at you. It is not looking at the Tower. It is looking at the distance to the Looper ahead of it, keeping it perfect.

**The Hangers.**

Between Towers, where the world returns to empty, luminous blue, you will find them by almost not finding them.

Stop drifting. Be still. Let the water hold you.

A vertical line resolves, ten, twenty meters long, striped hazard yellow and black. A filter-worm, thicker than your body, hanging mouth-up, mouth-down — it doesn't matter, there is no up — utterly motionless. Bristles around its maw filter the industrial circulation, the fine plankton sparks, the stray pollen pearls that have drifted too far from any Tower to be looped.

It does not sway. The water does not move it because there is no current to move. It hangs because hanging is its shape of equilibrium.

You can drift within arm's reach and it will not flinch. Not because it is unafraid. Because flinching would be expenditure without replacement. Its gut processes exactly as much as its cilia collect. Its length neither grows nor shortens. Erosion has polished even it: its stripes are weathered, its head is blunt and pitted like old stone.

There are hundreds between any two Towers, each alone, each exactly far enough from the next that they never compete. They have hung there forever. That is not poetry. Their tissue turns over without aging. A scale sloughs; a scale grows. 0.0

**The Scrapers.**

You have to be close to the bark to see them, inside those translucent pipeline branches.

Crab-things. Not crabs. Shells like unglazed ceramic, white and faintly ringing when they tap. Six, eight, ten legs — the number varies but the function doesn't. They walk the sap aqueducts inside and outside the Tower, where mineral scale precipitates from the endless refining — sugar to resin to wax — and clogs the valve-lips.

Scrape. Pass to the next valve. Scrape.

The scale grows at X microns per hour. They scrape at X microns per hour. If you watch one crab for an hour, you will see it move the length of your hand and yet the crab will still be in the same place, because another has taken its previous valve and it has taken another's. The pipeline never blocks. The crab never hungers, never molts into something larger. Its ceramic shell is pitted and re-glazed by its own secretions at the same rate.

They do not decide. Decision is variation. The scale appears; the leg moves. The loop closes.

### The Plankton Light — The Ones Who Are The Medium

And then there is everything too small to be a who, which together is the loudest who of all.

The water between is not empty. Hold still and you see it: sparks, motes, oxygen pearls, pollen grains, sap droplets, skin flakes from fronds the size of continents, all suspended, all not falling. Each one catching the diffuse midday light and throwing it back.

This is not debris. It *is* the population. Bacteria that refine, algae that press, ciliates that beat once every ten seconds exactly, because beating faster would stir the water and stirring is forbidden.

They make the smell: green and hot, ozone and crushed leaf and machine oil that is not oil but hot sap. They make the sound, too — not the big THUMP-clangs of the Towers, but the pervasive thrumming underneath, the factory-at-full-production hum that never spikes, never dips. The sound of a billion tiny valves opening and closing in statistical unison so perfect it becomes a tone.

---

None of them are happy. None of them are sad. Emotion would require a before and after, and there is no after here. There is only *during,* stretched infinitely.

If you stay long enough, your breathing will slow to match the water. Your own buoyancy will feel perfect. You will understand that you, too, are now an attendant — the one whose job is to witness that the volume does not change, that the chord does not vary, that the droplet you watched travel for an hour never arrived because it never needed to.

That is who lives in the High Suspension: a forest that became a continent that became an industry that needed no workers, and so grew its own keepers out of its own breath.

They do not live *in* equilibrium.

They are the way equilibrium lives when it has enough water to forget that it was ever supposed to fall.
