It doesn't live *in* the city.

The city is what lives.

### The Lithovore

Beneath you - kilometers beneath you, deeper than foundation or bedrock - is the Builder.

You will never see it. No expedition has cut deep enough, because cutting here is not cutting, it is wounding, and the stone closes.

But you can feel that it is one thing.

Not a colony. A continuum. A mycelium the size of a province, white and cool and dry as marrow, threading through basalt like roots through soil. It does not eat light. It eats rock. It digests the plateau and exhales it, grain by patient grain, the way a coral exhales a reef or a kidney makes a stone.

This place breathes because it breathes. You stand on its back.

The pavements that sigh cool, odorless drafts up through their pores - that is inhalation. The channels under the avenues that hum when the wind passes over - those are bronchi. The towers, the ziggurats, the colonnades that lead nowhere - they are not buildings. They are lungs, gills, radiators, fruiting bodies. Structures for exchanging mineral for charge, mass for thinness, solidity for altitude.

It is old enough that growth and erosion have reached an agreement. It pushes a new terrace up a millimeter per decade on the west side while the wind sands a balcony to nothing on the east. Nothing is constructed. Everything is accreted, sweated out, like salt.

And it is mostly asleep. Asleep the way a continent is asleep.

It does not know you are there. Your weight is less than dust on its skin. Your footsteps do not register. If you put your ear to the rose-veined salt in the deepest cistern at midnight - when the laminar wind pauses between breaths - you can hear it anyway: not a heartbeat, but a settling. A faint *tick... tick...tick* of concentric growth rings adding themselves inside stone. The sound of a mountain remembering how to be a little taller.

It is the only thing here that belongs here.

Everything else is a guest, a parasite, or a mistake that learned to stay.

### The Gardeners

They look like inhabitants first. They are not.

Think of tortoises made by a potter who had only ever heard a tortoise described. Low, domed, the size of a serving platter, the size of a manhole cover. No head, no tail in any functional sense - just a continuous skirt of salt-crystal lip that drags on the pavement. Their backs are the same rose-veined salt as the towers, flaking mica, warm from within though the air is always cool.

They have no eyes. They have no mouth you can see. If you crouch for a long time and watch one, you will see the fringe underneath pulse, laying down a warm, pinkish paste that hardens to stone in an hour.

They are not smoothing the city for you. They are not repairing damage. This is grooming. Preening.

A bump in the boulevard that offends the Lithovore's sense of airflow - smoothed.
A pore choked with windblown grit - cleared and re-sealed with a dab of fresh mineral spittle.
A hairline crack that grew a hand-span overnight because you remembered it wrong - filled, lovingly, from the inside.

They move at the speed of ice. A meter an hour, if motivated. They cross a plaza in a day. If two meet, they do not acknowledge each other. They simply go around, leaving two faint, parallel grit-trails like snail tracks in dust, slowly effaced by the dust devils.

And they are tireless and stupid and utterly peaceful, until one is overturned.

You find one on its back sometimes, its underside exposed - not flesh, but a hollowed-out geode of densely packed white filaments, dry and cottony, humming faintly with static. Something has scooped it clean with surgical precision, leaving the empty dome like a bowl.

The Gardeners do not react to the empty domes of their kin. They just go around them too.

They can wait forever. If you stand in one's path to see what it will do, it stops. It does not try to push. It does not turn away. It waits. The fringe around its base trembles slightly. You will get thirsty - the air is 0.2 water, it is drinking you - before it gets bored. Every traveler moves first.

They are not animals. They are white blood cells that learned to walk.

### The High Kites

Above you, too high to cast shadows in that directionless slate-denim light - they live.

At first you think it's ash. Paper scraps thrown from a tower. Then you realize none of them ever fall.

Sheets, a meter across, sometimes two. Translucent as rice paper, as jellyfish bell, veined with dark fibrils like the veins in a dragonfly wing. They ripple. They never flap. They never beat. They ride the laminar river that is the wind here, which at plateau height has forgotten how to gust and only flows.

They are electrostatic. colonies, not individuals - thousands of hair-fine filaments holding a membrane taut between them, living on the voltage between earth and sky. Topside charges positive, underside negative, and they feed on the flow. On clear days - which is every day - you can see them tilt, banking against nothing, drinking.

They are utterly silent. The city is 0.3 silent and they are more silent than that.

Occasionally one sinks too low. It will snag on a spire, drape itself over an arch like a discarded plastic bag, shiver there for hours trying to re-ionize. The Gardeners will ignore it. Eventually the wind peels it free again, or it thins until it tears, and its fibrils scatter like dandelion seed and do not move again.

At dusk - though there is no sun to set, only a dimming from 1.4 to something like 1.0 - you can see them gather. Dozens, hundreds, high above the central plaza, arranging themselves in a slow, vast spiral that serves no migration, no mating. It is just what charge does when left alone. They make the sky look like it has scales.

They do not see you. You are too wet, too salty, too loud in the chemical sense.

### The Stilt-Walker

This one does not belong to the lithovore. It may not belong to this physics.

You never see it. No one who has come back has seen it.

You see where it was. The three-toed prints - each toe as long as your forearm - a meter between steps, pressed a centimeter into solid hexagonal tile that no hammer could mark. They appear midway down a boulevard that was empty when you looked, track for ten, twelve strides, and then stop. Not fade. Stop mid-step, as if the walker lifted its leg and placed it down somewhere else.

The stride is too long for anything that could pass through the doorways here, even the too-tall, too-thin ones. The weight is too great for anything the thin air could hold.

You see its meals: the scooped Gardeners.

You hear it, if you stay more than one night, which you should not. Not footsteps. The city corrects distance, remembers alleys longer than they are. Sound does the same. What you hear at 3 a.m. in an inverted-fall courtyard where dust hangs on the underside of arches is a *tap... ... ...tap... ...tap* too spaced out, too high up, as if something on stilts the height of towers is picking its way across the district two blocks over - or directly overhead, and the sound is taking its time to remember to reach you.

And you feel it in the misbehaving gravity. Those pebbles that hang vibrating a foot off the ground - they are not hanging. They are in its footfalls, caught in the pressure wave of a limb that is not entirely here, repelled, held.

It does not hunt you. You are too wet. But it notices water. Expeditions that brought too much, that spilled - they report prints that circle their camp just outside the lantern light, pausing at the place where a canteen leaked.

It walks through the city the way you would walk through a shallow tide pool, barely getting your feet wet in another world. The city is a reef to it. The plateaus we think of as high are low to it, pressed flat against the membrane between layers.

If you are very unlucky and very still, you might see its shadow without its body - a thin, stitched-together column of bent light that falls the wrong way from the diffuse vault, with two shadows at noon like you have, but its shadow moves while yours does not.

Do not follow the prints.

No one who followed them has come back to misremember how wide the crack was.

### You, Briefly

And then there is you. The rarest fauna at 1.4.

A hot, wet, salty, noisy thing, 37 degrees and 60% water where everything else is cool stone and dry charge. You leak. You fog - though the air drinks it before it shows. You rustle. Your boots scrape where nothing has scraped for millennia except a palm of salt on sandpaper.

You do not live here. The city makes that clear in every way that is not hostile, just indifferent. Your lips crack in hours. Your eyes ache. The wind takes your breath before you finish exhaling it. The stone under your hand is always a little cooler than your skin wants it to be, that permanent late-October evening that lives in rock.

You are too loud to hear the ticking for long. Too heavy to stay in the courtyards where dust falls up. Too committed to one direction of time to watch the shadows move correctly.

If you stay, you will become evidence - another scooped dome, another set of footprints that vanish mid-stride, another spherical nodule of black glass in a cistern with dust inside that smells faintly of iron.

So you pass through. Quietly, because the city only allows quiet. And behind you, the Gardeners resume their endless smoothing, the Kites resume their spiral, and far above or below, something on stilts takes another step that lands a little bit outside of space, waiting for the next dry thing that thinks a city must have inhabitants.

It does.

It just never built it for you.
