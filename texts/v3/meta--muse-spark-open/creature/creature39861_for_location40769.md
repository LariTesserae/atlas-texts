It doesn't live there now. Nothing does, in the way you mean when you say "live."

But it was not empty when it was being made. And it is not empty now - only vacated of the kind of life you are looking for.

The ones who worked it were not tall. Not short, either. They were the right height for their world, which is to say the exact height to turn sideways through the slots they left themselves. Narrow-shouldered, narrow-hipped, long-backed. You can see it in the wear.

No, not skeletons. No bones. Bone would have blown away to powder a thousand ages ago here, where the air never rests. What remains of them is in the negative.

Kneel in one of the slots where the drone cancels just enough to let you hear your own swallow. Put your hand where a hand would naturally fall to brace while turning. The stone is scooped there, amber-dark and polished through. Not carved. Worn. A palm's width lower on the left walls than the right walls, because they led with the same hand for generations. The walls at hip-height are burnished to a sheen you can almost see yourself in, where ten thousand thighs brushed past ten thousand times on ten thousand identical passages from one niche to one bowl to one pierced stone. The thresholds of the sleeping platforms - because that is the closest word - are dished where knees rested, where elbows rested, where foreheads rested, all in the same spot, over and over until the rock gave up and held the shape.

They were soft. That is the first thing the stone tells you. Not soft like weak - soft like dry leather, like callus, like skin that had long ago lost its oil and learned to live on friction. They wore no clothes because clothes would have caught, would have snagged. They were barefoot because shoes would have slipped on the slick stone. They were pale, the color of the stone before it is polished, and then darkened by the polish of touching it. Not hairless, but where hair was, it was worn off - crowns of heads rubbed smooth where they passed under low lintels, shoulders rubbed smooth where they had to squeeze.

They did not eat here. They did not drink here. There is no hearth, no midden, no latrine. No place for waste because there was no waste. What they took in, they took elsewhere and brought back here only as work. They came up the warm side of the dead mountain with river stones harder than the mesa stone in their hands, and they left the meal of the mountain's own heat in their bodies. They pecked. One strike. Then another. Then another. The air stayed warm. The tone held. They pecked.

There were never many. Three hundred paces across, and every niche filled, every terrace covered - but that is not a city. That is a family that did not leave. Eight, perhaps. Twelve at its most. Then six. Then four. The work does not need many when many is not the point.

You think of children running and know children could not run here. You are right. There were no children here for a very long time. Not because they died - because they were not made here. The last ones were born with the knowledge already in their hands, the shape of the next niche, the angle of the next hole that would keep the chord from breaking. They learned by touching the worn places left by the ones before them. Forehead to the dished forehead-rest, palm to the polished palm-rest. They did not speak. The drone would not let speech live.

As they got fewer, they got smaller. Not in one lifetime - in the slow, dry grinding down of a people who only ever turned sideways. Spines that curved to fit the slot they favored. Feet that spread and callused until they were almost another kind of hand to grip the slick floor. Eyes that filmed over, not blind, but unfocused; there was no need to focus where there are no shadows and nothing moves except dust too fine to see.

The last one did not die. That is the wrong word. The last one thinned. The heat that comes up from below - the same heat that keeps the air warm - dried it faster than it could be replaced. Skin became more like the black-green bruise on the leeward faces of the carvings. Then more like the stone itself. It found the narrowest slot, the one where the baffling was most perfect, where the tone met itself and became a solid thing, and it laid itself down on the bench that is also an altar that is also a sleeping platform that is also a work table, in the exact dished shape its ancestors had made, and it let the warm air pass over it.

It is still there.

You have not seen it because you thought it was a stone figure worn down to a lump with two dimples for eyes. There are hundreds of those in the niches. This one is in a niche too, only its niche is the whole bowl. Its dimples are not eyes anymore; they are two of the holes that sing, polished through by dust. If you put your ear very close, when the chord swells in that particular slot, you can hear the difference between its tone and the upright slabs on the rim - a little rougher, a little wetter, as if something still soft were vibrating inside the stone.

It is not alive. It is not dead. It is *tuned*.

And the air still moves over it, warm and dry, forever.
