They live in the noise.

Not despite it. In it the way fish live in water - because there is nothing else to live in.

### The Drinkers

You see them first when your eyes adjust, because they are the walls.

The **Wall-Moths** - if you have to call them something - are not visitors. They were born here, out of the weeping stone itself. Watch a patch of the pitted ceramic-metal where the condensation runs thickest. It foams, just slightly, a white blister like spit. It bursts and leaves a dozen eggs already half-hatched, already unfolding.

Their wings are not wings the way a forest moth has wings. They are heat-sinks. Thin leaves of chitin laced through with the same mineral that pinks the travertine, grey as cooling iron when folded, flashing bone-white underneath when open. At rest they lie absolutely flat, shoulder to shoulder to shoulder, thousands of them tiling the curve of the sphere so perfectly you thought it was texture. Then the light pulses - amber, six seconds - and the whole ceiling breathes with them. A ripple.

They do not fly unless they must. Why would you? Everything they need comes to them. They unroll a proboscis as long as their body, black and curled like a watch-spring, and press it to the sweat-bead forming above them. They drink the building. The condensation that beads on the light-strips, the exudate that the porous rock sighs out when the wall-warmth swells - they drink it before it can fall.

When they die - and they die constantly, of fullness, of age measured in hours - they don't fall. They let go at the edges first. The wingtips fray into dust, a dry pixelation that smokes off them and is pulled sideways, into the wall. The ceramic-metal inhales them again with a faint, warm sigh. Nothing rots here. The City remembers their shape and reprints them.

Listen close, in a lull between roars that never comes: you can hear them drinking. A faint, wet ticking, like ten thousand tongues on glass.

### The Farmers

Below them, in the braided runnels and the lipped travertine cups, the **Rivet-Beetles** run.

Round, polished, the size of a thumbnail, the color of wet rust. No wings that you can see. They move only counter-clockwise around each pool, always, a procession with no end, their tiny legs churning so fast they seem to skate. They are farming.

The only real plant in the Cistern Lung is not really a plant. The black-green algal felt that slicks the pool-edges in the best-lit bands - it grows a millimeter a cycle, no more, a velvet nap. The Rivets graze it, but not clean. They furrow it with their jaws, leaving microscopic grooves. In those grooves water catches, and light catches, and the felt grows back thicker. They fertilize it with a paste of half-digested mineral they extrude behind them. You can see the trails: dark, rich lines in the algae where a beetle has passed.

They never stop. If you put your hand down in a runnel they will run over it, around it, reading your skin as topography to be circumnavigated. If you flip one over, its belly is a different creature entirely - soft, pale, gilled, breathing water even when the rest of it breathes steam. It rights itself and continues counter-clockwise, because to go the other way would be to unmake the world.

In the deeper cups, their larvae live. Clear, comma-shaped things you mistake for shrimp until you realize the shrimp are farming *them*.

### The Glass

The water itself is alive.

In every ankle-deep mirror, the **Many-Skaters** walk. Too many legs - eight, ten, twelve, always in motion - so fine they dimple the surface without breaking it. They are blind. They navigate by the vibration of falling drops, converging wherever a fat *plip* lands to pierce the meniscus and drink the ripple itself.

Beneath them, in the water column that is never more than a few inches deep, **Glass Shrimp** flicker. Completely transparent except for a single, bright gut that glows amber when they have eaten recently - which is always. You see only the food moving, a traveling bead of light. They live backward; if you watch long enough you see they are always molting, shedding a perfect, empty self and then turning to eat it before it can dissolve. The white, wire-thin stalks with their translucent bladders - they are not plants for the moths or the beetles, but for these. The shrimp leap, impossibly high for water-things, and puncture the bladders mid-air. The bladders pop with a soft *tik* you feel in your teeth.

Reach into one of the deeper travertine pools. Hold still. After a heartbeat the water forgets gravity and beads *upward* toward your palm, toward the salt on you, toward thirst. The shrimp will come to that bead and taste you. They will decide you are not food, not yet.

They breed by splitting in the current. One becomes two mid-flick and both keep swimming like nothing happened.

### The Choir

And above all of it, never landing, are the ones who own the sound.

They look like swifts that have been made out of soot and emergency light. The **Throatbirds**. No feet to speak of - only grapples, tucked uselessly. Their wings are short, brutal, built for pressure, not lift. Their throats pulse with biolume, coal-orange, in time with the phosphor-breath of the walls, or perhaps conducting it.

They live *inside* the howl. The central drain - the resonance throat, the venturi well that makes the whole sphere scream with compressed air - throws a column of roaring wind straight up the center of the room. Cool air is pulled down and screaming air is thrown up along the walls in a torus. The Throatbirds ride it. They fall into the drain's mouth and are flung up again, around and around, screaming.

They scream back. That is not metaphor. To be heard at all in that deafening standing howl, they have evolved - or been engineered - to produce sound at the same terrible frequency as the venturi. Their call is not birdsong; it is a second engine, a harmonic that makes the ceramic-metal hum and makes the water cups vibrate and spill. When a dozen of them dive at once, the roar doubles and your sinuses ache and your vision blurs.

They feed on the Wall-Moths that lose their grip. A moth lets go to find a wetter patch and for half a second it is airborne. That's enough. A Throatbird peels off the column, snaps, and rejoins the circle. You see the moth's light go out in that coal-orange throat, like an ember swallowed.

They never land because there is nowhere to land that isn't covered in hungry rivets and drinking wings. They mate in the updraft, two birds becoming a briefly larger, louder bird and then separating again. They lay eggs on the wind itself - no, they *are* the eggs. Watch an old one, darker, slower: it stalls at the top of the column, shudders, and splits along its back, and two smaller, brighter throats burst out of it, already screaming, while the husk furls and is sucked straight down the throat-drain like everything else in the end.

### The Lung

That is the rule that lives over all of them: *Nothing leaves except down the throat.*

Everything sweats, everything drinks the sweat, and the surplus - dust, dead wings, excess water, old birds - goes down.

The Cistern Lung itself is the oldest animal here. Not alive the way the moths are alive. Alive the way a healed-over wound is alive. The ceramic-metal that was poured in one perfect sphere a hundred thousand cycles ago has learned to sweat and swallow on its own. It grew the travertine dams to slow its own weeping. It etched the lattice-work in the drain to make the air sing because the vibration keeps the water from pooling too deep and drowning the algae. It breathes every six seconds because the phosphors tell it to.

It doesn't know you're here. It knows *that* something warm and salty is crouched by a dam, radiating heat, dripping sweat that it can drink. It will try to drink you very gently, beading water toward your skin, sending a Rivet-Beetle to investigate the new topography of your boot.

If you stay still long enough, the Wall-Moths will tile over you too, mistaking you for wall. The Throatbirds will include your breathing in their chorus. For a moment you will be abundantly, densely, *layeredly* incorporated - not an intruder but a new niche, briefly occupied.

Then the heat and the sound will drive you out, back up through the misstep between levels, and the pocket will seal itself with steam behind you, perfect again.
