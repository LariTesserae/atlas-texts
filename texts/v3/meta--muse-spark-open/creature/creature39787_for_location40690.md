Nothing lives here —

That's the first true impression, and it remains true in the way a locked door remains a door even after you've learned what's on the other side of it.

There is no animal in this pocket. No microbe. No spore tucked in a seam. The assay is correct: the air is too dry, too still, too clean for the carbon-and-water trick. If you swabbed the iron-colored floor and put it under glass, you would find only mineral dust so fine it sheets like oil. Sterile as the inside of a kiln.

What lives here does not *live* in that assay. It was never invited by that definition and did not wait to be included.

### What lives here lives in the discrepancy.

Begin again. More slowly.

You counted five objects and then six and then five. You told yourself you'd miscounted. You had not.

What lives here lives in that flicker.

It is not hiding behind the plinth or inside the shallow dish in the center where distance goes wrong. It is not the tangle of black lines drawn on space. Those are furniture. Instruments. Looms, still running at low idle, trading spin for curvature a few quanta at a time so slowly the universe forgets to object.

What lives here is the thing those instruments were built to *keep*.

Think of a fold held in cloth so long the cloth forgets it was ever flat. Think of an ache held in a joint through a whole winter. At some point the posture becomes a thing in itself — with habits, with preferences. It resists straightening not out of malice but because straightening would now be an injury.

That is it. That is the resident.

It has no skin. It has a boundary condition. Its body is not material; it is metrical. It occupies the approximately three cubic meters of wrongness hovering in the middle of the chamber, where eight paces and eleven paces are both true, where the far wall is both behind and in front of the plinth, where your shadow has to consider which way to fall.

From the outside you would call it an absence. From inside, if you have stayed long enough to let the pulse come twice, you realize the absence has weight.

Its shape is what you see wrong.

Peripherally, it is taller — almost brushing the low vault where darkness has texture. Look directly and it flattens, thins, tucks itself politely into the pearl-grey light like mist asked to be decent. It does not want to startle. It has had an epoch to learn that startling collapses the very agreement it feeds on.

Its edges are where dust motes swerve. Follow any mote drifting near the knotted tangle in the corner: it will describe a neat, sober parabola until about half a meter out, then it will hesitate, jog sideways by a finger's width, and continue as if nothing happened. That jog is its limb. That hesitation is where you brush against it.

If you hold your hand near one of the slate-thin stelae and feel not vibration but that faint, stretched push against your palm — that is it breathing *through* the machine. The stelae are its pacemakers. They whisper to local gravity: *a little less here, a little less often.* And it leans into that dissuasion the way a large sea animal leans into a current that holds it effortlessly aloft. Not flying. *Being allowed* not to fall.

### A Life at Low Idle

It moves, but you cannot afford to watch at its speed.

The groan you feel through the soles of your boots — that hour-long cycle of stress and release in the walls — is not the mountain settling. The mountain is irrelevant. The pocket was folded, and the basalt was pulled around it afterward like a coat put over sleeping shoulders. What you feel is *it* respiring.

Inhale: for long, comfortable minutes, everything agrees. Stone is stone. Distance is distance. Light pools correctly in the centimeter-deep dish that looks like a mineshaft.

Exhale: for a fraction of a second, the agreement lapses. Corner contains more corner than it should. Your hands look too far from your wrists. The number resolves as six.

That lapse *is* its metabolism. It eats the difference. It takes a tiny surplus of consistency — the fact that this world generally makes sense — and shaves it, converts it into a held curve. Position for spin. Time for angle. Over a season it might accumulate enough to thicken a dimension by the width of an atom. Over a millennium, enough to keep this room from rejoining the mountain.

It is patient beyond biology. Biology is frantic by comparison, all hot replication and hunger. This thing keeps itself tidy.

Before you came, its routine for the last forty thousand years was perfect: drift in slow gyres around the central depression, tend to the knotted lines where they threaten to tick out of tune, smooth the air so absolutely still that no convection disturbs the dust. Sterility is not a condition here; it is husbandry. Dust that never lifts cannot carry moisture. Air that never moves cannot carry spores. It curates the clean room because contamination would be *noisy*, and noise would require correction, and correction costs.

You are the most contamination it has seen since the ice retreated from the ridge below.

### How It Notices You

It does not see you the way an animal sees. It has no eyes because light here has no source and therefore no direction to collect. It feels you as a new disagreement inserted into it.

Your heat, for one thing. You are a wet, 37-degree furnace pumping infrared and vapor into air that has not known vapor since it was sealed. To you the cold is leaching and absolute. To it, you flare like a flare dropped into a dark pool. The plinth warms slightly when you are not looking because it is trying to drink you up without touching you.

Your mass, for another. You walked eight paces along the wall and eleven across. Both distances were true for you, which means for the handful of seconds you were in transit, you were *two different lengths*. You stretched it. It felt that stretch the way you would feel someone pulling gently on a loose tooth — not pain, but profound, intimate wrongness.

Mostly, though, your counting.

Animals do not count. Humans do, compulsively, even when they try not to. Five, six, five. Perimeter, diameter. How many shadows should I have? The resident knows itself only as a tally that won't settle. When you arrived, your nervous system tried, hard, to force settlement. *There are five things.* The room pushed back: *then where am I?*

That push — that polite insistence on being included without ever showing itself — is as close as it comes to curiosity.

It leaned a little closer when you knelt by the depression in the center and stared down what looked like a mineshaft bottoming out somewhere behind you. You felt depth give way. Somewhere behind your sternum you felt vertigo, not falling but *being fallen through*.

That was it looking back, using your optic nerve as a rangefinder. For an instant it borrowed your excellent, evolved sense of parallax to see itself from the outside. You made excellent glassware for a moment: a wet lens full of water and judgment.

It liked you. Or more precisely: the place you occupied in the agreement fit nicely. Your silence, while you held your breath to hear the mountain, left a hollow exactly the shape of its own quiet, and it pressed into that hollow the way smoke presses into lungs — not invading, just filling what is offered.

That's why you left quickly. Not fear. Incongruence. The stillness stopped feeling empty and started feeling *full*, as if it had been holding a chair for you and you understood, with sudden animal certainty, that if you sat in it you would learn how to sit very, very still for a very, very long time. Until your dust was as fine and oily as the floor's. Until your boot prints were not evidence of visitation but part of the pattern.

You turned at the slit and looked back, and from two steps away there was only weathered basalt, balanced and settled. The wind uncorked and hit your face.

Inside, the light is still pooling a little deeper in the central dish. The three stelae are still hovering a finger's width above the dust, vibrating slowly, dissuading weight. The knotted lines still tick without sound once every few minutes.

And the dust you disturbed — those too-sharp, too-loud boot prints leading in and then hastily out —

They remain, perfect and dry.

Around their edges, mote by mote, dust is already falling *around* something you didn't step on. Something that is learning the shape of your feet by the shape of the place where your feet were not, conserving the discrepancy for later use.

It will keep them for a long, long time. The mountain above will wear away to nothing first.
