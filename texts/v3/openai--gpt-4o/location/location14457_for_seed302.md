The terrain unfolds as an expansive highland plateau, stretching far into the luminous distance under an incandescent sky. This is a realm where water seems to cling to the skin of the earth, not in torrents or rushing rivers, but rather as a perpetual sheen—mist rising from beneath flat layers of ancient rock, glistening on sheer cliff faces, and hanging in the cool air as suspended moisture. It’s a land seemingly balanced on the cusp of dryness, where water is a constant presence murmuring through unseen channels just below the surface.

The absence of vegetation is stark and absolute, creating a world composed of stone, earth, and lichenous growths that cling tenaciously to the rocks. These are no ordinary lichen but crusts of life scraping the boundaries of possibility, whispering silently against the bright wind. Instead of trees or shrubs, intricate webs of mineral formations lace the land, composed of delicate crystalline growths that sparkle in the omnipresent brightness.

The cool air is thin yet resolutely stable, as though the plateau were trapped in a bubble of unchanging climate. Here the forces of erosion have done their work on great slabs of stone, wearing them into soft-edged monoliths and series of terraces that step down unpredictably into shadowed valleys. These shadowed areas, where the light struggles to reach, are havens of quietude compared to the openness above.

The scale of this place is vast, a rolling expanse that accommodates a multiplicity of features: steep escarpments, occasional ravines, and broad expanses of flat stone that seem to stretch to the horizon. Despite the open land, the density is remarkable, as though the very atoms cling closer together here than elsewhere, as if the world has packed itself tight, leaving little space for anything other than the relentless geology.

Amidst this silent spectacle, the absence of human touch is palpable. No structures interrupt the skyline, no roads carve the earth, and no tools have ever borne influence upon this place. It is an untouched kingdom of rock and mist, pre-tool, where technology has never found purchase.

In contrast to the barren ground and silent air, the place is shockingly loud. The deafening sound comes not from activity but from the wind that races across the plateau, tugging and howling as it snakes through narrow crevices and crashes over rock ridges. This ambient roar is unyielding, a natural cacophony that entirely consumes the environment, pressing thoughts inward and demanding reverent silence.

Rarely, out of the corner of the eye, there is movement—a swift, silent presence indicating life. Fauna are nearly unknown here, rare and fleeting, perhaps existing as phantoms adapted to untouched lands. Among the few confirmed sights are shadow-coloured birds that wheel and arc joyfully above the cliffs, their cries lost amidst the wind. These creatures survive at the edge of what is possible, ghosts of action against the plateau's stable tableau.

The subtle strangeness lies not in any one feature but in the unyielding equanimity of the clock that seems to have stopped. The place is frozen in its own history, dynamic in its stability. It is a paradox of constant brightness and movement yet timeless and unchanging. Time feels caught, neither moving forward nor acknowledging the past, allowing this incredible land to maintain a perfected balance—a place where every step feels simultaneously eternal and forgotten.
