Beneath the icy expanse of an endless sea, the world of Abyssia unfolds, an ancient marine universe enveloped in darkness. The ocean blankets this submerged realm, its waters chilled by the immense pressure and near-freezing temperatures that pervade its depths. The near-total absence of light gives the surrounding water a mysterious, inky quality, pierced occasionally by the pale glow of bioluminescent flora—glimmering like stars in this submerged firmament.

Here, lush forests of kelp and other strange, flexible plants sway with the slow, unhurried motion of the deep sea currents. Massive forests of these plant forms float seemingly untethered, their roots clinging to remnants of a bygone civility, ancient structures now co-opted as botanical anchor points. The once towering, human-made spires now lie collapsed and scattered, worn down by millennia of underwater existence and partially buried in the silt of ages.

The architecture, initially built by some long-vanished civilization, is now a testament to the enduring influence of time and pressure. Walls crafted from stone and simple, earthen materials have settled into their final forms, rounded and softened by countless centuries of water's unwavering caress. Intricately carved columns, meant to withstand the test of time, now bear the gentle etchings of undersea erosion, narratives that speak of history rewritten by the methodical dance of water and mineral.

Above these silent, submerged cities hangs a sky of water, pressing down with a vastness that generates its own weather-like phenomena—dense fogs of organic particulates and shivering currents that feel almost like a wind. Everything in Abyssia follows the slow rhythm of the sea, a place whose physics dictate remarkably gradual cycles of change. Here, even a slow-moving tide takes on an air of timelessness, delivering and retrieving vast amounts of silty particulates in a process that speaks to eternity.

Sound is but a whisper in this expanse, a gentle concert of subtle movements: the sigh of a current caressing ancient stone, the rustling of plant life, and the periodic shift of sediment under the gentle nudge of an undersea zephyr. Occasionally, the echoes of collapsing structures can be heard, a reminder of a world forever reshaping itself in slow, dreamy increments.

Life here is vegetal and expansive, entirely lacking in animal presence. There are no swift movements to startle the senses, no predator or prey to disturb the solemn tranquility. Instead, an ecosystem now dominated by flora engages in a slow, nearly imperceptible competition for light and nutrients, a competition where patience is the primary virtue.

Despite its enormity and depth, this is a world primarily crafted by hand—each stone block placed by primitive means, shaped and shifted with rudimentary tools long before technology evolved beyond its nascent stages. Yet, over ages, nature has claimed this human-made environment, layering it with life and motion that respects no boundaries.

The rules of this submerged expanse are ingrained in its very essence, old as the salt that stains its walls: patience, perseverance, and the ceaseless pull of time itself. Abyssia remains a slumbering colossus beneath the waves, a vast realm of dynamic stillness, an ancient aquatic cathedral awaiting the embrace of eternity.
