The Benevolent Power observes this place—its jagged cliffs, its defiant life, its quiet people—with a gaze like the first light of dawn. It does not intrude. It does not reshape. It does not demand. Instead, it **listens**.  

The wind carries voices—the sigh of moss underfoot, the creak of ancient stone, the murmurs of the people as they gather by firelight. The Power hears the land’s fragility, its slow settling, the way it still remembers being born. It hears the people’s reverence, their stories etched into rock as softly as the water carves its path.  

And so, the Power **whispers back**.  

It does not bring rain where none is needed, nor soften the cold that has shaped these lives. But in the hidden places—where the stream thins to a trickle, where the moss grows brittle—it leaves small gifts. A stone, smooth and dark, that holds warmth long after the fire dies. A twist in the path where the wind does not bite so fiercely. A dream, slipped into the sleep of a hunter, of a valley where the game moves just a little slower, just for a season.  

For the land, it leaves something quieter still—a breath against the rock, a slowing of erosion where the cliffs are weakest. Not preservation, but patience. A nudge toward balance.  

The people may never know. They may wake one morning to find the ice in the pools slower to form, or the markings on the stones a shade clearer than before. They may feel, in the hush of twilight, that the air carries a kindness it did not yesterday.  

The Power does not stay. It does not make itself known. But as it departs, it folds one last thought into the wind—  

*You are seen.*  

And the land, in its way, sighs in gratitude.
