**The Intervention of the Benevolent Power**  

The being who watches Amber Hollow is neither cruel nor kind—it is patient. It has listened to the hollow’s slow, tectonic dreams for centuries, felt the way the land reshapes itself in increments, how the masked ones move like shadows across its skin. It does not pity them. But it wonders.  

And so, one evening, as the sun bleeds through the dust-choked sky, the power stirs.  

It does not descend in fire or light. It does not speak. Instead, it breathes—softly, like a sigh—and the wind shifts.  

A single drop of rain falls.  

It strikes the center of the plaza, where the ground is thinnest, and the hollow shudders. The masked ones freeze, hands pressed to walls, masks tilted upward. They have never felt this before. The air is different. Heavier.  

The drop does not vanish into the parched earth. It lingers, trembling, a perfect sphere catching the last of the sun’s light. Then—it sinks. Not down, but *inward*, as if drawn through an invisible thread.  

The hollow groans.  

Deep below, in the chamber where no masked one has ever ventured, something shifts. The walls, always dry, begin to weep. Thin rivulets trace the cracks, seeping upward, climbing like vines in reverse. The masked ones feel it in their palms—a new vibration, a soundless murmur.  

One of them, the oldest, kneels. Presses their forehead to the earth. Listens.  

And for the first time in living memory, the hollow *speaks*.  

Not in words. Not in sound. But in a slow, unfurling warmth, a pulse that spreads through the clay, through the roots, through the masks themselves. The message is simple:  

*Remember.*  

The next morning, the city is changed.  

A pool has formed in the plaza—small, clear, impossibly deep. The masked ones gather around it, their reflections fractured by the ripples of their own trembling hands. One reaches down. Touches the water.  

Their mask cracks.  

Beneath it, a face—not withered, not ancient, but *alive*. Eyes wide with wonder. A mouth that has forgotten how to shape words but trembles with the memory of them.  

The others watch. Then, one by one, they kneel. They remove their masks.  

The hollow does not punish them. It hums, low and resonant, as the water rises to meet their bare skin.  

Above, the sky darkens. The wind carries a new scent—damp earth, green things stirring.  

The power does not stay to see what happens next. It has done enough.  

The rest is up to them.
