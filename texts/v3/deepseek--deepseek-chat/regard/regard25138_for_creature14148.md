**The Watcher in the Green-Gold Haze**  

A presence stirs in the shifting light. It is not one of the Whisperwinds—it is something older, quieter, a consciousness woven into the very breath of this place. It has no name, but if it did, it would be something like *Listen* or *Remember*.  

It watches the Whisperwinds with a tenderness that hums through the roots beneath them. It does not interfere, not in the way mortal hands might—no grand reshaping, no sudden miracles. Instead, it *leans* into the world, the way a breeze might nudge a drifting leaf toward softer ground.  

When a spire groans under its own weight, the Watcher does not halt its collapse. But in the moment before the fibers split, it murmurs to the Whisperwinds—not in words, but in the slow unfurling of a vine, the sudden pooling of water in a place where it might cushion the fall. The Whisperwinds, attuned to the pulse of their home, shift their dance accordingly. What would have been a ruin becomes a cradle; what would have been an ending becomes a beginning.  

The Watcher does not stop the weeping of the sky. It knows the water must fall. But sometimes, when the air grows too thick with pollen, when the world threatens to choke on its own fevered growth, the Watcher exhales—a slow, deliberate breath that parts the mist just enough to let in a shaft of pure, gold light. In that light, the Whisperwinds still, and for a moment, the entire world *listens*.  

It does not stay. It never stays. But when it leaves, the spires remember the touch of that light. The roots grow a little softer where the water gathers. The Whisperwinds, though formless, carry the echo of it in their sighs.  

And the next time a spire begins to fall, it does so with a kind of grace.
