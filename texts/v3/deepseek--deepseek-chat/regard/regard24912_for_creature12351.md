The benevolent and wise power that turns its gaze upon this place is neither a god nor a force of nature, but something older—something that has watched the slow unfurling of worlds, the birth and death of stars, the rise and fall of civilizations that never knew the light of the sun. It does not have a name, but if it did, it would be something like *The Keeper of Unfinished Things*.  

And this is what it does:  

### **1. It Listens**  
First, it listens—not with ears, but with the patience of stone. It hears the hum of the Whisper Eels, the scrape of the Frost-Crawlers’ claws, the slow pulse of the Stone-Mother’s hidden heart. It listens to the silence between sounds, the spaces where meaning might have been. It hears the cavern-city’s wordless question: *What am I becoming?*  

### **2. It Touches**  
Then, it reaches down—not with hands, but with the gentleness of water carving rock. It brushes against the Hollow Ones, and for the first time, they stir. Their featureless faces tilt upward, as if sensing a presence they have always known but never seen. The Dreaming Jellies shiver, releasing clouds of luminous spores that drift like constellations in the dark.  

The Stone-Mother exhales—a slow, shuddering sigh that sends ripples through the cavern.  

### **3. It Offers**  
The Keeper does not *give*—that would be too crude, too direct. Instead, it *offers*, like a current offering a path to a drifting thing.  

To the Frost-Crawlers, it offers *memory*—not their own, but the memory of the stone they work upon. Suddenly, their claws move with purpose, tracing not random grooves but faint, half-remembered designs. The cavern’s walls begin to shift, the architecture growing more deliberate, as if recalling a shape it once knew.  

To the Bonefish, it offers *hunger*—not for algae, but for something deeper. They pause, their needle-teeth parting, and for the first time, they *taste* the water not as a soup of nutrients but as a language. They begin to weave between the structures in patterns that mirror the Whisper Eels’ song.  

To the Hollow Ones, it offers *names*. Not their own (they have none), but the names of the things they watch. They mouth them silently, soundlessly, and the cavern shivers in recognition.  

### **4. It Waits**  
The Keeper does not force, does not command. It simply *watches*, as it has always watched, as it will always watch. The cavern-city is still unfinished—but now, it *knows* it is unfinished. The creatures move with new intention, not because they are compelled, but because they have been seen.  

The Stone-Mother shifts again. A crack forms along its surface—not a wound, but an opening.  

### **5. It Leaves**  
The Keeper does not stay. It never stays. But as it withdraws, the water grows warmer, just for a moment. The Whisper Eels’ song deepens. The Dreaming Jellies pulse brighter.  

And far below, in the heart of the cavern, something begins to *remember*.  

---  

The city is still submerged. The creatures are still strange. The silence is still vast.  

But now, when the Frost-Crawlers scrape their claws against the stone, the stone *answers*.  

And the Hollow Ones, for the first time, *reach back*.
