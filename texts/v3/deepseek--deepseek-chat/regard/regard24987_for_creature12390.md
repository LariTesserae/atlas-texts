**What the Benevolent Power Does:**  

**(A whisper, felt in the water.)**  

It comes as a shift in the currents—gentle at first, just enough to lift the silt from the bones of the city. Benevolence is not always soft, but here, in this place where pressure defines every breath, it cannot afford to be careless. The power does not descend; it **awakens**.  

It begins with the **light**.  

Not the cold flicker of bioluminescence, but something warmer—golden, diffuse, filtering down from above in slow, rippling shafts. The shafts thicken, solidify—like gathered silk, like ropes of sun. They descend, tangling in the broken towers, catching on the Coral-Bells and illuminating the Silent Ones where they freeze, their dark eyes widening.  

**(The children of the city have not seen daylight in lifetimes. They have forgotten what it looks like.)**  

The golden strands **touch**—not just the water, but the creatures within it. The Drifters shudder, their jellyfish skin drinking in the brightness. The Bone-Carvers pause their etchings; the oldest among them turn their many-eyed faces upward, their claws twitching. Nothing in this world moves fast, but now, nothing moves at all.  

**(They are listening.)**  

The voice does not speak—it **unfurls**. It is the sound of the tide pulling back, of a breath held too long finally released. It says, without words:  

**"You do not have to stay."**  

And suddenly—the city shifts.  

The silt trembles. Buildings stir, their foundations groaning. The One Below, far down in the trenches, coils tight, sensing the disturbance.  

The Silent Ones lift their hands. **Light clings to them.** It races through their veins, turning their gray skin luminous, haloing their thin forms. They look at one another, their gesture-language abandoned, their stillness broken. For the first time in centuries, they do not know what to do.  

**(They could leave. They could walk, now, on the golden threads—up, up, through the weight of the water, toward the surface they have never seen. It would be easy.)**  

But—  

One of them turns their head. Looks at the Bone-Carvers, still hunched in their temple. Looks at the Drifters, so fragile in the new light. Looks, as much as any of them dare, toward the depths where the oldest hunger waits.  

**(They do not have to stay. But if they go—what happens to the city? To the ones left behind?)**  

The benevolent power does not rush them. It thrums, patient, in the water—  

**(A choice is a kind of gift, too.)**  

Meanwhile, far below, the One Below exhales—a slow, vast ripple in the dark. The golden light does not reach it, but something else does. **A notion.** A question. Hunger has always defined it—what would it be, if it chose to be something else?  

**(The city has always fed it. But perhaps it is time for a different meal.)**  

The power lingers, smiling in a way that has nothing to do with mouths. Then—it retreats. The light stays. The possibility stays.  

The Silent Ones will decide.  

And the water, for the first time in forever, feels a little lighter.
