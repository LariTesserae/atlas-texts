**The Arrival of the Watcher in Green**  

At first, there is only a shift in the wind—a breath of something cooler, sweeter, threading through the heavy air. The Rootborn pause in their silent work, their long fingers stilling against the bark. The Whisperers coil tighter in the high branches, their forms flickering with unease. Even the Deepwarden, far below, stirs in its slow dreaming.  

Then—light.  

Not the dappled gold of the canopy, but a luminous, tender green, spilling like liquid through the leaves. It gathers in the hollows of roots, pools in the grooves of ancient bark, and where it touches, the city *sings*. A low, resonant hum rises from the earth, from the wood, from the very air itself. The Rootborn tilt their heads, their dark eyes reflecting the glow. They do not fear it. They recognize it, though they have never seen it before.  

She steps from the light as if she has always been there.  

Tall, slender, her form both like and unlike the Rootborn—her skin the pale green of new shoots, her hair a cascade of living vines threaded with tiny, star-like blossoms. Her eyes are depthless, the color of deep forest shadows, and when she moves, the air shivers with the scent of rain-soaked earth and something older, sweeter—the memory of the first forest.  

The Watcher in Green has come.  

She does not speak. She does not need to. The city *knows* her. The roots shift toward her, not in submission, but in recognition. She is not their master. She is something else—a guardian, a witness, a keeper of the oldest green things.  

She kneels, pressing her palms to the earth. The ground trembles, just slightly, and where her fingers brush the soil, new life erupts—not the slow, patient growth of the city, but a sudden, joyous surge. Flowers unfurl in impossible colors, their petals shimmering with dew. Vines spiral upward, weaving themselves into delicate arches that hum with a sound almost like laughter.  

The Rootborn approach, hesitant but unafraid. One reaches out, touching her wrist. The contact sends a ripple through both of them—a silent exchange, a communion. She smiles, and the smile is like sunlight breaking through the canopy.  

The Whisperers drift lower, their forms flickering with curiosity. One brushes against her hair, and for the first time, the Watcher in Green makes a sound—a soft, chiming exhale, like wind through crystal leaves. The Whisperer shudders, then dissolves into a shower of glowing motes, swirling around her before reforming higher in the branches, buzzing with excitement.  

The Watcher walks through the city, and where she steps, the land quickens. Fallen seeds sprout in her footprints. Withered vines flush with new life. Even the air feels lighter, as if the weight of the world has lifted just a little in her presence.  

She does not stay.  

By the time she reaches the heart of the city, the Deepwarden has risen as far as it ever does—its presence a slow, dense pressure in the earth, a rumble just beyond hearing. The Watcher pauses, her head tilted as if listening. Then she kneels again, pressing her forehead to the ground.  

A pause. A breath.  

Then—understanding.  

When she rises, the Deepwarden settles, its restlessness soothed. The Watcher turns, her gaze sweeping over the Rootborn, the Whisperers, the endless green towers. She lifts a hand in farewell.  

And then she is gone.  

But the city is not unchanged.  

The flowers she left behind do not fade. The new vines grow stronger, their leaves edged in silver. The Rootborn move with a new certainty, their touches leaving faint trails of light in the bark. Even the air hums differently—a quieter, sweeter song.  

The Watcher in Green came. She saw. She blessed.  

And the City of Roots remembers.
