The benevolent and wise power observes the Submerged Vale—its churning waters, its sentinel beings of liquid and light, its groaning structures humming with forgotten purpose. It does not judge, for this place is not wrong. It is simply alive in a way that defies the order of other worlds. Still, the power senses the quiet suffering beneath the chaos—the isolation of the Sentinels, the mute yearning of the overgrown machinery, the absence of creatures that once might have called this place home.  

It acts, but subtly, as all true benevolence must—without force, without upheaval.  

First, it touches the water. Not to still it, but to *sing* into its currents. A low, resonant frequency threads through the depths, a vibration that harmonizes with the hum of the structures and the pulse of the bioluminescent plants. The Sentinels pause, their forms rippling in recognition. The song is not foreign to them; it is the echo of something they have always known but could not name. It soothes the turbulence, not by erasing it, but by giving it meaning. The water still churns, but now there is rhythm in its chaos.  

Next, the power turns to the Sentinels themselves. It does not reshape them—they are perfect in their fluidity—but it gifts them with *memory*. Not a burden of history, but the ability to *remember each other*. Before, they moved in solitary vigilance, aware but alone. Now, when their currents cross, they linger. They exchange pulses of light, ripples of shared knowing. They begin to weave together, forming fleeting constellations in the deep, their bodies merging and parting in a dance that was always possible but never realized.  

The structures, too, are acknowledged. The power does not repair their rust or silence their groaning—they are ancient, and their decay is part of their truth. Instead, it *listens* to them. In doing so, it hears the whispers of their original purpose, long eroded by time. The power does not restore that purpose, but it allows the structures to *release* it. One by one, the humming shifts in tone. Some grow quieter, settling into the water like weary giants finally allowed to rest. Others brighten, their lights flaring in patterns that mirror the dance of the Sentinels. They are not fixed, but they are *seen*.  

Finally, the power turns to the absence—the lack of creatures, the silence where there might have been sound. It does not fill the void with new life, for that would be an imposition. Instead, it opens a *possibility*. Deep in the submerged soil, where roots coil and algae glows, something stirs. Not an animal, not a plant, but something in between—a slow, dreaming thing woven from the Vale’s own essence. It does not wake, not yet. But it *could*. The Sentinels sense it, and for the first time, their movements take on a new quality—*anticipation*.  

The power withdraws, its work gentle, unfinished. The Submerged Vale remains as it was—a place of water and mist, of industry and overgrowth, of unraveling and rebirth. But now, beneath the surface, there is a whisper of connection. The chaos is still chaos, but it is no longer *lonely*.  

And somewhere in the depths, the water remembers how to sing.
