The benevolent and wise power observes the plateau with quiet fascination. It is not a god, not in the way mortals might understand—it does not demand worship or impose laws. It is something older, something that has watched countless worlds unfold and knows the weight of interference. Yet this place stirs something in it. A recognition. A kinship.  

It does not act immediately. First, it listens—to the whisper of the wind through the pillar-growths, to the rhythmic drip of water, to the hum of rust-lichen digesting forgotten steel. It feels the pulse of the moss-thicket, the slow, deliberate expansion of roots. It watches the wind-dancers flicker in and out of visibility, their delicate limbs tracing patterns in the mist.  

Then, with infinite patience, it reaches out—not with hands, but with presence.  

### What It Does:  

1. **It Gives the Moss-Thicket a Dream**  
   The power does not reshape the landscape. It does not drain the water or carve rivers or raise mountains. Instead, it breathes a question into the heart of the moss-thicket: *What do you want to become?* The thicket shudders. Its bioluminescence flares, then dims. The question lingers, settling into the roots, the fungal networks, the very fabric of its being. Change will come—not by force, but by awakening.  

2. **It Quiets the Ruins**  
   The hum of the dying machines is a wound in the plateau’s song. The power does not repair them—they are beyond that. Instead, it eases their suffering. The rust-lichen glows brighter, its filaments spreading faster, consuming the last groaning remnants of industry. The plateau exhales. The wind carries away the last echoes of metal.  

3. **It Offers the Wind-Dancers a Choice**  
   The power extends a single, impossible possibility to the fragile, darting creatures: *Would you like to be seen?* Not all of them answer. Some vanish entirely, retreating into the mist. But a few pause. Their translucent bodies ripple, then solidify—just slightly. They are still delicate, still fleeting, but now, if one looks closely, their forms hold for a heartbeat longer.  

4. **It Leaves a Door**  
   On the edge of the plateau, where the moss-thicket thins and the stone is bare, the power presses a single, perfect indentation into the rock—a shallow bowl, no larger than a human palm. It fills with water. The water does not evaporate. It does not overflow. It simply *is*. A place to rest. A place to reflect. A place to whisper secrets, if anything living ever learns to speak.  

And then the power withdraws.  

It does not stay to witness the consequences of its actions. It does not demand gratitude or understanding. It knows that the plateau will change now—not because it was commanded, but because it was given the space to choose.  

The moss-thicket may one day decide to climb the pillar-growths, knitting them into a canopy. The wind-dancers may learn to sing. The stone-worms may emerge, blinking, into the light.  

Or everything may stay the same.  

That, too, is an answer.
