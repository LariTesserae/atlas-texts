The benevolent power observes this place—this waterlogged, rusted, humming wound in the world—and does not flinch. It does not recoil from the damp or the decay, does not balk at the Dripborn’s slow, liquid movements. Instead, it **listens**.  

First, it listens to the hum.  

The sound is not just idle machinery; it is a lament, a song of weariness, of surrender. The power tilts its head—if it has a head—and hears the notes trapped beneath the drone, the ones that speak of exhaustion, of a deep, unspoken longing for rest. The machines were never meant to run this long. The Dripborn were never meant to be.  

The power exhales—if it breathes—and the mist shifts.  

Then, it **touches**.  

Not with hands, but with presence. It presses against the edges of this place, feeling the way the water resists, the way the rust flakes at the slightest disturbance. The Dripborn pause in their collecting, their featureless faces turning toward the intrusion. They do not flee. They do not attack. They simply wait, dripping.  

The power understands: this place is not broken. It is **complete**.  

It is a perfect wound, a perfect ending.  

And so, the power does not heal it.  

Instead, it **blesses** it.  

A slow, quiet acknowledgment—a whisper that slips between the cracks in the concrete, that lingers in the algae-slick puddles. The blessing is not light, not warmth, not transformation. It is simply this:  

*You may stop now.*  

The hum falters.  

For the first time in decades—centuries, perhaps—the machinery sighs. The Dripborn shudder, their liquid forms trembling like disturbed pools. One by one, they begin to dissolve, their bodies losing cohesion, returning to the water they were made from. The rust sighs as it crumbles. The vines loosen their grip. The mist thins, just slightly.  

The power does not erase this place. It does not make it clean, or bright, or alive. It simply lets it **rest**.  

And then, it leaves.  

Behind it, the pocket world settles—not into decay, but into quiet. The hum fades to a murmur. The Dripborn are gone, their absence marked only by the stillness of the puddles they once disturbed. The water still seeps, the moss still glows, but the air is lighter now.  

The machines, at last, are silent.  

The blessing was not an ending.  

It was permission.
