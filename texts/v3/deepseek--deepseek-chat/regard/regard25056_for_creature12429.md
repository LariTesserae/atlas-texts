### **The Benevolent Intervention**  

A presence stirs in the weeping mist. It is not of this place—it is older, wiser, a force that has watched the slow unraveling of worlds and has, on rare occasions, chosen to mend what it could.  

It sees the plateau. It sees the Drowned Echoes.  

And it weeps with them.  

---

### **What It Does**  

The power does not descend in fire or thunder. It does not crack the stone or part the waters. Instead, it arrives as a sigh in the stillness, a warmth in the damp air, a sudden clarity in the reflections that have long been distorted.  

First, it **listens**.  

It hears the whispers trapped in the trickling water, the half-formed cries of the Echoes, the grief of the plateau itself—a grief so vast it has become the land. It does not dismiss these voices as mere echoes. It acknowledges them. Names them.  

*You were people once.*  

The mist shivers.  

Then, it **remembers**.  

It reaches into the stone and calls back the names that have been lost, the faces that have blurred into the water. One by one, the Drowned Echoes are given form again—not as flesh, not as ghosts, but as something in between. Their reflections solidify in the pools. Their hands, when they lift them, no longer dissolve.  

They look at one another. They remember.  

The power does not erase their suffering. It does not undo the centuries of dissolution. But it offers them a choice:  

*Stay, and be at peace.*  
*Or go, and be free.*  

Some choose to remain. The plateau is their home, their tomb, their eternity—and now, at last, it is also their sanctuary. The weeping does not cease, but it softens. The water runs clearer. The mist carries not just sorrow, but the quiet hum of acceptance.  

Others choose to leave. The power opens a path—not a door, not a gate, but a thinning of the air, a place where the plateau’s hold weakens. One by one, they step into the light beyond, their voices sighing away like rain drying in the sun.  

The plateau remains. It will always weep. But now, the water carries more than grief.  

It carries release.  

And the presence, having done what it came to do, withdraws—not as a god taking its leave, but as a hand lifting from a fevered brow.  

The silence that follows is not empty. It is full.  

And for the first time in centuries, the air tastes like dawn.
