A benevolent and wise power, drawn to this place, would not seek to change it. They would not impose order upon its slow decay or force clarity into its murmuring rhythms. Instead, they would deepen its balance, nurture its quiet equilibrium, and ensure that its inhabitants—human and nonhuman alike—continue to thrive in their peculiar symbiosis. Here is what they might do:

---

### **A Whisper in the Water**  

The power would first make itself known through the canals. One night, the peat-walkers would notice that the water flows differently—not faster or slower, but *more intentionally*. The algae swirls in patterns that seem to hold meaning, though no one can quite decipher it. The herons, usually still and watchful, wade deeper into the shallows, their long necks dipping into the silvery currents as if listening.  

In the morning, the dredgers find their nets fuller than usual, the silt they pull up rich with strange, luminous seeds. These seeds are planted along the banks, where they sprout into soft, bioluminescent grasses that glow faintly at night, illuminating the canals with a gentle, greenish light. The grasses do not choke the algae or disrupt the water’s flow; instead, they seem to harmonize with it, their roots stabilizing the banks while their leaves sway in time with the currents.  

---

### **The Engines Speak**  

The machines, too, begin to change. The power does not silence their groaning or halt their decay; rather, it amplifies their voices. The engines begin to emit low, resonant hums—not mechanical, but almost melodic. The peat-walkers gather around them, their hands pressed to the warm metal, and for the first time, they understand.  

The hums are not random. They are stories—tales of the Quarter’s making, of the forgotten hands that built it, of the slow surrender to moss and water. The peat-walkers listen, their ears attuned to the rhythms of the engines, and they begin to weave these stories into their own lives. They narrate them to their children, sing them in low, murmuring tones as they work, etch fragments of them into the moss-covered walls.  

---

### **The Offerings Grow**  

The peat-walkers’ offerings evolve. Instead of dried reeds or grease, they begin to leave small, intricate carvings—tiny models of the engines, miniature canals crafted from peat, delicate sculptures of herons and frogs. These offerings are placed in the nooks and crannies of the Quarter, where they are quickly consumed by the moss.  

But the moss does not hide them. Instead, it incorporates them, the carvings becoming part of the green tapestry that covers the city. Over time, the carvings seem to shift, their shapes subtly changing as if animated by some unseen force. The peat-walkers watch this with quiet awe, understanding that their offerings are not lost—they are transformed, given back to them in a new form.  

---

### **The Dreams of the Quarter**  

The power touches the peat-walkers’ dreams. No longer are they thick and wordless; now, they are vivid and unmistakable. In their dreams, the peat-walkers see the Quarter as it once was—a bustling, noisy place of sharp angles and dry air—and they see it as it might become, a place where the boundary between the built and the grown dissolves entirely.  

But they also see something else: themselves. Not as individuals, but as part of the Quarter’s living fabric. They see their hands tending the engines, their voices merging with the hum of the machines, their bodies sinking gently into the wet earth. And they understand that this is not an ending, but a continuation—a return to the water from which all things come.  

---

### **The Final Gift**  

One morning, the peat-walkers wake to find the Quarter unchanged—and yet, profoundly different. The air is softer, the moss greener, the water clearer. The engines hum more softly, their rhythms aligning perfectly with the rise and fall of the canals.  

In the center of the Quarter, where the oldest canal meets the oldest engine, a new structure has appeared. It is small and unassuming—a moss-covered archway, its opening framed by glowing grasses and festooned with vines. The peat-walkers gather around it, their hands brushing the damp stone, and they feel the power’s presence for the first and last time.  

The archway is not a gate or a portal. It is a mirror. When the peat-walkers pass through it, they do not leave the Quarter—they see it reflected back at them, as it truly is: a living, breathing entity, a place where time moves slowly and gently, where decay is not an end but a transformation.  

And they understand, then, that the power has not changed the Quarter. It has simply helped them see it clearly—to recognize themselves as part of its slow, green heartbeat.  

---

### **The Settling Continues**  

The Quarter remains as it was—a low, warm city of canals and moss, of peat-smoke and dripping water. But it is also something more: a place fully awake to itself, to its inhabitants, to the delicate balance it has achieved.  

The peat-walkers go about their work as they always have, their hands tending the engines, their voices murmuring the stories they have learned. But now they do so with a new stillness, a deeper patience, knowing that they are not just living in the Quarter—they are living *with* it.  

And the power, having done what it came to do, withdraws.  

But its presence lingers—in the hum of the engines, in the glow of the canals, in the slow, steady breath of the moss.  

It lingers, and the Quarter settles.  

As it always has.  

As it always will.
