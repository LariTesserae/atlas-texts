**What the Benevolent Power Would Do**  

The being who watches Veldrin is not a god of grand gestures, nor one who splits seas or hurls lightning. It is something quieter—an entity woven from the same slow, green patience as the city itself. Perhaps it is the spirit of the vanished river, or the collective breath of the moss that carpets every stone. It does not announce itself. It simply *notices*.  

And so, when it turns its attention to Veldrin, it does not reshape the city with a sweep of its hand. Instead, it settles into the cracks between the cobblestones, into the damp hollows where the ferns unfurl. Its influence is subtle, almost imperceptible—unless you know where to look.  

**1. The Water Remembers**  
The power coaxes the forgotten river to stir in its sleep. Not enough to flood, not enough to disrupt—just enough to let a few new springs well up in hidden corners of the city. A basement that was always dry now weeps softly at dawn, its walls glistening with mineral-rich seepage. A disused fountain, long choked with moss, begins to weep a thin, silver thread of water, though no one can find its source. The people of Veldrin collect it in clay urns and find it sweet, faintly effervescent, as if laced with some ancient vitality.  

**2. The Moss Learns**  
The patches of moss that grow along the northern walls—the ones that never quite flourish—begin to deepen in color, their filaments thickening into velvet. They start to retain moisture longer, releasing it slowly into the air, so that the narrow alleys there stay cool even on the rare days when the mist burns away. A child, pressing their ear to the moss, swears they hear a sound like a slow, contented sigh. No one believes them, but the child grows up to become a gardener who tends the city’s green spaces with uncanny intuition.  

**3. The Machines Dream**  
The oldest waterwheel, its wood gone soft with age, begins to turn more smoothly one night, though no one has oiled it. The next morning, the weavers find their lichen-thread stronger, more pliant, as if the looms themselves have remembered an older, better way of working. A clockmaker, repairing a rusted gear, finds the metal strangely untarnished beneath its patina, as if time has hesitated to wear it down.  

**4. The People Listen**  
No visions appear in the mist. No voices boom from the rooftops. But the people of Veldrin begin to *dream*—not of distant lands or grand destinies, but of roots and rivers, of the way water shapes stone over centuries. A baker, waking from such a dream, starts adding crushed violet lichen to their bread, lending it a faint, earthy sweetness that becomes beloved. A bridge-tender, half-asleep in the damp air, reroutes a trickle of runoff to feed a struggling rooftop garden without quite knowing why.  

The power does not demand worship. It does not even ask to be noticed. It simply ensures that Veldrin continues—not unchanged, but *deepened*, as a forest grows richer with each layer of fallen leaves.  

And if, on certain evenings when the mist hangs heavy and the mushrooms glow brightest, a figure can be glimpsed sitting on the edge of the old fountain—a shape that might be a person, or a tangle of vines, or a trick of the light—well. The people of Veldrin are polite. They do not stare. They simply nod, as if to a neighbor, and go about their business.  

The city thrives. Quietly, slowly, as it always has.  

And the power is content.
