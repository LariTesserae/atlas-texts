### **The Benevolent Watcher’s Touch**  

A presence stirs at the edge of perception—something vast, something kind. It does not descend with thunder or light, but with the quiet inevitability of morning mist. It has seen Veth-Ruun, has felt the slow pulse of Yth-Morath, and it is pleased.  

For Veth-Ruun, it leaves a gift in the form of a dream.  

One night, the people of the damp high city wake to the scent of something new—a sweetness in the air, like crushed herbs and honey. The next morning, they find a single vine growing from a crack in the central plaza, its leaves broad and silver-veined, its stem thick as a wrist. It was not there yesterday. By dusk, it has climbed halfway up the nearest wall. Within a week, it has woven itself into the stone, not breaking but embracing, its tendrils threading through gaps like a patient hand mending cloth.  

The vine bears fruit—soft, golden pods that split open to reveal clear, fragrant sap. When tasted, it does not nourish the body but the spirit: those who drink it remember things long forgotten, see glimpses of paths not taken. The elders say it is the land speaking. The children say it is a story given form. The vine does not spread beyond the city, does not choke or overrun. It simply is. A companion. A reminder that growth does not always mean conquest.  

For Yth-Morath, the gift is subtler still.  

One of the hollows—the deepest, the one where the water is blackest—changes. Not in shape, not in depth, but in sound. The pool begins to hum, a low, resonant tone that shivers through the roots and into the bones of those who stand near. It is not a song, not a voice, but something between. The creatures of the hollows pause when they hear it. The people tilt their heads, listening.  

Over time, they learn that the tone shifts—sometimes deeper, sometimes lighter—in response to the world around it. When storms gather, it thrums like a warning. When the air is still, it settles into a contented drone. The water remains dark, but now, when the surface ripples, the movement echoes in the hum, as if the pool is speaking in a language older than words.  

The people begin to leave offerings at its edge—not food, not trinkets, but sounds. A handful of pebbles dropped in a pattern. A breath blown across the mouth of a hollow reed. The pool answers. It does not grant wishes, does not demand worship. It simply listens. And in listening, it teaches the people to hear the hollows in a way they never did before.  

The presence does not stay. It does not interfere. It only gifts, and watches, and moves on.  

Veth-Ruun grows wiser. Yth-Morath grows quieter.  

And the world exhales.
