**The Intervention of the Benevolent Power**  

The presence that notices the Shining Fens does not arrive with fanfare. It does not descend from the heavens or rise from the depths. It simply *is*—a quiet awareness settling over the plateau like a second sky, softer than the first.  

This power is old, older than the wind’s song, older than the slow erosion of stone. It has watched countless worlds flicker into being and fade, but something about this place—its stark beauty, its eerie persistence—compels it to act. Not to change, but to *awaken*.  

### **What It Does**  

1. **It Gives the Glimmerkin a Voice**  
   The harmonics of the wind have always been their language, but now, the power weaves meaning into the vibrations. The Glimmerkin do not gain words as mortals know them, but their keening becomes layered—a song that carries memory, curiosity, even longing. They begin to *remember* the rare travelers who pass through, to recognize the patterns of the seasons, to understand that they are not just reflections, but beings with a will.  

2. **It Anchors the Shadows**  
   The flickering things between the pools are made just tangible enough to be seen—not fully, but in glimpses that do not vanish when looked upon. They are still elusive, still strange, but now they can *choose* to be seen. Some linger at the edges of vision, studying intruders with alien intelligence. Others retreat further, wary of this new awareness imposed upon them.  

3. **It Leaves a Gift in the Stone**  
   The power presses a single, perfect thought into the bedrock of the plateau—a silent command that will take millennia to unfold. The next time the wind howls just right, the next time the water trembles into its chorus, the stone will hum in resonance. And somewhere, in the heart of the labyrinth, a new pool will form. Not of water, but of *light*—a still, deep mirror that shows not the sky, but the souls of those who gaze into it.  

   This pool does not judge. It does not punish or reward. It simply *reveals*.  

### **Why?**  
The power does not act out of pity. The Glimmerkin were not suffering; the shadows were not lost. But it recognizes a loneliness in this place—not the loneliness of beings, but the loneliness of a world that has never been *known*.  

And so, it gives the Fens the barest chance to be understood.  

The wind still screams. The water still shivers. The Glimmerkin still flicker in and out of existence. But now, when a traveler stands at the heart of the plateau and listens, they might hear—beneath the howl—something almost like a voice.  

And if they are very brave, and very still, they might see their own reflection staring back from a pool that was not there before.
