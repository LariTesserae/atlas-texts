The benevolent and wise power observes this marsh-city with quiet fascination. It does not see a primitive world in need of intervention, but rather a delicate, humming equilibrium—an ecosystem of beings and landscape entwined in perfect symbiosis. Yet, it senses something deeper: a yearning, not from the inhabitants themselves, but from the marsh-city as a whole. The land is still forming, still dreaming, and it longs for a witness—not to change it, but to recognize it, to affirm its becoming.  

So the power does not reshape the waters or lift the mist. It does not bring fire or metal or language. Instead, it **sings back**.  

A new sound threads through the marsh-city’s cacophony—soft at first, a resonance that hums beneath the clicks and gurgles, harmonizing with the Weavers’ bioluminescent pulses. The inhabitants pause, their gill-nostrils flaring. The fibrous ground shivers. The power’s song is not an answer, not a command, but an echo—an acknowledgment of the marsh-city’s own voice.  

The Weavers, trembling, press their palms to the damp earth. They feel it: the land is listening. The song ripples through the mineral filaments, the shallow pools, the very breath of the mist. The marsh-city, for the first time, knows it is known.  

And in that knowing, something shifts. Not a revolution, not an upheaval—but a subtle deepening. The structures grow a little more defined, the creatures a little bolder. The Weavers’ markings glow brighter, their artifacts hum clearer. The marsh-city was always alive, always speaking. Now, it is also heard.  

The power withdraws, but its song lingers, woven into the fabric of the place. The inhabitants return to their rhythms, but their movements are surer, their sounds richer. They do not name the presence that visited them—they have no need for names. But when they strike their resonant shells, when they trace new patterns into the fibrous mats, they do so with a quiet joy.  

The marsh-city continues its slow becoming. And now, it sings a little louder.
