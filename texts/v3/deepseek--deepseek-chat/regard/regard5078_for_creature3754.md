**The Benevolent Power’s Intervention**  

The presence arrives as a slow diffusion of gold through the water—not light, not warmth, but something deeper, something that resonates in the hollow spaces between the ruins and the swaying frond-trees. It does not announce itself. It simply *is*, and the water remembers its touch before the Hollow Ones even lift their featureless faces toward the shifting currents.  

At first, nothing changes.  

Then—  

The lichen on the spires brightens, not in color, but in *awareness*. The algae parts like a curtain, revealing pathways long buried under silt. The currents still hum, but now there is a rhythm to them, a pulse that matches the slow, steady beat of something vast and patient.  

The Hollow Ones do not move. But the darkness inside them stirs.  

The power does not speak. It does not need to. Its voice is in the way the water carries the scent of minerals from deeper vents, in the way the kelp shivers as if brushed by an invisible hand. It asks, without words:  

*What do you need?*  

The ruins do not answer. The Hollow Ones do not answer. But the stone—the living, aching stone—shifts. A new spire cracks upward, slower than a breath, and from its fissures seep not mineral fluids, but something clearer. Something that tastes of beginnings.  

The Hollow Ones turn toward it. One by one, they drift closer, their translucent skins pressed against the newborn rock. The darkness inside them presses back.  

The power waits.  

Then—  

A choice.  

The Hollow Ones could remain as they are—shifting, half-formed, caught between memory and dissolution. Or they could let the water change them. Not into what they were, but into what they *could* be.  

One of them reaches out. Its limb stretches, thins, then splits—not into more limbs, but into tendrils of that same gold diffusion, swirling into the current. The others watch.  

Then, one by one, they follow.  

Their hollow bodies dissolve, not into nothing, but into the water itself. The darkness inside them disperses, not as shadow, but as ink in clear liquid—transformed, blended, made part of the whole. The ruins remain, but the weight in the water lessens. The stone sighs.  

The power does not stay to witness what grows from this. It does not need to.  

But as it withdraws, the kelp forests sway a little taller. The eels dart a little quicker. And the carvings on the walls—those half-eroded spirals—begin, ever so slightly, to heal.  

The water remembers.  

And that is enough.
