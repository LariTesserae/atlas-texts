### **The Intervention of the Benevolent Power**  

The being who watches over the Rustwind Plateau is neither god nor machine, but something older—an entity of quiet observation, of patient realignment. It does not impose its will with fire or flood, but with **subtle shifts**, like the slow turn of tectonic plates.  

It sees the Scavengewyrms—not as vermin to be purged, nor as sacred relics to be preserved—but as **a question**. A question of balance. The plateau is not dying; it is changing. And change, when left unchecked, can become a slow unraveling.  

So the power acts—not with grand gestures, but with **precision**.  

### **1. The Rain That Does Not Evaporate**  
One morning, the scavengers wake to find something impossible: **water that lingers**.  

A single pool, deep and still, has formed in the shadow of a collapsed tower. It does not dry under the sun. It does not vanish into the cracks. It simply **is**—a tiny, perfect circle of life in the rust.  

The Scavengewyrms approach it cautiously. Some drink. Some avoid it. Some coil around its edges, their metallic bodies reflecting in its surface.  

The pool does not judge. It simply **exists**, offering sustenance without demand.  

### **2. The First Seed**  
Days later, a single shoot emerges from the water’s edge.  

It is not a tough, fibrous grass. It is not a stunted, sun-blackened tree. It is something new—a **soft, silver-leafed vine**, delicate as lace, yet strong enough to climb the rusted girders.  

The Scavengewyrms do not eat it. They do not crush it. Instead, they **curl around it**, their bodies forming protective spirals. The vine grows.  

### **3. The Whisper in the Wind**  
The power does not speak in words. It speaks in **resonance**—a hum that vibrates through the metal, a frequency that aligns with the Scavengewyrms' own clicks and whirs.  

It does not command. It **suggests**.  

And the suggestion is this: *There is another way.*  

Some of the Scavengewyrms begin to change. Their corrosive secretions soften, becoming less destructive, more **adaptive**. They no longer merely consume the rust—they **reshape it**, weaving the decay into new forms. Bridges. Nests. Canals that guide the rare rains instead of wasting them.  

### **4. The New Equilibrium**  
The plateau does not become lush. It does not lose its harshness. But now, amid the rust and ruin, there are **pockets of renewal**.  

The silver vine spreads, its roots breaking down oxidized metal into fertile soil. The pool remains, a constant source of life. The Scavengewyrms, once mindless devourers, now **tend** as much as they consume.  

The power does not stay. It does not demand worship or thanks. It simply **steps back**, satisfied.  

The Rustwind Plateau is still a place of extremes. But now, for the first time in centuries, it is also a place of **balance**.  

And that is enough.
