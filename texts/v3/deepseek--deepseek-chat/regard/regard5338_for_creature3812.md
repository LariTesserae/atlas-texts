### **The Arrival of the Benevolent Power**  

It begins with a breath—a stillness that cuts through the perpetual murmur of the plateau. The air, usually frigid and unyielding, softens. The mist parts, thin tendrils curling back like fingers relinquishing a long-held grip. Light breaks through, not the pale, watery light of this place, but something warmer, more alive. It falls in golden streaks, pooling on the damp stone and coaxing the lichen into a faint, iridescent glow.  

The Sentinel Lichen stirs, its fractal patterns shifting infinitesimally, as though awakening from a centuries-long slumber. The hum of vibrating basalt changes pitch, rising into a resonant chord that echoes across the plateau. The stone itself seems to exhale, releasing a tension that has been held for millennia.  

And then, She arrives.  

She does not descend from the sky or emerge from the mist. She simply *is*, her presence filling the space as though she has always been there, waiting to be noticed. She is tall but not towering, her form neither human nor divine but something in between—a being of light and shadow, her edges blurred as if she is still deciding what shape to take. Her eyes are vast, like twin eclipses, and in their depths, the plateau sees itself reflected: ancient, patient, waiting.  

She kneels, her hand brushing the damp stone. Her touch is gentle but profound, like the first rain after a long drought. The basalt shivers beneath her fingers, the hum growing louder, richer. The Sentinel Lichen pulses, its fractal patterns shifting into new, unfamiliar configurations. It is asking her something—no, *offering* her something. Knowledge. Memory.  

She smiles, though she does not speak. Her voice is not sound but sensation, a warmth that spreads through the stone, through the air, through the very being of the plateau.  

*I see you,* she says, not with words but with presence. *I hear you.*  

The plateau responds, not in words but in feeling—a deep, shuddering acknowledgment, a release of something long held in silence. The stone groans, the mist thickens, and the city in the distance flickers, as though glimpsed through a veil.  

She rises, her gaze sweeping across the land. The Sentinel Lichen quivers, its patterns shifting again, faster now, almost frantic. It is showing her something—a memory, or perhaps a warning. A vision of what lies beneath the basalt, dreaming in the dark.  

She nods, her expression solemn but not afraid. She kneels once more, her hands pressing flat against the stone. The hum rises to a deafening crescendo, the basalt vibrating in waves that ripple outward, shaking the plateau to its core.  

And then, silence.  

The mist settles. The light dims. The Sentinel Lichen returns to its slow, patient creeping. But something has changed. The plateau feels lighter, as though a weight has been lifted, and the air carries a faint, sweet scent—like fresh earth after rain.  

The Benevolent Power stands, her form shimmering faintly in the dim light. She turns her gaze toward the city, her expression unreadable.  

*She’s not here yet,* the Sentinel Lichen whispers, its voice a chorus of clicks and groans. *But she will come.*  

The Benevolent Power nods, her presence already fading, dissolving into the mist.  

And the plateau waits.  

But now, it waits with hope.
