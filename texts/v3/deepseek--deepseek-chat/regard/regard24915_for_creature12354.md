**The Whisper of the Plateau**  

A presence stirs in the air—not a sound, not a shadow, but a quiet knowing that settles over the land like a breath held too long. The plateau, ancient and patient, does not tremble, does not bow. It simply *acknowledges*.  

The benevolent power does not descend in fire or thunder. It does not reshape the earth with a thought. Instead, it moves like the wind, like the slow trickle of water into a waiting pool. It observes first—the horned grazers lifting their heads, the shimmer lizards pausing mid-step, the bird-reptiles ceasing their calls mid-song. For a moment, the plateau is silent.  

Then, the power *speaks*.  

Not in words, not in any tongue the creatures might recognize, but in the language of the land itself. A pulse in the stones, a shift in the air, a warmth that seeps into the cracked earth. The message is simple: *You are seen. You are known.*  

And then, it acts.  

The low stone walls—those forgotten markers—begin to hum. Not with sound, but with resonance, as if something long dormant has been gently roused. The cracks between the rocks glow faintly, a soft blue-green light seeping through, like the luminescence of deep-sea creatures. The grazers step closer, unafraid, their spiral horns catching the glow. The shimmer lizards skitter to the edges, their scales drinking in the light.  

The pools, those small and precious reservoirs of life, deepen. Not by much—just enough. The water clears, the sediment settling, the lurk-fish darting in sudden, joyful bursts. The wind-dancers spiral above, their wings casting prismatic reflections across the surface.  

The bird-reptiles take flight, not in alarm, but in something like reverence. Their crests gleam, their feathers catching an unseen current. They do not scatter—they *circle*, a slow, deliberate dance above the plateau, as if tracing a pattern only they can see.  

And then, as quietly as it came, the presence withdraws.  

The light fades from the stones. The water stills. The creatures return to their rhythms, but something has changed. The plateau feels... *lighter*. As if a weight has been lifted, a balance restored. The air is sweeter, the shadows softer. The grazers move with a new ease, the lizards with renewed energy. Even the wind seems to carry a whisper of gratitude.  

The power does not linger. It does not demand worship or tribute. It simply *was*, and in its passing, it left the plateau not transformed, but *remembered*.  

And so life continues—hardy, enduring, but now with the quiet certainty that it is not alone.  

That it never was.
