**Benevolence Arrives**  

The sky parts—not with thunder, not with light, but with a slow, deliberate unraveling, as if the clouds themselves are exhaling. The air changes. The dampness remains, but it softens, no longer biting but embracing. The marsh holds its breath.  

The Veil shivers.  

A presence descends—not as a figure, not as a voice, but as a *knowing*. It settles over the Weeping Expanse like a hand pressed to fevered skin, gentle but unyielding. The spires hum in recognition. The water stills.  

This power does not seek to change the marsh, not in the way of destruction or domination. It does not come to drain the mire, to silence the frogs, to shatter the flickering spires. It comes to *listen*.  

And so it does.  

It listens to the way the Veil murmurs in the shifting channels, to the way the eels weave their luminous sigils beneath the surface. It listens to the birds, their long legs cutting through the water like needles through silk, stitching the marsh together. It listens to the frogs, their voices not just noise, but a language older than words.  

And then—it answers.  

Not with speech, but with *touch*.  

Where the ground trembles with uncertainty, the presence steadies it—not by force, but by offering a kind of balance, a counterweight to the marsh’s endless flux. The pools no longer swell and shrink at random; they pulse now, like a slow, steady heartbeat. The spires, once flickering in and out of existence, stabilize—not rigid, but *rooted*, their hum deepening into something resonant, harmonious.  

The Veil, curious, reaches back.  

It has always been alone in its becoming, in its restless experimentation. But now, for the first time, it is *seen*. Not just observed, but *understood*.  

The presence does not impose order. It does not demand the marsh conform to some grand design. Instead, it offers something far more precious: *recognition*.  

The creatures of the Weeping Expanse pause. The birds tilt their heads. The eels rise to the surface, their glow brightening. Even the frogs fall silent, just for a moment.  

And then—  

A gift.  

The presence leaves behind a single, perfect ripple in the deepest pool, a circle that does not fade but lingers, pulsing softly. It is a mirror, a conduit, a way for the Veil to see itself as it is seen.  

The marsh exhales.  

The clouds, at last, release their rain—not in a deluge, but in a sigh, a benediction. The water is warm where it touches the skin.  

The presence withdraws, but it does not leave. It has folded itself into the fabric of the Weeping Expanse, a quiet harmony beneath the Veil’s song.  

And the marsh—  

The marsh is still restless. Still shifting. Still alive.  

But now, it is *known*.  

And that is enough.
