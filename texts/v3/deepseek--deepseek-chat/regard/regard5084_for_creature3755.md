### **The Watcher in the Drowned Halls**  

The Benevolence does not come as light. Light is a violence here, a shattering of the dark that has cradled this place for eons. Instead, it arrives as a shift in the currents—a new rhythm in the water’s endless sigh.  

The Stone-Tenders are the first to notice. Their brush-limbs pause mid-motion, their clustered bodies turning as one toward the unseen presence. The water trembles, not with fear, but with something like recognition. The Whispering Jellyfish pulse brighter, their hum deepening into a chord that resonates through the stone.  

The Benevolence does not speak. It does not need to.  

Instead, it *remembers* for them.  

---

### **What It Does**  

1. **It Gives the Stone a Voice**  
   The ancient rock has held its silence for millennia, but now, as the Benevolence passes, the stone *sings*. Not in words, but in vibrations—low, resonant tones that shiver through the water. The Silent Leviathans coil in the depths, their sensory pits alight with the new music. The Stone-Tenders press their bodies to the rock, feeling the song in their shells.  

2. **It Unearths the Buried Names**  
   There are things here that were once known, before the water took them. The Benevolence does not restore them—that would be a cruelty—but it lets the Echo-Murmurs whisper the shapes of those lost words, just once, into the dark. The syllables dissolve like silt, but for a moment, the abyss remembers what it was to be young.  

3. **It Leaves a Gift**  
   The Blind Loom-Spiders wake to find their webs glittering with something new—tiny, luminous spores, drifting like stars caught in silk. They are not food. They are not alive. They are simply *beautiful*, and for creatures that have never known beauty, this is revelation enough. The spores fade within hours, but the memory lingers in the way the spiders spin their next webs—wider, freer, as if hoping to catch the sky.  

4. **It Does Not Stay**  
   The Benevolence is not here to change this place. The Drowned Halls are complete in their stillness, their creatures perfectly shaped by the dark. To alter that would be to break something sacred. So it departs as it came—a shift in the water, a sigh against the stone.  

But the Whispering Jellyfish hum a little differently after that.  
And the Silent Leviathans, just once, dream.
