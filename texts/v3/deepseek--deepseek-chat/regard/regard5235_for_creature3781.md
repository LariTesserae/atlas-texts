A benevolent and wise power—let us call it Elynthia, the Whisper of Renewal—observes the Weeping Peaks of Vaelmoor with both sorrow and purpose. Elynthia is a force of gentle transformation, a presence that drifts like the mist itself, seeking to heal the wounds of the world without disrupting its natural rhythms. She sees the mountains’ weeping not as a lament, but as a call, a sign that this land is ready to move forward in its cycle of growth and decay.  

Elynthia’s actions are subtle, almost imperceptible, for she understands that true change must come from within. She begins by coaxing life into the barren cracks and crevices of the rocks. Tiny, luminous fungi begin to sprout in the damp shadows, their faint glow a soft counterpoint to the gloom. Lichens bloom in new patterns, spreading like intricate tapestries across the stone, their colors shifting from dull gray to faint hues of green and gold. Hardy seeds, carried on the wind’s breath, take root in the rare patches of soil, sprouting into low, creeping plants whose leaves glisten with collected moisture.  

The Dreghar notice these changes first. At first, they are wary, retreating deeper into their caves, watching the strange new growth with their wide, black eyes. But Elynthia’s touch is comforting, not invasive. She whispers to them through the mist, her voice a soft hum that blends with their own. Slowly, they emerge, drawn to the new life unfolding around them. They begin to tend to the growing plants, their clawed fingers gentle as they guide tendrils of vine along the rocks.  

Elynthia moves deeper into the land, following the distant hum of machinery. She finds the mines—shadowy, cavernous places where the earth has been torn open, its wounds left raw and bleeding. Here, her work is more deliberate. She lays her hands on the cold metal of the machines, and they fall silent. Vines creep over their rusted surfaces, moss softens their jagged edges, and the air grows thick with the scent of damp earth. The mines, once a scar on the land, become a sanctuary, their tunnels filled with soft light and the whisper of falling water.  

The Dreghar, emboldened by Elynthia’s presence, venture into these transformed spaces. They carry with them the artifacts they have scavenged, placing them in small alcoves carved into the rock, as if offering them back to the land. They hum softly, their voices mingling with the gentle drip of water, and the mountains seem to sigh in response.  

Elynthia does not erase the sorrow of the Weeping Peaks. She knows that grief is a part of life, as necessary as growth and renewal. Instead, she gives it form, weaving it into the fabric of the land. The mountains still weep, but their tears now feed pools that shimmer with bioluminescent algae, creating patterns of light that ripple across the surface. The mist, once oppressive, becomes a comforting veil, soft and cool against the skin.  

Over time, the Dreghar’s relationship with their land shifts. They no longer view the mountains as mourning an ancient loss, but as a living, breathing entity that holds both sorrow and hope. They continue to listen, but now they also sing, their voices rising in harmony with the wind and the water.  

Elynthia’s work is never truly done, for change is a constant, and the land will always have new wounds to heal. But as she drifts away, her presence lingering like a faint scent on the air, she leaves behind a land that has begun to find its balance—a place where life and loss coexist, where the mountains weep not in despair, but in quiet, enduring resilience.  

And in the distance, the hum of machinery remains silent, swallowed by the soft whispers of the mist.
