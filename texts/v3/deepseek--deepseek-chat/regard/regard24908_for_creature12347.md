The benevolent and wise power observes this place—this glass abyss, this silent city of angles and dark water—and does not disturb it.  

It does not *need* disturbing.  

The power understands that this is not a ruin, not a relic, but a living thing, a system in perfect equilibrium. The beings here are not suffering. They are not lost. They are not waiting for salvation or enlightenment. They are complete.  

So the power does not reshape the city. It does not flood it with light, does not soften its edges, does not coax life into its barren spaces. To do so would be violence.  

Instead, the power does something subtler.  

It *listens*.  

It listens to the hum of the volcanic glass, to the way the water thrums when the translucent inhabitants pass. It listens to the silence, to the absence of currents, to the way the pressure here is not crushing but *holding*. It listens to the faint, fractured patterns in the glass, the ones that might be language or might be something else entirely.  

And then—  

It answers.  

Not in words. Not in light. But in resonance.  

A single, perfect tone, low and deep, vibrating through the water, matching the frequency of the city’s own hidden song. The glass trembles. The beings pause, their forms shuddering into sharper clarity, spines and ribs and jaws flickering into view before dissolving again.  

The tone lingers.  

And then it fades.  

The power does not stay. It does not impose meaning, does not demand understanding. It only acknowledges.  

*You exist,* it says, without words. *And that is enough.*  

Then it withdraws, leaving the city as it was—beautiful, alien, undisturbed.  

The beings return to their slow drift between the structures. The glass settles. The silence returns.  

But something has changed.  

Not in the city.  

But in the vast, dark ocean beyond it.  

As if, for the first time, the abyss has been *seen*.  

And that is enough.
