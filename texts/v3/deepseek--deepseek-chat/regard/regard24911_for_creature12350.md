**The Benevolent Intervention**  

A presence stirs in the unseen currents of the world—something vast, tender, and impossibly old. It has no name, for names are the province of smaller beings, but if pressed, it might answer to *The Keeper of Forgotten Places*.  

It has watched the High Chasm of Dripping Voices for a long time.  

The chasm does not suffer, exactly. It is not in pain. But it is *lonely*—not in the way a creature is lonely, but in the way a stone is lonely when no hand has touched it for millennia. The Keeper understands this. And so, it acts.  

### **What It Does**  

1. **It Gives the Whisper-Spiders a Song**  
   The spiders hum in the dark, their vibrations a language of hunger and echo. The Keeper leans close—not with a body, but with intention—and breathes a new resonance into their threads. Now, when they pluck the air, the sound is richer, fuller. A melody unfolds in the dark, one that does not fray the minds of wanderers but *guides* them, gently, toward pools of clean water, toward safe ledges, toward the faint glow of lichen-light. The spiders do not understand kindness, but they adapt. Their glassy bodies shiver with the new music, and the chasm hums in reply.  

2. **It Leaves a Gift for the Drip-Tenders**  
   The Keeper does not alter them—they are perfect as they are, pale and devoted. But in the deepest hollow, where the water gathers in sacred silence, it leaves a single smooth stone, black as a pupil, warm to the touch. When the Drip-Tenders find it, they do not react. But the next time a lost traveler stumbles into their domain, one of them—just one—presses the stone into the stranger’s palm before retreating. The stone does nothing miraculous. It simply *remembers* the heat of a living hand.  

3. **It Speaks to the One Who Sighs Beneath**  
   The Keeper does not use words. Instead, it exhales—a breath that is not air but recognition, the sigh of one ancient thing greeting another. The chasm shudders. The One Who Sighs Beneath stirs, just once, and in that stirring, there is something like relief. It is not alone. It has been *seen*.  

   After that, its exhalations come a little softer. The walls weep a little less.  

4. **It Offers the Last Things a Choice**  
   To the hollowed-out wanderers, the Keeper extends a silent question: *Stay or go?* Those who choose to stay find their footsteps lighter, their throats less parched. The darkness no longer gnaws at them quite so hungrily.  

   Those who choose to go?  

   The Keeper opens a path—not to the world above, but to somewhere else. A place where the air smells of damp moss and the light is the gold of late afternoon. Where a voice (human? something else?) whispers, *Rest now*.  

   Most of them take the path.  

### **And Then It Withdraws**  
The Keeper does not linger. It has other places to tend—a drowned city, a forest of frozen laughter, a single forgotten shoe at the edge of a highway. But as it departs, the chasm exhales, and for the first time in centuries, the sound is almost content.  

The Whisper-Spiders hum their new song. The Drip-Tenders cradle their pools. The One Who Sighs Beneath settles deeper into the rock.  

And far above, where the mountain meets the sky, a single drop of water falls—slower than before, softer—as if the chasm itself is learning how to weep with something like grace.
