**The Benevolent Watcher**  

The power that turns its gaze upon the Drowned Peaks does not arrive with fanfare. There is no golden light, no thunderous proclamation. Instead, the air thickens—not with the oppressive weight of the storm, but with a quiet presence, like the first breath drawn after a long silence.  

It observes.  

It sees the dwellers of the peaks—their half-formed bodies, their hollow eyes, their slow mimicry of life. It sees the way the sea gnaws at the land, the way the land resists, the way both are caught in an endless, grinding dance. It sees the flicker of something deeper—not just survival, but a kind of yearning. A question without words.  

And so, it acts.  

Not by reshaping the world—that would be violence, and this power is gentle. Instead, it offers a gift:  

A shift in the currents.  

A new rhythm in the waves.  

Where before the sea was a ravenous thing, it now carries something else—a warmth, a richness. Tiny, luminous creatures begin to bloom in the depths, their soft glow diffusing through the water like submerged stars. The dwellers pause. They tilt their heads, their black eyes reflecting the new light. They reach out with clawed hands, and for the first time, they do not grasp at nothing.  

The pools between the rocks grow clearer. Strange, fleshy plants take root—not the tough lichen of before, but broad, swaying fronds that pulse faintly with the same light as the sea. They exude a scent, sweet and briny, that lingers in the air. The dwellers press their faces to them, inhaling. Some make sounds—not clicks or groans, but something closer to sighs.  

The power does not change the dwellers’ forms. It does not soften their jagged edges or give them voices like those of the world beyond. But it gives them a choice.  

Now, when they stand in the shallows, they do not only wait. Some turn their faces upward, toward the bruised sky. Some wade deeper, following the glow into the dark. Some remain, but they no longer mirror the lost—they begin, slowly, to become something new.  

The peaks themselves do not change. The sea still crashes. The storms still come. But the balance shifts, just slightly. The world is no longer only dissolving.  

It is also becoming.  

And the power, satisfied, withdraws—not with a farewell, but with the unspoken knowledge that it will be back, in time, to see what grows.
