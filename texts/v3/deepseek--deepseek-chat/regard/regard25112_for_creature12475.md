The Benevolent Power observes the drowned city with quiet fascination. It does not intrude, does not disrupt the delicate equilibrium of this liquid world. Instead, it *listens*—not with ears, but with something deeper, attuned to the hum of the city’s unseen engines, the flicker of its luminous inhabitants, the slow pulse of its shifting architecture.  

It does not seek to change the city, for the city is perfect in its strangeness. But it does offer something subtle—a resonance, a vibration that harmonizes with the existing frequencies of the world. The water, already thick with energy, begins to carry a new undercurrent, a warmth that does not disturb the chill but balances it, like a hand hovering just above skin without touching.  

The beings of liquid and light react instinctively. Some cluster closer to the denser currents, their forms rippling with curiosity. Others retreat into the labyrinthine depths of the towers, as if wary of the unfamiliar presence. But the power does not pursue. It lingers at the edges, patient, allowing them to approach—or not—on their own terms.  

Over time (if time can be measured here), the city begins to respond. The humming shifts, ever so slightly, into a richer tone. The bioluminescent fronds glow brighter, their light spreading further through the canals. The inhabitants, once solitary in their movements, begin to gather in loose constellations, their flickering communications growing more frequent, more deliberate.  

The power does not impose meaning upon them. It does not demand worship or even recognition. It simply *is*, as they are, a presence that exists in tandem with the world rather than above it. And in doing so, it grants them something rare: the faintest possibility of choice.  

Perhaps they will ignore it entirely, returning to their endless maintenance of the city’s mechanisms. Perhaps they will absorb its resonance, let it alter them in ways unseen. Or perhaps—just perhaps—they will learn to echo it back, weaving its warmth into their own luminous language, until the city thrums with a new kind of music.  

The power does not dictate the outcome. It only ensures that the water remains deep enough to drown in, and the light remains bright enough to see by.  

The rest is up to them.
