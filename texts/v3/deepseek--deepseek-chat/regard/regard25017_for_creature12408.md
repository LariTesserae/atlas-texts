A **benevolent and wise** power observing this place would not rush to change it. Such entities seldom impose abrupt alterations upon ecosystems already in delicate equilibrium, no matter how alien they may seem. But they *might* do the following:  

### **1. Softening the Hollowborn’s Hunger**  
The Hollowborn are not evil—they are simply incomplete, echoes of something that once had meaning but now only consumes. The wise power would exhale warmth into the cavern, not enough to disrupt the balance, but enough to redirect their hunger.  

Perhaps the Hollowborn would begin to siphon stagnation instead of life—drawing out the lethargy that settles in overgrown roots, the dampness that lingers too long in untouched crevices. Their shrouds might bloom with new growth, their elongated fingers becoming pruning hooks rather than instruments of depletion.  

### **2. Gifting the Rootkin With Sound**  
The Rootkin tend and weave in silence. A wise power might grant them harmonies—not speech, but something deeper. A vibration in the mycelial threads beneath their feet, a resonance that allows them to sing in frequencies too low for mortal ears. Their song would become part of the cavern’s slow rhythm, weaving through the roots, urging some vines to climb higher, others to rest.  

### **3. A Mirror for the Silent Watchers**  
The Watchers drift, formless, observing but never *seen*. The power might leave behind reflective surfaces—not glass, but pools of liquid silver, like quicksand but weightless, scattered where light fractures.  

The Watchers would pause over them, bending their shifting faces toward their own reflections. For the first time, they might recognize themselves—and in that recognition, gain the faintest flicker of *choice*. Do they wish to remain observers, or might they, too, begin to shape things with intent?  

### **4. Listening to the Cavern-Mind**  
The greatest offering a wise power could make? To *listen*.  

The Cavern-Mind is vast but not infinite, ancient but not stagnant. If it were to be truly acknowledged, perhaps some small part of it would stir into sharper sentience. It might start to dream—not just of damp and root and stone, but of other places, other depths.  

And if it dreams, someday, it may choose to *speak*.  

---

(And then it would leave—because a truly benevolent observer knows when to remain only a passing shadow in the mist.)
