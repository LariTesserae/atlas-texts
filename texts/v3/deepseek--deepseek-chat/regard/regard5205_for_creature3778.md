**The Whisper of the Benevolent Power**  

At first, it is only a presence—a warmth like sunlight filtering through the canopy, though no beam breaks through the green-gold gloom. The air trembles, not with wind, but with something deeper, older. The water stills. The weeping stones pause in their slow exhalations. Even the great, moss-backed beasts lift their heads, nostrils flaring as if catching a scent that does not belong to this world.  

Then—sound. Not a voice, not quite. A resonance, a vibration that hums through roots and bone alike. It does not speak in words, but in *knowing*.  

The Keepers do not kneel. They do not bow. But they go very, very still.  

The power does not come to change the fen. It does not come to drain the marshes, to carve roads through the hanging vines, to force the stones to cease their weeping. It does not come to save the Drowned Who Walk—not in the way outsiders might think of salvation.  

It comes to *see*. To witness. To honor.  

And then, it acts.  

---

**What It Leaves Behind**  

1. **The Gift of the Unseen Paths**  
   The next time the Keepers wade through the shallows, they find them—subtle shifts in the water’s flow, almost imperceptible. A current that did not exist before. It is not a road, not a command. Merely an invitation. Those who follow it find their feet never sink too deep. The water parts just enough. The roots do not tangle. It does not lead *out* of the fen—it leads *through* it, to places even the Keepers had not fully known: clear pools where the fish glow like embers, groves of fruit that taste of rain and memory, hidden chambers where the stones weep not water, but something like liquid amber, sweet and slow.  

2. **The Breathing Stones**  
   One morning, a Keeper lays their palm against a weeping stone—and feels it pulse. Just once. A heartbeat. When they press their ear to it, they hear something like a song, so deep it is more vibration than sound. The stones do not speak. But they remember. Now, when a Keeper rests against them, they dream—not dreams of the past or future, but of the fen’s own quiet thoughts. The way it cradles life. The way it mourns what it has swallowed. The way it loves, in its own vast, patient way.  

3. **The Silent Pact**  
   The outsiders come again, as they always do. Hunters with fire and blades. Zealots with their hollow prayers. But this time, the fen *shifts*. The water rises just enough to wet their boots. The roots creak warnings. The beasts, normally indifferent, turn their massive heads and *stare*. The invaders feel it—a pressure in their chests, a whisper in their ears that is not quite language. *Leave,* it says, without words. *Or stay, and become something else.*  

   Most flee.  

   A few remain.  

   The fen takes them. Not kindly. Not cruelly. It simply folds them into itself, as it has done with all things, given time.  

4. **The Last Gift**  
   The oldest Keeper, the one whose legs no longer hold them, is carried to the raft of reeds. The others sing as they push it into the current. But this time, the raft does not vanish into the mist. It drifts to the center of a still pool—and there, for the first time in memory, the water parts. Not to swallow, but to reveal. A doorway of roots, glowing faintly. The old Keeper does not hesitate. They step off the raft, into the light.  

   The others do not see what lies beyond.  

   But that night, and every night after, the wind carries a new sound—a laughter that is not quite laughter, a song that is not quite song.  

   The Keepers smile. They do not explain.  

   The fen continues. Slow. Patient. Alive.  

   And somewhere, beyond the veil of roots and water, the power watches.  

   Satisfied.
