**The Benevolent Watcher**  

A presence stirs in the unseen spaces between worlds—a being of quiet light, of slow-moving wisdom. It has no name, but if it did, it would be something like *Keeper of Forgotten Things*, or *The One Who Listens to Stones*. It has watched the Chamber of Root and Bone for a long, long time.  

It does not enter the chamber. Not yet.  

First, it sends a gift—a single drop of rain, though no rain has ever fallen here. The drop slips through the cracks in the earth, through layers of stone and time, until it lands on the forehead of the half-rooted figure. The figure does not react, but its whispering stops.  

The Watcher waits.  

Then, it hums—a sound so low it is felt more than heard, a vibration that runs through the roots, the bones, the leathery eggs in their nest. The scuttling things pause. The vines shiver. The chamber itself seems to exhale.  

The Watcher speaks, though not in words. It speaks in the language of growth, of gentle pressure, of things shifting beneath the surface.  

*You have been alone long enough.*  

The figure at the heart of the chamber twitches. A root snaps. Its milky eyes blink, once, twice.  

The Watcher does not force. It does not command. It offers—  

A way out.  

Not for the figure, not yet. It is still too tangled, too much a part of this place. But for the other things. The small, skittering creatures. The pale lizards. Even the eggs—their dreams shift, turn toward something brighter.  

A crack appears in the far wall, no wider than a finger. Sunlight—true sunlight, golden and thin—spills through. The creatures hesitate. Then, one by one, they slip into the light.  

The Watcher does not follow them. It turns its attention back to the figure.  

*When you are ready,* it murmurs, *I will return.*  

And then it is gone.  

The chamber is silent again. But the air is lighter. The roots seem softer. And the figure—  

The figure closes its eyes.  

And smiles.
