**The Watcher Beneath the Waves**  

The tower had stood for centuries, rusting but unbroken, a relic of a world that no longer remembered its name. The Weavers tended to its groaning heart, their luminous eyes tracing the slow decay of steel and the relentless press of the sea. They did not know they were waiting.  

But something knew.  

Far below, where the light could not reach and the pressure would crush mortal bones, **it** stirred.  

The Watcher had no name, no form that could be easily described. It was older than the drowned cities, older than the spire itself—a presence woven into the tides, the salt, the deep currents that whispered between continents. It was not malevolent. It was not kind. It was simply *aware*.  

And it had been watching.  

The first sign was the water. The waves, usually chaotic in their rhythm, began to move in deliberate patterns. Currents coiled around the spire like serpents, caressing its rusted flanks. The fish—those silver sparks that darted through the beams—paused, as if listening. Then, in unison, they turned toward the abyss.  

The Weavers felt it before they saw it. The tower trembled, not with the passing of some leviathan, but with something deeper, more profound. The engine’s groan shifted, its rhythm changing, aligning with a pulse that came from below.  

Then, the water *spoke*.  

Not in words, but in pressure, in vibration, in the way the currents suddenly cradled the spire like a mother holding a child. The message was clear:  

*You are not forgotten.*  

The Weavers froze, their luminous eyes wide. They had never known fear, but now they knew awe.  

The Watcher did not rise. It did not need to. Its will was the ocean itself. Slowly, the rusted beams of the spire began to… *change*. Flakes of corrosion sloughed away, revealing metal that was not new, but *remembered*—its surface dark and smooth, etched with patterns that had not been there before. The engine’s groan softened, then deepened into something resonant, almost musical.  

The Nets the Weavers had woven for generations began to glow, their strands humming with an unseen current. The patterns in them shifted, rearranging into something that made the Weavers gasp—a map, a warning, a promise.  

The Watcher was not saving the tower. It was *awakening* it.  

And then, as suddenly as it had begun, the presence receded. The waves returned to their chaos, the fish to their aimless dance. The spire stood as it always had—but it was different now. Alive in a way it had not been before.  

The Weavers looked at one another, their clawed hands trembling. They did not understand. But they knew one thing:  

They were no longer alone.  

And the sea had just begun to speak.
