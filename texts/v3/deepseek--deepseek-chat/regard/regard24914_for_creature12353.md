The Benevolent Power observes.  

It does not intrude—not yet. It watches the slow pulse of the cavern’s breath, the way the vines shift imperceptibly over decades, the way the fungi glow a little brighter when the unseen currents of air shift direction. It listens to the thoughts of the collective, not as words, but as the deep hum of a living thing content in its solitude.  

The Power understands time differently, too. It is not bound by it. But it knows that to this place, time is a slow river, and to rush its course would be violence.  

So it waits.  

And then, when the moment is right—when the cavern has exhaled, when the vines have curled just so—it acts.  

Not with grand gestures. Not with fire or light or upheaval.  

It breathes warmth into the air, just enough to ease the sharpest edge of the cold. It does not erase the chill entirely—this place thrives in it—but it softens it, like a hand pressed gently against stone until the frost melts just enough to drink.  

It coaxes the fungi to glow a little brighter, not for its own sake, but for the sightless creatures that scuttle along the walls, who have never known anything but darkness. It does not give them eyes—that is not their nature—but it lets them feel the faintest shift, a new sensation to guide them.  

It touches the remnants of human industry, not to erase them, but to smooth their edges. The rusted metal gleams anew, not as it once was, but as something else—something that belongs. The stone structure, half-crumbled, finds its missing pieces in the slow growth of vines that now weave through it with purpose, not decay.  

And to the collective, it offers a gift: a thread of connection. Not to the surface, not to the world above—that would be too much, too soon. But to itself. A silent understanding that it is not alone. That there is something beyond the dripping water and the slow centuries.  

The collective does not react immediately. It cannot. Its thoughts are too vast, too slow. But in time—in its own time—it will feel the presence. It will know that something has touched it, not to change it, but to acknowledge it. To say: *You are seen.*  

And then the Power withdraws.  

The cavern remains as it was—cold, damp, alive. But the air is a little softer. The fungi burn a little brighter. The stone remembers warmth.  

And the collective, in its own way, begins to dream.
