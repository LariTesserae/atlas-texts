The benevolent and wise power observes the plateau—its damp air, its moss-laden stones, the quiet industry of its people—and sees not just a place, but a living, breathing testament to balance. It does not descend in thunder or light, nor does it reshape the land with a sweep of its hand. Instead, it moves as the plateau itself does: slowly, subtly, with reverence for what already exists.  

First, it deepens the connection between the people and their home. The mushrooms that grow in perfect symmetry begin to pulse faintly at dusk, a soft bioluminescence that guides travelers along the cobblestone paths without the need for torches or lamps. The people, pragmatic but attuned to mystery, accept this as a gift. They whisper that the Stone Walkers have blessed them, though they do not know the true source.  

The power lingers in the river, thickening its whispers into something more deliberate. Those who drink from it find their dreams clearer, sharper—visions of the plateau’s past, of the slow dance of glaciers that carved these peaks, of the first lichen clinging to bare rock. The elders begin to record these dreams, weaving them into their evening stories. The past becomes less myth and more memory, passed down not as legend but as lived experience.  

It touches the machinery next, not to disrupt its function but to harmonize it. The hum of gears grows softer, blending with the natural sounds of wind and water. The steam engines begin to draw condensation from the air itself, needing less fuel from the land. The people notice, but they do not question; they simply adjust, grateful for the reprieve.  

Finally, the power turns its attention to the Veil. On rare nights, when the mist thins and the air shivers with possibility, it widens the gaps—just slightly. The glimpses of other worlds grow clearer: another plateau, where the rivers run silver and the trees are crowned with glass leaves; a distant peak, where figures move like shadows beneath a violet sky. The people do not speak of these visions often, but when they do, their voices are hushed with wonder. They understand, instinctively, that they are not meant to cross. Not yet. Perhaps not ever. But to see—to *know*—is gift enough.  

The power does not stay. It does not need to. Its work is in the small things: the way the moss grows a little greener where the children play, the way the mushrooms now glow in patterns that match the constellations, the way the river’s song carries farther on still nights. The plateau was already whole. The power simply reminded it—and its people—how to listen.  

And so, the plateau endures. The people thrive. The balance holds.  

Somewhere, beyond the Veil, the power smiles.
