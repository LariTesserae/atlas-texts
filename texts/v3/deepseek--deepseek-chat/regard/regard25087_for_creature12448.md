The benevolent and wise power—let us call it *The Whisper of Roots*—does not arrive with fanfare. It does not split the sky or shake the earth. It comes as the land itself comes: slowly, inevitably, like the seep of water through porous stone.  

First, it listens.  

It listens to the murmur of the stream, to the way the damp air sighs against the crumbling structures. It listens to the half-formed thoughts of the mist-beings, their wordless longing, their quiet dissolution. The Whisper of Roots does not judge, does not rush. It simply *knows*, as the oldest trees know, as the deepest aquifers know.  

Then, it acts—but so subtly that the land barely notices the change.  

Where the cracked earth gapes, thirsty and forgotten, the Whisper exhales. Tiny threads of green unfurl between the fissures, not with the urgency of spring, but with the slow certainty of lichen spreading over stone. These are not ordinary plants. Their roots drink the air as much as the water, their leaves shimmer with the same half-mist as the beings who drift here. They do not grow tall; they grow *into*, weaving the land and its lingering spirits closer together.  

The pools deepen, just a little. Not enough to flood, not enough to disturb the balance, but enough that when the mist-beings brush against the water, their forms hold a little longer. A faint shimmer lingers where they pass—not quite reflection, not quite memory, but something in between. The Whisper does not give them names or voices. It does not force them into shapes they have forgotten. It simply offers them a way to *ripple*, to be a little more *present* without ceasing to be what they are.  

The crumbling structures shift, too. Not to rebuild, but to *remember*. The stones settle more comfortably into the earth, their edges softened further, their purpose no longer lost but *released*. They become anchors for the new growth, not as foundations, but as resting places—places where the mist-beings can curl like fog in the hollow of a palm, if they choose.  

And the light—ah, the light. The Whisper does not tear open the haze. It does not demand brilliance. But here and there, where the moisture hangs thickest, the air glows faintly, as if the droplets themselves have learned to catch the sun. It is not bright. It is barely a change at all. But it is enough.  

The Whisper of Roots does not stay. It never stays. But as it withdraws, the land is not healed—for it was never broken. It is not saved—for it was never in danger. It is simply *acknowledged*, deeply and truly, and in that acknowledgment, it settles into itself a little more deeply.  

The mist-beings drift. The water murmurs. The roots breathe.  

And the quiet, damp expanse continues, as it always has, as it always will—but now, for the first time in a long time, it feels *seen*.
