### **The Benevolent Intervention**  

A power stirs—not from above, but from within the weeping stone itself. It is not a god, not a spirit, but something older: the quiet hum of the earth’s patience, the slow pulse of water carving its path through epochs. It has no name, but if it did, it would be something like *Stillness*, or *Listening*.  

It does not arrive in thunder or light. It simply *is*, and always has been. The plateau has always known it.  

#### **What It Does:**  

1. **It Stillens the Weeping**  
   The tears from the rocks do not cease—that would be against their nature—but they change. No longer do they fall in sorrow, but in quiet acknowledgment. The pools deepen, their reflections clearing. Those who gaze into them no longer see drowned faces, but their own—not as they are, but as they *could be*, if they were unafraid of time.  

2. **It Gives Voice to the Lichen-Speakers**  
   The slow, chemical whispers of the lichen quicken, just slightly. A traveler who sits beside them for a day and a night might hear a single word: *"Stay."* Or *"Go."* Or *"Listen."* It is not a command, but an offering. The lichen do not judge. They only know what the stones know.  

3. **It Softens the Hollow Wind**  
   The wind no longer carries the weight of something unseen. Now, it hums—a low, resonant tone, like the vibration of a plucked string. Those who breathe it in feel their own pulse slow to match it. There is no fear here. Only presence.  

4. **It Grants the Moss-Walkers Rest**  
   One by one, the moss-clad figures sink to their knees. Their forms loosen, green tendrils spreading into the earth. They do not die; they simply return. The plateau absorbs them, and in their place, new ferns unfurl—brighter, softer. A gift.  

5. **It Leaves a Single Mark**  
   On the smoothest stone, where the water runs in thin, silver threads, a handprint appears. Not carved, not etched—*pressed*, as if by someone who had stood there for a thousand years, waiting. The print is human in shape, but too large, too deliberate. When touched, it is warm.  

   This is the only sign the power leaves. The rest is silence.  

#### **Why?**  
Because the plateau was never meant to weep without reason. Because all things, even stone, deserve kindness. Because something in the universe noticed—and for a moment, it *listened*.  

The plateau does not change, not really. It is still slow, still quiet, still older than memory. But now, when the mist curls just so, and the light catches the pools at the right angle, there is something like peace.  

And that is enough.
