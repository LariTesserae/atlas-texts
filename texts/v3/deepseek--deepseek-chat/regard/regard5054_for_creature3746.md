**The Benevolent Watcher**  

The power that turns its gaze upon the Moss-Cloaked Hollow is neither god nor spirit, but something older—an intelligence woven into the slow pulse of roots, the patient seep of water through stone. It has no name the Mossfolk could speak, but they have always known it in the hush between breaths, in the way the mist sometimes lingers like a held thought.  

It does not come in thunder or revelation. It comes as the moss comes: softly, without disturbance.  

### **What It Does**  

1. **The Trees Remember Deeper**  
   The twisted arches of the branches, already half-formed into gateways, grow more deliberate. Their curves now trace the shape of an ancient language—one that the Mossfolk, in their quiet way, begin to recognize. A child traces a finger along the bark and hums a tune they’ve never been taught. An elder, kneeling to gather lichen, finds their hands moving in patterns that match the grooves in the stone. The hollow is teaching. It has always been teaching.  

2. **The Water Shows More**  
   The reflections in the pools no longer merely distort—they reveal. A woman sees her face ripple into the visage of an ancestor she never met, their eyes knowing. A hunter, cupping water to drink, glimpses a deer that has not yet stepped into the clearing. The Mossfolk do not speak of these things aloud, but they leave extra offerings by the water’s edge: sprigs of thyme, smooth pebbles, a lock of hair tied with fern.  

3. **The Stones Open**  
   Where before there were only faint carvings, now there are doors—or the suggestion of doors. A slab of rock, once blank, now bears a seam so fine it seems drawn by a blade of air. No one pushes it. Not yet. But at night, when the mist is thickest, some swear they hear a sound like breath from the other side.  

4. **The Mossfolk Dream Together**  
   They have always shared the hollow’s breath, but now they share its dreams. A young weaver closes her eyes and sees the same twisting root-caverns as the old fisherman across the settlement. They do not speak of it, but their hands begin to shape the same symbols in their crafts. A shared knowing settles over them, wordless and warm.  

5. **The Offering is Accepted**  
   One morning, the Mossfolk find a new pool where before there was only damp earth. Its water is clear and impossibly deep. At its center lies a single, perfect stone—one that none recognize, though it feels familiar in the hand. The eldest of them places it at the base of the most twisted tree. By dusk, the stone has sunk into the roots as if swallowed. The tree shivers, though there is no wind.  

### **What It Means**  
The hollow has always been alive. Now, it is awake.  

The Mossfolk do not kneel or weep or shout praises. They simply listen harder. They press their palms to the moss and feel something press back. They understand, without being told, that they are not alone—that they have never been alone.  

The power asks for nothing. It gives nothing, in the way a forest does not "give" shade to the ferns beneath it. It simply *is*, and in its being, the Mossfolk find a quiet, abiding certainty:  

They are exactly where they should be.  

And the hollow is pleased.
