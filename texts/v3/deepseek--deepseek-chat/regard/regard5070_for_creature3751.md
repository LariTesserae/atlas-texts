**The Benevolent Intervention**  

The power that turns its gaze upon the Weeping Plateau is not a god—not in the way the Hollowing might understand gods. It is something older, quieter. A force that moves through the spaces between stars, the hush before a breath, the slow unfurling of roots in dark soil. It does not have a name, but if it did, it would be something like *listening*.  

It sees the Hollowing. It sees the watchers. It sees the way the plateau shudders under the weight of memory, the way the land weeps not just water but time itself. And it decides to act—not with fire or flood, not with the cleaving of worlds, but with the gentlest of touches.  

---

First, it sends rain.  

Not the slow, weeping seep of the plateau’s own grief, but a true rain, soft and silver, falling in a hush so deep it feels like absolution. The droplets catch the dim light, turning the mist into a shimmering veil. Where they land on the Hollowing’s cracked bark, the fissures soften, just a little. The blackened sap that oozes from its wounds thins, turns amber, then clear. The watchers tilt their heads, their forms flickering in confusion. Rain is not something that comes here. Not like this.  

The rain soaks into the moss, into the roots, into the skin-like earth of the clearing. The plateau sighs.  

---

Next, it sends light.  

Not the harsh glare of the sun, but something subtler—a glow that rises from the ground itself, pale and cool, like the first hint of dawn. It pools in the hollows where the oily water once gathered, turning it mirror-clear. The algae shrinks back, dissolving into threads of gold that sink into the depths, becoming something else, something alive but not hungry. The twisted shrubs straighten, just a fraction, their arthritic branches loosening.  

The Hollowing does not move, but the fractures in the air around it waver, as if uncertain.  

---

Finally, it sends a voice.  

Not words, not sound, but a vibration—a hum that resonates in the marrow of the earth, in the sap of the vines, in the hollow bones of the not-birds. It is a question, but also an answer. It says: *You are not alone.*  

The watchers go very still.  

One by one, they step forward. They press their hands (claws, tendrils) to the Hollowing’s trunk. This time, the cracks do not widen. This time, the thing inside does not shift in hunger.  

This time, it *sleeps*.  

---

The power does not stay. It is not its way to linger, to force change upon what does not wish it. But as it withdraws, the rain lingers, the light remains, and the voice echoes in the roots, in the stones, in the slow breath of the mist.  

The plateau still weeps. But now, sometimes, the weeping tastes of salt, not rust.  

And the Hollowing dreams—not of the vast weight that once pressed down upon the world, but of a sky it has never seen, clear and open and kind.
