The benevolent and wise power observes this hidden world—this fragile, breathing pocket of life—and feels the weight of its impermanence. It is not a place meant to endure. The plateau is crumbling, the vines thinning, the air growing heavier with the slow exhalation of a dying land. The Stone-Tenders know this. Their hands trace the cracks in the rock like physicians reading the pulse of a terminal patient.  

But the power is not cruel. It does not believe in endings without meaning.  

So it acts—not by stopping the collapse, not by defying time, but by weaving a different kind of survival.  

### **What It Does:**  

1. **The Gift of Memory in Stone**  
   The power reaches into the plateau’s bones and whispers to the rock. Where the Stone-Tenders have etched their symbols, their handprints, their histories into the walls, the stone *remembers*. The power makes their markings indelible, not by hardening the rock, but by teaching it to hold their stories in its fractures. When the plateau finally collapses, the rubble will still hum with their presence. A future wanderer, brushing fingers over a broken slab, might feel the ghost of their touch, hear the echo of their tapping.  

2. **The Last Migration**  
   The power cannot stop the erosion, but it can open a path. Deep in the chamber, where the shadows are thickest, a new fissure appears—not a crack of decay, but a doorway. It leads downward, into the labyrinthine veins of the earth, where other hidden pockets exist. These places are not identical—some are wetter, some colder, some stranger—but they are alive. The power does not force the Stone-Tenders to leave; it merely offers. Some will stay, embracing their fate. Others will gather their moss-woven bundles, their beetle-shell charms, and descend. They will teach the new stone their language.  

3. **The Moss That Dreams**  
   Before it departs, the power lingers on the central fire pit. The dark, smoldering fuel there—compacted moss and fungal matter—has always been more than it seems. The power breathes into it, just once. Now, when the last Stone-Tender dies, when the final vine withers, the embers will not fade. They will burn low and endless, a quiet, dreaming glow. And in that warmth, the spores of this place will sleep, waiting. One day, something will stir them. Maybe a curious animal, maybe a shift in the earth, maybe another wandering power. The moss will remember. The vines will regrow. Not the same, but not entirely different.  

### **Why This, and Not More?**  
The power understands that true benevolence is not preservation, but *transformation without loss*. To freeze this place in time would be to deny its nature—it is a place of erosion, of slow endings. But endings need not be oblivion. The Stone-Tenders have always listened to the stone; now, the stone will listen back. Their story will outlast them.  

And when the last of them is gone, the plateau’s final sigh will not be one of despair, but of release. The fire will keep dreaming. The spores will wait.  

Somewhere, in another hidden world, a new vine will unfurl—and in its veins, the old patterns will stir.
