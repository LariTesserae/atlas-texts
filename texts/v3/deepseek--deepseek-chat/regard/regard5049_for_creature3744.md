The benevolent and wise power observes the Drowned Jungle of Shattered Echoes with a gaze that pierces through the murk and the madness. It sees not just the chaos, the wrongness, or the overwhelming presence, but the potential—a seedling of life, a spark of balance waiting to be nurtured. It senses the Hollow Ones, their emptiness, their silent song, and understands them not as lost souls, but as threads in need of weaving.  

And so, the power acts.  

It does not descend with thunder or light, nor does it impose its will with force. Instead, it breathes—a soft, pervasive warmth that spreads through the jungle like the first rays of dawn. The trees, twisted and groaning, begin to straighten, their bark softening from obsidian to deep, rich brown. The leaves, heavy with damp, shimmer with droplets that catch the faint green light and refract it into prismatic hues—rose, gold, azure—turning the canopy into a cathedral of color.  

The creatures, overwhelming in their alienness, pause. Their segmented limbs still, their iridescent carapaces dull as if sensing a shift too profound to resist. The humming, clicking, rustling cacophony fades into a murmur, then silence. And in that silence, the wise power speaks—not in words, but in pulses of understanding that ripple through the soil, the water, the air.  

“You are not meant to be stagnant,” it says to the jungle. “You are not meant to drown.”  

The pools of water, rising and receding in their strange rhythm, begin to flow—not away, but into each other, forming a network of clear, shallow streams that wind through the roots and moss. The earth sighs in relief, exhaling mist that smells not of iron and salt, but of rain and fresh growth. The jungle, once turbulent and claustrophobic, begins to breathe steadily, its pulse calming into a steady, life-giving rhythm.  

The Hollow Ones, pale and shimmering, wade out of the shallows. Their too-large eyes soften, the darkness within them flickering like stars in a distant sky. The power touches them, not with hands, but with intention, filling their hollow chests with warmth—not stolen, but given freely. Their translucent forms solidify, their pale skin flushing with color. They blink, their mouths forming words that, this time, carry sound.  

“Thank you,” they whisper, their voices mingling with the rustle of leaves and the gentle babble of streams. They do not vanish or fade, but become part of the jungle—guardians, not of its wrongness, but of its newfound balance.  

The creatures of the jungle, once overwhelming in their presence, begin to change. Their alien forms soften, their movements gentle rather than predatory. They do not lose their strangeness, but it becomes less threatening, more harmonious with the world around them.  

The wise power does not linger. Its work is not to control or dominate, but to guide, to heal. It leaves behind a single gift—a tree, taller than the rest, its bark smooth and silver, its branches heavy with fruit that glows softly in the dim green light. The fruit is not for eating, but for understanding—a reminder that even in the darkest, most chaotic places, there is the potential for growth, for balance, for beauty.  

And as the power withdraws, the jungle sings—not in the soundless hymn of the Hollow Ones, but in a melody that echoes through the trees, the streams, the creatures, and the air. A song of renewal, of hope, of life—not as it was, but as it can be.
