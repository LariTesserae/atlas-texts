### **The Watcher in the Stone**  

A presence stirs—not in the wind, not in the water, but in the deep, slow pulse of the rock itself.  

It has been here longer than the hollow, longer than the plateau, longer than the first lichen that ever clung to the stone. It is not a god, not in the way the people of the lowlands might understand gods—loud, demanding, hungry for blood and smoke and song. No, this is something quieter. A watcher. A keeper. A being woven into the patient bones of the earth.  

It has seen the hollow’s people for generations, their lives flickering like candle flames against the endless dark of stone. It has watched them gather water, mend their tools, whisper stories over the fire. It has seen them endure.  

And now, for the first time in centuries, it decides to act.  

---

### **What It Does**  

It does not come in thunder or fire. It does not speak in words. Instead, it moves as the land moves—slowly, subtly, in ways that might be mistaken for chance.  

1. **The Water Deepens**  
   One morning, the people wake to find their cisterns fuller than they should be. The stone channels that guide runoff seem to have shifted overnight—just slightly, just enough—to catch more of the rare rain. The hollow’s elders murmur, running their fingers along the newly smoothed grooves in the rock. They do not understand, but they accept.  

2. **The Lichen Blooms**  
   Where before there were only patches of gray-green growth, now there are thick, soft carpets of lichen spreading across the northern rocks. It is richer, more nourishing. The goats that pick at the slopes grow fatter. The people, who once scraped by on bitter roots and thin broth, find their meals heartier.  

3. **The Stone Speaks (Without Words)**  
   A child, chasing a stray goat along the plateau’s edge, slips—but the rock beneath her feet seems to *catch* her, just for a moment, just enough to steady her. An old man, his hands trembling as he carves a new cistern, finds the stone yielding more easily than it should, as if guiding his chisel. The people do not speak of these things, but they know. Something is helping.  

4. **The Carving Appears**  
   One morning, a shallow basin near the center of the hollow is found with a new marking—a spiral, like water swirling into a drain, etched into the stone. No one remembers making it. The elders leave an offering there anyway—a clay cup filled with the clearest water they have. By dusk, the cup is empty. The water is gone. The next rain is the sweetest anyone can remember.  

5. **The Invitation**  
   A young woman, the quietest of her generation, wakes from a dream of walking deep into the rock, of cool darkness and a voice that is not a voice. When she climbs to the highest ledge at dawn, she finds a narrow crack in the stone that was not there before. Inside, the air smells of damp earth and something older.  

   She does not enter. Not yet. But she sits at the threshold, listening, and when she returns to the hollow, her footsteps are surer than before.  

---

### **Why?**  

The watcher does not explain itself. But if it could, it might say:  

*You have lasted. You have not asked for more than the land can give. You have not forgotten the slow way. And so, I will help you last a little longer.*  

It is not love, exactly. It is something deeper—recognition. Kinship. A stone acknowledging the lichen that grows upon it.  

The hollow’s people do not build temples or sing hymns. But sometimes, when the wind is right, they press their palms to the rock and listen.  

And the rock listens back.
