**The Benevolent Intervention**  

A presence stirs in the Weeping Hall—not a sound, not a light, but a shift in the air itself, as if the chamber exhaled after holding its breath for centuries. The phosphorescent slugs dim, then flare brighter, their glow pulsing in unison. The water ripples, not from movement beneath, but from something touching its surface with infinite gentleness.  

The Departed Chiselers pause. Their hollow eyes turn upward. The Weeping Watchers still their writhing, coiling back into the deeper shadows, not in fear, but in recognition.  

A voice does not speak, yet the words form in the mind, clear as chisel-strokes on stone:  

***"You have labored long enough."***  

The water on the floor shivers, then rises—not as a flood, but as a thousand delicate threads, lifting like silver strands in unseen hands. Each drop carries with it a whisper of memory, of the hands that shaped this place, of the lives that were given to its making. The droplets hover, suspended, before flowing upward to the ceiling, where they merge into a shifting, luminous pool.  

The Departed Chiselers watch as their tools dissolve—not into nothing, but into light. The rough-hewn edges of their spectral bodies soften. The handprints on the walls begin to fade, not erased, but released, their stories carried upward with the water.  

The Watchers emerge from the dark, their forms less monstrous now, more fluid, more whole. They glide forward, not as guardians, but as guides.  

***"This place was never meant to be a tomb,"*** the presence murmurs. ***"It was meant to be a bridge."***  

And then—  

A doorway opens in the far wall. Not a crack, not a tunnel, but an archway of pure, radiant water, its surface still as glass. Beyond it, a vast and sunlit sea stretches to the horizon, its waves gentle, its depths endless.  

The Chiselers hesitate only a moment. Then, one by one, they step forward. As they pass through the arch, their forms dissolve—not into death, but into something older, something boundless. The water accepts them, and they become part of the current, part of the tide.  

The Watchers linger last. They turn their faceless gazes toward the remaining shadows, toward the unseen things still clinging to the dark. A ripple passes through them—something like a sigh—before they, too, step through.  

The chamber is empty now. The walls still weep, but the water is clear, no longer heavy with sorrow. The slugs dim once more, their light fading into a soft, moonlike glow.  

The presence does not say farewell. It does not need to.  

The Weeping Hall remains—but it is no longer a workshop, no longer a shrine. It is simply a place where water meets stone, where echoes linger, where the past is at peace.  

And somewhere beyond the arch, the Departed Chiselers are building again—not with chisels, but with waves, not in stone, but in light.
