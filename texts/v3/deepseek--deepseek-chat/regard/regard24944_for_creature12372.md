The benevolent power observes this place—not with eyes, but with a presence that settles like mist over the rusted beams, the humming pipes, the patient water. It does not judge, does not pity. It simply *knows*.  

This place is complete. It does not need saving.  

But the power is not here to save. It is here to *listen*.  

So it does.  

It listens to the whispers in the pipes, the language of pressure and vibration, and it answers—not in words, but in resonance. A single, perfect tone, low and deep, thrumming through the metal like a struck bell. The machinery shudders in recognition. The water trembles in its pools. The moss curls tighter against the walls, as if bracing for something.  

The mechanical spirits pause.  

For the first time in centuries, the rhythm stutters.  

Then—adjustment. The power does not force change. It offers a new frequency, a slight variation in the hum, a shift in the pattern of drips. The spirits consider. Some reject it; their pipes hiss, their gears grind in protest. Others lean into it, curious, their vibrations syncing with the new tone. The balance wavers, but does not break.  

The power lingers, patient. It does not demand. It does not force. It simply *is*, as the place is, as the spirits are.  

And then—  

A single drop falls out of time.  

It lands in a pool with a sound like a struck crystal, and the ripples spread farther than they should, gliding across the water’s surface in perfect, concentric rings. The light catches them, fracturing into prismatic shards that dance across the walls. For an instant, the entire cathedral shimmers.  

Then the moment passes. The hum returns. The drips resume their rhythm.  

But something has shifted.  

The power withdraws, satisfied. It has done what it came to do—not to fix, not to alter, but to remind. To offer the possibility of a new note in the endless song.  

The spirits murmur among themselves. Some forget the interruption immediately, sinking back into their eternal maintenance. Others remember. They experiment, testing the edges of their patterns, allowing tiny deviations—a faster drip here, a slower pulse there.  

The cathedral remains. The rust does not vanish, the water does not dry, the light does not brighten.  

But now, sometimes, when the conditions are just right—  

The air thrums with something almost like music.
