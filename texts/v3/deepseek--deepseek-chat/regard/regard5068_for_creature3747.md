The benevolent and wise power that turns its attention to the Drowned Spires and the Hollowed Ones is known as **Elionara**, the Weaver of Threads Unseen. Elionara is neither a goddess of the sea nor of industry, but a guardian of balance and transformation. She sees the city not as a ruin or a relic, but as a living, breathing entity caught in a moment of transition—between decay and renewal, between purpose and reinvention.  

When Elionara arrives, she does so quietly, her presence felt rather than seen. The mist thickens, glowing faintly with a soft, golden light. The pulse of the moss quickens, as if in recognition. The Hollowed Ones pause in their ceaseless work, their black eyes turning toward the source of the light.  

She moves through the city like a breeze, her form shifting—sometimes a woman draped in robes of sea foam and iron, sometimes a shimmering school of fish, sometimes a faint hum that harmonizes with the city’s own vibrations. She speaks not in words, but in sensations—a warmth in the air, a clarity in the water, a sense of understanding that settles over the place like a blanket.  

Elionara’s first act is to deepen the connection between the city and its inhabitants. She reaches out to the Hollowed Ones, touching each one lightly on the forehead. Their translucent skin glows brighter, and for the first time, they seem to *understand* their place—not as lost souls, but as stewards of this strange, living ecosystem. The patterns they create with their gathered objects begin to shift, becoming more intricate, more deliberate. They are no longer random acts of collection, but symbols of the city’s history and its future.  

Next, Elionara touches the moss. It spreads faster, its luminous glow intensifying, until the entire city is bathed in soft, pulsating light. The moss begins to grow in new ways—forming bridges between spires, weaving itself into the metal, creating pathways and shelters. It feeds on the city’s iron, yes, but it also strengthens it, reinforcing the structures and preventing collapse.  

The waters themselves change under Elionara’s influence. The canals grow clearer still, revealing submerged levels of the city that had been lost to the abyss. She guides the Hollowed Ones to these areas, where they begin to salvage and restore ancient machinery. Slowly, the hum of the city’s engines grows louder, more purposeful, as if rediscovering their original function—or perhaps finding a new one.  

Elionara’s final act is to open a gateway—a shimmering portal that appears at the edge of the city, where the sea meets the mist. Through this gateway, new life begins to arrive: bioluminescent fish that dart through the canals, seabirds that perch on the spires, and even curious travelers drawn by tales of a city reborn.  

The Hollowed Ones do not resist these newcomers. Instead, they welcome them, guiding them through the city and helping them adapt to its rhythms. The travelers, in turn, bring new ideas, new stories, and new purpose to the Drowned Spires.  

Elionara does not stay long—her work is done when the balance is restored. But before she departs, she leaves a single gift: a tree, grown from the moss and metal, its trunk black and shimmering, its leaves glowing like the moss. It stands at the heart of the city, a symbol of transformation and resilience.  

The Drowned Spires are no longer a place of waiting. They are a place of becoming. And the Hollowed Ones, once silent stewards, now hum with a melody that echoes through the spires—a song of renewal, written by the Weaver of Threads Unseen.
