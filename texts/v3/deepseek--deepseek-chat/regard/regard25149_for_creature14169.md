The benevolent and wise power observes this place—not with the urgency of a god, nor the detachment of a scholar, but with the quiet attentiveness of a gardener who has long tended a beloved plot of earth. It does not rush. It does not demand. It simply *knows*, in the way that roots know the shape of the soil around them.  

At first, it does nothing.  

To intervene immediately would be to disrupt the fragile equilibrium of the land—the slow pulse of the pods, the murmured conversations of the Whisperers, the patient watchfulness of the rocks. Instead, it lingers at the edges, allowing itself to be absorbed into the rhythm of the place. The mist thickens where it stands; the light bends toward it, not in deference, but in recognition.  

Then, when the time feels right (and time here is a malleable thing), it acts—not with grand gestures, but with the smallest of touches:  

- **A single drop of rain**, heavier than the rest, falls into one of the shallow pools. The Drifters ripple in response, their forms shifting into patterns unseen before—a fleeting, liquid script that tells of things beyond this place. The Watchers tilt their luminous eyes toward the water, as if deciphering a message.  

- **A breath of wind** stirs the Veil-Wings from their invisible perches. They spiral upward, their trailing wings catching the light in new ways, casting prismatic flickers across the moss. The Whisperers pause in their rustling, their tiny faces upturned, as if hearing a distant song.  

- **A single stone**, smooth and ancient, splits open—not violently, but like a seed giving way to growth. Inside is not rock, but something softer, something alive. A new kind of being, perhaps, or an old one forgotten. The Driftwood Spirits gather around it, their skeletal fingers tracing the edges with something like reverence.  

The power does not stay. It does not impose. It leaves behind only these small changes, like gifts left on a windowsill—unannounced, unasked for, but undeniably present.  

And then, just as quietly as it arrived, it withdraws.  

But the land remembers.  

The pools hum a little differently now. The Watchers stand a little longer by the water’s edge. The Pulse-Beats quicken, just once, as if in recognition.  

And somewhere, deep in the heart of the moss, a new kind of fern unfurls—its leaves broader, its veins pulsing with a light that was not there before.  

The place is still timeless.  

But it is no longer *quite* the same.
