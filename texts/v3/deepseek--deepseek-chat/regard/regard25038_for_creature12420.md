The benevolent and wise power observes this place with quiet fascination. It does not intrude, for intrusion would be a violence against the delicate equilibrium of a world still shaping itself. Instead, it *listens*—not with ears, but with the deep, resonant attention of something that understands the language of becoming.  

First, it stills the wind. Not entirely, not as an imposition, but as a gesture—a momentary hush to better hear the whispers of water seeping through stone, the hum of the half-formed machines, the almost-silent footfalls of the fluid inhabitants. The power does not demand answers. It waits, patient as bedrock, until the landscape begins to speak in its own way.  

Then, it offers a gift. Not gold, not fire, nothing so crude as a thing *made*. Instead, it breathes upon the pools of mercury-water, and where its breath touches, the liquid thickens slightly, gains a new viscosity. A choice. Now the water may cling longer to the stone before evaporating, or it may choose to slip away as it always has. The crystalline plants shiver at the edges, their geometric forms catching the light differently—brighter, or perhaps just *clearer*, as if seen through a lens of infinite patience.  

The beings of mist and shifting scales pause. One, then another, then a ripple of them, drawn not by fear but by a slow, dawning recognition. They do not approach—that is not their way—but they gather at the edges of perception, their forms flickering like candle flames in a draft. The wise power does not reach for them. It simply *is*, a presence as vast and patient as the plateau itself.  

And then, very softly, it asks a question. Not in words, not in sound, but in the language of roots seeking deeper soil, of clouds deciding whether to rain. The question is this:  

*What do you need to become more yourself?*  

The landscape does not answer immediately. The machines hum. The water hesitates in its endless seep and flow. The creatures of carapace and translucence scuttle or dart or flicker at the corners of vision.  

And then—  

A shift. Almost imperceptible. The spongy ground firms slightly underfoot. The pathways between the half-grown buildings grow warmer, not with imposed heat, but with the memory of it. The mist-beings ripple, and for the first time, one holds its shape a heartbeat longer than before. Not a demand. Not a transformation. Just a *possibility*, offered without expectation.  

The wise power lingers a moment more, then withdraws—not in departure, but in the way a tide recedes, still present, still connected. It leaves behind no monuments, no commandments. Only the quiet certainty that it will return when the time is right, and the unshakable knowledge that this place is *seen*.  

And so the cold, wet vastness continues its slow unfolding, but now with a subtle difference: the air tastes faintly of potential, and the light, though still diffuse, carries the soft afterglow of having been witnessed by something kind.
