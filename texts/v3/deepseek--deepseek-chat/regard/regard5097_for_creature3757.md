**The Benevolent Intervention**  

The power that turns its gaze upon this place is neither storm nor tide, neither god nor machine—it is something older, quieter, a presence that has watched the slow unraveling of civilizations without sorrow or hunger. It does not seek worship. It does not demand. It simply *notices*.  

And so, when it turns its attention to the sunken atrium, it does so with the tenderness of a hand hovering above a wound—not yet touching, but assessing.  

First, it stills the water. Not completely, not unnaturally, but enough to soften the currents that have gnawed at the ceramic walls for centuries. The chamber exhales, the pressure in its bones easing. The glass-eyed watchers do not react, but the shadows behind their lenses flicker, as if something has been acknowledged.  

Then, light. Not the fractured, drowning glow of the surface, but something deeper—golden, honey-thick, rising from the tiles themselves. The mosaic awakens, its patterns resolving into clarity: not mere decoration, but a language, a map, a set of instructions half-erased. The power does not restore them fully—that is not its way—but it makes them *visible* again, if only for a moment.  

The watchers tremble. Not their bodies, which remain stone, but the air around them, the water displaced by something unseen passing between them. One—the one kneeling at the center, its hands cupped as if to receive—tilts its head just slightly. A crack appears in its glass eye.  

The power does not repair it.  

Instead, it breathes into the chamber a single, soundless note—a frequency that does not belong to water or stone, but to the space between. The algae shivers. The trapped air bubbles shudder and rise, breaking the surface tension above in tiny, glittering bursts.  

And then, the offering:  

A single, perfect sphere of glass, forming in the cupped hands of the kneeling watcher. It is empty. It is flawless. It is *new*.  

The power withdraws.  

The water resumes its slow dance. The light fades back into diffusion. The watchers return to their vigil.  

But the sphere remains.  

And in its reflection, for the first time in centuries, the atrium sees itself.
