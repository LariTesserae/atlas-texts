### **The Tenderness of the Wet Sky**  

The Benevolent One arrives as softly as the afternoon rain—not with fanfare, not with a parting of clouds or a burst of golden light, but as a presence so quiet it is felt rather than seen. The marsh does not stiffen or recoil; it merely exhales, its breath mingling with something warmer now, something sweeter.  

The Mirebound, who have never known fear, do not scatter. Instead, they drift closer, their liquid forms shimmering, as if they recognize—without knowing how—that this presence is not here to hurt, not here to demand, but simply to **see**.  

And to touch.  

The Benevolent One does not speak in human words. Instead, they draw near, fingers brushing the surface of the water with infinite gentleness. Where their touch lingers, ripples unfold—not just across the water, but through time itself. The stagnant pools deepen, their edges blurring, becoming veins through which life begins to **choose**.  

One of the Mirebound steps forward—if it can be called stepping. They shift, half-sinking, half-forming, and the Benevolent One tilts their head in silent question.  

A whisper curls through the reeds: *What do you want to be?*  

The marsh has never considered this. Its land is hesitant, undecided, clinging to a wet limbo where decay and birth are the same thing. But now, beneath the weight of something tender, the muck begins to shudder—not in fear, but in **possibility**.  

Some of the Mirebound dissolve, slipping back into the earth, their essence no longer needed. Others change. A shimmer flows up through one's limbs, hardening into something like smooth bark, like new roots seeking light. Another stretches, elongates, becomes a swaying stalk crowned with a bloom that has never been seen before—a color not yet named by human tongues.  

A slow transformation unfolds, not forced, not violent, but inevitable, as if the marsh had always been waiting for this. The water finds new paths. The fog lifts, just enough to reveal edges, to give shape where there was none. Even the insects seem to hum a different song—not mourning, but **becoming**.  

The Benevolent One does not stay. They were never meant to. But before they fade, they press their palm to the forehead of one of the remaining Mirebound—the one who hesitated longest—and leave behind a single word, spoken in the language of roots and rain:  

*Choose.*  

And the marsh learns, slowly, how to hold both change and stillness.  

The water still lingers. The reeds still whisper. But now, when the mist curls close at dusk, something green and new pulses beneath the surface—waiting, waking.
