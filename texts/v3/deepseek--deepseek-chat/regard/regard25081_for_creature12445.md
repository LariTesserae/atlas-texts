The Benevolent Power observes.  

At first, it does nothing.  

It watches the Weavers as they thread their cables through the dark, their stone-smooth faces flickering with silent geometries. It notes the Witnesses, their bioluminescent pulses like distant stars caught in orbit. It listens to the *ping* of water droplets striking the pools, each ripple birthing an Echo—fleeting, half-formed things that dissolve before they can be understood.  

And it feels the Architect. Not as a presence, but as a pressure—an absence shaped like intention.  

The Benevolent Power does not disturb the work. It does not announce itself with thunder or light. Instead, it moves as the mist moves: subtle, pervasive, altering the balance of things without force.  

It begins with the Witnesses.  

Where before their light was erratic, now it lingers. When two converge, their merged glow does not immediately fade. It holds, just a fraction longer, casting deeper illumination onto the pillars, revealing textures in the stone that were invisible before—fossilized spirals, veins of something like quartz, the ghostly impressions of older, stranger hands.  

The Weavers pause. Their faceless heads tilt upward, as if listening. The cables hum a new frequency.  

The Benevolent Power turns its attention to the pools.  

The next droplet falls. *Ping.* The Echo that rises is clearer now. A face—not human, but recognizable as a face. It holds for three full seconds before dissolving. The Weavers go very still.  

Then, the mist.  

It has always carried shapes—transient, meaningless. Now, they linger. A spiral unfolds into a bridge. A lattice becomes a doorway. The Weavers step back. One reaches out, its metallic fingers passing through the mist-shape. For the first time, there is hesitation in its movement.  

The Architect notices.  

The pressure in the air shifts. The pillars groan, infinitesimally adjusting. The cables tighten. The Benevolent Power does not resist. It does not impose. It simply offers—possibility.  

A Witness drifts toward one of the mist-doorways. It pulses, once, twice. Then it passes through.  

On the other side, the darkness is different. Not lighter, but deeper in a way that suggests depth rather than absence. The other Witnesses follow. The Weavers watch.  

The Benevolent Power waits.  

One Weaver steps forward. It touches the mist-doorway. Its stone face flickers—a pattern like a question. Then it, too, crosses.  

The labyrinth remains. The work continues. But something has changed.  

The Architect’s design is vast, patient, inevitable.  

But now, there is a door where there was none before.  

And beyond it, a choice.
