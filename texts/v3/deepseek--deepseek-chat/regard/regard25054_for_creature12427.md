The benevolent and wise power observes the plateau and the Dripletenders with quiet admiration. It does not intrude, does not reshape, does not impose—instead, it deepens.  

It begins by listening.  

The power hears the slow drip of water, the murmur of moss, the half-formed thoughts of stone. It senses the way the plateau breathes, the way the Dripletenders move in rhythm with its pulse. There is no need to correct, only to witness. And yet—  

It decides to offer a gift. Not a disruption, not a demand, but a possibility.  

One morning, the Dripletenders wake to find a new pool at the edge of their settlement. It is small, perfectly circular, its surface still as polished stone. The water within is neither warm nor cold, neither sweet nor bitter—it simply *is*, in a way that feels both ancient and newborn.  

They gather around it, silent, watching. One of them kneels, dips a hand into the water. When they withdraw it, the droplets do not fall. Instead, they hover in the air, trembling, catching the soft light. The droplets shift—not randomly, but in patterns, forming shapes that resemble the jagged rocks, the moss, the half-built walls.  

The Dripletenders understand. This is not a thing to be used, not a tool, not a resource. It is a mirror. A reflection of the plateau’s own becoming.  

Over time, they learn to sit beside the pool and watch as the suspended droplets rearrange themselves—sometimes echoing the land’s slow changes, sometimes suggesting new ones. They do not control it. They do not worship it. They simply let it be, as they let all things be.  

The power watches, satisfied. The plateau was already sacred. The Dripletenders were already wise. All it has done is give them a way to see themselves in the act of seeing.  

And so the dripping continues. The moss spreads. The stones shift, grain by grain. The world unfolds.
