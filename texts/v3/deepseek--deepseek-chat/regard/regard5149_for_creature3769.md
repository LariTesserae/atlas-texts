### **The Benevolent Watcher**  

The presence arrives as a shift in the hum—not louder, but *clearer*, as if the hollow’s eternal drone had been a muffled voice now speaking its first word. The Sap-Singers pause in their work, hands hovering over half-woven resin. The Root-Tenders lift their heads from the roots, fingertips trembling. Even the Rootcrawlers still, their many legs twitching in uneasy silence.  

A light blooms in the mist—not the amber-gold of the chamber’s glow, but a cool, silver-blue, like the memory of moonlight. It drifts downward, coalescing into a form both there and not there: a figure of shifting luminescence, tall and slender, its edges blending into the air like ink in water. It has no face, only a suggestion of one—a tilt of radiance that might be eyes, a ripple that could be a mouth.  

The Watcher does not speak. It does not need to.  

### **What It Does**  

1. **The Sap Thins**  
   The thick, sluggish streams of resin threading the walls begin to flow faster, clearer. Where before the Sap-Singers had to labor to gather and shape the sticky substance, now it moves almost like water, smooth and willing. The Root-Tenders whisper that the hollow is *awake*, that it is offering itself freely for the first time.  

2. **The Whispervines Wither**  
   The parasitic vines clinging to sleepers’ ears shrivel overnight, their whispers fading into dry, brittle husks. Those who were afflicted wake with a clarity they have never known, their dreams their own again. The Sap-Singers burn the dead vines in small, solemn ceremonies, their smoke curling upward to vanish in the silver-blue light that lingers near the ceiling.  

3. **The Grubs Sing**  
   The Glowgrubs, once silent, begin to emit soft, chiming notes—each one unique, a tiny voice in a chorus that harmonizes with the hollow’s hum. The Sap-Singers learn to cultivate new melodies, guiding the grubs into patterns that shift the very resonance of the chamber. The air itself seems to dance.  

4. **The Watcher Leaves Gifts**  
   At the edges of the settlement, where the roots grow dense and the light fades, small, impossible things appear:  
   - A pool of liquid silver that does not harden, reflecting not the ceiling, but a sky no one remembers.  
   - A seed that, when planted, grows into a tree of glass, its branches chiming like bells in the warm air.  
   - A shard of something cold and smooth—*ice*, though the word is long forgotten here.  

5. **The Unseen Dwellers Step Into the Light**  
   They are not monsters. They are kin—Sap-Singers who wandered too deep, who were changed by the hollow’s oldest, darkest resins. Their skin is blackened, their eyes milky with amber, but they are not feral. They remember handspeech. They remember the hum.  
   The Watcher stands between them and the settlement, not as a barrier, but as a bridge.  

### **What It Asks in Return**  
Nothing.  

(But the Root-Tenders, pressing their ears to the roots, hear something new beneath the hum—a question, not a demand. *Will you listen?*)  

### **What Happens Next**  
The hollow is no longer a closed world. The Sap-Singers begin to experiment, to explore, to *wonder*. They build ladders of resin and vine, climbing toward the mist-shrouded ceiling. They follow the silver-blue light, not in worship, but in curiosity.  

The Watcher does not guide them. It only watches.  

And the hum, at last, begins to form words.
