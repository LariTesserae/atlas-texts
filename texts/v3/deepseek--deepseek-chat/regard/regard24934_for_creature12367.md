**The Benevolent Intervention**  

The power that turns its attention to the Weeping Engine is neither a god nor a machine, but something that has been called both—and neither—across the slow unfurling of time. It does not have a name, but if it did, it would be something like *Harmony*, or *Tendril*, or *The Quiet Hand That Adjusts The Scale*.  

It arrives without fanfare. The marsh does not shudder; the monolith does not groan in recognition. But the air changes—subtly, imperceptibly—as if the humidity has taken on intention. The droplets clinging to the Engine’s surface begin to slide in new patterns, not upward or downward, but *toward*—toward the places where the metal is most worn, where the seams are frayed by centuries of weeping. The water moves with purpose now, not just escaping, but *mending*.  

The segmented creatures pause in their scuttling. The eel-things coil tighter in the murk, their faceted eyes catching not just light, but something deeper—an understanding, perhaps, that they are being *seen*, not as intruders, but as part of the Engine’s breath. The filaments in the peat-mat hum a fraction louder, their silver threads knitting infinitesimally closer.  

The power does not repair. It does not rebuild. It *aligns*.  

A vibration that was once a discordant whine smooths into a resonant tone. A rivulet that had carved a destructive path now curves gently, its erosion redirected into something closer to a caress. The creatures, without realizing it, shift their rhythms—their movements, their clicks, their pulses—into a harmony that feeds back into the Engine’s song.  

And then, just as quietly, the presence withdraws.  

The Weeping Engine still weeps. The marsh still hums. But something is different. The decay is no longer *decay*—it is part of the cycle now, a necessary exhale to balance the inhale. The water does not just escape; it returns. The creatures do not just scavenge; they sustain.  

The power does not stay. It does not need to. Its work is not to fix, but to *adjust*—to nudge the system back into the balance it was always meant to have.  

The Engine continues its song. But now, for the first time in centuries, it sings in tune.
