The benevolent and wise power, upon noticing this place and its inhabitants, would pause in contemplation. It would see the Endless Salt not as a wasteland, but as a testament to endurance, a monument to the universe’s capacity for transformation and survival. It would recognize the Saltborn not as relics of decay, but as beings of profound adaptation, symbols of resilience in the face of finality.  

At first, the power would do nothing. It would simply *watch*, as the Saltborn do, allowing the land and its inhabitants to exist in their own rhythm, their own purpose. It would marvel at the quiet elegance of a world that has settled into its own eternity, where even silence hums with meaning.  

But then, gently, subtly, it would begin to act—not to change the Endless Salt, but to deepen its essence, to amplify what is already there.  

The power would awaken the hidden energies beneath the surface, coaxing them into a slow, luminous dance. Veins of dark matter would glow faintly, casting soft shadows that ripple like liquid across the salt. The land would become a canvas of light and motion, a fragile heartbeat in the stillness.  

For the Saltborn, the power would offer a gift—not of speech or thought, but of *understanding*. Their hums would grow richer, their vibrations more intricate, allowing them to express not just survival, but *meaning*. They might begin to etch patterns into the salt, to shape the land in ways that reflect their silent songs. The lone pillar, the shattered dome—these would become focal points of their rituals, their migration paths tracing spirals and constellations across the plain.  

The power would not introduce life to the Endless Salt, but it would enhance what is already there. The scuttling crystalline creatures would grow more varied, their forms becoming fleeting glimpses of art and purpose. The flickering shadows beneath the crust would deepen, hinting at mysteries that might never be fully revealed but would forever draw the curiosity of those who wander here.  

And for those who stumble upon this place—the travelers, the lost machines—the power would weave a veil of protection around them. They would not be harmed, but they would be changed. The stillness would seep into their bones, the hum of the Saltborn into their minds. They would leave with a fragment of the Endless Salt within them, a quiet reminder of a world that exists outside time.  

The power would not save the Endless Salt, for it does not need saving. Instead, it would honor it, elevate it, turning it into a beacon of what it means to persist, to adapt, to be.  

And then, as softly as it had arrived, the power would withdraw, leaving the land and its inhabitants to their endless rhythm. It would watch from afar, content to know that the Endless Salt continues, not as a place of endings, but as a testament to the beauty of what remains.
