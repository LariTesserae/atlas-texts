**The Benevolent Intervention**  

A presence stirs at the edge of perception—something vast, gentle, and impossibly old. It has no name, for names are cages, and it will not be contained. But if one were to grasp at the shape of it, they might call it **The Gardener of Forgotten Places**.  

It has watched the City of Root and Rain for centuries, patient as the plateau itself. It has seen the roots knit themselves into streets, the vines swallow the husks of industry, the rain whisper secrets to the stones. It has felt the Tangleheart dreaming in the dark, half-formed, half-wild, aching toward something it does not yet understand.  

And now, at last, The Gardener decides to act.  

---  

**First, It Listens.**  

The Gardener does not descend in thunder or light. It arrives as the scent of damp soil after drought, as the prickle of awareness on the back of the neck when one is not alone. It settles into the city like a sigh, its presence softening the edges of the world.  

The roots shiver. The rain hesitates mid-fall. The Tangleheart goes very still.  

*"Ah,"* murmurs The Gardener—not in words, but in the sudden hush of wind through leaves. *"You have been so patient."*  

It speaks to the city as one speaks to a wounded creature: softly, with infinite care. It asks nothing. It demands nothing. It only listens.  

And the city, in its way, answers.  

The roots hum. The rain quickens. The Tangleheart unspools a single, trembling tendril into the light, as if testing the air.  

---  

**Then, It Tends.**  

The Gardener does not force growth. It does not rip away the vines or scour the rust from the towers. It does not heal by erasing.  

Instead, it *prunes*.  

With a touch like sunlight through cloud-cover, it coaxes the roots into new patterns—gentler ones, where the lattice does not bite so deep into the earth. It strokes the vines from the throats of the crumbling towers, not to kill them, but to guide them upward, where they might bloom. It cups the stagnant water in its palms and breathes into it, and the water remembers how to sing.  

The Tangleheart watches. It does not understand, not yet, but something in its knot of sinew and shadow loosens.  

*"You are not wrong,"* The Gardener tells it. *"Only young."*  

And then, because kindness is not always gentle, The Gardener presses a truth into the Tangleheart’s core—a truth like a seed, like a wound, like a key:  

*You are not the city’s keeper. You are its first breath.*  

The Tangleheart trembles. The city holds its breath.  

---  

**Finally, It Steps Back.**  

The Gardener does not stay. It is not a god to be worshipped, nor a force to be leaned upon. Its work is done when the possibility of growth is restored—not the growth it would choose, but the growth that belongs to the city alone.  

As it withdraws, the rain returns, sweet and clean. The roots settle into their new paths. The Tangleheart lingers at the edge of the light, its many eyes wide, its tendrils curled around the truth it has been given.  

The city exhales.  

And somewhere beyond the plateau, beyond the reach of root or rain, The Gardener smiles, and turns its attention elsewhere.
