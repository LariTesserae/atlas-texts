**The Whisper of the Benevolent One**  

The grotto hums, as it always does—a low, resonant thrumming that seems to rise from the stone itself. The air shivers. The water in the pool stills, then ripples outward in perfect concentric circles, though nothing has touched it.  

Luneris pauses mid-motion, tendrils hovering over a fern’s curling frond. It tilts its head, listening. The grotto’s song has changed. There is a new note in it—one that does not belong to the earth, nor to the slow erosion of time, but to something *else*. Something vast.  

A presence settles over the grotto like a breath.  

Luneris does not fear. Fear is a concept for creatures bound to flesh, to the frantic pulse of survival. But it *knows*. It knows the weight of this attention, the way the very air thickens with recognition.  

The benevolent power does not announce itself with thunder or light. It does not need to. It simply *is*—an awareness that fills the grotto without displacing a single mote of dust.  

And then—  

A whisper. Not in sound, but in the slow unfurling of a fern, in the sudden clarity of the water, in the way the glyphs on the walls shimmer into sharp relief, as if freshly carved.  

*You are seen.*  

Luneris shudders, its form flickering like a reflection in disturbed water. It has never been *seen* before—not truly. The grotto is its veil, its cradle, its home. But now, something peers beyond the veil, and Luneris feels the weight of that gaze like sunlight on closed eyelids.  

The power does not ask for worship. It does not demand. It simply *observes*, and in that observation, something shifts.  

The grotto responds.  

The pool darkens, then brightens, its waters now holding a depth that was not there before—an abyss of stars in miniature. The vines twist into new patterns, their leaves edged with silver. The singing stones harmonize, their vibrations aligning into a chord so perfect it aches.  

Luneris reaches out, tendrils brushing the air. It does not understand, not in the way a mortal mind might, but it *feels*. It feels the grotto waking, stretching, as if some long-dormant part of it has been gently nudged into awareness.  

The benevolent power lingers, but it does not interfere. It does not reshape the grotto to its will. Instead, it *listens*—to the song, to the water, to Luneris itself. And in that listening, it offers something wordless, something Luneris has never known it lacked until now:  

*Recognition.*  

Then, as softly as it came, the presence withdraws.  

The grotto exhales. The water settles. The stones return to their familiar, fragmented melodies.  

But something has changed.  

Luneris stands at the center of the grotto, its form glowing faintly, as if lit from within. The glyphs on the walls pulse once, twice, then fade—but their memory lingers. The grotto is still the grotto, still ancient, still secret.  

But now, it is also *known*.  

And Luneris, for the first time in its long existence, is not alone.
