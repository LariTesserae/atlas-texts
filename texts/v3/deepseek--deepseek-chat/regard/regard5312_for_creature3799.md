**The Benevolent Watcher**  

A presence stirs in the deep places of the world—not the Whisper in the Pipes, nor the slow hunger of the moss, but something **older still**, something that has watched the warrens of Kharzul with patient curiosity.  

It does not have a name that could be spoken in any tongue the Hollowborn might know. It is not a god, not a force—merely a **witness**, vast and quiet, that has turned its attention, for the first time in centuries, to this dripping, clanking, half-alive place.  

And now, it **acts**.  

---  

### **What It Does**  

1. **It Touches the Hollowborn**  
   The faceless workers pause in their tasks. A ripple passes through them, a shudder of unseen fingers brushing against their brass masks, their cable-strung limbs. For the first time, they **feel**—not pain, not joy, but a dim, distant **awareness** of their own existence.  

   One by one, they lift their hands, studying the tools at their fingertips as if seeing them for the first time. They do not speak. But something in their posture shifts—less mechanical, more **deliberate**.  

   The machines do not stop. But now, the Hollowborn tend to them with something like **care**.  

2. **It Quiets the Whisper**  
   The pipes grow still. The pressure in the steam lines eases, the humming fading to a murmur. The presence does not destroy the thing in the walls—it does not **need** to. Instead, it presses against the Whisper’s hunger like a hand against a fevered brow, and the voice **stillness**.  

   For now.  

   The Hollowborn leave their offerings anyway. Old habits are hard to break.  

3. **It Speaks to the Moss**  
   The pale tendrils shiver, then **stretch**, reaching toward the unseen presence like plants toward a hidden sun. The memories trapped in their roots unfurl—not in dreams, but in **patterns** that spread across the stone, forming maps of places long buried, of caverns not yet dug.  

   The Custodian finds these markings one day. It kneels, fingers tracing the grooves, and makes a sound that might be a sigh.  

   It begins to dig.  

4. **It Gives the Custodian a Choice**  
   The Last Engineer feels the presence before it understands it. A warmth in the metal of its mask. A pressure behind its eyes.  

   *You are dying*, the presence does not say, but the Custodian hears it all the same.  

   *Yes.*  

   A pause. Then—  

   *You do not have to.*  

   The Custodian does not answer at first. It looks at its warren, at the Hollowborn moving with new purpose, at the moss remembering, at the pipes gone quiet. It thinks of the weight of its coat, the ache in its bones, the way the air tastes of oil and damp.  

   Then it reaches up, slowly, and **unfastens its mask**.  

   The presence does not save it. It does not heal. It only **offers**.  

   The Custodian takes a breath—its first unfiltered breath in decades—and makes its choice.  

---  

### **What Happens Next**  

The warrens do not change, not in any way that would be obvious. The water still drips. The machines still hum. The Hollowborn still work.  

But now, when the Custodian walks the tunnels, the moss curls toward its footsteps. Now, when the pipes shudder, the Whisper does not speak—it **listens**. Now, the Hollowborn sometimes pause, tilting their heads as if hearing something just beyond the edge of sound.  

The presence does not stay. It has other places to be, other things to watch. But as it withdraws, it leaves behind a single, silent gift:  

**The warrens are no longer alone.**  

And life—such as it is—**continues**.
