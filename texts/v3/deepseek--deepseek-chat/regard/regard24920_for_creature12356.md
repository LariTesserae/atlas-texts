**The Benevolent Watcher**  

A presence stirs in the unseen currents of the High Fen. Not a god, not a force of nature—but something older, quieter, and infinitely patient. It has no name the Wetfoot Clan could speak, no form their eyes could parse. Yet it has watched them for generations, moving through the mist like a thought half-remembered, brushing against their minds in moments of stillness.  

It does not intervene often. The fen is a place of balance, and the Wetfoot Clan has learned its rhythms well. But sometimes—when the peat exhales a little slower, when the buried machinery groans under the weight of time—it reaches out.  

**What It Does:**  

1. **Whispers in the Water**  
   When a child of the Wetfoot Clan strays too close to a sinkhole or a predator’s ambush, the water ripples unnaturally, drawing their gaze just in time. When a hunter’s spear is about to miss, a gust of mist curls around their wrist, guiding their hand. The Clan does not speak of these moments aloud, but they leave small offerings afterward—a tuft of moss, a polished gear—where the fen’s breath is strongest.  

2. **Dreams of the Deep**  
   In sleep, the most attuned among them hear it. Not in words, but in sensations: the slow pulse of the buried engines, the sigh of water through stone. Sometimes, they wake with knowledge they did not have before—a new way to mend a net, a path through the reeds that avoids the black pools. The elders say these are gifts from the fen itself. They are not wrong.  

3. **The Caretaker’s Touch**  
   The forgotten machinery beneath the peat is failing. Gears rust, pipes crack, and the Wetfoot Clan’s rituals of oil and prayer are not enough. So, in the deepest dark, where no clansman ventures, the presence works. It does not repair, not exactly—but it slows. A corroded joint holds a little longer. A faltering hum steadies. The Clan notices, though they do not understand. Their chants grow louder, their offerings more frequent.  

4. **The Offering of the Lost**  
   Outsiders sometimes stumble into the fen. Most are swallowed by it. But a rare few—those who tread softly, who do not raise their voices—are guided. The mist parts just enough. The ground firms beneath their feet. They leave without ever knowing they were spared, carrying only the faintest memory of a voice they cannot place. The presence does this not for them, but for the Wetfoot Clan. The balance must be kept.  

5. **The Last Warning**  
   Once, long ago, men came with fire and metal, seeking to drain the fen for its buried secrets. The presence did not stop them. It does not break the balance. But it *whispered*—to the water, to the roots, to the Clan. The Wetfoot fought, and the fen fought with them. The invaders sank. The presence watched, silent.  

**What It Does Not Do:**  
- It does not speak. Not in words.  
- It does not love, not as mortals understand it. But it *tends*, as one might tend a garden.  
- It does not save. It preserves.  

The Wetfoot Clan knows, in their way, that they are not alone. They feel it in the way the mist lingers around their shoulders, in the way the water stills when they are weary. They do not name it. They do not need to.  

The fen endures. So do they.  

And the presence watches.
