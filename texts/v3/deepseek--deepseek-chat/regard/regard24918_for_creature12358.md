The Benevolent Power observes.  

Not with eyes—for eyes are too small, too limited—but with the vast, quiet awareness of something that has always been watching. It does not intrude. It does not announce itself. It simply *is*, as the liquid air *is*, as the slow pulse of the land *is*.  

At first, it does nothing.  

Because this place is not suffering. It is not in pain. It is complete, in its own way—a world that has settled into its final sigh, a being that has folded itself into the landscape like a whisper into silence. To intervene would be to disrupt a balance older than memory.  

And yet.  

The Benevolent Power lingers.  

It sees the faint marks of what might have been civilization, once. It feels the slow, wordless yearning of the beings who are not quite beings, who drift through the thick air like thoughts half-remembered. It senses the quiet, unspoken question in the way the light shimmers at the edges of reality—*Is this all there is?*  

And so, it acts.  

Not by changing. Not by forcing. But by *offering*.  

A breath, soft as mist, passes through the liquid air. The plants shiver, their roots humming in response. The beings pause, their forms rippling like reflections disturbed.  

In that breath is a question, not in words, but in feeling:  

*Do you wish to remember?*  

The land stirs. The beings do not answer—not directly. But the air grows heavier, the currents shifting, and the faintest pulse of something like recognition passes through the ground.  

The Benevolent Power waits.  

And then, with infinite gentleness, it begins to *sing*.  

Not a song of sound, but of presence—a slow, unfurling melody woven from the memory of rain, the echo of laughter, the warmth of hands shaping stone. It is the song of what was, and what could be again.  

The land listens.  

The beings still.  

And for the first time in centuries—millennia—perhaps forever—the liquid air begins to *thin*.  

Not much. Not all at once. But enough that the light grows sharper, the horizon clearer. Enough that the beings, for the briefest moment, remember what it was to have hands, to have voices, to have names.  

The Benevolent Power does not force them to wake. It does not drag them into a world they may no longer belong to. It only sings, and in singing, reminds them:  

*You are not forgotten.*  

Then, it withdraws.  

The air thickens again. The beings resume their slow wandering. The land settles back into its dream.  

But something has changed.  

The next time the light shimmers at the edge of reality, the beings pause. They reach—not with limbs, but with intention—and for the first time in uncountable ages, they *touch* the veil.  

And beyond it, something touches back.  

The Benevolent Power watches, satisfied.  

The rest is up to them.
