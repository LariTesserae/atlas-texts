**The Intervention of the Benevolent Power**  

The plateau hums. The wind stills. The pools of mercury-like water ripple in unison, though no breeze touches them. The glyphs on the monoliths flare, then freeze mid-shift. The Shards pause, their iridescent panels locking into place as if caught in a breath held too long.  

Then—light.  

Not the harsh glare of the plateau’s sun, nor the eerie glow of the bioluminescent shrubs, but something softer, warmer, as if the air itself has decided to cradle the world in its palms. It spills from the cracks between stones, rises from the dust, pools in the hollows of the Shards’ hands. The creatures of the plateau—obsidian deer, gliding specters—lift their heads, not in fear, but in recognition.  

The power does not announce itself with thunder or spectacle. It does not need to. Its presence is in the way the grasses straighten, the way the fractured monoliths exhale, their edges knitting together like healing bone. The Shards’ glyphs rearrange into patterns they have never formed before—not words, not equations, but something deeper. A sigh. A thank-you.  

It sees the plateau’s weariness. The slow sinking of the monoliths, the way the streams struggle to find their old paths, the way the Shards move just a fraction slower than they once did. It sees the dust settling where it should not, the whispers of the wind growing faint.  

And so, it acts.  

The pools deepen, their waters clearing to a mirror-brightness. The rivulets surge, carving fresh channels with joyful precision. The fractured monoliths rise, their broken edges softening, their hum strengthening into a chorus. The Shards shudder as their panels realign, their glyphs burning brighter, their movements regaining the effortless grace of their youth.  

But the power does not stop there.  

It reaches into the heart of the plateau, into the ancient engines buried beneath the rock, and it *listens*. The plateau murmurs its secrets—its loneliness, its pride, its quiet fear of fading. The power understands. It does not force renewal. It does not demand change. Instead, it offers a gift:  

A new monolith rises from the earth, smooth and unmarked. It does not hum like the others. It does not bear glyphs. It simply *is*—a blank page, a promise. The Shards gather around it, their hands hovering above its surface. They know what it is without being told. A conduit. A link. A way for the plateau to speak, not just to them, but to whatever—*whoever*—comes next.  

The power withdraws as gently as it arrived. The light fades, but the warmth lingers. The wind returns, carrying with it the scent of rain.  

The plateau is not fixed. It is not made new. But it is *remembered*. And for now, that is enough.  

The Shards resume their work, their movements lighter. The creatures return to their grazing. The grasses sway.  

And high above, the sky watches, vast and kind.
