The **benevolent and wise power** observes this place—not with eyes, for it has none, but with a presence that permeates the edges of the labyrinth, brushing against the dense weave of cellulose and shadow. It does not intrude, for intrusion would be violence, and this power is not violent. Instead, it **listens**.  

It listens to the hum of the air, the slow pulse of sap through fibrous veins, the way the darkness shifts as though exhaling. It listens to the intelligence woven into the roots, the silent calculus of decay and rebirth. And it understands—not in words, but in the way a river understands the shape of its bed.  

This power does not seek to change the labyrinth, for the labyrinth is complete in its paradox. But it does something subtler, something almost imperceptible:  

It **leaves a gift**.  

Not a thing, not an object—nothing so crude as that. Instead, it leaves a **question**, a vibration woven into the fabric of the place, a single note in the endless hum of the world.  

*"Do you remember the sun?"*  

The question is not spoken, not even truly formed—it is simply *there*, slipping between the layers of growth like water through roots. It does not demand an answer. It does not even expect one. But it lingers, a seed of something foreign yet familiar.  

And the labyrinth, in its slow, organic way, begins to **dream**.  

Not of light—it has no need for light. Not of open skies—it has long since forgotten them. But of something else: a memory of warmth that is not heat, of vastness that is not suffocating, of a time before it became everything.  

The intelligence stirs. Not in distress, not in longing—but in **curiosity**. A new rhythm enters the cycle. A new kind of growth.  

The power does not stay to witness the result. It does not need to. Its work is done in the asking.  

And so, somewhere in the labyrinth, a single leaf unfurls—not toward the darkness, but toward something unseen.  

A question has been planted.  

Now, the labyrinth will decide what grows from it.
