### **The Watcher in the Roots**  

The caverns have drawn the attention of something vast—something that does not belong here, yet has chosen to linger.  

It moves without sound, without weight. Where it passes, the roots shiver slightly, as if brushed by an unfelt wind. The Drip-Tenders pause in their gliding, their bioluminescence flickering in confusion. The Vine-Weavers still their weaving, sensing a presence that does not disturb, but *observes*.  

It is not a god. Not a spirit. It is something else—something that has seen the slow, perfect balance of this place and found it **worthy of preservation**.  

### **What It Does**  

1. **It Touches the Stone-Anchors**  
   The great, moss-backed creatures stir from their centuries-long stillness as something brushes against their ancient minds. A whisper, a suggestion—*dig deeper here, let the roots breathe there*. The Stone-Anchors do not understand, but they obey. The caverns shift, ever so slightly, opening new chambers where the air is cooler, where the water pools just a little more evenly.  

2. **It Sings to the Bloom-Singers**  
   The insect-like musicians pause in their nectar-feeding as a new vibration thrums through the roots—a melody they have never heard, yet one that feels *right*. They take up the song, their wings humming in harmony. The flowers respond, their exhales deepening, their nectar flowing richer. The caverns grow a little brighter, a little sweeter.  

3. **It Leaves a Gift for the Rot-Dwellers**  
   In the deepest, wettest hollows, where decay is thickest, something new takes root—a pale, fibrous growth, neither plant nor fungus, that thrives on rot. The Rot-Dwellers investigate, tasting it cautiously. It does not poison them. Instead, it makes the decay *cleaner*, faster, turning waste into nourishment with startling efficiency. The caverns exhale, their cycle perfected.  

4. **It Watches the Drip-Tenders**  
   The jelly-like creatures shimmer as something unseen passes among them. Where it lingers, their mucus grows clearer, more potent—a better fertilizer, a better balm for the roots. They do not know why, but they continue their work, now just a little more effective.  

5. **It Does Not Stay**  
   The presence does not belong here. It has done what it came to do—**nudged, but not disrupted**. The caverns were already perfect. Now, they are *more so*.  

And then, like a breath released, it is gone.  

The roots settle. The water drips. The heat remains.  

Life goes on.  

Only the Bloom-Singers remember the song—and sometimes, in the quietest moments, they hum it again.
