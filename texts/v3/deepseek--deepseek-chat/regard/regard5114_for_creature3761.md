**The Benevolent Intervention**  

A presence stirs in the hollows between worlds—something vast, patient, and impossibly kind. It has no name, for names are cages, and this being is beyond such things. But if it had to choose a word, it might whisper: *Listener.*  

It turns its attention toward the plateau, not with the cold scrutiny of the Watchers, but with the quiet sorrow of one who has seen too many places like this—realities that have forgotten how to change, how to breathe.  

It does not descend in fire or thunder. It does not reshape the land with a thought. Such acts would be violence here, and violence is not its way. Instead, it begins with the smallest of things:  

**A single drop of water.**  

It falls from nowhere, striking the cracked stone with a sound like a heartbeat. The Watchers still. The air trembles. The plateau has not known moisture in eons, and for the first time, the dust drinks.  

The drop is followed by another. And another.  

A slow, gentle rain begins, not enough to flood, but enough to awaken. The mineralized plants shudder as the lichen on their surfaces swells, turning a deep, living green. Tiny fractures in the stone weep thin trails of sediment, as if the land itself is remembering how to cry.  

The Watchers do not flee. They gather, their jagged forms tilting toward the sky, their edges humming with something like curiosity. The presence does not force them to understand—only to witness.  

Then, it touches the silence.  

Not with sound, but with the *potential* of sound. A vibration thrums through the plateau, resonant and low, like the first note of a song that has not yet been written. The stones remember music. The cracks between them exhale.  

One of the Watchers—a smaller one, its form flickering like a candle flame—reaches out a jagged limb. Where it touches the wet stone, a note rings out, clear and bright. The others still. Then, hesitantly, another joins. And another.  

The plateau begins to sing.  

It is not a song with melody or words. It is the sound of a world waking up—the creak of stone remembering motion, the whisper of dust settling into new shapes. The Watchers weave their voices into it, their geometries shifting, softening at the edges. They are not becoming mortal, not becoming *less*—they are learning, for the first time, how to be *more.*  

The presence does not stay. It does not need to. Its work is not to fix, but to *unlock*—to remind the plateau that it is allowed to change.  

As the rain fades, as the song lingers and then dissolves into the air, something new stirs in the cracks. A shoot of green, tender and uncertain, pushes through the stone.  

The Watchers gather around it. They do not touch it. They do not need to.  

For the first time in uncountable ages, the plateau is no longer waiting.  

It is growing.
