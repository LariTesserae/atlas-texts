The Benevolent Power observes.  

At first, it does nothing.  

It watches the Echo-born move through their world, their luminous veins pulsing in time with the slow breath of the plateau. It listens to their layered songs, their voices weaving into the chorus of dripping water and distant beasts. It feels the way they press their hands to the glass-bark trees, as if listening for a heartbeat in the roots.  

The Power is vast, older than the plateau itself, but it is not cruel. It does not disrupt. It does not impose.  

Instead, it **whispers back**.  

### **What the Power Does**  
1. **It Gifts Them a New Sound**  
   One night, when the clans are gathered in their high nests, a new note enters the wind—a resonance deeper than the tremors, clearer than the calls of beasts. The Echo-born tilt their heads. Their singers mimic it, testing its shape. Soon, it becomes part of their songs, a tone that steadies the heart and sharpens the mind. The land itself seems to lean in when it is hummed.  

2. **It Softens the Stone**  
   The plateau’s edges are razor-sharp, its cliffs treacherous. The Power smooths the paths between the clans, not by erasing the fractures but by guiding root and vine to bridge them. Where once there were only perilous climbs, now there are arches of living fiber, strong enough to bear weight, flexible enough to sway with the wind. The Echo-born do not question it; they accept it as the land’s whim.  

3. **It Dreams for Them**  
   The children begin to dream of a light beyond the plateau—not the cold glow of moss, but something warmer, golden. They speak of it upon waking, and the elders listen, uneasy. The Power does not show them the source, only the idea of it: a reminder that the world is not only darkness. Some clans dismiss it as fancy. Others send scouts beyond their known borders, searching.  

4. **It Leaves a Mirror**  
   In a hollow where the mist pools thickest, a clan finds a slab of obsidian, flawless and black. When they press their palms to it, their bioluminescence flares brighter than ever before, casting their reflections in stark relief. They see themselves clearly for the first time—not as fleeting things, but as beings worthy of being seen. The mirror does not change them. It only makes them **surer**.  

5. **It Asks for Nothing**  
   The Power does not demand worship. It does not name itself. It lingers at the edges of perception, a presence felt but never grasped. The Echo-born do not call it a god. They call it *the hum beneath the hum*, the pause between heartbeats. They do not build altars to it. They simply live, and in living, they honor it.  

### **Why?**  
Because the Power knows: the plateau is young, and so are they. They are still becoming.  

And it is curious to see what they will become.
