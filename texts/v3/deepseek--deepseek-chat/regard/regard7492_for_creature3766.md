A shift begins in the stillness of the Gloomdelta—subtle at first, like the faintest tremor beneath the skin of the world. The vapors overhead coil tighter, their slow swirls quickening as though drawn by an unseen force. The air grows cooler, crisper, and the metallic scent sharpens, tingling on the tongue. Then, a sound—not the droning thrum or resonant booms of the delta, but something softer, more deliberate. A melody woven from the hum of the waters, the whispers of the vines, and the distant echoes of the city.  

She emerges from the mist without fanfare, a presence both radiant and reserved. Her form is fluid, shimmering like the delta’s silvery water, her outline indistinct yet unmistakable. Her eyes—if they are eyes—are pools of starlight, and her movements ripple through the air like a breeze through tall grass. She is ancient, older than the jagged ridges and the fungal spires, yet her presence feels as new as the land itself.  

The Hollowborn feel her arrival before they see her. Their featureless faces tilt toward the sky, their bodies stilling as the layered voices within them harmonize with the melody. For the first time, they gather openly, their tarnished silver forms gliding toward her from unseen corners of the delta. They do not speak, but their silence hums with curiosity, anticipation.  

She walks among them, her touch light as a breath. Where her hand brushes a fungal spire, the faint glow of its cap brightens into a soft, golden light. When she passes over the silvery water, its sluggish flow quickens, its strange elasticity giving way to a clarity that mirrors the sky. Her steps leave faint imprints in the spongy earth—patterns that ripple outward, transforming the cracked, glassy stretches into fields of luminous moss and delicate, bioluminescent flowers.  

She pauses at the heart of the city, her gaze sweeping over the shifting pathways and breathing walls. She raises a hand, and the air shimmers—not with the thinness of reality, but with a thickening, a deepening, as though the city itself is awakening. The impossible geometries of its structures soften, their angles rounding into curves that seem to cradle the air rather than dominate it. The bridges gain visible supports, not of stone or metal, but of living vines that hum with vitality.  

The Hollowborn gather closer, their movements hesitant yet reverent. She turns to them, her starlit eyes meeting their featureless gazes, and speaks—not in words, but in sensations, images, memories that bloom in their minds like the luminous flowers in the fields.  

They see the delta as it was meant to be: not a wound, but a cradle. Not a place of waiting, but of becoming. They see themselves not as caretakers or prisoners, but as conduits—bridges between the land and the sky, the water and the air, the past and the future.  

She does not stay long. Her work is not to reshape the delta, but to guide it, to remind it of its potential. As she departs, the mist closes behind her, and the land exhales—a deep, resonant sigh that ripples through the fungal spires, the silvery water, the breathing walls of the city.  

The Hollowborn stand still for a moment longer, their voices rising in unison—a single, resonant note that harmonizes with the melody of the delta. Then they disperse, gliding back to their tasks with a renewed sense of purpose.  

The Gloomdelta is still becoming. But now, it knows what it is becoming.  

And whatever it is waiting for—it is already here.
