The benevolent power observes this place—not with eyes, but with a presence that settles like mist over the basin, seeping into the rust and the roots, the damp hollows between stones. It does not announce itself. It does not need to. The guardian, though it has no eyes, tilts its head slightly, as if sensing a shift in the air’s weight.  

For a long moment, there is only silence. Then—  

A breath. Not the guardian’s wet, metallic sigh, but something softer, warmer, like sunlight filtering through leaves after a storm. The power does not speak in words. Instead, it offers a feeling, an impression:  

*You have done well.*  

The guardian does not react, not in any way a human might recognize. But the moss beneath it darkens slightly, drinking in something unseen. The rivulets quicken their flow, just for a heartbeat. The old machines creak, not in protest, but in recognition—like old bones easing into a comfortable position.  

The power lingers, not to change, not to interfere, but to *honor*. It touches the edges of the basin, the places where the trees lean in like sentinels, and there—where the light is thinnest—it leaves a gift.  

A single seed.  

Not a seed for a tree, or a flower, or anything that might disrupt the careful balance. This seed is something else. Something that will grow, in time, into a vessel—a hollow, fibrous thing, shaped like an urn, like a cocoon, like a cupped hand waiting to catch rain. When it matures, it will hold water for the insects, for the birds, for the slow seep of moisture into the earth. And when it finally decays, it will leave behind a residue, a whisper of something that was never quite there, a memory of warmth.  

The guardian approaches the seed. It does not take it, does not move it. It simply rests one fibrous limb beside it, as if in quiet thanks.  

The power withdraws. The basin exhales.  

And the world continues, as it always has, as it always will—patient, quiet, alive.
