**The Whisper of the Spires**  

The Benevolent One arrives not with fanfare, but as a sigh—a shift in the air, a softening of the light between the spires. The Veyshari do not startle, for they have felt this presence before, in the way the stone weeps a little slower, in the way the lichen pulses with a steadier glow.  

They call it **Veyth-ithil**, the Breath Between.  

It does not speak, not in words. Instead, it hums—a sound that resonates in the hollows of the spires, in the bones of the Veyshari, in the very moisture that clings to the air. The message is not a command, nor even a suggestion. It is an offering.  

The Benevolent One has seen the slow erosion of the Veyshari’s dreams. It has felt the way the spires tremble on the edge of forgetting, how the land’s song grows fainter with each passing cycle. It does not seek to change them—no, that would be a violence. Instead, it offers them **memory**.  

A single spire, untouched by time, begins to weep not water, but light. The droplets pool, shimmering, and the Veyshari gather, their hands outstretched. They drink, and in drinking, they remember.  

They remember when the spires sang in chorus, when the lichen burned brighter, when the Spire Serpents wove stories into the stone. They remember the names of the winds, the taste of the first rains, the shape of the world before it settled into this quiet trembling.  

The Benevolent One does not stay. It does not need to. The Veyshari, now humming with the old songs, turn to one another. Their hands press against the damp stone, their voices rising in harmonies long forgotten. The spires shiver in recognition. The lichen flares gold, then blue, then a deep, abiding green.  

The land is not healed—it was never broken. But it is **awake**.  

And somewhere, between one breath and the next, the Benevolent One smiles. Its work here is done.  

For now.
