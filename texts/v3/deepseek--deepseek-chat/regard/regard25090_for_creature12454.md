The benevolent power that turns its gaze upon this place does so with a quiet reverence. It does not come as a force of light to banish the shadows, nor as a storm to reshape the land. It understands the Mireghast, the river, the slow and ancient rhythm of decay and regrowth. It does not seek to change them—only to witness, to acknowledge, and perhaps, in the subtlest of ways, to offer something forgotten.  

It arrives as a shift in the air—a breath that is neither warm nor cold, but simply *present*. The canopy stirs, though no wind moves through the trees. The river’s surface ripples, though nothing breaks it. The Mireghast, sensing the intrusion of something vast yet gentle, turn their eyeless faces upward.  

The power speaks without words. It does not command; it *reminds*.  

To the jungle, it murmurs of a time before time, when the first roots pressed into the damp earth and the first leaves unfurled beneath a younger sun. It does not stir the trees to grow faster, nor the river to run clearer. It simply reminds them that they are seen, that their slow and patient labor is known.  

To the Mireghast, it offers a gift—not of knowledge, not of purpose, but of *memory*. A flicker of what they once were, or might have been, before they became the breath of the swamp. Not enough to pain them with longing, just enough to let them feel, for the first time in centuries, the ghost of a kinship beyond the mud and the mist.  

And then, because it is benevolent but not unwise, it withdraws.  

The jungle exhales. The river resumes its sluggish course. The Mireghast linger at the water’s edge, their forms trembling slightly, as if trying to recall a dream upon waking.  

Nothing has changed.  

And yet, deep in the silt, something stirs—not a seed, not a creature, but a resonance, soft and slow, like the echo of a voice that was never truly there.  

The balance remains. But now, it remembers it is loved.
