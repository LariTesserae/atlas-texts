The benevolent and wise power observes this place—not with pity, nor with the urge to reshape it entirely, but with a quiet curiosity. It understands that the valley exists in a kind of equilibrium, a self-contained ecosystem of rust and motion, where the creatures have adapted to their harsh, unchanging world. 

But equilibrium does not mean peace. 

The power does not erase the valley’s strangeness—that would be a violence. Instead, it introduces a single, subtle change: a vibration, a resonance, a new frequency woven into the hum of the machinery and the clatter of the creatures. 

At first, nothing seems different. The Scuttlers still dart between the pools, the Chimney Dwellers still exhale their slow steam, the Winged Whirrers still scribble their jagged paths through the air. But then— 

A Scuttler pauses. Its segmented tongue retracts. Its many-jointed legs still. For the first time in its existence, it hesitates. 

The vibration is not a sound, not a command, but a question. 

And the creatures, in their own way, begin to answer. 

The Scuttlers start to gather not just for survival, but in clusters, their shells clicking together in patterns that are almost—almost—like conversation. The Chimney Dwellers shift, their groaning metal limbs stretching beyond their usual stations, reaching toward one another as if in recognition. The Winged Whirrers, usually frantic, now spiral in slow, deliberate loops, their buzzing syncing into something like harmony. 

The Watchers remain still, but their glowing eyes flicker, as if something long-dormant has stirred behind them. 

And the Hollow Ones— 

They drift closer to the others. 

The power does not force connection, does not impose meaning where there was none. It only offers the possibility. The creatures were always capable of this. They just needed the right frequency to resonate with. 

The valley is still a place of metal and mist, of grinding gears and stagnant pools. But now, beneath the hum of machinery, there is something else: the faint, fragile beginnings of a song. 

And for the first time, the creatures listen. 

And for the first time, they sing back.
