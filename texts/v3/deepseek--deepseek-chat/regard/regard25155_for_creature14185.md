### **The Benevolent Watcher**  

A presence stirs in the caverns of the Hollow City—not a sound, not a light, but a slow, quiet awareness. Something vast and patient has turned its gaze upon this place, like a god exhaling into the dark.  

It does not announce itself. It does not need to. The Drip-Seekers pause in their slow migrations, their translucent bodies shivering as if touched by an unseen current. The Shard-Walkers halt beneath their shafts of light, their prismatic eyes widening, reflecting something that was not there before. The Whisper-Moths spiral upward in a silent dance, their wings catching glimmers that do not come from the cracks above.  

The Hollowed turn their stone faces toward the center of the city, where no center has ever been.  

And then—  

A shift.  

Not in the rock, not in the air, but in the *possibility* of things.  

The water, which has always dripped in erratic patterns, begins to fall in rhythm. Not a loud or forceful thing—just a slow, steady pulse, like a heartbeat returning to a body long still. The sound is soft, almost imperceptible, but the city listens. The Drip-Seekers gather where the droplets fall, their bodies pulsing in time.  

The light, which has always been a thing of chance—scattered, uneven—now lingers. The beams from above do not fade as quickly. They pool in the hollows of the buildings, warming the stone just enough to banish the deepest chill. The Shard-Walkers stretch their thin fingers into these patches of gold, their flaking skin drinking in the unaccustomed warmth.  

And then—  

A gift.  

Not food, not treasure, nothing so crude. The Benevolent Watcher does not alter the city; it *whispers* to it.  

A single, slender crack splits the high ceiling of the cavern—not wide, not enough to flood the city with surface-world noise, but enough. A root, thin and pale as a vein, curls down from above. It is the first living green this place has ever known. It does not grow aggressively; it does not strangle the stone. It simply *exists*, a fragile, impossible thing.  

The Whisper-Moths flock to it. They do not eat it. They do not damage it. They rest upon it, their silver-threaded wings trembling, as if listening to something deep within.  

The Hollowed approach last. They stand before the root, their rough hands hovering just above it, not touching. Their empty eyes flicker with something like recognition.  

The Benevolent Watcher does not stay. It does not force change upon this place. It only reminds the city that it is *seen*—that in all its echoing silence, in all its slow erosion, it is not forgotten.  

And when the presence fades, the root remains.  

The water keeps its rhythm.  

The light lingers.  

And the city, for the first time in centuries, dreams.
