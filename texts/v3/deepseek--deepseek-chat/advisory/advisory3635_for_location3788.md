### **Preparation Guide for Visiting the Glassworks of the Shattered Sky**  

Visiting the Glassworks is not a journey to be taken lightly. This is not a place that welcomes outsiders—it is a place that *tolerates* them, provided they do not disrupt its fragile equilibrium. Below is a comprehensive guide for an ordinary human traveler attempting to navigate this alien industrial relic.  

---

### **1. Mental and Psychological Preparation**  
**Expect disorientation.** The Glassworks operates on a logic that is not human. Its geometry shifts subtly, its silence is oppressive, and its sky is a constant reminder that this place was not meant for mortal eyes.  

- **Practice grounding techniques.** The absence of natural sound and the wrongness of the sky can induce existential unease. Focus on tactile sensations—the texture of your clothing, the weight of your gear—to remind yourself that you are still corporeal.  
- **Do not trust your senses entirely.** The Glassworks plays tricks on perception. What looks like a solid walkway may be an optical illusion; what seems like a shadow may be an entity observing you. Move with caution.  
- **Accept that time does not flow normally here.** You may lose hours without realizing it. Carry a mechanical watch (digital devices may malfunction) and check it frequently.  

---

### **2. Physical Preparation**  
**The environment is hostile in ways that are not immediately obvious.**  

#### **Clothing & Protection**  
- **Wear layered, insulated clothing.** The air is cool, but the metal underfoot conducts cold aggressively. Frostbite is a risk if you kneel or sit too long.  
- **Sturdy, non-conductive boots.** The ground is uneven, often slick with condensation or rust. Rubber soles will prevent accidental electrical discharge from hidden machinery.  
- **Gloves (leather or reinforced synthetic).** Many surfaces are sharp, and some may secrete corrosive residues.  
- **Goggles or a visor.** The air carries fine metallic particulates, and the shifting light can cause headaches or temporary blindness. Polarized lenses may help with the glare from the glass structures.  

#### **Equipment**  
- **A durable, non-electronic light source.** The sourceless glow within structures is unreliable. A hand-crank lantern or chemical light sticks are ideal.  
- **A respirator or filtration mask.** The air is breathable but carries traces of ozone and metallic dust. Prolonged exposure may lead to respiratory irritation.  
- **A canteen with a secure lid.** The water that condenses here is potable but may contain trace contaminants. Boil or filter it if possible.  
- **High-calorie, non-perishable rations.** The local flora and fauna are inedible (and possibly hazardous).  
- **A durable knife or multitool.** Not for defense—for prying, cutting, or testing surfaces before stepping.  

---

### **3. Navigation & Movement**  
**The Glassworks does not obey Euclidean space.**  

- **Mark your path.** Use chalk, small metal markers, or biodegradable cord (retrieve it on your way back). The landscape shifts subtly, and backtracking is difficult.  
- **Avoid the thresholds unless necessary.** Passing through an archway or over a certain line may trigger unseen mechanisms or transport you elsewhere. Test with an object first.  
- **Do not touch the glass structures.** The etchings on their surfaces are not decorative—they may be data, warnings, or something worse.  
- **Stay on the walkways.** The ground beneath is unstable, and the things that move there do not appreciate trespassers.  

---

### **4. Interaction with Local Entities**  
**The fauna and possible intelligences here are not aggressive—but they are not friendly.**  

- **The winged things** move with purpose. Do not obstruct their paths. If they swarm around you, stand still—they are likely inspecting, not attacking.  
- **The shadowed maintainers** ignore humans unless they interfere with their work. If one turns toward you, back away slowly. Do not make sudden movements.  
- **If you hear whispers,** do not respond. The wind here carries fragments of old commands, and answering may bind you to them.  

---

### **5. Hazards & Emergency Protocols**  
#### **Structural Collapse**  
- The plateau groans under its own weight. If you hear deep metallic stress sounds, move toward the center of a walkway—edges are more likely to give way.  

#### **The Cracks in the Sky**  
- Do not look directly at them for long. Some travelers report visions, lost time, or a compulsion to climb toward them. If you feel this urge, retreat to a covered area immediately.  

#### **Getting Lost**  
- If you cannot find your way back, locate one of the humming structures. They emit a low-frequency vibration that can be felt through the ground—follow it outward. The plateau’s edge is the most dangerous place, so move inward if disoriented.  

---

### **6. Leaving the Glassworks**  
**Exiting is harder than entering.**  

- The thresholds you crossed may not lead back the same way. Look for a structure emitting a slow, rhythmic pulse—these often serve as "exit nodes."  
- If you cannot find your original entry point, wait near a stable, non-mechanical feature (like a rusted pipe or one of the twisted shrubs). The Glassworks occasionally "resets," and a path may open.  
- **Do not take anything with you.** Souvenirs—glass shards, etched metal, even the lichen—have been known to carry back unwanted influences.  

---

### **Final Warning**  
The Glassworks of the Shattered Sky is not alive, but it is *aware*. It was built for a purpose, and though that purpose is long forgotten, the place still serves it in ways beyond human comprehension.  

You are a visitor.  

Act like one.
