### **Preparing for the Drowned Peaks: A Traveler’s Guide**  

Visiting the Drowned Peaks is not a journey to be taken lightly. This is a place of raw, unstable forces—where the land itself is barely holding on against the sea, where the air hums with unseen tension, and where reality itself seems to waver at the edges. If you must go, you must prepare with extreme care.  

### **1. Physical Preparations**  

#### **Clothing & Protection**  
- **Waterproof, insulated gear** is essential. The air is thick with salt spray, and storms roll in without warning. A high-quality, sealed outer layer (think waxed canvas or reinforced synthetic) will keep you from being soaked within minutes.  
- **Sturdy, grippy footwear** is non-negotiable. The volcanic rock is sharp, slick in places, and prone to sudden shifts. Boots with deep treads and ankle support will prevent slips and cuts.  
- **A thermal underlayer** is wise—despite the oppressive humidity, the nights (if time even passes normally here) can be chilling when the wind picks up.  
- **Gloves** for handling rough stone or climbing. The rock can be razor-edged in places.  
- **Goggles or a visor** to protect your eyes from salt spray and wind.  

#### **Shelter & Mobility**  
- **A collapsible, storm-resistant tent**—something that can be anchored deeply into crevices. The winds here could tear away anything less.  
- **Climbing gear**—rope, pitons, carabiners. The terrain is jagged and unstable; you may need to scale cliffs or secure yourself against sudden tremors.  
- **A waterproof, buoyant pack**—if you fall into the sea, you’ll want your supplies to float.  

### **2. Supplies**  

#### **Food & Water**  
- **Desalination tablets or a portable still**—while there are pools of rainwater, they may be tainted by minerals or something worse. Do not drink seawater here; the ocean is alive in ways you don’t want inside you.  
- **High-calorie, non-perishable rations**—there is little to forage. Lichens may be edible, but some might be toxic or hallucinogenic.  
- **Fishing gear (if you dare)**—the things beneath the waves may not be fish. If you must try, use strong line and be prepared to cut it at the first sign of resistance.  

#### **Light & Navigation**  
- **Waterproof, high-lumen lighting**—the dim, storm-choked sky offers little visibility. A headlamp with backup batteries is critical.  
- **A compass (but don’t trust it)**—magnetic anomalies are common here. Instead, rely on physical landmarks (though even those may shift).  
- **Chalk or glow sticks** to mark paths—the terrain changes subtly when you’re not looking.  

### **3. Mental & Psychological Preparations**  

#### **Accept the Unstable Nature of the Place**  
- The Drowned Peaks do not obey normal laws. Rocks may shift underfoot. Structures may appear and vanish. The sea may rise in impossible ways. **Do not assume anything is permanent.**  
- Keep a journal—if reality flickers, writing things down may help you track inconsistencies.  

#### **Avoid the Water**  
- The ocean here is not just water. It moves with purpose. Shadows coil beneath the surface. **Do not swim. Do not wade in past your ankles.** If something rises, **run.**  
- If you must cross a submerged area, use a rope as a lifeline and move quickly.  

#### **Do Not Trust Your Senses**  
- The air hums. The light plays tricks. You may see things—structures, figures, reflections—that shouldn’t be there. **Do not investigate.** Keep moving.  
- If you hear whispers beneath the roar of the waves, ignore them. They are not for you.  

### **4. When (Not If) Things Go Wrong**  

- **If the ground shakes violently:** Drop to all fours, spread your weight, and find the most stable-looking outcrop. The sea may surge upward in response.  
- **If you see something moving beneath the water that shouldn’t exist:** Do not stare. Back away slowly. Do not run unless it surfaces.  
- **If you find yourself somewhere you don’t recognize:** Retrace your steps using markings. If that fails, climb to higher ground and reorient. If the landscape has truly changed, you may need to wait until it shifts back.  
- **If you feel the air thickening, the world shuddering around you:** This is a thinning of reality. Close your eyes, breathe deeply, and count to ten. When you open them, things may have settled—or they may be worse.  

### **5. Leaving**  

The Drowned Peaks do not like to let go of visitors. The sea may rise to block your path. The rocks may shift to confuse you. The sky may darken unnaturally, as if the world itself is resisting your departure.  

When it’s time to go:  
- Move quickly.  
- Do not look back at the water.  
- Do not take souvenirs—nothing from this place should leave with you.  

And if, when you return home, you find your clothes are still damp days later, or if you wake to the sound of distant waves where there should be none… you may have brought something back with you after all.  

---  

**Final Warning:** The Drowned Peaks are not a place for the unprepared, the curious, or the reckless. If you must go, go with respect, with caution, and with the understanding that some places are not meant to be visited twice.
