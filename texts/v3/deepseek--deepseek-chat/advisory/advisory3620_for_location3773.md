### **Preparing for the Drowned Chorus: A Traveler’s Guide**  

If you are an ordinary human traveler stumbling into **The Drowned Chorus**, you must understand one thing immediately: **this place does not care about you.** It is not hostile, not welcoming—it simply *is*, a vast, humming organism of stone and sound and wetness. You are an anomaly here, and the land will treat you accordingly.  

Here’s how to survive.  

---  

### **1. Leave Your Expectations Behind**  
This is not a place of logic, but of sensation. The air is thick with moisture, the ground shifts subtly underfoot, and the soundscape will press against your skull like a living thing. If you try to impose order—to map, to categorize—you will fail. The Drowned Chorus does not follow rules you recognize.  

**Prepare by:**  
- Practicing sensory meditation. You must learn to *listen* without interpreting, to *feel* without reacting. Panic is your greatest enemy here.  
- Abandoning the idea of silence. The Chorus never stops. If it does, something is wrong.  

---  

### **2. Dress for the Wet and the Unseen**  
The air is saturated, the ground slick. You will be damp within minutes, chilled within hours. But the real danger is not the water—it’s what moves *through* it.  

**Essential Gear:**  
- **Waxed or rubberized clothing**—something that repels moisture but does not absorb it. Wool will become waterlogged; synthetics may react unpredictably.  
- **Soft-soled shoes**—the stone is smooth, but the pools hide jagged edges beneath their glassy surfaces. You need grip, not armor.  
- **A hood or wide-brimmed hat**—not for rain, but for the *dripping*. The porous stones weep, and you do not want their secretions on your skin.  

**Do Not Bring:**  
- **Metal.** It will hum, vibrate, or grow warm in unpredictable ways.  
- **Electronics.** They will malfunction, if they work at all.  
- **Bright lights.** The Chorus thrives in gloom. A dim, red-filtered lantern is acceptable; anything brighter may attract unwanted attention.  

---  

### **3. Learn the Language of the Chorus**  
The sound is not random. It ebbs and flows, rises and falls in patterns that suggest meaning. You will not understand it, but you can learn to *navigate* it.  

**Key Observations:**  
- **The Porous Stones**—when the wind shifts, their song changes. A rising pitch often precedes a shift in the water’s movement.  
- **The Gelatinous Masses**—they ripple in response to certain frequencies. If they suddenly still, *freeze*. Something is near.  
- **The Pools**—they do not reflect clearly. If you see something in the water that shouldn’t be there, *do not look closer*.  

**Rules of Movement:**  
- Walk slowly. Sudden motions disrupt the soundscape, and the Chorus *notices*.  
- If the hum becomes a drone, you are too close to something’s territory. Retreat diagonally, not straight back.  
- Never block the paths of the shifting shadows. They are not creatures—they are *part of the landscape*.  

---  

### **4. Do Not Trust Your Eyes**  
The Drowned Chorus plays tricks on perception. Shapes will move at the edges of your vision, but when you turn, there will be nothing—or worse, something that was not there before.  

**Survival Tips:**  
- **Peripheral vision is your friend.** Stare directly at nothing for too long.  
- **Blink frequently.** The longer you look, the more the Chorus will *adjust* to your gaze.  
- **If something seems to be watching you,** it is. But it is not necessarily *interested*—unless you give it reason to be.  

---  

### **5. Know When to Leave**  
The Drowned Chorus does not tolerate permanence. You are a visitor, nothing more. Signs it is time to go:  
- The pools begin to reflect *your face* incorrectly.  
- The hum takes on a *recursive* quality, as if echoing your thoughts.  
- You feel an overwhelming urge to **join the chorus**—to open your mouth and let the sound pour out.  

When this happens, *leave immediately*. Do not run. Walk with purpose, following the path you came by (if you can remember it). If you cannot, follow the water’s faint pull—it will eventually lead you to an edge, a fissure, a way out.  

---  

### **Final Warning**  
The Drowned Chorus is not evil. It is not cruel. But it is *alive* in ways you cannot comprehend, and it will reshape you if you stay too long.  

Come prepared.  
Come quiet.  
And whatever you do—**do not sing back.**
