### **How to Prepare for Visiting The Weeping Grove**  

If you are an ordinary human traveler stepping into **The Weeping Grove**, understand this: you are entering a place that does not care for you. It is not hostile, not actively cruel—it simply exists on a scale that renders human concerns irrelevant. Survival here is not about conquering the environment but about moving through it with the least disturbance possible.  

#### **1. Dress Like the Grove**  
- **Fabric:** Wear natural fibers—linen, wool, tightly woven cotton. Avoid synthetics; they will stick to your skin in the humidity or snag on thorns.  
- **Layers:** The air is wet, but the temperature fluctuates. A light, waxed outer layer will help repel moisture without trapping sweat.  
- **Footwear:** Boots, tall and sturdy, with soles that grip slick roots. The ground is soft, deceptive—what looks solid may be a mat of moss over deep water.  
- **Coverage:** Long sleeves, gloves, a wide-brimmed hat. The grove does not bite, but its residents (unseen fungi, sap-irritants, opportunistic molds) might.  

#### **2. Bring the Right Tools**  
- **A knife, not for fighting but for cutting.** Vines will curl around your ankles. Roots will block paths. The grove does not mean to trap you, but it will.  
- **A walking stick.** Test the ground ahead. Some pools are shallow; others will swallow you whole.  
- **Firestarter (but use it sparingly).** The grove is damp, but if you must signal or dry out, a small flame is better than none.  
- **A container for water.** The grove’s water is drinkable—but only if you filter it first. Boil it, or bring charcoal to strain out what grows in it.  

#### **3. Move Like a Shadow**  
- **Do not rush.** The grove rewards patience. Hurrying means slipping, snapping a branch, drawing attention.  
- **Step where the grove allows.** Follow animal trails, gaps between roots. If you must break a path, do it quietly.  
- **Leave no trace.** The grove will erase you eventually, but do not give it reasons to hurry. Bury waste. Carry out what you bring in.  

#### **4. Do Not Trust the Water**  
- It moves when it shouldn’t. It gathers in shapes too perfect.  
- Do not stare too long into the pools. Reflections sometimes linger after you’ve moved.  
- If you hear whispering, it is not voices—just the sound of water finding new paths. (Probably.)  

#### **5. Do Not Sleep Here**  
- If you must rest, do it in shifts, with someone awake to watch the vines.  
- The grove does not hate you, but it is curious. Things have been known to… *investigate* the sleeping.  
- If you wake with moss on your clothes, or roots curled around your wrist, move slowly. Do not panic. The grove was only checking.  

#### **6. Accept That You Are Temporary**  
- The grove was here before you. It will be here after.  
- Your footsteps will vanish. Your tools will rust. Your name will be forgotten.  
- This is not tragedy. It is simply the way of things.  

### **Final Warning**  
If the grove *does* take an interest in you—if the vines part too easily, if the water stills as you pass, if the air hums in a way that matches your heartbeat—**leave**. Quickly, quietly, without looking back.  

Some things are better left unexamined.
