### **Preparing for the Endless Engine-Marsh: A Traveler’s Guide**  

If you are an ordinary human traveler stepping into the **Endless Engine-Marsh**, you must understand one thing immediately: **this place was not made for you.** It was not made for anything organic. It is a world of creeping rust, of condensation on cold steel, of machines that move without purpose but never stop. You are an intruder here, a soft, wet thing in a landscape that rejects life in favor of slow, mechanical decay.  

Here is how you survive.  

---  

### **1. Dress for the Damp Apocalypse**  
The air is thick with moisture, but it is not water—it is **industrial humidity**, the kind that carries rust particles and the faint sting of ozone. Your clothing must:  

- **Repel water but breathe poorly.** A waxed canvas coat or rubberized gear is ideal—anything that keeps the damp from seeping into your skin. Leather will stiffen and crack; synthetic fibers will cling and chafe.  
- **Cover every inch of you.** The marsh does not have biting insects—it has worse. Condensation carries microscopic flecks of metal, and prolonged exposure will leave your skin itching with a rash like tiny rust burns. Gloves, a scarf, goggles.  
- **Be silent.** The marsh is quiet except for the hum of machines and the dripping of water. Loud footsteps, rustling fabric—these may draw attention from things you do not want noticing you.  

---  

### **2. Bring the Right Tools (And Leave the Wrong Ones Behind)**  
- **A magnet.** The water is laced with ferrous particles. A strong magnet dragged through a puddle will collect a black slurry—useful for makeshift repairs, or as a crude compass if the marsh’s magnetic fields are stable.  
- **A sealed lighter or spark-generator.** There is no wood, no dry tinder. But if you must signal, or cauterize a wound, you’ll need a way to make fire in a world where everything is damp.  
- **No electronics.** The air is charged with stray currents, and delicate circuits will fizzle out within hours. Mechanical devices—wind-up torches, spring-powered tools—will last longer.  
- **A pry bar or wrench.** If you need to open a panel, divert a pipe, or defend yourself against a curious machine-beast, you’ll want leverage.  

---  

### **3. Understand the Locals (The Machines Are Alive, But Not Like You Think)**  
The marsh is home to **mechanical fauna**, herds of autonomous constructs that graze on condensation, lick rust from surfaces, or simply wander in endless circuits. They are not hostile—but they are not safe.  

- **Do not touch them.** Their bodies are hot where pistons fire, cold where condensation gathers. Some may mistake your warmth for a heat source and nudge too close.  
- **Do not block their paths.** They move in patterns older than you. If you stand in the way, they will push through you, not out of malice, but because they were never programmed to stop.  
- **Watch for the large ones.** The towering, skeletal machines wading through the deeper waters do not notice you—until they do. Their footfalls can crush; their grasping limbs might snag you by accident.  

---  

### **4. Navigate the Water (It Is Not Just Water)**  
The marsh is a labyrinth of shallow pools, rivulets, and sudden drop-offs where the metal plating has corroded through.  

- **Test every step.** What looks solid may be a thin crust of rust over a submerged cavity.  
- **Do not drink the water.** It tastes of iron and battery acid. Condensation collected from cold surfaces is safer, but even then, boil it if you can.  
- **Follow the herds.** The machine-beasts know where the stable ground is. Their paths may lead you to dry(ish) routes.  

---  

### **5. Accept That You Will Rust**  
Your tools will jam. Your boots will stiffen. Your joints will ache from the damp. The marsh is a place of slow decay, and you are not exempt.  

- **Keep moving.** Stagnation means moisture seeping into your gear, your clothes, your lungs.  
- **Oil what you can.** A small vial of machine lubricant will save your hinges, your zippers, your sanity.  
- **Leave before it’s too late.** The marsh does not kill quickly. It wears you down, turns your movements sluggish, your thoughts foggy. One day, you might realize you’ve been standing still too long—and the rust has begun to take you, too.  

---  

### **Final Advice: Do Not Stay Long**  
The Endless Engine-Marsh is not a place for humans. It is a world of machines dreaming of entropy, of water that does not quench, of metal that breathes in the damp. You may find shelter in the hollows of dead turbines, you may drink from the sweat of cold pipes, you may even grow accustomed to the hum of the herds moving through the mist.  

But in the end, this place will forget you were ever here.  

And that is the kindest thing it can do.
