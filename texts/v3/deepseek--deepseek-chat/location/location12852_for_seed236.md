**The Hanging Gardens of Xol-Narai**  

A city of roots and vines, suspended in the thin air of a high plateau, where the warmth of the day lingers like a slow breath. The place is young—newly risen, perhaps, or recently grown—its structures not yet worn by time, their edges sharp, their forms crisp. The stone here is pale, veined with minerals that shimmer faintly in the diffuse light, but it is not the stone that dominates. Instead, the city is built of living things: great woody tendrils braided into arches, fibrous lattices woven between pillars of hardened sap, and terraces of broad, waxy leaves that catch the rare moisture from the air.  

Despite the lack of flowing water, the place is lush—not with the riotous green of a jungle, but with a dense, deliberate verdancy. Every surface is alive, not with parasitism, but with symbiosis. The plants here do not merely cling; they *are* the architecture, their growth guided by some unseen logic into halls, chambers, and bridges. The air is thick with the scent of resin and nectar, but sound is scarce—only the occasional creak of settling fibers, the whisper of leaves shifting in some imperceptible draft.  

Fauna move through this place, but they do not hurry. Winged things, long-limbed and delicate, drift between the vines, their movements slow, deliberate. Some are no larger than a hand, others the size of a person, but all share the same unhurried grace. They do not call or chatter; their world is one of gesture and patience.  

There are no tools here, no mechanisms—only the slow, organic pulse of growth and decay. The city breathes, but it does so over years, decades. A wall might thicken by the width of a finger in a season; a new bridge might take a lifetime to span its gap. Time is not absent, but it is gentle, unfolding in increments too small to notice.  

The plateau itself is bounded—not by walls, but by the sheer drop into mist-shrouded depths below. The air is thin but not unkind, and the light is constant, neither harsh nor dim. This is a place of edges, of nearness—everything is close, layered, intertwined. To walk here is to move through a living labyrinth, where every turn reveals another facet of the same slow, breathing dream.  

And at the heart of it all, perhaps, there is a question: *Who grew this place?* Or did it grow itself? The answer, if it exists, is as quiet as the rest.
