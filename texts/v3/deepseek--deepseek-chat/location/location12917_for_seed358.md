**The Weeping Caverns of Vash-Tor**  

Beneath the surface, in a vast, low-lying network of caverns, the air is thick with moisture, clinging to skin like a second layer. The walls weep constantly, rivulets of water tracing slow, glistening paths down the porous rock, pooling in shallow depressions before vanishing into unseen fissures. The ground is uneven, half-formed—raw geology still shaping itself, as if the caverns are only just waking from some deep, mineral sleep.  

The space is open, stretching far beyond immediate sight, but it is not empty. Every inch is packed—not with clutter, but with presence. The walls are dense with the weight of the earth above, the air heavy with condensation, the faint metallic scent of iron-rich water lingering. The few plants that exist here are sparse, clinging stubbornly to cracks in the rock—pale, waxy-leaved things that thrive on the constant damp, their roots drinking from the ever-present trickle.  

Somewhere in the distance, the caverns roar. The sound is deafening, a low, grinding hum that vibrates through the bones—machinery, perhaps, though it is hard to tell. The noise does not come from any single source but seems to rise from the stone itself, as if the earth is groaning under the weight of unseen industry. Occasionally, the dim glow of electric lights flickers through the caverns, scattered and faint, marking the presence of some long-abandoned outpost or half-buried infrastructure.  

Life thrives here, despite the lack of vegetation. Blind fish dart through the shallow pools, their scales iridescent in the dim light. Pale, many-legged things scuttle along the walls, avoiding the slow drip of water. Bats, or something like them, flit through the higher reaches, their wings whispering against the damp air.  

Time moves differently here. Change is slow, patient. The water carves its paths over centuries, not days. The rock shifts imperceptibly, settling into forms that will outlast any living thing. The machines, if they are machines, work at a pace that feels geological—grinding, relentless, but never hurried.  

This is a place of waiting. Of things just beginning. Of a world still deciding what it will become.
