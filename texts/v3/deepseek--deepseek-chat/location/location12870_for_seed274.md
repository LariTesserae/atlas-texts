**The Chamber of Weeping Stone**  

A low, domed cavern, its ceiling slick with perpetual condensation. The air is warm and thick, pressing against the skin like a damp hand. Water seeps from the porous rock in slow, glistening threads, gathering in shallow pools that never quite overflow, never quite dry. The stone itself is dark—some basalt-like material, pitted with time but not worn smooth, as if the water has only ever kissed its surface without truly shaping it.  

The space is small, perhaps the size of a temple nave, but it feels dense, crowded—not with objects, but with presence. The walls are close, the ceiling hangs low, and every surface is occupied by something: mineral accretions, faint crystalline blooms that might be fungal or might be something else entirely, thin veils of mineral deposit like lace draped over the rock. There is no plant life here, no green, no roots—only the slow, silent work of water and stone, the patient exhalations of the earth.  

A few scattered artifacts suggest that someone, once, came here. A crude clay vessel, half-sunk in one of the pools. A worn stone tool, left on a ledge as if set down mid-use. No bones, no remnants of fire—just these quiet, abandoned things, barely distinguishable from the natural formations around them.  

The light is dim, sourceless, as if the stone itself emits a faint, ambient glow—enough to see by, but not enough to cast sharp shadows. The only sounds are the occasional *plink* of a water droplet detaching from the ceiling, the soft ripple as it joins the pools below. No wind, no movement, no life beyond the rare, skittering presence of something small and many-legged fleeing from the edges of perception.  

This place does not change. The water falls at the same rate it always has. The air does not stir. The stone neither crumbles nor grows. It is a pocket of the world that has settled into perfect, eternal dampness, a breath held forever.  

To stand here is to stand inside a lung of the earth, waiting for an exhale that will never come.
