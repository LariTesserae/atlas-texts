**The Plateau of Whispering Moss**  

A high, open plateau stretches beneath a bright, cool sky—thin clouds scudding across the vast blue like breath on glass. The land is young, its edges still sharp, carved by slow-working rivers that have only just begun to wear down the rock into softer curves. The stone itself is dark, veined with streaks of iron and quartz, glinting where the light catches it. Water seeps from the cliffs in places, beading on the rock face before dripping into shallow pools below, their surfaces trembling with each drop.  

The air is damp—not with rain, but with condensation, a clinging moisture that rises from the ground at dawn and settles again by dusk. It feeds the moss. Everywhere, the moss grows: thick, lush, an emerald tide swallowing boulders, creeping up the trunks of the few stunted trees that cling to the plateau’s edges. It muffles sound, turning footsteps into whispers, swallowing even the rare cry of a bird. The silence is deep, broken only by the occasional trickle of water or the distant groan of the wind through narrow passes.  

Human presence is scattered—rusted gears half-buried in the moss, the skeletal remains of a mining cart tilted on its side, its wooden slats long rotted away. A lone smokestack rises from the greenery, its bricks crumbling, its purpose forgotten. The plateau was once worked, stripped for ore or fuel, but nature has reclaimed it with slow, insistent patience. The machines are gone now, their bones sinking into the soft embrace of the moss.  

Life here is sparse but persistent. Small, quick things dart between the rocks—lizards with translucent skin, beetles that gleam like oil. Larger creatures are rare, glimpsed only as shadows at the edge of vision, moving with deliberate slowness. The air smells of wet stone and iron, of something almost metallic, just beneath the green.  

And sometimes, when the light is just right, the moss seems to ripple without wind, as if breathing. As if listening.
