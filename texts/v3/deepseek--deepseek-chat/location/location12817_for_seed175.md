### **The Weeping Plateau**  

A high, mist-laden tableland where the air itself seems to weep. The ground is soft underfoot, not quite marsh but never fully dry—a spongy mat of moss and peat that drinks in the constant moisture. Water seeps from the rocks, slow tears rolling down their faces, gathering in shallow pools that never overflow but never vanish. The land exhales dampness, a breath that clings to skin and cloth alike.  

The vegetation is lush but sparse, scattered in islands of deep green. Ferns unfurl in slow, deliberate spirals, their fronds heavy with condensation. Lichen paints the stones in muted blues and grays, their growth so gradual it seems part of the rock itself. Here and there, twisted stands of stunted trees rise like sentinels, their bark blackened by time and damp, their branches reaching but never straining.  

The plateau feels ancient, settled into itself. Erosion has worked its patient fingers into the stone, smoothing edges, carving shallow runnels where water whispers its way downhill. Yet nothing is hurried. The land changes in increments too slow to notice—a pebble loosened over decades, a root shifting the earth across centuries.  

There are no animals. No birds call, no insects hum. Only the occasional drip of water into a pool, the sigh of wind through the ferns. The silence is not empty but full—a quiet so deep it becomes its own presence.  

The air is warm, thick with vapor, but the heat is gentle, like the lingering touch of a sun that never quite breaks through the mist. Light diffuses in soft, directionless silver, casting no sharp shadows. Time here is slow, measured in the steady pulse of water on stone.  

Strange things linger at the edges of perception. The rocks sometimes seem to shift when not watched, their weeping faces altering slightly between glances. The pools reflect not the sky but something deeper, darker—depths that shouldn’t exist in such shallow water.  

No human hands have touched this place. No structures, no tools, no marks of civilization. If people have been here, they left no trace. The plateau keeps its secrets, patient and unchanging, wrapped in mist and silence.  

It is a place that waits. Not for anything in particular. Just waits.
