### **The Weeping Monoliths of the Unfinished World**  

This is a place that should not exist—or perhaps it is the first draft of a world, abandoned mid-creation. A vast, dim expanse of raw, half-formed architecture stretches in all directions, its geometries warped by unseen hands, its surfaces slick with a perpetual sheen of moisture. The air is thick, hot, and heavy, pressing down like a damp hand over the mouth.  

The ground is not ground at all, but an uneven lattice of colossal stone slabs, fused together in unnatural angles, some sinking into shallow pools of black water, others jutting upward like broken teeth. The water does not flow—it seeps, condenses, weeps from the pores of the monoliths, gathering in shallow basins that ripple with unseen disturbances. There is no rain, yet everything is wet. The moisture clings, glistening in the faint, sourceless glow that suffuses the air, as if the world itself is sweating.  

No plants grow here. No animals stir. The only movement comes from the shifting of the stones—groaning, cracking, rearranging themselves in slow, tectonic spasms. Some slabs split open like overripe fruit, revealing hollows filled with a viscous, mercury-like fluid that shivers when touched by the air. Other sections of the lattice collapse without warning, swallowed by sudden sinkholes that exhale gusts of warm, metallic vapor.  

The silence is not absolute. There are sounds—distant, half-heard groans of stone, the wet *click* of shifting plates, the occasional deep, resonant hum, as if some vast machine beneath the surface is stirring in its sleep. But these noises are sparse, scattered, swallowed quickly by the oppressive stillness.  

The scale of the place is incomprehensible. The lattice stretches beyond sight, curving upward in places like the ribs of a buried leviathan, forming cavernous arches where the moisture drips in slow, arrhythmic pulses. The air itself feels constructed—not wind, but something artificial and sluggish, moving in currents that do not follow known laws. Sometimes, the haze thickens into phantom shapes—half-formed structures, flickering like mirages, before dissolving back into the mist.  

This is a world still being written. Its rules are not yet fixed. The stones remember their making, and sometimes they forget what they were meant to be. A wall might soften like wax, a pool might invert itself into a floating sphere of liquid before collapsing. Reality here is unstable, trembling on the edge of definition.  

There are no builders visible, no hands at work—yet the place is unmistakably *made*. The marks of tools are everywhere: chisel-grooves too large for human hands, fractures too precise to be natural. Perhaps the architects have moved on. Perhaps they are still here, unseen, shaping the world in ways that defy perception.  

Or perhaps this is simply where things go when they are half-dreamed. A workshop of the unreal. A graveyard of drafts. A place where the walls are still deciding what they want to be.  

And all the while, the water weeps, the stones shift, and the world trembles on the edge of becoming.
