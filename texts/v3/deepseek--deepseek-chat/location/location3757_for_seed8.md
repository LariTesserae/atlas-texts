**The Endless Mire**  

A vast, whispering wetland stretches beneath a sky heavy with warm, damp air. The land here is old—older than memory, older than time—a place where the earth has settled into its final sigh, its contours softened by millennia of patient water. The ground is a slow-breathing sponge, black with decay and rich with the scent of peat, its surface dimpled with countless shallow pools that catch the sky in fractured mirrors.  

The water is everywhere, but never deep—just a persistent presence, seeping up through the ground, beading on the leathery leaves of sparse reeds, dripping from the gnarled roots of ancient, half-submerged trees. Their trunks are twisted and hollow, their bark long since sloughed away, leaving behind skeletons of heartwood that gleam like wet bone. The air hums with moisture, thick enough to taste, and the horizon shimmers with heat and rising mist.  

Life here is slow, deliberate. Dragonflies hover in the thick air, their wings catching the light in iridescent flashes. Frogs croak from hidden hollows, their voices low and resonant, blending with the distant, rhythmic creaking of the trees as they shift in the sluggish breeze. There are no sudden movements, no sharp edges—only the slow pulse of the land itself, the gradual sinking of roots, the imperceptible creep of water carving its way through the peat.  

The soundscape is a layered murmur—the drip of condensation, the occasional plop of a fish or turtle breaking the surface, the rustle of reeds as something unseen moves through them. The wind, when it comes, is a sigh, not a roar.  

Time here is not marked in hours or days, but in the slow collapse of rotted stumps, the gradual filling of a depression with rainwater, the silent spread of algae across a stagnant pool. The land is patient. It has seen everything, and it is waiting.  

At the edges of perception, there is a sense that this place is not just old, but final—that it has reached the end of its cycles, that it will not change again, only persist, damp and warm and whispering, until the world itself wears away.
