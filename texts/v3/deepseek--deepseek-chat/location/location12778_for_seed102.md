**The Chamber of Root and Bone**  

Beneath the skin of the world, nestled in the dark embrace of stone, lies a hidden pocket—a cavern no larger than a temple hall, its ceiling low and ribbed with ancient, calcified roots. The air is warm, thick with the scent of damp earth and something richer, muskier, like the breath of a sleeping beast.  

The walls are smooth where water once ran, long ago, but now they are dry as old bone, their surfaces etched with faint, spiraling grooves—perhaps the work of some forgotten burrower, or the slow drag of a tool wielded by hands that have since turned to dust. The floor is uneven, scattered with loose stones and patches of soft, luminous moss that glows a dim, fungal green, casting just enough light to see by, if one’s eyes adjust.  

Life thrives here, despite the absence of water in any free-flowing form. Thick vines snake down from above, their leaves broad and waxy, drinking moisture from the air itself. They tangle with the skeletal remains of something larger—ribs like curved blades, a skull like a broken amphora—half-consumed by the slow creep of vegetation. Small, quick things scuttle in the shadows: beetles with iridescent shells, pale lizards that move like liquid, their tongues flicking at the air.  

The sound is overwhelming, a chorus of clicks and rustles, the distant drip of condensation somewhere deeper in the rock, the occasional low groan as the earth shifts imperceptibly. But nothing truly changes here. The vines grow, but so slowly it is almost imperceptible; the bones do not decay. The air does not stir.  

There are signs of those who came before—a crude knife left on a flat stone, its edge chipped but still sharp; a circle of blackened pebbles where a fire might have burned, though no ash remains. Whoever they were, they left no other trace.  

This is a place of waiting. Of things half-buried, half-alive. A secret kept by the earth itself.
