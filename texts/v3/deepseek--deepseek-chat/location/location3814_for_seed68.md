**The Damp Hollows of Vek-Tal**  

The air here is thick with the scent of wet stone and iron-rich soil, a cool breath clinging to the skin. The Hollows are a place of contradictions—lush greenery spills over sharp, freshly carved rock, as if the land itself is still settling into its form. The ground is uneven, pocked with shallow pools where rainwater gathers in smooth basins, reflecting the pale sky in fractured silver.  

A low mist drifts between the buildings of Vek-Tal, a town built into the hollowed-out ribs of an ancient riverbed. The structures are squat and sturdy, their foundations sunk deep into the damp earth, walls patched with moss and rusted metal plates. Chimneys exhale slow, sooty breaths into the air, the hum of machinery a constant drone beneath the chatter of voices and the distant rush of water.  

The vegetation is wild but contained—ferns unfurl in the cracks of cobblestones, vines creep up the sides of brick smokestacks, and clusters of broad-leafed plants gather where the light filters through the haze. The trees are young, their bark still smooth, their roots gripping the raw earth like fingers digging for purchase.  

Life is abundant but scattered. Birds with iridescent feathers flit between rooftops, their calls sharp and rhythmic. Small, quick creatures dart through the undergrowth, their eyes gleaming in the dim light. Occasionally, something larger moves at the edges of perception—a shadow slipping between buildings, a ripple in the water where nothing should be.  

The soundscape is layered—the steady drip of condensation from pipes, the groan of metal under strain, the murmur of voices carried on the damp air. There is no hurry here. Change comes slowly, in the creeping spread of moss over brick, in the gradual deepening of erosion channels in the soft stone.  

Vek-Tal feels like a place caught between states—neither fully wild nor entirely tamed, neither ancient nor newborn. It is a town built on land that is still deciding what it wants to be.
