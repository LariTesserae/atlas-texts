**The Weeping Hollows of Vaelis**  

Beneath the surface of the world, in a cavern system still young and restless with the memory of its formation, lies the Weeping Hollows—a place where water seeps from every crack, where the air is thick with condensation, and where the walls themselves seem to weep. The stone is fresh, its edges still sharp, its surfaces slick with mineral-rich runoff that glistens in the dim, bioluminescent glow of the cavern’s few light sources.  

The Hollows are a contradiction—both vast and claustrophobic. The ceilings are cathedral-high, arching into darkness, but the space feels enclosed, pressed in by the weight of the world above. The air is warm, almost stifling, thick with the scent of wet stone and something faintly metallic, like the aftertaste of lightning. The walls are lined with strange, vein-like formations, pulsing faintly with a slow, rhythmic light—some kind of engineered bioluminescence, grown rather than built, its origins lost to the city’s past.  

This is a drowned metropolis, half-swallowed by time and water. The streets—if they can be called streets—are canals, shallow but persistent, their surfaces rippling with unseen currents. The buildings are sleek, seamless, their architecture defying the logic of stone and mortar, as if they were extruded whole from the earth itself. Some lean precariously, their foundations softened by the constant damp; others stand pristine, untouched by decay, their surfaces repelling water like oil.  

Sound here is strange—echoes linger too long, bouncing off the wet walls in overlapping whispers. The drip of water is constant, a rhythm just shy of musical, punctuated by the occasional distant groan of settling stone. There is no wind, no birdsong, no rustle of leaves—only the hollows’ own voice, the sound of water and the hum of forgotten machines buried deep in the rock.  

The vegetation is lush but alien—thick, rubbery fronds sprout from cracks in the stone, their surfaces iridescent in the low light. Some curl inward, as if in prayer; others stretch toward the faint glow above, their movements slow but perceptible. There are no animals here, no skittering of insects or darting of fish—only the plants, and the water, and the silent, watchful presence of the city itself.  

The Hollows are young, still settling, still shifting. New passages open without warning; old ones collapse, swallowed by sudden floods. The water is patient, relentless. It carves its way through stone with a precision that feels deliberate, as if guided by some unseen hand.  

And above it all, the faint hum of something vast and incomprehensible—the city’s hidden heart, perhaps, or the echo of whatever technology built this place. It thrums just beneath hearing, a vibration in the bones, a reminder that this place was not meant to be empty.  

But it is.  

For now.
