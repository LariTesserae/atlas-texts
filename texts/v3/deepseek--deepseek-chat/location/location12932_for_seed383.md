### **The Hollow City of Dripping Stone**  

Beneath the surface of a world long worn smooth by time, the Hollow City stretches through caverns of black glass and porous rock. The air is cool, damp with the slow breath of underground rivers that whisper unseen through cracks in the earth. Water beads on the walls, trickling in thin, erratic veins—sometimes pooling in shallow basins, sometimes vanishing into the dark. The stone is ancient, its edges softened by millennia of condensation, its surfaces pocked with hollows where moisture has eaten away at the rock.  

This is a city of echoes and absence. Its structures—low, blocky buildings carved from the living stone—are scattered unevenly across the cavern floor, as if some great hand had once arranged them and then forgotten. The architecture is simple, functional: doorless arches, stepped platforms, and narrow alleys that twist unexpectedly into dead ends. There is no wood, no woven fiber—only stone, shaped by hand tools into crude but enduring forms. The city feels both abandoned and alive, its emptiness humming with unseen movement.  

Light comes from above—great shafts in the cavern ceiling, where some distant surface has cracked open to the sky. The beams fall in slanted columns, illuminating patches of the city in stark brightness while leaving the rest in deep shadow. The air is thick with dust motes, swirling in the light like slow fire.  

There are no plants here. No moss clings to the walls, no roots creep through the stone. The only life is animal—small, quick things that scuttle in the dark, their forms indistinct, their presence felt more in the flicker of movement at the edge of vision than in any clear sighting. They make no sound, or almost none—just the faintest scrape of claw on stone, the barest rustle of something passing.  

The city is not still. Though its bones are ancient, its spirit is restless. The air shifts unpredictably, currents moving against each other in silent turbulence. Sometimes a distant rumble shudders through the rock, a reminder that the earth here is not entirely settled. The water, too, is never constant—sometimes rising in sudden, shallow floods that recede just as quickly, leaving behind new patterns in the sediment.  

And always, the silence. Not total, but close—a world of held breath, where sound is swallowed by the weight of stone. The drip of water, the scuff of a footstep, the distant skitter of unseen creatures—these are the only interruptions.  

This is a place of half-formed things, of edges worn smooth by time but never fully erased. A city that was never meant to be lived in, yet persists. A world without green, without decay, only the slow patience of stone and the quiet, restless stirrings of what moves in the dark.
