**The Endless Grid**  

A vast, sun-blasted plain of interlocking hexagonal plates stretches to every horizon, their edges fused seamlessly in a geometry so precise it hums with intent. The surface is bone-dry, a desert of engineered alloy and ceramic, its metallic sheen reflecting the relentless white-gold light of a hovering sun. No water has touched this place in epochs—perhaps none ever has. The air is still, thin, and warm, carrying no scent but the faint ozone tang of dormant machinery.  

The plates are not uniform. Some are smooth as mirrors, others etched with fractal patterns that spiral into microscopic depths, their purpose long forgotten or never meant to be known. Between them, in the narrowest of seams, a faint glow pulses—not light, exactly, but something deeper, a subvisual thrum of energy that suggests the Grid is not dead, only waiting.  

Occasionally, a disturbance: a plate shifts, fractionally, with a soundless shudder, realigning itself to some unseen calculus. The movement sends vibrations through the ground, imperceptible to touch but felt in the bones, a reminder that this place is not static, only slow beyond human reckoning.  

There is life here, though not life as we know it. In the hollows between plates, where shadows pool like liquid graphite, thin filaments of something organic yet not biological stretch and retract, sensing the air. They are sparse, rare, and utterly silent. Perhaps they are maintenance drones, or the last descendants of a biomechanical ecosystem. Perhaps they are something else entirely.  

The sky is a flat, depthless blue, unmarked by cloud or bird. The silence is near-total, broken only by the rare, distant groan of the Grid adjusting itself—a sound so deep it is less heard than remembered.  

This is a place built to last. A place that has outlived its builders, its purpose, perhaps even its own memory. It does not decay. It erodes only in the sense that mountains erode: over spans of time that laugh at human scales. It is ancient, yes, but not dying. Merely settled into its final, perfect form.  

And it is watching. Not with eyes, but with the weight of its presence, the sheer density of its existence. Every plate, every seam, every pulse of buried light is a word in a language no one speaks anymore.  

You stand on the skin of a god that has forgotten it is divine.
