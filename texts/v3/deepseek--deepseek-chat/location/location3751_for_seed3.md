### **The Folding City of Vhorth**  

At the edge of the world, where the mountains claw at the sky and the air is thin enough to taste like metal, there is a city that does not sit upon the earth so much as *unfold* from it.  

Vhorth is a place of slow fractures, of stone that breathes. The streets are not paved—they are *revealed*, split open by some unseen pressure, their edges still raw and glistening with damp. The buildings rise like teeth, jagged and uneven, their facades half-carved by wind and half-grown from the living rock. Some structures lean drunkenly, their foundations still shifting in the slow, tectonic yawn of the land. Others are skeletal, their iron frames exposed, rusting in the perpetual mist that clings to the city like a second skin.  

The air is cool, thick with the scent of wet stone and iron-rich soil. Water seeps from cracks in the walls, pooling in hollows before vanishing again into the earth. There are no rivers here, only the occasional trickle, the silent weeping of the stone. Moss clings to every surface, a velvet green that softens the harsh angles of the architecture. Ferns sprout from gutters, their fronds trembling in the faint, directionless breeze.  

The city is loud, but not with voices. The sound is the groan of settling foundations, the distant hum of machinery buried deep in the rock. Factories exhale steam through vents in the mountainside, their chimneys belching black smoke that lingers in the low-hanging clouds. The streets are sparsely populated—people move through them like shadows, wrapped in thick coats, their footsteps muffled by the damp.  

The wildlife is abundant but strange. Birds with too many eyes nest in the eaves of half-formed buildings. Something large and furred slinks through the alleys at night, leaving behind prints that vanish by morning. Occasionally, a section of the city will *shift*—a wall will ripple like water, a doorway will appear where none stood before. The locals do not speak of it.  

Time moves differently here. The city is being born, but it is a birth measured in centuries, not hours. The streets widen imperceptibly, the cliffs erode grain by grain. The people of Vhorth live in the cracks of this slow transformation, building their lives in the spaces between the stone’s breaths.  

And sometimes, when the mist is thick enough to swallow sound, you can hear the mountain itself dreaming.
