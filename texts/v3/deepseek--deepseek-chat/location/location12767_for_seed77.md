**The Vast Constructed Desert**  

A sprawling, low-lying expanse of baked and buckling stone stretches to the horizon, its surface cracked like the glaze of an ancient kiln. This is not a natural desert—it is made, every inch of it, though by what hands or forces, none remain to say. The ground is a patchwork of geometric slabs, their edges worn soft by time, their surfaces pocked with shallow depressions where moisture gathers in fleeting pools.  

The air is thick with heat, pressing down like a smothering hand. There is no wind, only the slow, suffocating weight of stillness. Yet, despite the aridity, water exists here—not as rivers or lakes, but as condensation, as sweat on stone, as dampness seeping up from hidden seams in the architecture of the world. In the pre-dawn hours, the air cools just enough for dew to form in the hollows, glistening briefly before the sun drinks it away.  

Vegetation is sparse, but not absent. Strange, fibrous growths cling to the cracks between slabs, their roots probing downward in search of moisture. They are not plants as we know them—more like lichen, or perhaps something older, something that thrives on the slow decay of the constructed ground itself. They do not sway or rustle; they simply exist, patient and unchanging.  

The silence is profound. No birds call, no insects hum. The only sounds are the occasional creak of cooling stone, the faint whisper of evaporating water, the distant groan of the desert settling into itself. The land is active, but its movements are slow, geological—shifts and fractures that take lifetimes to unfold.  

Above it all, the sky is vast and indifferent, a dome of pale, heat-blanched blue. Sometimes, clouds form—not the towering thunderheads of fertile lands, but thin, gauzy veils that drift without purpose, shedding little rain. When they do, the water vanishes almost instantly, swallowed by the thirsty stone.  

This is a place of waiting. Of erosion just beginning, of a world still deciding what it will become. It is empty, but not lifeless. It is made, but not finished. And it stretches on, endless and unchanging, under the weight of the sun.
