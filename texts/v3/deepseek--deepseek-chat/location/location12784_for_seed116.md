**The Drowned Spire**  

A skeletal tower rises from the endless sea, its blackened metal bones crusted with salt and draped in thick ropes of kelp. The structure is ancient—older than the drowned cities that sleep beneath the waves, older than the last dry land that vanished beneath the tides. It stands alone, a needle of rust and defiance, its hollow chambers filled with the echoes of the deep.  

The water here is never still. Waves surge against the tower’s base, their rhythm a constant, thunderous drone. The air is thick with salt and the metallic tang of corroding steel. Warm currents coil around the spire, carrying schools of silver fish that dart between its girders like living sparks. Above, the sky is a bruised purple, heavy with storms that never quite break.  

The tower is not empty. Strange life clings to its surfaces—bioluminescent barnacles pulse in the shadows, their blue-green glow flickering in time with the waves. Colonies of translucent crabs scuttle along the beams, their shells like frosted glass. Higher up, where the wind howls through broken windows, seabirds nest in the hollows of forgotten machinery, their cries sharp against the roar of the sea.  

This place is a relic of industry, a thing built when the world still had builders. The mechanisms within are rusted but not dead—somewhere deep inside, a great engine groans, its pistons still grinding out a slow, senseless rhythm. The tower was never meant to be submerged, yet here it stands, half-consumed by the ocean, its purpose lost but its form enduring.  

The water is deep here, unfathomably so. Below the tower, the abyss yawns, home to things that move in the dark. Sometimes, vast shapes glide past the outer beams, their bodies indistinct in the gloom. The tower trembles when they pass.  

There is no land in sight. Only the spire, the sea, and the slow, inevitable work of erosion. The waves will take this place too, in time. But not yet. Not yet.
