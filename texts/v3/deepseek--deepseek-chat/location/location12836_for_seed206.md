**The Boiler Room of the World**  

Deep in the sodden underbelly of an unnamed industrial city, where the streets steam and the air hums with the pulse of unseen machinery, there is a place where the world exhales.  

It is a pocket of warm, wet stillness—a single chamber, no larger than a forgotten storeroom, tucked beneath the weight of pipes and concrete. The walls are not walls at all, but the gnarled, knotted roots of some vast, unseen tree, pressing in from all sides like the ribs of a living thing. They weep constantly, their bark slick with condensation, their crevices pooling with water that never quite overflows. The roots have grown around pipes, swallowing them whole in places, their surfaces studded with rivulets of rust where metal surrenders to slow digestion.  

The air is thick, warm as breath, and smells of wet earth and engine oil. The light is dim, filtered through a single grime-caked vent high above, casting a sickly yellow glow over everything. It is enough to see by, but only just—enough to make out the shapes that move here.  

Because this place is not empty.  

The water that seeps from the roots collects in shallow, blackened pools, and in them, things stir. Pale, blind crustaceans scuttle over submerged gears, their shells clicking softly against the metal. Slender, translucent worms undulate in the film on the water’s surface, their bodies catching the light like oil slicks. Occasionally, something larger shifts in the deeper shadows—a slow, sinuous motion, a ripple where no ripple should be.  

The sound is constant: the drip of water, the creak of straining roots, the distant, rhythmic thud of machinery, felt more than heard, a bass pulse through the walls. It never changes. It has been this way for decades. Perhaps longer.  

This is a place of perfect equilibrium. The roots drink the water that seeps in; the water carries the rust and the rot, feeding the roots in turn. The creatures here do not starve. They do not multiply. They simply *are*, as much a part of the room as the pipes, as the light, as the hum of the city above.  

Nothing is built here. Nothing needs to be. The world made this place itself, and the world sustains it.  

You could crouch here for hours, listening, watching, waiting for something to change.  

It won’t.
