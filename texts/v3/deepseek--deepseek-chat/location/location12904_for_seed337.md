**The Damp Hollows of the Undercity**  

Beneath the surface of the world, in a vast, open cavern where the air is cool and thick with the scent of wet stone, the Undercity clings to the walls and floors like a lichen crust. The space is immense—a sprawling network of half-formed tunnels and hollows, their edges still raw, as if the rock itself is hesitating between solidity and dissolution. Water seeps from the ceiling in slow, irregular drips, pooling in shallow basins or running in thin, whispering rivulets down the rough-hewn walls. The moisture is constant but never abundant—just enough to keep the stone slick, the air damp, the inhabitants always aware of the slow, patient work of water.  

The city is packed tight, a warren of stacked dwellings carved directly into the rock or built from salvaged stone and fibrous, pale fungi that grow in the dim light. There is no wasted space. Every ledge, every crevice is occupied—by homes, by workshops, by the occasional struggling patch of underground moss or blind, creeping vines that twist their way through cracks in the architecture. The people here are builders, but their tools are simple: chisels of hardened bone, hammers of dense mushroom-wood, ropes woven from sinew and root. They work in quiet industry, expanding their city deeper into the stone, shaping what is still unshaped.  

The light is steady but dim, cast by bioluminescent lichen cultivated in hanging clay pots, or by the occasional flicker of fat-burning lamps. The glow is just enough to see by, to navigate the tight alleys and switchback staircases that wind through the cavern. The air is alive with the sounds of dripping water, the scrape of tools, the murmur of voices—never loud, never chaotic, but always present, like the hum of a slow-turning mill.  

Life here is sparse but persistent. Blind fish dart in the shallow pools; pale, many-legged things scuttle into shadows when disturbed. Larger creatures are rare—perhaps the occasional bat, or something stranger, glimpsed only at the edges of the light. The people of the Undercity do not hunt so much as they cultivate, coaxing what little life they can from the damp and the dark.  

This is a place still becoming. The stone is young, the caverns still settling. The city grows into the spaces left by retreating water, by crumbling rock, by the patient labor of hands that have known no other home. It is a world of quiet motion, of slow expansion, of damp and cool and closeness. A place where every breath carries the taste of wet earth, where every sound is softened by the weight of stone above. A city beneath the world, waiting for its shape to fully emerge.
