The landscape stretches endlessly into a vast, whispering darkness—a continent of living plants that breathes as one entity, a single organism scaled to the dimensions of a planet. Here, the boundaries between individual plants dissolve into a seamless tapestry of green and black, a living mass that pulses faintly with an inner rhythm, though no wind stirs its leaves. The air is thick, warm, and heavy with moisture, clinging to the skin like a second layer. Water seeps from every surface—from the glossy leaves that drip steadily, from the spongy ground that squelches faintly underfoot, from the weeping trunks of trees that tower into the unseen canopy above. This is not a marsh but a forest of perpetual condensation, where water hangs in the air as a mist, never quite falling, never quite rising.

The vegetation is total, overwhelming, absolute. It is not merely a collection of plants but a dominion of green life that has consumed all else. The ground is a mat of roots and moss, soft and yielding, constantly shifting as if alive. Above, the branches interweave into a solid canopy, so thick that no light penetrates—only the faintest glow, a bioluminescent shimmer that emanates from the leaves themselves, casting everything in a ghostly green hue. The trunks of the trees are not separate entities but fused columns, their bark smooth and damp, weeping a clear, viscous sap that pools in hollows and trickles into unseen streams. There are no clearings, no breaks in the green—only an endless, suffocating density of life.

Despite its vastness, the landscape feels claustrophobic, as though the sheer mass of vegetation presses inward on the senses. The silence is profound, broken only by the occasional drip of water or the faint creak of shifting branches. No animals stir here—no birds, no insects, no unseen scurrying in the undergrowth. This is a world without fauna, where plants have claimed every niche, every role, every breath of existence. The absence of life other than plants feels eerie, as though the world has been stripped of something fundamental.

Yet, within this stillness, there is a strange, industrial hum—not loud, but pervasive, a vibration that seems to emanate from the plants themselves. It is as though the forest is a vast machine, its roots and stems and leaves working in unison to some incomprehensible purpose. The sap that flows from the trees is not merely water but a nutrient-rich fluid that fuels this living engine. The forest feels alive in a way that transcends biology, as though it has evolved into something more than a collection of plants—a single, planet-sized organism that operates on principles beyond understanding.

The air smells of damp earth and greenery, with a faint metallic tang that hints at the strange processes at work. The moisture clings to everything, condensing on leaves and skin alike, pooling in hollows and trickling into unseen channels. The ground is uneven, not from erosion but from the constant growth and movement of the plants beneath the surface. Roots push upward, forming ridges and hummocks, while others sink back into the soil, creating depressions that fill with water. The landscape is young, fresh, still in the process of forming itself, though it feels timeless, as though it has always been this way and always will be.

Time here feels stagnant, frozen in an eternal moment. The forest does not change, does not grow or decay, but exists in a state of perfect equilibrium. The sap flows, the leaves drip, the hum persists—but nothing ever shifts, nothing ever ages. It is a world caught in a single breath, a snapshot of a living process paused indefinitely.

This is a place of quiet strangeness, where the rules of the familiar world no longer apply. The sheer scale of the forest is incomprehensible, stretching beyond the horizon in every direction, its own weather systems forming within the canopy. Clouds of mist rise and fall, but no rain falls—only the endless drip of condensation. The forest bends physics to its will, creating a world where plants are not merely living things but the foundation of reality itself.

To walk here is to step into a living dream, a place where the boundaries between life and landscape blur, where the air itself feels alive, and where the silence speaks louder than any sound. It is a world untamed, untouched by human hands, yet suffused with an industrial hum that hints at a deeper, stranger purpose. This is the Plant-Continent, a place of water and green and darkness, where the rules of existence are rewritten, and the familiar is left far behind.
