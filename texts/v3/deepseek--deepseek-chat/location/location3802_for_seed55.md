**The Drowned Cathedral of the Endless Tides**  

The world is water—endless, black, and heavy. A vast submerged cathedral, its vaulted ceilings lost in the crushing dark above, its pillars worn smooth by millennia of slow currents. The stone is ancient beyond reckoning, its edges softened into curves by the patient caress of the deep. The walls are carved with glyphs no living hand remembers, their meanings dissolved into the silt.  

The water is cold—not the sharp bite of ice, but the deep, abyssal chill of a place that has never known warmth. It presses in from all sides, thick with the weight of ages. The currents move sluggishly, shifting in slow, cathedral-wide breaths, stirring the sediment in lazy spirals.  

And yet, life persists. Thick curtains of kelp and luminous algae cling to the pillars, their fronds swaying in the unseen tides. Bioluminescent nodules pulse faintly in the dark, casting ghostly halos that flicker and fade. The vegetation is lush but strange—leafless, rubbery, adapted to the eternal night. It grows in dense, tangled masses, filling the spaces between the pillars, softening the hard lines of the drowned architecture.  

There is no sky here, no sun—only the endless black above, pressing down like a lid. The floor is a thick slurry of silt and broken stone, littered with the remnants of forgotten craftsmanship: rusted tools, half-buried statuary, the skeletal remains of scaffolding that once held up a civilization now lost to the tides.  

The silence is near-total. The water swallows sound, muffling even the slowest movements. Occasionally, a distant groan echoes through the stone—some vast, unseen pressure shifting in the deep. But for the most part, there is only the quiet.  

This place was built—every inch of it, every arch, every glyph—but by whom, and for what purpose, is lost. The builders are gone, their names erased by the water. Only their work remains, sinking slowly into the silt, settling into its final form.  

The world does not hurry. It has all the time in the universe to decay.
