**The Verdant Abyss**  

A vast, steaming basin stretches beneath a bruised sky, its edges lost in mist and distance. This is a land where the air itself is thick with life—humid, cloying, pressing against the skin like a living thing. The heat is relentless, a wet weight that clings to every surface, drawing sweat from stone and flesh alike.  

The ground is a patchwork of damp stone and spongy moss, veined with thin rivulets that appear and vanish with the whims of condensation. Water does not flow here so much as it *seeps*—rising in beads on the undersides of leaves, pooling in hollows between roots, trickling in slow, unseen currents beneath the surface. The air tastes of iron and rot, of green things growing and dying in the same breath.  

And grow they do. The vegetation is not merely abundant—it is *total*. Towering ferns unfurl their fronds like the fingers of some colossal hand, their undersides studded with glistening spores. Vines as thick as a human arm twist skyward, strangling the skeletal remains of older growth, weaving a lattice so dense that in places the canopy is solid, a second earth suspended in the air. The trees themselves are monstrous, their trunks swollen with stored water, their bark peeling in great fibrous strips to reveal flesh-like wood beneath. Some have fused together where their roots met, forming hybrid behemoths that groan in the wind.  

The sound is overwhelming. A constant drone of insects, a chorus of unseen creatures shrieking, clicking, hissing. The air thrums with wings—some no larger than a grain of rice, others broad as a man’s chest, their passage stirring the hanging moss into swaying veils. Beneath it all, the wet *crunch* of something moving, feeding, dying.  

The land is ancient, worn smooth by time but never tamed. Erosion has carved deep channels into the stone, but the plants have reclaimed them, their roots knitting the earth back together in slow, stubborn defiance. The few signs of human presence are half-swallowed: a crumbling wall of stacked stones, its mortar long since dissolved; a rusted blade wedged in the crook of a tree, the wood grown around it like a scab.  

And the creatures—oh, the creatures. They move through the green shadows in flashes of scaled hide and chitin, too fast to track, too many to count. Some are small, skittering things that vanish into the undergrowth at the slightest tremor. Others are vast, their passage marked by the shuddering collapse of foliage, their calls echoing through the canopy like distant thunder.  

This is not a place meant for people. It is a world unto itself, breathing, pulsing, alive in ways that defy understanding. The air shimmers with unseen currents, the ground shifts beneath unseen burrowers, and the plants—always the plants—stretch their tendrils toward the weak, filtering light, waiting.  

To stand here is to stand inside the belly of something vast and hungry. And it is watching.  

Always watching.
