**The Gloomdelta**  

A vast, low-lying basin stretches beneath a sky choked with slow-swirling vapors, their edges lit by a dim, diffuse glow—not quite daylight, not quite twilight, but something in between, as if the sun were a memory half-recalled. The air is warm and thick, clinging to the skin like damp velvet, carrying the scent of wet stone and something faintly metallic.  

The land itself is young, raw—freshly carved by unseen forces. Jagged ridges rise abruptly from the spongy earth, their edges still sharp, not yet worn smooth by time. Between them, shallow channels weave like veins, sometimes dry, sometimes pulsing with sluggish, silvery water that doesn’t quite behave like water should. It beads on surfaces, clings in strange, elastic strands, and in places, it hums.  

Vegetation erupts in dense, chaotic patches—lush and overgrown, but not where you expect it. Towering fungal spires, their caps glowing faintly in the gloom, cluster around the edges of mineral outcroppings. Vines thick as arms twist through the air, suspended between structures that might be trees, if trees were made of layered, interlocking plates of something more like bone than wood. Their leaves ripple faintly even when there is no wind.  

The sound is overwhelming. A constant, droning thrum fills the air, layered with clicks, chitters, and distant, resonant booms that shudder through the ground. It’s not clear what makes the noise—perhaps the vibrating water, perhaps the throbbing growths that pulse along the fungal towers, perhaps something unseen moving through the mist.  

And yet, for all the life, the space feels empty. Vast stretches of cracked, glassy earth stretch between the pockets of density, as if the landscape itself is still deciding where to put things. Occasionally, something moves in the distance—a shadow too large, a flicker of motion too quick—but nothing lingers in the open.  

At the heart of the basin, a city rises—or perhaps it is growing. Its structures are impossible geometries, surfaces too smooth, angles that shouldn’t hold weight but do. Bridges arc between towers with no visible support, and the air around them shimmers faintly, as if reality is thinner there. There are no roads, only shifting pathways that seem to rearrange themselves when no one is looking. The inhabitants, if there are any, are not visible. But sometimes, the walls breathe.  

This is a place still becoming. A wound in the world that hasn’t decided whether to heal or fester. The water is alive, the plants are watching, and the sky is listening. You walk carefully. You don’t stay long.
