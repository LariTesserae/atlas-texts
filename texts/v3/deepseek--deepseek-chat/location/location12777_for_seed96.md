**The Glass Marsh of the High Spires**  

The world is a vast, gleaming wound—a landscape of fractured glass and weeping stone, stretching across the high plateaus of an ancient, abandoned forge. The air is cool and thick with condensation, beading on every surface, trickling in slow, deliberate rivulets down the jagged spires that rise like frozen lightning strikes from the ground. The light here is relentless, bouncing between the glassy facets, refracting into prismatic shards that paint the ground in fleeting rainbows.  

This is a place without life as we know it. No plants, no animals—only the slow, mineral breath of the earth itself. The ground is a mosaic of freshly formed glass, still sharp-edged, barely worn by time. It was made, all of it, shaped by hands long gone or processes too slow to witness. The water does not pool so much as seep, rising from hidden fractures, condensing on the undersides of overhangs, dripping in a rhythm so slow it feels like the land itself is counting seconds.  

The air is quiet, save for the occasional chime of glass shifting against glass, the faint hiss of moisture evaporating from warm surfaces. The scale is open—you can see for miles across the glittering expanse, but the density is overwhelming, every inch occupied by the glass formations, the slick mineral flows, the towering spires. There is no emptiness here, only saturation.  

And yet, there is something alive in the way the light moves, the way the water traces paths that seem almost intentional. Perhaps this is a world of patterns, not creatures—a place where energy writes itself into the land, where the slow pulse of condensation and evaporation is the closest thing to a heartbeat.  

You walk carefully. The glass is sharp, the ground treacherous. The world does not welcome or reject you. It simply is—bright, wet, silent, and waiting.
