**The Subterranean Steam Gardens of Yth-Vaar**  

Beneath the surface of a scorched and wind-blasted world, the air thickens into a damp, metallic haze. You descend through a crack in the earth—a fissure lined with blackened basalt, smoothed by centuries of slow, seeping moisture—and emerge into a cavern no larger than a cathedral’s nave. The ceiling is low, domed, and studded with glassy nodules that catch what little light filters down from above, refracting it into dim, shifting veils of amber and green.  

The heat is oppressive, just shy of scalding. The walls weep. Rivulets of mineral-rich water trace winding paths down the rock, pooling in shallow basins before vanishing into unseen drains. The sound is constant: a low, industrial thrum, like the heartbeat of some vast, buried machine. It pulses through the stone, vibrating in your teeth. Occasionally, a gout of steam hisses from a vent in the floor, adding its voice to the chorus.  

The cavern is lush—not with life, but with something stranger. Thick, rubbery tendrils of a translucent, gel-like substance cling to the walls, their surfaces glistening with condensation. They pulse faintly, as if breathing, though there is no wind here, no creature to disturb them. Some are veined with luminescent filaments, casting eerie, bioluminescent halos. Others are threaded with copper and iron, grown around ancient pipes that snake through the rock like the roots of some forgotten industry.  

Scattered throughout are the remnants of human work—rusted gears half-swallowed by the gelatinous growths, a skeletal framework of steel beams supporting nothing, a single corroded control panel still blinking with a dull, stubborn light. The machinery is old, long since claimed by the cavern’s slow metabolism, but it has not decayed so much as been *incorporated*. The walls are pocked with alcoves where tools and instruments have been set into the stone with deliberate care, though by whom—or what—is unclear.  

There is no animal life here. No scuttling things, no echoes of wings or claws. Only the slow, rhythmic expansion and contraction of the cavern itself, the steady drip of water, the hum of unseen engines. The air smells of ozone and wet metal, of something alive and yet not alive.  

Time moves differently here. The heat and the sound and the dim light conspire to stretch moments into eternities. You could stand for hours watching the steam rise, watching the tendrils sway in some imperceptible current, and never notice the passage of the world above.  

This is a place of deep equilibrium. The heat does not fade; the moisture does not dry. The machines do not fail, not entirely. They simply become part of the garden.  

And the garden grows. Slowly. Patiently. Without need for sun or wind or anything but the steady pulse of the earth’s hidden heart.
