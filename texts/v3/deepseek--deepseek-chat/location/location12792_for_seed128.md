**The Weeping Engine**  

A vast, humming monolith rises from the marsh, its blackened surfaces slick with perpetual condensation. The air is thick with the scent of wet metal and slow decay, the warmth pressing against your skin like a living thing. This is a place where water does not fall but *emerges*—seeping from rivets in the structure’s flank, pooling in the hollows of its ancient, eroded channels, dripping from the edges of unseen mechanisms in a rhythm just shy of musical.  

The ground is a spongy mat of engineered fibers, woven to mimic peat but threaded through with silver filaments that pulse faintly in the dim light. Vegetation is sparse but deliberate—tufts of black reeds with glass-like stems, their roots drinking from the constant moisture, their tips glinting with something sharper than dew. Occasionally, a ripple moves through them, though there is no wind.  

Life thrives here, but it is not the life of any natural world. Tiny, segmented creatures scuttle across the monolith’s surface, their carapaces iridescent with oil-slick hues. Larger things move in the water—shapes that might be eels, if eels were made of linked, articulated segments, their bodies coiling and uncoiling in the murk. Their eyes, when they surface, are faceted, catching the dull glow of the structure’s ambient luminescence.  

Sound is everywhere—a deep, resonant thrumming from within the monolith, the clicking and whirring of unseen mechanisms, the occasional metallic groan as some vast, buried system shifts. It is not loud in the way of a storm, but in the way of a machine that never sleeps, a vibration that settles in your bones.  

The air shimmers at the edges of vision, hinting at forces barely contained. Sometimes, the water moves against gravity, droplets sliding upward along the monolith’s surface before vanishing into seams too fine to see. The rules here are not the rules of any world you know—this is a place built by minds (or things) that understood physics as a suggestion, not a law.  

And yet, for all its strangeness, the Weeping Engine feels *settled*. The erosion is not decay, but a kind of wearing-in, like the patina on a well-used tool. The water has carved its paths over centuries, the creatures have adapted to their roles, the structure hums with the quiet assurance of something that has existed, just like this, for a very long time.  

You stand in the shadow of a god that does not know your name, and the marsh breathes around you.
