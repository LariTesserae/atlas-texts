**The Dripping Vaults**  

Beneath the surface, the air hangs thick and warm, pressing against your skin like a damp cloth. The darkness is not absolute—somewhere, water glistens on the walls, catching what little light seeps through cracks in the ceiling far above. The cavern is young, its contours still sharp, untouched by the smoothing hand of time. Stalactites dagger down, their tips beading with moisture that falls in slow, irregular drips—each one a tiny event in the silence.  

The walls are veined with thin streams of water, trickling down in rivulets that gather in shallow pools scattered across the uneven floor. The stone is dark, almost black, but where the water runs, it shines slick and metallic, as if the rock itself sweats. The air smells of wet stone and something faintly mineral, a whisper of iron or sulfur.  

Life here is sparse but insistent. Patches of luminous moss cling to the walls, their glow a dim, eerie green, just enough to outline the cavern’s contours. In places, thicker clusters of fungi sprout—pale, fleshy things that seem to pulse faintly in the dark, feeding on the damp and the warmth. There are no animals, no scuttling things or echoes of movement—just the slow respiration of water and growth.  

At the far end of the chamber, half-submerged in one of the larger pools, sits the ruin of some forgotten machine. Its metal carcass is rusted but still recognizable—a piston, perhaps, or part of a pump, now seized in place by corrosion. The remnants of pipes snake away into the walls, their purpose long lost. Whoever left it here did not stay. The cavern has reclaimed it, wrapping the metal in a thin skin of mineral deposits, turning industry into geology.  

Time moves differently here. The water drips at its own pace. The moss spreads in imperceptible increments. The machine sinks deeper into the pool, millimeter by millimeter, year by year. There is no hurry. The Vaults will wait.  

And in the silence, if you listen long enough, you might hear the cavern breathe—a slow, wet exhalation, as the earth shifts somewhere in the dark.
