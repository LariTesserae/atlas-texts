**The Chamber of the Hungry Veins**  

You stand in a vast, domed cavern, its ceiling lost in a darkness so thick it seems to swallow even the idea of light. The air is furnace-hot, pressing against your skin like a living thing, and the silence is broken only by the occasional, unsettling *click*—something shifting, something tasting the air.  

The walls are not stone, not in any familiar sense. They are a dense lattice of fibrous, vein-like structures, pulsing faintly with a dull, internal glow—not enough to illuminate, only to outline their own writhing forms. The veins are packed so tightly that there is no true surface to the cavern, only layer upon layer of them, knotted and coiled, some as thick as your arm, others fine as threads. They are dry, leathery to the touch, but not brittle—they yield slightly, like muscle under skin.  

There is no water here, not in any recognizable form. The veins secrete a thin, waxy residue that coats everything, a substance that resists evaporation even in this heat. It smells faintly metallic, like old blood and rust.  

And the cavern is alive with movement. Not with creatures as you know them, but with *presences*—shapes that flow between the veins, slipping through the lattice as if it were liquid. They are not separate from the environment; they are extensions of it, limbs or tendrils or mouths that emerge, quest, and withdraw. Some are slow, deliberate, others flicker like half-seen shadows. They do not hunt, exactly. They *sample*. A brush against your sleeve, a whisper of contact against your ankle—testing, absorbing.  

Scattered throughout the chamber are remnants—not built, not crafted, but *left*. A half-buried curve of smooth material, too regular to be natural. A cluster of vein-things that have grown around and into it, as if digesting it. Elsewhere, a fragment of something like ceramic, etched with marks that might have been writing once, now worn into illegibility by time and the slow caress of the chamber’s inhabitants.  

This place is ancient. It has existed in this state for longer than memory, longer than language. The veins do not grow or decay; they simply *are*, shifting in endless, slow reconfiguration. The things that move among them are neither predator nor prey—they are processes, manifestations of the chamber’s own metabolism.  

You are not meant to be here. The heat, the silence, the probing touches—they suggest a system that has no reference for you. A system that is complete unto itself.  

And yet, you are being *considered*.
