**The Endless Salt**  

A vast, bone-white plain stretches to every horizon, cracked and fissured like the skin of some ancient, slumbering beast. The ground is not sand, not stone, but a fine-grained salt, compacted into brittle plates that shift underfoot with a sound like distant glass breaking. There is no water here—not now, not for epochs. The air is cool, thin, pressing down with the weight of altitude, yet somehow empty, as if the sky itself has been hollowed out.  

The land is old. Not just old—*finished*. It has been worn down to its essence, every peak eroded, every valley filled, until all that remains is this flat, unyielding expanse. The salt is not pure; veins of something darker run through it, metallic or organic, glinting faintly in the pale light. The sun hangs low, diffused, casting no sharp shadows, only a dull glow that makes the air feel like tarnished silver.  

There are structures here—or the ghosts of them. A lone pillar, half-sunk into the salt, its surface etched with glyphs that might be writing or might be the scars of some long-forgotten chemical reaction. A shattered dome, its ribs exposed like the skeleton of a long-dead leviathan. They are not ruins, exactly. They feel deliberate, as if they were built to decay, to dissolve back into the salt from which they came.  

The wind moves, but there is no sound. Or rather, the sound is so faint, so sparse, that it might as well be silence—a whisper of granules shifting, a distant hum that could be machinery or could be the land itself exhaling.  

Life is rare. Not absent, but *waiting*. A scuttling thing, armored in crystalline plates, drags itself between the cracks. Something else—a shadow, a flicker—darts beneath the salt crust, too fast to see. They are not animals as we know them. They are remnants, survivors of a world that has already ended, living on in the hollows of this final landscape.  

And yet, despite its stillness, the land is not dead. It is *turbulent* beneath the surface. The salt shifts unpredictably, whole sections rising or collapsing without warning. Strange energies flicker at the edges of vision—a shimmer in the air, a pulse of light from deep below. The ground itself seems alive with some incomprehensible process, as if the entire plain is a machine, or an organism, or something else entirely, cycling through its final motions before—what? Another transformation? Or simply the long, slow fade into absolute stillness?  

This is a place where time has run its course. A place that has settled into its own eternity. And yet, even here, something stirs.
