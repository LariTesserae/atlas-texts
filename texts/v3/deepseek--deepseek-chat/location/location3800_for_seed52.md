### **The Drowned City of Vhalei**  

The air is thick with the scent of wet stone and slow-growing moss. Warm, but not oppressive—a clinging dampness that settles on the skin like a second layer. The sky is a bruised twilight, dim and diffuse, as if the sun struggles to pierce the perpetual haze. When rain comes, it is soft, almost apologetic, misting over the ruins rather than drenching them.  

Vhalei was once a city of impossible geometries, its towers built from materials that bent light and gravity in ways modern physics cannot explain. Now, it is half-swallowed by time and water. The streets are canals, sluggish and dark, fed by underground springs that well up between the cracks in the pavement. The buildings—some still standing, others collapsed into jagged teeth—are coated in luminous lichen, their surfaces etched with the remnants of circuitry that hums faintly when touched.  

The land around the city is young, still settling. The earth shifts in slow, groaning sighs, new fissures opening to reveal veins of iridescent mineral deposits. The vegetation is lush but sparse, great towering ferns and rubbery-leaved vines that cling to the ruins, their roots threading through the bones of the old metropolis. There are no birds, only the occasional skittering of something small and many-legged retreating into the shadows.  

Sound here is rare. The water murmurs, the wind stirs the leaves, but there are no voices, no machines, no cries of animals. The silence is broken only by the distant, irregular thrum of the city’s last functioning structures—massive, buried engines that no one remembers how to control.  

And yet, the place is alive. The water moves, the plants grow, the ruins exhale. The air shivers with unseen energies, remnants of the technology that once shaped this world. Sometimes, if you stand still long enough, you can feel the city dreaming.
