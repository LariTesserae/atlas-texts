**The Singing Hollows of Veyth-Ul**  

At the edge of perception, where the air hums thick with unseen life, lies the **Singing Hollows**—a place where the world has grown too thin, and something else breathes through the cracks.  

### **The Terrain: A Living Labyrinth**  
The ground is not ground at all, but a vast, spongy lattice of interwoven roots, stems, and fungal threads, fused into a single breathing organism. It rises in low, undulating hills, never more than a few meters high, but so dense with fibrous tissue that walking upon it feels like pressing into the flesh of a sleeping giant. The air is warm, damp, thick with the scent of fermenting sap and the musk of unseen creatures moving beneath the surface.  

Water does not pool here—it weeps. The roots drink from hidden aquifers, exhaling moisture in slow, glistening beads that trickle down into the hollows below. Occasionally, a heavier rain passes through, and the entire structure shudders, its capillaries swelling with sudden life.  

### **The Architecture: A City That Grew Itself**  
This is not a ruin. It was never built. Yet it is undeniably **urban**—a settlement of organic causeways, arched fungal buttresses, and chambers hollowed out by some ancient, deliberate force. The walls are striated with growth rings, their surfaces etched with spiraling patterns that might be language, or might simply be the way the world writes itself here.  

There are no tools, no machines—only the slow, inevitable logic of biology. Staircases coil like vines; doorways gape like the mouths of enormous flowers. The structures are not inhabited—not by anything that would recognize itself as a builder—but they are **used**.  

### **The Sound: A Deafening Chorus**  
The air thrums. It is not wind, not water, not the calls of beasts—it is the land itself, vibrating in a constant, dissonant hymn. The roots resonate like harp strings, the hollow chambers amplify unseen currents, and the creatures that live here—**abundant, restless, alien**—add their voices to the din. Somewhere beneath the surface, something vast shifts in its sleep, and the sound deepens, rattling the bones of those who stand too close.  

### **The Life: A Fractured Ecosystem**  
The fauna here do not obey the rules of predator and prey. They are shapes without fixed forms, flickering between states—a swarm of winged things that dissolves into mist, a many-legged shadow that flows like liquid between the roots. They are **abundant**, but the space is **empty**—they exist in pockets, appearing and vanishing as if stepping through unseen doors.  

The plants are not separate from the creatures. They are the stage, the actors, and the audience. Vines twitch with independent motion; flowers pulse with bioluminescent signals. Somewhere in the deeper chambers, there are things that might be trees, or might be gods, their trunks braided with glowing veins.  

### **The Rules of This Place**  
- **Time is soft here.** The Hollows do not age—they **remember**, and sometimes they forget. A path that was open yesterday may today be sealed behind a sudden growth of thorns.  
- **Sound is substance.** To whisper is to risk summoning. To shout is to reshape the air.  
- **The water is alive.** It does not quench thirst—it speaks. Those who drink from the weeping roots hear voices in their dreams.  
- **Nothing is truly empty.** The spaces between things are not voids—they are thresholds.  

### **Atmosphere: A Whisper at the Edge of Reality**  
The light is **normal**, but it bends strangely, as if the air itself is slightly warped. Shadows cling too long; reflections move a second too late. The warmth is not oppressive, but it is **constant**, like the breath of something vast and patient.  

This is not a place for permanence. It is a place of **processes**, of cycles that do not end but spiral outward, folding reality into shapes that should not hold.  

To stand in the Singing Hollows is to stand inside a living riddle—one that hums, and shifts, and does not care if you solve it.
