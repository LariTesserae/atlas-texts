**The Sunken Atrium**  

A vast, submerged chamber, its vaulted ceiling lost in the shimmering haze of water above. The light here is bright but diffuse, refracted through layers of shifting currents, casting liquid gold across the smooth, ceramic walls. Everything is made—every surface, every contour—a deliberate construction now drowned, its purpose long forgotten.  

The floor is a mosaic of worn tiles, their patterns softened by time but still precise, geometric, speaking of hands that measured and placed them with care. The water is warm, almost body-temperature, carrying the faint metallic tang of ancient pipes still leaching minerals into the brine. Small air pockets cling to the upper edges of the chamber, trembling with each slow surge of the tides.  

Vegetation is sparse—just a few strands of dark algae swaying in the currents, clinging to the joints where wall meets floor. No fish dart through the water, no crustaceans scuttle across the tiles. The only movement is the water itself, restless but rhythmic, sloshing against the walls with a hollow, echoing sound.  

The space feels intimate despite its grandeur, a pocket of order in the endless blue. The edges fade into shadow, but the center is illuminated, as if the chamber were designed to draw the eye inward, to some focal point now lost to time. Perhaps there was once an altar here, or a mechanism, or simply an empty dais where someone once stood. Now, there is only water, and light, and the slow, patient erosion of centuries.  

Outside, beyond the walls, the ocean presses in—but here, in this sunken atrium, the world is still, suspended between drowning and remembrance.
