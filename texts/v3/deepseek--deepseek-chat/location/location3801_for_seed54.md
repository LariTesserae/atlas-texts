**The Weeping City of Gloam**  

A high plateau of black basalt rises from the mist, its edges weeping constant rivulets of water that never quite freeze, never quite flow freely—just seep in slow, glassy trails down the obsidian cliffs. The air is thick with condensation, a cold damp that clings to the skin like the breath of something vast and patient. The ground is slick, not with mud but with a mineral sheen, as if the stone itself is sweating.  

This is a place without plants. No roots, no leaves, no photosynthesis—only the slow exhalations of the earth. The city sprawls across the plateau, its structures not built but *grown*, extruded from the rock in fractal spires and bulbous domes, their surfaces etched with glyphs that shift when not observed. The architecture is dense, layered, pressing in on itself like cells in a hive, corridors winding in non-Euclidean spirals that defy the eye. The streets are narrow, not by design but by some deeper logic, as if the city is still forming, still deciding what shape it ought to take.  

The darkness here is not absence but presence—a living gloom that pools in the hollows, thick as liquid. Light is rare, flickering in brief arcs between towers, some form of energy discharge that leaves no heat, no spark, only afterimages burned into the retina. The sound is overwhelming: a low, resonant hum, the vibration of unseen machinery or perhaps the plateau itself, groaning under the weight of something too large to comprehend.  

And there is life here—not fauna as known elsewhere, but *movement*, entities of shifting density that flow through the streets like schools of fish in a black ocean. They are not creatures of flesh but of pattern, of interference, their edges blurring into the architecture as if they are part of the city’s slow metabolism. They do not notice you. Or perhaps they do, and you are too small to register.  

Time here is not linear but cumulative. The plateau is eroding, but so slowly that it might as well be eternal. The city is being assembled and disassembled at once, its structures melting back into the rock even as new ones push through, glistening and raw. The water keeps weeping. The hum never stops.  

This is a place that was never meant for you. But it tolerates your presence, for now.
