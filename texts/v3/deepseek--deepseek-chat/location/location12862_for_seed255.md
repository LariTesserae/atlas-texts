The landscape is a labyrinth of damp, echoing darkness, a place where the air itself feels weighted with moisture, though its source is elusive. The ground beneath is not earth but a mosaic of slick, polished stone tiles, interlocked with a precision that speaks of human hands, though the hands themselves are absent. The stones are cool to the touch, and their surfaces glisten faintly, as if perpetually wet with condensation. Water pools in shallow depressions, silent and still, reflecting nothing but the oppressive blackness above. Occasionally, a droplet falls from somewhere unseen, ringing out with a sharp, metallic *ping* that reverberates through the space, loud and solitary.

This is a place of construction, though not of buildings in the traditional sense. Massive, rough-hewn pillars rise from the ground, their surfaces jagged and unfinished, as though carved by tools barely capable of shaping the stone. They loom like sentinels in the dark, their tops lost in the void above. Between them, suspended in the air, are shimmering webs of cable—primitive yet intricate—woven from some dull, metallic fiber. They hum faintly, vibrating with an unseen energy that pulses irregularly, like a heartbeat. The sound is low and pervasive, a bass drone that mingles with the occasional *ping* of falling water to create an unsettling symphony.

There is no vegetation here, no green life to soften the harshness of the stone and metal. Instead, the air is thick with a faint, powdery mist—not water vapor, but something else, something that clings to the surfaces and dances in the weak currents of air. It smells of rust and old dust, and it coats the skin like a fine ash. Occasionally, the mist coalesces into fleeting shapes—patterns that seem almost geometric, spirals and tessellations that vanish as quickly as they form. These are not alive, but neither are they inert; they are traces of something older, something that prefigures life.

The fauna, if it can be called that, is sparse and strange. Small, bioluminescent creatures flit through the darkness, their forms indistinct and fluid. They glow faintly, pale blue or green, and their movements are erratic, as though driven by forces beyond their control. They seem neither predator nor prey, but observers, witnesses to the construction of this place. Their light is faint, barely enough to illuminate the outlines of the pillars and cables, but it is enough to suggest the scale of the space—open, vast, extending far beyond the immediate, yet dense with the interwoven structures of stone and metal.

The atmosphere is one of anticipation, as though the place itself is waiting for something to happen. The dampness, the darkness, the occasional bursts of sound—they all feel like precursors to an event still unfolding. Even the air seems alive with potential, charged with a strange, almost electric tension. The rules of this world are unclear, but one thing is certain: this is a place in motion, a place being shaped by forces both seen and unseen, a place where the boundaries between the made and the unmade are fluid and uncertain.
