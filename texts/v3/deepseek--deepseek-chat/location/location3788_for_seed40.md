**The Glassworks of the Shattered Sky**  

A high plateau stretches beneath a sky that is not quite right—its blue too thin, its clouds too geometric, like the stretched remnants of an old fresco. The air is cool, carrying the scent of damp metal and faint ozone, the lingering breath of unseen machines. Water does not fall here so much as it *condenses*—beads forming on the rusted ribs of towering structures, dripping in slow, irregular pulses onto surfaces that drink it greedily before it can pool.  

The land is young, its edges sharp. The plateau is not natural but *made*, a vast industrial foundation laid over something older, something that occasionally groans beneath the weight. The ground is a dense lattice of riveted plates, their seams weeping thin trails of rust where moisture gathers. In places, the metal has buckled, revealing glimpses of strata beneath—layers of ceramic, glass, and something darker, something that moves when not observed.  

Vegetation is sparse but stubborn. Lichen clings to the undersides of pipes, their surfaces a mottled gray-green. Occasionally, a twisted shrub forces its way through a crack in the plating, its leaves waxy and reflective, as if grown from discarded machine parts. The plants here do not sway; they shiver, reacting to currents in the air that nothing else feels.  

The structures dominate. They are not buildings so much as *organs*—great skeletal frameworks of steel and glass, their surfaces etched with patterns that shift when viewed from different angles. Some are hollow, their interiors lit by a sourceless glow, while others hum with unseen mechanisms. Walkways stretch between them, suspended on cables that sing faintly in the wind. There are no doors, only thresholds, and crossing one is less a matter of steps and more a matter of *acceptance*.  

The fauna is abundant but silent. Winged things flit between the structures—not birds, not insects, but something in between, their bodies segmented like old typewriter keys. They move in precise formations, never colliding, never straying from their paths. Larger shapes move in the shadows beneath the walkways, their forms indistinct but purposeful. They do not hunt. They *maintain*.  

Sound is rare, and when it comes, it is wrong—a distant grinding that stops before it can be located, a whisper that might be language or might be the wind through broken glass. The plateau does not echo. It absorbs.  

Time here is slow, measured not in hours but in the gradual accumulation of rust, the imperceptible sag of a beam, the slow crystallization of moisture into strange, geometric shapes on cold surfaces. Change happens, but it happens beneath notice, until suddenly, irrevocably, it doesn’t.  

And above it all, the sky is cracked. Not broken, not yet, but fissured—fine lines spiderwebbing across the dome of the world, through which something watches. Something that is not light, not dark, but the absence of both.  

This is a place that was built to last.  

It was not built for you.
