**The Shattered Teeth**  

The land is a jagged puzzle of obsidian spires and basalt columns, thrust upward by some ancient violence, their edges still sharp as if the earth had only just finished screaming. The air is cool and damp, clinging to the stone in a thin mist that never quite lifts, never quite falls as rain. Water seeps from cracks in the rock, pooling in shallow basins before vanishing again into the labyrinth below. The ground is uneven, treacherous—every step could be a stumble into a hidden crevasse, every ledge a knife’s edge.  

Life here is sparse but tenacious. Lichen clings in patches to the black stone, glowing faintly in the dimness, their bioluminescence casting eerie halos on the wet rock. Stunted, gnarled trees twist up from the rare pockets of soil, their roots gripping the stone like skeletal fingers. They bear no leaves, only thick, waxy needles that drip condensation into the silence.  

And yet, for all its barrenness, the place teems with movement. The fauna are overwhelming—not in numbers, but in presence. Huge, slow-moving things with too many legs pick their way across the cliffs, their chitinous shells clicking softly against the stone. Blind, pale creatures slither through the fissures, their bodies segmented and glistening. Above, winged things with leathery membranes coast on the updrafts between the spires, their calls echoing in the hollows.  

The sound is constant but diffuse—a low hum of wind through narrow passages, the distant drip of water, the occasional shudder of rock settling. It is a land that breathes slowly, shifts imperceptibly. Time here is measured in erosion, in the gradual rounding of edges that have not yet had the chance to soften.  

And strangest of all—sometimes, in the deepest dark, the stone itself seems to whisper.
