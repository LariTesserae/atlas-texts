The landscape is an ancient, hollow city carved into the heart of a vast, subterranean plant-mass. The air is cool, still, and heavy with the scent of damp cellulose, a faint fungal musk lingering at the edges of perception. There is no water here, not in the traditional sense—no rivers, no lakes, no rain. Instead, the ecosystem relies on a slow, ancient exchange of moisture absorbed through the fibrous walls of the plant-matter that surrounds everything. The city itself is built entirely from this plant material—walls, floors, and ceilings are all formed from cured, hardened fronds and stalks, woven together with a primitive craftsmanship that speaks of centuries of trial and error. The architecture is low, the ceilings pressing down as if the city has been slowly sinking under its own weight over millennia. 

The darkness is absolute, save for the faint bioluminescent glow emitted by patches of moss-like growths clinging to the walls. The light is soft, greenish-blue, and seems to pulse faintly, as if breathing. The soundscape is oddly loud—a constant, low hum that reverberates through the chambers, a deep resonance that seems to emanate from the plant-mass itself. Occasionally, there is a creaking, groaning noise, as if the city is adjusting to some imperceptible shift in its structure.

The plant-life here is total, all-encompassing. It fills every corner, every niche, every void. The streets—or rather, corridors—are narrow and winding, lined with towering, fibrous pillars that seem to support the ceiling, though their purpose is unclear. The walls are thick with layers of vines, lichens, and other growths, some soft and spongy, others tough and leathery. There are no animals here, no birds, no insects—only the plants, slowly growing, decaying, and regrowing in an endless cycle that has long since stabilized into a state of near-perfect equilibrium. 

The city feels empty, not in the sense of abandonment, but in the sense of absence. There are no signs of life, human or otherwise, save for the occasional tool or artifact—simple implements carved from plant material, left on benches or shelves as if forgotten. The silence is profound, though it is not truly silence—it is the absence of voices, of footsteps, of anything that might suggest a living presence. The dynamism here is slow, almost imperceptible—the gradual sag of a wall, the creeping spread of a vine, the faint crumbling of a ceiling beam. 

The strange-ness of this place lies in its contradiction—it is both urban and empty, ancient yet alive, loud yet silent. It feels like a city that was never meant to be inhabited, built by hands that left no trace, existing in a state of equilibrium that borders on stasis. The plant-mass around it feels almost sentient, as if it is aware of the city within its womb, tolerating its intrusion but never truly embracing it. The air is thick with the weight of time, with the knowledge that this place has existed for eons, and will likely exist for eons more, unchanged, unmoved, and utterly alone.
