**The Ashen Grove**  

A low basin of cracked earth stretches beneath a sky choked with perpetual twilight. The air is warm, thick with the scent of powdered stone and something faintly metallic, like rust or old blood. No wind stirs here, no rain has ever fallen—only the slow, patient breath of the land itself, exhaling dust in minute sighs.  

The ground is a mosaic of fissures, deep enough to swallow a hand but no deeper, as if the earth had once tried to split open and then thought better of it. Between the cracks, patches of bone-white lichen cling to the surface, their tendrils creeping in fractal spirals. These are the only vegetation, if they can be called that—pale, slow-growing things that thrive on minerals leached from the stone. They emit no scent, make no sound, and seem to pulse faintly in the dim light, though perhaps that is only a trick of the eyes.  

There is no water. No rivers, no pools, not even the memory of dampness. The basin exists in perfect aridity, its dryness so complete that the air itself feels brittle. Yet the land does not thirst; it has settled into this state as if it were always meant to be. The cracks in the earth are not wounds—they are simply how the ground breathes.  

The scale is intimate, a place no larger than a forgotten courtyard. Its boundaries are marked by low ridges of fused stone, smooth and black as if once molten, though no heat lingers in them now. Beyond, the land fades into deeper shadow, featureless and uninviting. Within the grove, space is sparse—each lichen patch, each fracture, exists in isolation, with wide stretches of bare ground between them.  

No animals stir here. No insects hum, no unseen creatures scuttle beneath the rocks. The silence is absolute, save for the occasional whisper of settling dust. Even the lichen grows without sound.  

This is a place that does not change. The cracks do not widen, the lichen does not spread quickly, the air does not shift. Time here is a slow, unmeasured thing, and the grove has no need for it. It simply *is*, as it has been, as it will be.  

A human standing here would feel the weight of that stillness, the pressure of a world that has no need for them. There are no tools left behind, no marks of civilization—only the grove, patient and indifferent, waiting for nothing at all.
