**The Amber Hollow**  

A sunken city of baked clay and bone-dry vines, nestled in the belly of a vast, crumbling crater. The air is thick and sluggish, heavy with the scent of hot stone and the faint, resinous perfume of the vegetation that claws its way up every wall. The crater’s rim looms high, a jagged silhouette against a sky choked with ochre dust, filtering the light into a perpetual, honeyed dusk.  

This is a place of absence. No rivers, no wells, no dampness at all—just the slow, granular whisper of wind over parched earth. The city itself is a labyrinth of low, domed structures, their walls fused together by time and pressure, their surfaces etched with the ghostly imprints of hands and tools long forgotten. The streets are narrow, winding, barely wide enough for two to pass, and they coil inward like the spirals of a fossilized shell, leading always toward the center—where something vast and hollow hums beneath the earth.  

The plants here are lush, but strange. They do not grow from soil, but from the walls themselves, their roots splitting the clay like veins of green lightning. Their leaves are thick, waxy, almost translucent, holding within them a faint, bioluminescent glow that pulses in time with the wind. Some whisper when touched, releasing clouds of golden pollen that hang in the air like suspended fireflies.  

There is no animal life. No birds, no insects, no scuttling things in the shadows—only the plants, and the occasional shudder of the ground as the hollow beneath exhales. The sound is low, resonant, felt more than heard, a vibration that lingers in the bones.  

The people here—if they can be called that—are few. They move in silence, wrapped in layers of sun-bleached cloth, their faces obscured by masks of woven fiber and polished stone. They do not speak. Instead, they press their palms to the walls, to the ground, as if listening. Their tools are simple: chisels of flint, ropes of braided vine, clay vessels that never hold water. They build nothing new. They only tend.  

At night, the city breathes. The vines tighten, the walls shift, the hollow beneath groans. The masked ones retreat into the deepest chambers, where the air is cool and the sound is muffled. By dawn, the streets are different. A doorway sealed. A path rerouted. A new crack in the clay, exhaling the scent of something old and deep and waiting.  

This is a place of patience. A place where the land is still deciding what it wants to be.
