### **The Damp High City of Veth-Ruun**  

At the edge of a vast, mist-laden plateau, the city of Veth-Ruun rises from the damp stone like a dream half-remembered. The air is cool, thick with the scent of wet moss and the faint metallic tang of condensation on ancient rock. The land here is young—freshly carved by unseen hands, its cliffs still sharp, its ravines unsoftened by time.  

The plateau stretches wide, an open expanse where the horizon is always just beyond reach. The ground is uneven, scattered with shallow pools that catch the sky’s reflection when the sun breaks through the low-hanging clouds. The water is never still for long—sometimes it seeps into the porous stone, sometimes it gathers in slow-moving rivulets that vanish into unseen cracks. The land drinks when it is thirsty, exhales when it is full.  

Veth-Ruun itself is a city of layered stone, its buildings carved directly into the cliffs or stacked in precarious tiers along the plateau’s edge. The architecture is primitive but deliberate—rough-hewn blocks fitted together without mortar, arches that seem to grow from the rock itself. There are no grand towers, only low, sturdy structures that huddle close to the earth. The streets are narrow, winding, following the natural contours of the stone rather than any human plan.  

The vegetation is lush but sparse—patches of deep green moss, clusters of wiry shrubs with broad, waxy leaves, and the occasional twisted tree clinging to the rock with roots like fingers. There is life here, but it does not crowd. The fauna are abundant but quiet—small, quick creatures with dark fur or iridescent scales, flitting between shadows. Birds with muted calls nest in the crevices, their songs soft, almost hesitant.  

The city is not silent, but neither is it loud. The wind hums through the gaps in the stone. Water drips. Footsteps echo faintly, then fade. The people of Veth-Ruun move with a slow certainty, their tools simple—stone blades, woven nets, clay vessels. They do not hurry. Time here is a slow river, and they are content to drift with it.  

There is something unusual about this place. Perhaps it is the way the mist clings just a little too long, or how the light bends strangely when it passes through the hanging moisture. Perhaps it is the way the city feels both ancient and newborn, as if it has always been here, yet is still deciding what it will become.  

The plateau stretches on, unchanging, unyielding. The damp air settles. The city breathes. And the world holds its breath.
