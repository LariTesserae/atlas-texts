**The Glass Wastes of Vorthis**  

Beneath a sky of perpetual dusk, the land stretches—a vast, undulating plain of black glass, smoothed by eons into a rippling obsidian sea. No water has ever touched this place, nor has any plant taken root. The air is warm, thick with the scent of ozone and the faint metallic tang of distant industry. The horizon shimmers, not with heat, but with the slow, almost imperceptible refraction of light through the glassy expanse.  

This is a land of silence. The wind, when it comes, does not howl but whispers across the polished surface, a sound like fingers dragging over crystal. Footsteps, if there were any to be heard, would ring hollow, absorbed by the dense, ancient material beneath. The only true sounds are the distant hum of machinery—somewhere beyond the curve of the land—and the occasional, unsettling *ping* of the glass settling, a noise like a struck tuning fork resonating through the earth.  

The city rises in the distance, a jagged silhouette against the dim sky. Its structures are angular, built from the same black glass as the land, fused and reforged into towers, bridges, and domes. No light spills from its windows; the inhabitants, if there are any, do not require it. The streets are empty, the air still. The city does not decay, nor does it grow. It simply *is*, a monument to some forgotten purpose, its machinery grinding on in perpetual, silent industry.  

Life here is scarce—not absent, but rare. Things move in the corners of vision, quick and low to the ground, their forms indistinct. Perhaps they are creatures of chitin and bone, adapted to the glass, or perhaps they are something else entirely, things that do not belong to any known taxonomy. They do not disturb the silence.  

The world feels old. Not dying, not decaying, but long past the point of change. The glass beneath your feet has been here for millennia, perhaps longer. It remembers nothing, reflects nothing. It is the end result of some vast, unfathomable process, a landscape worn down to its barest essence.  

And yet, there is strangeness here. Sometimes, the glass shifts—not from movement, but from some deeper, slower adjustment, as if the land itself is breathing. Sometimes, the distant hum of the city changes pitch, just for a moment, before settling back into its eternal rhythm.  

This is a place where time has stopped. Or perhaps it never began.
