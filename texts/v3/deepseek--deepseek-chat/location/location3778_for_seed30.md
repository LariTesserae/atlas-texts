**The Weeping Plateau**  

A high, warm marshland stretches under a dim sky, its air thick with condensation that clings to everything—skin, stone, the sparse, wiry vegetation that clumps in the sodden earth. The ground is soft, yielding, not quite liquid but never fully solid, as if the plateau itself is caught between forming and dissolving. Water seeps from the rocks, slow tears rolling down their faces, carving faint, embryonic channels that vanish and reappear with each passing hour. The land is young here, raw, still deciding what it wants to be.  

The few structures scattered across the plateau are strange—low, smooth domes of some material that shifts slightly under touch, neither metal nor stone, humming faintly with an energy that makes the teeth ache. They are remnants, or perhaps precursors, of a technology that defies understanding. Some are cracked open, spilling out geometries that twist the eye, while others sit whole but empty, waiting.  

Life thrives here, though it is not always visible. The air thrums with the calls of unseen creatures—deep, resonant pulses that shudder through the ground, answered by higher, skittering clicks from the reeds. Occasionally, something large moves just beneath the surface of the marsh, sending ripples through the standing water. The fauna are abundant but fleeting, shadows darting at the edge of vision, shapes that might be limbs or wings or something else entirely.  

Sound is everywhere—the drip of water, the groan of shifting earth, the distant, rhythmic thrumming of the domes. The plateau is never silent, never still. It breathes, shifts, decays and rebuilds itself in slow, ceaseless motion.  

The light is dim, filtered through a haze that never quite lifts, casting everything in a muted, silvered glow. At times, the mist thickens, swallowing the horizon, and the plateau feels small, intimate, a single room in an endless house. Then it clears, and the land stretches again, vast and lonely.  

This is a place of becoming. The water is patient. The stones are learning their shapes. The domes hum with forgotten purpose. And beneath it all, something stirs, restless and half-formed.
