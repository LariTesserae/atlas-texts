**The Glass Wastes of Hissing Silence**  

A vast, shattered plain stretches beneath a bruised sky, its horizon lost in the churn of slow-moving dust storms. The ground is not rock, not sand, but something between—sheets of volcanic glass, fractured into jagged panes that rise like the teeth of a broken comb. Some are black as obsidian, others streaked with veins of sulfur-yellow and iron-red, catching the dim light in dull, greasy reflections. The air is warm, thick with the scent of ozone and powdered silica, and though there is no water, the glass sometimes weeps thin trails of mineral residue, leaving ghostly white tracks that evaporate before they can pool.  

This is a land without life, but not without motion. The glass shifts constantly, not from wind or erosion, but from some deeper instability—perhaps the slow heave of the planet’s crust, perhaps something stranger. Sections of the plain buckle without warning, sending up sprays of razor-edged fragments that chime faintly as they settle. Elsewhere, new fractures spiderweb outward, splitting the ground with a sound like ice cracking underfoot. The glass does not break cleanly; it splinters into shapes that seem almost deliberate, as if the land itself is trying to form something and failing.  

There are structures here, though none built by hands. Towers of fused glass rise at odd intervals, their surfaces rippled and warped as though they were once molten and then frozen mid-flow. Some lean precariously, their bases half-swallowed by the shifting ground. Others stand straight, their peaks lost in the haze. They are not ruins—they are still forming, still changing, growing taller or collapsing in slow, grinding cascades.  

The sky is a dull purple, streaked with darker bands where the atmosphere churns. There is no sun, only a diffuse glow that seeps from no particular direction, casting long, uncertain shadows. Sometimes, a flicker of light passes overhead—not a star, not a meteor, but something shapeless and quick, gone before the eye can fix on it. The silence is not absolute. There is the hiss of settling glass, the groan of subterranean pressure, the occasional distant *pop* of a fracture propagating through the plain. But these sounds only emphasize the absence of anything living.  

This is a place of raw geology, a world still deciding what it wants to be. The rules here are not fixed. Gravity feels unreliable in patches; some of the glass shards float inches above the ground, vibrating faintly as if caught in an unseen current. Time, too, seems uneven—some fractures spread in seconds, others take hours to creep across a single pane. There is no death here, because there was never life. Only formation and reformation, endless and directionless.  

If you stood here long enough, you might start to see patterns in the glass—faces, landscapes, architectures that dissolve as soon as you focus on them. The land is dreaming, but it does not dream of anything that exists. Or perhaps it dreams of everything at once, and this is the closest it can come to coherence.  

You could walk forever in this place and never find an edge. But you would not walk forever. The ground would swallow you eventually, or the glass would rise up in some new configuration, and you would become just another fleeting shape in its slow, indifferent dance.
