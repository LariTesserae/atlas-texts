**The Chamber of Hungry Echoes**  

Beneath the skin of the world, in a cavern carved by no water, lies a place of dry warmth and murmuring industry. The air is thick with the scent of hot metal and something older, something mineral and sharp, like rusted iron filings pressed into stone. The walls are smooth, not from erosion but from some forgotten process—perhaps machined, perhaps grown—their surfaces striated with faint, rhythmic grooves, as if the rock itself had been turned on a lathe.  

The cavern is small, no larger than a cathedral, but dense with presence. Strange, spindly growths—neither plant nor fungus—cling to the walls in sparse clusters, their fibrous bodies glowing faintly in the dim, amber light cast by phosphorescent nodules embedded in the ceiling. They pulse irregularly, like slow, tired hearts. The floor is uneven, littered with jagged outcroppings of dark basalt and the scattered remnants of machinery: a gear half-buried in dust, a skeletal frame of rusted struts, the hollow shell of some cylindrical vessel, its purpose long lost.  

Sound dominates this place. A deep, resonant hum thrums through the stone, the vibration of unseen engines buried deeper still. It is joined by the skittering of countless chitinous limbs—small, many-legged things scuttling in the shadows, their bodies armored and segmented, their movements quick and precise. They are everywhere, just beyond the edges of vision, their clicking and scraping layering into a constant, restless chorus. Occasionally, something larger shifts in the dark, a slow, heavy motion that sends a ripple of disturbance through the unseen swarm.  

The air does not stir. There is no wind, no draft—only the dry, unmoving heat, pressing against the skin like a weight. The machines, if they are machines, do not change. The hum never falters. The creatures do not age. Time here is a locked wheel, turning without progress.  

And yet, something watches. Something vast and patient, coiled in the deeper dark, where the light does not reach. You can feel it in the way the echoes distort, in the way the hum sometimes deepens, as if in answer to a thought you did not speak aloud.  

This is a place that does not want you. But it will not ask you to leave.  

It will wait.
