**The Submerged Foundry**  

Beneath the surface of a vast, black ocean, the world is a hollow cathedral of industry—an endless lattice of iron and glass, stretching into the abyss. The water here is not natural; it is thick, slow-moving, threaded with currents of coolant and runoff, a slurry of oil-slicked brine that never quite settles. The pressure is immense, but the architecture holds, groaning under the weight of leagues of water above.  

**Terrain:** The floor is a grid of rusted platforms, each one a city-block wide, suspended by chains thicker than ancient trees. Below, the true bottom is unseen, lost in the murk. The structures rise like the ribs of some colossal machine, their surfaces pitted with barnacles and corrosion, their edges sharp where time has not yet softened them. Tunnels yawn open—maintenance shafts, transport conduits, access points for things that no longer move.  

**Materials:** Everything is metal, glass, ceramic—nothing organic, nothing that grew. The walls are riveted steel, the walkways grated mesh, the supports cross-braced like the skeleton of a sunken dreadnought. Some surfaces still gleam where automated cleaners pass; others are furred with strange, synthetic algae, feeding on the chemical seepage.  

**Atmosphere:** The water is cold, but not lifeless. Heat bleeds from vents, from the humming cores of forgotten reactors, creating pockets of warmth where the currents twist. The light comes from phosphorescent strips, from flickering bulbs behind reinforced glass, casting a sickly yellow-green glow that does not so much illuminate as it does define the edges of the dark.  

**Sounds:** The deep hum of machinery, the groan of metal under stress, the distant thrum of turbines still turning somewhere in the abyss. Occasionally, a sharp *clang* echoes through the structure—something shifting, something collapsing. The water carries sound strangely; sometimes it is muffled, swallowed by the void, and other times it rings clear as a bell.  

**Ecology:** The fauna here are scavengers, opportunists—sleek, armored things with too many joints, skittering along the girders. Some are mechanical, jury-rigged, their bodies patched together from spare parts. Others are flesh, but strange, adapted to the pressure, the chemicals, the absence of sun. They hunt in the shadows, darting between the struts, vanishing into maintenance shafts.  

**Rules of the World:**  
- **Nothing Rots.** There is no decay, only erosion, only the slow grind of metal on metal. What dies is either consumed or preserved, pickled in the briny deep.  
- **The Foundry Breathes.** The currents shift in cycles, pulling in cold water from the depths, expelling warmed effluent in great, slow exhalations.  
- **Light is Currency.** The glow-strips fade with time. Those who control them control movement, safety, territory.  
- **The Machines Remember.** Somewhere in the dark, something is still running, still following old protocols. It does not care that the world above is gone.  

**The Feeling:** This is not a place that was drowned. It was *built* underwater, designed to endure the crush of the abyss. It was always meant to be here. And now, left to its own devices, it has begun to adapt—not to fail, but to change, to become something else entirely.  

The Submerged Foundry does not sleep. It waits.
