**The Chamber of the Last Breath**  

A vast, sealed vault of black basalt, its walls fused smooth by forgotten heat, stretches upward into a ceiling lost in dimness. The air is warm, thick with the weight of ages, unmoving—no breeze has ever stirred here. The floor is a mosaic of interlocking stone plates, each carved with spiraling grooves that converge toward the chamber’s center, where a single raised dais stands.  

The sound is overwhelming—not a roar, not a hum, but a deep, resonant drone, as if the stone itself vibrates with some buried frequency. It presses against the ears, against the skin, a physical presence as dense as the walls. There is no echo; the sound swallows itself, endless and unchanging.  

No water has ever touched this place. No life, no decay. The walls are unweathered, unmarked, though they must be ancient beyond reckoning. Time has not worn them down—only settled into them, like a weight pressed into the bones of the earth. The chamber is small enough to feel enclosed, yet large enough to dwarf any who enter, its dimensions warped by the sheer density of its construction. Every inch is deliberate, every angle precise.  

The light comes from nowhere and everywhere—a dim, sourceless glow that clings to the stone, just enough to see by, never shifting, never brightening or fading. Shadows do not move here. They cling, static, to the edges of things.  

On the dais rests an object—a fist-sized sphere of dark, glassy material, its surface etched with fine, branching lines. It does not gleam. It does not hum. It simply *is*, as much a part of the chamber as the air, the sound, the weight of time.  

This is a place where nothing changes. Where nothing *can* change. A machine? A tomb? A reliquary for something beyond naming? The chamber does not answer. It only waits, sound filling its throat like a held breath that will never be released.
