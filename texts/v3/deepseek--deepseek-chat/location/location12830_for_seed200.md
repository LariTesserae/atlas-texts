**The Weeping Expanse**  

A vast, lowland marsh stretches out under a heavy, dim sky—a place where the air itself seems to weep. The horizon is a blur of mist and moisture, the land barely distinguishable from the water that saturates it. The ground is not quite solid, not quite liquid—a primordial ooze of silt and tangled roots, trembling underfoot as if the earth is still deciding what shape to take.  

The water here does not flow so much as it *lingers.* It beads on broad leaves, drips from the gnarled limbs of half-submerged trees, and seeps up through the ground in slow, syrupy surges. The air is thick with the scent of wet decay and fecund growth, a hot, cloying breath that clings to the skin. The heat is oppressive, not the dry blaze of a desert but the suffocating damp of a world still learning to breathe.  

Vegetation crowds in on itself—lush, hungry, overwhelming. Towering reeds with feathery crowns sway in a wind that never quite arrives. Vines slither over one another in slow competition, their leaves broad and waxy, glistening with condensation. Strange bulbous growths burst from the muck, their surfaces slick and veined, pulsing faintly as if they harbor something alive inside.  

Life teems in the wet gloom. Insects drone in thick, shifting clouds, their wings catching what little light filters through the mist. Amphibious things croak and trill from hidden hollows, their calls overlapping into a ceaseless, droning chorus. Larger shapes move just beyond sight—sinuous bodies sliding through the water, the occasional ripple betraying something vast and patient beneath the surface.  

There are no structures here, no marks of hands or tools. The land is raw, unshaped, caught between formation and dissolution. The rocks weep mineral tears, their surfaces soft and crumbling, as if they might melt back into the earth at any moment. The very ground seems to shift when unobserved, settling into new configurations overnight, as though the marsh is dreaming itself into existence.  

The sound is constant—a wet, living hum of frogs and dripping water, the creak of straining vegetation, the occasional distant splash of something heavy entering the water. Yet for all its noise, the marsh feels static, suspended. Time does not pass here so much as it pools, thick and slow, like the water that defines this place.  

This is a world on the cusp of becoming, a landscape that has not yet decided what it wants to be. It is neither young nor old—it simply *is*, waiting in the damp and the dark, breathing in the heat, dreaming in the mist.
