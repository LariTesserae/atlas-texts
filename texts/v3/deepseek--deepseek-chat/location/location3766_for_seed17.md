**The Endless Foundry**  

The world is a vast, submerged forge—an ocean of molten metal held in perpetual suspension, neither cooling nor flowing, its surface rippling with the slow, unseen currents of unseen forces. Above, the sky is a ceiling of black iron, pocked with ancient rivets the size of cities, their heads crusted with the residue of forgotten smeltings. The air is thick with the smell of scorched ozone and the metallic tang of rust.  

You stand on a high plateau—one of many—jutting from the liquid steel like the last teeth of a drowned continent. The ground beneath your feet is not rock but a dense lattice of slag, brittle and porous, formed in the moments when the foundry briefly stilled, eons ago. There are no plants here, no organic life at all—only the occasional fungal growth, black and vein-like, creeping along the edges of the plateau, feeding on whatever trace elements the metal rejects.  

The sound is deafening, a ceaseless roar of industry: the groan of distant pistons, the hiss of escaping steam from fissures in the iron sky, the deep, resonant hum of unseen machines laboring beneath the metal sea. The noise is so constant it becomes a kind of silence, pressing against your skull like a second atmosphere.  

Structures rise from the plateau—spindly towers of riveted steel, their surfaces pitted by the corrosive mist. Some are intact, their windows dark; others have collapsed into twisted skeletons, their girders splayed like the ribs of long-dead giants. They are not ruins, exactly. They were never meant to last. They were built for a purpose, and that purpose has either been fulfilled or forgotten.  

The fauna here are not creatures of flesh but of moving metal—crab-like constructs skittering across the slag, their bodies assembled from scrap and piston-joints, their movements erratic, as if driven by some half-remembered directive. Larger things glide beneath the surface of the molten sea, their silhouettes distorted by the heat haze, their presence marked only by the occasional ripple.  

Time does not pass here. The foundry does not change. The metal does not cool. The machines do not stop. The world is locked in an eternal moment of industry, a single breath held for millennia.  

You are not supposed to be here.  

But the foundry does not care.
