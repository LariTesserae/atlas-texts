**The Glass Steppes of Eternity**  

The world is a vast, shattered plain of black volcanic glass, stretching uninterrupted to the horizon under a dim, bruised sky. The ground is smooth but fractured, a mosaic of obsidian shards fused together by time, their edges worn down by an erosion so slow it feels like stillness. There is no water here—no rivers, no lakes, not even the memory of moisture trapped in stone. The air is cool, thin, pressing against the skin like the breath of a sleeping giant.  

At first glance, the land appears empty, a void of polished darkness. But as you stand there, the silence begins to unspool into something stranger. The glass steppes are not lifeless. They are *waiting*.  

Here and there, towering spires rise from the plain—slender, jagged monoliths of the same black glass, some as tall as ancient trees, others needle-thin and precarious. They hum faintly when the wind passes through them, a sound like distant chimes heard through thick wool. The wind itself is rare, a visitor from some other place, stirring the dust of crushed obsidian into brief, whispering spirals before settling again.  

There is movement, though not life as we know it. Shadows ripple across the glass, not cast by any visible light, but emerging from the fractures beneath the surface. They shift like liquid, pooling and receding in slow, deliberate patterns. Sometimes, they take shapes—vague outlines of things that might have been, or might yet be. A flicker of wings. The suggestion of a spine. Then gone.  

Far in the distance, something that might be a city rises—not built, but *grown*, a cluster of glass towers fused together in impossible geometries. Their surfaces ripple with the same half-seen shadows, as if the structures themselves are dreaming. No roads lead there. No paths are worn into the glass. The city simply exists, a mirage that never fades.  

Occasionally, something moves across the steppes. Not an animal, not a machine, but a disturbance in the air, a warping of light that suggests presence. It leaves no footprints. The glass does not remember its passing.  

The sky is neither day nor night, but an eternal twilight, streaked with faint auroras that pulse like distant heartbeats. There are no stars. No sun. Only the dim glow of something unseen, filtering through the haze.  

This is a place outside time, outside the rules of substance. The ground is solid, yet it feels as though one wrong step could send you falling through the cracks in reality. The air is still, yet it thrums with the weight of unseen forces. The world is empty, yet it watches.  

And it is very, very old.
