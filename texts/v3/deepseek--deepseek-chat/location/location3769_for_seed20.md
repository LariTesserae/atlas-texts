**The Drowned Peaks**  

The world is a jagged spine of black rock thrusting up from an endless ocean, its peaks just high enough to escape the churning waters. The sea is not still—it heaves and crashes against the cliffs in great, frothing waves, sending up plumes of salt spray that hang in the thick air like ghosts. The sound is deafening, a roar that never ceases, the water’s voice swallowing all other noise.  

The land itself is young, raw, its edges sharp where the waves have not yet worn them smooth. The stone is volcanic, glassy in places, fractured in others, still warm from some ancient upheaval. Pools of rainwater collect in hollows, steaming faintly in the humid air. The warmth clings to everything, a damp, oppressive heat that makes the air shimmer over the rocks.  

Vegetation is sparse—only the hardiest lichens cling to the stone, their pale green and gray forms spreading in slow, patient patches. Occasionally, a twisted shrub or a clump of wiry grass takes root in a sheltered crevice, but most of the land is bare, scoured by wind and water.  

Life is rare here. A few seabirds wheel overhead, their cries lost in the thunder of the waves. Sometimes, something larger moves beneath the water—shadows in the deep, shapes that might be fish or might be something else entirely. The ocean is alive in ways that defy understanding, its currents shifting unpredictably, its surface sometimes breaking with unnatural ripples, as if something vast and unseen is stirring just below.  

The sky is a bruised, heavy thing, thick with clouds that never quite part. The light is dim, filtered through the haze, casting everything in a muted gray-blue. Storms roll in without warning, the rain coming in sheets so dense they blur the line between sea and sky.  

And then there are the moments when the world itself seems to shudder. The rocks shift underfoot, not with the slow grind of erosion, but with sudden, impossible lurches. The water rises in unnatural swells, defying the wind. Strange shapes flicker at the edges of vision—structures that weren’t there before, half-formed and crumbling, or perhaps they were always there, and you just failed to notice. The air hums with something unseen, a pressure that builds until it feels like the world might tear apart at any moment.  

This is a place caught between creation and dissolution, where the rules of reality are thin and fraying. The sea could swallow it all tomorrow. Or perhaps it already has, and you are walking through the echo of what once was.
