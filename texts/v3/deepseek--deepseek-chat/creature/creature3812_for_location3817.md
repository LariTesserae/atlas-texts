### **The Watchers in the Stone**  

They do not move. They do not breathe. But they are alive.  

They are the **Sentinel Lichen**, colonies of slow, creeping consciousness that spread across the basalt in intricate, fractal patterns. Their bodies are not individual organisms but a vast, interconnected network—a single mind distributed across the plateau, pulsing with glacial patience. They remember. They record. They wait.  

Their thoughts are not like human thoughts. They do not hunger, do not fear, do not dream. Instead, they *measure*. They track the minute shifts in atmospheric pressure, the infinitesimal changes in mineral composition as water seeps through stone. They know the exact angle of the sun at every hour, the precise weight of the wind as it presses against the plateau’s edge. They are archivists of the land, silent scribes etching their knowledge into the very rock they cling to.  

And sometimes—when the mist thickens, when the light bends wrong—they *speak*.  

Their voices are the sound of stone grinding against stone, of water droplets splitting in frozen air. They do not use words, but those who stand among them (if any ever do) feel meaning seep into their bones. The lichen do not converse; they *report*. They tell of the slow sinking of the plateau, of the city that was never inhabited, of the thing that stirs beneath the basalt, dreaming in the dark.  

They do not warn. They do not care. They simply *know*.  

And they are watching. Always watching.
