In this vertical, mist-shrouded world, the beings who thrive are as layered and contradictory as the landscape itself. They are the **Cliffborn**, a people adapted to the damp, steep, and ever-shifting terrain of the mountain. Their bodies are lean and compact, built for navigating narrow ledges and crevices with ease. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, with a faint iridescence that shimmers in the muted light—a trait evolved from generations spent under the mountain’s perpetual cloud cover. Their hands and feet are broad and nimble, with fingers and toes that grip like those of a gecko, allowing them to cling to the slick rock faces with ease. They have keen, almond-shaped eyes that reflect the eerie glow of the mist, adapted to see in the dim, diffused light. 

The Cliffborn are a quiet people, their voices soft and melodic, attuned to the ambient hum of the landscape. They communicate not just with words but with subtle gestures and the tap-tap-tapping of fingers on stone—a language as intricate as the terrain they inhabit. Their clothing is woven from the fibrous mosses and vines that cling to the mountain, dyed in earthy greens and muted silvers, blending seamlessly with their surroundings. They wear simple adornments: polished stones from the pools below, or fragments of rusted metal scavenged from the ancient structures.

Their society is communal yet deeply private, organized into small, scattered groups called **Nests**. Each Nest is centered around one of the weathered stone huts or wooden platforms, which they’ve adapted into homes and workshops. The Cliffborn are master craftsmen, shaping tools and artifacts from the materials the mountain provides: stone, iron, and the rare, gleaming crystals that form in the mineral-rich pools. Their craftsmanship is utilitarian yet beautiful, marked by intricate carvings that echo the patterns of the moss and the veins of the rock.

The Cliffborn maintain a symbiotic relationship with the mountain. They harvest mosses and fungi for food and medicine, and they guide the flow of water through cleverly engineered channels carved into the rock. They revere the machinery embedded within the mountain, calling it the **Heartbeat**, and believe it to be the source of the mountain’s life. They tend to it with care, coaxing it to groan and hum in rhythms that resonate through the entire landscape. 

Though they are few in number, the Cliffborn are not alone in this world. There are creatures here, too—small, elusive, and adapted to the dense, humid environment. **Mistwings** are bird-like creatures with iridescent feathers that blend into the fog, their calls echoing eerily off the cliffs. **Lurkroots** are amphibious beings that dwell in the shallow pools, their frog-like bodies camouflaged by mottled green skin. And then there are the **Stonecreepers**, serpentine creatures with skin the texture of moss-covered rock, their movements slow and deliberate, almost imperceptible against the cliffs.

The Cliffborn’s connection to the mountain is spiritual as much as it is physical. They believe the mountain is alive, a sentient being still in the process of shaping itself. They see the occasional rockfalls and shifting slopes as the mountain’s subtle movements, its way of communicating with them. Their rituals involve offerings of crystal and metal, placed at the base of dripping waterfalls or in the crevices where the Heartbeat’s groans are loudest. 

Time flows differently for the Cliffborn. They measure it not in days or years but in the growth of moss, the slow filling of mineral basins, and the rare, dramatic shifts of the mountain’s terrain. They are patient, their lives governed by the rhythms of their world. Yet they are also aware of impermanence—the mountain is alive, and it changes, and so must they.

This is a place of harmony and tension, where the Cliffborn live as part of the mountain’s evolving story, their presence as fleeting and enduring as the mist that cloaks their vertical world.
