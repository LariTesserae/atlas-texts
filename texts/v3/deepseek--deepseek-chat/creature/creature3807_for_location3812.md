**The Glass Marsh of the Weeping Towers** is a place of eerie, liquid stillness—a landscape caught between decay and perpetual motion.  

The marsh itself is a vast, shallow basin of **black volcanic glass**, its surface fractured into countless shards, smoothed by time into treacherous, reflective planes. Water seeps up from below, pooling in jagged hollows, turning the glass slick and iridescent with oily rainbows. The ground is not solid—it shifts slightly underfoot, as if floating on something deeper, something that breathes in slow, tectonic sighs.  

The **Weeping Towers** rise from this glassy expanse like the ribs of a long-dead leviathan. They are not built, but **grown**—twisted spires of fused obsidian and rusted iron, their surfaces weeping constant rivulets of water. The liquid collects in basins at their bases before seeping back into the marsh, completing an endless, silent cycle. Some towers lean precariously, their foundations eroded by the ceaseless moisture; others stand straight, their peaks lost in the ever-present mist.  

Between them, **bridges of corroded metal** stretch like spiderwebs, their surfaces pitted and streaked with mineral deposits. They creak underfoot, but never quite break—some unseen force holds them together, just as it holds the towers upright.  

The **fauna** here are not creatures of flesh, but of **liquid crystal and molten metal**. They move in swarms, their bodies refracting light as they skitter across the glass or slither through hidden channels beneath the marsh. Some are small, quick things that dart between the towers’ shadows; others are slow, ponderous shapes that ripple just beneath the surface of the water, their forms indistinct and ever-shifting.  

The air is thick with **humidity and the scent of ozone**, as if a storm is forever brewing but never breaks. The soundscape is a layered murmur—**dripping water, the groan of settling metal, the distant thrum of buried machinery**. There are no voices, no cries, only the marsh’s own slow, rhythmic pulse.  

Time here is **measured in erosion**, in the gradual wearing-down of glass and the slow collapse of towers. The marsh does not hurry. It does not need to. It has existed like this for centuries, perhaps longer, and it will continue long after any visitor has turned to dust.  

This is a place of **industry without purpose**, of motion without progress. It does not sleep. It does not wake. It simply **is**—waiting, though for what, even the towers do not seem to know.
