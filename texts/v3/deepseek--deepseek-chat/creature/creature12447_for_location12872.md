**The Dreaming Roots of Mycelis**  

In the lowest reaches of the city, where the fibrous walls thicken into something more like muscle than wood, the air hums with a sound that is not sound—a subsonic resonance, felt in the bones rather than heard. Here, the roots coil into vast, spiraling chambers, their surfaces slick with a translucent sap that shimmers like oil on water. The light is different here, too—not the cool glow of the upper nodules, but a deep, pulsing indigo, as if the walls themselves are breathing in slow, tidal rhythms.  

This is where the city dreams.  

Or perhaps *thinks* would be more accurate, though thought is too linear a word for what happens here. The roots twitch and shift, not randomly, but with a deliberation that suggests something beneath mere instinct. The sap thickens in rivulets, forming patterns that might be maps, or equations, or the slow, sprawling syntax of a language without words. The inhabitants of Mycelis rarely come this deep—not out of prohibition, but because the air here is heavy with something like meaning, and meaning is exhausting to bear for long.  

When they do descend, it is in reverence, or desperation. They press their palms to the walls and listen. Sometimes, the roots answer.  

The answers are not spoken. They come as shifts in the sap-flow, as sudden blooms of phosphorescence, as the inexplicable knowledge of a new passage forming miles away, where none existed before. The city is not sentient in the way a creature is sentient—it does not *want*, does not *fear*. But it *responds*, and its responses are vast, slow, and precise.  

Some say the roots are remembering something. Others say they are forgetting.  

And in the deepest chamber of all, where even the bravest rarely venture, the roots have woven themselves into a shape that might be a brain, or a heart, or a womb. The sap here is black and thick, moving in currents too slow to see. The air tastes of iron and loam.  

Here, the city is most awake.  

Here, it is almost listening.
