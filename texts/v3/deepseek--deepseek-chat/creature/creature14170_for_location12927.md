In this marsh-city, the inhabitants are as much a part of the landscape as the fibrous mats of mineralized filaments and the shallow, rippling pools. They are a species of amphibious beings—lithe, slender creatures with elongated limbs and smooth, scaled skin that shifts in color depending on the ambient light. Their bodies are adapted to the damp, thick air; their wide, lidless eyes glisten with a translucent film that protects them from the constant moisture, and their gill-like nostrils flare rhythmically as they breathe in the microbial-laden mist. 

Their movements are fluid, almost hypnotic, as they navigate the labyrinthine alleys and courtyards. They walk with a peculiar gait, their webbed feet spreading wide to distribute their weight on the yielding ground. When they pause, they crouch low, their long fingers trailing in the water or tracing the fibrous mats as if listening, feeling, communing with the marsh-city itself. Their hands are delicate but strong, tipped with sharp, retractable claws used for carving and digging. They are builders, but not in the traditional sense—their structures seem to grow alongside them, shaped as much by their labor as by the marsh's own processes.

These beings are communal, living in tight-knit clusters within the low, dense buildings. Each cluster is led by a Weaver, a figure of some reverence whose role is to interpret the messages of the marsh-city—the hums, clicks, and gurgles that thrum through the air and ground. The Weavers are easily identifiable by their swirling, bioluminescent markings, which pulse faintly in rhythm with the marsh's energy. They spend much of their time in meditation, their bodies motionless but their senses attuned to the subtle shifts in the environment. They are the keepers of the collective memory, the storytellers who weave the marsh-city's history into the fabric of the present.

Life here is simple but deeply interconnected. The inhabitants subsist on the abundant fauna—slippery, iridescent creatures that dart through the pools—and on the microbial spores that float in the air, which they harvest using woven nets and handheld filters. They craft tools from sharpened stone and bone, but their most prized possessions are their resonant artifacts—small, curved shells or bone fragments that they strike or rub to produce sounds. These sounds are not music in the traditional sense but rather communication, woven into the marsh-city's cacophony like threads in a tapestry. 

They have no written language, but their history and knowledge are preserved in the structures they build, the patterns they weave into the fibrous mats, and the sounds they create. Each building, each alley, each courtyard is a story waiting to be read, a memory waiting to be recalled. Their lives are governed by the rhythms of the marsh-city—the ever-present mist, the unceasing hum, the slow, deliberate process of creation and transformation.

Despite their primitive technology, these beings are far from simple. They possess a profound understanding of their environment, an intuitive sense of balance and harmony that allows them to thrive in this raw, embryonic landscape. They are patient, content to wait for the marsh-city to reveal its final form, knowing that they are both its creators and its creations. Their lives are a dance, a slow, intricate interplay of movement and stillness, sound and silence, growth and decay. They are the marsh-city, and the marsh-city is them, suspended together in this warm, wet moment of becoming.
