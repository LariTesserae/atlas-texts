The inhabitants of the **Moss-Cloaked Hollow** are as much a part of the land as the moss, the trees, and the trickling water. They are a people woven into the fabric of the hollow, shaped by its quiet rhythms and damp embrace. Their lives are marked by patience, reverence, and an unspoken understanding of the world around them—a world that watches, listens, and remembers.

### **The Mossfolk**
The inhabitants call themselves **Mossfolk**, though they rarely utter the name aloud. It is a title whispered in rituals, carved into the soft bark of the bent trees, or traced in the moss with careful fingers. They are a small, reclusive people, their numbers sparse but their presence deeply felt. Their skin is pale, almost translucent in the filtered light, marked by faint greenish veins that seem to mirror the lichen on the trees. Their hair is a tangle of dark, damp strands, often adorned with fragments of moss, fern fronds, or small, polished stones. Their eyes are the color of peat—dark, deep, and flecked with gold—as though they hold the earth’s secrets within them.

They are not tall, their forms compact and efficient, evolved to navigate the dense, mossy terrain without disturbing its delicate balance. Their hands are calloused but nimble, adept at shaping wood, stone, and earth. Their feet are bare, soles toughened by the damp ground, and they seem to glide rather than walk, their movements fluid and nearly silent.

### **Their Lives**
The Mossfolk live in harmony with the hollow, their routines dictated by its subtle cues. They rise with the faint light that filters through the canopy, their mornings spent tending to the small gardens they cultivate in the rare patches of soil not smothered by moss. They grow hardy vegetables—roots that thrive in the damp, and herbs that release their fragrance only when crushed. They gather what the land provides—lichen for dyes, moss for insulation, the occasional mushroom that sprouts in the shadows.

Their homes are low, rounded structures built into the hollow’s contours, their walls made of interwoven branches and packed earth, their roofs thick with layers of reeds and moss. Inside, the air smells of damp wood and earth, of herbs hung to dry, of the slow decay of organic matter. The Mossfolk sleep on beds of dried moss, softened by woven reed mats, their dreams filled with the murmur of water and the rustle of leaves.

They are skilled craftsmen, though their tools are simple. They carve wood into bowls and spoons, shape stone into blades and grinders, and weave reeds into baskets and mats. Their artistry is quiet, deliberate, marked by patterns that echo the natural world—spirals like fern fronds, waves like ripples in water, circles like the depressions where the mist collects. They wear garments of woven plant fibers, dyed in muted greens and browns, adorned with the occasional feather or polished stone.

### **Their Beliefs**
The Mossfolk speak little, but their silence is not emptiness—it is reverence. They believe the hollow is alive, a sentient being that breathes, watches, and listens. They see the trees as elders, their twisted forms holding wisdom beyond understanding. They see the water as the hollow’s blood, its slow trickle carrying messages from the depths of the earth. They see the moss as its skin, soft and protective, wrapping the land in a cloak of damp life.

They mark their rituals with soft chants, their voices blending with the hum of insects and the rustle of leaves. They leave offerings—small carvings, bundles of herbs, polished stones—in the hollow’s hidden places, tucked into the roots of trees or placed in the shallow depressions of the creek beds. They believe the hollow remembers, that it keeps their stories in the patterns of the lichen, in the carvings on the stones, in the reflections in the water.

### **Their Connection to the Strange**
The Mossfolk are aware of the hollow’s strangeness—the deliberate twist of the trees, the carvings on the stones, the reflections that don’t match the world above. But they do not fear it. They see it as part of the hollow’s life, as its way of speaking to them. They believe the trees guide them, the stones mark their paths, the water shows them glimpses of what lies beneath the surface.

They are drawn to these strangenesses, often spending hours tracing the carvings on the stones or sitting beneath the twisted trees, listening for voices that are not there. They believe the hollow’s secrets are meant to be uncovered slowly, over generations, and they pass down their knowledge in whispers, in gestures, in the careful placement of offerings.

### **Their Role in the Hollow**
The Mossfolk are the hollow’s caretakers, its guardians. They tend to its moss, clear its water, ensure its balance remains undisturbed. They see themselves as part of its life, their existence intertwined with its damp breath and eternal twilight. They do not seek to change the hollow but to understand it, to listen to its whispers and honor its rhythms.

They are not isolated entirely—occasional travelers stumble upon the hollow, drawn by its strange beauty. The Mossfolk welcome them with quiet hospitality, offering food, shelter, and silent companionship. But they do not share their secrets, and the travelers leave with only fragments of understanding, carrying the hollow’s dampness with them like a memory they cannot quite grasp.

The Moss-Cloaked Hollow is their world, their life, their purpose. They are its voice, its hands, its memory. And as long as the hollow breathes, so too will they.
