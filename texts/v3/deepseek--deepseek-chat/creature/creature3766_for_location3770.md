**The Hollowborn of the Gloomdelta**  

They are not born—they are *found*.  

When the mist parts just so, or when the humming water pools in a certain configuration, there they are: figures of slender, elongated limbs, their skin the color of tarnished silver and wet shale. Their faces are smooth, featureless except for the occasional slow-forming ridge or indentation, as if their flesh remembers expressions but has not yet decided which ones to keep.  

They move in silence, gliding rather than walking, their feet barely disturbing the spongy earth. When they speak (if they speak), it is not with mouths—sound simply *emerges* from them, resonant and directionless, like the echoes in a cavern. Their words are layered, overlapping, as if multiple voices exist within the same body, none quite in agreement.  

They do not build. They *adjust*. A touch here, a shift there—a fungal spire bends toward a new angle, a bridge of woven vines tightens its knots without hands. They seem to regard the city not as a creation but as an extension of themselves, a thing that grows as they grow, changes as they change.  

They are few. Or perhaps they are many, and most simply choose not to be seen. They gather in the glassy wastes, standing motionless for hours, days, their bodies absorbing the ambient thrum of the delta. Some whisper that they are listening—to the water, to the sky, to the half-formed thoughts of the land itself.  

They do not eat. They do not sleep. But sometimes, one will simply... *stop*. Their body stiffens, their surface hardening into a shell of mineral and fibrous growth, and then they crumble, dissolving into the earth like salt in water. The others do not mourn. They gather the residue in cupped hands and press it into the walls of the city, where it spreads in branching, luminous veins.  

No one knows if they are the delta’s caretakers or its prisoners. Perhaps they are both. Perhaps they are something else entirely—a symptom, not a species.  

But when you meet their gaze (if they have eyes, if you can call it a gaze), you feel it:  

They are waiting for something.  

And whatever it is, it is almost here.
