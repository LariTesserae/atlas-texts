### **The Submerged Engine**  

**Appearance:**  
The inhabitants of the drowned city are not creatures of flesh, but of rust and current—**the Drowned Mechanics**. They are humanoid in shape, but their bodies are hollow, skeletal things, their bones replaced with corroded pipes, their veins with frayed cables. Their skin is a translucent membrane stretched over a lattice of metal, pulsing with the faint glow of bioluminescent fluid. Their faces are smooth, featureless masks, save for the occasional flicker of light behind their eye sockets, like distant sparks in a dying furnace.  

**Movement:**  
They drift through the ruins with eerie precision, their limbs moving in slow, deliberate arcs, as if following unseen currents. When they must move quickly, their bodies shudder apart—ribs unlatching, limbs detaching—only to reform moments later, reassembled by some unseen force. They leave no footprints, only faint disturbances in the silt, swirling patterns that dissolve as quickly as they form.  

**Behavior:**  
The Drowned Mechanics do not speak. Instead, they communicate through the hum of machinery, the groan of shifting metal, the rhythmic pulsing of their internal fluids. They are drawn to the city’s great engines, clustering around them like moths to a flame. Some press their hands against the rusted hulls, as if listening. Others kneel in the silt, their fingers tracing the edges of broken gears, their movements reverent, almost mournful.  

They do not seem to notice intruders—not unless the machinery is disturbed. Then, their heads snap up in unison, their hollow eyes fixing on the source of disruption. They do not attack so much as *absorb*, their bodies unfolding like broken clockwork, engulfing whatever has trespassed. The victim is not eaten, not torn apart—merely *incorporated*, their form dissolving into the Mechanics’ shifting anatomy, another cog in the drowned city’s endless, grinding cycle.  

**Purpose:**  
They are not alive, not truly. They are echoes, remnants of the city’s builders, their consciousnesses long since dissolved into the mineral-rich waters. Now, they are little more than extensions of the machinery itself, tending to the engines with mindless devotion. They do not sleep, do not rest—only drift, and repair, and wait.  

And when the great engines finally fail, when the last of the city’s light gutters out, the Drowned Mechanics will still be there, their hands pressed to the cold metal, their bodies still humming with a purpose they no longer remember.
