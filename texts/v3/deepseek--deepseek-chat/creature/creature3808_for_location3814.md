The inhabitants of Vek-Tal are as much a part of the Hollows as the damp air and creeping moss. They are a people shaped by the contradictions of their environment, sturdily built and yet perpetually adapting, their lives intertwined with the slow-moving currents of the land.

The **Hollowfolk**, as they call themselves, are a resilient lot. Their skin carries the faint pallor of those who live under perpetual mist, though their cheeks are often flushed from the labor of their daily routines. Their hands are rough and calloused, accustomed to the grip of tools and the texture of damp stone. They dress in layered garments—thick wool cloaks weathered to muted greens and grays, patched with scraps of leather and rubberized canvas to keep the moisture at bay. Their boots are heavy-soled, designed to navigate the uneven terrain without slipping on slick surfaces.

The Hollowfolk are artisans and scavengers, their lives shaped by the resources the land grudgingly yields. They are miners, delving into the soft rock for veins of iron and copper, and engineers, piecing together machines that wheeze and groan in the mist. Their craftsmanship is utilitarian but not without beauty; the tools and machinery they create are adorned with intricate etchings and engravings, as if to remind themselves that even in this damp, heavy place, there is room for artistry.

They are not a talkative people, but when they speak, their words carry weight. Conversations are slow and deliberate, often accompanied by the rhythmic tapping of tools or the clink of mugs filled with bitter, herb-infused tea. Their humor is dry, their laughter low and rumbling, like the distant echo of water over stone.

Children in Vek-Tal grow up surrounded by the Hollows, their games a mirror of the land itself. They chase each other through the twisting alleyways, leap over the shallow pools, and climb the young trees that cling to the raw earth. They are taught early to be cautious of the shadows that move at the edges of perception, though they are also taught to respect them. The Hollowfolk have learned that the land holds secrets—some benign, others less so—and that it is better to live in cautious harmony than to provoke what cannot be understood.

The ruling class of Vek-Tal is not one of nobility but of knowledge. The **Mist Wardens** are a council of elders and engineers, their authority derived from their understanding of the land and the mechanics that keep the town functioning. They meet in the **Spire of Echoes**, a tower of rusted metal and moss-covered stone that rises above the low rooftops. Its windows are clouded with condensation, its halls filled with the hum of machinery and the scent of damp parchment. The Wardens are a pragmatic group, their decisions guided by the slow, inevitable changes of the Hollows rather than the fleeting whims of ambition.

Religion in Vek-Tal is quiet but pervasive. The Hollowfolk worship not gods but **The Flow**, the unseen currents of water and mist that shape their lives. Shrines are simple affairs—carved stones draped in moss, basins filled with rainwater, or rusted gears arranged in intricate patterns. Offerings are left in silence—a sprig of greenery, a shard of metal, a handful of earth. The Hollowfolk believe that all things are connected by The Flow, and that to live in harmony with it is to find balance in a place that is perpetually shifting.

There are tales of creatures in the Hollows—beings that move like shadows, that ripple the water without leaving a trace, that whisper in the damp air when the mist grows heavy. Some say they are spirits of the land, others that they are remnants of something older than Vek-Tal itself. The Hollowfolk do not hunt these creatures, nor do they seek them out. They treat them with the same cautious respect they afford the land, understanding that some mysteries are best left undisturbed.

Life in Vek-Tal is slow but not stagnant. Change comes in the creeping spread of moss, in the gradual deepening of erosion channels, in the quiet hum of machinery that adapts to the land’s rhythms. The Hollowfolk are a people caught between states—neither entirely wild nor fully tamed, neither ancient nor newborn. And yet, they endure, their lives intertwined with the damp earth and the misty air, their stories written in the slow, inevitable flow of The Flow.
