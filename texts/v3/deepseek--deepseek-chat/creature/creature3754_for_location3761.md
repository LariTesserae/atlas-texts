**The Hollow Ones of Varei-Thal**  

They do not swim. They do not breathe.  

They are the dwellers of the ruins, the slow-moving figures that haunt the algae-choked archways and crumbling towers. Their bodies are hollow—not empty, but filled with something else. Something that shifts.  

You see them first as silhouettes against the dim glow of the lichen, their forms elongated, their limbs too many and too thin. Their skin is translucent, stretched tight over nothingness, and beneath it swirls a darkness that is not shadow, not water, but something older. Something that remembers when the stone was soft.  

They do not speak. They do not need to. Their communication is in the way the currents bend around them, in the way the kelp recoils when they pass. They move with purpose, though what that purpose is remains unclear. Perhaps they are rebuilding. Perhaps they are unmaking.  

Their faces are smooth, featureless save for the occasional ripple, as if something beneath is testing the limits of its prison. Sometimes, a limb will split—not painfully, but lazily, like a drop of ink dispersing in water—and then reform, longer, thinner, more suited to whatever task awaits.  

They are drawn to the carvings on the walls, their fingers (if they have fingers) tracing the spirals with something like reverence. Or hunger. It is hard to tell.  

When they notice you (and they will, eventually), they do not attack. They do not flee. They simply turn, their bodies warping slightly to face you, and then—  

They wait.  

For what, you do not know.  

But the water grows heavier.  

And the stone beneath your feet (if you had feet) trembles.  

Just a little.
