**The Weeping Grove**  

A warm, sodden hollow where the air itself seems to drip. The land is low here, cupped like a palm holding rainwater, and the earth exhales moisture in slow, visible breaths. The water is never far—it beads on leaves, seeps up between roots, clings to the undersides of fungal shelves in trembling droplets. The ground is soft, yielding, a quilt of moss and peat that swallows footsteps whole.  

Vegetation is king. The grove is a riot of green, a place where plants have not just grown but *conquered*. Trees rise like cathedral pillars, their trunks wrapped in vines that pulse with slow, sap-heavy life. Their canopies knit together so tightly that the sky is only a rumor, glimpsed in fractured blue through the occasional gap. The undergrowth is a dense tapestry of ferns, creepers, and bulbous fungi, each fighting for space in the humid dark. Some plants grow atop others, layers upon layers, until the whole grove feels like a single, breathing organism.  

The air hums—not with insects (they are rare here, strangely absent) but with the creak of wood, the sigh of leaves, the occasional *plink* of water falling from some unseen height. The sound is thick, muffled by the vegetation, but persistent, a low chorus of organic machinery.  

Human presence is scattered, half-digested by the grove. A rusted pump leans drunkenly near a stagnant pool, its handle long since seized by vines. A few planks of a collapsed shed peek out from beneath a mound of moss, their nails weeping orange streaks into the wood. The remnants of industry are here, but they are losing—slowly, inevitably—to the slow crush of roots and the patient work of rot.  

Time moves differently here. Growth is relentless but unhurried. Trees shift their weight over decades, roots nudge stones aside over centuries. The grove is not young, not wild—it is ancient, settled into its own rhythms, a place where change is measured in the gradual tilt of a trunk or the slow swallowing of a forgotten tool.  

And strangeness lingers at the edges. The water sometimes gathers in shapes too deliberate to be random—perfect rings on still pools, droplets that hang midair a moment too long. The vines occasionally twitch when nothing has touched them. The grove feels *aware*, not in the way of a mind, but in the way of something vast and patient, watching with the indifference of a landslide.  

This is not a place for people. It is a place that tolerates them, briefly, before folding their works back into the green.
