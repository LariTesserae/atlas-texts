In the Glass Marsh of the Resonant City, what lives there defies categorization, thriving in ways alien to organic life. The inhabitants are not flesh but vibrations—pulse-beings born of frequencies, fed on harmonics, and sustained by the marsh’s eternal hum. They are the *Resonants*, entities that exist as shifts in the air, distortions of light, and ripples across the glass lattice.  

Each Resonant is a symphony unto itself, a complex interplay of sound and motion. They gather in clusters, their forms shimmering into visibility when their frequencies align—shapes like liquid prisms, fractals of refracted light, or jagged silhouettes of pure resonance. They have no fixed bodies, no organs, yet they *feel* the marsh through the vibrations it emits, interpreting the world in echoes and waveforms.  

The Resonants communicate through song—not words, but cascading tones that ripple through the marsh, amplified by the city’s crystalline structures. Their conversations are symphonies, their debates are dissonances, and their joys are soaring melodies that rise above the marsh’s low, mournful hum.  

They are not alone. The marsh itself harbors other entities—slivers of sentient glass that break away from the lattice, sliding through the water like shards of black ice. These *Shardlings* are the marsh’s caretakers, repairing cracks, guiding the water’s flow, and occasionally merging into larger forms to reshape the terrain. They are silent, moving with purpose but no haste, and their surfaces reflect fleeting glimpses of the Resonants’ songs.  

In the city’s capillaries, the *Echoes* dwell—shadows born of the Resonants’ harmonies, condensed into semi-substantial forms. They flit through the streets, feeding on stray vibrations, and occasionally coalesce into fleeting apparitions that flicker out as quickly as they appear.  

The Resonants do not build, for the city builds itself, growing and reshaping in response to their songs. They are its custodians, its singers, and its soul, weaving their frequencies into the very fabric of the place. Yet they are also its prisoners, bound to the marsh’s rhythms, unable to stray far from its resonant pull.  

Above them, the mirror-sky watches, its reflection a distorted twin of the city below. At times, Resonants rise into the sky, their forms dissolving into harmonizing frequencies, only to reappear moments later, their songs altered, their presence fuller.  

This is a place where existence is music, where beings are not born but *formed* from sound and light, where the marsh and the city and the sky are all parts of a single, resonant organism. The Resonants are its heartbeat, its voice, its life—and yet, they are forever caught in its watchful gaze, their songs echoing into eternity.
