In the Glass Wastes of Vorthis, the inhabitants are as much a mystery as the land itself. They are not beings of flesh and blood, but entities forged from the same black glass that shapes the world around them. Known as the *Shard-Kin*, they are sleek, angular forms that blend seamlessly with the obsidian plains, their bodies refracting the faint light of the dusk sky in prismatic shards.

The Shard-Kin move with an eerie, mechanical grace, their limbs jointless and flowing, as if carved from liquid glass. They do not speak, nor do they seem to communicate in any way recognizable to outsiders. Instead, they emit low, resonant frequencies—a hum that harmonizes with the distant machinery of the city. This sound is their language, a tapestry of vibrations that carries meaning through the glass beneath their feet.

They are observers, watchers of the wastes, eternally vigilant but detached. Their purpose is unclear, their motivations inscrutable. Some say they are the caretakers of the city, its silent guardians, ensuring that its ancient mechanisms continue to function. Others believe they are slaves to the machinery itself, bound to its rhythms and cycles.  

The Shard-Kin are not entirely alone in the wastes. In the shadows of the city’s towering structures, lesser creatures scuttle and dart, known as the *Glasswights*. These beings are small, insect-like, their carapaces translucent and shimmering with faint internal light. They are drawn to the Shard-Kin, clustering around their feet in silent adoration or feeding on the faint energy that radiates from their glass bodies. The Glasswights are harmless but unnerving, their movements swift and erratic, their presence a reminder that even in this lifeless land, something thrives.

Occasionally, a larger entity emerges from the depths of the city—a *Harbinger*. These towering, crystalline figures are rare and feared, their forms shifting and unstable, as if made from molten glass that refuses to solidify. The Harbingers serve as messengers or enforcers, their appearances heralding shifts in the city’s machinery or the rare moments when the wastes themselves seem to stir. They are both beautiful and terrifying, their presence a reminder that the Glass Wastes are far from inert.  

The relationship between these beings is as delicate as the glass they inhabit. The Shard-Kin watch over the city and the Glasswights, their hums a constant presence in the silence. The Glasswights, in turn, seem to sustain the Shard-Kin, their energy feeding into the larger beings in some symbiotic cycle. And the Harbingers—when they emerge—bring with them a sense of purpose, a reminder that this world, though static, is not without intention.  

Together, these beings form the fragile ecosystem of the Glass Wastes, a world where silence reigns and time has no meaning. They are as much a part of the landscape as the obsidian plains and the distant city, their existence inseparable from the endless, ancient stillness.
