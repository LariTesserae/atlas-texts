In this drowned city, the inhabitants are neither fish nor machine, but something in between—beings of liquid and light, their forms shifting like mercury in the palm of a hand. They are the architects of this place, though whether they built it or simply emerged from its depths is unclear. Their bodies are translucent, threaded with veins of bioluminescence that pulse in time with the city’s hum. They have no fixed shape, no bones or rigid structure, only a density that resists the water’s push, a presence that bends the currents around them.  

They move in slow, deliberate patterns, gliding through the canals and into the open mouths of the towers, their passage leaving faint ripples that linger longer than they should. Some are small, no larger than a human hand, darting like silver minnows between the fronds of glowing vegetation. Others are vast, amorphous masses that fill entire chambers, their edges bleeding into the surrounding water. They communicate in bursts of light and vibration, their language a series of coded flickers that travel through the liquid medium, carrying meaning in the pauses between pulses.  

Their society—if it can be called that—is built around the city’s unseen mechanisms. They tend to the shifting structures, their bodies flowing into cracks and seams, reinforcing where needed, dismantling where necessary. They are both maintenance and muse, their very presence altering the architecture in subtle ways. Some say they are the city’s immune system, responding to imbalances with eerie precision. Others believe they are its consciousness, the collective mind that guides its evolution.  

They do not eat, not in any conventional sense. Instead, they absorb—light, motion, the faint electrical charge that thrums through the water. They are drawn to the denser currents, the metallic-gray canals, where they linger, their forms thickening as they take in whatever energy the city provides. Occasionally, one will dissolve entirely, its essence dispersing into the liquid, only to coalesce elsewhere, reformed but not quite the same. Death, if it comes for them, is not an end but a redistribution, a return to the system.  

They are curious, these beings, though their curiosity is slow and measured. They study intruders—rare creatures that slip in from the edges of the world—with a detached fascination, encircling them, testing their boundaries. They do not seem hostile, but neither are they welcoming. They simply *are*, as much a part of the city as the water itself.  

And sometimes, when the currents shift just so, when the light catches them at the right angle, they flicker into visibility—not as individuals, but as a network, a web of glowing threads that stretch across the city, connecting every tower, every canal, every drifting mote of light. In those moments, it becomes clear: they are not just the inhabitants of this place. They *are* the place. Alive, aware, and waiting—though for what, not even they may know.
