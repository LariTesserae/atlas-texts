**The Glass-Eyed Watchers of Vorthax**  

They do not walk. They do not crawl. They *slide*—bodies of blackened glass and mercury, limbs stretching and retracting like liquid shadows. Their forms are humanoid only in the loosest sense: elongated torsos, too many joints, fingers that split and reform like fractals. Their faces are smooth, featureless planes except for their eyes—great, bulbous lenses that protrude from their skulls, reflecting not light, but *movement*.  

They do not speak, but they *record*. Every shift in the air, every ripple in the mercury pools, every flicker of the mirage-towers is caught in their gaze, stored in the swirling depths of their glass skulls. Their heads are hollow, filled with a slow-churning fluid that glows faintly, pulsing in time with the hum of the land.  

They are not alive, not in any way that matters. They are not dead, either. They are *preservers*, archivists of a dying world. They collect moments—not memories, not emotions, but raw, unfiltered *instances* of existence. A footstep that never fell. A breath that was never exhaled. A scream that never left a throat.  

Sometimes, they stop. Their bodies freeze mid-motion, their lenses dilating as they focus on something unseen. Then, with a sound like shattering crystal, they *unfold*—ribs peeling open like petals, revealing a hollow cavity where a heart should be. From this void, they pull forth a fragment of time—a shimmering, distorted sliver of reality—and press it into the ground. The glass plains absorb it greedily, the fractures sealing momentarily before splitting anew.  

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not bleed. But if you listen closely—if you stand very, very still—you can hear the whispers trapped inside them. Not voices. Not words. Just *echoes* of things that were almost real.  

And sometimes, when the hum of the land grows too loud, they tilt their heads back and *scream*—a soundless, gaping rupture in the air that makes the mirage-towers shudder.  

Then they go back to their work.  

The Weeping Flats will not remember itself.  

So they remember for it.
