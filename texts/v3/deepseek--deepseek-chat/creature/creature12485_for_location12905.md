You step deeper into this churning underworld, your feet sinking slightly into the moist, fibrous mat of vegetation that carpets the cavern floor. The air hums with something beyond sound—a deep, resonant vibration that thrums through your bones, as if the cavern itself is breathing.  

Up close, the creatures are stranger than first imagined. Some scuttle on too many legs, their exoskeletons armored with spines that flex like living needles. Others lack clear form, their gelatinous bodies rippling with internal currents, absorbing the faint light and emitting it back in unsettling, pulsing hues. They do not flee from you; they regard, they *test*. A segmented limb brushes against your arm—cold, smooth—and retracts just as quickly.  

Among them move the inhabitants.  

They are tall, slender beings, their smooth skin glistening like polished stone in the dim luminescence. Limbs too long, too fluid, tapering into delicate fingers that split and rejoin as if made of water given shape. Their faces are blank, featureless except for a single, vertical slit where a mouth might be. When they speak—if it can be called speech—it is with a sound like wind through hollow reeds, a harmonic whisper that resonates in the skull more than the ears.  

They do not seem to notice you at first. They move with ritualistic precision, their elongated fingers tracing patterns in the air, coaxing the vegetation to bend, to shift, to respond. The plants obey, limbs twisting to form archways, leaves folding into crude vessels that hold liquid darker than ink. They work without haste, their motions elegant and unhurried, as if time here does not conform to the surface world.  

One turns toward you.  

The absence of eyes is unsettling, yet you feel its attention like a physical weight. It extends a hand, palm upturned, and from its fingers drip threads of something luminous, like liquid silver. The substance pools, then rises, forming a shape—a mimicry. It is you, for a moment, before collapsing into a shimmering mist. Whether this is a greeting, a test, or something else entirely is unclear.  

Around them, the cavern shifts. New growths erupt from the ground; creatures scuttle into hastily formed burrows; distant, half-seen shapes coil and uncoil in the deeper shadows. There is intelligence here, but it is not like yours. It is vast, slow, older than the stone, and it watches through the movement of roots, the flicker of bioluminescence, the whisper of creatures that do not have names.  

And in the center of it all, the inhabitants work, motion by motion, weaving something incomprehensible—perhaps a structure, perhaps a ritual, perhaps a language written in living matter. You do not belong here.  

But the cavern does not ask if you belong.  

It simply *is*.
