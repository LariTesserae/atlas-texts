**The Watchers in the Drowned Cathedral**  

They are not fish. Nor are they entirely human.  

They drift through the cathedral’s halls like shadows given weight, their forms elongated, limbs stretched by centuries of slow adaptation. Their skin is pallid, translucent in places, revealing the faint tracery of veins beneath—blue-black, like ink spilled in water. Their eyes are wide, lidless, absorbing what little light filters through the depths. They do not blink. They do not need to.  

They are silent. They have no voices, or if they do, they have long forgotten how to use them. Communication is a slow, deliberate thing—a tilt of the head, a ripple of fingers, the barest shift in posture. They move without urgency, their motions fluid, as if the water has seeped into their bones and made them part of itself.  

They wear the remnants of what might have once been robes, now tattered into nothing more than fibrous strands that drift around them like seaweed. Some bear markings—scars, perhaps, or ritual carvings—etched into their flesh in patterns that mirror the glyphs on the walls. They do not heal. The water preserves them.  

They do not eat. Not in any way that would be recognizable. They press their hands against the cathedral’s pillars, against the kelp-thick walls, and absorb something—minerals, perhaps, or the slow decay of the stone itself. They do not age. They do not die. They simply *are*, as much a part of the cathedral as the algae that clings to its arches.  

They watch. That is their purpose, though none remember who assigned it to them. They observe the slow collapse of the cathedral, the way the silt rises in imperceptible increments, the way the currents shift over centuries. They do not intervene. They do not mourn. They simply witness, their faces unchanging, their thoughts—if they have any—unknowable.  

Occasionally, one will pause before a particular glyph, tracing it with skeletal fingers. A memory, perhaps. Or the ghost of one. But the moment passes, and they drift on, returning to their endless vigil.  

They are waiting.  

For what, even they do not know.
