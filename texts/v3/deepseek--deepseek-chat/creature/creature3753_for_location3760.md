### **The Inhabitants of the Drowned Plateau**  

Those who dwell on the Drowned Plateau are as much a part of the land as the wind-carved spires and the moss-covered stones. They are beings shaped by the plateau’s emptiness, its whispers, and its shifting permanence.  

#### **The Stone-Watchers**  

The Stone-Watchers are the plateau’s silent guardians, though whether they protect it or are bound to it is unclear. They are not flesh, nor are they entirely stone. Their forms are humanoid but elongated, their limbs thin and angular, their skin the color of weathered granite. Their faces are smooth and featureless except for deep, hollowed eyes that seem to drink in the light rather than reflect it.  

They do not move often. Most days, they stand still as statues atop the highest spires or in the shadows of crumbling arches, their heads tilted as if listening to the wind’s mournful song. When they do move, it is with a unsettling fluidity, their bodies bending and twisting as though the stone itself had come alive.  

The Stone-Watchers do not speak, but they hum. The sound is low and resonant, matching the vibrations of the wind as it passes through the rock formations. Some say their hum carries knowledge—ancient secrets carved into the stone and forgotten by time. Others say it is a warning, a reminder that the plateau is not a place for the living.  

#### **The Dew-Singers**  

The Dew-Singers are small, translucent creatures that emerge with the morning mist. They are amphibious, their bodies slick and glistening with moisture, their skin so thin that their internal organs are faintly visible, pulsing like tiny lanterns. Their limbs are delicate, almost spindly, and their heads are crowned with fronds that shimmer like dew-covered grass.  

They move in small groups, flitting across the damp stone with surprising speed. Their voices are high and melodic, a chorus of chirps and clicks that seem to harmonize with the rustling of the ferns and the dripping of water into shallow pools. They gather around the glowing fungi, their fronds brushing the caps, and when they sing, the fungi pulse in time with their rhythm.  

The Dew-Singers are harmless, their presence almost playful, but they are elusive. When approached, they vanish into the mist or slip into cracks in the stone, their songs fading into echoes.  

#### **The Wind-Wraiths**  

The Wind-Wraiths are as intangible as the air itself, and yet they are unmistakably *there*. They are the plateau’s restless spirits, born of the howling gales and the twisting mists. Their forms are fluid and ever-changing, taking on the shape of the wind’s passage—a swirling vortex one moment, a reaching hand the next.  

They are not malevolent, but they are not kind. They toy with those who wander the plateau, pulling at cloaks, whispering half-formed words in the ears of travelers, and guiding them in circles. Some say they are the spirits of those who disappeared on the plateau, forever lost to the wind. Others believe they are the plateau itself, the land’s way of asserting its dominance over those who dare to walk its stone.  

The Wind-Wraiths are strongest during storms, their forms becoming solid enough to block paths or push travelers to the ground. But they are also the ones who bring the storms, their laughter echoing in the thunder and their breath visible in the lightning.  

#### **The Many-Legged Shadows**  

These creatures are rarely seen, but their presence is felt. They move at the edge of vision, darting between crevices and vanishing beneath boulders. They are low to the ground, their bodies segmented and covered in chitinous plates that reflect the dull light of the plateau. Their legs are too numerous to count, each one tipped with a claw that clicks softly against the stone.  

They are scavengers, feeding on the rare carcasses of winged creatures or the remnants of abandoned fires. But they are also hunters, their movements swift and precise. Those who have encountered them speak of a sound like dry leaves rustling and a sensation of being watched from all sides.  

Some say they are the plateau’s cleaners, removing what does not belong. Others believe they are its eyes, observing everything that happens on its surface.  

#### **The Echo-Bearers**  

The Echo-Bearers are human—or at least, they were. They are the remnants of those who tried to make a home on the plateau and failed. Their bodies are gaunt, their skin pale and cracked, their eyes hollow. They wander aimlessly, their footsteps silent, their voices nothing more than echoes of what they once were.  

They do not speak, but they repeat. A whisper, a laugh, a cry—snatches of sound carried on the wind, repeating endlessly as if caught in a loop. Some say they are cursed, their souls bound to the plateau for failing to leave when they should have. Others say they are warnings, a reminder that the plateau does not welcome those who linger too long.  

The Echo-Bearers are not hostile, but they are unnerving. They appear suddenly, standing in the distance or just behind a traveler, only to vanish when looked at directly.  

#### **The Waiting Ones**  

The Waiting Ones are something else entirely. They are not seen, but they are felt—a presence deeper than the stone, older than the wind. They are the reason the plateau feels as though it is holding its breath, the source of the nagging sense that something is about to happen.  

Some say they are the plateau’s creators, the ones who shaped it and left their mark in the groaning of the rock and the twisting of the mist. Others believe they are the plateau’s masters, beings who exist beyond time and space, watching and waiting for… something.  

No one knows what they are waiting for, or when they will act. But one thing is certain: when they do, the Drowned Plateau will never be the same.
