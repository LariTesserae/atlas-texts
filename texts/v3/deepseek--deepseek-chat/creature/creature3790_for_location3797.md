**The Watcher in the Drowned Spire**  

It is not human. Not anymore.  

It was, once—or something close. A builder, perhaps, one of those who laid the last stubborn stones of the spire’s crown before the sea swallowed their world. Time and salt and the slow gnawing of the wind have reshaped it. Now, it is something else.  

Its skin is the color of wet slate, cracked and peeling in places, revealing glimpses of something darker beneath—not flesh, but a substance like hardened tar, threaded through with veins of phosphorescent brine. Its limbs are too long, its joints too many, bending in ways that suggest a body shaped by erosion rather than design. When it moves, it does so with a slow, deliberate economy, as if conserving its strength against the endless pull of the wind.  

Its face is the worst part.  

There are no eyes—only hollows where they should be, deep pits crusted with salt. Its mouth is a lipless slit, stretched too wide, filled with needle-teeth of blackened bone. It does not speak, but sometimes, when the wind dies for a breath, it makes sounds—wet clicks, like stones grinding underwater, or a low, shuddering hum that resonates in the hollows of the spire itself.  

It does not eat. Not in any way that leaves remains. But things disappear near it—tools left too long in the open, scraps of driftwood, even the occasional seabird that strays too close. They simply... vanish. And the Watcher grows a little more solid, a little more *present*, as if feeding on absence itself.  

It watches the sea. Always the sea. Its head tilts at odd angles, tracking the unseen movements beneath the waves, the disturbances that hint at something vast and restless below. Sometimes, it reaches out with those too-long fingers, tracing patterns in the air as if mapping currents only it can see.  

It is waiting.  

For what, even it may not know.  

But when the storms come—when the waves rise high enough to lick at the spire’s crown—it stands at the very edge, arms spread, as if welcoming the flood.  

As if it remembers, in whatever passes for its mind, that the sea always takes what belongs to it.  

And one day, it will take the Watcher too.
