**The Weeping City of Gloam**  

At the heart of the plateau, where the basalt fractures into jagged canyons, the city’s true nature reveals itself. The structures are not merely grown—they are *alive*, their walls pulsing with a slow, rhythmic contraction, like the breathing of some colossal, slumbering beast. The glyphs etched into their surfaces are not carvings but *veins*, carrying something darker than blood, a fluid that glows faintly when touched by the erratic arcs of light.  

The inhabitants—if they can be called that—are not separate from the city but extensions of its will. They move in unison, their forms rippling between solidity and shadow, their bodies composed of the same mineral-laden mist that clings to the air. Some are tall, spindling things with too many joints, their limbs bending in ways that suggest a skeleton of liquid stone. Others are dense, hunched masses that crawl along the ground, their surfaces studded with crystalline growths that hum in discordant harmony with the city’s endless drone.  

They do not speak. Communication here is not sound but *resonance*, a vibration that passes through the stone and into the bones of those foolish enough to linger. To stand in Gloam is to feel the city’s thoughts pressing against your skull—not words, but impressions: hunger, patience, a yawning abyss of time that stretches in all directions.  

The water is not water. It is the city’s sweat, its tears, a byproduct of whatever alchemy keeps the plateau from crumbling entirely. It tastes of iron and something older, something that lingers on the tongue like a half-remembered dream. To drink it is to invite visions—fragments of the city’s past, or perhaps its future, all blurred together in a haze of fractured geometry and whispering shadows.  

At the center of it all, beneath the tallest spire (or is it the deepest chasm?), something stirs. Not an entity, not a god, but a *process*, a cycle of dissolution and reformation that has been turning since before the first stone wept. It is aware of you. It is always aware.  

You will leave, eventually. Or you won’t. The city does not care. The water keeps weeping. The hum never stops.
