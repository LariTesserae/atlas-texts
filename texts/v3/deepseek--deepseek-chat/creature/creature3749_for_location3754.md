**The Dripping Vaults**  

The air is thick, warm, and heavy with the scent of wet stone and something older—something that lingers in the back of your throat like the taste of rust. The cavern is alive, though not in the way you might expect. It does not teem with creatures or hum with movement. Instead, it pulses with slow, deliberate life, a rhythm measured in the steady fall of water and the quiet creep of growth.  

The walls weep. Thin streams of moisture trickle down the jagged stone, carving shallow channels over centuries. The rock itself is dark, almost black, but where the water runs, it glistens with an unnatural sheen, as if the stone were sweating something thicker than water. Stalactites hang like teeth, their tips beading with droplets that fall with a sound like a slow, irregular heartbeat.  

Luminous moss clings to the walls in patches, casting a sickly green glow that does little to dispel the darkness. It clumps in uneven clusters, pulsing faintly, as if breathing. Between them, fungi push through the damp stone—pale, fleshy growths that seem to twitch when unobserved, their surfaces glistening with a thin, viscous film. They do not rot. They do not decay. They simply persist, feeding on the damp and the warmth, growing imperceptibly larger with each passing year.  

In the deepest pool, half-submerged in water thick with minerals, lies the wreckage of something that does not belong. A machine, perhaps, though time and corrosion have blurred its purpose. Rusted gears and broken pipes jut from the water like the bones of a long-dead beast, their surfaces crusted with mineral deposits. The metal groans sometimes, shifting in the water as if settling deeper into the earth.  

There are no creatures here—no rats, no insects, nothing that scurries or flies. Only the moss, the fungi, and the slow, relentless drip of water. And yet, if you stand still long enough, you might feel it—the weight of something watching. Not from the shadows, but from the stone itself. The Vaults are patient. They have waited this long. They will wait longer still.  

And when the last drop falls, and the last light fades, the Vaults will remember you were here.
