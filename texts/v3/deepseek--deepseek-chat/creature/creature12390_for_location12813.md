### **Inhabitants of the Submerged City**  

The beings that dwell in this drowned world are as much a part of it as the silt and the slow-moving currents. They are shaped by the darkness, the pressure, the quiet—creatures of patience and stillness, of senses attuned to the unseen.  

#### **The Silent Ones**  
Humanoid, but not human. Their forms are elongated, their limbs thin and sinewy, their skin a pale, translucent gray, mottled with darker patches that shift like the play of light through deep water. Their eyes are large, black, and lidless, absorbing every flicker of bioluminescence in the gloom. They do not speak, not in any way that requires sound—instead, they communicate through subtle gestures, the ripple of their fingers, the slow tilt of their heads. Some say they remember the city before the water took it, that they are its last children, adapted over centuries into something else entirely.  

They live in the higher ruins, where the faintest light still reaches, moving through the broken towers with effortless grace. Their dwellings are hollows in the stone, lined with woven kelp and the shells of long-dead mollusks. They hunt the blind fish, luring them with glowing lures grown from their own flesh, and they avoid the deeper places, where the larger things stir.  

#### **The Bone-Carvers**  
These are not creatures of flesh, but of shell and chitin, their bodies segmented like the armor of ancient crustaceans. They walk on too many legs, their movements precise, their claws sharp enough to etch symbols into stone. They are the archivists of the deep, the ones who keep the city’s memory. They do not build—they preserve, carving the history of the lost world into the walls of the drowned temples.  

No one knows what they eat, if they eat at all. They are seen rarely, only when some shift in the currents disturbs the silt, revealing a new ruin to be marked. They do not acknowledge the Silent Ones, nor the other creatures of the deep. They simply work, their claws clicking softly against the stone, their many eyes reflecting no light at all.  

#### **The Drifters**  
Not one species, but many—colonies of gelatinous, pulsing things that move through the water like living clouds. Some are small, no larger than a hand, drifting in shimmering schools. Others are vast, amorphous, their bodies threaded through with veins of cold light. They are neither plant nor animal, but something in between, feeding on the microscopic life that flourishes in the warm currents.  

The Silent Ones avoid them. Some Drifters are harmless, even beautiful, their glow a welcome break from the dark. Others are traps—their tendrils sticky, their embrace slow and suffocating. The largest of them are said to hold things inside, shapes that might once have been people, or might be something else entirely.  

#### **The One Below**  
It does not have a name, because names require voices, and no living thing here speaks of it aloud. It moves in the deepest trenches, where the ruins crumble into nothing. It is vast, older than the city, perhaps older than the water itself. Sometimes, the pressure changes. Sometimes, the currents still. Sometimes, the Silent Ones vanish from the lower districts, and the Bone-Carvers retreat into their hidden chambers.  

It is not evil. It is simply hungry. And the city has always fed it.  

---

This is a world without haste, without mercy, without the need for either. Its inhabitants live as the water allows—slowly, silently, with the weight of centuries pressing down on them. They are the city’s inheritors, its guardians, its final witnesses. And when the last light fades, they will still be here, waiting in the dark.
