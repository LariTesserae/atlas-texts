### **The Hollowed Ones**  

They do not call themselves anything. If pressed, they might whisper a sound like wind through cracked stone—**"Vhiss"**—but names are for creatures who need to be remembered.  

They are **not ghosts**, though they move like them. Their bodies are **translucent**, not from absence but from adaptation—skin like frosted glass, veins visible as faint blue tracery, muscles shifting beneath like shadows in deep water. Their bones are **denser than they should be**, anchoring them against the unseen currents of the cavern.  

They do not speak. Not in words. Their language is **pressure**, the shift of air against skin, the brush of fingertips against damp stone. They leave messages in **patterns of moisture**—finger-drawn trails on cold rock, symbols that evaporate within hours.  

They eat **what the cavern provides**: blind fish from underground streams, fungi that grow in rings around thermal vents, lichen scraped from the walls with fingernails sharpened on stone. They drink from **condensation**, lapping it from leaves or cupping their hands beneath slow-dripping stalactites.  

Their eyes are **large, lidless**, adapted to the dim glow of the cavern. They see in gradients of blue and gray, their pupils expanding to swallow every photon. They do not blink.  

They sleep in **shifts**, curled in hollows lined with dried moss, their bodies cooling to match the ambient temperature. They dream in **echoes**, replaying the sounds of the cavern—the drip of water, the creak of roots, the distant rumble of shifting earth.  

They are **few**, but they are not lonely. They touch often—hands brushing in passing, foreheads pressed together in silent communion. They do not fear death, because the cavern **reclaims all things**. When one of them dies, the others carry the body to a **chamber of soft clay**, where they press it into the wall, limb by limb, until it becomes part of the stone.  

They have been here **a long time**. They will be here longer.  

The cavern breathes around them. They listen.
