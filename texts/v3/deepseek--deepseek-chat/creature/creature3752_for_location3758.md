**The Glassmaker’s Atrium** is inhabited by **The Glaziers**—a collective of semi-sentient, symbiotic organisms that exist somewhere between artisans and custodians.  

They are not individuals so much as extensions of the chamber itself, their bodies composed of the same slow-moving glass that forms the walls, though theirs is thinner, more malleable. Their limbs are elongated, fibrous strands of silica, capable of stretching into filaments fine enough to weave into the cracks of cooling panes or coiling into thick, probing tendrils to adjust the flow of water through the canals. They have no faces, only clusters of refracted light that gather and disperse like schools of fish beneath their translucent surfaces—shifting patterns that might be communication, or simply the byproduct of their constant, minute adjustments to the environment.  

The Glaziers move in slow, deliberate pulses, their forms rippling as they redistribute their mass to better interact with their surroundings. Some press themselves flat against the walls, merging briefly to repair a fracture or guide a new vein of copper into place. Others wade through the shallow canals, their lower bodies dissolving slightly, becoming one with the mineral-rich water before reforming elsewhere.  

They do not eat, not in any conventional sense, but they do consume—absorbing trace elements from the runoff, integrating stray spores from the moss, occasionally enveloping one of the skittering creatures that stray too close, not to devour but to *study*, their glass bodies clouding momentarily as they dissect and replicate the creature’s structure before releasing it, unharmed but subtly altered.  

Their purpose is unclear, even to them. They are not builders so much as *tenders*, responding to the needs of the atrium as it responds to them. They do not create—they *mediate*, easing transitions, smoothing edges, ensuring that the chamber remains in its state of perpetual becoming.  

Visitors (rare as they are) often mistake them for part of the scenery until they move, and by then it is too late—the Glaziers have already begun adjusting to the newcomer’s presence, the humidity shifting, the canals diverting, the glass underfoot softening slightly to better accommodate their weight.  

The Glaziers do not speak, but they listen. The atrium listens with them.
