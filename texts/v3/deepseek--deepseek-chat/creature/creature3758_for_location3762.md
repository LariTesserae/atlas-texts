### **The Hollows of Yth-Morath**  

Beneath a sky of perpetual twilight, where the sun never rises but never fully sets, lies the vast, sunken expanse of Yth-Morath. The land is a labyrinth of hollows—great, bowl-like depressions in the earth, each one cupping its own secret. The air is thick with the scent of damp soil and slow decay, of roots exhaling into the dark.  

The ground is soft, yielding underfoot, covered in a carpet of black moss that drinks sound rather than muffles it. Footsteps vanish here. Voices carry only so far before the earth swallows them. The hollows themselves vary—some shallow, barely dips in the land, others deep enough that their bottoms are lost in shadow. The walls of these pits are striated, layered with sediment that tells stories in colors of rust, ash, and bone.  

At the center of each hollow, there is always water—not a lake, not a pond, but something in between. Dark, still pools that ripple only when something unseen brushes the surface. The water is cold, clear when cupped in the hands, but impenetrable when gazed into from above. Some say the pools have no bottom. Others whisper that they are the eyes of the land itself, watching.  

The trees here are gnarled, their bark like cracked leather, their branches twisting upward as if straining toward a light they will never reach. Their roots sprawl across the hollows, half-buried, half-exposed, forming natural bridges between the pits. Between them grow clusters of pale, fleshy fungi, glowing faintly in the dimness, their light just enough to guide—or mislead.  

The creatures of Yth-Morath are few, but they are old. Blind, slow-moving things with too many legs or too few eyes, things that do not flee from sound because sound does not last here. There are birds, but they do not sing—they croak, low and rattling, from the high branches. Sometimes, something larger moves in the deeper hollows, something that makes the water tremble at the edges.  

The people here, if they can be called that, are seldom seen. They live in the spaces between the hollows, in huts woven from roots and draped with lichen. They speak little, and when they do, their voices are like the wind through dead leaves. They know the paths between the pits, the safe ways across the roots. They know better than to stare too long into the water.  

Yth-Morath does not change. The hollows do not fill. The trees do not rot. The water does not stir unless it chooses to. Time here is not marked by days or nights, but by the slow sinking of footsteps into the moss, by the gradual creep of roots over stone.  

The land is patient. It has waited this long. It can wait forever.
