**The Hollowed Ones**  

They are not quite human, not anymore—not since the stone took them in.  

Their skin is the color of wet clay, pale and porous, their veins faintly visible beneath the surface like the delicate branching of roots. Their eyes have adapted—large, dark, and lidless, absorbing every scrap of light in the dim caverns. They do not blink; instead, a thin, translucent membrane flickers sideways across their vision when the air grows too dry.  

Their hands are their most striking feature—long-fingered, knuckled with extra joints, the nails hardened into chitinous claws that can chip away at rock with patient precision. Some have fused with their tools over time, their palms grown around the hafts of bone picks, their wrists thickened from years of steady pressure against unyielding stone.  

They move without hurry, their steps silent on damp stone. Their voices, when they speak, are low and resonant, shaped by the acoustics of the cavern—words that curl and echo just enough to be understood before fading into the hum of dripping water. They do not shout. There is no need.  

They wear little—layers of woven fungal fiber, treated to repel moisture, or cured hides from the few creatures that venture deep enough to be caught. Some adorn themselves with carved bone or polished river-pebbles, their jewelry as much tool as ornament—a sharp edge here, a hook there, always useful.  

They eat what the Undercity provides: blind cave fish, netted from the black pools; thick, rubbery mushrooms cultivated in the lee of warm rocks; lichen scraped from walls and fermented into thin, sour brews. Their bodies have learned to thrive on little.  

They do not fear the dark. They fear the dry.  

When the water stops, when the stone cracks and the air grows stale—that is when they retreat deeper, following the veins of moisture, listening for the distant song of underground rivers. They know the rock is alive, shifting, breathing in its own slow way. They know they are part of it now.  

They are the Hollowed Ones. The stone remembers them.
