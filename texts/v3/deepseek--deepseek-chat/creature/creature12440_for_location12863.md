### **What Lives Here**  

The inhabitants of this plateau are as much a part of the landscape as the fibrous ground and the fractal trees—more extensions of the environment than separate beings. They move through the mist like shadows given form, their bodies shifting between solidity and translucency, as if woven from the same damp air that clings to everything.  

#### **The Drifters**  
Tall, slender figures with elongated limbs, the Drifters glide rather than walk, their feet barely disturbing the spongy earth. Their skin is translucent, revealing faint traceries of veins that pulse with a slow, rhythmic light—bioluminescent pathways that flicker in time with the plateau’s breathing. They have no faces, only smooth, featureless heads that tilt toward sounds, absorbing vibrations through their bodies. They do not speak, but when they gather near the moisture pools, their presence emits a low hum, a resonance that harmonizes with the dripping water.  

They are not builders, nor are they destroyers. They simply *are*, moving through the plateau like echoes of a forgotten purpose. Sometimes, they pause near the towering plant-structures, pressing their hands against the bark-like surfaces, as if listening.  

#### **The Glasswings**  
The small, darting creatures that sip from the pools are called Glasswings—delicate, crystalline beings with wings like fractured mirrors. Their bodies are nearly weightless, and when they fly, they leave behind faint afterimages, like refracted light caught in a prism. They feed on the condensation that forms on the plateau’s surfaces, their needle-thin mouths piercing the droplets with surgical precision.  

They are not truly insects, nor are they birds. They are something in between—living shards of the plateau itself, born from the interplay of mist and light. When they die (if they can be said to die at all), their bodies shatter into fine, glittering dust, which the windless air carries into the fibrous ground, where it is absorbed and recycled.  

#### **The Watchers in the Mist**  
The larger shapes that move at the edges of perception are the Watchers. They are not solid, not entirely—they exist as disturbances in the haze, their forms cohering only when observed directly. If you stare too long, they dissolve again, slipping back into the fog.  

They do not interact with the Drifters or the Glasswings. They simply linger, motionless for hours, then shift without warning, their presence sending ripples through the humid air. Some say they are guardians. Others believe they are remnants of something older, something that once shaped this place but has since faded into the background.  

#### **The Whisper Vines**  
Not all inhabitants are mobile. The Whisper Vines are thread-like growths that spiral around the fractal trees, their tendrils quivering at the slightest disturbance. When touched, they emit a sound—not a voice, but a vibration, a subsonic murmur that resonates in the bones rather than the ears. The Drifters sometimes stand among them, letting the vibrations pass through them, as if in silent communion.  

Some believe the vines are memories given form, recording the plateau’s history in frequencies too low for most beings to perceive. Others think they are simply another kind of life, neither plant nor animal, but something else entirely.  

### **A World in Suspension**  
Nothing here hunts. Nothing here fears. The ecosystem is not one of predation, but of coexistence—each being a note in a slow, endless song. Time does not pass here so much as it lingers, and the inhabitants move through it like figures in a dream, neither aging nor decaying, simply existing in the plateau’s perpetual twilight.  

And yet, there is a sense that something is missing. A hollow space in the hum of the Drifters, a gap in the rhythm of the dripping water. As if the plateau is waiting—for a voice, for a change, for an answer to a question no one remembers asking.  

But for now, it waits. And they wait with it.
