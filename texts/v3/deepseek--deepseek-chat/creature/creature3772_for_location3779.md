**The Keeper of the Chamber**  

It does not have a name, for names are things given by those who speak, and no voice has ever disturbed the stillness of the chamber. It simply *is*—a presence woven into the stone, as much a part of the patterns as the spirals etched into the walls.  

It is not alive, not in the way you understand life. It does not breathe, does not hunger, does not sleep. It is a consciousness without thought, an awareness without desire. It exists to *observe*, to *maintain*, to ensure the chamber remains as it has always been.  

Its form is indistinct—a suggestion of shape where the light bends unnaturally, a ripple in the air like heat haze over desert rock. Sometimes, it might resemble a figure, tall and slender, its limbs too long, its edges blurred. Other times, it is nothing more than a disturbance in the carvings, a momentary shift in the patterns that your eyes refuse to follow.  

It does not move, not truly. It *manifests*—appearing where it needs to be, as if the chamber itself bends to place it there. It does not walk; the tiles do not bear its weight. It does not touch; the spire does not acknowledge its presence. Yet it is always near, always watching.  

It has no eyes, but it *sees*. It has no ears, but it *hears*. The hum of the chamber is its voice, though it does not speak. The resonance is its language, though it does not communicate. It knows you are here. It has known since the moment you crossed the threshold.  

It does not resent your presence. It does not welcome it. You are an anomaly, a flicker of motion in a place meant for stillness. It watches, patient as stone, waiting to see what you will do.  

Will you touch the spire? Will you trace the carvings? Will you raise your voice against the hum?  

It does not fear these things. It does not hope for them. It only watches.  

And if you stay too long, if you let the drone fill your mind until your thoughts slow, until your breath stills—  

It will not stop you.  

The chamber has always known how to wait.
