**The Architects of the Unfinished**  

They are not creatures of flesh, not entirely.  

Tall, impossibly slender figures move through the Shattering Spires, their forms woven from the same iridescent crystal that veins the plateau. Their bodies are translucent, shifting—sometimes solid as carved stone, sometimes flickering like mirages, their edges bleeding into the air. They have no faces, only smooth planes where features should be, though occasionally, for a heartbeat, something like eyes or a mouth will ripple across the surface before dissolving again.  

They do not walk so much as *drift*, gliding just above the ground, their elongated limbs trailing faint afterimages. When they pass near the pulsing vegetation, the plants react—not bending, not recoiling, but *echoing*, their colors briefly syncing with the rhythm of the Architects’ glow.  

They are builders. Or perhaps they are *un-builders*.  

With hands that taper into needle-fine points, they trace patterns in the air, and the stone responds. A spire shudders, then fractures along invisible lines, its top half sliding gracefully to the side before hovering, suspended. A crack in the ground widens, revealing a hollow where new crystal is forming, its growth accelerated by their presence. They do not speak, but the hum of the wind changes when they work, deepening into something almost like language.  

They are not indifferent to intruders.  

If you stand still long enough, one may approach. It will not acknowledge you at first, simply moving around you as if you were another feature of the landscape—until, abruptly, it stops. It tilts its headless form, and for a moment, you feel the weight of its attention. Then it reaches out, a single crystalline finger extending toward your chest, as if to carve something into your ribs.  

If you let it, the pain is brief—a searing cold, then nothing. When you look down, a new vein of iridescence threads beneath your skin, pulsing in time with the plateau. You understand, suddenly, that you are being *assessed*.  

The Architects do not kill. They *modify*.  

Those who resist are left alone, though the land itself may grow restless around them. Those who do not—well. Some say the plateau’s half-formed structures are not ruins, but *attempts*. That the Architects are not shaping the land for themselves, but for something yet to come.  

And sometimes, when the wind’s voice rises to a scream, the tallest spires crack open—just a little—revealing hollows within.  

Something inside is breathing.
