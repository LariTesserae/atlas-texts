In this place, the inhabitants are as much a part of the land as the moss that clings to the stone and the vines that sway in the unseen wind. They are the **Echo-born**, a people shaped by the plateau’s contradictions—both fragile and enduring, fleeting and eternal.  

### **Appearance**  
The Echo-born are tall, their forms lean and elongated, as if stretched by the weight of the void above. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, threaded with faint veins of bioluminescence that pulse in time with the land’s own glow. Their eyes are large, black and reflective, adapted to the eternal twilight, seeing not just with light but with the subtle vibrations of sound and movement. Their hair is sparse, fine strands like silken moss, often braided with fragments of glassy bark or woven with luminescent spores.  

They wear little, for the land itself clothes them—layers of lichen and fibrous plant matter grown into their skin, forming natural armor that shifts and regenerates with the seasons. Some adorn themselves with carved bone or the shed exoskeletons of the plateau’s larger creatures, their ornaments whispering as they move.  

### **Society & Culture**  
The Echo-born live in scattered, nomadic clans, their settlements temporary—woven nests of fungal fibers and sinew, built high in the fractal canopies or nestled within the hollows of the glass-barked trees. They leave no permanent marks, for the land erases them quickly; their history is oral, passed down in chants that mimic the wind’s howl and the drip of water on stone.  

They are a people of sound. Language is a layered thing, half-spoken, half-sung, with meanings carried in pitch and rhythm as much as in words. They communicate with the creatures around them, mimicking calls to lure prey or warn of danger. Their songs are maps, their lullabies are warnings, and their mourning rites are wordless, a harmony of breath and vibration that lingers in the air long after they have moved on.  

### **Survival & Ritual**  
The Echo-born do not farm; they follow. They track the migrations of bioluminescent swarms, harvest the fruiting bodies of fungi that bloom in the wake of tremors, and siphon the condensed water from the glass-bark trees. They hunt, but carefully, for the beasts of the plateau are dangerous, and some are said to be older than the clans themselves.  

Their rituals are tied to the land’s cycles. When the cold rains come, they cut shallow channels in their own skin, letting the water mix with their blood before it is offered back to the earth. When a clan member dies, their body is suspended among the vines, left to be reclaimed by the ecosystem—a final act of symbiosis.  

### **Beliefs**  
They do not worship gods, but they listen. The plateau speaks in tremors and echoes, and the Echo-born believe it is alive, a vast, slow-thinking entity that tolerates them as one tolerates a fleeting thought. Some clans claim to hear whispers in the dark, voices that are not their own. Others believe they are the land’s dream, and when it wakes, they will cease to be.  

### **Encounters**  
To meet an Echo-born is to be studied before being acknowledged. They are cautious, their movements deliberate, their voices low. They might offer a traveler a drink from a hollowed seed, or they might vanish into the mist without a sound. They do not lie, but neither do they explain. Their words are riddles wrapped in song, and their gifts often carry unintended consequences.  

They are not hostile by nature, but they are fiercely territorial when the land demands it. Cross them at the wrong time, and you may find yourself lost in a labyrinth of sound, your own echoes turned against you.  

### **Fate**  
The Echo-born do not fear extinction. They believe they will fade when the plateau is done with them, dissolving back into the hum of the living world. Until then, they endure, half-seen and half-heard, as much a part of the darkness as the light they carry within.
