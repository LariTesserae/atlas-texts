**The Inhabitants of the Wet Machine-Land**  

They call themselves *The Keepers of the Drowned Pulse*, though outsiders—rare as they are—simply refer to them as *the Wetfolk*. They are not quite human, not quite machine, but something woven between the two, their bodies adapted to the slow, rhythmic breathing of the landscape.  

Their skin is pale, almost translucent, stretched over lean frames that seem to ripple when they move, as if their flesh is barely containing the water inside. Their veins are dark, visible beneath the surface like roots under wet soil, and their fingers are long, ending in nails that have hardened into something closer to chitin—useful for prying open rusted metal panels or scraping away layers of moss.  

Their eyes are the most unsettling feature: large, glassy, and entirely black, reflecting the dim glow of the bioluminescent flora. They have no pupils, yet they see perfectly in the low light, tracking movement not by sight alone but by shifts in humidity, by the minute vibrations of machinery underfoot.  

They dress in layers of oilcloth and frayed synthetic fiber, garments that never fully dry, clinging to their bodies like a second skin. Some wear belts of salvaged metal, tools dangling from them—copper wires, wrenches smoothed by use, vials of thick, iridescent liquid harvested from the weeping seams of their machines.  

They do not speak in words as we know them. Instead, they communicate in a language of hums, clicks, and the occasional wet, guttural syllable—sounds that mimic the machinery they maintain. When they must convey something complex, they press their palms to the metal walls of their structures, letting the vibrations carry meaning through the network of pipes and conduits that lace the settlement.  

Their society is built around the *Great Engine*, a monolithic structure half-buried in the marsh, its pistons rising and falling with the slow inevitability of a heartbeat. They tend to it ceaselessly, adjusting valves, replacing corroded parts, whispering to it in their resonant tongue. They believe—no, they *know*—that the Engine is alive, that it dreams, and that its dreams shape the land around them.  

They do not eat, not in the traditional sense. Instead, they press their mouths to certain nodes in the metalwork, letting brackish fluid seep into their throats, sustaining them. They sleep in shifts, curled in alcoves where the heat of the machinery keeps them warm, their bodies twitching in time with the Engine’s rhythm.  

They have no concept of time as outsiders measure it. There is only the Pulse, the endless cycle of condensation and evaporation, the slow grind of gears, the gradual growth of the black-barked trees. They do not age so much as they *erode*, their bodies becoming more like the landscape with each passing year—until, at last, they become part of it, their bones sinking into the marsh, their voices joining the hum of the machines.  

They do not fear death. They fear only the silence of the Engine.  

And so they keep it running. Forever.
