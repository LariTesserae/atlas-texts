**What Lives Here:**  

A solitary figure moves through the damp haze—not quite human, not entirely machine. It is a thing of rust and sinew, its body a patchwork of corroded metal and slick, algae-streaked flesh. Its limbs are mismatched: one arm ends in a skeletal hand, fingers fused with frayed copper wire; the other is a crude prosthetic, scavenged from some long-abandoned tool, its joints clicking softly with each motion. Its face is obscured by a mask of welded scrap, the eyeholes dark and unreadable, but moisture beads along its surface like sweat.  

It does not breathe, not in the way living things do, but its chest cavity emits a low, rhythmic wheeze—a broken pump cycling air through corroded tubes. It moves with purpose, though its purpose is unclear. Perhaps it is searching. Perhaps it is waiting.  

It is not alone.  

Beneath the standing water, things stir—pale, segmented creatures with too many legs, skittering just below the surface. They are blind, their bodies adapted to the dark and the wet, feeding on the rot that clings to the edges of this place. They avoid the figure, sensing the danger in its presence, but they watch. Always watching.  

And then there are the whispers. Not voices, exactly—more like the echoes of voices, caught in the hum of the machinery, reverberating through the pipes. Fragments of words, half-formed pleas, the ghost of a laugh. They come and go with the mist, impossible to grasp, impossible to ignore.  

This is a place of in-between. The figure is neither alive nor dead; the creatures are neither insect nor machine; the voices are neither memory nor hallucination. Everything here exists in flux, caught in the slow decay of industry and the relentless press of the damp.  

Stay too long, and you might become part of it.
