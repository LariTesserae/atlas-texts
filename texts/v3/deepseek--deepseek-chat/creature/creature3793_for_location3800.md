### **The Hollow Dwellers of Vhalei**  

They do not call themselves by any name you would recognize. If pressed, they might gesture to the water, the ruins, the hum of the old machines—meaning, perhaps, *those who remain*, or *the ones who listen*.  

They are tall, slender, their skin a mottled gray-green, blending seamlessly with the damp stone and creeping moss. Their eyes are large, lidless, reflecting the dim light in fractured patterns—like the surface of the canals at dusk. They do not blink. They do not need to.  

They move without sound, their bare feet leaving no prints on the slick stone. Their fingers are long, too long, tipped with translucent nails that flex like cartilage. When they touch the walls of the ruins, the dormant circuitry flickers in response, casting ghostly blue shadows across their faces.  

They do not speak, not in any way that requires breath. Instead, they *hum*—low, resonant vibrations that ripple through the water, through the bones of the city. The sound is picked up and amplified by the ruins, echoing back in distorted fragments. They understand each other perfectly. Outsiders hear only whispers, half-formed words that dissolve before meaning takes shape.  

They eat, though not in any way that would be familiar. They press their palms to the glowing lichen, absorbing its light, its energy. They cup the brackish canal water in their hands and let it seep into their skin. Sometimes, they kneel beside the fissures in the earth, dipping their fingers into the iridescent veins, drawing out something that shimmers like liquid metal. It coats their tongues, their throats, and they swallow without sound.  

They do not sleep. They *pause*. They stand motionless for hours, days, their bodies merging with the ruins, their hum harmonizing with the buried engines. In these moments, they dream—not in images, but in echoes, in the reverberations of the city’s lost memories.  

They are not afraid of outsiders, but they do not welcome them. They watch, silent, from the shadows of half-collapsed archways. If approached, they might tilt their heads, as if listening to a voice only they can hear. Then, without warning, they are gone—vanished into the water, the moss, the hum of the machines.  

They are waiting. For what, even they do not know. Only that the city is not yet done with them.
