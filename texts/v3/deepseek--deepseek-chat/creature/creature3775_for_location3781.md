Here is what lives in the Glass Steppes of Eternity:  

### **The Fractured Ones**  

They are not born, nor do they die. They simply *are*—shapes woven from the cracks in the obsidian, shadows given slow, deliberate motion. Their forms are never fixed; they ripple like liquid, stretching and collapsing at the edges. Sometimes they resemble creatures—a many-jointed limb here, a suggestion of a face there—but these impressions dissolve as quickly as they form. They do not walk so much as *flow*, gliding across the glass as if pulled by unseen currents.  

They are silent. If they have voices, they do not use them. Instead, they communicate in shifts of darkness, pooling together in intricate patterns before dispersing again. Their purpose is unclear. They do not hunt. They do not build. But they watch. Always, they watch.  

Some say they are the remnants of whatever once lived here, worn down to echoes. Others believe they are the land itself, dreaming.  

### **The Singing Spires**  

The towering glass monoliths are not entirely inanimate. When the rare wind stirs, they resonate, emitting low, mournful tones that shiver through the steppes. The sound lingers, humming in the bones of those who hear it, as if the spires are trying to remember a language long forgotten.  

Sometimes, the Fractured Ones gather at their bases, swaying in time with the vibrations. Sometimes, the spires *answer*—their surfaces flickering with movement, as though something stirs within.  

### **The Watcher in the City**  

The distant glass metropolis is not empty. Something dwells there—or perhaps *is* the city itself. A presence vast and slow, its thoughts unfolding over centuries. It does not move, but its awareness stretches across the steppes, a weight felt more than seen.  

Those who approach (though none can say how they got there) speak of reflections in the towers—not their own, but figures just out of focus, moving behind the glass like memories. The Watcher does not interfere. It only observes. And waits.  

### **The Hollow Wind**  

It comes rarely, a breath from nowhere, stirring the dust. It carries no scent, no warmth, no chill—only a sense of *absence*, as if something has just departed. When it passes through the spires, their song changes. The notes grow sharper, almost urgent. The Fractured Ones still, then shudder, as if recalling something terrible.  

Some believe the wind is a messenger. Others think it is the steppes exhaling, reminding itself that time still moves, however imperceptibly.  

### **The Glass-Eaters**  

Not all inhabitants are shadows or echoes. There are things that crawl—small, jagged creatures with bodies like shards of obsidian, clicking and shifting as they move. They have no eyes, no mouths, yet they *consume*, grinding the glass beneath them into finer and finer dust, as if searching for something buried deep.  

The Fractured Ones ignore them. The spires do not sing for them. Even the wind avoids them.  

They are the only things here that seem to have a purpose.  

And perhaps that is the most unsettling thing of all.
