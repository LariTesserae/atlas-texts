**The Saltborn**  

They are not creatures of flesh, not entirely. Their bodies are sheathed in a brittle, translucent carapace—salt and silica fused into jagged plates, shifting like cracked ice with every movement. Limbs too long, too many-jointed, taper into hooked points, adapted not for grasping but for *anchoring*, for clinging to the unstable crust of their world. Their faces—if they can be called faces—are smooth, featureless planes, save for the occasional fracture where something dark and liquid seeps through, evaporating almost instantly into the parched air.  

They do not speak. They *vibrate*. A low, resonant hum thrums through their bodies, communicating in pulses that travel through the salt itself, a language of tremors and pauses. To an outsider, it would sound like the land settling—another whisper in the endless silence.  

They move in slow, deliberate migrations, following invisible currents in the salt, paths known only to them. Some say they are drawn to the veins of darker matter that thread the plain, that they feed on it, or worship it, or perhaps are slowly being consumed by it. Others claim they are the last custodians of the buried structures, tending to the dissolving ruins in rituals that make no sense to anyone but them.  

They are not hostile. Not exactly. But they are *aware*. When something foreign enters their domain—a traveler, a lost machine, a storm-born scrap of another world—they gather. Not to attack, not to welcome, but to *observe*. They circle at a distance, their faceless heads tilted just so, their hums shifting into a new, questioning rhythm. And then, inevitably, they lose interest. The intruder is left alone, abandoned to the salt and the stillness.  

No one knows how long they have been here. No one knows if they were born of this place or if they came from somewhere else, adapting until they became indistinguishable from the land itself. But they endure. They persist.  

And when the ground shudders, when the hidden energies beneath the salt surge and flicker, they do not flee. They press their bodies to the earth, as if listening.  

As if waiting.
