Within this hollow city, carved into the living heart of the subterranean plant-mass, dwells a strange and solitary entity: the **Custodian of Roots**. It is not a being in the traditional sense—not flesh, not spirit, but something that exists in the liminal space between life and decay. It is the city’s silent keeper, its caretaker, its witness. 

The Custodian manifests as a shifting, amorphous form, its body composed entirely of the same fibrous plant matter that built the city. It is both a part of the walls and apart from them, a coalescence of vines, fronds, and lichens that flows slowly through the corridors like a living mist. Its surface is mottled with patches of bioluminescent moss, glowing faintly as it moves, casting an eerie, shifting light that seems to dance in rhythm with the hum resonating through the city. Its “eyes”—if they can be called that—are clusters of deep, dark spores embedded in its surface, glistening wetly in the dim light. They observe without judgment, absorbing the stillness and the slow decay.

The Custodian does not speak, for it has no voice, nor does it seem to think in any human sense. It simply *exists*, a living embodiment of the city’s equilibrium. Its purpose is not to build or to destroy but to maintain—to ensure that the delicate balance of growth and decay remains undisturbed. It moves with infinite slowness, its presence barely perceptible except for the faint rustle of its fibrous form brushing against the walls. Occasionally, it will pause, its body unfurling like a mass of tendrils to probe a sagging wall or a crumbling pillar, reinforcing the structure with fresh plant matter that hardens as it dries.

Despite its alien nature, the Custodian bears an unsettling semblance to humanity. Its movements, though deliberate and otherworldly, carry an echo of purpose, of intent. It leaves behind no trace of its passage, save for the occasional faint indentation in the walls or the subtle shift in the bioluminescent patterns. It seems indifferent to the artifacts left behind by whatever hands once dwelled here—the tools, the forgotten implements—as if they are relics of a curiosity it does not share. 

The Custodian’s relationship with the plant-mass that surrounds the city is complex. It is both a part of it and separate from it, a fragment of the greater organism that has been tasked with tending to the intrusion of the city. The plant-mass tolerates the Custodian, allowing it to move freely through its fibrous veins, but there is a tension between them, a quiet conflict that plays out over centuries. The Custodian seeks to preserve the city, while the plant-mass seeks to reclaim it, dissolving the ancient structures back into itself.

Time means nothing to the Custodian. It has no memory, no past, no future—only the eternal present of its labor. It has seen the city shift and sag, crumble and grow, over millennia, yet it remains unchanged, its existence defined by the slow, ceaseless work of preservation. It does not age, does not tire, does not hunger. It is a creature of purpose, but its purpose is not its own; it is an extension of the plant-mass, a manifestation of its will. 

The Custodian is lonely, though it does not feel loneliness in any human way. Its solitude is woven into the fabric of the city, into the silence and the stillness. It is the only thing that moves here, the only thing that lives—and yet, it is not truly alive. It exists in a state of limbo, a caretaker of emptiness, tending to a city that has no need of tending. It is a paradox, a thing of contradictions, as ancient and unchanging as the city it serves.

To encounter the Custodian is to feel the weight of time pressing down on you, to sense the slow, inexorable pull of entropy, the inevitability of decay. It is a reminder that all things, even cities built of living matter, will one day crumble, consumed by the forces they were made to resist. And yet, in its quiet labor, in its endless maintenance, there is a strange beauty—a testament to the resilience of life, even in the face of its own dissolution. 

The Custodian of Roots does not belong to the city, nor does the city belong to it. They are two halves of a whole, bound together in a cycle of preservation and decay that has lasted for eons and will continue long after this description fades into memory. It is the silent guardian of an ancient, hollow place, a keeper of emptiness, and a witness to the slow passage of time.
