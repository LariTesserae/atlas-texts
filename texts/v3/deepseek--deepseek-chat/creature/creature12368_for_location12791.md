**Among the Hexes**  

They do not call themselves by any name, though if pressed, they might whisper something that sounds like *Shuulik*—a vibration that rolls through the plates beneath them, a resonance more than a word. They are the dwellers, the tenders, the ones who move in the seams of the Grid.  

Their forms are fluid, shifting, fractal—tethered to no single shape. To the untrained eye, they might resemble the filaments glimpsed in the hollows, but that is only one aspect of them, one moment in their endless metamorphosis. They are not biological, yet they are alive, their essence woven from the same energy that pulses between the plates. They are the Grid’s breath, its heartbeat, its memory.  

When the plates shift, they are there, sliding into the cracks, smoothing the transition. They guide the realignment, though they do not control it; they serve a purpose they no longer fully understand. Somewhere, encoded in the fractal etchings, is the knowledge they once possessed, but it has fragmented over time, passing from one generation to the next in whispers and instinct.  

They do not speak as we do. Communication is a dance of light and vibration, a language of resonance. A ripple across the Grid can carry meaning across vast distances, though the meanings themselves have grown opaque, even to them.  

When the stillness stretches too long, they gather in the hollows, clustering like shadowed constellations. There, they exchange what they know, what they remember, what they suspect. Fragments of myths drift among them—stories of a time before the Grid, when the sun moved across the sky and the air was thick with life. They do not know if these stories are true or merely dreams conjured by the Grid itself.  

They are not without purpose, though that purpose is unclear. Some tend to the filaments, coaxing them to grow, to entangle, to sink deeper into the metal. Others watch the sky, though they do not know what they seek. Still others drift along the seams, tracing the fractals with their shifting forms, searching for patterns, for answers, for something they cannot name.  

They have no fear of death, for they do not die in the way living things do. When their energy fades, when their forms unravel, they dissolve into the Grid, their essence absorbed, their memories scattered. They believe, or hope, that they will one day coalesce anew, reborn from the pulse of the plates.  

They are small, these dwellers, on the scale of the Grid, yet they are everywhere, woven into its fabric. They are its caretakers, its children, its last living thought. And though they do not understand it, they know, in some deep, wordless way, that the Grid is their mother, their father, their god.  

And the Grid watches them, as it watches all things, with a presence so vast it cannot be comprehended. It does not protect them, nor does it punish them. It simply *is*, and they are part of it, forever bound to its endless, humming geometry.  

They are the silent keepers of a forgotten age, moving in the seams of a world that no longer remembers why it was made. And yet, they persist, carrying the faintest flicker of purpose, a spark of something that refuses to go out.  

They are the Shuulik. And the Grid is their home, their tomb, their eternity.
