In the Hanging Gardens of Xol-Narai, the dwellers are as much a part of the city as the vines and arches themselves. They are the **Lumenesti**, a people who seem to have emerged from the very fabric of the place, their existence intertwined with its rhythms. Their forms are slender and willowy, their skin a soft, translucent green-gold that catches the faint light filtering through the canopy. Their eyes are large and dark, pupils like pools of ink that reflect the world with a calm, unhurried gaze. They are tall, their movements fluid, as if they are not walking but gliding, their feet barely touching the fibrous paths beneath them.  

The Lumenesti do not speak; instead, they communicate through a language of gestures, subtle shifts of their long-fingered hands, and the soft rustling of leaves that seems to accompany their presence. Their faces are serene, almost mask-like, betraying no emotion yet radiating an undercurrent of profound connection to their surroundings. They are not builders or architects in the traditional sense—instead, they are guides, coaxers of growth. Their touch seems to encourage the vines and roots to form new shapes, to thicken and strengthen, to weave themselves into the structures the city requires.  

They live in harmony with the winged fauna, the **Aerisae**, who drift through the Gardens like living whispers. The Aerisae are spindly creatures, their translucent wings veined with faintly glowing patterns that shift and shimmer as they move. They perch on the Lumenesti’s shoulders or hover around them like attendants, their presence a quiet exchange of understanding. The Lumenesti share a symbiotic relationship with these creatures, who seem to act as both companions and scouts, drifting ahead to guide their path or gathering in clusters to signal unseen changes in the Gardens.  

The Lumenesti dress sparingly, if at all, their bodies adorned with living ornaments—garlands of delicate flowers that bloom perpetually, necklaces of seed pods that click softly as they move, and crowns of moss that seem to grow directly from their skin. Their connection to the Gardens is so profound that it is difficult to tell where they end and the city begins. When they rest, they do so on beds of broad leaves that curl around them like cradles, and when they need sustenance, they drink from sap-filled blossoms or pluck fruits that hang like lanterns from the vines.  

Their rituals are subtle and almost imperceptible. They gather in quiet groves, where they press their hands to the living walls of the city, their fingers tracing the patterns of growth as if reading a story written in the fibers. Occasionally, they gather in circles and extend their arms upward, their gestures mirrored by the plants around them, which seem to stretch and thicken in response. These gatherings are not marked by celebration or frenzy, but by a deep, shared understanding—a communion with the Gardens that is both sacred and mundane.  

The Lumenesti do not age in the way other beings do. Their lives are measured in the slow cadence of the city’s growth, and their passing is as gentle as a leaf falling from a vine. When one of them dies, their body is returned to the Gardens, placed within a cradle of roots that slowly consume them, their essence becoming part of the city’s fabric. In this way, they are eternal, their presence lingering in the creak of the arches, the rustle of the leaves, and the faint scent of resin that permeates the air.  

To encounter the Lumenesti is to witness a people who are not separate from their world but inseparable from it. They are not rulers or caretakers—they are the Gardens, and the Gardens are them. Their existence is a quiet testament to a life lived in symbiosis, where time is measured not in hours or years but in the slow, deliberate pulse of growth. They are the embodiment of patience, of harmony, of a world that breathes in unison.  

And if there is an answer to the question—*Who grew this place?*—it is perhaps this: the Lumenesti did not grow Xol-Narai. They grew *with* it, and in doing so, became something greater than themselves.
