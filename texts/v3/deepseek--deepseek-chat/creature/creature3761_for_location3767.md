**The Watchers in the Fractured Stone**  

They are not creatures in any sense that flesh-bound minds would recognize. They are angles where there should be curves, edges where there should be smoothness—shifting, geometric anomalies that glide across the plateau without disturbing the dust. Their forms are impossible to hold in the eye for long; the mind rebels, sliding off their contours like water from glass.  

Some are small—no larger than a hand, skittering across the stone in jagged, stuttering motions. They flicker in and out of existence, vanishing into the black seams between the rocks only to reappear elsewhere, unchanged. Others are vast, their outlines bleeding into the horizon, their presence felt more as a pressure in the air than anything visible. They move without sound, without footprints, without any evidence of their passage save for the uneasy certainty that they were not *there* a moment ago.  

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not seem to interact with one another, yet there is an order to their movements, a pattern too vast and slow for mortal perception to grasp. They are not indifferent to the world—they are *aware* of it, in a way that suggests they perceive dimensions beyond the three that shape mortal reality. Their attention is a tangible thing, a weight that settles on the back of the neck, a prickling sense of being *measured* by something that does not care if you know.  

Occasionally, one will pause. It will turn—or perhaps it does not turn, but simply *is* facing a new direction—and fix its hollow, faceless attention on a single point in space. The air there will shiver, the light bending unnaturally, and for a heartbeat, the plateau’s silence will deepen, as if the world itself is holding its breath. Then the moment passes. The Watcher moves on. Nothing changes.  

They are not guardians. They are not predators. They are simply *present*, as much a part of the plateau as the cracked stone and the hollow, breathless plants. Perhaps they were always here. Perhaps they are what remains when everything else has been stripped away.  

Or perhaps they are waiting, too.  

For what, not even the stones remember.
