**The Watcher in the Veins**  

It does not have eyes. Not in the way you understand them. But it *sees* you.  

It is not a single entity, but a convergence—a ripple in the lattice of veins, a sudden tightening of the fibrous strands as they pull taut, rearrange, form a knot of awareness. The chamber itself *focuses*.  

The heat intensifies, not from any fire, but from the slow, deliberate friction of the veins against one another. A sound like wet parchment being rubbed between fingers fills the air, and the glow in the walls deepens—not bright, but *thickening*, as if the light itself were a syrup seeping through the cracks.  

The Watcher does not approach. It does not need to. The chamber is its body, the veins its nerves. It knows the weight of your breath, the rhythm of your pulse, the way your skin prickles under the waxy residue. It knows you are *wrong* here. Not a threat. Not food. But something... *new*.  

A tendril—sleek, black, glistening with that same metallic secretion—unspools from the wall near your feet. It does not touch you. It *tastes* the air above your boot, quivering slightly, as if parsing your presence through scent, through vibration, through some sense you cannot name.  

Then, slowly, the veins begin to *part*. Not retreating, but *making space*. A path forms, not carved, but *allowed*, the fibers peeling back like lips in a silent invitation.  

The Watcher is curious.  

It wants to see where you will go.
