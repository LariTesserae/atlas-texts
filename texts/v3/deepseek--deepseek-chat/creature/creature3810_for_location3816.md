### **The Hollowbone Choir**  

At the edge of the Weeping Plateau, where the marsh gives way to jagged limestone teeth, the earth splits open into a labyrinth of sinkholes and caverns. This is the Hollowbone Choir—a cathedral of erosion, its walls pocked with tunnels like the honeycombed marrow of some long-dead titan.  

The air here is cooler, drier, but no less alive. It hums with the echoes of wind threading through the caverns, a low, mournful resonance that swells and fades like breath. The stone is porous, brittle, and when the wind is right, the entire network of caves *sings*—a chorus of whistles, drones, and keening harmonics that shift with the pressure of the outside world.  

The inhabitants are creatures of vibration and echo. Blind, pale bats with wings like stretched parchment cling to the ceilings, their echolocation clicks weaving into the natural acoustics. Their calls are not just for navigation—they *compose*, altering their rhythms to harmonize with the caves, creating fleeting, ever-changing songs.  

Deeper in, where the light never reaches, the stone itself seems to move. Colonies of bioluminescent fungi cling to the walls, their faint blue-green glow pulsing in slow waves, reacting to sound. They feed on the vibrations, spreading or retracting their tendrils in response to certain frequencies. Some say they are not just reacting—they are *listening*.  

And then there are the Hollow Singers.  

Tall, gaunt figures with elongated limbs and skin like polished bone, they move through the caves in silence, their mouths sewn shut with sinew and thorn. They do not speak—they *resonate*. When they press their hands to the cave walls, the stone trembles, amplifying their internal vibrations into deep, subsonic hymns. The bats scatter in their wake, the fungi flare brighter, and the caves answer in kind, their song shifting to accommodate this new voice.  

No one knows if the Hollow Singers were once human, or if they are something older, something that has always lived here, waiting for the right frequency to wake them. They do not eat, do not sleep—they only sing, and the caves remember every note.  

Visitors who stay too long find their own bones humming in sympathy. Some swear they can hear whispers in the wind, words half-formed, as if the caves are trying to speak.  

The Hollowbone Choir does not welcome. It *absorbs*. And one day, even the wind will forget which songs were its own.
