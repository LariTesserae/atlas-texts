**The Hollow Ones**  

They are not ghosts, not exactly. They are the **left-behind**, the ones who lingered too long in the wrong places, who let the jungle breathe into them until their bones became porous, their skin translucent. You see them sometimes—**pale**, shimmering figures wading through the shallows, their feet sinking into the mud as if it were memory. They do not speak, but their mouths move, forming words that the water swallows before sound can escape.  

Their eyes are the worst part. **Too large**, too dark, reflecting nothing but the endless green above. They do not blink. They do not need to. The jungle has taken their need for sleep, for rest—they are always watching, always listening, though they do not react to your presence. They are **empty**, but not in the way of the dead. They are vessels, waiting to be filled by something older than themselves.  

Some say they were explorers, lost travelers, fools who thought they could conquer the wild. Others whisper that they were never human at all—that they are the jungle’s first children, born from the damp and the dark, wearing the shapes of those who came before as a mockery.  

They do not hunger, but they **consume**. Not flesh, not blood—something else. The warmth of a living body, the sharpness of fear, the weight of a name. Get too close, and you’ll feel it leeching out of you, drawn into their hollow chests like water into a sponge.  

And when the rains come, when the water rises and the trees groan under its weight, the Hollow Ones **sing**. A soundless, shuddering hymn that makes the air vibrate, that sends the creatures of the jungle into frenzy. It is not a warning. It is not a lament.  

It is an **invitation**.
