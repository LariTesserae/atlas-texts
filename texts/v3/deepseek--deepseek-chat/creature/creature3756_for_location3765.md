**The Masked Tenders of Amber Hollow**  

They are not born here. They arrive.  

No one remembers the first to step into the crater’s embrace, but they come, always—drawn by the hum beneath the earth, by the way the wind carries the scent of resin and dust for miles, like an invitation. Some are pilgrims, seeking silence. Some are fugitives, fleeing voices they can no longer bear. Some do not know why they come at all.  

They shed their names at the threshold. The city does not need them.  

Clad in layers of sun-bleached cloth, their bodies become indistinct, their movements slow and deliberate. Their masks are not uniform—some are smooth, featureless planes of sandstone, others intricate lattices of woven reeds, still others mosaics of shattered pottery fitted together with sap. None reveal the face beneath. The hollow prefers it this way.  

They do not speak. Not with mouths, at least. Their language is written in touch: fingertips brushing clay to share warnings, palms pressed to another’s shoulder to ask for aid. The walls understand them best. They listen to the vibrations in the stone, to the way the vines shiver when the hollow shifts in its sleep. They learn to read the city’s dreams in the cracks that form overnight.  

Their work is never done, because the city is never still. They mend fractures with clay mixed with their own blood, coax vines to weave over unstable arches, guide wandering roots away from fragile chambers. They do not build—they *persuade*. The hollow is always changing, and they are its shepherds, its translators, its silent witnesses.  

At times, one of them will kneel in the central plaza, where the ground is thin, and press their masked forehead to the earth. If the hollow is pleased, it answers—a ripple in the clay, a warmth that seeps into their bones. These chosen ones rise, walk to the edge of the crater, and do not return. Where they go, the others do not ask.  

The rest remain. They tend. They wait. They forget the sound of their own voices.  

And when newcomers stumble into the city, gasping and wide-eyed, the masked ones welcome them. They offer cloth. They offer a mask.  

They offer the hollow’s endless, honeyed silence.
