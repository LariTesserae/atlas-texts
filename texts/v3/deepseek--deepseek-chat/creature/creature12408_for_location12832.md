**What Lives Here:**  

The cavern is not uninhabited—though what dwells within it is not easily seen.  

The **Silent Watchers** are the first inhabitants you sense. They are not creatures of flesh, but of shifting mist and shadow, coalescing briefly in the beams of light before dissolving again. They move with eerie synchronicity, drifting along the currents of air, their forms flickering between humanoid and something far less definable. They do not speak, but their presence hums with a low, resonant frequency, like the distant vibration of stone settling deep beneath the earth.  

Then there are the **Rootkin**, beings woven from the vegetation itself. Their bodies are tangled masses of vines, moss, and fungal growth, moving with deliberate slowness, their limbs extending and retracting like tendrils seeking purchase. Their faces—if they can be called faces—are knots of fibrous matter, hollows where eyes might be, openings that pulse faintly as if breathing. They tend to the cavern’s flora with an almost reverent precision, pruning, guiding, sometimes consuming excess growth. They do not seem hostile, but they do not acknowledge outsiders either. They simply *are*, as much a part of the cavern as the air or the stone.  

Occasionally, something darker stirs—the **Hollowborn**. These are the true predators of the depths, skeletal figures draped in tattered shrouds of lichen and decaying leaves. They move in silence, their elongated limbs folding unnaturally as they stalk the edges of the light. Their hunger is not for flesh, but for warmth, for sound, for the intangible traces of life. They are drawn to movement, to voices, to the heat of living things. To encounter one is to feel the air grow thin, the cold deepen, as if the very essence of vitality is being leeched away.  

And beneath it all, there is the **Cavern-Mind**—not a singular entity, but a presence woven into the roots, the mist, the stone. It does not think as mortals think; it *pulses*, it *shifts*, it *grows*. It is the whisper in the vines, the slow drip of water shaping rock over centuries, the unseen force that guides the Silent Watchers and the Rootkin alike. It is neither benevolent nor malevolent—it simply *is*, vast and ancient and indifferent.  

This is a place where life does not conform to the rules of the surface world. Here, existence is slow, patient, cyclical. The inhabitants do not live as we understand living—they persist, they adapt, they become part of the cavern just as the cavern becomes part of them.  

To stay here long is to risk forgetting that there was ever anything else.
