### **The Hollowed Ones**  

They move through the drowned city like shadows cast by unseen light—human-shaped, but not human. Their bodies are hollow, their skin translucent, stretched thin over empty spaces where organs should be. When they pass through beams of filtered sunlight, their forms shimmer like jellyfish, revealing veins of glowing fluid pulsing beneath the surface.  

They do not speak, but they hum—a low, resonant vibration that travels through the metal walkways and makes the glass tremble. Their mouths are sealed shut, fused by some long-ago transformation, yet they seem to communicate through shifts in pitch, in rhythm. They are not blind, but their eyes are black voids, absorbing light rather than reflecting it.  

They do not eat, yet they gather. In their hands (if they can be called hands—more like elongated, boneless appendages) they carry objects: rusted tools, shards of glass, fragments of machinery. They arrange these in patterns on the walkways, in alcoves, along the edges of canals. No one knows what the patterns mean. Perhaps they are prayers. Perhaps they are warnings.  

They do not sleep. They stand motionless for hours, days, suspended in doorways or half-submerged in the water, their hollow bodies buoyant. When they move again, it is with purpose, as if responding to some silent call.  

They are not alive, not in the way we understand life. But they are not dead, either. They are preserved, adapted, part of the city’s slow metabolism. They do not age. They do not rot. They simply *are*, as much a fixture of this place as the iron spires or the glowing moss.  

And sometimes, when the mist thickens and the water grows still, they turn their heads—all at once, in perfect unison—toward something unseen in the depths.  

Then, just as suddenly, they return to their silent work.
