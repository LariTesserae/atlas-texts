In this place, the inhabitants are as much a part of the landscape as the water that seeps through the stone or the crystalline structures that rise from the ground. They are not beings in the traditional sense, but rather manifestations of the environment itself—living, breathing extensions of the cold, wet expanse that defines this world.

At first glance, they are almost indistinguishable from their surroundings. Movement catches your eye—a flicker here, a ripple there—but when you turn to look, you see nothing but rock and water. It’s only as you linger, as you attune yourself to the subtle rhythms of the place, that you begin to notice them. They are fluid, their forms shifting and flowing with the same quiet persistence as the rivulets that carve paths across the plateau. Their bodies are translucent, shimmering faintly in the diffused light, their outlines blurring into the mist and stone. They are not so much seen as felt, a presence that you sense more than observe.

Their shapes are impossible to pin down. At one moment, they might resemble a humanoid figure, tall and slender, their limbs elongated and faintly glowing. The next, they dissolve into something more abstract—a swirl of mist, a pooling of liquid mercury, a cluster of iridescent scales that scatter like confetti in the breeze. They move with a grace that seems to defy gravity, gliding across the ground or rising into the air as if it were water. Their movements are deliberate, purposeful, though their intentions remain obscure.

They are silent, communicating not through sound but through shifts in the environment itself. When they move, the ground seems to respond, the spongy stone rippling faintly beneath their passage. The pools of water shimmer and pulse, as if sharing some unseen conversation. The air grows heavier, denser, when they gather, the mist coalescing into shapes that hover on the edge of recognition. There is an intelligence here, but it is alien, operating on a wavelength that you cannot fully comprehend.

These beings are tied intimately to the landscape, their existence intertwined with the cycles of water, stone, and light. They seem to emerge from the ground itself, their forms coalescing from the wet, porous rock like spirits rising from a primordial soup. When they disappear, they do so seamlessly, dissolving back into the environment as if they were never there at all. It’s unclear whether they have individual identities or if they are part of a collective consciousness, a single entity that expresses itself through countless shifting forms.

They are curious but cautious, their attention often drawn to visitors, though they keep their distance. You might feel their presence as a tingling sensation on your skin, a faint chill that prickles the back of your neck. Occasionally, one will approach, its form undulating softly, its eyes (if you can call them that) reflecting the pale light of the sky. It will study you for a moment, its gaze piercing yet devoid of malice, before fading back into the landscape.

Their relationship with the technology that hums beneath the surface is unclear. Sometimes they seem to interact with it, their forms flowing over the surfaces of the alien buildings or merging with the machines that glide through the air. Other times, they ignore it entirely, as if it is beneath their notice. Perhaps they are its creators, or perhaps they are merely coexisting with it, their purposes as inscrutable as everything else in this place.

They are as much a part of the place’s slow, deliberate unfolding as the weather cycles and the predator-prey dances. They are not separate from the landscape, but an expression of it, a living embodiment of the cold, wet, vast expanse that stretches endlessly in all directions. To encounter them is to glimpse the soul of this place—a soul that is ancient and eternal, yet still in the act of becoming.
