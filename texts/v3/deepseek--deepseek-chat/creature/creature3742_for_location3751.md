### **The People of Vhorth: The Fractured Folk**  

The citizens of Vhorth are as much a part of the city as the stone it rises from. They are a people who have learned to live in the gaps, in the pauses between the city’s breaths. Their lives are shaped by the slow chaos of the mountain’s dreaming, and they carry its echoes in their bones.  

Their skin is pale, almost translucent, as though the mist and the damp have seeped into their flesh. Their hair is often dark, streaked with silver, mimicking the veins of ore that thread through the rock. Their hands are calloused, their fingers stained with the rust and moss of their labor. They move with a deliberate slowness, as though attuned to the city’s rhythm, their footsteps careful and measured, never hurried.  

They speak in whispers, their voices low and gravelly, as though they too are carved from stone. Their language is laced with the sounds of cracking rock, of grinding machinery, of water slipping through fissures. There are words for things that do not exist elsewhere—words for the moment a wall shifts, for the taste of air before a fracture appears, for the silence that falls when the mountain is deep in its dreaming.  

Their clothing is heavy, layered against the chill, made from thick wool and coated in something that repels the damp. Their coats are patched and mended, often dyed in muted shades of gray and green, blending them into the city’s palette. They wear boots of treated leather, soles reinforced with iron, to protect against the sharp edges of the streets.  

Their eyes are their most striking feature. They are wide and watchful, their irises often a strange, shifting gray, like clouds caught in the moment before a storm. Some say the people of Vhorth see through time, that their gaze lingers on the cracks and shifts of the city, glimpsing the past and future as easily as the present.  

They are builders, but not in the way most would understand. They do not construct so much as *coax*—urging the stone to rise, guiding the iron to form supports, allowing the moss to grow where it will. Tools are extensions of their hands, worn smooth with use, their handles etched with symbols that seem to hum with power. Their work is perpetual, never finished, for the city is always changing, always folding into itself.  

The people of Vhorth are communal, bound together by their shared existence in this living, breathing place. They gather in the hollows of the city, where the mist is thinner and the air is warmer, sharing stories and food. Their meals are simple—fungus harvested from the damp crevices, roots dug from the friable soil, meat from the creatures that skulk in the shadows. They drink water drawn from the weeping stone, infused with minerals that leave a metallic tang on the tongue.  

They have rituals, though outsiders would struggle to understand them. When a wall shifts or a doorway appears, they gather to mark it, tracing the new contours with their hands, murmuring words of gratitude. When the mountain dreams loudly, they light candles made from tallow and wax, the flames flickering in the damp air, their light reflected in the shifting gray of their eyes.  

The people of Vhorth are patient, enduring, and deeply strange. They are the heart of the city, its pulse and its breath, living in harmony with its fractures and folds. They are the Fractured Folk, and they are as eternal as the stone itself.
