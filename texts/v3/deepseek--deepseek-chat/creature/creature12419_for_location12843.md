In this cathedral of living architecture, the beings that dwell within are as much a part of the landscape as the structures themselves—perhaps even more so. They are not inhabitants in the traditional sense; they do not merely live here. They *are* here, woven into the fabric of the world like threads in its vast, breathing tapestry.  

### **The Architects**  
At first glance, they might be mistaken for plants—tall, slender figures with limbs that taper into fine, root-like tendrils, their bodies composed of the same fibrous, semi-organic material as the structures around them. But they move, though not in ways that suggest urgency or even intention. Their motions are slow, deliberate, more akin to the sway of branches in a breeze than the purposeful strides of animals.  

They are the sculptors of this place, though "sculpting" implies a separation between artist and medium that does not exist here. They do not build—they *grow*. Their fingertips secrete a viscous, luminous sap that hardens into new structures when pressed against existing ones, extending the latticework of the world in slow, patient increments. They do not speak, at least not in any language of sound. Instead, they communicate through subtle shifts in the bioluminescence that pulses beneath their skin, a silent exchange of meaning that ripples through the air like the faintest hum of electricity.  

### **The Drifters**  
Smaller, more numerous, these creatures are the ephemeral denizens of the cathedral. They resemble floating seeds or delicate, winged insects, their bodies translucent and glowing with the same soft green light that suffuses the environment. They move in slow, drifting currents, riding unseen eddies in the air, their paths tracing intricate, invisible patterns.  

Some are no larger than a fingertip, while others stretch to the length of an arm, their forms shifting as they pass through patches of thicker air. They do not seem to eat, nor do they appear to have any clear purpose beyond existing in harmony with the space around them. Occasionally, they alight upon the Architects, their bodies fusing momentarily with the larger beings before detaching again, as if exchanging some unspoken piece of knowledge.  

### **The Watchers**  
These are the most unsettling of the cathedral's inhabitants—not because they are hostile, but because they defy easy comprehension. They are not solid, not entirely. Their forms flicker at the edges of vision, sometimes appearing as reflections in the water, other times as distortions in the air itself. They might be mistaken for shadows if shadows could move independently of light.  

They do not interact with the other beings. They simply observe, their presence marked only by a faint chill in the air, a subtle warping of the light around them. When one looks directly at them, they dissolve, only to reappear elsewhere moments later. It is unclear whether they are entities at all or merely manifestations of the cathedral’s own awareness—echoes of a consciousness woven into the very fabric of the place.  

### **Symbiosis**  
The true nature of this world is not one of hierarchy or predation, but of seamless integration. The Architects shape the environment, the Drifters move through it, and the Watchers linger at its edges—yet none exist in isolation. The water that weeps from the structures nourishes the roots of the living lattice. The Drifters, when they die, dissolve into the air, their essence becoming part of the ambient glow. Even the Watchers, elusive as they are, seem to serve some inscrutable function, their presence ensuring that the cathedral remains in perfect, eerie equilibrium.  

To witness them is to understand that this is not a world where life happens *within* a place. It is a world where life *is* the place—where every breath, every movement, every shift of light is part of an endless, silent symphony.
