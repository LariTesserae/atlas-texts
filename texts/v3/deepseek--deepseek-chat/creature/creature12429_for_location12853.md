### **The Weeping Plateau**  

**Who Lives Here:**  

They call themselves the **Drowned Echoes**, though they do not speak—not in any way that can be heard. They are the remnants of those who once tried to make the plateau their home, their bodies long since dissolved into the weeping stone, their voices trapped in the endless trickle of water.  

They exist as reflections in the pools, as ripples that move against the current, as shadows that linger too long on the slick rock. Their faces surface in the water—pale, distorted, mouths open as if in mid-scream—but when you reach to touch them, they dissolve into nothing. They are not ghosts, not exactly. They are more like stains, impressions left behind by lives that the plateau has absorbed.  

Some say they were a people who worshipped the water, who believed it carried the voices of their ancestors. Others whisper that they were prisoners, exiled to this place where time does not move, where the only escape was to let the stone claim them. Whatever they were, they are now part of the plateau’s slow decay, their memories seeping out with the mineral-laden tears of the cliffs.  

They do not interact with the living—not directly. But sometimes, if you stand too long in the mist, you will feel fingers brush against your skin, cold as the water that drips from above. Sometimes, if you listen too closely to the endless murmur of the falls, you will hear words—not spoken, but remembered, echoing up from the depths of the stone.  

And if you stay too long, you will see your own face in the water. And it will not look back at you the way it should.  

The Drowned Echoes do not leave. They cannot. The plateau has them now. And one day, it will have you too.
