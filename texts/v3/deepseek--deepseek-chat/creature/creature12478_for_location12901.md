**The Hollowborn**  

They are not creatures of flesh, not truly. They are born of the marsh’s weeping, shaped from the slow seep of black water and the sigh of rotting reeds. Their bodies are translucent, like water held in a trembling hand, their forms shifting with every ripple of the mist. Limbs too long, fingers too many, they drag themselves through the shallows on bellies that never dry, leaving behind trails of slick, silvery slime that glistens before dissolving into the marsh.  

Their faces are hollows—not eyes, not mouths, just shallow depressions where features should be, as if the marsh itself forgot to carve them. Yet they see. They hunger. When they move, it is not with steps, but with a slow, liquid unfolding, limbs unspooling like tendrils of fog before coiling back into something resembling a shape.  

They do not speak, but the marsh speaks for them. The croak of a frog becomes a shuddering moan. The drip of water from a cattail sharpens into a wet, clicking whisper. They mimic, imperfectly, the sounds of the world around them—not to communicate, but to lure.  

They feed on warmth. The heat of a living thing pulled into the water is a feast, a flicker of vitality in their cold, damp world. They do not kill quickly. They press close, their bodies leaching the heat slowly, savoring the fading pulse until all that remains is another hollow thing, bloated and still, sinking into the peat.  

Sometimes, when the distant machinery groans loud enough to shake the water, the Hollowborn twitch. Their forms ripple, as if disturbed. For a moment, they almost remember something—something before the marsh, before the weeping. Then the sound fades, and they forget again.  

They are not evil. They are simply wet, and cold, and hungry.  

And the marsh is very, very large.
