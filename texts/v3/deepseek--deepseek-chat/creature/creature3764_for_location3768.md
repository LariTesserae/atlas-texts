**The Weeping Plateau**  

The air here is thick with moisture, clinging to skin and stone alike. The plateau rises sharply from the lowlands, its edges softened by constant weeping—water seeps from the rock faces in slow, glistening rivulets, as if the land itself is sweating under the relentless heat. The stone is young, still sharp in places, its fractures fresh and unweathered. Yet already, the water has begun its work, carving shallow channels that glisten under the muted light.  

The vegetation is lush but sparse, scattered in pockets where the water collects. Strange, bulbous plants with translucent leaves swell with stored moisture, their roots gripping the porous rock. Some drip a slow, viscous sap that hardens into amber-like beads on the ground. Others pulse faintly, their inner veins glowing with a bioluminescent hum, casting eerie halos in the perpetual twilight.  

There are structures here—or remnants of them. Slender, skeletal towers of an unknown alloy rise at odd intervals, their surfaces etched with patterns that shift when viewed from different angles. They hum, barely audible, resonating with some unseen energy. Whether they were built or grown is unclear. Perhaps both.  

The fauna is rare but unsettling. A six-legged lizard skitters across the rocks, its hide shimmering like oil on water. Occasionally, something larger moves in the distance—a shadow that flickers at the edge of vision, gone before it can be fully seen. The air carries the faint scent of ozone and wet earth, and the only sounds are the slow drip of water and the occasional, distant chime of metal on stone.  

This is a place of quiet tension, where the land is still forming, still deciding what it will become. The water shapes it, the heat bakes it, and the strange remnants of an advanced—or perhaps alien—presence watch over it all, humming their inscrutable song.  

It feels like standing in the throat of a sleeping giant, waiting for the next breath.
