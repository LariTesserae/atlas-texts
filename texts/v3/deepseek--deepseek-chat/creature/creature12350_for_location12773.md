### **The Inhabitants of the High Chasm of Dripping Voices**  

#### **The Whisper-Spiders**  
Small, many-legged things with bodies like polished obsidian, their limbs long and delicate, tapering into fine, hair-thin filaments. They do not spin webs in the traditional sense—instead, they weave with sound. Their abdomens vibrate at frequencies just beyond human hearing, plucking the moisture-laden air like harp strings, creating faint, resonant hums that ripple through the chasm. These vibrations guide them, allowing them to navigate the dark without sight. They cling to the walls in clusters, their legs twitching in silent conversation, their presence marked only by the occasional shimmer of reflected light on their glassy carapaces.  

They are not predators, not exactly. They feed on the echoes of movement—the tremors of other creatures, the shifting of stone, the slow drip of water. Their bodies absorb these minute disturbances, metabolizing them into energy. When something larger passes by—a wandering traveler, perhaps—the Whisper-Spiders fall still, listening intently, their limbs raised like antennae. If the intruder lingers too long, the spiders will begin to hum in unison, a sound that builds imperceptibly until the very air vibrates with pressure. Few who hear it remain sane.  

#### **The Drip-Tenders**  
Pale, amphibious beings, their skin translucent and slick, stretched thin over elongated, boneless bodies. They move with a slow, undulating grace, clinging to the wet rock with adhesive pads on their fingertips. Their faces are smooth, featureless save for a single vertical slit where a mouth might be—a slit that parts only to drink, never to speak.  

Their purpose is obscure but vital. They tend to the chasm’s slow water, guiding droplets along invisible channels with gentle touches, coaxing them into hollows where they gather into tiny pools. These pools are sacred, though to whom or what, none can say. The Drip-Tenders guard them jealously, their bodies curling protectively around the water when disturbed. They do not attack, but they do not retreat either—they simply wait, unmoving, until the intruder leaves or dies of thirst.  

Some say they are not creatures at all, but manifestations of the chasm itself—its caretakers, its custodians. Their bodies, when broken, dissolve into a thin, milky fluid that seeps back into the stone.  

#### **The One Who Sighs Beneath**  
Something vast lives in the deepest cleft of the chasm, where the walls press so close that even the Whisper-Spiders dare not venture. It is not a beast, not in any conventional sense—it is a presence, a weight, a slow exhalation that stirs the air once every few hours.  

Its body (if it has one) is hidden, but its breath is unmistakable—a deep, shuddering inhale that pulls moisture from the walls, followed by a long, wet exhale that leaves the stone glistening anew. The sound is not loud, but it is inescapable, a bass note beneath all other noises in the chasm.  

The Whisper-Spiders avoid it. The Drip-Tenders sometimes gather near its resting place, pressing their hands to the rock as if in prayer. Some say it is the mountain’s dreaming heart. Others say it is something older, something that was here before the mountain, waiting.  

It does not hunger. It does not hunt. But it knows when something foreign enters its domain. And sometimes, very rarely, it sighs a little deeper—as if in recognition.  

#### **The Last Things**  
Not all inhabitants of the chasm are native. Some are lost. Some are left.  

Foolish explorers who ventured too deep, their bodies now hollowed out by time and damp, their skin grown thin as parchment, their voices stolen by the Whisper-Spiders. They wander, mute and sightless, their fingers tracing the walls as if searching for a way out that no longer exists.  

And then there are the ones who came willingly—the seekers, the devotees, those who heard the chasm’s call and could not resist. They sit in the dark, cross-legged, letting the Drip-Tenders stroke their faces with cold, wet hands. They do not eat. They do not sleep. They listen.  

And eventually, they begin to hum.
