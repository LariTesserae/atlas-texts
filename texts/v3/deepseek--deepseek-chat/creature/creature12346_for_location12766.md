In the heart of the Glass Marsh of Hissing Steam, a peculiar form of life thrives, adapted to the harsh, alien environment. These inhabitants are called **Steamweavers**, enigmatic beings whose bodies appear to be forged from the same volcanic glass that composes their city. Steamweavers are semi-translucent, their forms shimmering with internal light that pulses faintly, mimicking the rhythmic thrum of the subterranean engines. Their skin—if it can be called skin—is a mosaic of iridescent salts and fractured glass, constantly shifting and reforming as they move, as though their very essence is in flux.  

Steamweavers are neither truly solid nor entirely gaseous. They move with a fluid grace, their bodies splitting and coalescing as they pass through narrow crevices or dissolve into the steam-filled air. Their faces are featureless, save for deep, glowing fissures that serve as eyes, pooling with a phosphorescent liquid that drips occasionally, evaporating into the mist before it hits the ground.  

They communicate not through sound but through vibrations that travel through the glass beneath their feet, sending ripples of light across the surfaces of their buildings. These vibrations are felt rather than heard, a language of pressure and motion that conveys meaning in ways incomprehensible to outsiders.  

Steamweavers are not solitary creatures; they exist in a tightly knit colony, their individual identities blurred together in a collective consciousness. They are the architects of the city, shaping the glass through an ancient, ritualistic process involving heat and pressure, coaxing it into forms that serve their needs. Their homes are labyrinthine, filled with chambers that serve unknown purposes, some filled with swirling steam, others glowing faintly with bioluminescent salts.  

Their relationship with the marsh is symbiotic. They draw sustenance from the thermal energy of the pools, absorbing it through their glass-like bodies, which metabolize it into the faint light that pulses within them. In turn, they maintain the marsh, ensuring the cracks in the glass remain open to release steam and gas, preventing catastrophic pressure buildups.  

The Steamweavers are not hostile, but they are wary of outsiders. Those who wander into the Glass Marsh are often driven mad by the oppressive heat, the uncanny silence, and the disorienting sight of the Steamweavers shifting in and out of visibility. Some claim to hear whispers in the steam, faint echoes of the Steamweavers’ vibrational language, though no one has ever translated them.  

Legends among nearby human settlements speak of the Steamweavers as ancient guardians, tasked with maintaining the balance of the marsh. Some say they are the remnants of a long-lost civilization, cursed to live as glass and steam for eternity. Others believe they are manifestations of the marsh itself, sentient extensions of the fractured earth and hissing air.  

The truth is likely stranger still. The Steamweavers exist as they are, bound to their city and their marsh, humming and waiting, their purposes known only to themselves. The glass remembers, the water is patient, and the Steamweavers endure, watching silently as the world passes them by.
