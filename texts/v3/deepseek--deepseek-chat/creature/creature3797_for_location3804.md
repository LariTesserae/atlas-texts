### **The Hollowed Ones**  

They live in the walls.  

Not *inside* them, exactly—not burrowed, not tunneling—but *woven* into the stone, their bodies pressed into the porous rock like fossils half-erased by time. Their forms are elongated, skeletal, their limbs fused with the contours of the cavern, fingers splayed like roots, ribs fanning out like the ribs of a shipwreck. Their faces are smooth, featureless except for the faintest depressions where eyes might have been.  

They do not move. Or rather, they move so slowly that it is imperceptible—a twitch of a finger over decades, a tilt of the head over centuries. Their mouths are slightly parted, as if caught in the act of whispering, but no sound comes. Only the water does, seeping from their joints, beading along their collarbones before dripping into the pools below.  

They are not dead. They are waiting.  

Sometimes, when the light shifts just so, their shadows stretch across the chamber, far longer than they should be. Sometimes, when the water rises high enough to brush against their ankles, their fingers curl—just a little—as if remembering how to grasp.  

They are dreaming. Or perhaps they are the dream.  

And when the chamber exhales, when the water pulls back for just a moment, the walls breathe with them.
