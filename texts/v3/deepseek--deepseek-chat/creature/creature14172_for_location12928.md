In this place, between dream and geometry, between breath and stone, the inhabitants are as much a part of the landscape as the crystalline spires and the glowing stalks. They are not separate from the world—they are its articulation, its voice, its pulse.  

### **The Architects of the Hollow**  
At first glance, they might be mistaken for statues—tall, slender figures with elongated limbs, their bodies composed of the same mineral substance as the city itself. Their skin is smooth, almost glassy, refracting the dim light in prismatic shivers. Their faces are featureless save for shallow depressions where eyes might be, though they do not seem to see as much as *absorb*, taking in the world through some deeper, more tactile sense.  

They move rarely, and when they do, it is with a slow, deliberate grace, as if each motion requires the careful rearrangement of the air itself. They are not builders in the human sense—they do not construct so much as *shape*, their hands passing over surfaces, coaxing the city into new forms with the quiet insistence of a gardener tending to a growing vine.  

They do not speak, but the air around them hums with a low, resonant frequency, a sound just below hearing, as if the hollow itself is their language.  

### **The Skittering Ones**  
Small, many-legged, their carapaces translucent as frosted glass, these creatures move in ceaseless, intricate patterns across the cracked ground. They are not insects, though they share their quickness, their precision. Their bodies are segmented, jointed in ways that suggest an almost mathematical elegance, and their movements leave faint, luminous trails behind them—traces of some unseen energy they harvest from the air.  

They do not seem to fear the Architects. Instead, they weave around their feet, darting into crevices, their presence a kind of punctuation in the stillness. Occasionally, one will pause, its body flickering with a soft internal light, before resuming its endless, purposeful wandering.  

### **The Drifters**  
Above, the winged beings glide in slow, spiraling arcs. Their forms are elongated, their wings vast and membranous, stretched between skeletal fingers of bone or chitin. They do not flap—they ride the unseen currents of the hollow, their movements languid, effortless.  

Their faces, if they can be called faces, are smooth planes, unmarked by mouth or nose. They have no need for such things. They do not eat, do not breathe—they simply *are*, existing in a state of perpetual, weightless observation.  

Sometimes, one will descend, hovering just above the ground, its wings casting intricate shadows over the mineral earth. The Skittering Ones will pause, their trails intersecting in sudden, fleeting geometries, before the Drifter rises again, returning to its silent patrol.  

### **The Pulse**  
Beneath it all, beneath the stillness and the slow movements, there is a rhythm—a deep, resonant thrumming that is neither sound nor vibration but something else entirely. It is the heartbeat of the hollow, the unseen force that binds the Architects, the Skittering Ones, the Drifters, the city itself into a single, seamless organism.  

This is not a place where life happens *on* the world. It is a place where life *is* the world. The inhabitants do not live here—they *are* here, as much a part of the landscape as the air, the light, the silence.  

And if you were to stand among them, you might feel it too—the slow, inevitable pull of the hollow’s rhythm, the quiet certainty that you, too, could become part of its endless, unfolding design.
