**The Glass-Eyed Watchers**  

They do not move. They do not breathe. They stand in rows along the submerged colonnades, their bodies slender and elongated, carved from the same smooth stone as the chamber itself. Their faces are featureless save for their eyes—great, round discs of glass, set deep into their sockets, catching the fractured light and holding it like trapped fire.  

They are not statues. Or if they are, they were not meant to be. Their limbs are too articulated, their postures too poised—some kneel, some reach, some cradle empty hands as if waiting for an offering. Their glass eyes gleam with an unsettling awareness, though they do not track movement. They simply *watch*, unblinking, as the water stirs around them.  

If you press your palm to their chests, you will find no pulse—but the stone is warm, as if something hums beneath the surface. Their fingers are jointed, their wrists hinged, and if you were to lift one, you would find it lighter than it should be, hollow in places where flesh or bone ought to be.  

They do not speak. But sometimes, when the currents shift just so, the glass eyes flicker—not with light, but with something darker, something swimming behind the lenses. A shadow. A pupil, dilating.  

And then it is gone.  

They were not always here. Or perhaps they were, and the chamber was built around them. Either way, they wait. Patient. Unyielding.  

Watching.
