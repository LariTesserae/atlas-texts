**The Glassworks of the Shattered Sky**  

### **Inhabitants:**  

#### **The Architects of Fracture**  
Slender, elongated figures with limbs that taper into glass filaments, their bodies composed of overlapping panes—some clear, some clouded, some reflecting not the present but distorted glimpses of other angles of this place. They move in silence, gliding along the latticework of the plateau, their fingers leaving behind trails of frost where they touch metal. They do not speak, but their presence hums at a frequency that vibrates fillings loose from teeth. Their purpose is unclear, but they are always *adjusting*—realigning beams, polishing surfaces that were already flawless, occasionally pausing to press their palms against the ground as if listening.  

When they notice an intruder, they do not react with hostility. They *rearrange*. Walkways shift subtly, leading the uninvited in circles. Doorways that were there a moment ago now open into sheer drops. And sometimes, if one lingers too long in a single spot, the Architects will gather just beyond peripheral vision, their reflections multiplying in the glass until the trespasser is surrounded by infinite, watching selves.  

#### **The Dripborn**  
Pale, amphibious creatures that emerge from the condensation, their skin translucent, their veins glowing faintly with captured light. They move in slow, deliberate motions, their wide mouths always parted as if waiting for the next drop to fall. They are scavengers, licking moisture from rivets, peeling lichen from surfaces with delicate, needle-tipped tongues. Occasionally, one will pause and *sing*—a sound like water hitting hot metal, brief and sizzling. The others never react, but the structures around them shiver in response.  

They are not afraid of outsiders, but they are curious in a detached way. They will cluster near warmth, their bulbous eyes reflecting firelight in strange, prismatic splits. Touch one, and its skin will adhere to yours momentarily before peeling away, leaving behind a patch of dampness that never quite dries.  

#### **The Clockwork Ravens**  
Not birds, not machines, but something caught between—feathers replaced with overlapping brass scales, their beaks clicking shut with the precision of lock mechanisms. They roost in the hollows of the tallest structures, their eyes lenses that focus and unfocus independently. They do not eat. They *collect*—scraps of metal, shards of glass, occasionally a wandering Dripborn that strayed too high.  

They communicate in coded bursts of sound, a language of ticks and whirrs that sometimes syncs up with the groaning of the plateau beneath. When they take flight, their wings move in perfect, rhythmic arcs, never flapping out of time with one another. They are always watching the cracks in the sky.  

#### **The Things Beneath the Plates**  
You do not see them. You see their *absence*—a shadow that moves against the grain of the others, a ripple in the condensation that defies gravity. Sometimes, a section of the latticework will buckle slightly, as if something large shifted in its sleep. The Dripborn freeze when this happens. The Clockwork Ravens go silent.  

The Architects continue their work, but their movements become slower, more deliberate, as if they are trying not to wake whatever slumbers below.  

#### **The Sky Gazers**  
They stand at the edges of the plateau, where the latticework frays into nothingness. Humanoid in shape but elongated, their limbs too many-jointed, their heads tilted permanently upward. Their faces are smooth, featureless except for a single, vertical slit where a mouth should be. From this slit, a thin, silver thread extends upward, vanishing into the fissures of the sky.  

They do not move. They do not react. They are *waiting*. For what, even the Architects do not know.  

But sometimes, on windless days, the threads *twitch*.  

And far above, something twitches back.
