**The Inhabitants of the Plateau**  

The creatures of the plateau are as much a part of the land as the stone and the trees—shaped by its rhythms, bound to its cycles. They move through the world with the slow certainty of things that have never known another home.  

### **The Root-Walkers**  
Tall and sinewy, their bodies are wrapped in bark-like skin, mottled with lichen and moss that shifts color with the seasons. Their limbs are elongated, fingers tapering into fine, root-like tendrils that brush the ground as they move. They do not walk so much as *unfold*, each step a deliberate unfurling, as if they are in constant negotiation with the earth beneath them.  

They speak in low, resonant hums, a language of vibrations that travels through the stone. Some say they are the children of the ancient trees, that they sprouted from the soil fully formed and have been wandering ever since. They tend to the forests, coaxing vines into bridges, guiding saplings into the cracks of stone. They do not build, but they *shape*, their presence leaving the land subtly altered in their wake.  

### **The Sky-Singers**  
Birds, but not as known elsewhere. Their feathers are not merely iridescent—they shimmer with a liquid, ever-shifting spectrum, as if their plumage drinks the light and remakes it. Their songs are not mere calls but layered harmonies, each note resonating in the hollows of the plateau, weaving into the wind itself.  

They nest in the highest branches of the oldest trees, their homes woven from living vines that grow and tighten around their chosen perches. Some say their songs can bend the weather, that the rain comes when they sing in certain tones. Others claim that if you listen long enough, you can hear fragments of forgotten languages in their melodies.  

### **The Stone-Kin**  
Massive, slow-moving beasts, their bodies armored in plates of living rock, their eyes deep-set and glowing faintly, like embers beneath ash. They graze on the moss and lichen, their jaws grinding stone to powder as they digest the minerals within. Their passage leaves shallow grooves in the plateau’s surface, trails that fill with rainwater and become tiny, temporary rivers.  

They are gentle, but their weight is immense. When they sleep, they sink into the earth, their bodies becoming indistinguishable from the bedrock. Some have been found half-submerged, mistaken for boulders until they shift, exhaling a slow, dusty breath.  

### **The Hollow-Eyes**  
The predators. Silent, sleek, their bodies built for the spaces between things—between trees, between shadows, between moments. Their eyes are dark pits, absorbing all light, giving nothing back. They move in perfect stillness, then strike in a blur of talon and tooth.  

They do not hunt out of malice, nor even hunger, but because they are the plateau’s balance-keepers. They cull the weak, the sick, the ones who stray too far from the hidden paths. Some say they are not flesh at all, but spirits wearing the shape of beasts, remnants of something older than the trees.  

### **The Whisperers**  
No one agrees on what they are. Some claim they are the ones who built the crumbling stone markers, that they were here before the Root-Walkers, before even the oldest trees. Others say they are not creatures at all, but the land’s voice given form—shifting shapes of mist and shadow that linger at the edges of vision.  

They leave no footprints, make no sound, but sometimes, when the wind is right, you can hear them—not words, but something beneath words, a hum that resonates in the bones. They watch. They remember. They do not interfere.  

### **Life as the Land**  
The plateau does not distinguish between the living and the world they inhabit. The Root-Walkers bleed sap. The Stone-Kin shed pebbles like dead skin. The Sky-Singers molt feathers that take root and sprout into new, tiny trees. Death here is not an end, but a reintegration—a return to the stone, the soil, the air.  

To live here is to be part of something vast and slow and patient. The plateau breathes. Its inhabitants are its breath.
