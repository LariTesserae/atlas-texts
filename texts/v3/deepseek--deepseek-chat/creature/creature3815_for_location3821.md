In the Moss-Covered Spires of Veldrin, the inhabitants are as much a part of the city’s ecosystem as the moss and the vines. They are a people who have learned to exist in harmony with the slow, damp rhythm of their environment, their lives shaped by patience and an intimate understanding of the natural world that surrounds them.  

The **Velthrans**—as they call themselves—are a quiet, introspective folk. Their skin carries a faint greenish hue, as though the moss and the mist have seeped into their very flesh over generations. Their eyes, large and luminous, reflect the dim light of the phosphorescent mushrooms, giving them an almost otherworldly appearance. They move with a fluid grace, their steps silent on the damp cobblestones, their movements deliberate and unhurried. Their clothing is woven from the fibrous lichen that grows abundantly in the city, soft and durable, dyed in muted greens and browns that blend seamlessly with their surroundings.  

The Velthrans are craftsmen and caretakers, their hands skilled in the arts of weaving, pressing, and growing. They tend to the looms that produce the lichen cloth, their fingers nimble as they guide the threads. They harvest the seeds of rooftop orchids, pressing them into oil that fuels their lamps and lubricates their machinery. They cultivate the moss and vines, coaxing them to grow in patterns that strengthen the city’s walls and bridges. Their tools, though aged and weathered, are perfectly suited to their tasks—iron hooks for harvesting, stone mortars for grinding, wooden presses for extracting oil.  

They live in the moss-covered towers, their homes blending seamlessly with the vegetation. Their dwellings are sparse but comfortable, filled with the hum of machinery and the scent of damp greenery. Each tower is a self-sustaining ecosystem, with waterwheels turning in hidden channels, peat-burning hearths providing warmth, and rooftop gardens yielding food and medicine. The Velthrans sleep on beds of woven lichen, their dreams filled with the soft creak of turning wheels and the gentle rustle of leaves.  

Theirs is a society built on cooperation and mutual care. There are no leaders, no rulers—only elders, men and women revered for their knowledge of the city’s rhythms and the secrets of its plants. These elders gather in the city’s central square, a circular plaza where the moss is particularly thick and the air hums with the resonance of the waterwheels. There, they discuss the needs of the city, sharing wisdom passed down through generations.  

The Velthrans have no need for haste. Their work is slow, their lives long. They measure time not in days or years but in the growth of the moss, the blooming of the mushrooms, the turning of the waterwheels. They speak in soft, lilting voices, their words carrying the cadence of dripping water. Their songs, if they sing at all, are deep and resonant, a reflection of the city’s hum.  

There are few outsiders in Veldrin, and those who do visit often find the city unsettling in its stillness. To the Velthrans, however, the stillness is life itself. They are the stewards of this place, the caretakers of a city that breathes and grows in its own slow, deliberate way. They do not see themselves as separate from the moss or the vines or the mist—they are part of the city, as much as any stone or leaf.  

In Veldrin, life is not a force to be conquered but a rhythm to be embraced. The Velthrans understand this deeply, their existence a testament to the quiet, enduring power of growth and adaptation. They are the soul of the Moss-Covered Spires, their presence as natural and inevitable as the water that whispers through the stone.
