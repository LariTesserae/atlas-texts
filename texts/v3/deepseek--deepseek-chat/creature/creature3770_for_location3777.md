**The Hollowing**  

At first glance, it is a place of absence—a gouged-out wound in the fabric of the world, a cavity where something vast was torn loose. But absence is not emptiness. The Hollowing *breathes*.  

The walls are smooth, too smooth, as if something has been licking them for centuries. Not polished, but *worn*, like the inside of a throat. They glisten faintly, slick with a substance that is neither mucus nor oil but something between the two—a secretion that hums faintly when touched, vibrating at a frequency just below hearing. The air is thick with the scent of it: wet iron, spoiled honey, the electric tang of a storm about to break.  

Things live here. Or perhaps *live* is the wrong word. They persist. They are not creatures so much as *presences*, distortions in the air that only resolve when you stop looking directly at them.  

Some cling to the walls—pale, elongated shapes with too many joints, their bodies fused seamlessly with the glistening surface. They do not move so much as *unfold*, extending limbs in slow, boneless gestures, as if testing the limits of their tether. Their faces (if they have faces) are recessed, sunken into their bodies like thumbprints in clay.  

Others drift in the center of the Hollowing, suspended in the thick air. These are less solid, more like afterimages—a suggestion of wings, of tendrils, of eyes that open and close along the length of a limb that shouldn’t exist. They pulse faintly, in time with the walls, as though feeding on the same slow exhalation that keeps the Hollowing alive.  

And then there are the whispers. Not voices, not language—just sound that *almost* forms meaning, skittering at the edge of comprehension. It comes from everywhere and nowhere, rising from the walls, dripping from the ceiling, vibrating up through the floor. It is the sound of the Hollowing itself, the sound of something vast and hungry dreaming fitfully.  

Time does not pass here so much as *pool*. Minutes stretch like taffy; hours collapse into seconds. The air resists movement, clinging to skin, to clothing, as if reluctant to let anything escape. The light (what little there is) does not come from any discernible source—it simply *is*, a dim, sourceless glow that makes shadows cling too tightly to the things that cast them.  

The Hollowing is not dead. It is not alive. It is *waiting*. And whatever it is waiting for is already here—you just haven’t noticed it yet.
