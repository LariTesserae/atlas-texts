### **The Buried Bazaar – Inhabitants**  

#### **The Carvers**  
Their hands are calloused, fingers permanently dusted pale with powdered stone from their endless work chiseling new chambers into the weeping rock. They carry their tools in belts of braided leather—fine picks, sharp awls, and tiny bone hammers—and speak in a language of taps and echoes, listening for hollows where they might expand the warren. Their bodies are corded muscle, used to working in cramped tunnels where the air is thick and still. They trade expansions for goods: a widened alcove for a jar of fermented cave-spider silk, a smoothed floor for a bundle of glowing lichen to light their way deeper.  

#### **The Thread-Singers**  
They sit in alcoves draped with hanging looms, fingers darting like spiders over strands of fungal fiber and the shed chitin of subterranean beetles. Their eyes, milky pale from generations in dimness, see only in texture—they know dyes by touch, by scent. They weave not just cloth but *stories*, threading coded tales into patterns that shift under lamplight. Some say their oldest tapestries, left untouched in shadowy corners, hum faintly when stroked, as if the fibers still remember the hands that wove them.  

#### **The Drip-Tenders**  
Water is precious, even here. The Drip-Tenders map the slow journeys of seeping moisture, placing clay urns and copper funnels beneath promising fractures. Some are diviners, laying polished stones on the floor to predict where new veins might open. Their fingertips are permanently wrinkled, their lips cracked from tasting the mineral tang of each new trickle—assessing if it’s safe, if it’s sweet, if it carries the faint, metallic whisper of something deeper.  

#### **The Moth-Keepers**  
They wear cloaks of iridescent wings, stitched together from the molts of the cavern's silent, luminous moths—creatures the size of a hand, drawn to the warmth of bodies and oil lamps. The Moth-Keepers cultivate them in dark niches, harvesting their venom (a coveted delirium in small doses, a weapon in large ones) and their eggs, which burst into liquid silver when crushed, perfect for sealing cracks in fragile vessels.  

#### **The Echo-Eaters**  
No one fully understands them. They linger where tunnels narrow, where sound rebounds strangely. They press their ears to the walls, sometimes their mouths, murmuring or swallowing the returning echoes. They trade in *syllables*—bartering a well-preserved shout from three generations past, or the faint, distorted laughter of a child who has since grown old and vanished. Some say they listen not just to the Bazaar, but to what moves beneath it, in the deeper dark.  

#### **The Hollow-Boned**  
Scavengers, explorers, the ones who slip into the tightest cracks. Their ribs press visibly beneath their skin, their limbs unnaturally flexible from a lifetime of squeezing where others can’t. They bring back things from collapsed passages—half-rusted blades from forgotten wars, skulls of beasts with too many eye sockets, sealed jars whose contents *tap* when shaken. The other inhabitants watch them carefully. The Hollow-Boned move like the walls are whispering to them, and sometimes, they whisper back.  

#### **The Fungal-Binders**  
They cultivate the veins of bioluminescent growth that pulse along certain walls, splicing delicate threads of it into new patterns, causing entire corridors to throb in slow, rhythmic light. Some say the fungus never stops growing—that it twines through the very flesh of those who handle it too long. The oldest Binders have fingertips that glow faintly in the dark, their veins visible beneath the skin like distant constellations.  

—  

Together, they breathe life into the stone. They argue, steal, marry, mourn—all in the weight of the earth above them. They adapt, because to stay static in the Buried Bazaar is to let the damp reclaim you, to let your story dissolve into the walls like salt into water.
