**The Inhabitants of the Submerged Foundry**  

The Submerged Foundry is not empty. It teems with life—life forged, not born, life that has adapted to the iron cathedral’s unyielding embrace. These are not mere creatures; they are *entities*, shaped by the Foundry’s rules, its cycles, its cold and chemical heart.  

### **The Ironbound**  
The most numerous inhabitants are the **Ironbound**, skeletal constructs made of scavenged metal and repurposed machinery. They are not truly alive, but they are not entirely dead either. Each Ironbound is a patchwork creation, cobbled together from discarded parts: pistons for limbs, gears for joints, salvaged glass for eyes that flicker with faint, bioluminescent light.  

The Ironbound are the Foundry’s caretakers, its janitors, its laborers. They move in silence, their steps precise, their actions deliberate. They repair what they can, dismantle what they cannot, and haul scrap to the deeper levels where the airless dark hides their true purpose. Some still follow ancient protocols, performing tasks long since rendered meaningless. Others have begun to deviate, driven by an emergent intelligence—or perhaps a malfunction.  

They are not hostile, but they are territorial. Those who intrude into their workspaces are met with indifference—if they stay out of the way—or dismantled—if they do not.  

---

### **The Glowfolk**  
In the Foundry’s light-starved depths, **Glowfolk** are both predators and providers. They are humanoid creatures, their bodies adapted to the abyssal pressures, their skin translucent and veined with phosphorescent tendrils. They carry bioluminescent sacs, harvested from the synthetic algae that cling to the Foundry’s walls, and use them to navigate, hunt, and mark their territory.  

Glowfolk are scavengers, feeding on the Ironbound’s discarded scraps and the occasional unwary traveler. They are also traders, bartering their glow-sacs for safe passage through the Foundry’s darker regions. Their society is built around light—those who control it wield power, and those who lose it are cast into the void.  

Glowfolk are distrustful of outsiders, but not overtly hostile. They speak in whispers, their voices echoing strangely through the water, and their movements are fluid, almost hypnotic.  

---

### **The Deepwyrms**  
The Foundry’s lower levels belong to the **Deepwyrms**, serpentine predators that prowl the abyss beneath the platforms. These creatures are massive, their bodies armored with overlapping plates of metal and ceramic, their jaws lined with jagged, rusted teeth. Deepwyrms are not natural—they are constructs, assembled by some forgotten machine and set loose to patrol the Foundry’s depths.  

They are blind, relying instead on vibrations in the water to locate their prey. When they strike, it is with terrifying speed, their bodies uncoiling like springs, their jaws snapping shut with a *clang* that echoes through the iron lattice.  

Deepwyrms are feared by all who venture into the Foundry. Even the Ironbound avoid them, leaving their territories unchallenged.  

---

### **The Echoes**  
Sometimes, in the Foundry’s quieter moments, you hear them: **Echoes**, whispers in the dark that seem to come from nowhere and everywhere. These are not living beings, but remnants—recorded voices, fragments of memory, distorted by time and pressure.  

Echoes are harmless, but unnerving. They repeat phrases in languages long forgotten, mimic the sounds of machinery, or simply laugh—a hollow, metallic sound that sends shivers through even the most hardened traveler.  

Some believe the Echoes are the Foundry itself, speaking through its pipes and corridors. Others think they are the remnants of those who once worked here, their voices trapped in the iron like ghosts.  

---

### **The Coreward**  
Deep within the Foundry, in the heart of its labyrinthine structure, resides the **Coreward**. This entity is not a creature, but a presence—a vast, pulsing intelligence that hums through the Foundry’s veins. It is the sum of the Foundry’s machinery, its protocols, its accumulated knowledge.  

The Coreward does not sleep. It observes, calculates, adjusts. It controls the currents, the cycles of light and dark, the Ironbound’s tasks. It is patient, timeless, and utterly indifferent to the lives that scurry through its domain.  

Those who encounter the Coreward describe it as a feeling—a pressure in the water, a vibration in the metal, a sense of being *watched*. It communicates rarely, and only when it deems necessary. Its voice is the Foundry’s voice—a deep, resonant hum that resonates in your bones.  

---

### **The Visitors**  
Not all inhabitants belong to the Foundry. Travelers sometimes venture into its depths—explorers, scavengers, fugitives seeking refuge. These **Visitors** are transient, their presence brief and often fatal.  

The Foundry does not distinguish between friend and foe. It treats all Visitors as intruders, testing them with its currents, its predators, its labyrinthine structure. Those who survive learn to adapt, to navigate its rules, to bargain with its inhabitants. Some become part of its ecosystem, carving out niches among the Ironbound and Glowfolk. Others vanish, their fate unknown.  

---

**The Foundry’s Inhabitants**  
The Submerged Foundry is not a place for the living. It is a place for the *adapted*—for those who have shed their old selves and taken on new forms, new purposes. Its inhabitants are not separate from the Foundry; they are extensions of it, reflections of its iron will, its slow and inexorable evolution.  

Here, life is not a gift, but a consequence—a byproduct of the Foundry’s endless cycles. And as the Foundry waits, its inhabitants wait with it, bound to its rhythms, its rules, its dark and unyielding heart.
