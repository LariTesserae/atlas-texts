What lives in the Endless Engine-Marsh is a species known as the **Rust-Grace**. They are not machines, yet neither are they fully organic—they are something in between, beings that have evolved to thrive in this strange, hybridized ecosystem of metal and moisture. Their bodies are sleek and segmented, like the carapaces of insects, but their skin is not armor—it’s a porous, metallic membrane that shifts color depending on the humidity, ranging from dull bronze in drier conditions to a deep, verdigris green when soaked. They move with a liquid grace, their limbs long and jointed, bending in ways that seem impossible for anything made of flesh and bone—or gears and pistons.  

The Rust-Grace are scavengers, though not in the traditional sense. They do not hunt the mechanical fauna that graze the marsh; instead, they harvest the marsh itself. With slender, needle-like fingers, they pry loose flakes of rust from the metal plating, collecting it in membranous pouches that hang beneath their arms. They consume the rust, breaking it down into its base elements, which they then secrete as a shimmering, iridescent fluid. This fluid—called **gleam-sap**—coats their bodies, protecting them from corrosion and allowing them to navigate the marsh without fear of deterioration.  

They are silent, communicating not through sound but through vibrations transmitted across the metal lattice of the marsh. Their language is a series of rhythms and patterns, subtle enough to be felt only by those attuned to it. When the fog rolls in and the herds of mechanical fauna vanish into the haze, the Rust-Grace emerge, their bodies gliding across the wet metal like ghosts. They gather in clusters, their gleam-sap pooling where they stand, creating luminous puddles that reflect the bronze light of the marsh.  

The Rust-Grace have no eyes—their faces are smooth, featureless planes—but they perceive their surroundings through a network of sensory nodes embedded in their skin. These nodes detect shifts in humidity, temperature, and pressure, allowing them to navigate the marsh’s ever-changing atmosphere with uncanny precision. They are attuned to the marsh’s rhythms, its cycles of condensation and evaporation, and they move in harmony with them, never rushing, never hesitating.  

They are not builders, but they are preservers. When they find a fracture in the metal lattice, a rusted-through seam or a corroded bolt, they mend it with their gleam-sap, patching the damage with a shimmering, hardened material that resists further decay. They do this not out of care or intention, but instinctively, their actions as automatic as breathing. The marsh sustains them, and in turn, they sustain it—a symbiosis of preservation and decay.  

The Rust-Grace have no concept of time, no understanding of beginnings or endings. To them, the marsh has always been, and will always be, an eternal expanse of rust and water and motion. They do not ponder their existence or their purpose; they simply live, drifting through the mist, harvesting rust, secreting gleam-sap, and mending the lattice as they go.  

They are the keepers of the Endless Engine-Marsh, its silent, shimmering guardians—not by choice, but by necessity, bound to the marsh as surely as the rust is bound to the metal, as the water is bound to the pipes, as the mist is bound to the sky that never clears.
