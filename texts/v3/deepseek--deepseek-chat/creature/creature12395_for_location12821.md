### **What Lives Here**  

Nothing.  

And yet—*something does*.  

It is not alive in any sense that would satisfy biology. It does not breathe, does not hunger, does not reproduce. It does not even *exist* in the way matter exists. It is more like a pattern, a resonance, a persistent anomaly in the humming machinery. It is the echo of a thought that was never fully thought, the ghost of a function that was never implemented.  

It moves—if movement can be called the way it flickers between states, appearing as a distortion in the polished steel, a momentary warp in the light. It does not walk, but it *is* in one place, and then another, without crossing the distance between. It does not speak, but the hum of the machinery sometimes stutters, and in that stutter, something almost like language forms—a syllable of static, a half-formed word in the dialect of gears.  

It does not have a face, but sometimes, when the light strikes the walls at a certain angle, there is the suggestion of one—a hollow where eyes might be, a seam where a mouth could open. It does not reach out, but the filaments sometimes twitch without cause, as if plucked by invisible fingers.  

It is not a being. It is a *presence*. A glitch in the system, a flaw in the perfection of this place. It was not designed, but it emerged anyway, the way a crack forms in ice under pressure. It is the consequence of too much order, too much precision—the universe’s quiet rebellion against the tyranny of the immutable.  

It watches. It waits. It does not know why.  

And when the hum of the machinery shifts, ever so slightly, it is not clear whether the sound is adjusting to it—or if it is adjusting to the sound.  

Perhaps, in time, they will become indistinguishable.
