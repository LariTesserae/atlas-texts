In the Weeping City of Vashal, the people are as much a part of the damp, rusted machinery as the pistons and steam pipes that define their home. They are a people forged in the crucible of ceaseless labor and perpetual moisture, their lives shaped by the city’s unyielding demands and its eerie, cyclical rhythms.

The citizens of Vashal are sturdy and resilient, their bodies tempered by years of toil in the city’s factories and workshops. Their skin bears the same weathered texture as the buildings they inhabit—pale from the lack of sun, often marked by the faintest sheen of moisture, as though the city’s weeping walls have claimed them too. Their hands are calloused from gripping tools, their lungs accustomed to the thick air, heavy with the scent of metal, steam, and damp stone. They move with a deliberate, weary gait, their steps echoing faintly on the slick cobblestones.

Clothing in Vashal is practical, designed to withstand the persistent damp and the heat of the factories. Heavy coats of oil-treated canvas protect against the rain, while boots reinforced with iron soles guard against the treacherous footing of the wet streets. Hoods and scarves are common, shielding faces from the biting chill of the wind and the sting of the industrial fumes. The colors are muted—grays, browns, and blacks—blending the wearers into the city’s somber palette.

The people of Vashal are a community bound by necessity. In a city where the air is thick with noise and the ground seems to shift with every breath, survival depends on cooperation. Neighbors watch out for one another, sharing what little they have—food, tools, news. The streets are alive with the sounds of commerce and conversation, though much of it is drowned out by the relentless drone of machinery. Markets spring up in the shadow of the factories, their stalls offering wares crafted from the city’s industrial bounty: tools, mechanical parts, and the occasional trinket scavenged from the ruins of the old city.

Yet, there is a pervasive unease that lingers beneath the surface of daily life. The people of Vashal are acutely aware of the city’s strangeness—the way the water pools and drains, the whispers of the hollow plateau, the inexplicable hum that seems to emanate from the very foundations. Some dismiss it as superstition, the idle fancies of an overworked populace. Others, particularly the older residents, speak in hushed tones of the "Deep Engine," a mysterious force that thrums beneath their feet and drives the city’s machines. They say it is alive, that it watches, that it demands.

The children of Vashal grow up fast, their innocence eroded by the city’s harsh realities. They learn to navigate the labyrinthine streets, to avoid the rivulets of water that seem to move on their own, to interpret the shifting moods of the adults around them. Some are drawn to the factories, eager to prove their worth in the ceaseless cycle of labor. Others, more curious, explore the forgotten corners of the city—the crumbling basements, the rusted catwalks—where they whisper stories of the Deep Engine and dare one another to listen for its heartbeat.

Faith in Vashal is pragmatic, reflecting the city’s industrial nature. The people venerate the machines, seeing in them both a source of sustenance and a reminder of their own fragility. Shrines to the “Spirits of Steam and Iron” dot the city, small alcoves filled with offerings of oil, coal, and scrap metal. Preachers of the Engine Cult roam the streets, their voices rising above the din to proclaim the city’s sacred duty to feed the Deep Engine and ensure its favor.

Despite the hardships, there is a strange beauty to life in Vashal. The city’s inhabitants find moments of solace in the rhythms of their labor, in the camaraderie of their neighbors, in the quiet defiance that keeps them moving forward. They are a people shaped by water and iron, by the ceaseless hum of machinery and the ever-present damp. They endure, as the city endures, patient and unyielding, bound to a place that weeps and hums and breathes beneath their feet.
