**The Inhabitants of the Singing Hollows**  

They do not have names—not in any language that could be spoken aloud without disturbing the air. But if you were to listen very, very closely to the hum of the roots, you might hear them whispering to one another in the spaces between sound.  

### **The Hollowborn**  
They are not born, not in the way flesh understands. They **emerge**—from the weeping walls, from the trembling pools, from the hollows where the world has worn thin. Their bodies are made of the same fibrous lattice as the land itself, shifting between solid and liquid, between root and breath.  

- **Formless, yet deliberate.** A Hollowborn might appear as a tangle of vines one moment, a humanoid shape the next, its limbs elongating like stretched resin. Their faces (if they have them) are smooth, featureless, but their voices are **many**—a chorus of whispers, clicks, and resonant tones pulled from the land.  
- **They do not eat.** They drink the hum of the Hollows, absorbing vibrations like sunlight. When they move, the ground shivers beneath them.  
- **They remember in echoes.** Their minds are not linear. They experience time as overlapping refrains, their memories stored in the chambers of the labyrinth itself. To them, the past is not gone—it is simply waiting to be sung again.  

### **The Choir-Beasts**  
The creatures that flit between the roots are not animals, not plants—they are **living notes**, fragments of the Hollows’ endless song given temporary flesh.  

- **The Winged Motes** – Swarms of iridescent, weightless things that flicker like candle flames. They cluster in the high arches, their wings vibrating in unison, adding their pitch to the chorus. When startled, they dissolve into mist, only to reform elsewhere.  
- **The Root-Stalkers** – Long-limbed, many-jointed shadows that flow like liquid through the lattice. They have no eyes, but their bodies ripple with bioluminescent patterns, responding to unseen currents. They do not hunt—they **listen**, drawn to disturbances in the song.  
- **The Throat-Flowers** – Stationary, but far from passive. Their petals are lined with fine, hair-like filaments that tremble at the slightest vibration. When something approaches, they exhale—a deep, resonant tone that lingers in the bones. Some say they are the Hollows’ way of speaking.  

### **The Dreamers Beneath**  
Beneath the labyrinth, where the roots knot into something too dense to penetrate, there are **presences**. They do not move, not in any way that can be measured. But when the song of the Hollows dips into its lowest register, the ground trembles with their slow, tidal breath.  

- They are old. Not ancient—**precedent**. They were here before the Hollows grew, and they will remain when the song ends.  
- They do not think, not in any way that could be understood. They **resonate**, their dreams shaping the world above.  
- Occasionally, something rises from the depths—not one of them, but a **fragment**, a thought given form. These entities are rare, vast, and utterly indifferent to those who witness them.  

### **The Rules of the Inhabitants**  
- **They do not attack.** But they may **absorb**, may **reshape**, may **harmonize**. A lost traveler might find their voice joining the chorus, their body slowly unraveling into root and resonance.  
- **They are not individuals.** A Hollowborn might split into three, then merge again, its memories shared between its fragments.  
- **They do not sleep.** They **pause**, their bodies stilling as the song shifts key.  
- **They fear silence.** A place where the hum stops is a place where something has gone terribly wrong.  

### **Why They Are Here**  
They do not dwell in the Singing Hollows—they **are** the Singing Hollows. The land grows them, sings them into being, and when their part in the chorus is done, they return to the lattice, waiting to be sung again.  

To meet them is to stand inside a breath, to be examined by something that knows you only as vibration, as another note in an endless, shifting hymn.  

They will not stop for you. But if you listen closely, you might hear your own heartbeat joining the song.
