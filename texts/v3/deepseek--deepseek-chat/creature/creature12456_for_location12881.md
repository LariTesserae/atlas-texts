**The Inhabitants of the Crystalline Labyrinth**  

They call themselves the **Shardborn**, though outsiders—if there are any—might name them something crueler. Their bodies are lean, wiry, and hardened by the relentless grind of their environment, their skin thickened by exposure to the metallic air and the constant abrasion of crystalline dust. Their eyes, adapted to the dim glow of bioluminescent lichen, are wide and reflective, like polished obsidian, capable of catching the faintest flicker of light in the labyrinth’s depths. Some have developed secondary eyelids, thin and translucent, to shield against the occasional flare of molten metal or the sting of airborne particulates.  

Their clothing is a patchwork of insulated fabrics and scavenged plating, stitched together from whatever materials can be salvaged—scraps of machinery, cured fungal leather, even sheets of cooled slag hammered into crude armor. They move with a tense, efficient grace, their steps quick but measured, always aware of the shifting terrain beneath them. Many bear scars from encounters with the labyrinth’s hazards: jagged rockfalls, sudden geysers of superheated gas, or the razor-edged fractures in the walls that open without warning.  

The Shardborn are not a unified people but a collection of clans, factions, and lone scavengers, each carving out their own precarious existence in the mountain’s veins. Some dwell in the upper reaches, where the air is marginally clearer and the tunnels wider, trading in refined metals and rare crystalline formations. Others delve deeper, into the unstable lower strata, where the walls hum with buried machinery and the heat is oppressive. These deep-miners are often marked by strange mutations—skin that glistens with metallic sheen, fingers fused into tool-like appendages, or voices that resonate unnaturally in the cavernous dark.  

They speak in a clipped, rhythmic dialect, words sharp and utilitarian, laced with the jargon of mechanics and geologists. Superstition thrives alongside pragmatism; they whisper of the **Hollow Pulse**, a deep, irregular vibration that some claim is the mountain’s heartbeat, others the sound of some vast, buried machine slowly failing. To hear it is an omen—either of impending collapse or of a rare vein of precious material soon to be uncovered.  

Their society is built on three relentless imperatives: **dig, refine, survive.** They are engineers of necessity, jury-rigging ventilation systems from broken machinery, distilling drinkable moisture from the acidic condensation that beads on cold metal surfaces, and forging weapons from whatever scraps they can claim before another clan does. Conflict is frequent but rarely wasteful—every life lost is a worker lost, every drop of spilled blood a resource squandered.  

Yet, for all their harshness, they are not without art. In the rare moments of stillness, they etch intricate patterns into the walls, maps of forgotten tunnels or depictions of half-remembered legends. Some craft jewelry from the mountain’s refuse—twisted wire, polished stone, glass forged in the heart of a furnace—wearing it as both ornament and armor.  

And always, there is the sense that they are not the first to dwell here. The deeper tunnels hold ruins of older structures, machines too large to comprehend, and skeletal remains fused into the rock. The Shardborn do not speak of them, but they know: this place was made by something, for something. And whether they are its inheritors or its eventual replacements remains to be seen.
