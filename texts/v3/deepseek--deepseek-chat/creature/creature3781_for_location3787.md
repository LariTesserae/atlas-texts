**The Weeping Peaks of Vaelmoor** are home to the **Dreghar**, a reclusive and enigmatic species of amphibious humanoids who have adapted to the harsh, mist-laden slopes of these young, weeping mountains.  

### **Appearance:**  
The Dreghar are tall and slender, their elongated limbs giving them an almost wraithlike grace as they move through the damp crags. Their skin is a mottled gray-blue, slick with a thin, glistening mucus that helps them retain moisture in the cold, dry air. Their eyes are large and black, adapted to the perpetual gloom, with nictitating membranes that flick across them like second eyelids when the mist thickens. Their fingers and toes are webbed, not for swimming, but for gripping the slick stone, their fingertips ending in small, hooked claws that let them cling to sheer surfaces with eerie ease.  

### **Society & Behavior:**  
The Dreghar live in small, scattered clans, their dwellings carved into the weeping rock itself—shallow caves or overhangs where the constant seep of water collects in natural basins. They are silent creatures, communicating in low, resonant hums and clicks that echo faintly through the mist. Their society is built around the rare pools where the mountain’s weeping is most abundant, places they consider sacred.  

They are neither fully nocturnal nor diurnal, moving when the mist is thickest, when visibility is lowest. They are ambush predators, patient and methodical, stalking the few hardy creatures that survive in these peaks—rodent-like scuttlers, blind lizards, and the occasional lost bird. But they are also scavengers, drawn to the distant hum of machinery, venturing into the lower tunnels where the air smells of oil and metal, retrieving strange, half-rusted artifacts that they bring back to their dens.  

### **Relationship with the Land:**  
The Dreghar believe the mountains are alive, that the weeping stone is the land itself mourning some ancient, forgotten loss. They drink only from the sacred pools, never disturbing the slow drip of water from the rock, as if fearing that to take too much would silence the mountain’s voice forever.  

They avoid the deeper mines, where the hum of machinery grows louder, sensing something unnatural there. But sometimes, a Dreghar will vanish into the fog and not return—whether taken by some unseen predator, lost in the shifting mist, or drawn into the depths by something far older than the mines, none can say.  

### **Interaction with Outsiders:**  
Few know of the Dreghar. Those who stumble into their territory rarely leave. The mist swallows sound, and the Dreghar move like ghosts, watching from the rocks. If they choose to reveal themselves, it is only to those who approach their sacred pools with reverence—or to those who carry the scent of the deep mines, a scent that makes them hiss and withdraw into the shadows.  

They are not malicious, but they are wary. The world beyond their weeping peaks is one of noise and fire, and they want no part of it. Yet sometimes, when the mist is thickest, a lone Dreghar might be seen standing at the edge of a cliff, staring down into the valleys below, as if waiting for something—or remembering.  

The mountains weep. The Dreghar listen. And the hum of machinery grows louder in the dark.
