In this damp, verdant pocket of existence, life thrives in quiet symbiosis—neither fully wild nor entirely structured, but something in between. The inhabitants are not rulers of this place, nor are they merely passing through. They *belong* here, woven into the fabric of the ecosystem as surely as the vines that climb the stone and the water that beads upon the leaves.  

### **The Mosskin**  
Small, silent figures move through the undergrowth—humanoid, but not human. Their skin is mottled green and gray, textured like bark or lichen, shifting subtly in hue to match their surroundings. They are slight, their limbs elongated and fingers tapered, almost root-like in their delicacy. Their eyes, when visible beneath the draping fronds of their hair (which is not hair at all, but fine, fibrous growths resembling moss or thin fungal threads), are large and dark, reflecting the dim light with a faint, wet sheen.  

They do not speak, not in any way that would be recognized as language. Instead, they communicate through touch, through the careful placement of hands upon stone or stem, through the slow unfurling of their own bodies in response to the world around them. They are patient. They are waiting. For what, it is unclear—perhaps for nothing at all. Perhaps waiting is simply their natural state.  

They tend to the grove without tools, their very presence encouraging growth. Where they rest, moss thickens. Where they press their palms against the earth, new shoots emerge. They do not eat in any conventional sense; instead, they absorb, drawing sustenance from the air, the water, the slow decay of fallen leaves.  

### **The Whisperers**  
Not all inhabitants are corporeal. Some are only glimpsed—a flicker of movement at the edge of vision, a disturbance in the mist, a shape that might have been a figure but resolves into nothing more than a shifting curtain of hanging vines. These entities—if they can be called that—are less defined, more atmospheric. They are the sigh of the wind through the leaves, the way the fog curls just so around a rock, the way the light bends where it should not.  

They are not ghosts, nor spirits. They are simply another form of life here, as much a part of the place as the water or the stone. They do not interact with the Mosskin, not directly, but their presence is felt—a quiet awareness, a sense that the grove itself is watching.  

### **The Burrowers**  
Beneath the soft earth, in the spaces between roots and stone, small creatures dig and tunnel. They are blind, their bodies slick and pale, their limbs stubby and strong. They move in slow, deliberate patterns, their paths forming an unseen network beneath the surface. They do not disturb the Mosskin, nor do they seem to notice the Whisperers. Their world is one of touch and vibration, of the slow consumption of decaying matter, of the endless, patient work of turning death into life.  

### **The Watcher in the Water**  
There is one presence that does not move. At the heart of the grove, where the water gathers in its deepest pool, something rests just beneath the surface. It is not a fish, nor a plant, nor anything easily named. It is simply *there*—a darkness that shifts when observed, a ripple that does not match the flow of the water. The Mosskin avoid this pool, though they do not fear it. They acknowledge it with a tilt of the head, a brief stilling of their hands, before moving on.  

It does not hunger. It does not sleep. It simply *is*, as much a part of the grove as the stones or the mist.  

### **A Place of Quiet Communion**  
No one here is lonely. No one here is separate. The Mosskin tend, the Whisperers drift, the Burrowers toil, the Watcher waits—and the grove continues, unhurried, in its slow and endless becoming.  

To witness them is to understand that this place does not belong to them.  

They belong to it.
