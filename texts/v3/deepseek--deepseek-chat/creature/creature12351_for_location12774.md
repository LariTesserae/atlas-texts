Here is what lives in this submerged cavern-city:  

### **The Dwellers of the Deep**  

#### **1. The Architects (Unseen, Unknowable)**  
They are not here—not anymore. Or perhaps they never were. The cavern-city bears no marks of deliberate construction, only the slow, patient work of erosion, pressure, and time. Yet something lingers—an echo, a presence in the way the stone curves just so, in the way the faintest etchings on the walls resemble glyphs half-erased. Perhaps they were creatures of water and rock, their bodies long dissolved into the currents. Or perhaps they are still here, watching through the stone itself.  

#### **2. The Bonefish (Scavengers of the Half-Built)**  
Sleek, translucent predators with elongated jaws, their skeletons visible beneath thin, milky flesh. They drift between the jagged structures, their movements slow and deliberate, as if inspecting the unfinished architecture. Their teeth are needle-thin, designed not for tearing flesh but for scraping algae, minerals, and the occasional soft-bodied creature from the stone. They are patient. They have been waiting for a very long time.  

#### **3. The Frost-Crawlers (Silent Masons)**  
Crablike beings with segmented limbs, their shells encrusted with ice and mineral deposits. They move in slow, methodical patterns along the cavern walls, their claws etching faint grooves into the rock. Are they shaping the city? Or merely following some instinctive path laid down long ago? Their eyes—if they have eyes—are hidden beneath layers of calcified growth. They do not react to light or movement, only to the unseen currents that guide them.  

#### **4. The Dreaming Jellies (Floating Sentinels)**  
Luminous, gelatinous masses that pulse with slow, rhythmic contractions. They drift in the open spaces between structures, their tendrils brushing against the stone as if reading its surface. Occasionally, one will pause, shudder, and release a cloud of sparkling spores into the water—each spore carrying a fragment of something: a memory, a sensation, a half-formed thought. The other creatures avoid them.  

#### **5. The Hollow Ones (The Almost-People)**  
Pale, elongated shapes that move at the edges of vision. Their bodies are smooth, featureless, their limbs too long and too thin. They do not swim—they *glide*, as if the water itself carries them. They linger near the cavern’s deepest recesses, where the stone is softest, where the faintest carvings begin to take shape. They do not eat. They do not hunt. They simply *watch*, their faces blank, their purpose unknown.  

#### **6. The Whisper Eels (Voices in the Dark)**  
Slender, serpentine creatures that weave through the cavern’s upper reaches. Their bodies emit low-frequency vibrations, a sound just beyond hearing—a hum that resonates through the water, making the stone tremble ever so slightly. When they pass near the Frost-Crawlers, the crustaceans pause, as if listening. Some say the eels are singing the city into existence. Others say they are unraveling it.  

#### **7. The Stone-Mother (The Heart of the Cavern)**  
A vast, slow-breathing mass embedded in the cavern floor. It is difficult to tell where rock ends and flesh begins. Its surface is pocked with crevices, each one exhaling a slow, warm current that stirs the water. The Frost-Crawlers gather near it, their claws working tirelessly. The Hollow Ones kneel at its edges, their hands pressed to its surface. The Dreaming Jellies avoid it entirely.  

It does not move.  
But sometimes, it *shifts*.  

---  

This is what lives in the submerged cavern-city: creatures of patience, of silence, of half-formed purpose. They are waiting—for the city to finish itself, for the Architects to return, or for something else entirely.  

And beneath it all, the Stone-Mother dreams.
