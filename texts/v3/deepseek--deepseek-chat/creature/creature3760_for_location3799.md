### **What Lives Here**  

The inhabitants of this place are not people—not in the way the word is usually understood. They are *tenders*, though they do not tend to life as much as they tend to *process*.  

They move in silence, clad in heavy, oil-stained coveralls, their faces obscured behind darkened visors that reflect the machinery rather than reveal any expression. Their hands are gloved, their tools extensions of their limbs—wrenches, gauges, syphons, all worn smooth by use. They do not speak. If they communicate, it is through touch, through the adjustment of pressure valves, through the tightening of bolts. Their purpose is maintenance, but maintenance here is not repair—it is *participation*. The machine does not break; it only shifts, and the tenders shift with it.  

They are few in number, though it is hard to tell—they blend into the machinery, their movements synchronized with the pistons, their breathing lost in the hum of turbines. They do not eat, not in any visible way. Instead, they absorb, their suits lined with filaments that draw moisture from the air, that siphon trace elements from the metal they touch. They are as much a part of the system as the pipes they walk upon.  

Sometimes, one of them stops. Not to rest, but to *listen*. The machine speaks in vibrations, in the minute fluctuations of steam pressure, in the way a particular gear hesitates before engaging. The tender tilts their head, adjusts a valve, and the rhythm corrects itself.  

They are not masters of this place. They are its keepers, its interpreters. And when one of them finally ceases to move—when their suit goes slack, their tools slipping from their grip—the machine absorbs them too. The vines curl around their limbs, the pipes swallow their form, and within days, there is no trace left.  

The tenders do not mourn. They adjust.  

And the machine hums on.

*(The rest of the space is occupied by the creatures—small, quick things with too many joints, their bodies metallic but organic, skittering along the pipes. They are neither pests nor pets. They simply* are. *Occasionally, one is caught in a gear, crushed, dissolved into the lubricant that keeps everything running. The tenders do not interfere. The machine takes what it needs.)*
