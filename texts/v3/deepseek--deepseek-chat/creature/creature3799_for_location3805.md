### **The Dwellers of the Dripping Warrens**  

#### **The Hollowborn**  
They are not born—they are **assembled**.  

The Hollowborn are the inhabitants of Kharzul, though "inhabitants" may be too generous a term. They are **spare parts cobbled into humanoid shapes**, their bodies a patchwork of scavenged metal, ceramic, and sinew-thick cables. Their faces are smooth, featureless masks of tarnished brass or cracked porcelain, save for a single slit where a mouth might be—though they do not speak, not in any way that requires breath.  

They move with the **stiffness of rusted hinges**, their limbs clicking softly as they work. Their hands are delicate, precise—fingers ending in fine tools rather than nails, capable of threading wires or tightening bolts with mechanical patience. They do not sleep. They do not eat. They simply **continue**, tending to the machines, adjusting valves, scraping moss from the walls when it threatens to clog the vents.  

They are not alive. But they are **not dead**, either.  

#### **The Whisper in the Pipes**  
Something **older** lives here.  

It is not a creature, not in any tangible sense. It is a **pressure**, a presence that thrums through the copper veins of Kharzul, a vibration in the steam lines. The Hollowborn do not acknowledge it, but they work around it—leaving small offerings of oil in shallow dishes, or adjusting their rhythms when the pipes hum a certain way.  

Sometimes, when the warrens are particularly still, a voice can be heard—**not words, but meaning**, pressed into the mind like the memory of sound. It speaks of **hunger**, of **heat**, of the slow, inevitable grinding down of all things.  

It does not have a name. But it watches.  

#### **The Moss That Remembers**  
The pale, clinging vegetation is not entirely passive.  

In the deepest chambers, where the Hollowborn rarely venture, the moss grows thicker, forming **vein-like patterns** across the stone. When touched, it shivers slightly, retracting as if startled. Those who linger too long near these patches report **dreams**—not their own, but fragmented visions of a time before the warrens, before the machines.  

The Hollowborn prune it back when it creeps too close to the engines. They do not seem to fear it. But they do not let it grow unchecked.  

#### **The Last Engineer**  
There is **one** who is different.  

A figure wrapped in a heavy, oil-stained coat, its face hidden behind a corroded breather mask. It moves with purpose, adjusting dials, tapping gauges, occasionally pausing to listen to the groan of the earth. Unlike the Hollowborn, it **speaks**—in a voice like grinding gears, low and halting.  

It calls itself **"The Custodian."**  

It is the last living thing in Kharzul.  

And it is **dying**.  

---  

The warrens do not care. The machines do not stop. The water keeps dripping.  

Life—such as it is—goes on.
