In the Weeping Grove of Vith, the inhabitants are as much a part of the ecosystem as the trees and water that define it. Each being seems to have evolved in symbiosis with the grove, their forms and behaviors shaped by the grove’s sentient will. The very air here feels alive, thrumming with an ancient consciousness that watches, listens, and reacts.

The **Treefolk**, as the locals call them, are the grove’s guardians—creatures that blur the line between plant and animal. Their bodies are composed of supple wood and fibrous tendrils, their skin resembling bark but soft and pliant, like the inner flesh of a sapling. Eyes like polished amber peer from their faces, glowing faintly in the perpetual twilight. Their limbs are long and sinewy, ending in hands that branch into delicate fingers, capable of weaving vines or caressing the moss beneath their feet.  

The Treefolk move with a fluid grace, their steps silent on the spongy earth. They do not speak in words but communicate through a melodic hum that resonates with the grove’s own vibrations. Sometimes, they gather in circles around hollowed-out growths, their bodies swaying in unison, their hums harmonizing with the grove’s song. The people of the settlement believe the Treefolk are the grove’s protectors—or perhaps its emissaries—interpreting its desires and enforcing its unspoken laws.  

Then there are the **Lumenflies**, bioluminescent insects that drift through the grove like living stars. Their bodies are translucent, their wings veined with faintly glowing patterns that shift and change as they move. Lumenflies are drawn to the Treefolk, clustering around them in shimmering clouds, their light dancing in sync with the Treefolk’s hums. Some say the Lumenflies carry messages between the grove and its guardians, their flickers containing secrets too ancient for mortal minds to comprehend.  

The **Mosskin** are smaller, more elusive creatures—tiny, furred beings with bodies covered in soft, moss-like hair that blends seamlessly with their surroundings. Their eyes are wide and dark, their movements quick and skittish, as though they are perpetually on edge. Mosskin are scavengers, darting through the undergrowth to collect fallen leaves, berries, and the occasional dead insect. They weave these into intricate nests that hang from low branches, their homes camouflaged so well they are almost invisible.  

Beneath the surface, in the waterlogged soil, dwell the **Rootbound**—worm-like creatures with translucent bodies that pulse faintly with bioluminescence. They burrow through the mud, their long, segmented bodies intertwined with the grove’s roots. Rootbound are said to be the grove’s memory-keepers, their veins carrying stories and histories absorbed from the soil. Occasionally, they surface, their soft light illuminating the pools where the amphibians gather.  

The amphibians themselves are peculiar—lean, web-footed creatures with skin that glistens like wet stone. Their croaks echo through the grove, their calls forming a rhythm that seems to synchronize with the drip of sap and the rustle of leaves. Some have crests or frills that flicker with color when disturbed, a display meant to warn or attract.  

And then there are the **Grovesleepers**, beings rarely seen but often felt. They are the grove’s dreamers—hulking, amorphous shapes that rise from the earth during the grove’s most profound stirrings. Their bodies are composed of mud, moss, and tangled roots, their form shifting and reforming as they move. Grovesleepers are the grove’s subconscious made manifest, their presence heralding great change—whether growth, decay, or something incomprehensible.  

The people of the settlement live in uneasy harmony with these inhabitants. They leave offerings of food and trinkets at the base of certain trees, hoping to appease the grove and its guardians. They whisper stories of those who took too much or too quickly, tales of roots that rose like serpents, of vines that coiled tighter and tighter, of minds lost to the grove’s whispers.  

To enter the Weeping Grove of Vith is to become part of it, even if only temporarily. Its inhabitants are not separate from the grove but extensions of its will, its pulse, its ancient, dreaming heart. To walk here is to be watched, to be known, to be absorbed into something far greater than yourself. And in the grove’s darkest depths, something waits—patient, silent, and eternal.
