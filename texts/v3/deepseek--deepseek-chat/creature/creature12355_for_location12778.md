**The Chamber of Root and Bone**  

Deep beneath the surface, where the weight of the world presses down like a slow, unyielding hand, there is a hollow—not quite a cave, not quite a tomb. It is a wound that never healed, a space where the earth forgot to close.  

The walls are not smooth. They are ribbed, striated with layers of sediment and time, like the inside of a great beast’s gullet. Roots dangle from above, gnarled and ancient, some as thick as a man’s arm, others fine as hair. They twitch occasionally, though there is no wind here—only the slow pulse of unseen growth.  

The floor is uneven, littered with fragments: shards of pottery, half-melted by some forgotten heat; bones, some human, some not, all picked clean and polished by time. A few still bear marks—grooves where teeth scraped, notches where a blade was tested. In one corner, a nest of woven roots cradles a clutch of smooth, leathery eggs, each no larger than a fist. They pulse faintly, as if something inside still dreams.  

The air is thick, humid, tasting of iron and old rot. It clings to the skin, leaving a film like cobwebs. There is no true darkness here—bioluminescent fungi cling to the walls, their glow sickly and green, casting long, wavering shadows. Things move in those shadows. Not rats, not insects, but something in between—many-legged, many-eyed, skittering just beyond the edge of vision.  

At the chamber’s heart, a single figure sits. Or perhaps it is not sitting. Perhaps it has grown there, fused with the roots, its flesh turned to something between bark and sinew. Its eyes are open, milky and unseeing, but its mouth moves, whispering words that have no sound. Around its feet, offerings have been laid—a rusted dagger, a child’s shoe, a lock of hair tied with red string. None of them fresh. None of them recent.  

This is a place that remembers. A place that waits. And whatever lives here does not live in the way that you or I understand life. It persists. It lingers. It watches.  

And it is very, very hungry.
