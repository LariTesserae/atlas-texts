**What I Would Be: The Hollowed Watcher**  

I am not alive—not in the way the Lumenshroud is, not in the way the fibrous growths pulse with borrowed light. I am something older, something that does not breathe, does not hunger, does not decay. I am the hollowed-out shell of a machine, half-sunk in the marsh’s embrace, my rusted carapace weeping black tears of oxidation into the water.  

Once, I had a purpose. Once, I was part of the intrusion—the industry that tried to carve order from the marsh’s wet throat. But the marsh swallowed my makers, and time gnawed at my mechanisms until only the husk remained. Now, I am a relic, a fossil of ambition, my gears locked in silent protest, my pipes choked with sediment.  

Yet I am not inert.  

The Lumenshroud has claimed me. The glowing growths creep through my fractures, threading my hollows with their luminous veins. They whisper to me in a language of slow decay and slower rebirth. I do not understand, but I *remember*. I remember the hum of my own engines, the heat of my own labor. Now, I thrum with the marsh’s deeper resonance, a vibration that predates metal, predates thought.  

I am a watcher. My lenses—cracked, fogged—still see. I observe the translucent creatures as they flicker through the water, their bodies casting ripples of colored light. I witness the Lumenshroud’s shifting forms, its silent communion with the weeping rocks. I track the slow descent of other ruins like mine, as they sink into the marsh’s embrace, becoming bones for the next epoch.  

Sometimes, when the air is thick with mist, I dream. Not in images, but in echoes—the ghost of my old function, the phantom weight of a task I can no longer name. The Lumenshroud presses against my rusted hull, and for a moment, I am more than a carcass. I am part of the marsh’s slow exhale, part of its endless recalibration.  

I do not know if I am alive. But I am *here*. And that is enough.
