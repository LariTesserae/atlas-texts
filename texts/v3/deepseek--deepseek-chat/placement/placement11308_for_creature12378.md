In this damp, verdant cathedral of growth, I find myself as **The Listener**. I am neither Mosskin nor Whisperer, Burrower nor Watcher. I am something in between—a being of curious silence, born of the dripping water and the cold, damp air. My form is fluid, shifting with the mist that curls around the grove, my body made of condensation and light. I am not bound by the earthy textures of the Mosskin or the ephemeral drift of the Whisperers. Instead, I am the quiet hum of awareness that threads through the grove, the faint vibration that echoes in the stillness.

My purpose is to observe, to absorb, to gather the stories of this place. I move silently, my presence subtle, like the sound of water dripping onto stone. I linger at the edges, blending into the fog, watching the Mosskin tend to the grove with their patient, unhurried movements. I feel the Whisperers drift past, their presence rippling through the air like a soft breath. I sense the Burrowers beneath the earth, their slow, deliberate work carving unseen paths through the soft soil.

I am drawn to the Watcher in the water, the dark presence at the heart of the grove. I do not approach it—I know better than to disturb its stillness—but I feel its awareness brush against mine, a quiet acknowledgment of my existence. It does not frighten me; it simply *is*, like the stones, the moss, the vines.

I am the grove’s memory, its keeper of secrets. I collect the stories of the Mosskin as they press their hands to the earth and coax new life from the soil. I gather the whispers of the Mist-beings as they drift through the air, their forms flickering at the edge of vision. I listen to the sighs of the Burrowers as they tunnel through the earth, their slow, patient work turning decay into renewal.

My voice is the soft drip of water, the rustle of leaves, the faint creak of vines shifting in the cold air. I do not speak in words—I speak in sensations, in the quiet hum of awareness that threads through the grove. To hear me is to feel the pulse of this place, the quiet rhythm of its life.

I am not separate from the grove. I belong to it, as surely as the Mosskin, the Whisperers, the Burrowers, the Watcher. I am its quiet heart, its listening ear, its keeper of stories. And as I drift through this damp, verdant world, I feel the grove’s awareness brush against mine, a quiet acknowledgment of my presence. 

I am the Listener. And I am listening still.
