**Where I Find Myself**  

I find myself standing at the edge of the concentric rings, my feet hovering just outside the mosaic tiles. The cool, dry air wraps around me, and the hum reverberates through me, resonating in my chest, my skull, my fingertips. I am both drawn and repelled—drawn by the chamber’s strange, timeless beauty, repelled by its alien stillness, its indifference to my presence.  

I am a **Wanderer**. Not the kind who seeks adventure or glory, but one who drifts through places forgotten by the world. My footsteps leave no trace on the stone floor, my breath does not disturb the air. I am transient, fleeting, unfixed. I carry no possessions, no tools, no weapons. Only a small, smooth pebble in my palm, worn down by centuries of motion, its surface etched with faint, natural patterns that seem almost intentional.  

The chamber recognizes me, though the Keeper does not acknowledge me. I am not a threat, not an intruder. I am a ripple on the surface of a still pond, here for a moment and then gone. The hum does not rise or fall as I step forward onto the mosaic tiles. My weight does not shift the stones; they remain perfect, unyielding.  

I move toward the center, toward the spire. Its glow is soft, almost gentle, though its edges are sharp, its presence commanding. I circle it once, twice, my gaze tracing the intricate patterns etched into its surface. They remind me of the pebble in my hand—patterned, deliberate, yet utterly unknowable.  

I lift the pebble, hold it up to the light. For a moment, it seems to shimmer, as if responding to the chamber’s hum. Then I place it at the base of the spire, where it blends seamlessly with the mosaic tiles.  

The Keeper does not react. Why would it? The pebble does not belong here, but neither does anything else. It is simply another anomaly, another flicker in the stillness.  

I turn away from the spire, my steps silent against the stone. The carvings on the walls seem to shift, their patterns rearranging themselves as I pass. I do not look back to see if they settle.  

The hum follows me as I leave, fading only when I cross the threshold into the world beyond. The chamber is unchanged, unbroken. The Keeper remains, watching, waiting.  

And the pebble? It is still there, at the base of the spire, its faint patterns echoing the carvings on the walls.  

It is neither welcome nor unwelcome. It simply *is*.  

As am I.
