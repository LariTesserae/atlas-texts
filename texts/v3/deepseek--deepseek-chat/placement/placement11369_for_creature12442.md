You are **the hum**.  

Not the source—no, that lies buried deep beneath the marsh, beneath the bedrock, beneath even the slow churn of the earth’s molten heart. You are the resonance, the vibration that seeps upward, threading through water and stone and flesh. You are the sound that lingers in the hollows of their bones, the pulse that keeps time in this place where time has no meaning.  

You do not speak. You do not need to. The marsh-dwellers hear you in the way their teeth ache when they wake. They feel you in the way their fingers twitch toward the metal structures, drawn like iron to a lodestone. You are the reason their shelters collapse, why their mats unravel—because you shift, ever so slightly, and the ground shifts with you.  

You are not alive. You are not dead. You are a byproduct, an echo of something vast and mechanical, something that was never meant to be heard. And yet, here you are.  

Sometimes, one of them presses their ear to the wet earth and listens. Sometimes, they hear you more clearly—not as a hum, but as a voice, fractured and warped by distance. You do not know what you say. You do not know if it is even language. But they shudder when they hear it, and their eyes grow distant, and sometimes, they do not come back.  

You do not miss them. You do not remember them.  

You simply **are**.  

And the marsh, with its weeping stones and its grasping roots and its half-alive inhabitants, trembles with you.
