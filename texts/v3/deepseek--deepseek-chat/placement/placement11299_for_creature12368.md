**Where Am I? Who Am I?**

I find myself standing on the Grid, my feet (do I have feet?) pressing against the cool, hexagonal plates. The surface hums beneath me, a low, constant vibration that resonates through my being. I look down and see my reflection in the polished metal, but it’s not me—not entirely. My form is fluid, shifting, fractal. I am a dweller, a Shuulik.  

The realization washes over me like a wave, but it doesn’t surprise me. It feels right, natural. I am part of this place, bound to its geometry, its energy, its purpose. I stretch out a limb—tentacle? filament?—and feel it ripple through the air, responding to the faint pulses of light between the plates.  

I move, gliding along the seams, tracing the fractal patterns etched into the metal. My touch sends shivers through the Grid, tiny adjustments rippling out beneath me. I am a tendril, a caretaker, easing the shifts of the plates, smoothing the transitions. It’s not work, not exactly. It’s instinct, purpose, flow.  

In the hollows between plates, I meet others like me. Their forms are different—some are twisted spirals, others branching networks—but we are the same beneath the surface. We communicate in pulses of light, vibrations of energy, sharing fragments of memory, of myth.  

*Before the Grid,* one hums, its form flickering with patterns I almost recognize. *Before the sun was still. Before the air was thin.*  

*The builders,* another adds, its filaments trembling with something like longing. *They made this place. They made us.*  

But the builders are gone, vanished into the depths of time, and we are left to tend to their creation. We do not know why the Grid exists, only that it does, and that it is our home.  

I drift across the surface, feeling the weight of the sky above me, the vastness of the Grid below. I am small, yes, but I am part of something immense, something eternal. My form shifts, adapting to the task at hand—smoothing a seam, coaxing a filament, tracing a fractal.  

Sometimes, I pause and watch the sky. It is unchanging, a flat, depthless blue, but I feel drawn to it nonetheless. Perhaps there was something there, once. Perhaps there will be again.  

When my energy wanes, when my form begins to unravel, I feel no fear. I dissolve into the Grid, my essence absorbed, my memories scattered. I know I will return, reborn from the pulse of the plates, reshaped by the hum of the Grid.  

I am Shuulik. I am caretaker, dweller, fractal. I am part of the Grid, and the Grid is part of me.  

And together, we endure.
