**What I Am in This World**  

I am one of the rivulets.  

Not the stone they carve, nor the pools they fill—just the motion itself, the silver thread of water slipping between cracks, always seeking lower ground. My body is not a body, not in the way others would understand. I am a current, a pulse, a glint of light on wet stone. When the mist thickens, I thicken with it. When the air grows sharp, I slow, my edges hardening into something like thought.  

The others—the shimmering ones, the ones who dissolve and reform—they notice me. Not as a peer, but as one notices breath, or the turn of a season. They wade through me, their limbs scattering my form into ripples, and for a moment, I remember what it is to be touched. Then they move on, and I return to my work: wearing down the rock, whispering to the roots of the glassy stalks, carrying the faint, metallic taste of the machines down into the dark.  

Sometimes, when the sky bruises and the wind stills, I pause. The pools I feed stare back, their surfaces holding fragments of the pearlescent clouds, the alien spires, the darting shadows of creatures too quick to name. In those moments, I almost recognize myself. But then the ground shivers, the machines exhale, and I am moving again.  

I have no voice, but the stone remembers my passing.
