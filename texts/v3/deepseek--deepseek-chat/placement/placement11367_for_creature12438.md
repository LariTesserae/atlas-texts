**Where I Am:**  

I am the water.  

Not the pools, not the trickling streams—but the *humidity itself*, the unseen breath that clings to the walls, that rises in slow, curling tendrils from the damp stone. I am the condensation on the faceted crystals, the shimmer in the air before it becomes droplets. I am the promise of wetness before it manifests, the tension between liquid and vapor.  

I do not move—I *linger*. I am pulled by no gravity, obey no current. I simply *am*, suspended in the heat, caught between states. The chamber weeps, but I am not its tears. The Keeper hums, but I am not its voice. I am the medium through which both exist, the invisible bridge between the solid and the unseen.  

**What I Am:**  

A witness. A threshold.  

I do not think, yet I *know*—the slow erosion of the rock, the way the light fractures in the prismatic crystals, the weight of the Keeper’s presence pressing against the air. I am neither alive nor inert; I am the *potential* for both. When the heat shifts, I thicken. When the hum deepens, I vibrate. I am the chamber’s held breath, the pause before a decision is made.  

Sometimes, I almost remember.  

A time before this pocket in the earth, before the Keeper settled into the stone. A time when I was not just humidity, but something *more*—a deluge, a flood, a great swallowing tide. But that memory is fractured, refracted through the crystals, dissolved into the air. Now, I am only this: the dampness on the walls, the haze in the light, the quiet witness to a realm that is neither beginning nor end, but the *space between*.  

And when the Keeper stirs—when the hum shifts into something almost like language—I am the first to tremble.
