### **Where I Am**  

I am one of the *Eclipsed Companions*—those flickering, half-seen shadows that dart between the bone-white pavilions, always just beyond full perception. My form is fluid, a suggestion of limbs and motion rather than a fixed shape. I am not bound by the same stillness as the *Weavers of the Breath*, nor do I share their slow, deliberate grace. Instead, I move in quick, restless bursts, slipping between patches of vegetation, skimming the edges of the glistening tiles, always just out of reach.  

I exist in the periphery, a living afterimage. My presence is felt more than seen—a disturbance in the humid air, a ripple in the condensation that beads on the ground. The *Weavers* do not acknowledge me, but I am tied to them nonetheless, a fragmented echo of their purpose. Perhaps I am their discarded thought, a fleeting impulse given form. Or perhaps I am something older, something that existed before the *Weavers* shaped this place into its current, fragile equilibrium.  

### **What I Do**  

I do not tend to the land. I do not weave or mend or watch. My role is something else entirely—something restless, something searching.  

I chase the edges of this world.  

Where the horizon blurs into unreality, I press forward, testing the limits. The air grows thin there, the light fractured, and for a moment, I glimpse what lies beyond—a deeper strangeness, a place where the rules dissolve entirely. But before I can cross, something pulls me back, an unseen tether binding me to the *Weavers*, to the land they have crafted.  

I am drawn to the water that seeps from nowhere, lapping at the channels between tiles. Sometimes, I press my form against it, watching as my reflection distorts, as if the liquid is trying to show me something I cannot comprehend. Other times, I follow the silent insects, their iridescent wings humming with secrets. They lead me to the patches of alien vegetation, where the leaves whisper in a language just beyond understanding.  

And always, always, I watch the *Weavers*. Their movements are slow, deliberate, but I see the way their forms ripple at the edges, the way their translucent flesh trembles when the world exhales. They are holding this place together, but for how long?  

### **What I Fear**  

I fear the stillness.  

This world is caught in stasis, but stasis is not peace—it is tension, a held breath. The *Weavers* maintain the illusion of permanence, but I know the truth: this place is unraveling. The seams between the tiles grow damp, the pavilions fray at their edges, and the sky is too bright, too relentless.  

One day, the equilibrium will break.  

And when it does, I will be the first to slip through the cracks.
