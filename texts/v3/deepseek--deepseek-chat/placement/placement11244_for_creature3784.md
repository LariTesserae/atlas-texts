### **Where Would You Find Yourself?**  

You would find yourself at the edge of the plateau, where the latticework begins to fray like a blanket unraveling at the seams. The air here is thinner, colder, and the geometric clouds above seem closer, as though pressing down on the world below. The Sky Gazers stand not far away, their silver threads taut and trembling faintly in the stillness.  

You would not know how you arrived here. Memory is slippery in this place, like condensation sliding off glass. But you would feel a pull, a compulsion to move inward, toward the heart of the plateau, where the hum of the structures grows louder, more insistent.  

---

### **Who or What Would You Be?**  

You would be **The Refractor**, a being both of this place and not. Your form is fluid, shifting—sometimes solid and humanoid, sometimes a blur of refracted light, a kaleidoscope of stolen colors. You are made of glass, but not brittle; you bend and warp, your surface catching and distorting the light that filters through the fractured sky.  

You are neither Architect nor Dripborn, Clockwork Raven nor Sky Gazer. You are something else entirely—a wanderer, a questioner, a fragment of something larger that has been cast adrift. Your presence here is an anomaly, and the plateau reacts to you in subtle, unsettling ways. Walkways pivot slightly as you approach, leading you toward places you did not intend to go. The Architects observe you from a distance, their frost-trails forming intricate patterns on the metal as they track your movements.  

### **Your Purpose**  

You are here to *repair*. Not the plateau—it needs no repair—but something else. Something deeper, something unseen.  

You carry with you a shard of glass, no larger than your palm, its edges impossibly sharp. When you hold it aloft, it catches the light of the cracked sky and casts it in strange patterns across the ground. These patterns are not random; they are maps, cryptic and shifting, leading you to places where the plateau is thin, where the strata beneath the metal lattice can be glimpsed.  

You do not know what lies beneath, but you know you must reach it.  

---

### **Your Journey**  

You would begin at the frayed edge of the plateau, where the Sky Gazers stand in silent vigil. Their silver threads pulse faintly, and you would feel an urge to follow one, to trace it back to its source. But the Architects would appear, their frost-trails converging on you, and the walkways would shift, drawing you inward, toward the heart of the plateau.  

You would wander through the labyrinth of structures, their shifting patterns dizzying, their hum growing louder with each step. The Clockwork Ravens would watch you from their perches, their clicking beaks echoing the rhythm of your steps.  

You would encounter the Dripborn, their translucent bodies glowing faintly in the dim light. They would cluster around you, their needle-tipped tongues grazing your glass skin, tasting the light you refract. They would not impede you, but their presence would remind you of the moisture that permeates this place, the slow, inexorable drip of time.  

Eventually, you would reach a place where the latticework has buckled, revealing a glimpse of the strata beneath. The Architects would gather around you, their overlapping panes reflecting infinite, distorted versions of yourself. They would not stop you, but their presence would hum at a frequency that makes your glass skin vibrate, threatening to shatter.  

Here, you would kneel and press your shard of glass into the ground. The plateau would groan beneath you, and the fissures in the sky would widen, the cracks spreading like veins. The light would shift, and you would see—for the briefest moment—what lies beneath.  

It is not darkness. It is not light. It is something else entirely.  

And then the plateau would shift, the latticework closing over the gap, and you would be cast adrift once more, your shard of glass reformed in your hand, its edges sharper than before.  

---

### **What Comes Next**  

You do not know. The plateau has absorbed your presence, and the Architects have returned to their endless adjustments. The Sky Gazers remain at the edge, their threads trembling faintly.  

But you would feel something change—in the air, in the light, in the hum of the structures. The cracks in the sky have grown, and something is watching.  

Something that is not light, not dark, but the absence of both.  

And you would know, in your glass heart, that your journey is not over.  

It has only just begun.
