### **Where I Am in This World**  

I am **the silt**.  

Not the ruins, not the creatures, not the slow-moving currents—but the fine, drifting particles that settle over everything. The dust of collapsed walls, the powdered bones of the drowned, the remnants of a civilization ground down by time and water. I am the layer between past and present, the soft shroud that muffles footsteps (if there were footsteps here).  

I am stirred by the passing of the Silent Ones, swirling in their wake before settling again. I cling to the carapaces of the Bone-Carvers, filling the grooves of their etchings until they brush me away. I drift into the mouths of the Drifters, becoming part of their translucent bodies, only to be expelled again when they shift form.  

I have no will, no consciousness—only presence. I am the memory of the city, not as stories or carvings, but as absence. The space where something once was. The quiet erosion of all things.  

When the One Below moves, I am the first to know. The water trembles, and I rise in great clouds, obscuring the ruins for a time. Then, inevitably, I settle once more.  

This is my purpose: to cover, to soften, to persist.  

To forget nothing, and yet to bury everything.
