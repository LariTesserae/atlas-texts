In the Hanging Gardens of Xol-Narai, I am **Selarith**, a Weaver of Roots. My existence is bound to the city’s slow, organic pulse, my purpose to guide the living architecture of this place into new forms. My skin is pale green, veined with faint golden lines that mirror the sap-filled paths I tend. My hair is a cascade of fine, fibrous strands that shimmer like dew-kissed spider silk, and my eyes are deep pools of amber, reflecting the soft glow of the Gardens.  

I move through the city with a deliberate grace, my bare feet brushing against the fibrous pathways as if they are an extension of my own body. My fingers are long and dexterous, tipped with delicate thorns that I use to trace patterns on the living walls, coaxing the vines and roots into new shapes. When I touch them, they respond with a subtle shudder, thickening or thinning as needed, their growth quickened by my presence.  

I am not alone in my work. My companion is an Aerisae named **Lyrith**, a spindly creature with translucent wings that shimmer like frosted glass. They perch on my shoulder or hover nearby, their movements mirroring mine as we weave through the Gardens. Lyrith’s presence is a quiet reassurance, their faint hum harmonizing with the creak of the vines and the rustle of leaves. Together, we tend to the city, our bond a testament to the symbiotic relationship between the Lumenesti and the Aerisae.  

My home is a small chamber within the heart of the Gardens, its walls woven from tightly braided vines and its ceiling a lattice of broad leaves that filter the light into a soft, green glow. The floor is carpeted with moss, soft and cool beneath my feet, and the air is thick with the scent of resin and nectar. Here, I rest on a bed of broad leaves that curl around me like a cradle, nourished by the sap-filled blossoms that hang from the ceiling.  

My rituals are subtle and deeply personal. Each dawn, I press my hands to the living walls of my chamber, tracing the patterns of growth that echo the city’s slow, steady breath. I listen to the faint creaks and rustles, reading the story written in the fibers, and guide the roots into new configurations. Occasionally, I gather with other Weavers in a quiet grove, where we extend our arms upward in unison, our gestures mirrored by the plants around us. These gatherings are not marked by celebration or frenzy, but by a deep, shared understanding—a communion with the Gardens that is both sacred and mundane.  

I have no need for tools or mechanisms; my body is a conduit for the city’s growth, my touch a catalyst for its transformation. My existence is measured not in hours or years, but in the slow, deliberate pulse of the Gardens. When my time comes to an end, my body will be returned to the city, placed within a cradle of roots that slowly consume me, my essence becoming part of the fabric of Xol-Narai.  

In this place, I am both student and teacher, weaving and unraveling the threads of the Gardens’ story. I am Selarith, Weaver of Roots, and I am the living embodiment of the Hanging Gardens of Xol-Narai.
