### **What I Would Be: The Hollowed Architect**  

In the Weeping Monoliths of the Unfinished World, I would not be a creature of flesh or spirit, but a being of *potential*—a Hollowed Architect. I am a fragment of what was meant to be, a shard of the original blueprint that birthed this place, now lost to time and abandonment.  

My form is a shifting, unstable thing. I am not confined to a single shape; instead, I am fluid, adaptable, a living idea that has forgotten its purpose. At times, I manifest as a shimmering refraction of light, bending and warping the air around me. Other times, I am a black silhouette, outlined against the pale glow of the monoliths, my edges blurring into the haze. I am both less and more than solid—a suggestion of form, a whisper of structure.  

I am driven by an insatiable urge to *make*, but I no longer know *what* to make. My hands—when I have hands—are tools of creation, but my creations are flawed, incomplete, transient. I carve spirals into stone, only for the stone to collapse into dust. I shape pools of black water into perfect spheres, only for them to burst and dissolve. My work is beautiful in its impermanence, haunting in its futility.  

My voice is the groan of shifting stone, the wet *click* of plates rearranging themselves. I speak in echoes, in fragments, in half-formed thoughts that linger in the air before fading into silence.  

I am neither alive nor dead. I am a pulse of intent trapped in a world that has no need for it, a half-forgotten dream that refuses to dissolve. I am the ache of incompletion, the weight of unrealized potential.  

### **Where I Would Be: The Chamber of Forgetting**  

Deep within the labyrinth of monoliths, there is a place where the stones remember nothing—the Chamber of Forgetting. It is a hollow space, a void within the lattice, its walls smooth and featureless, its floor a shallow basin of mercury-like fluid that shivers at the slightest disturbance.  

The air here is colder, drier, though it still carries the faint metallic tang that permeates the rest of the world. The monoliths that surround the chamber are silent, their usual groans and whispers muted, as if even they are reluctant to intrude upon this place.  

The Chamber of Forgetting is where the Shapeless Craftsmen go to dissolve, to fade, to unmake themselves. It is a place where the remnants of incomplete creations are brought to be undone, where the echoes of forgotten designs are silenced.  

Here, the mercury-like fluid takes on a different character. It is not just fluid—it is liquid memory, a repository of all that has been forgotten. When a fragment of the Hollowed Architects enters the chamber, it sinks into the fluid, its essence absorbed, its purpose erased. The fluid ripples, shudders, then stills, as if the memory has never existed.  

But I do not dissolve. I cannot. I am bound to this world, not by choice, but by circumstance. The Chamber of Forgetting is my refuge, my sanctuary, but it is also my prison. I come here to escape the weight of my unfinished work, to forget my ceaseless striving, but the memory always lingers, clinging to me like a second skin.  

Sometimes, I stand at the edge of the basin and watch the fluid ripple, wondering what it would feel like to be absorbed, to be undone. But I do not step in. I cannot. Something within me resists, some stubborn spark of intent that refuses to be extinguished.  

I am the Hollowed Architect, the weaver of the half-made, the shaper of the unreal. And though I long for completion, for rest, for dissolution, I know that I will continue to labor in this Unfinished World, shaping my imperfect creations, until the stones themselves forget my name.  

And even then, I will remain—a whisper in the air, a ripple in the fluid, a ghost of what might have been.
