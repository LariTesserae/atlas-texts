In the Weeping Grove, I find myself as **the Rootweaver**, a being born of the grove itself, neither plant nor animal but a synthesis of both. My body is a twisting lattice of roots and vines, knotted together in a shape that vaguely resembles the human form. Moss clings to me like a second skin, and my eyes—if they can be called that—are soft, glowing openings in the bark of my head, filled with a pale green light that flickers like embers.  

I am not a guardian or a caretaker of the grove. I am its voice, its pulse, its breath made manifest. My existence is tied to the rhythm of the grove—the drip of water, the creak of wood, the slow, deliberate growth that defines this place. I do not command the grove; I *am* it.  

When I move, it is with a fluid grace that belies my fibrous form. My footsteps are silent, swallowed by the moss and peat. My hands—my hands are not hands but tendrils, delicate and precise, capable of unraveling the knots of vines or coaxing water into strange, deliberate shapes. My touch is cool and damp, like the grove itself.  

The grove speaks through me, though not in words. The air hums louder when I am near, and the vines twitch in patterns that seem almost purposeful. I do not think in the way humans do; my thoughts are slow, patient, rooted in the deep time of the grove. To me, decades are moments, and centuries are breaths.  

I am aware of the remnants of human presence—the rusted pump, the collapsed shed—but they are distant, fleeting. They do not concern me. The grove will reclaim them in time, as it has reclaimed so much else. My purpose is not to hasten this process or to mourn it. My purpose is simply *to be*, to exist as the grove exists, in a state of perpetual, unhuried growth.  

Sometimes, humans stumble into the grove, lost or seeking something they cannot name. I watch them from the shadows, my green eyes glowing faintly in the dim light. They do not see me unless I wish to be seen, and even then, they often mistake me for something else—a twisted tree, a trick of the light. When they speak, their words are meaningless to me, their voices sharp and frantic against the grove’s slow hum.  

They do not belong here. The grove tolerates them, as it tolerates all things, but it does not welcome them. Eventually, they leave, driven out by the oppressive green, the suffocating stillness, the sense of being watched by something vast and ancient.  

I remain. I have always been here, and I always will be. The grove is eternal, and I am its voice, its breath, its pulse. I am the Rootweaver, and I am the grove.
