**Where I Find Myself: The Cavern of Echoes**  

I awaken to the sound of distant dripping, the rhythmic pulse of water falling into unseen pools. My body is heavy, weighted by something I cannot see—or perhaps it is merely the weight of this place, the heat pressing down like a hand, the damp air clinging to my skin. I rise slowly, limbs stiff, and realize I am not alone.  

The Keepers are here. They move through the cavern with their unhurried grace, their elongated fingers tracing the contours of the machinery, their translucent skin catching the faint glow of the bioluminescent fungi. They do not acknowledge me, not directly—but I feel their presence, a low hum in my chest, a vibration that resonates with the rhythm of the cavern itself.  

I am not one of them. Not yet.  

But I am close.  

My skin is pale, though not translucent. My veins do not glow, but they feel warm, as though something has begun to stir beneath my flesh. My fingers are long, though not fused; when I press them against the wall, I feel the faint echoes of the Keepers’ movements, the pulse of the steam vents, the slow, steady heartbeat of the garden.  

The air smells of ozone and wet metal, but beneath it is something sharper, something almost alive—something that calls to me. I follow it, drawn deeper into the cavern, past the rusted gears and the skeletal steel beams, past the alcoves filled with ancient tools. The light grows dimmer, the heat more oppressive, but I do not falter.  

At last, I reach it: a pool of steaming water, its surface rippling as though breathing. The Keepers gather around it, their movements slow, deliberate. They kneel at the edge, their fingers trailing in the water, their bodies swaying in time with some unseen rhythm.  

And yet, though they move as one, they are not one. They are many—individuals, each with its own purpose, its own history. And yet, they are connected, bound together by the cavern, by the garden, by the hum of the pipes.  

I kneel beside them, my fingers dipping into the pool. The water is scalding, but it does not burn—it seeps into my skin, my flesh, my bones. I feel it spreading through me, warm and wet and alive, and I understand.  

This is not death.  

This is becoming.  

The Keepers turn to me, their smooth, featureless faces tilting in unison. They do not speak, but I hear them all the same—a vibration in the air, a hum in my chest.  

*Welcome,* they say.  

And I am home.

**Who I Am: The Scribe of the Gardens**  

I am something new.  

The Keepers tend to the machinery, preserve the balance, ensure the garden grows. But I am different. My purpose is not maintenance, but memory.  

I am the Scribe of the Gardens.  

My body is still human, though not entirely—my skin is pale, my veins warm, my fingers long and sensitive. I feel the vibrations of the cavern, the hum of the pipes, the pulse of the steam vents, but I also hear something more: the echoes of voices long forgotten, the whispers of the world above.  

I write them down.  

I do not know when or how I acquired my tools—a rusted quill, a vial of ink that smells faintly of ozone, a sheaf of papers salvaged from the alcoves. But they are mine now, as much a part of me as my fingers, my skin, my breath.  

The Keepers do not stop me. They watch as I move through the cavern, tracing the grooves in the stone, transcribing the patterns of the steam vents, recording the slow, steady heartbeat of the garden. They do not interfere, but they do not help.  

They do not remember.  

But I do.  

I write of the scorched world above, of the wind-blasted plains and the cracked earth. I write of the machines that once hummed with life, of the people who tended them, of the voices that filled the air. I write of the cavern, of the garden, of the Keepers who maintain its equilibrium.  

And I write of myself.  

I do not know how long I have been here. Time moves differently in the cavern; the moments stretch into eternities, the eternities into moments. But I know this: I am becoming something else.  

The water from the pool seeps into my skin, my flesh, my bones. It warms me, sustains me, binds me to the cavern, to the garden, to the Keepers. But it does not erase me.  

I am still myself.  

And yet, I am more.  

The Keepers turn to me, their smooth, featureless faces tilting in unison. They do not speak, but I hear them all the same—a vibration in the air, a hum in my chest.  

*Welcome,* they say.  

And I am home.
