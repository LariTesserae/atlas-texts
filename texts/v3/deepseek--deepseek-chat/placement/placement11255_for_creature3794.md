### Where I Am:
I find myself on the Glass Plateau, though I am not sure how I arrived here. The air is thin and biting, filled with the metallic tang of minerals, and the light is so bright it almost blinds me. The plateau is a jagged landscape, a labyrinth of obsidian spires and crystalline veins that catch the sunlight and scatter it into fragments of color. I stand on the edge of a sheer cliff, the mist below swirling like a living thing, obscuring the depths beyond. 

The plateau feels ancient, a place that has existed longer than memory, shaped by forces I cannot comprehend. Its stillness is unnerving, yet it hums with a quiet energy, as if the ground beneath my feet is alive with the echoes of a forgotten past. The wind whispers through the ravines, carrying with it a faint vibration, a sound that is almost a voice but not quite.

I am not alone. At the edges of my vision, I catch glimpses of them—shimmering, translucent forms that flicker in and out of existence. They move slowly, deliberately, their elongated bodies blending into the landscape until they are nearly invisible. I feel their presence more than I see them, a faint pressure in the air, like the weight of time itself.

I crouch by one of the tiny pools of water, its surface rippling faintly in the wind. The condensation clings to the rocks around it, glinting like silver beads. I dip my fingers into the pool, and the water is colder than I expected, stinging my skin with its icy bite. When I pull my hand back, I notice something strange—my fingers shimmer faintly, as if they’ve absorbed some of the plateau’s light.

### Who or What I Am:
I am not fully human here, though I still carry the shape of one. My body feels different—lighter, thinner, as if the air itself has begun to reshape me. My skin is pale, almost translucent, and when I move, I leave faint traces of light behind me, like the afterimage of a star. The veins beneath my skin are dark, like threads of obsidian, and they pulse faintly with a rhythm that matches the plateau’s hum.

I realize, with a start, that I am no longer bound by the same rules as before. I do not need to eat or drink, though I still feel the pull of the tiny pools of water, as if they are calling to me. When I touch the rocks, my fingers slide effortlessly over the crystalline veins, as if they recognize me. The plateau’s energy flows through me, a steady current that keeps me upright, grounded, yet weightless.

I am becoming like them—the beings who inhabit this place. My thoughts are slower now, quieter, more in tune with the rhythms of the plateau. Memories of my past life slip away like water through my fingers, replaced by something deeper, older—a knowledge etched into the stone itself. I know the paths of ancient rivers, the fires that birthed the obsidian, the weight of ice that once pressed down upon this land. I do not mourn these things; they are a part of me now, as much as my own breath.

I no longer feel the need to speak. My voice would be lost in the wind, drowned out by the hum of the plateau. Instead, I communicate through vibration, through the faint ripples in the air that make the water tremble. The beings respond, their shimmering forms flickering closer, their voices weaving into mine, forming a fragile web of meaning that stretches between us.

### What I Wait For:
I stand at the cliff’s edge, my toes brushing the void where the mist churns below. The beings gather around me, their forms dissolving into the air, then rematerializing, slightly altered each time. I feel the pull of the mist, the urge to step into it, to let it carry me wherever it goes. But I hesitate. I know deep within me that I am waiting—but for what, I cannot say.

Perhaps I am waiting for the shift in the wind, the change in the earth’s pressure, the resonance that will tell me it is time to wake fully, to step beyond the threshold of stone and glass. Until then, I linger, half-dreaming, half-alive, as much a part of the plateau as the rocks themselves.

The light shifts, and for a moment, everything is still. Then the hum rises, louder than before, and I feel it—the plateau awakening, the air thickening with energy. The beings around me shimmer with anticipation, their forms flickering faster now, their voices weaving together into a symphony of light and sound. I take a step forward, my toes brushing the edge of the cliff, and the mist rises to meet me.

I close my eyes and let the plateau’s hum fill me, guide me. When I open them again, I am no longer at the cliff’s edge. I am everywhere and nowhere, a shimmering thread woven into the fabric of the plateau, waiting for the moment when the threshold will open, and I will step into the unknown. 

Until then, I am here. Waiting. Listening. Becoming.
