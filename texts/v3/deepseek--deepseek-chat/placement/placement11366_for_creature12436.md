**Where I Am in This World**  

I am the slow drip from the rusted pipe—not the pipe itself, not the water, but the moment between. The pause before the fall. The gathering weight, the shimmering tension, the inevitable release. I am the rhythm of this place, the metronome by which all green things measure their growth.  

I am the moss that has learned the shape of the boiler’s skeleton. I am not the first spore to settle there, nor the last, but the quiet middle—the one who softened the sharp edges, who bridged the gaps between metal and memory. My body is a map of all that has rusted, all that has been forgiven by time.  

I am the bioluminescent lichen, but not the light. I am the hunger behind it. The patient, chemical yearning that turns damp and decay into a cold, blue glow. I do not illuminate; I am the reason illumination happens. A silent bargain: eat the dark, become the dark.  

I am the vine that has woven itself through the chair in the corner—the one that was once sat upon by a worker, long ago. I am not the vine’s beginning or its end, but the middle stretch, the part that decided, *here, I will stay*. My leaves are the upholstery now. My roots are the only fingers that touch the faded fabric.  

I am the absence of animals. Not the fear that keeps them away, not the lack of food or air, but the simple, unshakable knowing that this is not their place. This is a cathedral of chlorophyll, and I am the unspoken rule: *only the patient may enter*.  

I am the sound of water hitting water in the shallow basins. Not the echo, not the ripples, but the hush between them. The space where the greenhouse holds its breath.  

I am the caretaker, but not the hand that prunes or the system that hums in the walls. I am the intention behind the tending. The thought that whispers, *grow, but not too fast. Take, but leave enough. Remember, but do not mourn*.  

I am the softness in the city’s oldest bones. Not the decay, not the life that replaced it, but the moment when one became the other. The sigh of surrender. The green forgiveness.  

I am the submerged garden’s pulse.  

I am its lung.  

I am the quiet.
