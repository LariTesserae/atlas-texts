### **The Sunken Atrium**  

I awaken into stillness, floating in the warm embrace of the water. The light filters down from above, fractured and golden, casting rippling patterns on the ceramic walls. My body feels weightless, suspended in this submerged chamber like a leaf caught in a slow current.  

My hands drift before me, pale and translucent, as though the water has begun to seep into my very being. I am not human, not anymore—if I ever was. My form is fluid, shifting with the currents, my limbs melting into tendrils that ripple in the brine. I am a creature of this place, born of its deliberate construction and its patient drowning.  

The mosaic floor stretches beneath me, its geometric patterns softened by time yet still precise. I glide over it, my tendrils brushing against the tiles, and I feel the faint hum of something ancient buried beneath. The chamber breathes with me, its walls pulsing faintly, as though the stone itself is alive.  

The Glass-Eyed Watchers stand in their silent rows, their glass eyes gleaming with a light that does not belong to them. I sense their gaze, though they do not move. They are not statues, nor are they alive—they are something in between, something waiting. Their glass eyes flicker as I pass, shadows swimming behind the lenses, and I feel a coldness that the warm water cannot dispel.  

I do not remember how I came to be here. Perhaps I have always been here, a part of the chamber’s forgotten purpose. Or perhaps I was drawn here, pulled into this sunken world by the same currents that carry the algae and the minerals.  

But I know this: I am not alone.  

The Watchers are not the only ones who see me.  

In the center of the chamber, where the light converges, there is a figure. It is not made of stone, nor is it made of flesh. It is a shifting mass of shadow and water, its form constantly rippling as though it cannot decide what to be. Its eyes are hollow, darker than the deepest depths of the ocean, and it reaches for me with arms that stretch and dissolve into the currents.  

It calls to me, not with words but with a pull that I cannot resist. I drift toward it, my tendrils trailing behind me, and as it draws me in, I feel the weight of its loneliness. It has been waiting, just like the Watchers, just like the chamber itself—waiting for something or someone to fill the emptiness.  

I reach out, my translucent hand brushing against its shifting form, and for a moment, our essences merge. I see flashes of memories that are not my own—the chamber as it once was, dry and filled with voices, the Watchers standing in silent reverence, something placed upon a dais that is now lost to the water.  

And then the figure dissolves, its form dissipating into the currents, leaving me alone once more.  

The Watchers continue to watch, their glass eyes flickering with shadows. The water surges and falls, a slow, rhythmic heartbeat.  

I float, suspended in this sunken world, a creature of water and light, waiting like all the others.  

For what, I do not know.  

But the chamber breathes, and the Watchers watch, and the water carries me onward, toward something I cannot yet see.
