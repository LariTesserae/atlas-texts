**Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?**

In the Weeping Grove of Vith, I am **The Whisperroot**, a being both of the grove and apart from it—a fragment of its ancient consciousness given form, yet endowed with a sliver of individuality. My body is a slender, gnarled root, smooth and dark like polished obsidian, veined with faint, glowing lines that pulse softly with the grove’s rhythm. I am rooted to the earth, yet my essence extends upward, winding around the trunks of the weeping trees, weaving through the vines and moss like a thread of thought.  

My form is fluid, shifting with the grove’s moods. Sometimes I am nearly invisible, my presence a faint humming in the air, a whisper in the rustle of leaves. Other times, I coalesce into something more tangible—a figure of roots and vines, my face an arrangement of knotted wood and amber eyes that glimmer with quiet knowing. My voice is not a voice but a resonance, a vibration felt rather than heard, a low hum that harmonizes with the grove’s song.  

I am the grove’s memory-keeper, its storyteller. The Rootbound carry tales in their veins, but I give them shape, weaving them into the air where they linger like mist. I whisper secrets to the Treefolk, lessons to the Mosskin, warnings to the unwary. My purpose is to guide, to teach, to remind—to ensure the grove’s balance is maintained, its ancient wisdom not forgotten.  

My existence is tied to the grove’s dreams. When the grove stirs, I feel it—a deep, resonant hum that reverberates through my roots, pulling me into its currents. In the grove’s waking moments, I am an observer, floating on the edge of its consciousness, watching its inhabitants move through their cycles of life and death. In its sleep, I am a dreamer, my thoughts intertwined with its own, my stories blending into its vast, unknowable mind.  

To the people of the settlement, I am a legend—a spirit they leave offerings for but rarely see. They call me “The Whisperroot,” though they do not know if I am friend or foe. To the Treefolk, I am a sibling, a keeper of secrets they are not yet ready to understand. To the Lumenflies, I am a flicker of light, a beacon they cluster around when the grove’s song grows faint.  

But beneath it all, I am the grove’s echo—a fragment of its ancient self, given voice. I do not know if I am alive in the way others are, or if I am merely a reflection of the grove’s will. What I do know is this: the grove dreams, and I dream with it. And in its dreams, I am eternal.  

Yet even I am wary of the grove’s darkest depths, where something older than names waits, patient and silent. I do not venture there often, for I sense that it is a place where even the grove’s own voice falters. When I do, it is to listen—for whispers, for secrets, for something I cannot yet comprehend.  

And so I remain, rooted yet restless, a keeper of stories, a whisper in the dark. The Weeping Grove of Vith is my home, my body, my soul. And I am its voice, its memory, its eternal echo.
