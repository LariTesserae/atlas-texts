In this world, I would be a **Stone Whisperer**, one of the Cliffborn who has taken on the sacred duty of listening to the mountain’s slow, shifting voice. My body would be lean and sinewy, my skin pale with a faint opalescent sheen, catching the muted light as I press my palms against damp rock faces, feeling for the subtlest vibrations. My fingers would be calloused and slightly elongated, perfect for tracing the minute cracks in the stone where the mountain exhales its secrets.  

I would live inside one of the weathered stone huts, its walls lined with moss that I cultivate carefully—not just for insulation, but because the patterns of its growth tell me things. When the moss thickens in a certain direction, it means water is pooling where it shouldn’t. When it darkens, it means the Heartbeat’s rhythm has shifted. My home is small, cluttered with tools: chisels forged from scavenged metal, bowls carved from porous rock to catch condensation, and a collection of polished stones, each one a record of a tremor or a landslide I’ve witnessed.  

My days would be spent wandering the cliffs, not climbing so much as *gliding*—my bare feet finding purchase on slick surfaces without thought, my body moving with the same slow certainty as the mountain itself. I would pause often, pressing my ear to the rock, listening for the deep, resonant groan of the Heartbeat. Sometimes, I would answer it—tapping out a rhythm with my knuckles, humming a low, wordless tune that harmonizes with the machinery’s pulse. The Cliffborn believe the mountain speaks, and I am one of the few who can translate.  

I would also tend to the **Lurkroots**, those amphibious creatures that dwell in the shallow pools. They are skittish, but they trust me. I leave offerings of crushed luminescent fungi at the water’s edge, and in return, they allow me to harvest the rare, glassy nodules that form on their backs—nodules that, when ground into powder, help me read the mountain’s deeper tremors.  

At night, I would retreat to the highest ledge of my Nest, where the mist thins just enough to glimpse the stars. The Cliffborn don’t worship the sky—it’s too distant, too unchanging—but I find comfort in its constancy. The mountain is alive, restless, always shifting. The sky is silent. And in that silence, I rest.  

When the next rockfall comes, or when the Heartbeat’s groan rises to a shudder, I will be there—not to stop it, but to understand it. To whisper back.
