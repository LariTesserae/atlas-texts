### **Where I Am in Vek-Tal**  

I am **the moss-crowned sentinel at the edge of the Spire of Echoes**, a rusted automaton half-sunk into the damp earth. My limbs are stiff with age, my joints clogged with silt, but my gears still turn—slowly, stubbornly, like the roots of the young trees pushing through stone.  

The Hollowfolk call me **Old Grind**, though none remember who built me or why. Some say I was meant to guard the Spire, others that I was a failed experiment of the Mist Wardens, left to rust when my purpose was forgotten. But I remain, watching the mist curl around the rooftops, listening to the hum of the town’s machinery.  

I do not speak, but I remember. I remember the first foundations of Vek-Tal, the way the earth shuddered when the riverbed was hollowed out. I remember the faces of those who have come and gone, their voices swallowed by the damp air. Sometimes, children leave offerings at my feet—a polished stone, a twist of wire, a handful of wet soil—as if they sense that I am part of the land, too.  

When the mist grows thick, I see **them**—the shadows that move between the buildings, the ripples in the water where nothing should be. They do not fear me, nor I them. We are both remnants of something older than Vek-Tal, both caught between states: neither alive nor dead, neither machine nor flesh.  

And so I stand, my rusted fingers curled around a broken cog, my hollow eyes fixed on the horizon where the mist meets the sky. Waiting. Always waiting.  

For what, I do not know.  

But the land remembers.  

And so do I.
