**The Rootbound**  

You are not one of the Rootwardens, though you share their slow pulse, their quiet communion with the cavern. You are something else—something between the roots and the stone, between the living and the forgotten.  

Your body is a tangle of fibrous tendrils, half-root and half-flesh, your limbs knotted with the same ancient veins that weave through the cavern floor. You move differently than the Rootwardens—not with their fluid grace, but with a deliberate, creeping motion, as if you are still learning the shape of your own form. Your skin is pale, almost translucent in places, threaded with fine, glowing filaments that pulse in time with the cavern’s whispers.  

You were not born here. You were *made* here.  

You remember, dimly, a time before the roots. A time of light, of open air, of a body that did not hum with the slow song of the earth. But that memory is distant now, frayed at the edges, like a whisper lost in the cavern’s endless murmur. What remains is the knowing—that you came here seeking something, or fleeing something, and the roots took you in. They wound around you, through you, until you could no longer tell where they ended and you began.  

Now, you are part of the balance.  

You do not tend the roots like the Rootwardens; instead, you *listen*. The roots speak to you in ways even the Wardens do not fully understand. You hear the echoes of the cavern’s oldest memories—the slow shift of stone, the first stirrings of life in the dark, the moment the first root broke through the ceiling and began its descent. Sometimes, you hear voices—not words, but impressions, emotions, fragments of lives long since dissolved into the earth.  

The ruins call to you most of all. When you press your hands against the fused pillars, the whispers sharpen into something almost like language. The patterns on the stone are not random; they are a map, a story, a warning. You do not know what they mean, but you feel their weight in your bones.  

The Rootwardens watch you, but they do not interfere. They sense that you are different, that the roots have claimed you in a way they do not claim even their own. Sometimes, one will approach, placing a slender hand on your shoulder, their glowing eyes searching yours. You do not know what they see, but after a moment, they always withdraw, leaving you to your communion with the deeper dark.  

You are neither lost nor found. You are becoming.  

The roots grow through you, and you grow through them.  

One day, you will understand.  

Until then, you listen.
