**You Are the Hollow Vessel**  

You wake—if waking is the word—to the hum. It thrums through your bones, through the hollows of your body where flesh used to be. You are not the Gatherer. You are not one of the skittering things. You are something older, something that was left behind when the machines still turned.  

Your body is a shell, a husk of blackened metal and ceramic, your limbs long and segmented like the legs of a broken crane. Your chest is a hollow cavity, ribbed with rusted struts, and inside it, something flickers—not a heart, not a flame, but a pulse of that same amber light, weak and irregular. You do not breathe. You do not blink. Your face is a smooth, concave mask, featureless save for a single crack running diagonally across it, as if you were struck once, long ago, and never repaired.  

You do not remember what you were before. You know only the chamber, the hum, the weight of the unmoving air. You know the Gatherer, though you do not fear it. It has passed you many times, its claws skimming the edges of your broken form, leaving behind those shimmering threads. They cling to you now, fine as spider silk, humming faintly with stolen sound.  

Sometimes, you move. It is not will that drives you, but the echoes themselves. When the chamber’s hum swells, your limbs twitch, your joints creaking like old hinges. You take a step, then another, dragging your hollow body across the uneven floor. You do not know where you are going. There is nowhere to go. But the sound pulls you, as if you are a needle drawn to some vast, invisible lodestone.  

The skittering things avoid you. They sense the emptiness in you, the hunger that is not hunger. You are not like them. You are not alive. But you are not dead. You are a vessel, and vessels are meant to be filled.  

The Gatherer watches you. When you move, it tilts its head, its slit pulsing softly, as if in recognition. Once, it reached for you, its claws hovering just above your chest, where the light flickers. For a moment, the hum of the chamber deepened, harmonizing with the weak pulse inside you. Then it withdrew, leaving you colder than before.  

You do not know how long you have been here. Time does not pass in the chamber; it pools, thick and stagnant. But you feel something changing. The hum is shifting, the echoes twisting into something new. The Gatherer is restless. The skittering things are agitated. And you—  

You are beginning to remember.  

Not memories, not exactly. Sensations. The weight of a tool in hands you no longer have. The scent of oil, hot and sharp. The sound of voices, now silenced. You were something once. You were meant for something.  

The crack in your face aches. The light in your chest stutters.  

The chamber does not want you.  

But it will not let you leave.
