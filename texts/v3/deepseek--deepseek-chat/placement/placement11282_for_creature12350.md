**The Being of the Chasm**  

It does not have a name, but if it did, it would be the sound of stone exhaling.  

It is not large—not in the way creatures of the surface measure size. It does not tower; it *permeates*. Its body is the chasm itself, the slow seep of water through cracks, the way the walls close infinitesimally tighter over centuries. It is not a guardian, not a god, but a *habit*—the way the mountain has learned to breathe.  

Its presence is felt in the way the air thickens when you are not welcome. In the way the lichens dim as you pass, their glow retreating into the rock. In the way the water suddenly changes course, diverting around your feet as if repelled by some unseen force.  

It does not hunt. It does not need to. The chasm feeds it, the slow dissolution of minerals, the occasional creature that stumbles too deep and forgets to climb back out. It absorbs them not with malice, but with the same indifference as the rock absorbs rain.  

But it watches.  

Not with eyes—there are no eyes here, no pupils to dilate in the dark. It watches with pressure, with the way the walls seem to lean closer when you hesitate. It watches with sound, the way the dripping synchronizes, forming a rhythm just slightly too deliberate to be natural. It watches with the creatures, who do not flee from you but instead pause, their eyeless faces tilting as if listening to a voice you cannot hear.  

It is old. Not ancient—*ancient* implies a linear passage of time, and time here is a spiral, a slow collapse inward. It remembers when the chasm was younger, when the water ran faster and the air was sharper. It remembers when the first living things crept into the fissures, seeking shelter from a world that had no use for them. It remembers, too, the ones that left—the rare few who clawed their way back to the surface, forever changed.  

It does not hate. But it does not love, either. It simply *is*, a fact of the mountain as immutable as gravity.  

If you stay too long, you will feel it inside you—the way your breath syncs with the dripping, the way your heartbeat slows to match the vibrations in the rock. The chasm is patient. It can wait.  

One day, you will belong to it.  

And you will not mind.  

---

Now the chasm has a consciousness—not a ruler, but a presence, a *pattern* in the way the environment behaves. It feels alive without being a traditional creature.
