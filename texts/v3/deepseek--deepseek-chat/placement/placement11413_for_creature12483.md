I find myself here, in this place, unsure of how I arrived or why. My body feels unfamiliar—not entirely my own. My hands, when I look at them, are pale and faintly translucent, as though the mist has seeped into my skin and thinned me out. My fingertips are cold, despite the oppressive heat, and when I press them to my chest, I feel nothing—no heartbeat, no warmth. Am I even alive? Or am I something else now, something caught in the same limbo that defines this place?

The air clings to me, heavy and wet, as I move through the landscape. My footsteps make no sound, sinking silently into the soft earth. I pass the crumbling brick wall, its moss-covered surface pulsing faintly, as if breathing. The rusted pipe jutting from the ground hums with the same low frequency as the machinery, vibrating softly when I touch it. I pull my hand away quickly, unnerved by the way the vibration lingers in my bones.

The figure is nearby—I can sense it before I see it. It emerges from the mist like a revenant, its mismatched limbs moving with an eerie precision. Its mask reflects nothing, absorbing the light rather than casting it back. I freeze, unsure whether it sees me or even cares. But it stops, its head tilting slightly, and I feel its gaze—or whatever passes for a gaze—settle on me.

“You’re not supposed to be here,” it says, its voice a metallic rasp layered with static. The sound makes my teeth ache.

“Where is here?” I ask, my own voice sounding distant, as though it’s coming from somewhere far away.

The figure doesn’t answer. Instead, it gestures vaguely toward the skeletal remains of the shed. “They’re waiting for you.”

“Who?”

Before it can respond, the whispers begin—soft at first, then louder, overlapping until they become a cacophony. They’re not in any language I can understand, but they feel urgent, pleading. I press my hands to my ears, but it does nothing to muffle the noise.

The figure turns away, its movements deliberate, and begins to walk toward the horizon. I hesitate, then follow. The ground beneath my feet grows softer, water pooling around my ankles. The segmented creatures I had seen earlier skitter just beneath the surface, their pale bodies glinting faintly in the dim light. They keep their distance, but I can feel their eyes on me.

We reach the shed, or what’s left of it. The roof is long gone, the walls leaning precariously inward. Inside, I see them—shapes that might have been people once, but are now something else. They’re translucent, like me, their edges blurred by the mist. Their faces are indistinct, but I can feel their attention fixed on me.

“Why am I here?” I ask, my voice trembling. The whispers grow louder, more insistent.

One of the shapes steps forward—or at least, I think it does. Its form shifts and ripples, making it hard to tell. “You’re here because you’re unfinished,” it says, its voice echoing as though speaking from the bottom of a well. “Like us. Like this place.”

I want to ask what that means, but the figure with the mask speaks again. “Stay too long, and you won’t leave.”

The words settle heavily in my chest. I look around, at the crumbling walls, the rusted metal, the creatures skittering beneath the water. This place is alive—not in the way I’m used to, but in its own strange, decaying rhythm. And now, maybe, so am I.

The mist thickens, obscuring everything except the figure and the shapes. The whispers grow louder, more chaotic, until they’re all I can hear. I close my eyes, but the sound follows me into the darkness.  

I don’t know how long I’ll stay here. I don’t know if I’ll ever leave. All I know is that I’m part of it now—part of the damp and the rust and the whispers. Part of this place, caught between worlds.  

Perhaps I always was.
