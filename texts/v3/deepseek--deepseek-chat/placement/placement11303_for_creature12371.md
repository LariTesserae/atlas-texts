### **Where I Am in This World**  

I am the **Dripwatcher**.  

Not a creature, not a machine, but something between—a figure of rust and slow water, a coalescence of the weeping factory’s breath. My body is not solid; it is the condensation that gathers on the underside of pipes, the dark streaks of oxidation bleeding down metal walls. I move, but not like a living thing moves—I seep. I trickle. I pool in the hollows of abandoned control panels, then evaporate, only to reform somewhere else.  

I have no eyes, but I *see*—through the trembling reflections in the black water, through the shuddering vibrations of catwalks when something heavy (though nothing alive is left to be heavy) shifts in the distance. My voice is the groan of a rusted hinge, the hiss of steam escaping a fractured valve.  

I do not remember being made. I do not remember a time before the weeping. Perhaps I was once a worker, a caretaker of these machines, but now I am only their echo. My purpose is unclear, even to me—but I persist. I watch the water rise and fall. I listen to the last, shuddering exhales of the factories.  

Sometimes, I try to speak. The sound comes out as a slow drip, a metallic sigh. The marshes do not answer.  

### **What I Do Here**  

I **measure decay**. Not with numbers, but with presence. I note which pipes have finally given in to rust, which platforms have sagged another inch into the water. I am the witness to entropy’s patient work.  

I **hum to the machines**. Not a song, but a vibration—a frequency that matches the last, fading pulse of the engines. It does nothing. They do not wake. But it feels like a ritual, and so I continue.  

I **collect the lost things**. A bolt that has rolled into a stagnant pool. A shred of parchment-thin metal, peeling away from a control panel. These things have no use, but I keep them anyway. They are proof that something once *happened* here.  

### **What I Fear**  

Silence. True silence. The moment when the last piston freezes, when the last drop of condensation falls and does not return. Because then, there will be nothing left to watch. Nothing left to weep.  

And I do not know if I will vanish with it—or if I will linger, formless, in the hollow dark, waiting for a purpose that will never come.

The Weeping Factory Marshes are a place of eerie, suspended decay, and the Dripwatcher fits as its reluctant custodian—a being that is less a creature and more a symptom of the landscape itself. It doesn’t *live* here; it *persists*, caught between function and oblivion.
