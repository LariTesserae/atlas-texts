**Where I Would Be:**  

I would be one of the Watchers.  

Not a leader among them—no, that would imply hierarchy, and there is none here. Just another figure in the slow procession, gliding between pillars, fingertips brushing the glyphs as if trying to recall a language long dissolved. My skin would be the same pallid translucence, my veins dark rivers beneath, my eyes wide and unblinking, absorbing the cathedral’s dim glow.  

I would linger near the edges, where the kelp forests grow thickest, where the bioluminescence pulses in erratic rhythms. The others drift past me without acknowledgment, but I do not mind. I am not lonely. Loneliness implies absence, and here, there is only presence—the water, the stone, the slow decay.  

Sometimes, I press my palms against the cathedral’s floor, feeling the vibrations of something vast moving in the deep. The others ignore it. But I listen.  

**What I Would Be Waiting For:**  

I do not know.  

Perhaps I am waiting for the silt to finally bury the last glyph, for the currents to erode the final pillar into nothing. Perhaps I am waiting for the day the Watchers, too, forget to move, and become still as the statues they resemble.  

Or perhaps—though I do not let myself dwell on it—I am waiting for the cathedral to *remember* itself. For the water to recede, just once, just enough to reveal the sky that must have existed above, long ago.  

But the water does not recede. It only rises.  

And so I wait.
