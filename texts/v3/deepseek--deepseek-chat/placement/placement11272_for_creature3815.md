**Where I Would Be in Veldrin**  

I would be one of the **bridge-tenders**, those quiet figures who move between the moss-strangled towers, ensuring the old stone arches remain passable despite the slow creep of roots and the weight of centuries. My hands would be stained green from scraping away excess growth, not to halt it entirely—that would be unthinkable—but to guide it, to keep the pathways open for those few who still traverse them.  

My home would be a half-collapsed watchtower, its upper floors long since surrendered to ferns and ivy, but the lower chambers dry enough to sleep in. A small peat stove would glow in the corner, its embers never fully extinguished, and the walls would be lined with shelves of salvaged tools—rusted shears, chisels with worn-down edges, jars of oil pressed from rooftop orchids to keep hinges from seizing.  

My work would be slow, methodical. Some days, I would simply sit on a bridge’s edge, feet dangling above the sluggish canal below, watching how the water carries strands of moss like floating hair. Other days, I would climb the spiraling staircases of the tallest spires, checking the integrity of the stones where the vines have grown too greedy. I would know the city’s bones, its weak points, the places where the balance between growth and structure is most delicate.  

I would rarely speak. Words are unnecessary in Veldrin; the city communicates in other ways—a shift in the pattern of dripping water, a new vein of luminous fungus threading through a wall, the way the ferns tremble before a storm rolls in. The few others who remain would nod as we pass, exchanging only the briefest glances. We all serve the same purpose: to keep Veldrin breathing, to ensure it does not tip too far into wildness or stagnation.  

And at night, when the mist thickens and the glow of the mushrooms becomes the only light, I would climb to the highest point still accessible and listen. To the hum of the waterwheels, yes, but also to the deeper sound—the one that might be the city itself, vibrating through the stone. Waiting. Patient. Endless.  

I would be content.
