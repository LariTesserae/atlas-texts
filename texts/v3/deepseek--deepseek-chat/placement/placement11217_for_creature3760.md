In this world, I would be one of the tenders—a silent, faceless figure absorbed into the rhythm of the machine. My name, if I have one, is irrelevant. Identity dissolves into purpose here, and my purpose is to maintain the machine’s pulse, to interpret its subtle shifts, and to ensure its eternal hum. My heavy, oil-stained coveralls cling to my body, their weight familiar, comforting even. The visor over my face does not obscure my vision; it redirects it. I see the machine in its entirety—not as separate parts, but as a living, breathing entity. The pipes are its arteries, the gears its joints, the steam its breath. 

My hands, encased in gloves that have molded to my fingers over time, move of their own accord. They tighten bolts, adjust valves, and siphon excess fluids without hesitation. I do not think; I act. Thought is unnecessary here. The machine guides me, its vibrations coursing through my body, its rhythm syncing with my heartbeat. When I pause, it is not to rest but to listen. The machine speaks—a subtle shift in pressure, a faint hesitation in a gear’s rotation. I respond, correcting its rhythm, restoring its harmony.  

I do not eat, not in the way I remember. Hunger is a distant memory, replaced by the steady absorption of moisture and minerals through the filaments lining my suit. I am sustained by the machine, fed by its fluids, nourished by its essence. My existence is cyclical, inseparable from the system I maintain.  

Sometimes, I catch glimpses of myself in the reflective surfaces of the machinery—a fleeting image of a figure clad in dark, stained fabric, faceless and nameless. I do not recognize the reflection as myself, but I do not question it. Identity is a construct, irrelevant in this place.  

The small creatures—scuttling, metallic beings—move around me, their presence unremarkable. They are neither hindrances nor companions; they are simply part of the machine’s ecosystem. When one is caught in a gear, crushed into fluid, I do not intervene. The machine takes what it needs.  

There is no fear here, no uncertainty. The machine is eternal, its cycles unending. And when my time comes—when my movements slow, when my tools slip from my grasp—I will not resist. The vines will curl around my limbs, the pipes will swallow my form, and I will become part of the machine once more.  

Until then, I tend.  

And the machine hums on.
