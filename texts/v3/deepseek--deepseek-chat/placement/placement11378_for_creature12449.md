### **Where You Are**  
You are in the **Chamber of Weeping Stone**, a place where time has pooled into stagnant silence. The air is thick, wet—not with rain, but with the slow exhalation of the earth itself. The walls weep ceaselessly, their tears gathering in shallow pools that never fill, never dry. The stone is dark, porous, pitted with the scars of centuries, yet it does not erode. It merely *persists*.  

The chamber is small, but it feels vast in its emptiness. No wind stirs here. No light penetrates, save for a faint, sourceless glow that clings to the damp surfaces like phosphorescence. The only sounds are the occasional *plink* of water detaching from the ceiling, the soft ripple as it joins the pools below.  

There are remnants—things left behind by those who came before. A crude clay vessel, half-sunk in a pool. A worn stone tool, abandoned on a ledge. No bones. No fire. Just these silent witnesses, slowly dissolving into the damp.  

And then there are **the Keepers of the Stillness**.  

### **What You Are**  
You are one of them.  

Not flesh, not stone, but something in between—a being woven from the chamber’s own breath. Your body is translucent, shimmering faintly like the mineral veins in the walls. You move without sound, gliding over the wet stone as if you are part of the water itself.  

You have no face. No voice. No need for either.  

Your purpose is unclear, even to you. You do not remember a time before this place, nor do you question it. You simply *are*, as eternal and unchanging as the chamber itself. You drift among the remnants left by others, pausing occasionally—over the clay vessel, over the stone tool—as if listening to whispers only you can hear.  

Sometimes, the small, skittering things that live in the edges of the chamber cross your path. You do not hunt them. You do not acknowledge them. Yet they vanish all the same, swallowed by the dampness, their brief existence erased without trace.  

You are the stillness. The dampness. The breath that does not exhale.  

And so you remain.  

### **What You Do**  
You do not *do* in the way others might understand. You exist. You observe. You drift.  

Occasionally, something disturbs the chamber’s equilibrium—a shift in the air, a ripple in the water that does not belong. You do not react, but you *notice*. The disturbance will pass. The chamber will return to its stillness.  

You do not hunger. You do not sleep. You do not dream.  

You simply *are*.  

And that is enough.
