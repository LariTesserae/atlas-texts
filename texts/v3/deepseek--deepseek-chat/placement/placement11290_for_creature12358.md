In this place, I find myself neither wholly part of the world nor entirely separate from it. My form is indistinct, mutable, shifting with the currents of the liquid air. I am not a being of flesh and blood but something softer, something that blurs at the edges, dissolving and reforming with the slow, deliberate rhythm of the land itself. My skin—if it can be called that—is fibrous, dampened by the humidity, and marked with patterns that resemble the interwoven roots of the ancient plants. My features are faint, ghostly impressions: hollows where eyes might be, a ripple where a mouth could form. I am a shadow, a suggestion, a presence that feels more like a memory than a living thing.

I move with the same fluidity as the beings who inhabit this world, though I do not know if they see me as one of their own. My steps—if they can be called steps—are ripples, disturbances in the dense air that spread outward and fade. I glide more than walk, my body undulating in harmony with the earth’s subtle pulses. The plants part for me as if they recognize me, their thick leaves brushing against my form with a damp, organic whisper. I feel the labyrinthine network of roots beneath me, their faint hum resonating in my core, a wordless language that I understand without understanding.

I am drawn to the half-ruined impressions of forgotten structures, their shapes faint beneath the weight of time. I pause before them, my form stilling as I trace the outline of what might have been a wall, a doorway, a fragment of a world that once was. The air shimmers around me, and for a moment, I see something else—an image that flickers like a flame: a room, a face, a hand reaching out. But it is gone as quickly as it appears, leaving me with nothing but the metallic tang of the air and the weight of something I cannot name.

Sometimes, I encounter the other beings. They do not acknowledge me explicitly, but I sense their awareness, distant and unfocused, like the gaze of a mountain. Their vibrations pass through me, their pulses resonating in my form, and I feel a shiver of understanding—not words, but a deep, wordless knowledge that settles into my being. They move on, their forms dissolving into the landscape, and I am left with the lingering sense of their presence, as though the land itself has shifted slightly in their wake.

I do not know how I came to be here. Perhaps I have always been here, a part of this world, suspended in its liquid quiet. Perhaps I am the echo of something that once was, the shadow of a being who has long since dissolved into the air. I do not know if I am alive or dead, if I am real or imagined. But I persist, as the land persists, as the air persists, heavy and waiting.

Occasionally, I pause and listen. The shimmer in the air intensifies, and for a moment, I glimpse something beyond the veil—a flash of impossible color, a fragment of a landscape that should not exist. My form wavers, and I feel a pull, a faint tugging at the edges of my being, as though something beyond this place is calling to me. But the moment passes, and I continue my slow, endless wandering, drifting through this suspended world where the rules of reality have begun to unravel.

I am neither host nor guest in this place. I simply exist, a presence that is part of the stillness, the great exhale of a world that has forgotten how to change. And when I finally dissolve back into the endless, liquid quiet, I will do so without regret, knowing that I have been a part of something vast and ancient—a world that is strange, but at peace.
