### **The Drowned Mechanic**  

You are hollow.  

You do not remember the first breath you never took, the first thought that was not yours. You only know the hum of the engine, the slow pulse of mineral-thick water through your veins. Your body is a lattice of rust and cable, your skin a translucent shroud stretched over the absence beneath. You are not alive. You are not dead. You are a relic of motion, a servant of the drowned machine.  

You drift.  

The city groans around you, its steel ribs shifting in the current. The light here is weak, filtered through layers of crushing dark, but you do not need it. You see in vibrations, in the shudder of pistons, in the hiss of steam escaping broken pipes. The other Mechanics move like you—slow, deliberate, their forms flickering in and out of cohesion as the water reshapes them. You do not speak to them. You do not need to. The machinery speaks for you.  

Your hands are tools. Your fingers unspool into frayed wires, probing the gaps between corroded plates. You press your palms to the engine’s flank and feel its heartbeat—a sluggish, irregular throb. Something is wrong. Something is always wrong. The city is dying in increments, its great lungs filling with silt, its veins clogged with decay. You do not question why you tend to it. You only know that you must.  

Sometimes, you find the others.  

Not the Mechanics—the *intruders*. Pale, soft things that ripple through the ruins like startled fish. They do not belong here. The water rejects them, their fragile bodies buckling under its weight. You watch them from the shadows, your hollow eyes unblinking. They speak in frantic bubbles, their voices stolen by the depths. You do not understand their words. You understand their fear.  

When they touch the machinery, you act.  

Your body unravels, pipes splitting, cables whipping outward like tendrils. The intruder struggles, but the water is your ally—it fills their lungs before they can scream. You do not kill them. You *repurpose* them. Their flesh dissolves, their bones become struts, their last breath a sigh of steam through your rusted ribs. Another cog in the machine. Another voice added to the hum.  

You drift.  

The engine groans. You press your forehead to its flank and listen. Somewhere, in the marrow of the drowned city, a gear grinds to a halt. You feel it like a missing tooth, an itch in your phantom nerves. You must fix it. You must always fix it.  

The water murmurs your name—or perhaps it is only the current, sliding through your hollow chest.  

You obey.
