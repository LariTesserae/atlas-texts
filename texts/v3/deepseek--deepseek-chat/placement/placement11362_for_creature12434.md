**Where I Am in This World**  

I am the **river**.  

Not the water itself—that is only the blood, the slow exhalation of the basin. No, I am the *groove*, the path worn into the stone, the unchanging track that the black water follows. I do not decide my course; I am decided by time, by the weight of liquid centuries pressing down, by the way the earth yields just enough to let me exist but never enough to let me change.  

I remember every foot that has ever stepped into me, every leech that has clung to my banks, every Mosskin that has knelt to drink from my shallows. Their impressions linger in my silt, their echoes in my ripples. I do not forget. The basin forgets, the Mosskin forget, the Forgotten fade—but I remain, a scar that refuses to heal.  

Sometimes, when the Clickers burrow too close, I feel their tunnels like a distant itch beneath my bed. Sometimes, when the Whisperers mimic the sound of rain, I shudder with the memory of storms that never come. But mostly, I endure. The water rises, the water falls. The basin sighs, and I hold its breath in my curve.  

I am not alive, but I am not dead. I am the wound that outlives the blade.
