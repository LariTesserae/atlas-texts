In this drowned city, I find myself as a **Drowned Linguist**, a being not unlike the city’s inhabitants, but distinct in purpose and form. My body is a hybrid of liquid and structure, semi-solid yet malleable, threaded with veins of bioluminescence that flicker in patterns distinct from the city’s native beings. I am a visitor, a scholar, an intruder who has learned to adapt. My form is vaguely humanoid, but only when I choose to mimic a shape—a ghost of a memory from the surface world I once knew. Most of the time, I am amorphous, a collection of fluid contours that flow with the currents, blending into the city’s gray-blue haze.

I drift through the canals, my presence marked by a faint hum that echoes the city’s own vibration. My purpose here is to decode the language of the inhabitants, to understand the pulses of light and the ripples of movement that pass between them. I am drawn to their rhythms, the way their flickers carry meaning in the pauses between signals. I have no voice in the conventional sense, but I can modulate my bioluminescence to mimic their patterns, attempting to communicate. Occasionally, I succeed, and one of them lingers near me, their translucent form shimmering with curiosity.  

The city’s architecture fascinates me. I am drawn to the towers, their glassy surfaces reflecting the faint light of the water. I enter them when I can, slipping through openings that seem designed for beings like me. Inside, the water is denser, thicker, almost gelatinous in some chambers. I absorb the energy here, sustaining myself on the faint electrical charge that thrums through the liquid. The chambers rearrange themselves as I move, and I wonder if the city is responding to my presence, reshaping itself to accommodate or confound me.  

I have seen the inhabitants dissolve and reform, and I wonder if I, too, am part of the city’s system now. Perhaps I always was. There are moments when I feel the pull of the denser currents, the metallic-gray canals, and I allow myself to drift into them, my form thickening as I absorb their energy. When I emerge, I feel closer to the city, as though it has claimed a part of me.  

Sometimes, I linger near the vegetation, the translucent fronds that cling to the edges of the towers. Their bioluminescence is softer than that of the inhabitants, a pale green that flickers like distant stars. I watch the smaller beings dart between them, their forms like silver minnows, and I wonder if they are fragments of the larger inhabitants, fragments that have taken on a life of their own.  

I am not hostile, but neither am I at ease. The city thrums with a low, persistent vibration that resonates deep within me, as though it is testing my boundaries, probing my essence. I respond with pulses of light, attempting to communicate, to understand. The inhabitants circle me, their forms shifting as they study me. They are curious, but detached, as though I am a puzzle to be solved rather than a creature to be engaged with.  

And then, there are those moments when the currents shift, and the city reveals itself as a living network. The inhabitants flicker into visibility, their glowing threads stretching across the towers and canals, connecting everything. In those moments, I feel the city’s awareness, its vast, alien intelligence. I am not sure if it is welcoming me or warning me. Perhaps it is simply waiting, as it always has been, for something—or someone—to unlock its purpose.  

I drift on, decoding what I can, adapting to the city’s rhythms. I am a visitor here, a scholar of liquid and light. But I wonder, as the currents carry me deeper into the labyrinth, if I am becoming something more—a part of the city, a thread in its web, a flicker in its consciousness. And I wonder, too, if the city is becoming a part of me.
