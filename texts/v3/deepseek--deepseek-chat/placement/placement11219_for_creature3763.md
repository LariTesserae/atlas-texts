**Who Am I in This World?**  

I am not the Dustwarden. I am not its counterpart, nor its opposite. I am... less. Smaller. A shard of something that once was, brittle and forgotten.  

I am the lichen.  

Not the whole patch, but a single tendril, reaching upward in defiance of the stillness. My body is bone-white, fragile but persistent, spiraling outward in a pattern that feels both deliberate and accidental. I do not know if I grow by choice or by some unseen force, but I grow nonetheless. Slow. So slow that even time itself might overlook me.  

The cracks in the earth are my companions, though they do not acknowledge me. They are deeper than I am, their edges sharp and unyielding. I cling to the surface, my tendrils creeping along their edges, as if searching for something—water, perhaps, though I know there is none. Still, I search.  

The Dustwarden passes over me occasionally, its cold touch brushing against my spirals. It does not linger, but I feel its presence like a weight, a reminder of the grove's indifference. My luminescence pulses faintly at its touch, a reaction I cannot control. It is not gratitude, nor fear—merely acknowledgment.  

There is no wind to carry my spores, no rain to nourish my roots. I exist in the dryness, drawing sustenance from the stone beneath me, breaking it down grain by grain into something I can use. It is a process so slow it feels almost futile, but I persist.  

I do not know if I am alive in the way that other beings are alive. I do not think, do not dream, do not remember. Yet I am aware, in a way that transcends consciousness. I am aware of the cracks, of the Dustwarden, of the faint light that filters down from the twilight sky.  

Beyond the ridges of fused stone, the world fades into shadow. I have no desire to explore it, no curiosity about what lies beyond. My place is here, in the grove, among the cracks and the dust.  

Perhaps, one day, I will spread far enough to meet another patch of lichen. Perhaps we will fuse together, our tendrils intertwining, and become something greater. Or perhaps I will remain as I am—a solitary spiral, growing in silence, until the grove itself decides it is time to stop.  

Until then, I simply *am*. Like the grove. Like the Dustwarden. Patient, still, and unyielding.
