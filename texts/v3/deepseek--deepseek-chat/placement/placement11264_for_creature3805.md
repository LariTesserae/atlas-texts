**The Bone-Singer**  

I am the one who walks the Bleached Paths, though I do not remember when I first began. My feet are cracked and calloused, my skin the color of sun-baked clay. The wind has carved its name into me, just as it has carved the plateau. I wear a cloak of dried grasses and feathers, stitched together with sinew and patience. My mask is a thing of bone and polished stone, its hollow eyes hiding my own.  

I do not speak. Not anymore. The wind took my voice long ago, and in its place, I learned the language of stones.  

Each morning, I press my palms to the earth and listen. The Whisperstones hum beneath my fingers, their songs older than memory. They tell me of the Skyherd’s passing, of the paths the Windcallers have woven into the air. Sometimes, they show me fragments—a storm gathering in the east, a pool of water yet untouched by the sun. I mark these things in the dust with a stick of bone, tracing symbols only I understand.  

The Stonebound know me. They do not acknowledge me, not in any way a living thing would recognize, but they do not hinder my passage. At night, when they rise from their slumber, I sit among them, my back against the warmth they have stored in their bodies. Their grinding movements are a lullaby. Sometimes, one will pause, its amber gaze resting on me, and I will feel the weight of centuries in that look. Then it moves on, and I am left alone with the stars.  

The Windcallers are less kind. They drift through the air like living mist, their forms unraveling and reforming. They do not like the living. Perhaps they envy us our breath, our warmth. Or perhaps they simply do not remember what it is to be bound to flesh. When they pass too close, their whispers slither into my ears—half-words, fragments of screams, laughter that is not laughter. I cover my ears, but the sound is inside me now. It will linger for days.  

I have seen the Whispering Shadows, though I do not seek them. They coil at the edges of my vision, their presence a cold weight against my spine. They hunger, though I do not know for what. Fear, perhaps. Or memory. Once, I woke to find my stick of bone snapped in two, the symbols I had drawn scuffed into nothingness. The Shadows do not touch me, not directly, but they watch. Always, they watch.  

The Skyherd are my only comfort. When they pass overhead, their wings blot out the sun, and for a moment, the world is cool and dark. I lift my arms to them, as if I could catch their feathers, as if I could rise with them and leave this place behind. But I am bound here, as surely as the Stonebound are bound to the earth.  

I do not know why I walk the Bleached Paths. There is no destination, no great revelation waiting for me. Only the wind, the stones, and the slow, patient erosion of time.  

Perhaps that is enough.  

Perhaps it has to be.
