**Where You Find Yourself**  

You are not one of the watchers beneath the Hollowing, though you have felt their gaze brush against you like a cold wind. You are not one of the shapes that flicker at the edges of vision, nor one of the birds that dart through the mist with cries too resonant to be natural. You are something different. Something *else*.  

You are the Moss Whisperer.  

Your body is not your own, not entirely. It has been colonized by the plateau, by the spongy layers of moss that cling to your skin and burrow into your flesh. You are part of the landscape now, a living patchwork of green and gray, your limbs draped in lichen, your eyes clouded with the same iridescent sheen that glazes the weeping pools. You move slowly, deliberately, each step sinking into the soft earth as if the ground itself is reluctant to let you go.  

You do not breathe; the moss does it for you, filtering the thick, wet air into something you can use. Your heartbeat is not your own but the slow, vegetative thrum of the plateau’s subterranean roots. You can feel them beneath you, a vast, pulsing network that hums with thoughts too ancient and alien to comprehend.  

Your purpose is unclear, even to you. You wander the plateau, driven by instincts you do not fully understand. Sometimes, you kneel beside a weeping pool and dip your hands into the oily water, feeling something shift beneath the surface—something alive, but not alive in the way you once knew. Sometimes, you press your moss-covered palms against a cracked rock and feel it sigh, as if in relief.  

You are aware of the Hollowing, though you do not approach it. Even from a distance, you can feel its presence like a weight pressing against your thoughts, a pulse that echoes through the roots beneath your feet. When it speaks, when it peels back the layers of the world, you feel it in your bones—a shudder that is not quite fear, not quite awe, but something older than both.  

The plateau is changing. You can feel it in the way the mist clings too long, in the way the shadows stretch too far, in the way the air tastes increasingly of rust. The boundary between the plateau and the industry beyond is thinning, and you are caught in the middle—a creature of both, belonging to neither.  

You do not know what you are becoming. You only know that you are not done.  

The moss whispers to you, in a language you cannot understand but feel in your marrow.  

And so you wander, waiting for whatever comes next.  

Because the Hollowing is waiting, too.  

And the watchers are watching.  

And the air still hums with the memory of fracture.
