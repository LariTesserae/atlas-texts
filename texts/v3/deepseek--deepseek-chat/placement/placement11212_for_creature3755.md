In the Drowned Halls of the Old Earth, I find myself among the **Stone-Tenders**, those small, crablike creatures who scuttle across the smoothed stone, tending to the ancient rock with brush-like appendages. My body is covered in chitinous plates the color of slate, blending seamlessly with the stone I traverse. I am blind to the light, but my senses are finely tuned to the faint vibrations in the water, the subtle clicks and shuffles of my kin.

My purpose is clear yet enigmatic: to sweep silt and debris from the stone, to keep the ancient ribs of rock free from the detritus of the water. It is a ritualistic task, one that feels both mundane and sacred. Each stroke of my brush-like limbs is deliberate, a slow, patient act of devotion to the world around me. The water presses down on me, cold and heavy, but I am adapted to its weight, moving with a grace born of necessity.

I am communal by nature, clustering with others of my kind in hollows or beneath overhangs. Our faint clicks and shuffles form a language too subtle for outsiders to understand, a symphony of sound that resonates through the water. We share no words, but our movements speak volumes, conveying messages of unity and purpose.

Occasionally, I encounter other inhabitants of the Drowned Halls. The **Blind Loom-Spiders** with their glowing webs drift into my path, their delicate limbs weaving silken nets in the currents. I move carefully around them, respecting their fragile creations. The **Silent Leviathans** glide past, their massive, eel-like bodies a reminder of the scale of this world. I feel the vibrations of their movements, a distant hum that reverberates through my bones.

The **Whispering Jellyfish** drift by, their translucent bodies pulsing with a faint, phosphorescent glow. Their tendrils brush against the stone, leaving trails of light that quickly fade. The **Abyssal Worms** emerge from their burrows, writhing in the currents before retreating back into the silt. Their pale, luminescent skin glows dimly, a ghostly presence in the dark.

And then there are the **Echo-Murmurs**, the shapeless beings said to dwell in the deepest fissures. I have never seen them, but I have felt their presence— a mimicry of the groan of shifting rock, the sigh of the currents. Are they remnants of ancient gods, or mere anomalies of the abyss? I do not know, but their whispers linger in the water, a reminder of the mysteries that lie beneath the surface.

In the Drowned Halls of the Old Earth, I am a guardian of stone, a keeper of the ancient rhythms of water and rock. My existence is simple, yet profoundly connected to the world around me. I move through the darkness with a quiet purpose, tending to the stone, communing with my kin, and listening to the whispers of the abyss. Here, in the heart of the deep, I am at peace, a part of the eternal, shifting currents that define this forgotten place.
