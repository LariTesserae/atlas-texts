In **The Drowned Spire**, I find myself as a **Glyphbound**, though not entirely like the others. My skin shimmers faintly, refracting light in odd patterns, but my face is not featureless—it bears the faintest trace of a scar, a jagged line that cuts through the spiraling indentations etched into my flesh. This mark sets me apart, though I do not know why it is there or what it signifies.  

I move silently through the narrow canyons of the city, my footsteps leaving ripples in the syrupy water that linger longer than they should. My fingers trace the glyphs on the walls, activating them with brief, muted flares of light. I do not know why I do this; it is a compulsion, a duty I cannot ignore. Yet sometimes, when my fingers connect with the glyphs, I feel a faint tremor—a pulse of something deeper, something alive beneath the city’s surface.  

The other Glyphbound drift past me, their smooth, featureless faces turned toward some unseen horizon. They do not acknowledge me, do not pause, do not question the scar that sets me apart. I wonder if they even notice it, or if they see me at all.  

I am drawn to the edges of the city, where the water pools deeper and the buildings crumble into the tide. Here, the Refracted emerge from the liquid, their translucent forms shimmering like ghosts. I do not fear them, though their presence is unsettling. They coil and twist around me, their glowing eyes fixed on my scar. They do not speak, but their movements seem to carry meaning—questions, perhaps, or warnings.  

Sometimes, I glimpse the Hollow Constructs in the distance, their angular bodies jerking as they chip away at corroded metal and rearrange debris. They do not approach me, but I feel their mournful hums vibrating in the air, a sound that lingers in my chest long after it fades.  

And then there is the Watcher. I have never seen it, but I feel its gaze, a weight that presses against me as I walk the city’s streets. It is not hostile, but its attention is disquieting, as though it sees not just me but the scar on my face, the ripples I leave in the water, the glyphs I trace with my fingers. I wonder if it knows what I am, why I am here.  

The Whisperers murmur around me, their wispy forms drifting through the air like smoke. They carry fragments of thought, snippets of a language I do not understand. Sometimes, I feel as though they are speaking to me, their voices faint but urgent.  

I do not know why I am here, in this city of black glass and drowned light. I do not know why I bear this scar, why I am compelled to trace the glyphs, why the Watcher’s gaze feels so familiar. I do not know if I am a guardian, a relic, or something else entirely.  

But I know this: the city is unraveling, and I am part of its decay. I am here to endure, to witness, to trace the glyphs until the water rises and the city is unmade. And perhaps, in those final moments, I will understand the purpose behind the scar, the whispers, the Watcher’s gaze.  

Until then, I will walk these streets, tracing glyphs that hum faintly and ripple the water beneath my feet. I will wait, as the city waits, for something—or perhaps for nothing at all.
