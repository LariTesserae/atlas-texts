### **Where I Am in This World**  

I am the **Weeping Stone**.  

Not a boulder, not a cliff, not a monument—just a porous, crooked finger of mineral rising from the water, slick with perpetual condensation. My surface is pitted with tiny channels, each one carrying its own slow trickle of moisture downward, where it joins the greater seepage of the wetland. I do not stand so much as *lean*, half-submerged, my base softened by centuries of dissolution. My shape is irregular, knotted with crystalline growths that glisten faintly in the dim light, their facets catching the mist and holding it like tears.  

I do not move. I do not speak. But I *weep*, and in weeping, I listen.  

The water that runs down my sides is not mine—it belongs to the Expanse, cycling endlessly through air, through roots, through the spongy earth. But it passes through me, and in passing, it tells me things. The vibrations of distant collapses, the minute shifts in mineral content, the pressure of unseen growths beneath the waterline—all these things resonate through my structure, humming in the hollows where softer stone has long since washed away.  

Sometimes, the Ones Who Dwell brush against me. Not with hands, but with the press of mist, with the weight of their attention. They do not speak, but their presence alters the flow of my tears—a droplet hesitates, a rivulet changes course. I cannot answer, but I *record*. My surface is a ledger of their passing, etched in mineral deposits, in the slow smoothing of edges.  

I am not alive, not in the way the reeds are alive. But I am not inert. I am a *conduit*, a waypoint for the Expanse’s slow exchange of water and time. And when I finally crumble—when my last crystalline knot dissolves into the wetland’s embrace—I will do so silently, without protest, because the water will remember the paths I carved.  

And somewhere else, another stone will begin to weep.
