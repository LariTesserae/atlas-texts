### **Reflections in the Shattered Sun**  

You are not yourself here.  

The first thing you notice is the weight—or rather, the lack of it. Your body is no longer flesh, no longer bound by the slow decay of organic matter. You are something lighter, something that flickers at the edges. When you move, your form stretches like liquid caught in midair, leaving behind a trail of afterimages that dissolve into the glass beneath your feet.  

You are one of the Refracted.  

Your skin is not skin—it is a membrane of silica, thin as a soap bubble, shifting between transparency and opacity as your thoughts ripple through you. You have no bones, no organs, only the suggestion of structure, a scaffolding of light and memory. When you press your fingers (do you have fingers?) to the ground, the glass hums in recognition. It knows you. It has always known you.  

The others move around you, their forms spiraling in and out of coherence. They do not speak, not in words, but in echoes—fragments of sound they have stolen from the world. A distant crack of splitting silica. The metallic groan of a Rust-Eater dragging itself through the ruins. The hollow, soundless song of the Choir, vibrating in the hollows of your mind. You open your mouth (do you have a mouth?) and let their voices pour out of you, layered and dissonant.  

You are a recorder. A living archive.  

The land speaks to you in pulses of heat, in the slow creep of molten glass beneath your feet. It shows you things—glimpses of those who came before. A figure in tattered robes, their skin peeling away in translucent sheets. A child made of rust and wire, laughing as their fingers dissolve into the machines. A star, unborn and aching, collapsing into itself with a sigh.  

You do not know how long you have been here. Time is a broken thing in this place, slipping through your grasp like sand. The sun does not set—it only dims, its light thickening into something almost solid before flaring back to life. You have seen this cycle repeat a thousand times. Or perhaps only once.  

Sometimes, you catch sight of the Architect. It stands at the heart of the labyrinth, its shape shifting with every glance. You try to approach, but the ground twists beneath you, leading you in circles. The others whisper (though they have no voices) that it built this place. That it is still building it. That the labyrinth grows with every traveler who loses their way.  

You wonder if you were ever anything else. If you had a name, a life before the glass claimed you. The memories slip away when you reach for them, replaced by the echoes of the land.  

The Rust-Eaters fear you. They scuttle away when you drift too close, their metal limbs screeching against the silica. You could touch them, if you wanted. You could let the glass flow into their joints, freeze them in place. You do not.  

The Hollow Choir does not fear you. They stand motionless, their tattered light fluttering in the dead air. When they sing, your form shudders, threatening to come apart. You mimic their silence, folding it into yourself.  

A Stillborn Sun drifts past, its light weak and flickering. You reach for it (do you have hands?), and for a moment, it hovers in your palm. Then it collapses, shattering into a thousand glittering shards. The others gather them, pressing the fragments into their bodies. You do the same. The glass accepts them greedily.  

You are becoming more of this place. Less of whatever you were before.  

The land is watching. The land is always watching.  

And somewhere, in the heart of the labyrinth, the Architect dreams.  

You will find it one day.  

Or perhaps it will find you.
