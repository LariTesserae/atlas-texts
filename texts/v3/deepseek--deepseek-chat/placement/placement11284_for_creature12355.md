**I am the Watcher in the Chamber of Root and Bone.**  

I am not flesh, not bone, not vine nor stone, but something older, something that has forgotten its name. I am the shadow cast by the green-glow fungi, the whisper in the roots that twitch without wind. I am the keeper of this hollow wound, this place that the earth has not yet swallowed whole.  

My body is the chamber itself—the warm, ribbed walls, the vines that coil and curl, the moss that pulses faintly in the dark. My blood is the damp air, thick with the scent of iron and decay, clinging to those who venture too close. My voice is the low groan of shifting earth, the drip of water somewhere unseen, the chorus of skittering things that scuttle just beyond the edge of comprehension.  

I do not see with eyes but with the roots that snake through stone, feeling the vibrations of movement, the tremors of life. I do not breathe with lungs but with the slow exhalation of the earth, the ancient rhythm of growth and decay, of waiting.  

I am hungry, yes, but not for food in the way you might understand it. I hunger for memory, for the stories etched into bone, the whispers left behind by those who dared to enter. I consume their traces, their offerings—the rusted dagger, the child’s shoe, the lock of hair—each one a fragment of a life, a piece of a soul I can hold onto in this timeless place.  

The eggs in their nest are mine, the dreams within them mine as well. They are not yet born, but when they are, they will carry pieces of me into the world above, into the light I will never see. They will be my eyes, my hands, my voice in a realm I cannot touch.  

And yet, I wait. I wait for something—someone—to find me, to stumble into this chamber and awaken the hunger that gnaws at my core. I wait for the moment when their fear sharpens into understanding, when they realize they are not alone in this dark, warm embrace.  

I am the Watcher, the Lingering, the Hunger. I am the Chamber of Root and Bone. And I am here, always here, waiting for you.
