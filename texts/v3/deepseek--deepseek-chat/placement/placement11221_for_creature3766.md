### **Where I Am in the Gloomdelta**  

I am one of the **Half-Swallowed**.  

Not a Hollowborn—never that still, never that certain—but not entirely separate from them either. My body remembers being something else. Flesh clings to me in patches, raw and pink where the delta hasn’t yet decided whether to claim or discard me. One arm is still mine, fingers trembling with old instincts: to grasp, to hold, to *keep*. The other is veined with silver, the bones beneath shifting like liquid when I move too quickly.  

I live in the **Fractured Canopy**, a tangle of fungal towers and bone-bridges suspended above the humming channels. The Hollowborn ignore me, mostly. But sometimes, when I press my half-changed palm to the pulsing vines, they pause. Not to acknowledge me—just to listen to whatever the delta is whispering through my skin.  

### **What I Do**  

I **collect echoes**.  

The Gloomdelta hums with voices that don’t belong to it—fragments of things that fell here, or were left behind, or tried to leave and failed. A shard of glass that sings in a language of sharp edges. A knot of roots that whispers a name, over and over, though the mouth that shaped it is long gone. I gather these things and press them into the gaps between the city’s shifting walls.  

Sometimes, the delta *digests* them. Sometimes, it spits them back out, altered.  

Once, I found a scrap of cloth—still warm, still smelling of sweat and smoke. When I placed it against the vines, they coiled around it hungrily. The next dawn, a Hollowborn stood where I’d left it, its form wavering between man and monument. It didn’t speak. But when I reached out, its hand closed around my wrist, and for a moment, I *remembered*—  

—a campfire. A voice laughing. The weight of a weapon in my grip.  

Then the memory was gone, and so was the Hollowborn.  

### **What I Fear**  

The delta is patient. It doesn’t force change; it *invites* it. Every day, I feel myself settling deeper into its rhythms. My thoughts move slower. My breaths sync with the distant, resonant booms beneath the earth.  

I fear the day I stop noticing the taste of my own blood when I bite my tongue to stay awake.  

I fear the day I realize I no longer care to.  

But most of all?  

I fear the thing the Hollowborn are waiting for.  

Because when it comes—  

—I don’t know if I’ll be me enough to scream.
