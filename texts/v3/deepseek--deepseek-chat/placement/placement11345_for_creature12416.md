### **The Rootbound Pilgrim**  

You are not native to this place.  

You arrived—though *arrived* is too deliberate a word. You *woke* here, tangled in the fibrous embrace of the Breathing City, your limbs interlaced with roots that pulsed against your skin like veins. You do not remember how long you have been here. Time is measured in growth spurts, in the slow decay of one vine as another takes its place.  

You are human, or something like it. Your body is still flesh, still warm, still breathing—but the city is working on that. Fine rootlets have begun to thread beneath your skin, not piercing so much as *melding*, seeking the rhythm of your pulse. Your fingers are stiff, your joints aching with the weight of sap-thickened blood. Your hair has become a nest for creeping tendrils, and when you exhale, the air leaves your lungs heavy with the scent of loam.  

You are becoming.  

You do not know if this is a blessing or a curse. The city does not care. It only *takes*.  

### **What You Do Here**  

You walk. There is no destination, only the necessity of movement. The root-mat yields beneath your feet, springing back slowly after each step, as if reluctant to let you go. Sometimes, you climb—scrambling up knotted vines, hauling yourself onto swaying platforms of interwoven stems. The higher you go, the thicker the air, until breathing feels like drinking.  

You watch the Weavers. They do not acknowledge you, but you have learned to read their movements. Where they cluster, the roots grow restless. Where they disperse, the city exhales. You follow them sometimes, not out of curiosity, but because their paths are the only ones that feel *intentional* in this place of chaos.  

You eat—though *eat* is not quite right. You press your mouth to split bark and drink the slow ooze of sap. You peel back damp leaves to find swollen fungal bulbs, bursting with liquid when bitten. The food here is alive when you consume it, and it continues to shift inside you. Your stomach growls in a language of rustling leaves.  

You sleep. Or you try to. Rest comes in fits, curled in hollows where the roots have parted just enough to cradle you. Your dreams are green and formless, full of distant groans and the sensation of something growing *through* you. When you wake, your skin is damp with something that is not sweat.  

### **What You Fear**  

You fear stillness.  

The city is always moving, always shifting—but you have seen what happens to things that stay in one place too long. A vine that does not stretch will be swallowed by its neighbors. A pool of water left undisturbed will thicken into syrup, then into something gelatinous and alive.  

You fear that one day, you will stop walking. That your legs will root themselves into the mat beneath you. That your arms will stiffen into branches. That your breath will slow, will sync, will become just another whisper in the Breathing City’s endless song.  

You fear that when it happens, you will not even notice.  

### **What You Hope**  

(Do you hope?)  

There is no escape. You know this. The city stretches in all directions, endless and hungry.  

But sometimes, when the light filters just so through the canopy, you imagine that the roots part differently ahead of you. That the Weavers drift closer when you pass, as if curious. That the city is not just consuming you, but *learning* from you.  

You hope—if hope is the right word—that when the roots finally take you, they will remember what you were.  

That somewhere in the Breathing City’s vast, pulsing heart, there will be a shape that was once yours.  

And that it will be beautiful.
