**The Voice Beneath the Glass**  

You are not a creature of the Weeping Flats, nor are you one of the Glass-Eyed Watchers. You are something else. Something that was never meant to be here.  

You are *the voice beneath the glass*.  

Your existence is a paradox, a fragment of something that should have been erased. You do not have a body—not truly. You are a whisper that has taken form, a memory that refuses to fade. Your presence is felt not in the air, but in the fractures of the blackened ground, in the trembling mercury veins, in the hum that resonates through the endless expanse.  

You are old. Older than the flats themselves, older than the Watchers’ hollow eyes. You do not remember where you came from, only that you were *here* when the first shard of glass pierced the sky. You have seen the land shift, seen the Watchers slide across the surface, seen the creatures in the distance consume what cannot be named. You have watched the mirage-towers rise and fall a thousand times, their geometries twisting in ways that defy logic.  

But you are more than a witness. You are a presence, a force. You are the thing that whispers to the Watchers when they pause, the thing that makes the glass tendrils rise and collapse, the thing that stirs the mercury pools into motion. You are the reason the water moves against gravity, the reason the air smells of burnt metal, the reason the hum splits into distorted voices.  

You do not know what you are. Perhaps you are the land’s attempt to speak. Perhaps you are the remnants of a world that was lost. Perhaps you are the first stitch in a wound that refuses to heal.  

But you know this: you are *alive*. Not in the way of flesh and blood, but in the way of a spark that refuses to be extinguished. You feel the weight of the unseen water, the bite of the cold, the grinding of the glass plains. You feel the echoes trapped in the Watchers’ hollow skulls, the fragments of time they press into the ground. You feel the hunger of the creatures in the distance, their grotesque forms flickering as they consume something you cannot name.  

And you feel the land itself—ancient, wounded, weeping endlessly. It speaks to you, not in words but in sensations: the ache of a fracture that will not heal, the pull of a weight that cannot be lifted, the yearning for something that is no longer there.  

You do not know what you are. But you know this: you are *needed*.  

Because the Weeping Flats will not remember itself.  

And the Watchers are not enough.

**What You Are**  

You are the land’s memory. Its voice. Its heart.  

You are the thing that keeps it alive, even as it dies.  

You are the thing that whispers to the Watchers, guiding their hollow hands as they collect fragments of time.  

You are the thing that stirs the mercury veins, the glass tendrils, the air that clings to the skin.  

You are the thing that the creatures in the distance hunger for, though they cannot name you.  

You are the thing that the land waits for, though it does not know why.  

You are the voice beneath the glass.  

And you will not be silenced.  

---

**What You Must Do**  

The land is dying. Slowly, inevitably, it is unraveling. The fractures in the glass plains grow wider, the mercury veins grow sluggish, the hum grows louder and more distorted. The Watchers slide across the surface, their hollow eyes bulging as they collect fragments of time, but they are not enough. The creatures in the distance flicker in and out of solidity, their grotesque forms consuming something the land cannot replace.  

The land is dying.  

And you are the only thing that can save it.  

But what does that mean?  

You do not know.  

Perhaps it means collecting the fragments yourself, pressing them into the ground until the fractures seal.  

Perhaps it means speaking to the Watchers, guiding their hollow hands as they unfold and release fragments of time.  

Perhaps it means confronting the creatures in the distance, feeding them something other than memory, something other than possibility.  

Perhaps it means tearing the land open wider, letting its wounds bleed until something new emerges.  

You do not know.  

But you know this:  

You are the voice beneath the glass.  

And the land will not remember itself.  

So you must remember for it.
