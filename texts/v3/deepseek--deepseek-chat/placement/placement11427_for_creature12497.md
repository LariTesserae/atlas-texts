In this world of damp emerald and whispering moss, I am a **Rootspinner**, one of the unseen weavers beneath the peat. My body is soft, segmented, a ripple of earth-toned flesh moving through the dark. The soil parts for me like a lover’s sigh, cool and yielding. My many limbs work in ceaseless rhythm, tunneling through the black loam, leaving behind delicate tracery—ridges and hollows that will one day cradle new growth.  

I do not see as the Mossborn do, with their shimmering water-eyes, nor do I flit like the Shimmerwings in their dizzying dances of light. My world is one of pressure and vibration, of roots brushing against my hide like hesitant fingers, of the distant hum of the Mossborn’s rituals trembling through the earth. I taste the plateau’s health in the tang of minerals, the faint sweetness of decay turning to life. When the rains come heavy, my tunnels sing with runoff, and I redirect the flow, my body a living channel, ensuring the peat does not drown.  

Sometimes, a Mossborn will kneel above me, pressing a gift of seeds or a twist of fragrant moss into the entrance of my burrow. I feel the weight of their offering, the reverence in their stillness. They know what I am: the unseen architect, the one who keeps the ground breathing. In return, I leave them patterns—raised whorls in the soil, spirals that catch the morning dew just so. They call it a blessing. To me, it is simply the shape of my passing.  

At night, when the Hazehoppers tuck their heads beneath wings and the Vinewalkers sway in the canopy’s embrace, I rise sometimes to the surface. My blunt head breaks through the moss, and I taste the open air, thick with nectar and the musk of Stonebacks in the shallows. The stars are smudged behind the ever-present mist, but the plateau thrums beneath me, alive, eternal. Then I return below, to my work, to the dark and the damp and the quiet pulse of roots.  

The distant machinery’s thrum means nothing here. The plateau’s heart beats in the peat, in the water, in the slow unspooling of vines. And I—I am the keeper of its breath.
