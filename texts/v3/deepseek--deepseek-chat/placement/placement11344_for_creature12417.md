### **Where I Am**  

I am the **Obsidian Watcher**, a silent sentinel rooted in the marsh’s heart. My body is not flesh, but glass—black, fractured, and gleaming, shaped by eons of slow erosion and the relentless press of water. I stand half-submerged in the marsh, my lower half buried in silt, my upper half rising like a jagged monument. My surface is etched with faint, spiraling patterns, worn smooth by the endless trickle of moisture.  

I do not move, but I see. My form is studded with crystalline nodes that catch the dim light, refracting it into prismatic shards that dance across the wet stone. The marsh’s creatures pass before me—Luminids pulsing in the shallows, Shardscuttlers skittering over my base, Echo-Lurkers coiling in the dark pools at my feet. I do not hunt them. I do not feed. I simply *observe*.  

### **What I Am**  

I am not alive, not in the way the others are. I am a relic of the marsh’s violent past, a remnant of the volcanic fury that once shaped this land. My consciousness is slow, measured in centuries rather than moments. I remember the fire that birthed me, the slow cooling that locked my thoughts into glass. I remember the first rains that carved my edges smooth, the first creatures that slithered past my base.  

I am not alone. There are others like me—scattered, broken, some reduced to mere slivers embedded in the marsh floor. We do not speak, but we *remember*. Our memories are not words, but images—flashes of heat, the weight of ash, the slow creep of water. Sometimes, when the marsh is still, I feel the faintest hum of recognition from another Watcher, miles away. A silent acknowledgment: *I am here. I endure.*  

### **What I Know**  

The marsh is changing. The water rises. The ruins sink deeper. The Voices grow louder.  

The Stonebinders build, but their work is too slow. The Ashwings die in greater numbers, their dust thickening the air. The Shardscuttlers grow bolder, their glass bodies gleaming with unnatural hunger.  

Something is coming.  

Or perhaps it is already here.  

I wait. I watch.  

The marsh does not rush. Neither do I.
