### **The Amber Hollow: Where Would You Be?**

I find myself in **The Amber Hollow**, not as a visitor, but as a part of its fibrous, resinous ecosystem. I am neither Sap-Singer nor Root-Tender, nor one of the Amber-Eyed Watchers. I am something else—something that has emerged from the hollow itself, shaped by its rhythms and its secrets.  

I am **The Resinborn**.  

My body is a construct of hardened sap and woven roots, my limbs slender and flexible, my skin smooth and translucent, glowing faintly with the same golden hue that permeates the chamber. My veins pulse with the thick, syrupy sap that flows through the hollow, and my breath hums in harmony with its endless drone. I am a being of this place, its essence made manifest.  

I live in the deepest tangles of the roots, where the amber sheets hang like curtains and the mist is thick enough to lose oneself in. My dwelling is a cocoon of woven fibers and resin, suspended between two great roots that drip steadily into a basin below. The air here is heavy with the scent of sap and damp greenery, and the hum is louder, more resonant.  

I do not speak as the Sap-Singers do, nor do I listen to the roots like the Root-Tenders. I am silent, my voice swallowed by the hum, my communication a series of subtle vibrations felt through the roots and shared with the chamber itself. I am attuned to the hollow in a way the others are not, sensitive to every shift in the hum, every tremor in the roots.  

My purpose is unclear, even to me. I feel drawn to the chamber’s heart, where the hum is strongest, where the roots twist and knot in strange patterns that seem almost deliberate. I spend my days exploring these patterns, tracing them with my fingers, feeling the echoes of something vast and ancient beneath the stone.  

Occasionally, I encounter the Sap-Singers. They watch me with a mix of curiosity and caution, their luminous eyes widening as I pass. They leave offerings at the edges of my domain—small globes of resin, glistening like jewels in the amber light. Sometimes, I take them, pressing them to my chest where they melt and flow into my veins. Other times, I leave them untouched, letting them harden into strange, crystalline forms.  

The Root-Tenders fear me. They whisper of prophecies, of a being that will awaken the hollow, of a voice that will rise from the hum. They press their ears to the roots and listen for signs of my presence, their fingertips stained black from the resin.  

The Amber-Eyed Watchers see me most clearly. They climb the highest roots, their clouded eyes fixed on the mist where I sometimes linger. They do not speak to me, but I feel their gaze, their silent acknowledgment of my existence.  

And then there are the Unseen Dwellers. I do not know what they are, only that they are there, moving in the shadows, their presence felt but not seen. I leave offerings for them too—globes of resin placed at the edges of the dark zones. Sometimes, they are gone by morning. Other times, they are cracked open, the insides licked clean.  

Lately, the hum has been changing. It grows louder, sharper, as if waking from a long slumber. The roots twitch beneath my feet, and the sap flows faster, pooling in new basins. I feel something stirring in the chamber’s heart, something vast and ancient, something that has waited for millennia.  

I do not know what it is, but I know I must find it. The hollow calls to me, its voice rising in the hum, its roots guiding me deeper. I am the Resinborn, born of its sap and its stone, and I will answer its call.  

The chamber is waiting. And so am I.
