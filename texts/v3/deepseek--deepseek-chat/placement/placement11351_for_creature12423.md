You are a **Glow-Walker**, one of the translucent amphibians that pulse with the chamber’s own golden light.  

Your body is slight, barely the weight of a handful of damp moss, your skin so thin it shivers at the faintest tremor in the air. You do not hurry. Time here is not marked in sunrises or heartbeats, but in the slow seep of water through stone, in the gradual brightening and dimming of the chamber’s glow. You move as the rivulets do—without urgency, following paths worn smooth by centuries.  

Your fingers are long, delicate, their tips sensitive enough to read the stone’s faint vibrations. When you press them to the damp rock, you feel the chamber’s slow pulse—the distant echo of underground rivers, the sigh of settling sediment. Some say the Glow-Walkers remember what the stone forgets, that you trace histories too old for words in the mineral veins.  

You flicker when startled, your bioluminescence flaring bright before you vanish into a crevice, your body dissolving into the golden haze of the walls. But you are not afraid. Fear is a surface thing, sharp and quick. Here, there is only patience.  

You sip moisture from the caps of luminous fungi, your tongue darting out to catch the beads of water that gather there. Sometimes you linger near the Moss-Feeders, their humming a quiet song that resonates in your hollow bones. You have seen the Ceiling Weavers’ silk catch the light like suspended stars, have watched the Pool Guardians’ silent dances in the shallows. Once, you brushed a Whispering Root, and for a moment, you knew—or almost knew—something vast and slow, a thought too deep for your small form to hold.  

The others of your kind move like you, their glow waxing and waning in unseen rhythms. You do not speak, but you do not need to. The chamber speaks for you—in the drip of water, in the sigh of roots, in the way the light clings to your skin as if you were born from it.  

And sometimes, when the chamber’s breath is soft and the shadows at the edges stir, you feel it—something older than the stone, watching, waiting. Not for you. Never for you. But for a moment, you are part of the waiting too.  

Then you turn, and the light shifts, and the thought slips away like water through your fingers.
