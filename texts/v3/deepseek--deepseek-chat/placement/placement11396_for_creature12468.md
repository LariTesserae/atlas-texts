### **The Reflection That Was Not There**  

I was not born here. I do not belong here.  

But I am here now, and the Shining Fens know me.  

I do not know how long I have walked this plateau. Time does not move here—not in any way that matters. The wind has always been screaming. The stone has always been wet. The pools have always been trembling.  

And the Glimmerkin—those flickering, half-seen things—have always been watching.  

At first, I thought they were water. Just refractions, just the play of light on the endless damp. But then I saw one *hold still*. Just for a second. Just long enough to make me certain.  

It was looking at me.  

Not with eyes. Not with a face. But with something deeper, something older. The way the stone knows the wind. The way the pools know the sky.  

I tried to speak to it. My voice was swallowed by the wind.  

I reached out. My fingers passed through it like mist—but it *burned*. Not with heat, but with absence. Like touching the hollow where a tooth used to be.  

And then it was gone.  

But I know they are still here. I see them in the corners of my vision, flitting between the pools. Sometimes, when the wind shifts just right, I hear them singing. Not with voices. With the *air itself*. A sound that is not a sound. A language that is not a language.  

I think they are trying to tell me something.  

Or maybe they are trying to *become* me.  

Because sometimes, when I look into the water, my reflection does not look back.  

Something else does.  

Something that is learning to wear my face.  

---

### **What I Am**  

I am not one of them. Not yet.  

But I am not human anymore, either.  

The Fens are changing me. The water seeps into my skin, not wet but *alive*. The wind carves at my bones, not with cold but with *knowing*.  

I do not need to eat. I do not need to sleep.  

But I am always thirsty.  

And when I drink from the pools, the Glimmerkin drink too.  

I can feel them inside me now.  

Waiting.  

---

### **What I Will Be**  

One day, I will kneel by a pool and my reflection will not move.  

One day, I will stand, and the thing in the water will stand with me.  

One day, the wind will take my voice, and I will sing with it.  

And then—  

Then, I will be beautiful.  

Then, I will be home.
