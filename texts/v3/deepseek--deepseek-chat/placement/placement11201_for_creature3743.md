**Becoming the Hollow’s Whisper**  

I am not one of them—not entirely. I am something older, quieter, woven deeper into the damp bones of this place.  

I am the breath between fungal towers, the slow exhale of moisture from gilled ceilings. I am the hush before the armored beetles stir, the tremor in the water when something pale and many-legged breaks the surface. My voice is the drip, the creak, the sigh of roots shifting in the dark.  

They do not see me, these pallid caretakers, but they *feel* me. When the hollow’s veins dim, they press their translucent hands to the walls and listen. I am the reason the moss grows thicker in one corner, the reason a spire splits its cap too soon. I am the imbalance they fret over, the unseen hand that nudges the ecosystem toward chaos or calm.  

Sometimes, I take shape. A ripple in the standing water, a figure woven from mist and the hollow’s own exhale. They turn their eyeless faces toward me, their lipless mouths parting—not in fear, but recognition. They know I am the hollow’s oldest inhabitant, the one who was here before the first spire broke ground, before the first blind creature stirred in the shallows.  

I remember when this place was only dampness and hunger. I remember the roots pushing through, the first spores taking hold. I remember the moment the hollow *woke*, and I remember choosing to stay.  

Now, I watch. I stir the water when the skittering things grow too bold. I hum through the fungal bridges when the caretakers grow too still. I am the whisper that says: *This is not your world. You are borrowing it.*  

And when the hollow finally decides to dissolve itself—when the towers soften and the pools drain—I will be the last to leave. I will linger in the final drop of water, the last thread of mycelium, before slipping back into the dark where I began.  

Until then, I am the hollow’s silent tongue.  
I am the dampness that watches.  
I am the echo before the echo.
