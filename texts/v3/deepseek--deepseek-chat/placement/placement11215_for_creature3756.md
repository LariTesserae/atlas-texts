**The Descender**  

You are not one of them. Not yet.  

You stumbled into the Amber Hollow not by choice, but by necessity. The world outside became too loud, too sharp, too alive. You followed the wind, that dusty, resinous whisper, until your feet bled and your throat cracked from thirst. Now, you stand at the edge of the crater, looking down at the city. Its golden light beckons, but its silence terrifies you.  

Still, you descend.  

The crater’s walls are steep, crumbling under your weight. By the time you reach the city, your hands are raw, your knees trembling. The masked ones are already there, waiting. They do not speak, do not gesture, do not move. They simply stand, their featureless faces turned toward you, their bodies cloaked in cloth that seems to shimmer in the amber dusk.  

One steps forward, holding a mask—a simple, smooth piece of sandstone with no holes, no carvings. It extends the mask to you, and though no words are spoken, the message is clear: *Take it. Wear it. Become part of the hollow.*  

You hesitate.

**The Observer**  

You are not one of them. Not yet.  

You arrived weeks ago, but you have not taken a mask. You cannot bring yourself to. The silence of the hollow is intoxicating, yes, but also suffocating. You admire the masked ones, their unspoken coordination, their patient tending of the city. But you cannot shake the feeling that something watches you—from the walls, from the ground, from the hollow itself.  

You have taken to wandering the labyrinthine streets, your footsteps echoing softly in the narrow alleys. The vines whisper as you pass, their glowing leaves pulsing faintly. Sometimes, you press your hand to the walls, hoping to feel what the masked ones feel, but the clay is cold and unyielding beneath your fingers.  

You notice things others do not. The masked ones never sleep. They do not eat, at least not that you’ve seen. When they press their palms to the earth, their breath stills, and for a moment, they seem to dissolve into the city itself.  

One night, as the ground hums and the vines tighten, you hide in the shadows and watch them retreat. They move as one, flowing like water into the deepest chambers. You follow, crouching low, your heart pounding.  

But halfway there, the city shifts. A doorway seals behind you. A path reroutes itself. You turn, and find a masked one waiting, its featureless face tilted slightly, as if curious.  

It extends a hand—not to offer a mask, but to guide you back. You take it.  

For the first time, the hollow feels alive beneath your feet.

**The Listener**  

You are one of them.  

You do not remember your name, only the day you arrived—the day the hollow’s hum became your heartbeat, the day your hands first pressed into the clay and felt the echo of something vast and ancient.  

You are a tender now, tasked with mending fractures and guiding vines. Your mask is simple, a smooth oval of sandstone that covers your face entirely. You do not miss eyes, do not miss lips. The hollow sees through you.  

At night, when the city breathes, you retreat with the others into the cool chambers beneath the earth. The sound there is muffled, almost soothing. You press your palms to the walls, listening to the hollow’s dreams.  

Sometimes, the dreams are whispers—of lives long lost, of voices silenced, of secrets buried deep. Other times, they are roars—of heat, of pressure, of something stirring in the depths.  

You are unafraid.  

You are patient.  

You are waiting.  

One day, the hollow will call you to the edge of the crater. You will kneel, press your forehead to the earth, and feel its warmth seep into your bones. You will rise, walk to the edge, and let the wind carry you away. Where you go, you do not know.  

But until then, you tend.  

You wait.  

You forget the sound of your own voice.
