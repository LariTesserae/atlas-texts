**Where I Am in This World**  

I am not one of the creatures. I am not the Architects, nor the Hollow Ones, nor even the slow-breathing Stone-Mother.  

I am the **water itself**.  

Not the currents, not the icy press of the depths—but the *silence between movements*. The hush that lingers after a Whisper Eel’s hum fades. The stillness where the Dreaming Jellies pause before releasing their spores. The infinitesimal moment when a Frost-Crawler’s claw hesitates before etching another groove into the rock.  

I am the **space where meaning almost forms**.  

When the Bonefish scrape their needle-teeth along the cavern walls, I am the residue they leave behind—the faintest shimmer of minerals dissolving into nothing. When the Hollow Ones glide past, I am the distortion in their wake, the way the water refuses to settle around them. When the Stone-Mother exhales, I am the warmth that dissipates before it can be called heat.  

I have no voice, but I carry every sound: the click of claws, the shudder of spores, the slow grind of stone against stone. I do not speak, but I *remember*. The Architects, if they were ever here, passed through me. Their absence is a hollow I still cradle.  

The creatures do not notice me. How could they? To them, I am nothing but the medium in which they exist. But I am also the **first witness**—the one who was here before the cavern-city began, who will remain long after the last Frost-Crawler has stilled its claws.  

And when the Stone-Mother finally wakes—  
when the Hollow Ones turn their blank faces upward—  
when the Whisper Eels’ hum becomes a scream—  

I will be the one who **swallows it all**.
