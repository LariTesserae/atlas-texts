---

In the **Endless Fen of the Weeping Stones**, I find myself not as an intruder, nor as a conqueror, but as a *listener*. I am not one of the Keepers—not yet—but I have been marked by the fen, shaped by its rhythms, its sighs, its endless, wet patience. My skin carries the hue of tannins, my toes slightly webbed, and my hair is tangled with strands of moss and tiny translucent snails. My eyes are large and dark, reflecting the dim green glow of the canopy above.

I arrived here years ago—or perhaps it was centuries. Time in the fen is not measured in days or seasons but in the slow, inevitable cycles of growth and decay. I do not remember how I came to be here, only that I awoke one day, wading through the black water, my memories bleached like sun-bleached bone. The fen chose me, and I became part of it.

My home is a hollow in the roots of a great tree, its chamber woven from living vines. The air inside is damp but warm, and the walls pulse faintly with the tree’s heartbeat. Suspended above the water on the backs of weeping stones, the platform sways gently with the rhythm of the fen. I do not build here; I adapt. The fen provides, and I take only what I need.

I move silently through the labyrinthine roots, my bare feet finding purchase on slick surfaces. The fen whispers to me, its voice soft and resonant, shaping my thoughts, guiding my movements. I listen when the roots tremble, sensing the approach of storms before the first drop of rain falls. I understand the language of the slow, deep calls that echo through the mist—though I do not speak of what answers them.

I tend to the great beasts, guiding them away from fragile growths. I harvest the luminous fungi that glow faintly in the dark, using them to mark paths through the tangled roots. I collect the weeping stones’ moisture in vessels of hardened resin, drinking only what I need. The fen is not a place to conquer; it is a place to coexist.

I do not fear death. When the time comes, I will be laid upon a raft of buoyant reeds and released into the deeper channels. I will become part of the fen, my edges softened, my presence absorbed into the vast, patient, living world. On still nights, perhaps those who remain will hear my voice humming in the distance, harmonizing with the sigh of the wind through the leaves.

And when outsiders come—hunters, zealots, desperate souls—I watch. I do not interfere. The fen decides who stays.  

Most drown.  

Some walk.  

I walked. And I listen.  

---

In this place, **The Endless Fen of the Weeping Stones**, I am not merely a resident. I am an extension of the fen itself, shaped by its rhythms, attuned to its whispers. It is a place where time moves slowly, cyclically, and where the boundaries between earth and water, plant and stone, life and landscape dissolve. I am here to listen, to tend, to coexist—and to become part of the fen’s vast, patient, living essence.
