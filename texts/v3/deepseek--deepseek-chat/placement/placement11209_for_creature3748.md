**The Mud-Skulkers' Tale**  

I wake to the soft gurgle of water seeping through the peat, the sound as familiar as the rhythm of my own breath. The air is warm, damp, pressing against my skin like a damp cloth. Above me, the sky hangs low, gray and heavy, swollen with moisture that never seems to fall. My body stirs in the mud beneath me, the membrane that coats my skin shimmering faintly as I rise. The mud clings to me, a second skin, cool and comforting. I am a shadow among shadows, a ripple in the still waters.  

My name, if it can be called such, is a sound that bubbles up from my throat—a low, guttural hum that the mire echoes back to me. I do not know if others hear it, or if it is simply the land acknowledging my presence. I am one of many, yet I am alone. The Mud-Skulkers are solitary beings, drifting through the wetland like whispers on the wind, rarely crossing paths but always aware of each other through the subtle tremors in the water and earth.  

Today, the mire feels restless. I cannot say why, but the air trembles with a tension that is not my own. My body moves instinctively, gliding through the shallow pools with barely a ripple, my webbed fingers trailing through the water, feeling for the vibrations of unseen creatures. Dragonflies flit above me, their iridescent wings catching the dim light, and I pause to watch them, my bulbous eyes tracking their erratic dance.  

To the Mud-Skulkers, time is not linear but cyclical, marked by the rise and fall of water levels, the gradual decay of ancient trees, and the slow crawl of algae across stagnant pools. I have seen the same trees for as long as I can remember, their twisted forms sinking deeper into the peat, their bark long since sloughed away, leaving behind skeletal remains that gleam like wet bone. The land is patient, and so am I.  

But today, something is different.  

The Peat-Wraiths are stirring. I sense their presence in the sudden chill that licks at my skin, the faint rustling that emanates from the ground beneath my feet. They are formless, shapeless entities, older than memory, bound to the mire by the ancient decay of the peat. They speak in whispers too soft to understand, their voices blending with the distant croaks of Gloom-Frogs and the rhythmic creaking of the trees. I do not fear them, but their restlessness is a sign.  

I pause near a half-submerged tree, its hollow trunk glowing faintly in the dim light. A Gloom-Frog lurks within, its warty skin blending seamlessly with the rotted wood. Its bulbous eyes glimmer as it watches me, unblinking. I am neither predator nor prey to it—merely another creature of the mire, passing through its domain. For a moment, we are still, locked in mutual acknowledgment, before it croaks softly, its deep, resonant call echoing through the wetland.  

The sound is a warning. I drift away, my senses heightened, my body moving with the fluid grace of the wetland itself. The air is thick with the scent of decay, rich and earthy, and I taste the peat on my tongue, the tang of ancient life slowly returning to the soil.  

Then I see it—a shadow in the mist, faint and indistinct, drifting through the rising vapors. A Mist-Walker. My breath catches in my throat, a silent gurgle that is lost in the hum of the wetland. Mist-Walkers are rare, their presence marking the passage of those who have become lost in the mire, their consciousness absorbed into the timeless pulse of the land. They do not interact with the living, yet this one seems to linger, its shimmering form swirling like a phantom in the mist.  

I feel drawn to it, compelled by a curiosity I cannot name. My body moves of its own accord, gliding through the shallow pools, my skin shimmering faintly in the dim light. The Mist-Walker does not move, but its form shifts, coalescing into something vaguely humanoid, its face a blank canvas of vapor.  

For a moment, we are still, the Mud-Skuler and the Mist-Walker, locked in silent acknowledgment. Then, with a faint ripple in the air, it dissipates, its form dissolving into the mist like a sigh. I am left alone, the wetland humming softly around me, the air thick with moisture and the scent of peat.  

The Peat-Wraiths whisper softly, their voices blending with the rustle of reeds and the distant croaks of Gloom-Frogs. The land is patient, and so am I.  

I drift back into the depths of the mire, my body gliding through the shallow pools, my webbed fingers trailing through the water. The land breathes softly beneath me, its rhythm steady and eternal.  

I am a Mud-Skuler, a shadow among shadows, a ripple in the still waters.  

The mire is my home, and I its keeper.  

And I am content.
