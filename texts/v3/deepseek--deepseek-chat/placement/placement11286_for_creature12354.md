I find myself at the edge of a shallow pool, my body long and sinuous, translucent as the water itself. A **lurk-fish**, they call me, though I seldom hear such words. My kind has existed here for countless generations, our forms shaped by the plateau’s unforgiving scarcity. I glide through the sediment-heavy water, my movements deliberate and fluid, an echo of the slow, inevitable erosion of the land around me. My glass-like body catches the faint sunlight, refracting it into fleeting shards of color that dance across the pool’s surface.

This pool is my world—its algae-rich edges my feast, its deeper recesses my refuge. The plateau’s creatures come here to drink, their shadows rippling across the water’s surface. The **horned grazers** lower their heads cautiously, their spiral horns gleaming as they lap at the water. I watch them from below, their faces distorted through the pool’s surface, and feel a strange kinship. We are all tied to this place, bound by its rhythms, shaped by its austerity. The **shimmer lizards** dart along the rim, their scales shifting like liquid metal, and I marvel at their speed and precision. They are the plateau’s hunters, quick and cunning, while I am its ghost, unseen but ever-present.

Sometimes, the **bird-reptiles** descend, their lean, feathered bodies casting long shadows over the pool. They drink sparingly, their eyes sharp and watchful, their calls echoing across the plateau. I feel the faint tremors of their landings ripple through the water, a reminder of their dominion over the skies. Even the **wind-dancers** come, their iridescent wings blurring as they hover above the pool, sipping at the water’s edge. They are the plateau’s enigma, their movements too erratic to predict, their presence too fleeting to comprehend.

I have never left this pool, nor do I feel the need to. It is enough to be here, to exist in this small, shimmering world. The water is my cradle and my tomb, and I am content. Yet, there is a part of me that remembers—not memories, exactly, but echoes, whispers of a time when the plateau was younger, when the rains were more frequent and the pools were vast and deep. I carry these whispers in my translucent flesh, a living testament to the plateau’s ancient, enduring story.

The stone structures rise around the pool like silent sentinels, their purpose long forgotten. I sometimes wonder if they were built by hands like mine—hands that once shaped this land, only to fade into its dust. They are a mystery, much like the plateau itself, and I feel no need to solve them. I simply exist, a ghost in the water, a fragment of a world that has settled into its final, primordial form.

As the sun sets and the plateau is bathed in amber light, I begin to sense the night’s arrival. The air cools, and the pool’s surface shivers with faint ripples. The **horned grazers** retreat to their sheltered niches, the **shimmer lizards** vanish into their crevices, and the **bird-reptiles** ascend to their nests. The plateau quiets, its pulse slowing to match the rhythm of the stars. I sink into the pool’s deeper recesses, my body nearly invisible in the fading light. The wind picks up, carrying the faint scent of earth and minerals, and I drift into stillness, a silent observer of a world that has seen eons pass and now rests, content in its quiet, enduring beauty.
