In **The Drowned Furnace**, I find myself as **Cinder**, a Pipewalker. My life is one of perpetual motion, of navigating the labyrinth of corroded pipes and submerged streets that form the veins of the dying city. My body is lean from years of climbing and diving, my muscles honed by the constant struggle against the weight of rust and water. My skin is pale, hardened by exposure to brine and ash, and my hands are calloused from handling tools salvaged from the wreckage of the city’s industrial past.

I wear a suit of cured leather, reinforced with brass plating, pieced together from scavenged materials and stitched with threads of sinew and steel. My goggles, with lenses of salvaged quartz, protect my eyes from the acidic steam that rises from the water below. A scarf soaked in fetid water muffles my face, filtering the bitter air I breathe. 

My days are spent patching leaking pipes, clearing clogged vents, and coaxing life from the groaning machines that keep the city alive, or at least prevent it from sinking further into the mire. I am amphibious, spending as much time underwater as above, diving into the flooded streets to repair the failing infrastructure below. My tools, forged from salvaged steel, are extensions of my body, each movement precise and practiced. 

The Scrimshaw Folk rely on me to maintain the systems that keep them alive, and I take pride in my work, despite the futility of it all. I am part of the closest thing the city has to a priesthood, my rituals centered around the furnaces that still burn despite the rising waters.

The creatures that inhabit the city are both allies and adversaries. The Rustmaws, armored crabs with carapaces of oxidized metal, are predators and scavengers, feeding on anything that falls into the flooded streets, including the unwary or unfortunate. The Smokeswifts, leather-winged birds with needle-like beaks, are drawn to the chimneys and vents, where they pick at the remnants of machinery and nest in the corroded hollows of the city’s tallest structures. And then there are the Ironveins, eels that writhe through the water, their bodies shimmering with oil-slick hues, their flesh said to carry a metallic tang that some of the Ashborn claim grants visions of the city’s past.

Life in The Drowned Furnace is a constant struggle against entropy, a battle waged in the shadow of decay. The Scrimshaw Folk know their city is dying, sinking deeper into the mire with each passing year, but we persist. We persist because we must, because the furnaces still burn, because the machines still turn, because the city’s heartbeat—though faint—has not yet fallen silent. We are a people forged in the crucible of corrosion, and our existence is a testament to the stubbornness of life, even in the face of inevitable ruin. 

As Cinder, I am a part of this testament, a Pipewalker who continues to fight against the rising waters and the relentless decay, not because I believe we can win, but because the act of struggle itself gives meaning to our lives in this drowned, metallic world.
