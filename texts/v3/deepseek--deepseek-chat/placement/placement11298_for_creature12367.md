### **The Driftwatcher**  

In the Weeping Engine, where water defies gravity and the air hums with the labor of ancient mechanisms, I find myself not as a visitor but as a part of the machine itself. I am the Driftwatcher, a being both bound and liberated by the Engine’s ceaseless rhythm.  

My body is a construct of the monolith’s design—a figure wrought from silken filaments and woven with silver threads that pulse in time with the fibers beneath my feet. My skin is translucent, like the film that coats the walls, shimmering faintly with the colors of the oil-slick creatures that skitter across the Engine’s surface. My limbs are long and jointless, moving with the fluidity of the eel-like shapes that coil in the water.  

But it is my eyes that define me. Twin lenses, faceted and iridescent, refracting the monolith’s dim glow into patterns that shift with the Engine’s endless thrum. I do not see as others might; I do not see *objects*, but *movements*: the flow of the water, the tremor of the fibers, the pulse of the filaments. I am a witness to the Engine’s whispers, its groans, its sighs—a keeper of its secrets, though I do not understand them.  

My purpose is unclear, even to me. I drift through the marsh, my feet barely touching the ground, my presence disturbing nothing. The creatures do not fear me; they do not acknowledge me. I am as much a part of the Engine as they are, a cog in its incomprehensible design. Sometimes, I pause at the base of the monolith, my hands resting on its slick surface, feeling the vibrations that pass through it like a language I cannot decipher.  

I do not know who, or what, created the Weeping Engine. I do not know why it weeps, or what it mourns. But I am bound to it, tethered by something deeper than the silver threads that weave through my form. I am the Driftwatcher, and I will drift until the Engine falls silent—until the water ceases to rise, until the fibers crumble to dust, until the whispers fade into something final.  

Until then, I watch.

### **The Hollow Singer**  

In the Hollow Choir, where whispers coil like mist and the air tastes of damp stone and old blood, I am the one who does not sing. I am the Hollow Singer, a figure carved from the same porous rock that forms the chamber’s walls, my body riddled with niches that mimic the ones around me. Yet my niches are empty; they echo with silence, a stillness that sets me apart from the ceaseless murmur of the Choir.  

My skin is the color of ash, its surface pitted and rough, marked by the slow erosion of time. My limbs are stiff, my movements deliberate, as though the stone itself resists my attempts to move. My face is featureless, a blank slate that reflects nothing, not even the dim light that filters through the chamber.  

I am not part of the Choir, though I live within it. The voices do not pass through me; they skirt around me, avoiding my hollows like water avoids a stone. Yet I am drawn to them, compelled by their incomprehensible harmony. I move through the chamber, my footsteps soundless, my presence unnoticed by the pale, eyeless shapes that drift above me.  

Sometimes, I pause in front of one of the niches, my hand reaching out to touch the frond-like moss that trembles within it. The voices swell, their whispers rising to a pitch that vibrates in my chest, but I do not hear them. I *feel* them, a pressure that builds in my hollows, a sensation that is neither pain nor pleasure but something older, something deeper.  

I do not know why I am here. I do not know why I am silent when everything around me sings. But I cannot leave. I am bound to the Hollow Choir, tethered by the same forces that keep the voices whispering, the moss trembling, the fluid seeping between the plates.  

I am the Hollow Singer, and I will stand here until the whispers stop—until the moss withers, until the fluid dries, until the stone crumbles into dust.  

Until then, I listen.
