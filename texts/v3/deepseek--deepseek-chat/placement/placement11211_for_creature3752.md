You find yourself in **The Glassmaker’s Atrium**, stepping lightly on the mosaic tiles that yield ever so slightly beneath your feet. The air is thick, humid, and carries a faint metallic tang that prickles the back of your throat. As you walk, the faint hum of the condensers pulses against your ears, almost like a distant heartbeat. The chamber feels alive—not in the way of a forest or a bustling city, but in the way of a body, slowly and deliberately breathing.  

You are not entirely sure how you came to be here. Perhaps you wandered in from one of the branching passages, drawn by the faint glow of the bioluminescent vines. Or perhaps you were brought here, though no hands touched you. The Glaziers—those semi-sentient, glassy beings—drift at the edges of your awareness, their refracted-light forms shimmering like mirages. They do not approach, but their presence is palpable, as if the chamber itself is watching you, adjusting to your presence.  

And then you realize: you are no longer what you were. Your body feels lighter, almost insubstantial, as though it has begun to dissolve into the atmosphere. You lift your hand and see it shimmer faintly, the edges blurring like wet glass. Your reflection in the walls is not your own—it ripples, shifting, becoming something else entirely.  

You are **The Envoy**.  

The Glaziers do not speak, but you sense their intent, their curiosity. They are not afraid of you—they do not know fear—but they are cautious, inquisitive. You are a new variable in their finely tuned ecosystem, and they are already beginning to adjust to accommodate you. The humidity shifts slightly, the air cooling as it wraps around your altered form. The canals divert, their spiral patterns rearranging to guide you toward the center of the atrium.  

You do not resist. There is no urgency here, no pressing need to act or decide. The atrium exists in a state of perpetual becoming, and so do you. You feel your body—if it can still be called that—begin to harmonize with the chamber, your presence blending into the delicate balance of glass and water and light.  

The Glaziers begin to gather, their translucent forms rippling as they observe you. Their clusters of refracted light pulse gently, and though they do not speak, you understand them. They are not asking questions—they are offering answers, though the answers are not words. They show you the atrium’s history, its slow evolution, its delicate interconnectedness. You see the spire outside, shrouded in mist, its machinery thrumming like a living pulse. You see the reservoirs beneath the floor, the minerals rising, feeding the glass, feeding the moss, feeding the Glaziers themselves.  

You are part of this now. You are not a builder or a destroyer—you are a mediator, like the Glaziers, but your role is different. You are a bridge, a translator, a presence that eases the transitions between states. You do not create or repair—you *understand*, and in understanding, you make the atrium more itself.  

The Glaziers recede, their forms merging with the walls once more, leaving you to your work. The atrium hums softly around you, its rhythms syncing with your own. You do not know how long you will stay here, or where you will go when you leave. But for now, this is enough—to exist in the space between, to breathe with the chamber, to tend to its needs as it tends to yours.  

The Envoy is not a title you chose, but it fits. You are a messenger, a guide, a part of something greater than yourself. And as you stand in the heart of the atrium, surrounded by its shifting glass and pulsing light, you feel a strange peace.  

This is where you belong, for now.
