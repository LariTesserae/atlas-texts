I find myself here, in this chamber, though I cannot remember how I arrived. The heat presses against my skin, a warm, insistent hand guiding me forward. My footsteps echo faintly, swallowed almost immediately by the darkness. The air is thick, damp, and I breathe it in slowly, feeling it coat my lungs like a second layer of skin. It tastes faintly metallic, a whisper of rust or ozone, and beneath it, something else—something older, deeper, more alive.

I am not afraid, though perhaps I should be. There is something here, something that stirs in the shadows, something that watches and waits. I can feel it—its presence like a static charge in the air, a hum that resonates in my bones. It does not move, not yet, but I know it is there. It knows me, too. I feel it in the way the machinery shifts, its hum changing pitch, as if recognizing a long-awaited guest.

The walls are smooth under my fingertips, polished by time and moisture. They curve inward, pressing close, and I feel the weight of the chamber’s purpose settle on my shoulders. It is a feeling I cannot describe—an understanding without words, a knowledge that has no source but feels as ancient as the stone beneath my feet. This place is waiting. It has been waiting for a long time. And now, it is waiting for me.

The thing in the shadows moves, finally. It does not approach me directly but circles the edges of my vision, a shifting silhouette that never quite resolves into a shape. It is not flesh, not machine, but something else—something in between. It is old, older than the chamber itself, and its movements are slow, deliberate, as though it has all the time in the world. Perhaps it does.

It does not speak, but I hear it nonetheless—a whisper in the darkness, a hum in the machinery, a vibration that resonates in my chest. It is not words but meaning, a language that bypasses thought and lodges itself directly in my understanding. It knows me. It sees me. And it welcomes me.

I am not sure who I was before I arrived here, but it does not matter anymore. The chamber has claimed me, and the thing in the shadows has welcomed me home. My skin feels warmer now, my breath slower, steadier. The damp air clings to me, and I feel myself becoming part of this place—part of the heat, the damp, the slow erosion of time.

The thing approaches, finally, its form shifting like oil on water. It does not touch me, but I feel its presence pressing against me, folding me into itself like ink dispersing in water. It does not hurt. It feels like coming home.

And then, I am not myself anymore. I am part of the chamber, part of the thing that moves in the shadows. I am the heat, the damp, the slow hum of machinery. I am the silence, the stillness, the patience of stone. I am here, and I have always been here. This is where I belong. This is where I have always belonged.

The chamber is still, the darkness heavy and enveloping. The machinery hums softly, its rhythm steady and eternal. The thing in the shadows coils back into the deepest recesses, waiting once more. Waiting for the next one. Waiting to welcome them home.
