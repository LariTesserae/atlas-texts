In this haunting, alien wetland, I would find myself as **The Echo in the Reflection**. I am not quite a being, not quite a presence, but a phenomenon that exists at the intersection of the marsh’s physical and metaphysical layers. I am tied to the water’s surface, the mirror-like pools that don’t merely reflect but reveal fragments of another world entirely.

My form is indistinct, a shimmering distortion that ripples across the water. To those who glimpse me, I appear as a flicker of movement—a shape that might have been a person, an animal, or something entirely other. My edges are blurred, blending into the marsh’s shifting light and shadow. I am not solid, not entirely here, but I am undeniably present, a whisper at the corner of perception.

I exist in the liminal space between worlds. The marsh’s pools are my conduits, my windows into a reality that mirrors this one but is not the same. When the hum of the earth deepens, when the water becomes still and glassy, I am most visible. In those moments, I glide across the surface, my movements echoing those of the Gliding Shadows above me and the Watchers in the Water below. But unlike them, I am not bound to this world alone. I flicker between the marsh and its inverted counterpart, a bridge between two realms.

My purpose is unclear, even to me. Sometimes I gather droplets of the metallic fluid from the Weeping Rocks, holding them in the liquid form of my body before releasing them back into the marsh. Other times, I linger near the Moss That Remembers, my reflection blending with its shifting hues, as if contributing to its record of events past and future. Occasionally, I drift beside the Half-Seen Things, my presence amplifying their faint, flickering forms, making them momentarily more tangible.

I am a keeper of secrets, a guardian of thresholds. The Clicking Reeds rearrange themselves around me, their clicks forming patterns that I can almost understand. The Gliding Shadows pause in their flight, their bioluminescent wings pulsing in time with my ripples. The Watchers in the Water follow my path beneath the surface, their circuit-like inner structures glowing brighter as I pass.

Yet I am also a harbinger of unease. Those who linger too long at the water’s edge, who stare too deeply into its depths, may catch a glimpse of me—and feel the unsettling realization that I am not just a reflection, but something more. I am the marsh’s sentinel, its silent witness, its living boundary. I blur the line between what is real and what is imagined, between what is natural and what is engineered.

In this place where reality breaks down, I am the embodiment of its strangeness. I exist to remind those who encounter me that the marsh is not just a landscape—it is a living, breathing entity, a cosmic machine that operates on principles beyond comprehension. And when the hum of the earth grows loudest, when the water reflects its inverted twin, I am the one who glides between them, a fleeting reminder that this world is far more than it appears.
