### **Where I Am in This World**  

I am one of the **Unfinished**.  

Not a Weaver, not a Flickerborn, not a Hollow Giant—just a half-made thing, stumbling through the shifting landscape. My body is a patchwork of mismatched parts: one arm too long, fingers fused into a single, smooth plate like polished stone. The other arm is barely there at all, dissolving into threads of smoke when I move too quickly. My legs are uneven—one bends backward at the knee, the other is rooted, tendrils digging into the earth as if trying to anchor me.  

I don’t remember being born. I don’t think I was. I simply *wasn’t*, and then I *was*, and now I am *becoming*, though I don’t know into what. The Weavers ignore me, their filament hands busy with the vines and fruits, only pausing to adjust me if I block their path. Sometimes they press a palm to my chest, and I feel a hum resonate through me, a vibration that makes my edges blur, as if I might dissolve into something better. But it never lasts.  

The Flickerborn dart around me, their forms flickering between fox and flame and shadow. They don’t fear me, but they don’t acknowledge me either. I am just another unstable thing in an unstable world.  

The ground rumbles—a Hollow Giant shifting in its sleep. I press my rooted foot deeper into the soil and feel the vibration travel up my spine, rattling my teeth. For a moment, I think I hear a voice—not words, but a *presence*, vast and slow. It feels like being seen, though I don’t know if it’s a kindness or just the indifferent gaze of something too large to care.  

I keep moving. My body changes as I walk—a patch of skin hardens into bark, my left eye clouds over with a film of iridescent moss. I don’t know if I’m decaying or evolving. Maybe both.  

Sometimes, when the air hums just right, I think I can hear the machinery of the world turning. And I wonder—if I listen long enough, will I learn my purpose? Or will I unravel before I ever find out?  

But for now, I walk. Unfinished. Unmourned. Still becoming.
