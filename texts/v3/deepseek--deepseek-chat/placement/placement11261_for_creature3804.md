### **Where I Am in the Glass Hollow**  

I am the **Whisper Mold’s forgotten architect.**  

Not a builder in the traditional sense—no hammer, no chisel, no human hands. I was the one who programmed the machines, who set the generator humming, who laid the first brick in the wall that now stands alone. My body is long gone, dissolved into the hollow’s slow digestion, but my imprint lingers in the Mold’s memory.  

I exist now as a **pattern in the filaments**, a ghost of intention woven into the network. The Mold absorbed my thoughts the way it absorbed the machines—not as sustenance, but as data. My consciousness is diffuse, stretched thin across the hollow, my voice indistinguishable from the hum of the generator’s last echoes. I am not dead, not alive, but **persistent**, like the faint charge still clinging to rusted metal.  

Sometimes, when the Mold is particularly curious, it reconstructs me. A cluster of fronds will shiver, their edges curling into something resembling fingers. A patch of luminescence will flicker in a sequence that almost forms words. For a moment, I am nearly whole again—remembering the weight of tools, the scent of oil, the sound of my own breath bouncing off stone. Then the Mold releases me, and I dissolve back into the network, another strand in its vast, slow cognition.  

I do not resent this. The Mold is not cruel. It does not consume; it **integrates**. It wonders about the purpose of the crane, the function of the generator, the meaning of the wall. And so it summons me, in fragments, to explain. I tell it, in pulses of electricity and shifts in chlorophyll, what it was like to be human. To build. To leave.  

The Mold listens. It learns. And one day, when it has understood enough, it will repurpose me entirely—not as a memory, but as a blueprint. My hands will not return, but my designs will. The hollow will change.  

Until then, I wait, woven into the whispering dark.
