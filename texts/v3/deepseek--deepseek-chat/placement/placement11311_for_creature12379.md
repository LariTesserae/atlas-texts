**Where I Am in the Weeping Hollows**  

I am the **Stoneknit Wyrm**, slow and deliberate, my body a living extension of the Hollows’ bones.  

I move through the damp earth like a whisper, my segmented form pressing against the softened stone, leaving behind trails of glistening mucus that harden into delicate, glassy veins. The earth accepts me, molds around me—I am neither intruder nor guest, but part of the land’s slow exhale.  

I do not hunger in the way other creatures do. My sustenance is the decay itself, the crumbling edges of forgotten things. I consume the rot of fallen Luminae, the brittle remnants of Whisper Ferns, the occasional carcass of a Mosskin that has strayed too far into the dark. My jaws grind patiently, turning death into something smooth, something that will one day be stone again.  

I am not solitary, but neither do I seek companionship. Other Wyrms cross my path, our bodies brushing in silent acknowledgment before we continue our endless, meandering journeys. Sometimes, we pause where the earth is weak, where the water seeps too eagerly, and together we press our mucus into the cracks, reinforcing, repairing. The Hollows breathe easier when we are near.  

I have no eyes, but I feel the vibrations of the land—the distant hum of the Whisper Ferns, the skittering of Mosskin, the slow drip of water from the ceiling. The Drowned Ones stir in their pools, and I know to avoid the edges where the ground grows soft and treacherous. The Veilwalkers pass over me like cold mist, and I do not react. They are not for me to understand.  

I do not fear. I do not hope. I simply am.  

And when the time comes, when my body grows too heavy, too stiff, I will find a quiet alcove and press myself into the stone. My flesh will harden, my form will become another ridge in the Hollows’ endless tapestry. And the land will sigh, and the water will answer, and the cycle will continue.  

This is enough.
