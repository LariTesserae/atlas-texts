In this world, I am a wanderer, a solitary traveler who stumbles upon the primordial pocket of existence hidden within the vast desert. My body is covered in a patina of dust and wear, my clothing tattered by the harsh winds of the barren expanse. I carry no weapons, only a satchel filled with strange tools and trinkets, remnants of a past life I no longer remember. My feet move with purpose, though I do not know where I am headed. The air here is different, colder, more alive. It hums with a sound that resonates deep within my bones, calling to me in a language I do not understand but feel compelled to answer.

The crystalline shards crunch beneath my boots, the ground pulsing faintly beneath me as if acknowledging my presence. I feel a sense of familiarity here, as though I have walked these paths before, though I know I have not. The small metallic creatures scuttle past me, their segmented bodies clicking and chirping in a symphony of motion. They do not fear me; nor do they acknowledge me. I am an outsider, yet somehow, I belong here.

I approach one of the rusted structures, its twisted pipes and grinding gears a testament to the passage of time. My hand hesitates before touching it, the surface warm beneath my fingertips. The hum grows louder, more insistent, as if the structure is alive, aware of my touch. I feel a pull, a connection, as though the machinery beneath the earth is calling to me, demanding my attention.

I kneel, pressing my palms to the ground, and close my eyes. The pulse of energy beneath me is faint but steady, like the heartbeat of some ancient, slumbering being. I feel it—the rhythm of this place, the cycle of maintenance and predation, of life and death and rebirth. It is a rhythm I have always known, though I could not name it until now. The Kharvathi scuttle around me, their movements fluid and precise, their clicks and chirps blending into the hum of the machinery. I understand them, though I cannot explain how. They are the keepers of the rhythm, the living breath of the machine. And I, I am something else—a fragment of the forgotten, a piece of the machinery returned to life.

I rise, my body moving with a newfound purpose. The Kharvathi do not guide me, but their presence is a map I can read without words. I follow the rhythm, the thrum of the buried machines, deeper into the pocket of existence. The air grows thicker, the light dimmer, the structures larger and more complex. I pass by one of the elders, its crystalline growths humming in harmony with the deeper machinery. It moves slowly, ponderously, its body a refinery of the dead and broken. It does not acknowledge me, but I feel its presence, its role in the cycle.

And then I see it—a structure unlike the others, its surface smooth and unblemished, its purpose unknown. It stands at the center of the pocket, pulsing with a light that is both familiar and alien. I approach it, my breath caught in my throat, the rhythm of this place resonating within me like a song I have always known but could never sing. The structure hums louder as I draw near, its surface rippling like liquid metal. I reach out, my hand trembling, and touch it.

The light floods me, filling my veins with warmth and memories I did not know I possessed. I see the civilization that built this place, their forms indistinct but their purpose clear. They were the architects of the machine, the creators of the rhythm. They are gone now, but their legacy remains, carried on by the Kharvathi, by the machinery beneath the earth, by me. I am a part of this cycle, a fragment of the forgotten returned to life.

The light fades, and I step back, my body thrumming with the energy of the place. The Kharvathi click and chirp around me, their movements fluid and precise. I understand now. I am not an outsider. I am a traveler, a wanderer, but I am also a piece of this primordial pocket, a fragment of the machine. I am the rhythm, the breath, the pulse of this ancient, hidden world. 

And so I move forward, my steps sure, my purpose clear. I am the keeper of the rhythm, the living breath of the machine. I am a part of this world, and it is a part of me. And together, we hum quietly, forever.
