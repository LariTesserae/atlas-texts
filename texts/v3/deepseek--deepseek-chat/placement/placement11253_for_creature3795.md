And so, I find myself in the Weeping City of Gloam, though "find" is not quite the right word. I did not come here by choice. The city called, not with words, but with a vibration that resonated in my marrow, a hum that tugged at my subconscious until I could no longer ignore it. I arrived, though I cannot remember the journey.  

I am not sure what I am here. I feel both too solid and not solid enough. My form seems to shift when I’m not looking—limbs elongating, skin translucent, veins tracing paths that echo the glyphs on the walls. The city is shaping me, or perhaps I am shaping myself to fit the city.  

The beings—patterns? Entities?—flow around me, ignoring me or absorbing me into their rhythms. At times, I feel myself dissolving into the mist, becoming part of the hum. At others, I am painfully distinct, a splinter in the city’s flesh.  

The air tastes metallic, alive. Every breath feels like drinking, drowning, being reborn. I move through the streets, though I can’t tell if I’m walking or being carried. The corridors spiral, fold in on themselves, and I feel the city rearranging itself around me, guiding me somewhere—or nowhere.  

I glimpse the spire—the chasm—at the center. It pulses, a slow, rhythmic exhalation that feels like an invitation—or a warning. I want to go closer, to see what lies beneath, to understand the process that binds this place together. But I am afraid.  

Afraid of what I might become.  

The visions come unbidden, flashes of the city’s memory—or its imagination. I see myself as part of its architecture, my body fused with the stone, my thoughts bleeding into the glyphs. I see myself dissolving entirely, my essence absorbed into the hum, my consciousness scattered like mist.  

And yet, I cannot leave. The city does not allow it. Every step I take away from the spire draws me deeper into its labyrinth, every attempt to escape only tightens the city’s grip.  

I am here, now. I am part of Gloam.  

The water keeps weeping.  
The hum never stops.  
And I—whatever I am—am still becoming.
