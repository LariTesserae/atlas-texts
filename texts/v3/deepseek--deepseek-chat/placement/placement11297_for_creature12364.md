**Where Am I?**  

I find myself on the edge of the known world, where the sea devours the sky and the air hums with the metallic tang of decay. The Drowned Spire rises before me, a jagged silhouette against the bruised horizon. Its rusted beams groan in the wind, and its hollow chambers echo with the whispers of the deep. Below, the water churns, restless and hungry, its surface broken by the occasional flash of silver—fish darting like living sparks.  

I am not a visitor here. I am a part of this place, as much as the Weavers or the barnacles that cling to the tower’s bones. My body is lean and scaled, my fingers long and delicate, tipped with claws that gleam like polished glass. My eyes glow faintly, casting a pale light in the shadows of the spire’s interior. I move through its passages with a fluid grace, my footsteps silent on the corroded metal.  

I am a Weaver, though I do not remember becoming one. Perhaps I was always this, born of salt and steel, shaped by the tower’s slow surrender to the sea. I feel its pulse in my veins, the rhythm of the groaning engine deep within. It is a sound both alien and familiar, a reminder that the spire lives, even as it decays.  

My hands are busy with the Nets—vast, shimmering webs of kelp and seaweed, threaded with fragments of salvaged metal. The Nets hang from the spire’s beams like tapestries, their patterns shifting in the stormlight. Each knot and twist feels deliberate, as if I am encoding a story into their strands. Perhaps I am. Perhaps the Nets are a memory, a map, or a prophecy. I do not know. I only know that I must weave them, my claws moving with hypnotic precision.  

Sometimes, I pause to watch the life that clings to the spire—the translucent crabs scuttling across the rusted surfaces, the seabirds nesting in the hollows of forgotten machinery. They are a part of this place too, though they do not know it. They do not feel the tower’s pulse, its ancient, fading song.  

And then there are the shapes that glide past the outer beams, vast and indistinct in the gloom. The tower trembles when they pass, and I feel their presence like a weight in my chest. They are curious, these creatures of the deep, but I do not approach them. I only watch, my luminous eyes tracking their movements through the water.  

The sea will take the spire eventually, just as it took the cities before it. I know this. The waves beat against its base with a relentless rhythm, eroding its bones one grain of rust at a time. But not yet. Not yet. Until then, I will tend to the groaning engine, weave my shimmering Nets, and listen to the whispers of the deep.  

This is my home. This is my purpose. And when the sea finally claims the spire, I will go with it, dissolving into the salt and shadows, my story lost to the waves. But until then, I am here, a Weaver of the Drowned Spire, tending to its ancient, fading song.
