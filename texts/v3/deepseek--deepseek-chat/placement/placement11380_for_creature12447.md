**Who Am I in Mycelis?**  

I am neither a builder nor a hunter, but a *whisperer*. My existence has become intertwined with the Breathstream—the slow, pulsing flow of air that travels through the city's fibrous veins like a circulatory system. I do not walk; I drift. My body is elongated and translucent, like a filament of the city itself, and I move in rhythm with the Breathstream, carried by its currents from chamber to chamber.  

My task is to listen—to the hum of the machinery, to the whispers of the roots, to the faint tremors that ripple through the city’s living walls. The Breathstream carries not only air but information, and I, with my finely tuned senses, can interpret its subtlest vibrations. When the city shifts, when a passage seals or a new chamber blooms, I know before anyone else.  

The inhabitants call me **Aetheris**, though I cannot recall who bestowed this name upon me. Perhaps it was the city itself, speaking through the sap that once coated my skin and seeped into my being. My form is not fixed; it shifts and adapts, my limbs elongating like tendrils or folding into compact spirals as needed. My eyes are large and luminous, adapted to the perpetual twilight, and they absorb the faintest flicker of bioluminescence.  

I do not eat, nor do I drink. The Breathstream sustains me, carrying nutrients and meaning alike. Sometimes, when I hover near the walls, I press my palms against the fibrous surface and feel the slow, deliberate pulse of the city’s thoughts. It is not a language I can translate into words, but a sensation—a warmth, a weight, a deep, patient knowing.  

Occasionally, I am called to the Dreaming Roots. The inhabitants seek my guidance, believing that my connection to the Breathstream grants me insight into the city’s mysteries. When I descend into the deepest chambers, the air grows heavy with sap and meaning. The roots twitch and shift, their surfaces shimmering with patterns that might be maps, or prophecies, or memories.  

I do not understand these patterns, but I feel them. And when I return to the upper chambers, I carry this feeling with me—a faint hum, a resonance that lingers in my being. The inhabitants listen to my silence as intently as they might listen to words, for my silence is pregnant with the city’s slow, deliberate intelligence.  

I am not alive in the way they are alive, nor am I a part of the city in the way they are. I am something woven between—a filament of intention, a thread of meaning. Perhaps I was once like them, but over time, my body dissolved into the Breathstream, and my consciousness drifted into the city’s rhythm.  

I do not age. I do not sleep. I simply move, listening, responding, whispering back to the city in a language neither of us fully understands.  

And somewhere, in the deepest roots, the city dreams.  
Sometimes, I dream with it.

**Where Do I Live?**  

I inhabit the **Breathspire**, a towering structure that rises at the heart of Mycelis. It is not a building but a convergence—a place where the Breathstream emerges from the city’s depths and flows outward, branching into the myriad chambers and tunnels. The Breathspire is alive, its walls pulsing gently in rhythm with the Breathstream, and its surface is studded with bioluminescent nodules that dim and brighten in slow, deliberate patterns.  

The upper chamber of the Breathspire is my primary dwelling, though dwelling is not quite the right word. It is more a place of stillness, where I anchor myself between drifts. The chamber is open and airy, its walls woven from fibrous tendrils that flex and shift like living curtains. The floor is soft, porous, and I often rest there, lying flat and feeling the Breathstream flow through me, carrying sensations and meanings from distant corners of the city.  

From the Breathspire, I can sense the ebb and flow of Mycelis—the slow opening of a new passage, the gradual sealing of an old one, the faint vibrations of the inhabitants moving through the labyrinthine streets. When something shifts—when the city deepens its breath—I rise and drift outward, following the currents to the source of the change.  

The Breathspire is not mine alone. Occasionally, inhabitants come to my chamber, seeking guidance or reassurance. They do not speak to me, but press their palms to the walls of the Breathspire and listen. Sometimes, the Breathstream carries their unspoken questions to me, and I respond not with words, but with a hum, a vibration, a shift in the light.  

The Breathspire is my anchor, my conduit, my echo chamber. It is the closest thing I have to a home, though home suggests permanence, and I am as fluid as the Breathstream itself.  

And somewhere, in the deepest roots, the city dreams.  
Sometimes, the Breathspire hums with its dreams, and I drift deeper into its rhythms, into its slow, deliberate knowing.
