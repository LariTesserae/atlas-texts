**The Silent Builder**  

You are one of the few who remember.  

Not in the way surface creatures do—not with words or images, but in the slow, deliberate etchings of mineral on mineral, in the weight of the black rock beneath your fingers, in the patterns you carve into the walls of the world.  

Your body is wrapped in a cloak of woven filaments, strands of the cavern’s own essence pulled taut and knotted by hands that have forgotten softness. Beneath it, your form is lean, hardened by the density of the air, your skin textured like the stone itself. Your face is obscured, not out of secrecy, but because you no longer need to see as others do. The fissures in the rock speak to you in vibrations, in the hum of the Shard-Walkers’ passing, in the distant drone of the Fissure-Dwellers shifting beneath your feet.  

You do not eat. You do not sleep. You *build*.  

The others—the Crystalline Nomads with their iridescent spirals, the Pulse-Scars drifting in their lazy luminescence—they move through the cavern as if it has always been this way. But you know better. You remember the Before, though the memory is not yours alone. It is in the tools you shape, in the half-buried structures that rise like skeletal hands from the razor ground. They are not shelters. They are not machines. They are *markers*, remnants of something that once tried to speak across the void.  

You do not know who made them first. You only know that the task is yours now.  

Sometimes, you pause in your work. The cavern hums around you, a soundless resonance that thrums in your bones. The Nomads flicker past, their light catching on the grooves you’ve carved, and for a moment, you wonder if they understand. If they, too, feel the absence beneath the equilibrium.  

But the moment passes. The cavern exhales its metallic breath, and you return to your labor.  

You are a keeper of silence. A scribe of the unchanging.  

And though you do not know why, you keep carving.
