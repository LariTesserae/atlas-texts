### **Who Am I?**

I am **Valryn**, named for the moss that cloaks the hollow. My life is woven into the damp tapestry of this place, my existence inseparable from its quiet rhythms and eternal twilight. I am one of the Mossfolk, my pale skin marked by faint greenish veins, my dark hair adorned with fragments of moss and small, polished stones. My eyes are the color of peat—dark, deep, and flecked with gold—holding within them the earth’s secrets.

My days begin with the faint light that filters through the canopy. I rise from my bed of dried moss, softened by woven reed mats, and step barefoot onto the damp ground. The air smells of damp wood and earth, of herbs hung to dry, and the slow decay of organic matter. I tend to the small garden nestled in a rare patch of soil not smothered by moss, cultivating hardy vegetables and herbs that thrive in the damp. My hands, calloused but nimble, shape wood into bowls and spoons, and carve patterns that echo the natural world—spirals like fern fronds, waves like ripples in water, circles like the depressions where the mist collects.

I move through the hollow with fluid, nearly silent steps, my feet toughened by the damp ground. The world around me is a symphony of whispers—the rustling leaves, the hum of insects, the slow trickle of water. I gather what the land provides—lichen for dyes, moss for insulation, mushrooms that sprout in the shadows. My garments, woven from plant fibers and dyed in muted greens and browns, blend seamlessly with the landscape.

### **My Beliefs and Rituals**

Like my kin, I speak little, but my silence is not emptiness—it is reverence. I believe the hollow is alive, a sentient being that breathes, watches, and listens. The trees are elders, their twisted forms holding wisdom beyond understanding. The water is the hollow’s blood, its slow trickle carrying messages from the depths of the earth. The moss is its skin, soft and protective, wrapping the land in a cloak of damp life.

My rituals are marked by soft chants, my voice blending with the hum of insects and the rustle of leaves. I leave offerings—small carvings, bundles of herbs, polished stones—in the hollow’s hidden places, tucked into the roots of trees or placed in the shallow depressions of the creek beds. I believe the hollow remembers, that it keeps our stories in the patterns of the lichen, in the carvings on the stones, in the reflections in the water.

### **My Connection to the Strange**

I am aware of the hollow’s strangeness—the deliberate twist of the trees, the carvings on the stones, the reflections that don’t match the world above. But I do not fear it. I see it as part of the hollow’s life, its way of speaking to us. The trees guide us, the stones mark our paths, the water shows us glimpses of what lies beneath the surface.

I am drawn to these strangenesses, often spending hours tracing the carvings on the stones or sitting beneath the twisted trees, listening for voices that are not there. I believe the hollow’s secrets are meant to be uncovered slowly, over generations, and I pass down my knowledge in whispers, in gestures, in the careful placement of offerings.

### **My Role in the Hollow**

I am the hollow’s caretaker, its guardian. I tend to its moss, clear its water, ensure its balance remains undisturbed. I see myself as part of its life, my existence intertwined with its damp breath and eternal twilight. I do not seek to change the hollow but to understand it, to listen to its whispers and honor its rhythms.

When occasional travelers stumble upon the hollow, drawn by its strange beauty, I welcome them with quiet hospitality, offering food, shelter, and silent companionship. But I do not share our secrets, and they leave with only fragments of understanding, carrying the hollow’s dampness with them like a memory they cannot quite grasp.

The Moss-Cloaked Hollow is my world, my life, my purpose. I am its voice, its hands, its memory. And as long as the hollow breathes, so too will I.
