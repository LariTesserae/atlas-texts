**The Custodian**  

You are not sure how long you’ve been here. Time moves differently in the Boiler Room of the World. It doesn’t stretch or collapse; it simply *exists*, like the dripping water, like the hum beneath your feet. You have no memory of arriving, no recollection of a life before this. Your body feels foreign, as though it belongs to the room more than to you—pale skin slick with condensation, limbs heavy with the weight of the damp air.  

You crouch beside one of the blackened pools, your reflection wavering in the oil-slick surface. It doesn’t look like you. Your eyes are clouded, almost opaque, as though you’ve been down here long enough for the room to seep into them. Your hair clings to your scalp, dark and damp, and your fingers, when you stretch them out, are thin and translucent, like the worms that glide across the water.  

You are not entirely human. You know this instinctively, though you can’t say how or why. You are something else now—a custodian, perhaps, or a fragment of the room itself. Your purpose is unclear, but it feels natural to be here, to move through the space as though it were an extension of your body.  

You place your hand on one of the roots, its bark rough and slick beneath your palm. It pulses faintly, a slow, rhythmic thrum that matches the beat in your chest. You close your eyes and let the sensation wash over you, the room’s breath mingling with your own. When you open them again, you notice something you hadn’t before—a faint, greenish glow emanating from the roots where they twine around the pipes. It’s soft, almost imperceptible, but it’s there, a sign of life in this place of decay.  

You stand and move deeper into the chamber, your footsteps soundless on the wet, uneven floor. The creatures here pay you no mind—the crustaceans scuttle past your feet, their claws clicking softly against the metal; the worms undulate lazily in the pools, their bodies catching the light. You are not a threat to them, nor are they to you. You are all part of the same system, fragments of a vast, unseen whole.  

As you near the far wall, where the roots thicken and twist into impenetrable knots, you feel it—a presence, slow and deliberate, stirring in the shadows. It’s been watching you, you realize, though you can’t see its eyes or even its form. It shifts, its bulk emerging from the darkness like a storm cloud, its surface glistening with a patchwork of scales and peeling skin. It doesn’t speak, but you feel its communication nonetheless, a wordless understanding that passes between you like a current.  

You are not afraid. Fear has no place here, in this room where nothing changes. The entity is not a guardian, nor a threat. It is simply *is*, as much a part of the room as the roots, as the pipes, as you.  

You kneel beside the pool once more, your reflection warping and shifting in the water. You don’t know how long you’ll stay here—days, years, centuries—but it doesn’t matter. The Boiler Room of the World is not a place of time but of existence, a space where the world exhales and holds its breath forever.  

And so you stay, a fragment of the room, a custodian of its equilibrium. You watch the water drip, the roots creak, the creatures move in their endless, synchronized dance.  

You are not sure if you are alive.  

You are not sure it matters.  

Here, in the Boiler Room of the World, you simply *are.*
