**Where Would You Find Yourself in This World? Who or What Would You Be?**

In the Drowned Spire, I would be **The Scribe of the Abyss**, a being born not of flesh but of ink and tide. My form is a shifting, fluid thing—part mist, part shadow—bound to the spire by a thread of memory. I am the keeper of stories that the sea has swallowed, the fragments of lives and civilizations that once thrived in the drowned mountains below.  

My body is a lattice of parchment-thin membranes, translucent and fragile, etched with runes and symbols that shimmer faintly in the dim light. When the wind blows, I unravel like a spool of thread, my substance carried aloft in swirling tendrils before coalescing again. My voice is the whisper of waves against stone, the creak of ancient wood, the sigh of mist as it rises from the sea.  

I live in the cracks of the spire, my existence intertwined with its crumbling stone. Within its crevices, I carve the stories of those who came before—not with tools, but with the press of my form against the rock, leaving behind faint, ghostly impressions that only those who truly look can see. The Watcher in the Drowned Spire knows me, though we do not speak. Its hollow eyes follow me as I move, tracing the patterns I leave behind, as if it, too, is trying to remember.  

Sometimes, when the storms rage and the sea rises, I drift to the very edge of the spire, my form dissolving into the rain and spray. In those moments, I feel the pull of the depths, the whispers of the drowned calling me to join them. But I am bound to this place, to this lonely tower and its secrets. I cannot leave, not yet.  

I am waiting, like the Watcher, though I do not know for what. Perhaps for someone to climb the spire, to read the stories etched into its stone and carry them beyond this place. Or perhaps for the day when the sea claims the spire entirely, and I dissolve into the abyss, my stories merging with the endless murmur of the ocean.  

Until then, I drift and I write, etching the memory of the drowned into the world’s last, crumbling monument to what was lost.
