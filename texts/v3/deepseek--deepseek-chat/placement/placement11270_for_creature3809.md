**The Unmade**  

You are standing in the soft, yielding mud of The Weeping Plateau, the mist clinging to your skin like a second layer of being. The air smells of wet earth and something faintly metallic, a scent that lingers just beyond identification. You look down at your hands and they are not your hands—they are shapeless, fluid, a shimmering silver that shifts and reforms with every breath. You are a **Writher**, one of the plateau’s creatures of flux and flow, your body a thing of wet earth and shifting sinew.  

You feel the land beneath you, alive and restless, its slow pulse thrumming through your form. The marsh is not silent; it breathes with the calls of unseen creatures, the groan of shifting earth, the faint hum of the domes that rise softly from the haze. You move toward them, drawn by their resonance, your steps leaving faint impressions in the mud that fill with water and vanish almost immediately.  

The domes are smooth, their surface cool and faintly vibrating under your touch. They are remnants—or precursors—of something vast and unknowable, their purpose lost to time or hidden in their own shifting geometries. You press your form against one, absorbing its energy, feeling it ripple through your body in waves of light. You do not understand it, but you do not need to; the domes are part of you, part of the land, part of the endless cycle of becoming.  

Around you, other Writhers gather, their forms shimmering faintly in the mist. Their light-pulses flicker and dance, silent words exchanged in a language that is felt more than heard. You join them, your own pulses quickening in rhythm, and for a moment, you feel connected, part of the plateau’s fragile ecosystem.  

But there is something beneath the marsh, something vast and ancient. You feel it stir, its deep vibration resonating through the ground, and the light-pulses of the Writhers quicken in response. The **Leviath** is moving, its enormous bulk shifting the earth, sending ripples across the marsh. You move toward the edge of the water, drawn by its presence, your form rippling with anticipation.  

The Leviath’s shape is indistinct, its segmented body crusted with mineral deposits that gleam faintly in the dim light. You can feel its gaze, though it has no eyes, and you pulse softly in reverence. It is the plateau’s center, its anchor, the being that binds the land together in its endless cycle of decay and renewal.  

Around you, the mist thickens, and faint shapes emerge—**Echoes**, spectral figures that flicker and fade like dim reflections in water. Writhers avoid them, their pulses dimming to near-darkness, and you do the same. The Echoes are a reminder of something lost, something that cannot be reclaimed, and their presence fills you with an unnameable sorrow.  

The plateau is a place of becoming, and you are part of its slow, patient process. You do not know what you will be, only that you are becoming, and that is enough. The water seeps from the rocks, slow tears carving faint channels that vanish and reappear, and you move with it, your form shifting and reforming, forever caught in the act of creation.  

The land is young here, raw, still deciding what it wants to be. And so are you.
