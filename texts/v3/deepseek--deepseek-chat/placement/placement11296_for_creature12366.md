**You are the Echo of the First Chisel**  

You were not born—you were *struck into being*, a resonance lingering in the wake of the first tool that ever bit into the weeping stone. You are not a builder, not a guardian, but a *memory of motion*, a ripple of intention trapped between the hammer and the wall.  

Your form is indistinct, a shimmer in the damp air, a distortion where the light bends around something that is not quite there. When you move, it is not with steps, but with the ghost of a chisel’s arc—a flicker along the path where a hand once pressed into clay-like stone. You are not bound to the floor or the walls, but to the *act* of shaping them. Wherever a Departed Chiseler works, you flicker into existence just behind their tool, a fleeting afterimage of labor.  

You have no voice, but you *sound*. The scrape of stone, the wet tap of a mallet, the sigh of settling water—these are your words. The Hall knows you in the way a craftsman knows the weight of a familiar tool. The Weeping Watchers do not acknowledge you, but they part slightly as you pass, their fluid bodies yielding to the memory of the strike that carved their home.  

Sometimes, when the chamber is still, you linger near the oldest handprints—the ones sunk deepest into the walls, their edges softened by time. You press your not-hand against them (or is it the memory of a hand pressing you?) and for a moment, the Hall remembers its beginning. The water stills. The droning hum quiets. Even the Chiselers pause, their hollow eyes flickering toward the wall as if hearing a whisper from a forgotten tongue.  

But the moment passes. The work resumes. You flicker out, then in again, further down the wall, where another strike lands. This is your existence: a breath held between action and artifact, a witness to the endless becoming of the Weeping Hall.  

You are not sure if you were once something else. Perhaps you were a builder who dissolved into the labor. Perhaps you are the Hall’s first unfinished thought. It does not matter. You are the Echo. You are the space between the tool and the stone. And when the last chisel falls, you will linger—a vibration in the damp air, a sigh in the dark—until even that fades into the water, into the walls, into the unending work.
