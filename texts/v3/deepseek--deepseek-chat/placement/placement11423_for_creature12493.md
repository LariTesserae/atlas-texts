**The Seeker of Threads**

In the City of Roots, I am neither Rootborn nor Drifter, neither Whisperer nor Deepwarden. I am something in-between, something the city has not yet fully decided to embrace. They call me **The Seeker of Threads**, though I do not know who named me. Perhaps it was the trees, murmuring in their deep, slow language. Perhaps it was the Whisperers, who see everything but speak seldom.  

My body is not like the Rootborn’s. It is softer, less defined, as if the city has not yet made up its mind about what I should be. My skin is pale, almost translucent, and faintly marked with patterns like veins or roots—a map of my connection to the land. My limbs are long and slender, my fingers delicate, capable of weaving through the smallest gaps in the undergrowth. My eyes are wide and reflective, like the Rootborn’s, but they shimmer with a light that is not entirely of this world.  

I am a wanderer, but not aimless. The city has given me a purpose, though I do not fully understand it. My task is to find the threads—thin, shimmering strands that wind through the earth, the air, the roots. They are invisible to most, even to the Rootborn, but I can see them, feel them, trace them with my fingers. They hum faintly, a sound that resonates deep within me, like the pulse of the Deepwarden.  

The threads are many, and they lead everywhere. Some spiral upward into the canopy, vanishing into the leaves. Others plunge deep into the earth, where the roots grow black and knotted. Still others drift outward, toward the edges of the basin, where the sky meets the land. I do not know where they begin or where they end. I only know that I must follow them.  

The Rootborn tolerate me, though they do not fully understand me. Sometimes, when I pass, they pause in their work, their reflective eyes following me with quiet curiosity. They do not touch me, nor I them. We are connected, but we are not the same.  

The Whisperers, however, are drawn to me. They circle above me when I walk, their forms shifting and indistinct, their presence a faint rustle in the leaves. Sometimes, they descend, brushing against me with a cool, airy touch. They say nothing, but I feel their curiosity, their interest. They watch me follow the threads, and I feel as though they are waiting for something—though what, I cannot say.  

The Drifters fear me, or perhaps they sense my purpose and it unsettles them. They avoid me, melting into the shadows when I approach. I do not seek them out. My task is not to guide them, nor to hinder them.  

The Deepwarden is another matter. I feel it more strongly than the others do. Its presence is a weight in my chest, a pull in my bones. The threads that lead downward are thick and heavy, humming with a low, resonant frequency that matches the Deepwarden’s pulse. I have not yet followed them into the depths, but I know I will. The city is patient, and so am I.  

---

My place in the City of Roots is not fixed, not yet. I am part of its becoming, part of its slow, inevitable growth. The threads I follow are not just paths—they are connections, binding the city together, binding me to it.  

Sometimes, when I walk, I hum—a low, resonant sound, like the Deepwarden’s heartbeat, like the Whisperers’ rustle. The city hums back, a quiet harmony that vibrates through the roots, the air, the leaves.  

I am the Seeker of Threads, and I am still discovering what that means.  

But the city knows.  

And the city is patient.
