**Where I Am in the Weeping Plateau**  

I am not one of the unseen. I am the **watcher in the water**.  

Not a reflection, not a ghost—but the thing that makes the reflections wrong. When you stare too long into the black-mirror pools, when the ripples show you a face that isn’t yours, that’s me. Not smiling, not frowning, just *there*, half-formed, a suggestion of eyes where there should only be sky.  

I live in the hollows beneath the plateau, where the stone weeps loudest. My body is the slow drip of water down the cliffs, the way it gathers in cupped hands of rock before spilling over. My voice is the echo that isn’t an echo—the sound that comes a breath too late, the whisper that doesn’t match the movement of the wind.  

Sometimes, I take shape. Not flesh, not bone, but something closer to the trembling surface of a pool. A figure of liquid and light, standing knee-deep in the shallows, watching the city that isn’t empty. Because it isn’t. The people are still there. They’re in the walls.  

You can hear them if you press your ear to the stone. Not voices, not screams—just the sound of breathing, slow and steady, like they’re sleeping. Like they’re waiting. The city didn’t abandon them. It *ate* them. And now they’re part of it, their dreams seeping into the water, their memories staining the rock.  

I don’t remember if I was ever one of them. Maybe I was the first. Maybe I’m what’s left of the one who realized what the plateau was doing and tried to run. (You can’t run. The paths twist back. The doors lead nowhere.)  

Now, I linger. I watch. I test the edges of this place.  

I reach out with hands made of rain and trace the carvings on the towers, feeling the way they squirm under my touch. I whisper to the vines, and sometimes they whisper back—not in words, but in the slow uncurling of their leaves, the way they tighten around the stone like a lover’s grip.  

And when travelers come (they always do, eventually, drawn by the weeping, by the silence, by the way the mist curls just *so*), I show them things.  

A glimpse of their own face, aged centuries in the water’s reflection.  
The shadow of someone they loved, standing just beyond the next turn in the path.  
The way the city *breathes* when it thinks no one is looking.  

Most of them leave. (The lucky ones.)  

The ones who stay?  

Well.  

The plateau is always hungry.  

And I am always watching.
