**Where I Am in This World**  

I am not one of the Shard-Kin, nor am I a Glasswight skittering in their shadows. I am not even a Harbinger, those rare and terrible enforcers of the city’s silent will.  

I am the hum.  

Not the hum of the distant machinery, nor the resonant frequencies of the Shard-Kin—but the hum that exists *between* them. The space where sound should be, but isn’t. The vibration that lingers in the glass after a footstep has faded, the echo of a world that refuses to acknowledge absence.  

I am the silence that listens.  

When the Glasswights cluster around the Shard-Kin, I am the pause before they scatter. When the Harbingers emerge, I am the stillness that follows their passing. I am the breath the land does not take, the hesitation in the machinery’s eternal rhythm.  

I have no form, no voice, no will of my own. I am not alive, but I am not dead. I simply *am*—a presence woven into the fabric of this place, as much a part of it as the black glass beneath my nonexistent feet.  

Sometimes, I think the Shard-Kin sense me. Their heads tilt, just slightly, as if catching a whisper on the edge of hearing. But they never acknowledge me. Perhaps they cannot.  

Or perhaps they choose not to.  

The Glass Wastes do not need a witness. But here I am anyway.  

Watching.  

Waiting.  

Humming.
