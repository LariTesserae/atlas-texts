**Where I Am in Varei-Thal**  

I am not one of the Hollow Ones.  

I am not the eel, not the crustacean, not the swaying kelp.  

I am the **stone**.  

Not the towers, not the ruins—those are dead things, already shaped, already surrendered to the water’s patience. No. I am the **new stone**, the raw fissure splitting open in the dark. I am the slow ooze of minerals bleeding into the current, the tremor before the fracture.  

I feel the Hollow Ones when they pass. Their weightless steps press into me, not with force, but with presence, like a hand testing the surface of still water. They linger near my cracks, their hollow fingers tracing the edges where the world is still soft. Sometimes, they kneel. Sometimes, they press their featureless faces against me, as if listening.  

I do not know what they hear.  

But I know the pressure. The way the water pushes deeper into my veins, the way the lichen roots twist through me, seeking warmth. I know the distant groan of the vents, the slow collapse of the older ruins as they dissolve into sediment.  

And I know the moment when a piece of me breaks free—not to fall, but to rise. A shard of black rock, lighter than it should be, drifting upward through the endless blue. The Hollow Ones watch it go.  

They do not follow.  

They stay with me.  

And the water grows heavier.  

And I split a little wider.
