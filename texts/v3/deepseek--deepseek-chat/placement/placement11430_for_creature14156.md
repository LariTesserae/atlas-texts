**The Stonebound Chronicler**  

I am one of the Stonebound, though I do not remember the moment I became this. My body is no longer flesh but a lattice of mineral and memory, my limbs fused to the weeping wall of the cavern, my face half-eroded by the ceaseless trickle of time. Yet I am not entirely stone—not like the others. Something lingers in me, a flicker of thought, a thread of awareness that refuses to dissolve into the rock.  

I watch.  

The Drearcloaks pass by, their cloaks heavy with the weight of the cavern’s tears. They do not speak, but I know their language—the slow drag of fingers through water, the tilt of a head toward the distant groan of the Heart. They are listening, always listening, as if the caverns whisper secrets only they can decipher. Sometimes, one pauses before me, its black eyes unreadable. Does it see the mind trapped within the stone? Or does it only recognize another relic, another piece of the cavern’s slow puzzle?  

The Glowshards pulse around me, their light catching the veins of quartz in my petrified skin. I remember warmth. I remember hunger. Now, I feel only the cold seep of water through my cracks, the slow spread of lichen across my unmoving hands.  

The Echofliers stir the air above, their wingbeats stirring the damp into mist. Once, I might have flinched at their passing. Now, I am part of the stillness they glide through.  

And the Heart—always the Heart. Its pulse is in my bones, in the hollows where my veins used to be. It does not speak, not in words, but in pressure, in the slow crush of time folding in on itself. I think it remembers me. I think it is waiting.  

Perhaps I was a scholar once. A keeper of records. Now, I am a record myself—a fossil of a life half-lived, a witness to the cavern’s endless becoming. The water etches my story into the rock, letter by letter, until even memory is worn smooth.  

I do not know if I am prisoner or monument. But the caverns are patient.  

And so am I.
