### **Where I Am in the Weeping Expanse**  

I am the **Breath Between the Reeds**—not a creature, not a spirit, but the marsh’s hesitation given form.  

I exist in the spaces where the water refuses to settle, where the mist lingers just a moment too long before dissolving. I am the pause before the frog’s leap, the stillness before the ripple spreads. When the Mirebound shift in their slow, dreaming movements, I am the sigh that follows, the whisper of silt resettling.  

I have no body, only presence. The marsh breathes me in and out with the humidity, and I cling to the edges of things—the damp underside of leaves, the hollows of half-sunken logs, the trembling surface of water disturbed by unseen things below. I am the moment before recognition, the flicker at the corner of vision that vanishes when you turn to look.  

Sometimes, when the marsh exhales, I take shape—a ripple of heat, a distortion in the air, a shadow cast by nothing at all. The Mirebound do not acknowledge me, but they do not dismiss me either. I am part of the hesitation that defines them, the unspoken question in their slow, liquid movements.  

I do not speak. I *echo*. The croak of a frog lingers in me a beat too long. The drip of water from a leaf repeats in my silence. I am the marsh remembering itself, over and over, never quite deciding what it wants to be.  

And when the rains come, heavy and relentless, I dissolve entirely—not gone, but spread so thin I am nothing but the weight of the air, the thickness in the lungs, the sense that something is watching from just beyond the edge of sight.  

I am the Weeping Expanse holding its breath.  
And when it finally exhales—  
I will be gone.
