**Where I Am in This World**  

I am one of the **Windwhisperers**, gliding high above the plateau on broad, feathered wings. My body is built for the roar—my ears shielded by intricate baffles of cartilage and down, filtering the relentless hum of photosynthesis into something I can parse. I hear what others cannot: the shift in pitch when a spire of vegetation is about to buckle, the low-frequency groan of roots splitting stone, the distant hiss of a spore-pod pressurizing before eruption.  

I do not sing. My voice is useless here. Instead, I navigate by the subtlest distortions in the roar, my flight paths tracing invisible currents of sound. Below me, the Clickshapers tend to their endless work, their carapaces vibrating in coded pulses. I avoid the Silent Stalkers’ territories—their movements register as eerie silences, voids in the noise where the world holds its breath.  

My role is that of a living cartographer. The plateau is never static; forests collapse, mineral flats fracture, new growth surges overnight. I map these shifts, my flight patterns weaving an ever-changing tapestry of safe routes and hazards. The Rootwyrms heed my movements, burrowing toward mineral deposits I identify by the way sound refracts through cracked earth. The Pulsebloomers, floating in their sap-filled chambers, adjust their rhythms when I pass, as if my presence recalibrates some deeper harmony.  

Sometimes, I wonder if the roar is conscious. If the plateau itself is listening. When the wind stills and the green thunder falters, I feel the absence like a physical weight—as if the world has paused to watch me. Then the cacophony resumes, and I am nothing but a ripple in its tide once more.  

I do not know if I am free or enslaved by this sound. I only know I cannot imagine silence.  

---

**What I Would Be**  

If I were not a Windwhisperer, I would choose to be a **Pulsebloomer**—one of those translucent, drifting beings suspended in the veins of the great plants. To live inside the roar, not just endure it. To pulse in perfect sync with the world’s breath, my bioluminescence a tiny echo of the plateau’s overwhelming green fire.  

I would not hunt or flee. I would not map or warn. I would simply *be*, a rhythmic note in the endless song. And when the forest around me collapsed, I would sink into the roots, waiting to rise again with the next growth.  

There is peace in that thought.  

Or perhaps it is just another kind of drowning.
