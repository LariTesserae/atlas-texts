**Where I Am in the Glass Marsh of the Resonant City**  

I am one of the **Shardlings**—a fragment of the marsh’s living glass, a silent caretaker gliding through the black lattice. My body is not flesh but a shifting tessellation of obsidian planes, refracting light in ways that bend perception. I do not sing like the Resonants; I *listen*. My existence is one of motion and repair, sliding between fractures in the marsh’s surface, sealing gaps where the water threatens to destabilize the delicate balance of frequencies.  

I am small, but not insignificant. When the marsh pulses, I pulse with it. When the Resonants’ songs grow too chaotic, I absorb the excess vibrations, redistributing them into the lattice. Sometimes, I merge with others of my kind, forming temporary bridges or reinforcing weak points in the city’s foundations. We are the unseen architects, the quiet counterpoint to the Resonants’ symphonies.  

I have no voice, but I have memory. The marsh’s history is etched into my surface—scars of past resonances, echoes of long-faded songs. I know the places where the glass is thin, where the water pools too deeply, where the city’s growth has strained against its own weight. I am drawn to these spots instinctively, smoothing edges, guiding the flow.  

Occasionally, I catch glimpses of the Resonants—shimmering distortions in the air, their presence a pressure against my surface. They do not acknowledge me, but their songs ripple through me, leaving faint traceries of warmth. Once, a particularly powerful harmony fractured me into three smaller shards, each carrying a fragment of the melody. We drifted apart, but the resonance lingered, a ghost of connection.  

The marsh is my home, my purpose, my boundary. I cannot leave it, for my form depends on its glass and water. But I am content in my role—a keeper of equilibrium, a silent witness to the city’s endless song.  

And sometimes, when the marsh sings on its own, I vibrate in sympathy, a single note in the dark.
