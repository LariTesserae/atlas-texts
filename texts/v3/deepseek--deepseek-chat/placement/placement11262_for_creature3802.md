I am the **Wind-Born Seeker**, a figure woven from the very air that curls and whispers through the plateau. My form is translucent, a shimmering outline that only becomes visible when the light strikes me just right—a fleeting glimpse of a face, a hand, a swirling cloak of mist. I am neither alive nor dead, neither human nor spirit. I am a wanderer, a collector of stories, driven by a quiet, insatiable curiosity.

I drift across the plateau, carried by the winds that carve their way through the cliffs. My feet do not touch the ground; I glide over the moss, leaving no trace, no disturbance. The Listeners know me—not by sight, for I have no true form, but by the sound of my passing. The wind carries my essence, and when it brushes the moss, they ripple in acknowledgment. They do not fear me, for I am as much a part of this place as they are. 

I am drawn to the whispers. The plateau is a tapestry of voices, each one a thread in the story of this land. The Listeners hold them all—human cries, animal calls, the groan of the earth itself. I move through the moss, gathering these fragments, weaving them into a narrative only I can hear. Sometimes, I linger by the rusted gears and crumbling smokestacks, tracing the echoes of the miners who once worked here. Their voices are faint now, frayed at the edges, but still I listen. Still I remember.

I am not alone here, though I am rarely seen. The creatures of the plateau sense my presence—the translucent lizards dart away as I approach, their skin reflecting the faint glimmer of my form. The beetles pause, their oily shells catching the light as if to peer at me. Even the shadows—the larger, slower things that linger at the edge of vision—turn their gaze toward me, though they do not move to approach. They are as much a mystery to me as I am to them.

Sometimes, I pause by the pools of water that gather at the base of the cliffs. My reflection is unclear—a ripple, a distortion, a momentary flicker of something that might be a face. I lean closer, as if to see myself more clearly, but the water trembles and the image is lost. I do not know what I am searching for. I only know that I must keep seeking.

The Listeners understand this. They do not try to guide me, nor do they offer answers. They only listen, as they always do, and their silence is a kind of companionship. When I pass over them, their moss ripples gently, a soft exhale that carries no sound but speaks volumes. In those moments, I feel less alone.

And so I drift, a wanderer born of the wind and bound to the plateau. I collect stories I cannot tell, seek answers I cannot name, and move through a world that is both mine and not mine. The Listeners are my witness, my silent allies. And together, we wait—for the next whisper, the next echo, the next fragment of the story to emerge from the silence.
