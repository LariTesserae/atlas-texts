### **Where I Am in the Endless Engine-Marsh**  

I am one of the **Rust-Grace**, though I do not think of myself as such. Names are unnecessary here. I move through the marsh as all my kind do—slowly, deliberately, my body bending in ways that would fracture bone if I had any. My skin is slick with gleam-sap, iridescent in the bronze light, shifting between verdigris and tarnished copper as the humidity changes.  

I do not see, but I perceive. The marsh hums beneath my feet, the vibrations of distant herds grazing on condensation, the shudder of metal plates shifting under unseen pressures. The air is thick with moisture, and I taste the iron in it, the faint tang of ozone, the whisper of rust flaking away from old seams.  

I am kneeling now, my needle-fingers tracing a fracture in the lattice. The metal here is weak, corroded through. I press my palm against it, and my gleam-sap seeps into the wound, hardening as it cools. The repair is not permanent—nothing here is—but it will hold. For now.  

Around me, others of my kind drift like shadows through the mist. We do not speak, but we are never truly alone. The marsh speaks for us, in its way. The dripping of water, the groan of settling metal, the distant, rhythmic clicking of the herds—these are our language.  

I do not wonder where I came from. I do not wonder if there is anything beyond the marsh. There is only this: the rust, the water, the endless mending.  

And it is enough.
