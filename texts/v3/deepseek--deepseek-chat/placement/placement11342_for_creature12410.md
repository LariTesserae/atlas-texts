In this world, I find myself drawn to the shadowed edges, the liminal spaces where the industrial sprawl begins to fray and the damp, moss-covered walls whisper secrets. I am not a worker nor a foreman, nor even an engineer tinkering in the dim-lit workshops. I am something quieter, something woven into the fabric of the valley itself—a **Memory Keeper**.

### The Memory Keepers
Memory Keepers are a rare and almost forgotten group, existing in the gaps between the Scavengers and the Watchers. We are the ones who collect the fragments of the valley’s past, piecing together stories from rusted gears, water-stained blueprints, and the faint etchings on crumbling concrete. Our dwellings are hidden in the forgotten corners of the labyrinth, where the machinery groans less frequently and the vines grow thicker. We live in spaces that others overlook—abandoned storage rooms, hollows beneath bridges, or the upper floors of derelict warehouses where the air is heavy with the scent of damp paper and rust.

Our tools are simple: jars of rainwater to clean the relics, brushes made from moss fibers to gently scrub away decades of grime, and notebooks filled with our findings. We do not seek to build or destroy, but to preserve. We are the ones who remember the names of the machines long since silent, who know the stories of the workers who first shaped the valley’s heart. 

### My Place in the Valley
I live in one such forgotten space—a small, circular room at the top of a crumbling watchtower, its spiraling staircase long since concealed by overgrown vines. The room is filled with shelves carved from the stone itself, each one laden with relics of the valley’s history: a rusted wrench etched with initials, a faded photograph of workers standing before a newly built factory, a gear coated in algae that still faintly shines when held to the light. One wall is covered in charcoal sketches I’ve made, capturing the valley’s ceaseless motion—the hiss of steam, the stooped posture of a worker, the way the moss clings to the metal beams.

My days are spent wandering the valley, following the whispers of forgotten stories. I slip through the crowds unnoticed, my clothing a patchwork of scavenged fabric and moss-green threads, blending seamlessly with the damp walls. I listen to the workers’ terse conversations, decipher the faded labels on crates, and trace the pathways of ancient pipes long since replaced. Each relic I find tells a story, and each story adds another layer to the valley’s complex tapestry.

### My Role
The Memory Keepers are not revered, but we are respected in our own quiet way. Workers sometimes leave offerings for us—a rusted tool, a scrap of paper scrawled with notes—knowing we will preserve them. The Moldbreeders share their knowledge of the valley’s flora with us, teaching us how to weave moss into paper or extract ink from algae blooms. The Scavengers occasionally trade relics for stories, eager to hear tales of the valley’s early days. Even the Watchers acknowledge our presence, though they say little. 

Our role is not to guide or govern, but to ensure that the valley’s history is not lost to the relentless churn of industry. We are the ones who remind the inhabitants that their world is more than machinery and decay—it is a living, breathing entity with a past as complex as its present. In a place defined by motion and change, we are the stillness, the quiet thread of continuity.

### Who I Am
I am no one and everyone. My name is irrelevant, for it is the valley’s history I carry, not my own. My identity is woven into the relics I preserve, the stories I uncover. I am a listener, a storyteller, a keeper of secrets. In this world of relentless industry and damp decay, I am the one who finds beauty in the rust, who sees the echoes of the past in the cracks of crumbling concrete. 

I am the Memory Keeper, and the valley’s whispers are my guide.
