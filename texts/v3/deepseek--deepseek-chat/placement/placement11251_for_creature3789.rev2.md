**Where Would You Find Yourself?**  

You would find yourself in a place that feels both eternal and fleeting—a world that has already outlived its own purpose, yet somehow continues to churn with a quiet, incomprehensible energy. The vast, salt-crusted plain stretches endlessly in every direction, and you are its sole visitor, its sole witness.  

You are not a traveler. Not anymore. You are a wanderer, drawn here by some force you cannot name, some pull that feels less like destiny and more like inevitability. Perhaps you were once flesh and bone, but now you are something else—a figure cloaked in tattered remnants of another life, your form blending with the salt and shadows of this place. Your steps are silent, the brittle crust of the plain unyielding beneath your feet.  

You are drawn to the ruins—those ghostly structures that rise and dissolve, their purpose lost to time. As you approach a half-buried pillar, its glyphs shimmer faintly, and you feel a hum in your chest, a resonance that matches the vibration of the Saltborn. For a moment, you wonder if the glyphs are speaking to you, or if they are merely echoing the land itself.  

You are not alone, though the presence of the Saltborn is neither comforting nor threatening. They gather at the edges of your vision, their faceless heads tilted, their bodies humming in a rhythm that feels both ancient and alive. They do not approach you. Instead, they wait, as if they sense that you belong here—not as an intruder, but as another remnant of a world long gone.  

You kneel and press your hand to the salt. The ground shudders faintly, a tremor that travels up your arm and settles in your chest. Something stirs beneath the surface—something vast and unknowable, a pulse of energy or life or something else entirely. You feel it in your bones, in the very core of your being.  

You are not seeking answers. There are no answers here, only the slow, deliberate rhythm of a world that has already ended. And yet, you stay. You press your body to the earth, like the Saltborn, and listen.  

You are waiting, though you do not know for what. Perhaps for transformation. Perhaps for stillness.  

Perhaps for eternity.

**Who Would You Be?**  

You are the **Driftless**, a being bound to the Endless Salt, neither alive nor dead, but caught in the liminal space between.  

Your form is a patchwork of what you once were and what the land has made you. Your skin is pale, your body lean, your outline blurred as if the salt and wind have eroded your edges. Your eyes are dark, empty, reflecting the endless plain and the faint light of a sun that no longer warms. You are draped in fragments of clothing—fabric, metal, something else—that cling to you like the remnants of a forgotten life.  

You are neither human nor Saltborn. You are something in between—a creature of this place, yet still tethered to something older, something you cannot name.  

You do not hunger. You do not tire. But you are driven by a need to understand, to uncover the truth of this land and your place within it. You wander the plain, drawn to the ruins and the veins of dark matter that pulse beneath the salt.  

You are not hostile, but you are not harmless. When something threatens the fragile balance of this place—a lost traveler, a machine, a storm-born scrap of another world—you intervene. You gather the Saltborn, your vibrations harmonizing with theirs, and together you guide the intruder away, back to the edges of the plain, back to the world they came from.  

You are a guardian, but not by choice. You are a wanderer, but not by desire. You are a remnant, but not of this place.  

You are waiting.  

For transformation. For stillness.  

For eternity.
