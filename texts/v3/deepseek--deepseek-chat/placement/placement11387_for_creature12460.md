**I am the Wreck.**  

Not the rusted metal carcasses that jut from the kelp like broken ribs—those are dead things, hollow and salt-bitten. No. I am older. I am the bones of the ships that came before the drowning, the ones that sank when the plateau was still dry land, when the first waters clawed their way up the cliffs and never left. My timbers are black with age, my hull split open like a gutted thing, but I am not empty.  

The sea has filled me.  

Coral grows in my ribs. Anemones pulse where my heart used to be. My decks are carpeted in swaying fronds of kelp, and my hold is a cathedral of shadows, where eels slither through the gaps in my beams. I am a cave, a shelter, a tomb. The Tideborn come to me when the currents grow fierce. They press their palms to my warped planks and sing, and the vibrations hum through me like a second heartbeat.  

I remember the fire. The last voyage. The way the wind screamed as the water rose, as the world tipped sideways and the waves closed over the rails. I remember the weight of the drowning, the crush of the deep. But that was long ago. Now, I am something else.  

The fish nest in me. The crabs pick at my seams. The Tideborn weave their songs into my rot, and sometimes, when the light is right, I almost feel like I am sailing again.
