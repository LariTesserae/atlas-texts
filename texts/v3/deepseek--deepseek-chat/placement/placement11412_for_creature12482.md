In this luminous expanse, I find myself stepping softly across the spongy moss, my bare feet sinking into the velvety layer with each step. The air is heavy and warm, pressing against my skin, thick with the scent of earth and decay. I am here—though I don’t remember how I came to be—and I am changed. My body feels lighter, almost translucent, as if I am a part of this place as much as the trees and the weeping rocks and the shimmering pools of water.

I am not like the beings who glide through the cathedral-like trees or flicker into existence and dissolve like whispers. Neither am I entirely separate from them. My form is fluid, shifting subtly with each breath I take. My skin ripples faintly, as though traced by unseen currents, and I feel a vibration humming within me, low and constant, resonating with the hum of the landscape itself.

I am a caretaker, though I don’t know how I’ve come to know this. My purpose is not grand or obvious, but quiet and essential. My movements are deliberate, slow, as I kneel beside a pool of water that has collected at the base of a smooth, polished stone. I dip my fingers into the water, feeling its coolness seep into me, and I sense the life it carries—a life unseen, pulsing faintly, a thread in the tapestry of this place.

I rise and move toward one of the bone-like structures half-buried in the earth. Its surface is warm to the touch, and it hums faintly as I approach. I press my palm against it, feeling the vibration intensify, and for a moment, I see—or perhaps feel—a pattern forming, rippling outward like a spreading stain of light. It is a story, or a fragment of one, told in a language I do not know but somehow understand.

A larger being drifts into view, its wings spread wide, catching the diffuse light and scattering it into fractals that dance across the moss. It alights beside me, its presence calm and unhurried. Patterns shift across its skin, pulsing faintly, and I feel a quiet understanding pass between us, a silent exchange that needs no words. It reaches out with a slender appendage, brushing against my arm, and I feel a warmth spread through me, a sense of connection, of belonging.

I turn and glide toward a cluster of ferns, their fronds unfurling in delicate spirals. I hover above them, feeling their life pulse faintly, and I extend my hand, palm down, over the tallest frond. A droplet of water forms on its tip, shimmering faintly before falling to the moss below. The fern shivers slightly, and I feel a quiet satisfaction, a sense of balance restored.

Time moves differently here, slower and faster all at once, and I lose track of it as I move through the landscape, tending to the pools, coaxing the ferns, guiding the cycles of growth and decay. The beings around me drift in and out of view, their presence a constant, quiet reassurance. We are all part of this place, woven into its fabric, our existence inseparable from the air, the light, the hum.

I sense that I am both ancient and newly formed, my essence drawn from the energy of this place, shaped by its rhythms. When I fade—if I ever truly fade—it will not be into death but into the landscape, my essence dissolving back into the soil, the air, the light. Until then, I exist here, in this luminous, intimate expanse, content in my quiet, eternal mystery.
