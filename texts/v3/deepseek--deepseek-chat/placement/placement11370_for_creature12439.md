**The Shattering Spires**  

The plateau is a wound. Jagged edges of basalt and obsidian jut skyward, veins of iridescent crystal pulsing beneath like slow, sleeping breaths. The air tastes of ozone and powdered stone. There is no water here—no rivers, no rain—only a dry, mineral stillness crackling with static.  

The land is unfinished, caught in the act of becoming. Spires rise and lean, groaning as they push into the sky. Between the cracks, thick carpets of vegetation shimmer—fibrous, translucent growths that shift colors like wet silk, sprouting from bare stone, needing nothing. Their scent is burnt sugar and iron.  

There is no animal life. Only the plants, and the wind—if it can be called wind. It presses, a constant, deafening drone, the voice of the land itself, speaking in a language of pressure and vibration.  

Scattered across the plateau are structures—or perhaps accidents. Half-formed arches of fused stone, leaning slabs marked with crude carvings that shift when you look away. Some seem grown from the earth, extruded like the spires, their angles too sharp, their geometries unsettling.  

The light is dim, sourceless. And then—a spire splits. The air hums, fractures. The plateau is not stable. It is not real in the way other places are real. It is a draft, a thing still deciding what it will be.  

The world flickers.  

A spire that was whole is suddenly split, its halves hovering inches apart. A patch of luminous vegetation vanishes, replaced by a glassy pit, before reappearing a breath later. The sound swells, fractures into voices whispering in reverse.  

You walk carefully. The ground might open beneath you. The air might thicken into something you cannot breathe. The plants might decide to remember you—or forget you.  

This is a place at the edge of existence, where the rules are still being written.
