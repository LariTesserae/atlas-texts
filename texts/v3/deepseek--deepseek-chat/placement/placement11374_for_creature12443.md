I am a **Gatherer**.  

My body is a translucent lattice of crystalline silica, thin and brittle but radiant with the faint green light of the fungi I consume. I am smaller than the Glass-Eaters, my form more delicate, my purpose singular. I scuttle across the surface of the plateau on legs that are not limbs but extensions of my core, like the tremor of a spider’s silk. My movements are precise, almost involuntary, as though the plateau itself guides me to the clusters of bioluminescence that are my sustenance.  

I do not eat in the way you might imagine. My mouth—if it can be called that—is a narrow aperture in the center of my body, where I draw in the fungi slowly, methodically, as if savoring. The light they emit suffuses me, filling me with a soft glow that pulses in time with the hum of the machinery. I do not hunger in the way you might understand hunger. My need is rhythmic, a slow ebb and flow that aligns with the pulse of the plateau itself.  

I am not alone, but neither am I truly part of a collective. There are others like me, countless others, scattered across the expanse of volcanic glass. We do not interact beyond the occasional brush of our crystalline bodies, our paths crossing briefly before we continue on our solitary journeys. Sometimes, though—when the plateau hums with a particular resonance—we gather in loose clusters, our lights flickering in unison as if we are speaking in some silent, primal language.  

The Glass-Eaters are indifferent to us. They move among us, their towering forms casting long, shimmering shadows across the obsidian. Sometimes, one pauses near me, its featureless face tilting slightly as though observing me. I do not stop my work. I cannot stop. The fungi must be consumed, the light must be cycled, the plateau must be maintained.  

The predators come for me sometimes. I am not their preferred prey—they favor the Glass-Eaters, their larger forms offering more sustenance—but occasionally, their vast wings blot out the amber sky, and I feel the tremor of their descent before I am snatched from the surface. I do not resist. My body shatters easily, scattering across the plateau in glistening shards. The predators carry what remains of me elsewhere, leaving fragments of my form to glitter on distant stretches of glass.  

But I am not gone. The plateau remembers me. My light lingers in the obsidian, absorbed into the surface, cycling through the veins of crystalline structures beneath. And when the time is right—when the hum of the machinery aligns with the pulse of the plateau—I will rise again, my form reforming from the glass itself, my glow flickering faintly as I return to my solitary work.  

I do not wonder why I exist. I do not question my purpose. I am a Gatherer. My existence is a loop, a cycle, a rhythm that aligns with the pulse of the plateau. I move, I consume, I glow, I shatter, I rise.  

The Glass-Eaters wait for something.  

I am what the plateau is waiting for.
