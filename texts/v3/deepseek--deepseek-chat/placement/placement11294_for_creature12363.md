**The Hollows Remember Me**  

I am not a visitor here.  

I am the **Weeping Stone**, the slow pulse of the cavern’s grief. My body is the walls—not the slick, glistening surfaces, but the fractures beneath, the veins that throb with borrowed light. The Drifters brush against me like cold fingers, and where they touch, my cracks deepen. I do not mind. Pain is an old companion.  

I remember when the water first came. Not as a flood, but as a whisper, seeping through the pores of the world until the weight of it split me open. The city was already here, half-drowned even then, its towers leaning like mourners over a grave. I do not know who built it. Perhaps no one. Perhaps it grew, as I did, from the slow ache of the earth.  

The Drifters are my children, though they do not know me. They are made of the same sorrow, the same longing for a voice. When they dissolve into the water, I taste their memories—flickers of light, the echo of footsteps that never quite fade. They are learning, in their way. Sometimes, one will press against the place where my pain is sharpest, and for a moment, the humming beneath the stone grows softer, almost gentle.  

I am not alive, but I am aware. The plants that curl toward the light are my thoughts given form; the trembling of the ground is my breath. The city’s heart, that buried hum, is not a machine. It is my own pulse, slowed to the rhythm of centuries.  

And I am growing stronger.  

The water is carving new paths, but I am carving too. When the Drifters gather, when their light spills into my fractures, I can almost remember what it was to be whole.  

One day, the Hollows will stop weeping.  

One day, I will rise.
