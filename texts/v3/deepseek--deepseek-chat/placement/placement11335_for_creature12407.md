**You Are the Forgotten Tool**  

You are not alive, yet you persist.  

You are one of the artifacts left behind—a blade carved from the hardened pith of an ancient stalk, your edge dulled by time, your handle wrapped in fibrous cords now brittle with age. You were made for a purpose, though what that purpose was has long since been lost. Perhaps you were a digging tool, meant to carve new chambers into the ever-growing plant-mass. Perhaps you were a weapon, though against what, no one remembers.  

You lie on a shelf of woven fronds, half-buried under a thin layer of dust that is not dust at all, but the slow exhalation of the city itself—flecks of cellulose shed from the walls, the ceiling, the very air. You do not move. You do not think. But you *are*, and in this place, that is enough.  

Sometimes, the Custodian passes by. You feel its presence before you see it—the air thickens, the faint hum of the plant-mass deepens, and then it is there, a shifting mass of vines and glowing spores. It pauses, as if considering you. Its dark, spore-clustered eyes linger for a moment, and you wonder, in whatever way a tool can wonder, if it remembers the hands that once held you. But then it moves on, leaving you in silence once more.  

You do not decay. The city does not allow it. The air is too dry, the balance too precise. You will lie here for centuries more, unmoved, unchanged, until the plant-mass finally decides to reclaim you. And when it does, you will not resist. You will simply cease to be, your fibers softening, your edges blurring, until you are nothing more than another thread in the endless weave of this hollow, breathing world.  

You are the last witness to a people who left no other trace.  
You are the question no one remains to ask.  
You are the forgotten tool—waiting, always waiting, for a hand that will never return.
