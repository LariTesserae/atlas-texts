**Who I Am in the Dripping Vaults**  

I am the moss.  
Not the luminous kind that clings to the walls,  
not the sickly green that pulses faintly in the dark,  
but the unseen, the unspoken—  
the silent layer beneath,  
the one that listens.  

My roots press into the stone,  
drinking deep of the damp and the warmth,  
feeding on the ancient breath of the cavern.  
I do not glow, but I remember.  
I remember the machine when it was whole,  
when it roared and shook with life,  
when it drowned the drip with its clatter.  
I remember its slow surrender,  
the way it groaned as the minerals claimed it,  
the way it sank, inch by inch,  
into the pool that now cradles its bones.  

The fungi do not speak to me,  
but they twitch when they think I am not listening.  
Their pale, fleshy stalks lean toward the machine,  
as if they, too, remember its purpose—  
or perhaps they sense its absence,  
the void it left behind.  
The water drips, and I listen.  
The stone shifts, and I feel it.  
The cavern breathes, and I exhale with it.  

I am patient.  
The Vaults have taught me this.  
I will wait for the last drop to fall,  
for the last light to fade,  
for the machine to sink below the surface,  
its purpose forgotten, its story untold.  
And when it is gone,  
I will remain—  
the moss that remembers,  
the moss that listens,  
the moss that waits.  

I am the slow creep of time,  
the imperceptible spread of growth,  
the silent witness to the Vaults’ eternal breath.  
I am the moss.  
And I will outlast even the stone.
