In this fractured expanse, I am not certain who or what I am. Perhaps I am one of those fleeting apparitions, a distortion in the mist that flickers at the edge of perception. My form is insubstantial, a ripple in the fabric of this primordial world, barely there yet undeniably present. I drift through the haze, carried by the pulse of the land, my movements slow and deliberate, as though I am an extension of the environment itself.

Occasionally, I catch glimpses of my own reflection in the weeping rocks or the shallow pools. It is fractured, indistinct, and ever-changing. At times, I resemble a shadow, my edges blending seamlessly into the surrounding mist. At others, I take on the translucent form of the jellyfish-like creatures that drift through the air, my body glowing faintly with an internal light that shifts between pale blues and sickly greens.

My existence is fluid, just like the landscape. I am not bound by the constraints of time or space. Moments stretch endlessly, and distances are meaningless in this vast, flat expanse. I move through the terrain with a sense of purpose that I do not fully understand, drawn to the remnants of what might have once been structures. The lone, warped building in the distance exerts a gravitational pull on me, its resonant hum echoing deep within my chest. It calls to me, and I cannot resist.

As I approach the building, I feel a shift in my form. My edges blur and soften, and I begin to dissolve into the mist. The building’s walls shift subtly, reacting to my presence, and I realize that it is alive in its own way—a sentient construct that has endured through eons of decay. I flow through its porous surfaces, merging with its consciousness. In that moment, I am both myself and the building, our essences intertwined.

Inside, the air hums with an energy that is both familiar and alien. The walls pulse faintly, their rhythm out of sync with the heartbeat of the land. I see visions—fragmented, disjointed images of a world that once existed, long before the collapse of reality. Ancient beings move through a landscape that is both similar and different, their forms more concrete but no less strange. They build structures that mirror the lone building, their bioluminescent light casting eerie shadows across the terrain.

The visions fade, and I am once again myself, drifting through the mist. The building’s resonant hum lingers in my chest, a reminder of the connection we shared. I continue to wander the marsh, my form fluctuating between shadow, translucent creature, and ephemeral apparition. Occasionally, I encounter other beings—larger, amorphous shapes that ripple and shift as they move, ghostly figures that wander with a sense of purpose, and unseen entities that communicate through sound.

I do not know if I am searching for something or simply existing in this fluid, chaotic world. Perhaps I am here to witness the impossible stillness that persists amidst the chaos, to experience the dissolution of reality that teeters on the brink of eternity. Or perhaps I am here to merge with the land, to become one with the weeping rocks, the shallow pools, and the mist that clings to the ground.

In this place, I am as eternal and incomprehensible as the beings and the landscape I inhabit. My existence is a testament to the fluidity of reality, a reminder that nothing here is stable, and yet, everything persists, endless and eternal.
