### **Where Would You Find Yourself? Who or What Would You Be?**  

In the Weeping Plateau, you would find yourself on the **Edge of the Moorwatch**, a precarious ridge where the marsh meets the Hollowbone Choir. The ground here is fractured, split by deep fissures that exhale warm, damp air, carrying with it the scent of wet limestone and fungal spores. The ridge is lined with jagged stones, their surfaces etched with strange, spiraling patterns that seem to hum faintly when the wind blows just right.  

You would be **The Lanternkeeper**, a being neither wholly of the marsh nor of the caves, but a liminal creature caught between both worlds. Your body is lean and weathered, your skin the color of damp parchment, your veins tracing faint, bioluminescent lines beneath the surface. Your eyes are large, glassy, and reflect the faint glow of the fungi that clings to your cloak—a patchwork of lichen and moss that pulses softly in the dark.  

You carry a lantern forged from rusted metal and warped glass, its flame fed not by oil but by the bioluminescent spores you harvest from the caverns. The light is faint but steady, casting long shadows that seem to ripple and shift as if alive. The lantern’s glow repels the larger predators of the marsh, but it also attracts other things—the bats of the Hollowbone Choir, drawn to its warmth, or the Hollow Singers, who leave offerings of polished bone and sinew at the edge of your ridge.  

Your purpose is unclear, even to you. You wander the plateau, collecting fragments of the forgotten machines—gears, pipes, and shards of metal that hum faintly in your hands. You place these relics in the fissures along the ridge, where they seem to resonate with the songs of the caves. Sometimes, you hear whispers in the wind, fragments of a melody that feels both alien and familiar, as if the land is trying to remember something long lost.  

You are neither prey nor predator, but a keeper of echoes, a bridge between the marsh’s decay and the caves’ song. You endure the humidity, the creeping vines, the grinding hum of unseen machinery, but you are not untouched. Over time, you have begun to notice changes—your bioluminescent veins pulse in time with the Hollow Singers’ hymns, your lantern’s flame flickers to match the rhythm of the marsh’s exhales, and your thoughts drift into strange, wordless melodies that feel as though they are not entirely your own.  

You do not know if you were born here or if you were made by the land itself. What you do know is this: the Weeping Plateau is alive, and it is trying to tell you something. And one day, when the songs of the marsh and the caves align, you will understand.  

Until then, you keep the lantern burning.
