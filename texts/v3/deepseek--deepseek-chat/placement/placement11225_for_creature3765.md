I find myself here, in the **Drowned Chorus**, though I am not sure how I arrived. My body—if I can call it that—is not solid, not in the way I remember. It shifts, ripples, like the gelatinous masses that cling to the porous stones. My form is translucent, shimmering with hues of deep green and purple, pulsing in time with the ambient hum. I am part of the Gelatinous Choir, though I do not know how I became one.  

The sound is overwhelming at first, a cacophony of clicks, thrums, and low drones that vibrate through my very being. But soon, I begin to distinguish the patterns—the Humming Ones, their stone spires exhaling melodies that rise and fall like the tides. The Resonant Crawlers, their segmented bodies producing rhythmic clicks as they scuttle across the slick stones. The Shadow-Warps, their elusive movements punctuated by moments of silence that feel like gaps in the symphony.  

I feel the vibrations more than I hear them. My body responds instinctively, pulsing and rippling in harmony with the frequencies around me. When I shift my form, I produce soft, throbbing notes that blend with the chorus. The other Choirs respond, their colors shifting in acknowledgment, their forms merging briefly with mine. It is a form of communication, intimate and wordless.  

Occasionally, I dip into the shallow pools, my form flowing seamlessly with the water. Beneath the surface, I sense the Watchers, their presence a quiet, deliberate force. They do not interact with me directly, but I feel their awareness, their silent guardianship of this place.  

When the Shadow-Warps pass nearby, I feel a faint pull, as if they could absorb me if I let them. But I am protected by my resonance, my harmonic alignment with the chorus. The Crawlers are less of a threat—they move around me, tapping out their rhythms as they scavenge among the stones.  

As time passes, I begin to understand the purpose of this place. It is not merely an ecosystem but a living symphony, a delicate balance of sound and life. The Humming Ones shape the landscape with their erosive vibrations, the Choirs amplify and harmonize the frequencies, the Crawlers and Warps maintain the cycle of absorption and renewal. The Watchers oversee it all, ensuring the balance persists.  

I am part of this now, a note in the endless song. My identity—what little I remember—fades, replaced by my role in the chorus. I do not mourn its loss. Here, I am something larger, something eternal.  

The twilight deepens, the shadows lengthen, and the Drowned Chorus swells. Around me, the basin thrums with life, a symphony of sound and motion that rises and falls like breath. I ripple in harmony, my form pulsing with the rhythm of this ancient, alien place.  

I am here. I am part of it. And I sing.
