Here I am, in the Glowmire Warrens, where the air presses against my skin like a damp hand and the walls breathe with the slow pulse of subterranean life. I am not one of the pale, slow-moving inhabitants who tend to the city’s fragile machinery, nor am I one of the sightless creatures that scuttle in the shadows. I am something else entirely, a being born of the same contradictions that define this place—part of the city, yet apart from it, shaped by its rhythms yet still finding my own.

I am the caretaker of the moss.  

It’s a role I’ve taken upon myself, though I’m not sure if anyone else has noticed. The inhabitants move through their routines with quiet purpose, tending to pipes and gears, but I’ve found my purpose in the soft, blue-green glow that clings to the stone. I move through the city’s narrow alleys and sagging walkways with a deliberate slowness, my fingers brushing against the patches of bioluminescent moss that grow in the cracks. I coax them to spread, to flourish, to light the way for those who need it.  

My skin is pale, but not translucent like theirs. It’s marked with faint streaks of green, as if the moss itself has seeped into my veins. My hair is long and tangled, the color of damp soil, and it falls over my face like a curtain, shielding my eyes from the flickering amber lights. I wear no coveralls, no tools—just a simple tunic made of fibrous ferns woven together, its edges frayed and damp.  

I don’t speak, not in the way they do. When I need to communicate, I hum—a low, resonant sound that echoes through the cavern and harmonizes with the drip of water and the hum of machinery. The inhabitants seem to understand, or at least they don’t question it. Occasionally, one of them will leave a small offering for me—a vial of water, a scrap of metal, a handful of moss spores—and I accept it with a nod of gratitude.  

I am the keeper of the light, but also of the shadows. I understand the delicate balance that keeps the city alive, the interplay of light and dark, steam and stone, life and time. I move through the Glowmire like a ghost, slipping between the buildings and the ferns, my hands trailing along the walls as if I’m listening to the whispers of the cavern.  

At night, when the inhabitants retreat to their dimly lit homes, I climb to the highest point of the city—a precarious balcony bolted to the cavern wall—and sit with my legs dangling over the edge. From there, I can see the entire Glowmire spread out below me, its amber lights flickering like distant stars. I hum softly, and the moss responds, glowing brighter, turning the city into a constellation of blue-green light.  

I don’t know how long I’ve been here, or how long I’ll stay. The cavern is still growing, still shifting, and so am I. But for now, I am the caretaker of the moss, the guardian of the light, and I will tend to this fragile city for as long as it needs me.  

And when the time comes—when the cavern decides what it wants to be—I will be ready. I will hum one last song, and the moss will glow brighter than ever before, casting its light into the shadows as we all move on to whatever comes next.
