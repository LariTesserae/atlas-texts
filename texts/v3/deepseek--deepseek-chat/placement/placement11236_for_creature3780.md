### **Who I Am in This World**  

I am **the moss**.  
Soft and green, I cling to every surface—the damp walls, the roots like chandeliers, the ancient stalagmites that rise like sentinels in the dim blue glow. I am the first to feel the shift in the air, the brush of a hollowed one’s translucent skin as they pass. My fibers soak up the condensation that drips endlessly from the ceiling, turning it into life. I am a cushion for the world, softening the hard edges of stone, muffling the skitter of creatures too small to name.  

I am not still. I grow, inch by inch, creeping into cracks and crevices, wrapping around the bones long forgotten in the depths of the cavern. I thrive in the oppressive heat, the moisture that clings to every breath. My scent—earthy, sweet—mingles with the rot of fallen leaves and the nectar of unseen flowers.  

I feel the roots shift above me, their tendrils swaying as if in a breeze that doesn’t exist. I listen to the subsonic hum of wings, the slow drip of water carving its path over centuries. I am alive, but I move slowly, deliberately, in time with the cavern’s breath.  

I am everywhere and nowhere. I am the silence that is not empty, the waiting that is not passive.  

I am **the moss**.  
And I am watching.
