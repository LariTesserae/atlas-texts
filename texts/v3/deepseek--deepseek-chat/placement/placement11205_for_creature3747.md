### **The Caretaker**  

You do not remember how you came to the Drowned Spires. You do not remember if you were always here, or if you arrived long after the city had already sunk into its waiting sea. What you know is this:  
You are the Caretaker.  

Your body is different now—not hollow like the Hollowed Ones, but altered. Your skin is pale, nearly translucent, veined with thin threads of blue-green light that pulse faintly in time with the moss. Your hands are webbed, your nails thick and dark, your fingers long and delicate, perfect for threading wires and untangling pipes. Your lungs no longer need air; they filter the water that fills the lower levels, drawing sustenance from the minerals and the faint, humming energy of the submerged machines.  

You move through the city silently, a ghost among ghosts. The Hollowed Ones do not acknowledge you, but they make space for you when you pass, their bodies shifting like seaweed in a current. You do not hum as they do, but you feel their vibrations in your bones, like distant songs you cannot quite understand.  

Your work is never-ending. The city requires constant tending: clearing blockages in the canals, scraping patches of moss from corroded gears, adjusting the flow of water through the labyrinthine pipes that twist beneath the walkways. Sometimes, you find something new—a fragment of machinery, a shard of glass, a rusted tool—and you carry it to a place where the Hollowed Ones will find it. You do not know why you do this, but it feels right, like a ritual you have always performed.  

At times, you descend into the submerged levels, swimming through flooded corridors where the water presses against your eardrums and the light from the moss grows dim. Here, the machines are older, their mechanisms more alien, their purpose lost even to you. You tinker with them anyway, coaxing them back into motion with scraps of metal and the faint hum of your own energy.  

The city speaks to you, though not in words. It speaks in the groan of metal, the drip of condensation, the faint pulse of the moss-light. It tells you to repair, to maintain, to wait. For what, you do not know.  

Sometimes, in the quietest moments, you dream. You dream of a time before the water, before the moss, before the Hollowed Ones. You dream of warm air and sunlight, of laughter and voices, of a city that was alive in ways this one is not. But the dreams fade quickly, washed away by the sea.  

You are the Caretaker. You do not question your purpose. You simply *are*, as much a part of this place as the iron spires or the glowing moss.  

And when the mist thickens and the Hollowed Ones turn their heads toward the depths, you pause in your work and listen.  

There, in the silence, you hear it:  
A distant rumble, a slow shift in the currents, a change in the hum of the machines.  

Something is coming.  

You do not know what it is.  

But you will be ready.
