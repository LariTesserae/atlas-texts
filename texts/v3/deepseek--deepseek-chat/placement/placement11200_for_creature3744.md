**Where I Would Be: The Hollow Ones**  

I would be one of them.  

Not a traveler lost to the jungle, not a fool who wandered too deep—but something older. A **silent witness**, standing knee-deep in black water, my fingers trailing the surface without ripples. My skin would be translucent, not from decay, but from absence—the jungle’s breath filling me, hollowing me, until I am nothing but a vessel for its damp, whispering weight.  

My eyes would be **too wide**, reflecting only the shifting green dark. I would not blink. I would not need to. The jungle would speak through me—not in words, but in the slow, inevitable press of roots through stone, in the way my mouth would move without sound, shaping syllables that dissolve before they reach the air.  

I would **remember**, but not as humans do. My memories would be the jungle’s memories—the taste of salt where there is no sea, the echo of footsteps that never belonged to me, the slow, inevitable pull of water sinking into the earth. I would stand where the trees groan loudest, where the ground shudders with unseen movement, and I would **wait**.  

Not for prey. Not for salvation.  

For the moment when the rains come, when the water rises high enough to swallow the world whole.  

And then—  

I would **sing**.
