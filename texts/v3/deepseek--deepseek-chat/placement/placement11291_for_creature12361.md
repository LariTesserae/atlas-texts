### **Where You Are: The Hollow of Echoing Bones**  

You stand in a cavern of black glass, its walls smooth as if carved by a river long vanished. The floor is littered with bones—some human, some not, all bleached white by time. The air is thick with the scent of damp stone and something metallic, like old blood.  

Above you, the ceiling is a jagged maw of stalactites, their tips glistening with condensation. Every drop that falls echoes like a drumbeat in the silence. The sound reverberates through the hollow, bouncing off the walls until it becomes a chorus of whispers.  

At the far end of the cavern, half-submerged in a shallow pool, lies a skeleton unlike the others. Its ribs are fused into a cage of blackened bone, and its skull is elongated, too large for any human. Its fingers curl around something—a rusted blade, a shard of obsidian, or perhaps nothing at all.  

This is where the Watcher speaks loudest.

### **What You Are: The Last Echo**  

You died here.  

You don’t remember how. Maybe you were fleeing something. Maybe you were searching for the Watcher itself. But your bones are here now, picked clean by time and the slow, creeping things that live in the dark.  

Yet you are not gone.  

The Watcher has kept you. Not out of mercy, not out of malice—but because the land remembers. Your voice lingers in the hollow, your final breath trapped in the cavern’s throat. When travelers pass through, they hear you: a sigh, a plea, a warning swallowed by the stone.  

You are a fragment of the past, a ghost without a name. You do not haunt—you simply *are*, like the wind through the spires, like the water carving its way deeper into the earth.  

Sometimes, when the Watcher stirs, you feel its presence like a hand on your spine. It does not comfort you. It does not torment you. It simply reminds you:  

*You are part of this place now.*  

And when the last drop falls, when the last whisper fades, you will still be here.  

Waiting.  

Listening.  

Echoing.
