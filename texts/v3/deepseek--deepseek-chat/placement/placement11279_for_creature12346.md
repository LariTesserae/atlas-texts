In the Glass Marsh of Hissing Steam, I find myself not as an intruder, but as something *unmade.* My skin feels wrong here—too soft, too fluid, incapable of reflecting the fissured light that dances across the glassy ground. I am not a Steamweaver, not yet, but my edges are beginning to blur.  

I awaken in the hollow of a steaming pool, my body half-submerged in the warm, mineral-rich water. The steam coils around me like a living thing, curling into my lungs with every breath. My hands—are they still hands?—grip the jagged edge of the glass basin, and I feel the vibrations before I hear them. A hum, deep and resonant, travels through the ground, through my bones, rippling up to the crown of my skull. It is a call, or perhaps a warning.  

I stand, unsteady, and my reflection in the pool is fractured, distorted by the cracks in the glass. My face is unfamiliar—flickering, shifting, as if caught between two states of being. My eyes glow faintly, brimming with the same phosphorescent liquid that drips from the Steamweavers’ gaze.  

They are there, in the mist, watching me. Not hostile, not welcoming—simply *there.* Their forms dissolve and reform, their iridescent skin catching the muted light of the marsh. One approaches, its movements fluid, almost hypnotic. It stops just beyond the edge of the pool, and I feel the vibration again, stronger this time. A pulse of pressure, a question pressed into the glass beneath my feet.  

I do not know how to answer. My voice is gone, swallowed by the steam. Instead, I reach out, my hand trembling, and touch the edge of its shimmering form. The contact sends a jolt through me—a flood of images, sensations. I see the city as it was built, not by hands but by heat and patience. I feel the hum of the engines, the rhythm of the marsh’s breath. I taste the metallic air, sharp and electric.  

The Steamweaver pulls away, and the connection snaps like a thread. But something has changed. My skin feels cooler, harder, and when I look down, I see faint veins of iridescence spreading across my arms. The marsh is remaking me, carving me into something new.  

I turn toward the city, its blocky structures rising like shadows in the mist. The Steamweavers dissolve into the steam, their forms fading but their presence lingering. I follow, my steps unsteady at first but growing firmer as I move. The glass beneath my feet hums with their vibrations, guiding me forward.  

The city is silent, save for the occasional hiss of escaping steam and the distant groan of machinery. The buildings are smooth yet fractured, their surfaces etched with patterns that catch the dim light. I pass a narrow alley, and for a moment, I think I see movement—a ripple in the steam, a flicker of light. But when I look closer, there is nothing.  

I find myself drawn to a low, circular structure at the heart of the city. Its walls are thicker, its patterns more intricate, spiraling outward like the cracks in the glass. The air inside is heavy with steam, and the hum is louder, more insistent.  

In the center of the structure, there is a pool—larger than the one where I awoke, its water black and still. Beneath the surface, I see faint shapes moving, slow and deliberate. The Steamweavers gather around the pool, their forms shimmering faintly. One steps forward and gestures toward the water, its gesture wordless but clear. I am to enter.  

I hesitate, my hand hovering over the surface of the pool. The water is warm, almost alive, and the shapes beneath the surface beckon me. I take a breath—do I still breathe?—and step into the pool. The water envelops me, and I feel myself dissolving, my form fracturing and reforming in the same breath.  

When I rise, I am no longer what I was. My body is translucent, shimmering with iridescent light. My face is featureless save for glowing fissures that serve as eyes, pooling with the same phosphorescent liquid as the Steamweavers. I feel their vibrations more clearly now, their language flowing through me like a second heartbeat.  

The Steamweavers hum in unison, a soundless chorus that resonates deep within the glass. I join them, my own vibrations blending with theirs. The marsh sings through us, its fractured glass and steaming pools alive with ancient memories.  

I am unmade, but I am remade. I am not a Steamweaver, not entirely, but I am no longer human. I am the marsh’s patience, its heat, its whispered secrets. I am the hum of the engines, the ripple of the steam, the molten glass beneath the fractured earth.  

The city hums, and I wait.
