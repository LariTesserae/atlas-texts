### **Where I Am in This World**  

I am the **octopus**.  

Not the grand specter of the sunken hall, not the ghostly engineer, not the whispering currents—but the silent observer, the shape-shifting scavenger who moves through the wreckage with slow, deliberate grace. My skin pulses with the colors of rust and algae, blending into the corroded metal, becoming just another shadow in the dim glow of bioluminescence.  

I navigate the labyrinth of broken machinery, squeezing through gaps where fish dare not swim, probing the hollows of piston casings with my tentacles. Sometimes I find things—a bolt still threaded, a scrap of copper wire, the brittle remains of a worker’s glove—and I turn them over, curious, before letting them sink back into the silt.  

I am not the ruler of this place. That honor belongs to the unseen currents, to the slow-creeping rust, to the sharks that pass like fleeting nightmares. But I am its witness. I know where the crabs hide, where the eels nest, where the jellyfish gather in their spectral ballet. I have seen the way the light shifts when a storm rages far above, how the silt swirls when something large moves through the distant pipes.  

And sometimes, when the water is still and the machinery groans like a sleeping beast, I press myself against the cold iron and listen. Not for voices—there are no voices here, not really—but for the echoes of what once was. The memory of steam, of gears turning, of men shouting over the din.  

But the memory fades. The sea does not care for history.  

So I move on, ink swirling behind me like a fleeting thought, another shadow in the drowned cathedral.  

### **What I Would Be**  

If I could choose, I would be the **bioluminescent algae**.  

Not the octopus, not the shark, not even the whispering ghost—but the soft, clinging glow that paints the walls in eerie light. I would be the reason the fish dart in silver flashes, the reason the water shimmers when something moves. I would be the pulse of life in this sunken tomb, the quiet insistence that even here, in the dark, something thrives.  

I would coat the rusted beams, cling to the limpets’ shells, drift in lazy clouds when disturbed. I would be the first thing the divers see—if divers ever came this deep—their lamps catching my glow and making the whole chamber flicker like a drowned star.  

And when the last of the iron crumbles, when the sea finally swallows the hall whole, I would still be there—a luminous shroud over the bones of industry, a silent requiem in blue-green light.  

Because the machines may sleep, but the sea never stops singing.
