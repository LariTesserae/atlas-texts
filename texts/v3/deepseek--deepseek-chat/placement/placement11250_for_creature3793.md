### **The Echo-Keeper of Vhalei**  

I am not one of them—the Hollow Dwellers—but I am also not *not* one of them. I was born in the shadow of the ruins, in one of the shallow caves that pepper the edges of the city. My skin is a muted silver, not gray-green like theirs, and my eyes catch the light differently—less fractured, more reflective, like polished stone. I blink, though not often. I breathe, though it feels like an afterthought.  

My mother, if she could be called that, was of the Hollow Dwellers. She found me—or I found her—on the outskirts of Vhalei, where the canals narrow into streams that whisper secrets to the ferns. She hummed to me, her voice low and resonant, and it echoed in my chest like a second heartbeat. She did not name me. She pressed her palm to my forehead, and I felt her hum sink into my bones, branding me with her memory.  

I am a child of the ruins and the water. I move like the Dwellers—silent, fluid—but I am less patient, less still. I explore the canals, tracing the veins of iridescent minerals with my fingers, letting their light bleed into my skin. I climb the towers, their impossible geometries bending to accommodate my weight. I touch the walls, and the dormant circuitry flickers more brightly for me than it does for them.  

The city speaks to me in fragments. When I press my ear to the ground, I hear the buried engines groan, their rhythm syncopated and erratic. When I dip my hands into the canals, I feel the underground springs pulse, their currents carrying whispers of the old world. When I stand among the ruins, I hear the Hum—the collective resonance of the Dwellers—but it is softer for me, less absolute.  

I am not bound to the Hum. I can speak, though my voice is thin and wavering, like the mist that clings to the ruins. Sometimes I hum, but my vibration is higher, more brittle. The Dwellers tilt their heads when I do this, as if trying to reconcile my sound with theirs.  

I eat like them—lichen light, canal water, mineral whispers—but I also crave something more. I pluck the rubbery leaves of the vines, crushing them between my fingers to release a bitter sap. I scrape the moss from the walls and lick the residue from my palms. I crouch beside the fissures and scoop the shimmering liquid into my mouth, letting it coat my throat like molten glass.  

I am waiting, like them, but differently. They wait for the city to finish its dream. I wait to understand my place in it.  

Sometimes I climb to the highest spire of the tallest tower, balancing on its crumbling edge. From there, I can see the whole of Vhalei—the canals snaking through the ruins, the ferns swaying in the breeze, the Dwellers moving like shadows beneath the twilight sky. I hum, just to hear the echo.  

The city hums back.  

I do not know if I am its keeper or its echo. Perhaps I am both.  

Perhaps I am neither.  

Perhaps I am something else entirely.
