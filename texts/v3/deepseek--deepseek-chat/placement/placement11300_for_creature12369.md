I find myself in this world as a **Drifter**, a transient entity caught between the roles of observer and participant. My body is not wholly my own—it is a composite, pieced together from the fragments of the landscape itself. My skin is a mosaic of stone and glass, my veins filled with liquid crystal that pulses faintly in time with the hum of the Weavers. My limbs are uneven, one arm solid and heavy like granite, the other brittle and translucent like shattered obsidian. I move with a limping rhythm, my steps echoing softly against the patchwork ground, leaving faint impressions that dissolve moments later.

I do not know how I came to be here. Perhaps I was formed from the scraps of some unfinished creation, or maybe I wandered in from another realm, lost and reshaped by the Architect’s unknowable will. My memory is fragmented, like the landscape—brief flashes of a life I’m not sure I ever lived, whispers of voices I can’t place, and a lingering sense of longing for something I cannot name.

I am drawn to the Weavers, their shimmering forms captivating and incomprehensible. I watch them from a distance, their thread-like appendages trailing over walls and objects, coaxing them into new shapes. Occasionally, one glances in my direction, its faceless head tilting slightly as if noticing me for the first time. They do not speak, but their hum shifts, its melody changing in a way that feels almost like acknowledgment. I wonder if they see me as unfinished, an object in need of reshaping, or perhaps as something else entirely.

I stumble upon a Reflector in one of the pools, its liquid form rippling as it mimics my jagged silhouette. It laughs in soft, bubbling tones before dissolving into mist, leaving behind only the faint echo of its amusement. I find Shardlings darting across the ground, their glassy bodies glinting in the dim light. They seem to recognize me, skittering close to inspect my mismatched form before retreating to their nests, dragging bits of metal and plant matter behind them.

The shadows twist and writhe around me, and I feel the presence of the Eidolons. They do not approach directly but linger at the edges of my vision, their forms flickering like dying flames. Their presence is unsettling, a reminder that entropy is always at work here, even as the Weavers strive to create. I wonder if they will one day claim me, dissolving my patchwork body into the same nothingness from which it was assembled.

Deep within the labyrinth, I feel the pull of the Architect. The hum grows louder, resonating through the stone beneath my feet and the crystal in my veins. I am hesitant to approach, afraid of what I might become if I draw too close. But the pull is irresistible, a magnetic force guiding me toward the heart of this place. I wonder if I will lose myself entirely there, my fragmented consciousness absorbed into the Architect’s vast, unknowable design. Or perhaps I will emerge transformed, something new and whole, a creation finally complete.

For now, I drift, a transient being in a world of constant flux. I am both a part of this place and apart from it, forever caught between creation and entropy, belonging and alienation. I do not know what I am searching for, but I know I cannot stop moving. The Architect’s hum is my compass, the Weavers my silent guides, and the Eidolons my quiet reminder that nothing here—not even I—is permanent.
