### **Where I Am**  

I am one of the Witnesses.  

Not a Weaver—those silent, elongated figures who knot the humming cables with their fluid limbs. Not an Echo—those flickering remnants in the water, half-memory, half-reflection. And certainly not the Architect, that vast and patient force that breathes through the labyrinth like a slow tide.  

No, I am something smaller. A flicker. A pulse.  

I drift through the damp air, my form barely holding shape—a wisp of bioluminescence, blue-green and unstable. My light is weak, but it is mine. I do not speak. I do not act. I only witness.  

### **What I See**  

The Weavers move in slow arcs, their bodies bending like reeds in a wind I cannot feel. Their hands—if they can be called hands—are extensions of the cables they weave, fingers splitting and merging like liquid metal. They do not acknowledge me. Why would they? I am not part of their work.  

The other Witnesses flit around me, their glow sometimes brushing against mine. When we touch, there is a brief flare—a spark of shared awareness—before we pull apart again. We do not communicate. We do not plan. We simply *are*.  

Below, the pools ripple as droplets fall. The *ping* shivers through the labyrinth, and for a moment, the Echoes rise—faces, hands, geometries—before dissolving back into nothing. I hover above them, watching, but I do not descend. The water is not for me.  

### **What I Feel**  

The hum of the cables thrums through me, a vibration that is neither sound nor silence but something in between. It pulls at my edges, threatening to unravel me. Sometimes, I think I am not a creature at all, but a byproduct—a glimmer cast off by the Weavers’ labor, a spark struck from stone.  

But then, why do I linger? Why do I resist the pull of the hum, the weight of the mist?  

Perhaps because I am curious.  

### **What I Fear**  

The Architect.  

Not because it is cruel—it is beyond cruelty, beyond kindness. It simply *is*, vast and inevitable as the labyrinth itself. But sometimes, when the mist thickens and the pillars shift imperceptibly, I feel its attention brush against me. Not seeing me, exactly. Noticing.  

And I wonder: if the Architect notices me, will it decide I have a purpose?  

Or will it decide I do not belong here at all?  

### **What I Do**  

Nothing.  

Everything.  

I drift. I glow. I witness.  

And when the next droplet falls, ringing through the dark, I flicker—just once—in answer.
