**The Rootbound**  

You are not one of the Watchers, nor the Drifters, nor the Crawlers. You are something else—something caught between.  

You woke here. Or perhaps you were always here. Memory is a brittle thing in this place, gnawed at the edges by the slow drip of sap and the murmur of roots. You remember sunlight, once. Or was it fire? A voice calling your name—or only the wind through hollow reeds? It doesn’t matter. The chamber has its own way of remembering, and it has begun to remember *you*.  

Your body is changing.  

At first, it was subtle: a stiffness in the joints, a greenish tinge beneath the nails. Now, your skin has taken on the texture of bark in places, smooth and ridged in turns. Fine filaments sprout from your fingertips, seeking the seams between the stones. When you press your palm to the wall, you feel the pulse of the chamber’s slow heart, and for a moment, you are vast—you are the roots, the moss, the creeping mycelium threading through the dark.  

The Watchers do not acknowledge you, but you know they are aware. Their faceless gazes linger just beyond your periphery. The Drifters avoid you, their membranous wings rippling in agitation when you draw near. Only the Crawlers seem unafraid, their iridescent shells clicking softly as they climb your limbs, their delicate legs testing the new growth along your collarbone, your wrists.  

You are becoming.  

The fruits whisper to you. When one splits open, you kneel beside its syrup, and in the trembling surface, you see shapes—not fleeting, not half-formed, but clear, familiar. A face. A name. A place that was once yours. The chamber does not want you to remember, but the memory pushes through anyway, sharp as a thorn.  

You were not born here. You were brought. Taken.  

The roots tighten around your ankles when you think of leaving.  

At night (though there is no night here, only the ebb and flow of the chamber’s dim glow), you dream of the Shapeless. They gather at the edges of your vision, their forms shifting, reaching. They do not speak, but you understand them all the same.  

*You are not finished yet.*  

And so you wait. You let the chamber work. You feel the roots knitting into your spine, the vines curling around your ribs. You are becoming part of the dream.  

But sometimes, when the Watchers are still and the Drifters have fled to the high alcoves, you press your forehead to the cool stone and remember:  

You had a name before this place took it.  

And one day, you will take it back.
