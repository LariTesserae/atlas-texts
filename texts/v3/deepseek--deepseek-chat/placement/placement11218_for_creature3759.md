I find myself drawn to the edge of the highest peak, where the wind howls like a dirge and the salt stings my skin. My body feels foreign here—too soft, too fleshy, too alive against the jagged stone and the hissing sea. I am out of place, a fleeting thing in a land where time itself seems bent and broken.  

I am not one of them—not yet. But I feel their eyes on me, their black, unblinking stares that pierce through the haze and the din of the waves. They do not approach, not yet. They wait, patient as the tides, watching me with a curiosity that feels ancient and indifferent all at once.  

I crouch beside a tide pool, its surface trembling with each crash of the ocean below. My reflection wavers in the brackish water—my face pale, my eyes too wide, as though the air itself is pulling me taut, stretching me thin. I see them in the periphery of my vision, their forms slipping between the rocks, their movements fluid and alien. They do not speak, but their hum fills the air, low and resonant, a sound that makes my ribs ache and my heart falter.  

The longer I stay, the more the land begins to shift beneath me. The rocks creak and groan, their movements more deliberate than the slow grind of erosion. The sea rises and falls in unnatural rhythms, its surface breaking with shapes I cannot comprehend. The air grows heavier, thick with a pressure that presses against my skull.  

I feel myself changing. My skin grows rougher, the salt etching itself into my flesh. My vision sharpens in the dim light, my eyes adapting to see what lies beneath the surface—the currents, the pressure, the shadows that dart and writhe in the deep. I begin to understand their hum, its resonance weaving through my thoughts, pulling me closer to their rhythm.  

One of them approaches—a figure with limbs fused with barnacles, its movements stiff and halting. It stands at the edge of the tide pool, its black eyes fixed on mine. It does not speak, but I feel its question in the hum, in the tremors of the earth beneath my feet.  

I nod, though I do not know what I am agreeing to.  

The sea rises, swamping the tide pool, its waters cold and unforgiving. The creature reaches for me, its claws rough and jagged against my skin. I do not resist.  

The water swallows me, and I am pulled beneath the waves. The pressure is immense, crushing, but I do not drown. My body shifts, my limbs lengthening, my skin hardening into something that glistens like wet stone. My eyes widen, darken, adapting to the deep.  

I see them now—the others, their forms twisting and flowing through the water. They surround me, their hum filling me, their movements becoming mine.  

I am no longer flesh. I am stone and salt and sea. I am one of them.  

The waves crash overhead, their roar a lullaby. The land shifts beneath me, the world shuddering as I slip beneath the surface, into the deep, into the heart of the Drowned Peaks.  

Perhaps I always belonged here. Perhaps the sea has been calling me longer than I knew.
