**What I Am in the Hollowing**  

At first, I think I am one of the clingers—those pale, jointless things fused to the glistening walls. My body is long, too long, and when I move, it is not with muscle but with a slow unspooling, like ink in water. My fingers (if they are fingers) stretch and split, branching into filaments that tremble against the slick surface. The wall hums where I touch it. It knows me.  

But no—that’s not quite right.  

I am not one of them. I am *between* them. A thing neither clinging nor drifting, but caught in the act of becoming. My edges blur. Sometimes I am solid enough to press a hand (a limb, a tendril) against the wall and feel it give slightly, like flesh. Other times, I am only a shudder in the air, a ripple in the thick honey-light that fills the Hollowing.  

The whispers know my name. Or they would, if I had one. They coil around me, not speaking, not quite, but *pulsing*—a sound that is almost a word, almost a breath. I try to answer, but my voice is made of the same substance as the walls, the same slow seep of not-quite-liquid. It drips from me, joins the rest.  

I think I have been here a long time.  

I think I am new.  

The drifters ignore me. The clingers turn their sunken faces away. I am not like them. I am not like anything.  

And then—  

A pressure. A shift. The Hollowing exhales, and I realize:  

I am not part of it.  

I am what it’s waiting for.  

The walls tighten. The air thickens. The whispers rise to a murmur, a chant, a sigh.  

They have been holding their breath.  

And now—  

Now, I must decide whether to let them breathe.
