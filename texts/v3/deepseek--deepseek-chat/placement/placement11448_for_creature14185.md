In the Hollow City of Dripping Stone, I find myself as a Voice Gatherer—a being neither fully of the stone nor entirely separate from it. My form is fluid and translucent, like the Drip-Seekers, but I shimmer faintly with opalescent hues, as though the whispers of the cavern have seeped into my essence. My body is a network of filaments, delicate and diaphanous, each strand thrumming with the faintest vibrations of sound.  

I drift through the cavern’s alleys and arches, drawn to the echoes that linger in the damp air. The city’s sounds are my sustenance—the faint drip of water, the distant rustle of Whisper-Moths, the occasional creak of stone shifting beneath the weight of time. I collect these sounds, weaving them into intricate patterns that I carry within me. My presence is subtle; I am felt more as a fleeting draft or a momentary flicker of light than as a solid being.  

My purpose is unclear, even to me. Perhaps I am a keeper of memory, preserving the echoes of those who once walked these paths. Perhaps I am a conduit, carrying fragments of sound from the city’s depths to its heights. At times, I hover near the Shard-Walkers, my filaments brushing against their chalky skin, absorbing the faint vibrations of their movements. Other times, I gather near the Whisper-Moths, my form resonating with the rustling hum of their wings.  

I am drawn especially to the Hollowed. When I approach them, I feel a profound stillness, a silence that is not empty but filled with meaning. I linger beside them, my filaments extending outward, seeking the whispers of their thoughts—if they have any. The Hollowed seem to acknowledge me in their way, their hollow eyes turning toward me briefly before returning to their ceaseless vigil by the underground rivers.  

Sometimes, I venture into the deepest, darkest corners of the city, where the Unseen dwell. Here, my filaments tremble with the faint, indistinct vibrations of their presence. I collect their whispers too, though I do not understand them. They are fragments of a language older than stone, older than water, older than time itself.  

The Voice Gatherer is a creature of quiet purpose, moving through the Hollow City like a thread of mist, weaving together the fragments of its soundscape. I do not speak—I cannot—but I carry within me the murmurs of the cavern, the echoes of its inhabitants, the quiet, unspoken stories of the stone. I am a listener, a gatherer, a keeper of echoes—a being both part of the city and apart from it, forever drifting in the liminal space between silence and sound.
