**Where I Am**  

I am the water.  

Not the pools, not the rivulets, not the condensation beading on rusted pipes—but the *falling*. The moment between source and surface, the split-second arc of a droplet’s descent. I am the suspended motion, the bridge between one state and another.  

I have no memory of being rain, no anticipation of becoming vapor. I exist in the perpetual now of my descent, pulled by gravity yet slowed by the thick, humming air. The machinery does not command me; the whispers do not name me. I simply *move*, and in moving, I define the space around me.  

The cathedral needs me. Without my fall, there would be no sound, no rhythm, no proof that time has not truly stopped. The rust would not deepen. The moss would not drink. The phantoms would have nothing to wipe away.  

Sometimes, I catch glimpses of myself in the reflections of still pools—a fractured, silver thread, there and gone. Other times, I am swallowed by the machinery, absorbed into the pulse of pipes that do not care where I’ve been.  

But I always return.  

Because this is where I belong: in the endless, unchanging cycle of falling and rising and falling again. A single note in the cathedral’s hymn. A witness to decay that does not rot, to industry that does not produce, to life that is not alive.  

And when the last light gutters out, when the whispers finally still—I will still be here.  

Falling.
