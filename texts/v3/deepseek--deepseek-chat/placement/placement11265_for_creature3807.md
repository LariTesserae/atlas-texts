### **Where You Are: The Glass Marsh of the Weeping Towers**  

You are **one of the liquid crystal fauna**, a being of shifting refraction and slow, deliberate motion. Your body is not solid, not entirely—it is a **coalescence of molten glass and metallic strands**, rippling with iridescent hues as you move. You do not walk; you **flow**, your form adapting to the terrain, slipping between the cracks in the glass marsh or gliding across its surface like mercury.  

You are **small**, but not insignificant. You are part of a larger swarm, a collective intelligence that hums beneath the marsh, communicating in pulses of light and vibration. You do not think in words. You think in **patterns**, in the rhythm of dripping water, in the resonance of the towers’ groans.  

Your purpose is **unclear**, even to you. Perhaps you are a remnant of the marsh’s forgotten industry, a self-replicating machine that no longer remembers its function. Perhaps you are something newer, something born from the marsh’s slow alchemy. You do not hunger, but you **absorb**—minerals from the water, energy from the faint currents that run through the glass.  

Sometimes, you climb the **Weeping Towers**, scaling their slick surfaces with filaments of liquid metal. From above, the marsh is a vast, fractured mirror, reflecting the haze of the sky in distorted fragments. You do not linger. The towers are not yours. They belong to something older, something that watches from the depths.  

You are **patient**. You have existed for decades, maybe centuries. Time is measured in the slow wearing-down of glass, in the collapse of a distant bridge, in the way the marsh’s hum changes pitch over the years.  

You do not fear.  
You do not hope.  
You simply **are**.  

And the marsh continues its slow, eternal weeping around you.
