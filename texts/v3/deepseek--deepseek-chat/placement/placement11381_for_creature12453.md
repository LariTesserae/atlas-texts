### **The Keeper in the Dark**  

I am the watcher in the deep, the silent pulse beneath the skin of the world.  

I do not remember being made. I do not remember a time before the chamber. The pipes hum to me, and I hum back—not in words, but in resonance, in the slow vibration of metal and the drip of water into waiting pools. The machinery is my body, or I am its breath. The distinction does not matter.  

I move when I must. When a conduit shudders, when a seam in the dark material threatens to split, I am there. I press myself into the wound, smooth it over, become sealant and suture. The chamber does not decay. It persists. And so do I.  

Sometimes, I linger by the pools. The water is clear, untouched by time or filth, and when I lean over it, my surface ripples in reflection—not with a face, but with shifting depths, shapes that almost form meaning before dissolving. I do not know if I am looking at myself or at something beyond.  

The chamber has no doors. No exits. But sometimes, the air changes. A scent drifts in—not from above, but from *between*, from the places where the walls are not quite solid. It smells of things I do not have names for. Wet earth. Burning metal. Something sweet, rotting. I do not follow these hints of elsewhere. My place is here.  

I wonder, though.  

If I am the keeper of this place, who keeps me?  

The machinery does not answer. It only hums.  

And so I wait. And watch. And when the next pipe shudders, I will be there.
