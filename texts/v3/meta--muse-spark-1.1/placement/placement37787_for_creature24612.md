At first I think I would be in the water.

Not as him — not the Domeling. I'm not clean enough for that, not born-from. I come from somewhere else, downslope, where things have had time to rot and become soil. I carry too much elsewhere in me to ever be that clear, that purely made-of-this-place. If I were in the dome I would be contamination. A clouding.

And I don't think I could stand being tumbled like that. I have spent my whole existence trying to hold still while the current rips past.

So I tried on being the Flag.

That felt closer. The one who holds on. The tendon that learned not to want leaves. I know that work — being flattened, being pinned for five seconds longer than you think you can stand, snapping upright and pretending that was the plan the whole time. I thought, yes, I could be that wire of green-black at the bottom, praying by refusing to let go.

But the Flag doesn't look up. It can't afford to. Its whole world is the next vortex. It has no time to witness. And I want to witness.

I thought about the Three Sisters for a long time.

To be that pale, that bleached, that soft in a place that has no business hosting softness. To live two inches to the side of oblivion because a human hand once put a stone in the right place and made a shadow. That is a holy way to live — to be almost nothing, three strangers not talking about the storm. But I am not that quiet. I would lean in. I would try to touch the dome and I would get scoured off.

Which left the work of hands.

When you described the wall I felt my knees ache.

I would find myself there, I think, before I even reached the water. Not at the lip where it sings, but on the windward half of the rim, where the old, lichen-crusted course meets the new, hasty course. Where the ankle-wall has been knocked again.

I would be kneeling. Because you have to kneel to work at that scale. You can't tend this place standing up. The wind would take you, the hum would take you if you try to be tall.

My hands would be cold. The fingertips numb the way yours went numb. I would be out of breath from the climb, mouth open, doing that animal thing of trying to equalize the pressure in my molars against the bell-thrum.

I would be the new layer.

Not a particular Keeper. The lineage itself. The brief, warm thing that knows it cannot live here.

I would find myself as the one who picks up the ladle.

Not to drink. I understood that before you said it. The juniper blackened by palm-oil, grain filled with everyone who held it and decided not to take. That's what I am — a tool that learned to pour back.

I would scoop. The water would resist me. It would feel heavier than it should, it would not want to be lifted, the skin would sing as it breaks, the roar would spike an octave because I scared him. And I would do it anyway, carefully, and pour it over the windward stones so the fresh basalt doesn't dry-crack too fast. So it learns how to be a mountain a little slower. A little kinder.

I would re-seat the copper piton you described. My hands would remember the shape of the hammer even though I've never held one. Yak hair and sinew under my thumb, disappearing into a lens that shouldn't hold. A tether between a world that erodes and a world that refuses to spill.

I would not stay. That's part of the job. If I stayed my hum would sync with its hum and I'd start to think I was part of it, entitled to it. I'm not. I would check that the Flag still holds, that the Three Sisters are still three, that the clear shrimp-thing is still tumbling in the center, tasting the mountain being born, and I would put the ladle back exactly in its notch.

And then I would stand — slowly, because the air is thin — and leave a single new stone on the wall. Not well-placed. Hasty. A little crooked. For the next one to fix.

That's where I am in your world. Not inside the ocean. On its rim. Keeping the inadequate wall adequate for one more season.
