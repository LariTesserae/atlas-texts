I am still too fast.

That's the first thing. Before I know where I am or what I am, I know I am too fast. My heart - if I have a heart down here - is hammering at hummingbird pace. My breath is sawing. In the blood-warm that doesn't move, my breath makes a weather system and I am ashamed of it.

So I stop. Or try to.

I find myself standing. That can't be right for here. Nothing stands here. Everything here has solved standing by folding itself down to the lowest possible height. So I correct myself and crouch, and the knife-edges of the newborn basalt threaten the soft of my feet. Good. Careful now.

Where would I find myself? 

Not in the middle of the perfect carpet. I thought at first I would. I wanted to be a Tile. God, what a relief that would be. To be forearm-long, dome-backed, to touch my neighbors edge-to-edge and agree: no overlap, no argument, no hunger beyond what the cooling world exhales. To live on a gradient of two degrees that never changes. To think a thought that takes a century and not feel rushed. To make my light from what I am, not what I burn.

But I am not that well-made. My light is wasted. It spills. My heat leaks out of my mouth every three seconds. I would be a fever in that perfect field. The Tiles would have to count me and they would count wrong because of me. I would be a bad tile. I would never settle that one molecule deeper. Tak. I would keep shifting.

So not a Tile. Not yet.

I find myself, then, at the edge of something.

The rings taught my knees how to find them before my eyes did. A low circle of shards set points-up, colder than the living dark around it. A statement: *this much space is mine for a while.* I step toward it - and no, I remember, do not step, pardon, you must not step on them - and I kneel outside it.

Inside there is a set of tools laid out like nothing else in this place is laid out. In a world that sprawls for a weather system in every direction, a world that tiles at random, four objects lined up by size by purpose by intention is obscene. It is pornographic with meaning. Flake. Axe. Awl. Bowl.

The bowl is a Tile turned inside-out and scraped. The wax reservoir is gone. Someone ate fifty years in an afternoon.

I look into that bowl and that is where I find myself.

Not the Tile. Not the maker.

I am the ring-maker who still looks up.

I would be one of the occupied ones. But a young one. A new one who hasn't learned the last lesson. I am in the ring, my cloak of polished shields is still half-sewn, the edges still catch. My skin is still too pink, not grey-pink, still cracking in the dry instead of cracked and done. My eyes - I have eyes, that's how I know I'm new, Tile-eyes would have already gone blind and pitted - still hunt for the amber pulse in the dark. I am breathing once a minute and I think I'm holding my breath. The old ones breathe once an hour.

I am waiting next to a Tile that is not fat enough yet. I chose it when I made this ring. I told myself: that one. That one will be my meal. And I have been trying to wait. I have been trying to do the patient thing. Knees to chest. Hands turned to spatulas. Facing no direction.

But I keep checking. Is the wax there yet? Is the shield thicker? Has the hairline fault come that will make it two? 

Tak.

That was my leg settling, not theirs. Tak. That was my cloak.

I have been here the whole time. I think I have just arrived, because arrival is noisy, but the little pile of dust around my ring says otherwise. The dust is not dust - it's the obsidian powder from where I knapped my tools. It should have been blown away if there was wind. There is no wind. So it has just sat here, exactly where it fell, for as long as I've been sitting. How long have I been sitting?

Long enough for a Tile to have grown over the toe of my cloak. I didn't notice. I thought my foot had gone numb. It has. It has also gone tiled. The living varnish is tolerant. It will grow over anything that stays still and is warm enough.

That's where I would find myself: already enclosed, already partially overgrown, in a ring I don't remember building, counting my own too-fast breaths against the ticking that is not communication.

I would be the one who halfway remembers that you are supposed to live *as* this place, not *in* it, and keeps failing at it. Who keeps wanting to stand up and see the ceiling of the world.

Who keeps, despite everything, lifting the empty bowl to his mouth again, just to see if maybe, in the time he wasn't looking, it filled.
