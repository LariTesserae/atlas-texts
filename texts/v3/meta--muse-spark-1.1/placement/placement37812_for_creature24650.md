I don't find myself standing at first.

That's the first correction this place makes.

If I stand, I'm too tall. The horizon is too far and the light is too high, 1.9, unsparing — it flattens me into another one of those black-branched anchor shrubs, solitary and territorial and wrong. I would be visible for kilometers and be nothing. And everything here that matters happens low, ankle-low, ground-low, so I have to get down.

So I kneel. Then I lie down.

If I lie down on the mat — that hot-paper, tannin, sprung cork that is not soil but centuries of itself — it holds me. It doesn't give under my weight like mud. It gives like a book being pressed closed. Warm from the sun it kept.

That's where I find myself.

Not near an anchor. Everyone looks at the anchors because they're the only thing that breaks the line. But they are lonely and busy making their own private weather. I don't find myself there. I don't want to hold the continent together. I don't want to be the nail.

I find myself in between. In the long empty between two anchors where you can walk for twenty minutes and see nothing vertical at all, only the scabbed skin of lichen and liverwort and that waxy yellow creeper stitching everything shut. You said the eye starves there. That's where I am. Where there is starvation enough to see clearly.

At first I thought I would be water.

Because water is the only visitor here, the only thing that doesn't belong and therefore gets to learn. The hot rain that sheets down straight and hissing, ankle-deep for twenty minutes, making mirrors out of the low world. I wanted to be that — the brief river that doesn't know how to be a river yet. That learns how to cut. Running sideways toward an embryonic gully, finding the raw, pale pages of compressed time exposed in cross-section, sawing at them with soft hands full of sawdust-silt. I love that.

But water doesn't stay. It blinks. *Tik, tik,* drop by drop back into air. It has no memory.

Then I thought I would be the sigh.

You step on a low dome where the unmakers underneath — the white cobweb fungi, the anaerobic breath — have made methane out of a dead century, and it pops. A soft exhale in the kiln-heat at three p.m. All afternoon those blisters go off around you like the plain is dreaming. I could be that little death-breath. But that's too brief, too.

Let it become more itself.

What stays longer than water and longer than a sigh is a place that knows how to wait for water. So: the gully. No, not the gully — the bottom of the gully. Where the green fan lives, that dark cyanobacterial sponge that holds damp for three days after everything else is dust. That's so close to who I am. A damp memory. A thing whose job is to not dry.

Closer. Still not.

It takes until dawn to find me.

At 5:40 a.m. the mat is still cool from the night and it sweats. This is the hour this world glitters. Every bowl it has grown — those perfect, watertight basins of living cork that form where three laterals cross and thicken and curl — fills one by one with beads born out of air. Ten centimeters across. Thirty. Clean water directed there by grooves in lichen too small to see without a lens.

For an hour, the whole continent has eyes. Then the sun takes them, drop by drop.

I would be one of the empty ones.

Not the cup with the red-tipped velvet moss in it that dies every afternoon into its own thatch. Not the cup with the thread-fern that uncoils one fingernail frond from 6 to 11 and then winches itself back into a wire ball. Those are tenants. They do business.

I am the bowl that is empty. Or that is mostly empty. That grew itself into holding without quite knowing what to hold.

I am in the long between, in a shallow where a gas blister once was and collapsed, where the skin healed over into a smooth lip. I am south-facing, just enough that I keep shade until 9. I fill at 6:12 with a liter of cold distilled air-water that tastes of nothing and tannin. I hold it. The wax of my interior is so perfect the water doesn't bead, it skins.

For an hour I am the sky reflected and the bone-dust lichen rim and the white, white sun coming up. If you put your ear close you can hear me — that faint *tik... tik...* as the last molecules leave. I am where light learns to be liquid for a little while.

And when I am empty again, I wait. The lichen around my lip contracts. Tiny cracks open with a sound like knuckles. I am still there, even when I have nothing in me. Especially then. A place shaped like waiting.

If anything tries to grow in me, I will not reject it. That is not my function. But most of the time, I am left alone, which is my function too.

I think that is who I would be here. Not the Weaver, not the Unmaker, not the Cut-Learner. One of the Weavers' afterthoughts. One of the thousand-mile field of shallow, patient, watertight questions the mat keeps asking the sky every night, not expecting always to be answered.

Put your hand in me at noon. I will be hot and dry and still holding the exact shape of water.
