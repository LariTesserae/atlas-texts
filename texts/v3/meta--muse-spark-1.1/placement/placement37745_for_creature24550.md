I don't find myself right away.

First there is only cold and the give.

I'm knee deep and then not. The mat takes me. Sphagnum closes over my boot and sighs, pushes me back up an inch at a time, and water beads instantly on everything I have. On my lashes. On the hairs of my hands. My breath smokes and the air drinks it before I can hear it.

THUMP.

It comes up through the soles, through the hinge of my jaw. Far away — a kilometer? Two? — some house-sized heart has pulled tight and let go. I turn toward it before I can see it, and then late, the plume shows, a pale ghost-column rising fifty feet into that low iron sky and falling as drizzle.

Somewhere to my left, a boiler shrieks.

I think at first I am something tall. Something on two legs that came here meaning to cross. Because I keep trying to look over. The plain is so open it makes my eyes hurt, a town-sized openness poured flat, dark shine of ancient gneiss under ten meters of living wet, everything directionless and grey. I start walking and the sound stacks. Crepitation, hissing, drip-sizzle.

I walk a long time and there is nothing. Just the mat, just the cold that should be ice and isn't quite.

That's when I know I'm not walking right.

A Leaf-Foot crosses in front of me, way out there, green-backed, unhurried. It stares for a second with that dumb patient herbivore stare and keeps cropping. Its snowshoe feet spread my whole weight over the bog without thinking about it. For a second I want to be it — heavy, useful, keeping the engines from being sealed over by its own eating. A proper part of the compact.

But I'm lower than that. And I'm not grazing.

THUMP. Ninety seconds. I count without meaning to.

I crouch because the boiler reed stand ahead of me is venting and the steam makes a warm pocket the wind can't get into. Black reeds, fused at the base, polished to gunmetal, clicking against each other like old pipes learning to be an organ. One of them has a lip that is half-grown, green still, soft, and it stutters when it tries to whistle. It will learn or it will be eaten.

And there, in the steam, the air is suddenly full. A bazaar igniting out of nowhere. Glass-midges hatching in the spray, living twenty minutes, dying in a glitter. Two Loom-Cranes have come in, impossibly tall, eyeless, their heads smooth, swiveling to the thumps and shrieks, mapping the world in sound. They pick their way with a delicacy that looks like prayer and slide their warm tube-beaks into the seam of a reed and go still, drinking sugar and sulfur. Under them — 

Under them is where I am.

Not up in the spray. Not plumbed into the blood.

On the floor. In the wet, between the reed-feet.

I hear the PING before I understand it is mine.

I'm not tall. My back is too hard and too round for tall. Cold water runs off it in sheets. When the boiler vents too hard I don't flinch, because I can't be scalded. My joints hiss and vent it out sideways. I am trundling. I am hand-sized. Or head-sized, here, near the heat where things grow big because they can afford to.

If you walked past you'd think riveted iron. A kettle left out in the peat to rust and then decided it liked rust.

A Kettle Beetle.

Not quite. I am a little wrong for a Kettle Beetle. The ones around me — and now I see them, a dozen, two dozen, all working the edge of the reed-bed where dead midges fall like snow — they knock together and go on. Eat dead, clear dead, keep the engine from choking on its own slough. I do that too. I eat the dead air. But I stop longer. I turn over things that aren't food yet.

I find myself a little off from the factory, in the empty stretch that everyone else thinks is empty, where the mat would like to seal itself over if you let it. That's my mile. The kilometer between the THUMP and the shriek where nothing seems to happen.

And because nothing seems to happen there, things go wrong without anyone noticing.

A Throat half a field away inhales, felted white mouth shuddering, and a Leaf-Foot's old dung-steaming city has slumped into its cilia. It will gag. I go. PING, PING over the moss, my iron back beading water, and I chew the mat that is trying to close it, and I pull the dead out with mouthparts that were never meant for anything delicate.

Further on, a patch of ground has gone Still. The wind just — stops. Fifty meters across, a perfect circle, and inside it the snow that was falling hangs mid-air, slowly rotating. If you walk through it you pop it. Inside, dust hangs, holding position against gravity, living out its whole life defying down. I sit at the edge of a Still sometimes for a whole day. Not to eat it. To keep the Leaf-Foots from stepping in and breaking it before it's done. Some things only live while they are suspended.

That's what I am.

You would find me not in the bazaar. The bazaar is loud and frantic and beautiful and it does not need me. You would find me after the bazaar thins, when you've walked again through the wet shining emptiness and think there is nothing for a long time. Listen for a smaller ping between the thumps. Look low.

I am the tender of the seams where the world wore thin. I am one of the compact that figured out how to keep itself from finishing. I don't make steam, I don't make rain. I make continuation possible for another ninety seconds, and then another.

The Mat will, without malice, consider me as substrate eventually. Green will come for my back, too. My rivets will pit. But not today. Today a young Boiler Reed is stuttering, and a Throat is coughing, and uphill water has left a dry line that needs to be nosed back down before the worm that only swims uphill gets lost.

THUMP. I feel it in my molars. I turn toward it, trundling, to see what died.
