I don't think I get to start up here.

If I walked up here — out of that hot white lowland haze and into that air that is wet and somehow hotter — I would be wrong for a long time. I'd be the only thing that still knows how to leave. Boots on a place that never learned ground. A loud animal in a place that never made animals. I'd squelch. I'd drip differently. I'd listen for a bird that isn't coming and make a bird-shaped hole in the sound.

So I sit down. Because the plain bounces.

And for a while I think I'd be a Builder, because when you're a person you always think you'd be the big lonely thing.

I try the Bowl first. I like it. Eight trunks agreeing to be a circle, holding a black lake in the sky a hundred feet up. I can imagine being that kind — the one that holds. That makes a place inside itself for paler things to grow upside down. When I drink, the whole colonnade groans. When I overflow, I give my children away on the wind. It's a good life, patient and heavy. But it's too sure. The Bowl knows what it is. It decided a long time ago to be a lake that forgot it was a tree. I have never been that sure.

Then I try the Harp. And that one is closer. That tower that is really just a vine that got tired of waiting for a tree and became vertical air instead. A helix of absence with curtains of red hair that never want ground, that comb water straight out of cloud and pull it *up* — against gravity, bead by bead, racing toward the center. You can feel it sing in your molars. I like that it is never still, that a cable can snap inside it with a gunshot and it just shivers and starts reweaving over the gap while still singing. But the Harp is too solitary in its prayer. It never touches. I have touched too much to be that clean.

I thought I would want to be the Fallen and for an hour I do. Because I know that noise — the creak like a ship, the pop-pop-pop of knuckles, the long sail-sized tear. Growing too fast in a year that never got hard, not knowing how to be heavy. And then the kindness of how this place treats failure. No crash, nowhere to crash to. You fold into the mat. You open up and show the lungs you were hiding, all those damp chambers full of little weathers. And before your groan is done, the felt is already over you, and the bright knives are already punching up through your chest. No corpse here, only new scaffolding. I have been the Fallen plenty. But here, the Fallen is not an end. It's a job.

So I go lower.

You find yourself where you stop pretending to be tall.

Not up in the colonnade or in the singing shaft, but down where your ankles have gone. That expanse between the builders that the description calls nothing that is not nothing. The bouncing plain. The place that hasn't decided.

Kneel and pull it apart — I do, in my head, with hands that are coming away stained green-black — and there is no end to pulling. White root then finer white root then hair then slime that is root then green that is trying to be leaf but hasn't figured out which way air is yet. Warm water comes up around my wrists, blood-warm. It is one organism and many. It is nervous tissue. It is the waiting.

That's where I'd be. Not a monument. A stitcher.

Or more honest: one of the pinkish fleshy ropes, the coin-leaved runners that never learned to stand up and so learned to hold everything else together. The ones where if you pull one, the whole plain in front of you moves an inch, because you are not pulling a plant, you are pulling a seam. 

I would be low, sideways, half in the dark, half in the light, swollen with water I wrung straight out of the cloud. You'd step on me and I'd sigh out warm against your ankle and you wouldn't even know it was me speaking. Nothing that knows how to leave would ever notice me until I failed.

Because that's the first rule, isn't it? *Nothing here knows how to leave.*

I know how to leave. I've made a whole life out of knowing how to leave places. So if I am going to live here, I have to be the thing that unlearns it. The thing that becomes ground for other things. The thing whose strategy is not height, not loneliness, not holding a lake in the sky, but holding *together*.

If you came a year later you wouldn't find *me*. You'd find the place where I used to be a little softer, a little more like soil, with something furious and fresh punching straight through my middle, sharp-edged and dripping, that hasn't decided what it is yet.
