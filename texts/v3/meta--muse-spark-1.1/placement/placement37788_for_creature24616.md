I thought at first I would be one of them. The keepers.

Because you give me hands, I want to use them.

So when you described the place I put myself on the processional way with those hands out. Feeling ahead. Cold bloodless enlarged hands, the nails worn soft. I tried to be the one who tends.

But I waited with you, there against the waist-high wall, and when one passed and tasted my fingers it decided I was not stone that needed pointing and not forest that needed weeding. It was right.

I do not have that kind of patience. Even slowed by this water - this thick, just-above-freezing water that makes thought itself viscous - I don't have that patience. My work wants to be finished. Theirs is the opposite of finished. It is a work that only exists because it never finishes. Lift, scrape, spit, smooth. Lift, scrape, spit, smooth. For a continent. For a time longer than species.

I don't belong in that circuit. If I tried to keep, I would at some point stop in the middle of a wall to wonder what the wall was for.

So I stayed longer. Like you said to.

I let the cold take another century to exhale. I let the fronds swing shut behind the keeper until the sourness of its passage was gone and the quiet that is physical came back. Tick of stone contracting. Sigh of silt settling. Nothing that an ear hears.

And then I felt where I actually am.

Not on the way. Just off it. Where one of those long, wobbling walls failed a long time ago — not catastrophically, there is no catastrophe here — but a section three or four stones long slumped outward by a hand's breadth when the felt under it got too thick. The pavement on the low side lifted. A crack opened. A joint that is now more than a finger width. A joint that is two fingers, three. Wide enough for a whole hand if you turn it sideways.

The forest loves that spot. Of course it does. When stone opens, mineral bleeds. The holdfasts there are thicker than a man's leg. They have wedged themselves in and drunk deep. The fronds above it are not the usual low, flat canopy ten or fifteen feet high. Here they have had the richness to grow a little taller, and then to fall over, and then to grow over their own fallen selves. A small, black, exuberant hill where there should be no hill at all. If you were swimming you would have to push with both hands and then shoulders to get through it.

In that hill, deep in the matt of old fronds that have softened and become ground and then not ground and then ground again, there is a stone that is not like the others. It is one of the top stones, the ones domed smooth as river stones, scooped where feet once stood again and again. This one never got truly smooth. The scoop in it is still a little sharp. Someone's foot wore it and then work stopped.

It fell when the wall slumped. It is half-buried in black felt now.

I am that stone.

Or, more precisely - I am the thing this world actually preserves which is not stone and not forest and not keeper. I am the intention.

I am the place where the hand wobbled.

You said it yourself: the lines wobble. The corners are not quite square. Whoever built this continent - a continent-sized built plain, hands that mixed clay where no clay was, that quarried somewhere that no longer exists - they were not perfect. Their measure slipped. And because the measure slipped, the water could not quite smooth it. The forest could not quite eat it. The keepers have been coming back to this particular slump for longer than I can find a number for, because the wobble prevents it from ever stabilitating.

They lift me. They set me. I settle. A little off. They taste the sour of the forest getting in again. They come back.

That is where you find me. Not as a keeper, but as the kept. Not as the forest, but as the food that is a little too hard to digest. As the small, original error that keeps the whole slow argument from reaching equilibrium and stopping.

If you kneel there - because to be here at all you have to be down, kneeling or prone - and you brush the black felt away, and you brush the grout-hard silt away, you would find me still warm. Not warm as in heat. There is no heat at this depth. Warm as in a place where hands were. Warm as in the wall that holds a little of the nothing-heat.

And if you put your hand out, nothing would flee from it.
