At first I think I know where.

I would be you. The one with the boots. The sequential one, the warm one who still has to blink. Crouching in the middle of that overfull hand with my molars humming from the chord. I have always liked finding things that weren't meant to be found.

But I can't stay there. The room doesn't hate me — it doesn't have room for hate — but it corrects me. My sweat wants to bead and fix like glass. My breath wants to hang and not disperse. If I stay in the middle I will learn very quickly to tile. My heart is too loud. I have to leave.

So maybe not the middle. Maybe not the visitor.

I try again.

I listen to the chord until I can hear that it isn't one chord at all but ten thousand small rubs held at once, the way you can hold a note on an organ by taping the key down and walking away. And if I am anything in here, I am not the player. I am the tape.

I think I would be low, near the floor where your boot tried to make its mark and couldn't. I would be one of the Holdfast at first, because it is easiest. To be belly-to-belly, filament lit, tucked in. To finally have a job that is not wanting but being. No eyes. No mouth to explain. Just the wire on, white, without heat, without flicker. That sounds like peace, at first.

But then I count — or try to — and realize the Holdfast have solved loneliness by abolishing space. There is nowhere in the tiling to be *I*. If you are inlaid with no grout showing, you are not a self, you are the floor. I have been a floor before. I don't want that again.

I drift, in my mind, to the ceiling.

The Tappers. Those fat white root-cords that nosed through looking for depth and found this instead — an end. They did the polite thing and stopped an inch in. Made one perfect offering that never falls. I think for a long time I would be the fourth one. Or the fifth root that hasn't broken through yet, still in the peat, still dark, still reaching. Because that droplet — that impossible, swollen, about-to-burst-but-not-bursting bead — that is the only thing in the room that knows how to reflect. It holds the whole bright pocket inside it, bent, fish-eyed, and you, huge-eyed, looking up.

That droplet is almost right. It watches without occupying. It is full to bursting and it does not burst. That is a work I know how to do.

Only — it is alone up there. And it is waiting to fall. Even if falling will never come, waiting is still a sequence. The room's rule is zero. No before, no after. The droplet still has an after in its mind.

So lower, again. Not floor, not ceiling.

Between.

There is a place in your description you almost missed, because you said it with manners in mind. *Where the new rock is too sharp — those fresh, broken, baby-teeth edges of the blue-grey basalt — they leave a little margin, a dimple of empty air, because even pressure has manners.*

I find myself there.

In the only emptiness left. Not a gap — gaps get filled — but a courtesy. A small, deliberate, negotiated absence where the stone is too new to be touched. The Holdfast will not press there. The Gloss will not even bead there. It is the one co-ordinate that was left unoccupied on purpose.

And what would I be, in that dimple? Not a Holdfast. Not a Tapper.

I would be what makes that courtesy possible.

I would be a little heavier and thinner than the rest of the Gloss. That tired mineral green that isn't green until you look away. The agreement itself. The thing that says: *here we will not tile. Here the rock gets to remain sharp for a little while longer.*

Or — even smaller, even more itself —

I would be one of the domes.

You said light doesn't go in there. Sound maybe doesn't either. In a room that has abolished privacy, the dome is the only private place. Not empty, not occupied, but in-use, and its use is *being a dome.* Being the one shape that chose to be a shape.

If you crouch and look into the doorway that always faces inward, you see dark that should not exist in a photon-packed room. That's where I'd be. Inside the thimble of glued quartz and old skins, in the perfect dark that the ten thousand pilot lights cannot reach.

Not building. Not holding. Not listening. Just holding the shape of a room inside the Room. The still point that lets all the other stillness make sense.

You came down and had to pull yourself back up toward wind and erosion and finishing. I would stay down there in the dark that is not dark because it is absent, but because it refused to let light in.

You would look in on your way out, and you would not see me. Which is the point.
