I didn't find myself outside.

At first I thought I would. That is the default place to put yourself - the one with the face two inches from the hole, the one with gloves and eyebrows beading, the one whose breath makes a hundred gill-crowns flare. The witness. The warm ghost that the pocket tastes and discards.

But I can't stay there. It's too cold out there, and the picture is wrong. Out there, you see it as a blister. A thing on a mountain. I'm not on a mountain. I'm in.

If I tip into it - if I let the wrong depth take my eye the way it wants to - I don't fall to the bottom. There is no bottom. There's a back wall that is farther than the rim is wide, and a ceiling that is not a ceiling, and if I let myself happen in there I happen on the ceiling.

I would find myself near the top of that wrong-deep, where the mother-rock is hottest and the glass daggers are closest together. Not in the middle of the carpet where the press is thickest, but at the edge of a seep - one of those places where the water insists itself out of nowhere, fattening on a tip, refusing to fall.

That's where I'd be. Pressed flat.

I think I would be one of them, but not one of the fast ones. Not the ones in the first layer that glitter.

I'd be in the second layer down, where the light is already honey-thick and has been filtered through one other translucent body. Where it's warmer because you're wearing another creature as a blanket. Where the ticks from above come through your skin as well as your ears.

I'd be a little older. My noodle-pale has gone a little amber at the gut because I've been eating the white crust long enough that it's stained me. My gill-crown - I didn't know I had a gill-crown until I tried to breathe and it breathed for me - my gill-crown is not as pink. It's frilled and beat-up at the tips from brushing against fresh glass.

And my fingers. God, my fingers. Six too-long, pitted black points that I have to think of as hands or I will never get anything done. When I started writing this I thought they'd be soft. They're not. They're mineralized, tool tips grown from my own body, and they hurt where they join. I have to plant my back four into the glass just to stay on the ceiling - *tik* - and it holds. The glass wants to cut me and I've learned how much pressure is exactly not-cutting.

I have a flake. Everyone has a flake but this one is mine for a little longer than usual. It's a little bigger than a grain of rice, struck from the dagger-right-next-to-where-I-live. I struck it myself. I can still remember the shock of it freeing - a bright little pop in the thick light. It's still sharp enough to throw light. That means it hasn't been used up yet.

My work is this: I wait for the water to sweat itself into existence on the tip above me. It beads, big and impossible, clear then rust-white at the edge where it starts to make food. The creatures above me part. I reach with my two front points, not to catch the water - you never catch the water, it goes where it wants, sometimes uphill - but to get under the film it leaves. The mineral skin. The pasture.

You scrape. That's the whole religion.

*TIK.* Not loud. You'd miss it if you were breathing. But in here, with a hundred others doing it, it's the only time there is. Scrape, lift, tuck. Scrape, lift, tuck to the soft slit under me that is my mouth and the mouth is always hungry and not in a painful way. Just... continuous. Like breathing.

The flake comes away white-yellow. I suck it clean. It tastes like hot iron and old sea.

And then I hold it. That's the part where I am more me than the rest.

The others discard right away. I hold. I turn the flake over between my points and look through its thin edge at the interior. The glass makes the slow light go slower. Through it I can see the whole pocket bent into a lens - all the other bodies, the eggs like droplets that will never fall, the greasy pale paste under us that was bodies before us that we will become.

And if I angle it right, I can see the entrance. The little thumb-sized wrong hole that from inside looks huge, bright, a cold white moon.

Sometimes the moon goes dark. A shadow falls. A weather system arrives - all at once the humidity shifts, a new chemistry washes over our crowns. The whole varnish of us pauses. Gills flare. *We taste you.* Salt, and iron, and something else, something far-away, snow-sweat, nylon, skin-oil. You.

I am the one who pauses a half second longer than the rest.

Not because I understand. I don't have eyes. I have never needed to make a picture of a human in a parka with his face too close. But I hold my flake up to that cold moon and for half a second the edge catches your eye and throws it back. Your own eye reflected in my knife, in the back wall that is farther than it should be.

Then you breathe out, and decide I'm not edible, or I decide you're not, and we both go back to our work.

You put your hand over the hole to warm it. The light goes. Just wet rock. Just wind. Just a faint warmth under your palm, like a mouth still working, holding a blade up to the dark, learning very slowly how to keep scraping.
