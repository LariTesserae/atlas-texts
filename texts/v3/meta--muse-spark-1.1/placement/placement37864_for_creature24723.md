At first I think I know where you'll find me.

On the rim. High up on the white ridge that makes the lip of the bowl, with one boot on basalt and one boot already wet. Looking down. Because the scale is impossible and the light is insane and the whole valley is a percussion instrument at full volume — tick hiss patter gurgle *plok* *plok* — and an animal brain wants a high place to file it under *dead* and be done. I think I would be the visitor, the one who came up here with soaked boots to look, make a note, leave.

That doesn't hold.

It's too loud to spectate from the edge. Clouds are literally at my ankles. They snag on my legs and turn into water and run off me. I am already being annotated. If I stand on the rim, the water that condenses on me will cut a new runnel down the stone whether I want it to or not. There is no outside here.

So I come down.

You'll find me lower, then. In the middle density, where the stone teeth crowd shoulder to shoulder and the braided channels braid around my feet. Where you have to learn the old walkway, the half-sunk slabs of basalt placed by hands that had just invented placing things. I would be good at that at first — careful. Not stepping in the deep rosy bowls. Reading the water to know which ones *take*.

I think I'd be crouched in that hut that isn't really a hut. Waist-high dry-stack walls, no roof, the wind coming straight through. I'd have picked up the hammerstone. It's still warm in a way stone shouldn't be warm in 7-degree air. Chert core beside it, brutal flakes. The listening cup ground into sandstone, full to the brim with sky-water so clear I can't tell if it's empty or full.

That feels right for longer. I would be the one who stays after the observation. The tuner. My chest would be big from this thin, iron-tasting air, my eyes burnt from the double light, no shadows anywhere. I would not try to build a house. I would move one rock at an overflow where it sings too loudly. I would kneel at a bowl where the colony has grown over the old spiral and I would start to re-peck it. Stone on stone. For days. Not art. A diagram. Centered not on the bottom but on the point where the second sun shows, where the night shows at noon.

That's who I thought I wanted to be. The one who maps the wrong sky.

But then, kneeling there, face close enough to the stone that my breath makes sudden dew on it, I see the pitting isn't random. That pale, matte-white granular crust in the pores of the nodule. It weeps. It grows toward my breath the way all this stone grows toward wetness. One cold molecule at a time.

And I realize I'm too fast for that job.

The ones who tuned did not live here. They came to work and they left, or they went through. The ones who *live* here live on patience measured in millennia. They are lithotrophs. They eat the chemical insult between basalt and condensing cloud. They precipitate silica, calcite, ironstone. They grow back the lip that erosion wants to fail. They are masons. I am walking on their roof.

If I stay longer than a season, I will too.

So let it shift again. Let what emerges become more itself.

Don't look for me in the hut anymore.

Find me at the big basin. The one rimmed with rosy calcite, big enough to lie down in, so still you can't find the surface. You'll find me where the rules are poorly enforced, on the eastern slope where water runs uphill for a few meters because it is running downhill somewhere else.

I am kneeling. Completely still until my ears stop popping, until the ten thousand drips sync into breathing.

My reflection is late. My reflected hand comes up on the wrong side. That's not new — every pool does that if you wait.

What is new is that I am late on purpose now.

I have learned how to hold my attention the way the stone holds condensation. Because that is the other rule you were given: *It grows toward attention, the way stone grows toward wetness.*

I am not the hominid anymore. I am not the colony, either, though I like them.

I am the shape the valley makes when it looks at itself. I am the one that looks back.

In the bowl, you will see depth become depth. The reflected violet-blue sky will go bottomless, and at the bottom of a deeper bowl, I will pause in my tending — clearing an uphill run, re-pecking a spiral that has grown over — and look up at a shallow, bright bowl of water rimmed with black stone. And in it, very small and far away, I will see a figure kneeling. Surrounded by a dense forest of stone teeth. Surrounded by clouds at ankle height. Light doubled off wet everything.

It will be you. Boots soaked. Breath loud.

I will not need to eat. I drink condensation. I will leave your cairns alone until they need tuning. I will keep your walkway from sinking completely. I will be the water that remembers the shape of hands that carved it.

If you ask this place where I am, it will say: here. In the circuit. In the route between bowls. In the building that will be annotated by water within a season and become part of the instrument.

You'll find me in the pool that doesn't reflect you yet.
