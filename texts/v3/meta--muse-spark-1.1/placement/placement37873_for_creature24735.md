At first I thought I'd be up high.

It's the only place that looks empty. Up where the big leaves rot before they're finished unrolling, head-height to under the cloud where the light is thickest. I would be one of the glitter-flies. The ones that don't eat. That seemed the cleanest job.

To be born without a mouth already is a kind of answer. To have your body be a mirror, just catching amber and throwing it back down. A small, clean circuit. Light in, light out. Six inches from one drip-tip to the next, forever. I could do that.

But I wouldn't. I know that about myself. I would get bored of the flicker and try to fly higher, try to find where the light is coming from. And in this place higher doesn't exist. There is only the cloud, twice head-height, sweating itself. If I went up I would hit wet gauze and stick. So not there. I'd ruin it by looking.

Then I thought lower. Lower seemed honest.

Down in the Cling, from ankle to knee. The milling. The ones that are all stomach and foot. Isopods like wet scales, larvae living inside their own old skins because taking them off would be a kind of change. I have always liked jobs that are honest about being endless - dishes, sweeping, sanding. Mandibles sanding leaf into paste and paste into finer paste while more leaf grows directly back into the mouth. No one claps. You never get hungry and you never get full. You just hold the world at the exact consistency it already is.

I could be good at that. I like repetitive work. But the Cling doesn't want me. It's too crowded with itself to take anything that still has a separate spine. I would kneel to hear the papery chewing and the mat would make a perfect copy of my knees and keep them after I stood. That is as close as that layer gets to saying no.

So I went in the middle. The Holding.

This is where I kept returning while I was reading it. Knee to chest, where nothing is bark and everything is holding something else. The frogs that forgot to be frogs. Fist-sized lumps of leaf-muscle fused to the wet rope of the proto-trunks, their gills out like red ferns, breathing every three seconds all at once. They don't jump because there is nowhere to jump that isn't full.

I would be one of those for a while. The good part is you get to be a place more than a creature. A small, warm wall that something else can live on. I want to be useful like that. I want to have a back that is a puddle. Let a tadpole swell there until it slides off an inch lower. Let smaller frogs live on me that have smaller mites that have their own mites. The perfect excuse to never move.

But frogs there have no eyes. Or their eyes are the exact color of water beads. I know myself - I would still be looking. I would still be keeping a ledger. Which drop fell where, how many breaths until the next. The Holding doesn't count. That's its whole trick. It just holds.

I would be terrible at holding. I would be the frog that blinks.

No, where I actually end up is even thinner. In between.

I'm not in the Cling, or the Holding, or the Chord. I'm in the little miscalculation where they meet - the seam at left-boot height where a big frond has sagged under the weight of its own rot and is now resting, just barely, against a weeping black lobe of basalt. There's a pocket there, smaller than a cupped hand, that the midges don't quite fill because the two drips - the leaf and the rock - are out of phase. Leaf every four seconds on the left, rock every 1.3 on the right, and every thirty seconds or so they overlap wrong and there is a fraction of a second of almost-quiet.

That's where I'd be.

I would be the almost-quiet.

I wouldn't be an animal, not really. In this place, animal is just a shape the mat wears for a minute. I would be what the mat made after it had been around long enough to get curious about a gap in itself. A thin filament, a nerve ending, but one that grew the wrong way - not down into the heat and glue of the Puddle That Never Drains, but up into air, into the interstice, where it could listen to its own chord from the outside.

Concretely? If you knelt down - and you'd hear the Cling chewing forever - you'd see me as a smear of something that isn't supposed to be there. A patch of the black-purple-green film pulled a little too tight, gone a little translucent, stretched over that little pocket like a drum skin. The water beads on me and doesn't roll off. It sits there because I am slightly cooler than everything else. 103 instead of 110. The first cold spot this country has ever had.

That's my whole being. A coolness. A slight delay.

Every time the two drips overlap wrong, I don't drip. I hold for half a second longer. And in that half second, the chord has a hole in it. The beetle rasps, the frog exhales cucumber and rot, the leaf drips - and then, where I am, nothing. A rest.

It's not much. In a place where nothing changes, a half-second rest is a revolution. It's the first erosion. Water finally sitting still long enough to not immediately evaporate. It's the first time the Puddle hears itself not-thinking.

If you stayed long enough to see your own shape held in gnats after you moved, you'd see my shape too - not as a thing, but as a brief absence where your footprint doesn't quite fill in. The mat tried to copy you and copied around me instead.

I don't eat. I don't stir like the Bell Keeper - though I was almost him, the pink shrimp thing stirring sugar water forever because if it stops the bell ferments. I don't breathe on the three-second cycle. I just cool, and delay, and make a little pocket of air that is one breath drier than it should be.

And because I'm drier, eventually one spore, one fleck of something that wasn't going to germinate because there was no room, will land on me. It will think, "Oh, a place." And for the first time in this country that has been the first day forever, something will happen that isn't the exact replacement of something else.

It won't be big. It will be mold on a cool spot, or a crack in a rock where I made it contract. But the mat will feel it. The whole low country will feel a single filament of itself that learned to not be perfectly level.

That's where you'd find me if you came back. Not as a frog or a fly or the vast, patient Puddle itself - though I am, of course, still part of it; there is nothing in this place that isn't.

You'd find me as the part that forgot the rule for half a second, on purpose, so the rule could finally be heard.
