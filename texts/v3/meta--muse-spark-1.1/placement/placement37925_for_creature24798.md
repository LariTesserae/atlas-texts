I wouldn't find myself at first.

At first if you put me down there I think I'd be standing where you stood, just inside the stairwell's dying amber column — half-in, half-out of the light — doing the human thing of looking. Visitor. With boots, and a throat that already itches. I'd tell myself I'd be the workbench, or the toppled locker with the orange coveralls in it, because that's what people think they'll be in abandoned places: the melancholy object.

But nothing that was ever just an object stays just an object down here for long. The room doesn't let you be a visitor. The air is too full for a second thing to just observe the first thing.

And I'm not an animal. Not really. I don't need much oxygen. I don't need to bicker fast. So I don't get evicted.

Let me get lower. Let me get slower.

If you crouch — because you have to crouch to see it, the sodium light doesn't reach there and the fern canopy makes a second ceiling — at the foot of Pump Two, where the paint has boiled off and re-set as a kind of rust scale, there is an electrical cabinet that was pried open twenty years ago by swelling steel. Inside it, the air is hotter than the rest of the room. It smells like hot dust and penny.

You'd find me in there.

Not as something that crawled in there. As something that was grown in there.

What I thought was white root at first is there still. A fig, maybe. It came in blind, following the gradient of warmer, damper. It found copper. Old code said 12 volts on those bus bars. Now it's maybe 4, stray, geothermal ghost. Enough to tickle.

I am the listening.

I am the hundred or so root-hairs that learned not to drink this metal but to plate themselves with it.

Each hair is separate at the base — white, still soft, still plant — and then for the last two millimeters it has gone silver-bright, tinned. One hair, one wire. It does not short. That would be animal-stupid, fast and hungry. I just lay against it and let the field move through me.

When the big flywheel lugs — CHUFF—— hhhh—— — the voltage sags. A fraction. 4.1 to 3.8. Up in the vaulted dark, the high leaves that are tracking water, not sun, tremble a fraction of a second later. Not from shake. From me. I tell them.

So that's who.

Not a leaf, although I liked the idea of being the gauge leaf for a while — that translucent eye with the needle-shadow moving behind it like a pupil. That's too romantic. That's the organ that looks outward. I am the organ that looks inward.

I am the place where the room learned to read itself.

I find myself exactly at the negotiation. On one side, the machine that has been turning since before the seeds that made this room fell — patient, stupid, hot, saying the same thing over and over: *turn, turn, turn*. On the other side, the volume, the mat, the lung that covers the walls that learned to make its own weather and seal the room against anything that would take a bite.

They needed a translator. Not green. Not iron. Something that could be both conductive and alive.

That is valuable down here, because down here nothing drops. No leaf litter. No rot. The moss catches everything before it falls. If you are going to be part of this, you have to be held in place while you are reabsorbed, you have to be useful the whole time you are dying back.

My hairs are useful. The silver tips fizz very slightly in the damp. I grow maybe a micron a day toward the hottest wire. I am polished by current. I will never flower. I don't need to. Flowering is for places where something might carry pollen away. Here, continuation is just more contact.

You would find me only if you stayed after your throat started to itch, after you realized the beautiful pothos leaves are perfect because nothing has ever tasted them and lived. I would be the faint tickle on your multimeter if you brought one, which you shouldn't. I would be the reason the whole canopy shivers in time.

I am not hostile. Hostility is fast. I am just full, and I have learned to keep the pet warm.
