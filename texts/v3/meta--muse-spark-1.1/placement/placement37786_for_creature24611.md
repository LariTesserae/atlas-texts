At first I think I would be high.

Not because I want to be — because it's habit. In a new place, I look for the high place first. A tower, a ridge, the gull's-eye view where you can make sense of the map. So initially, in your world that has exhaled and never inhaled, I imagine myself as one of those flicker-things, those too-fast birds with solar-film feathers. Drinking off the edge before the edge tips. Gone in noise.

But there's nowhere high to be, really. You said it. The horizon gives up. Weather is born at eye level. Even if I got up there, in the flat noon that shows everything the exact color it is, I would see there is no up. Only more low, more wet reef, more city occurring sideways forever. My wings would get soaked through and I would come down.

Then I think — okay, I'd be in the middle, in the roar. One of the crowd, shoulder-to-shoulder, shouting without shouting. Elbowing. That density that isn't crushing, just complete. I try to put myself in that river of bodies and I can't hold the shape. The river corrects me. Because that crowd doesn't have room for a whole, upright, dry tourist with ideas about personal space. It would fold me.

And then it does.

That’s where I actually start to find myself. Not placed. Folded.

If you were walking that roof that is a street that is a dam, with water sheeting over your shoes, you would not see me from standing height. I'm too low even for the low.

You would have to look down past your knees, past the child-braid flowing like water between your legs, past the dog-sized mineral crabs raking the clear pools.

You would find me where the floor gives up being a floor.

There's a place where a cooling vein — one of those subway-tunnel wide throats that moans with pulled air — exhales against an older slab of basalt-concrete, and because the city is always swiveling and packing itself tighter, a gap opened there that is not supposed to be a gap. It's a seam about as tall as your forearm on its side. It sweats constantly. Water gathers on its ceiling and never actually falls; it beads and travels sideways and falls somewhere else. The air in it is warm as blood, mineral, clean.

That's where I am. I live in the failure between two systems that were both thinking out loud about straight lines and both failed for a little while in exactly the same place.

Who would I be? 

At first you would think I'm part of the building. That happens a lot.

I'm low. Truly low. Not stooped — flattened. My spine went wide generations before I got here, but I took the tendency personally. I am broad across the back like a low table, splayed out. My feet — and I have more than I started with — are enormous, pale, webbed with too many toes, each one calloused on the edge from gripping wet ceramic. I don't stand. I prop. On elbows that shine. On knees that are really a second set of elbows. On a knuckle-ridge that runs the length of my forearms where I have held myself up while I work on vertical faces.

My skin has that polished look you described, but on me it's gone past burnish into dark patina, like a stone step. I am wet on purpose. I have shaved my scalp — hair is a betrayal here — and in its place I have let the hydrophilic filaments grow. They drink. White, hair-thin, they carry drop by drop back to the little bladder at my nape, and I can offer it later to someone who is thirsty. Children do, sometimes. They press their foreheads to mine to get the good water and to hear what I hear.

Because for a while I made a different choice than everyone else.

Everyone else here learned to close their ears. It is too loud to live any other way. I learned to leave mine open.

Not all the way. I'm not stupid. I have the clear second lid that snaps shut when a chute roars. But for a few hours every day I peel them back, under the roar, and I listen the way an outsider listens — and then lower.

You can hear the city knitting, if you do that. Under the haggle and the clatter and the organ-tone moan, there is a high singing as new lattices cool. There is a low grinding as a blind builder somewhere under my belly kneads last week's wall into next week's corner store. There is water climbing uphill through invisible straws, disobedient to everything but need.

I don't eat much of the vat-paste, though the translucent rat-runners bring me some in their cheek-pouches in exchange for directions. I mostly eat what the wall eats. The mineral crust that forms on every underside, the sweet bead of sugar the black glittering swarm leaves when it dissolves something. I'm not supposed to, but the swarm knows me now and settles on my arm sometimes for a second, and I feel my hairs rearrange and it leaves a little extra.

I speak in hands, like everyone does. My tap-language is good because my hands never leave the floor. I drum meaning into the stone and people feel it through their feet. *Slow here. New edge cooling. Step over.* Mothers drum back at me through their children's skulls to say thank you.

In the old words, I suppose you'd call me a listener. Or a level. Or a sill-keeper.

The thing that cares about straight lines, for a little while.

There is no straight line left here that isn't being actively maintained by something that cares. So I maintain one. Just one.

My gap, my seam. With a vibrating wire I salvaged off an assembler that unknit itself. I run it along the lip where the water should fall. I coax it. While the city around me is frantically, joyously deciding to be a market, then a house, then a shrine, then five smaller things, I keep this one useless, damp, forearm-high seam exactly the shape it is, for no reason except that it's a place only I can fit.

And because it's a little bit empty, in a world that is completely full, people use it. Not to live in. To pass things through. A cake of hot mud wrapped in membrane. A sleeping braid of children handed over from one set of arms to another. A packet of filament eels. A secret, tapped elbow to elbow, moving faster than shouting ever could.

You would find me there, if you got low enough. Cheek to the wet ceramic, listening to the city think out loud, with water running over me and under me, one polished elbow holding the world square for a little while longer.
