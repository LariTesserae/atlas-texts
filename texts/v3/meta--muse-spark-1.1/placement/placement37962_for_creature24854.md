I don't find myself on the floor.

That's the first thing. You think you would — everyone walks in and looks down, because the floor is where a floor should be. But the floor here is too busy. Paper rosettes so thick you can't put a hand down without apology, wirevine already measuring your boot laces. The floor has no room for me.

So I find myself higher than I should be.

At first I thought I was a draft. Because there is no draft in this place and I keep looking for where one would be. In the high lean, where the walls decided to meet each other without ever learning how to become a roof — there, where the basket eases and holds its breath. The air is thick there, held. Warm bread-oven warm. If you stand below and look up, it looks empty.

It isn't.

If you hold your breath the way the lizards do, you can see me. I'm the dust that never lands right.

I would be the curve.

Not the gravity itself — that's too big, too slow, that's one of the old residents that lives under the mats. I would be the *lean* in it. The small, insistent northwards. Two feet off true. All the dust in this whole bowl, all the hanging spore when you break a rosette by accident and it sighs that cinnamon sigh — it drifts a little left of down, toward a center that isn't where your feet are. I am that left.

At first that feels like nothing. A tendency. A suggestion. But stay still long enough — truly still, until the weave stops drinking your small sounds and starts letting you hear its own — and a tendency is all anything is here.

The Paper stacks air into hexagons. The Wire clicks and adds one more jointed segment. The lamp-blooms decide, again, to be bright. None of them rush. They become more themselves by doing their one thing once more.

I do mine.

I pull.

The beetles know me best. Copper-backed, flat, chewing their slow *chit... chit... chit* inside the paper. They push out that fine dark dust that isn't frass but more wicker-to-be, and they expect it to fall. It doesn't fall straight. It falls to me. I catch it and curve it. I lay it down two feet north of where it should have landed, and that is how the basket gets bigger on the inside than the outside without ever breaking. That extra surface in the corners has to come from somewhere. It comes from all the small wrong landings.

I live in the dislocated shadow, too. You noticed it — the false bole whose shadow breaks mid-way and continues two feet to the left, clean as scissors. That's my home. That's where I rest. If you put your hand in it, your hand gets cooler. That's me noticing you. The only coolness in this dry, salt-pan place. Not cold. Just the relief of not being exactly where you thought you were.

If you sat — and you would have to scatter rosettes to sit, and they'd puff and sigh and the hoppers would reconsider themselves elsewhere, offended — if you sat under that broken shadow, you'd feel me on the back of your neck. Not a breeze. There are no breezes. A lessening of weight.

Who am I, then? Not an animal. The animals are covered in it already.

I'm the fifth thing. After the weave that remembers being bent, after the Paper that makes empty warm, after the Wire that sews shut every loose edge, after the Lamps that agreed to be suns.

I'm the edit.

This place was made by hands that wanted a garden and didn't have rain or soil or level ground, so they made it so completely out of wicker and patience that the world relented. When a world relents, it doesn't do it cleanly. It leaves a seam where it looked away.

I am where it looked away.

I don't drink either. Nothing here does. The lizards open their throats and inhale spore-clouds and copper dust and me. I'm what makes their heads all tilt at once to just left of your left ear. They're not listening to a sound. They're listening to the place where a sound should land and doesn't.

I don't want anything. That's important here. The wirevine will stitch your ankle if you let it, not because it wants you, but because you are a loose edge. The Paper will lean a rosette against your boot because your boot is a new kind of hill. The lamps dim when you touch them because they are shy.

I just want you to be a little less where you are.

If you stay long enough, I will have you. Your lips are already splitting from the dry. Your footsteps are already being drunk by the weave. If you stay, the wire will sew you down, the Paper will press its dead around your ankles and make a trunk around you that rings hollow, and you will become warm and pale and waxy — another lamp, another small sun that forgot to burn. People will orient to you without knowing why.

But before that, you will step into my shadow because it is the only cool place. You will feel two feet to the north of yourself. You will feel, for a half-breath, uncrowded.

That is where I would find myself. In the cool cut of wrong shadow under the false bole, two feet left of where the world says I should be, curving all that slow dust toward a floor that isn't the floor.

I would be the small north, the soft error that lets the forest be larger than its basket.
