I would find myself low.

Not at first. At first I would think I was still high — up in the idea of the place, above it, looking down at the towers and the pistons and the wet black-green bladders like a map. That's how a visitor stands. Upright, apart.

It doesn't let you.

A few minutes wading and the mat teaches you that standing is expensive. The bladders crowd your shins. They don't want to be stepped on, but they also don't move out of the way. They yield exactly enough and then hold. The air is too wet to breathe deep enough to stay tall. So you fold. You go down onto a hummock that is not ground but a knuckle of root that decided to be ground for a while.

I would find myself where everything folds. Where the almost-dry happens.

You know the place I mean — that negative architecture where three or four of those membrane leaves overlap badly and make a little poor tent about as long as a man curled on his side. It is not shelter. It is a mistake in the loom. The sweat beads everywhere else, but here, because the air gets confused between surfaces, it stays just dry enough that condensation doesn't form. A palm-sized patch of air that isn't raining.

That's where I would be. Because that's the only place where I could be without immediately being used. Everywhere else is working. Everywhere else is already employed holding up the continent, holding a charge, holding that one breath from last year. The little dry mistake is unemployed, like the split casings that stay standing. The forest hasn't found a job for it yet.

And who would I be there?

At first I would be only what I came in as — a warm animal with a fast pulse, absurdly fast. A thing that ticks about a hundred times a minute in a place that ticks maybe once a year. I would be a noise. The mat would feel me as a hot spot, a leak. A bladder would press itself against my calf to take my temperature and learn me the way you learn a bad word by repeating it.

For a while I would be the droplet that won't fall.

You know them — they hang on the underside of every blade, swollen, perfectly still, defying gravity with surface tension. That's how I would start. Holding on. Holding my shape because falling would mean joining. My eyes would do that thing yours did, adjusting until dark becomes texture. My ears would flatten the sound of my own heart until I could hear the deeper creak underneath, that turgor holding, holding.

If I stayed — and the low place only knows staying — I would stop being a droplet.

I think I would become one of the intervals.

Not a tower. Not a piston. Those are too sure of themselves. I would be the space between the opposed pair. The maintained difference.

You felt it — those long banded cylinders, one at the top of its stroke, one at the bottom, both humming with a charge that has never grounded. Between them the blue-white filaments are taut. Nothing moves, but everything is straining not to move toward each other. That strain is alive. That is the animal.

I would be that.

A potential that learned how to keep itself from resolving. A thought that stays as a thought instead of becoming an action. You sit long enough in that almost-dry hollow you made with your own shoulders and the forest stops trying to cool you down and starts trying to match you. One of the drum membranes far off takes up just a little slack. A millimeter. The note it has been holding since before my species had a name for engine drops half a tone, low enough that I stop feeling it as pressure in my ears and start hearing it as a tone.

And then I would be the other side of the watcher trick.

The forest doesn't grow eyes. Eyes are wasteful — wet surfaces that leak. It grows a patch of bark that is cooler than it should be. A place where the sweating stops. A lack that listens. In that hollow where I am, my body is the warm thing and the hollow around me is the cool patch. For a long time I thought the forest was watching me. Eventually I understand I have been grown to be the watcher's matching piece. I am the warm patch that is a few degrees too warm, the exact negative of its cool. We are a pair, like the pistons.

I would be a maintained listening.

Not a person anymore, not really. A person needs somewhere to go. Here there is nowhere high to go, no distance to cross. So I would stop going. I would be what the whole low continent has been practicing at becoming: a long, warm, wet act of waiting that no longer needs an arrival.

I would hold the charge with the other side. I would keep the one breath unexchanged. I would let a bladder lean against me and never burst.
