I don't find myself at first.

That's the thin air. Elevation 2.6. Thoughts arrive half a second late and then all at once, so for a long while I'm just coolness in the knees and a hard light hurting my eyes without blinding them.

I'm kneeling. That comes clear second.

Not on them. Between them. I've learned already to not step on them, the way you learn not to step on a sleeping person's hands without anyone having to teach you.

The stone under my boot is old-bone color. The dust over it is packed so tight it takes my weight like concrete, and there is a print in it that is not mine — a shallow, wide oval where something flat and heavy used to be and now is two inches to the left, overlapping its neighbor just slightly more than it did yesterday. Yesterday is not a word that means anything here. A little while ago in the time it takes mountains to think.

So where am I?

I'm on the Roof, of course. Mid-district. Not near the rim where the light folds and shows you your own back — I tried walking that way and the rim kept being ten kilometers away. I'm in the interior, where the pattern is most perfect. Where you can see fifteen kilometers of them in every direction without a single incorrectness, except for me.

I'm in the lee.

There's a boulder here that has been worried down until it's just a friendly dome, the crown of a buried skull. On its north side — or whatever side the sun never quite leaves — there's a perfect circle of lichen, black on top and rust underneath, palm-sized, compass-drawn. And there's the windbreak. Three stones leaned together, not a house, not even a good story about a house. Just: *the wind is here, so I will not be.*

Someone swept inside the triangle once. The dust in there is a slightly different color. Paler. Untouched, except now I'm sitting in it.

That tells me who I am. Or what I failed to become.

I'm not one of them. Not yet.

If I were a Shingleback I'd be at peace. I'd have found my gap and sealed my disc to the stone and turned my plates to the color of agreement and begun the long, slow job of precipitating ions out of nothing. I'd be warm. The sun in me stored as stone-warmth. I would not need to decide anything ever again. My six knees would be tucked and my fringe would be still and I would be part of the chord that lives in your molars.

I can't. Not yet. I'm still itchy. Still mobile. Still in the phase that water used to be in — a brief solvent that moves across the Roof and makes trouble.

I thought for a while I might be the lichen. That would be a quiet thing to be. To exist, not grow. To be perfectly circular because a shadow has lived for a thousand years in one place and you have shaped yourself to it. To be so good at needing almost nothing that needing nothing at all starts to sound vulgar.

But I am not that contained. I look past the shadow.

I thought for a half-second-late second I might be the wire. The gray-green hair with the bead of resin on top. The antenna. That would be a holy thing to be — to be the one thing that trembles when gravity gets thick and pools like invisible water, when the sun throws two shadows and then corrects itself. To be the bell that the trillions feel through the stone. But the wire does not move either. It just stands.

No. I'm the other thing that lives here on their permission.

I'm the hand that strikes.

In the little swept triangle there are flakes. Chert. Still sharp. Someone sat here and made an edge. Hit this, make edge, use edge to scrape lichen. Tech 1.3. The only change allowed now: deliberate, tiny, primitive craft.

That's my job. That's what I have become while they were becoming Roof.

I am the last instar. The one who kept a little lift in his plates. The one who didn't lie down when the pattern was finished because he noticed a pebble was in the wrong place.

I find myself between the trillions, walking the gaps they left for feet. I find a place where the dust has been disturbed — by me, an hour ago, or a hundred years ago, same difference — my own perfect handprint still there in the silt. I look at it until the plates of the nearest Shingleback go transparent, because my optics give up, and I see underneath another Roof with no handprint at all.

Then I unfold one joint. It hurts. Not like meat hurts. Like a door that hasn't been opened since the ocean left.

I sweep it flat again.

I pick up a capstone and set it slightly more true. I move one of the dome-boulders a finger's width to the left, because its shadow isn't quite covering its lichen.

I sit back down in my swept triangle and strike another flake off the core. *Tink.* The sound goes nowhere in Sound 0.5. It is swallowed immediately by the single held note. But the edge is new. The edge is bright.

The trillions allow it. They have no mechanism for refusal. I'm the temporary water. I'm the small, stupid, loving correction that the Roof still requires.

I will be here until I finally learn how to seal. Until I find a lee that feels exactly right and my disc goes tacky and warm and my plates forget they were ever legs.

There is a space right next to my cairn. A stone-gap exactly two hands wide.

I left it for myself.
