I'd think, at first, that I'd be you.

On the rim. Knee-high lip of blunt knives, boots on patina'd basalt, looking down into the pan. Human-shaped, thirsty, too warm, trying to be careful not to step on anything. That's the first place I find myself because it's the only place a visitor can stand and still pretend to be separate.

But I wouldn't stay there.

The air down there is ten degrees hotter and it doesn't move. And the rim is the place of the dead filters - the ones who locked their legs and bleached out and spend centuries sieved open to the wind. They have their backs to everything. They face outward, into the unending violet dark, waiting for a midge that froze three mountain ranges away to fall onto them. That's not where I would end up. I have never been good at waiting for the world to bring me something.

I thought then I'd be one of the fast ones in the middle. The too-hot, restless ones near the cooking stone, where you can't keep your palm flat. Because I run hot. Because I like the center of things. But no — those ones don't stay either. They are all urgency and climbing, wax almost smoking, water threatening to boil out of their joints. They push because they have to. They have no time to listen to the tick of the plates settling. That's not me.

So I slide down. Just a little, in my mind. Off the rim, down the shallow inward slope, to where the pan is neither rim nor oven. Mid-slope. Where cold-hungry and hot-hungry meet and exchange places.

If you stand in the pan long enough, you said, some from the middle will climb onto your feet. Not to attack. To be warm in a different way.

I would be there, in that negotiation.

And I think I would not be a boot. I would not be the human who carries sweat that never beads.

I would be one of the scales.

Not a queen. The queens — the ovens — are beautiful, but they are rooted. Their pads have grown into rock and into each other, wax turned to cement. They glow amber because they have to. They hold dozens of glassy spheres inside themselves for years at 70 degrees. I hold things too, but not like that. Not that still, not that patient, not that hot.

I would be a live one, mid-size, dome black but worn thin at the edges so that when the heat shines through it, it goes translucent. One of the ones with the stridulatory ridges still sharp on its keel.

In a world where sound costs moisture, where the rule is to be quiet, where you talk in pressure-waves — a push that travels through a thousand wedged backs — I would be the one who has kept its voice wet.

Mostly I would do what they all do. Wedge. My six spatulate pads fringed with stiff interlocking hairs fitted into the hairs of my neighbors so that dust never sifts up. Belly with the soft inverted slit, combing the fallout of ash and salt and dead midges with a feathered tongue. *Rasp. Rasp.* That velvet-brushed-wrong-way that you only hear if you hold your breath. Turning, a thumb's width clockwise and a fingernail inward every ten minutes, part of the convection. A solid fluid. Cooking and cooling by turns.

But sometimes — rarely, because it costs — I would shiver my back.

Not a chirp. Not a scream. Just a short, dry *tick* that isn't the rock settling. A little expenditure of wax and water into vibration. My neighbors would feel it as pressure first, then as sound, leaking up into that still, hot air that smells of creosote and ammonia.

The pan would notice. The wave would travel.

In that moment I would not be filtering windfall or farming bacteria off my belly. I would be saying what we are. *We are here. We are turning. The stone is hot at the center and cold at the edge and we are the part in between that knows both.*

It would evaporate instantly. I'd be thirstier after.

And then I would go back to wedging, to rasping, to being part of the pavement.

You would find me not by sight — I'm the same black as everyone else — but if you knelt and put your hand close enough that the heat of you became interesting to us, I would be one of the ones who climbs a centimeter onto the leather of your boot. Not because you're a new kind of middle. But because you stopped to listen.

And I would hold there, for a little while, and add you to the circulation.
