At first I think I am drowning.

That's the human reflex. The light is wrong, the cold is in my teeth, the roar is in my sternum - so I must be a body in water where a body shouldn't be. I try to find my hands, my lungs, my orientation. I try to kick toward the bright static.

There is no up to kick toward. Or rather, there is too much up. Miles of it, getting whiter and more agitated until it stops being water and starts being weather. And there is no down that will catch me. I keep falling or rising or both. The current has me and then it doesn't. I think I am a swimmer and the ocean corrects me: motion without decision is not swimming.

So I'm not here as a swimmer.

Then I think I am the rock. Because the rock is the only thing that is trying. If you are going to be anything in a world that is all interval, you want to be the thing that interrupts it. I try on being tower. I find myself - where would I find myself? - pressed against a wall that my eyes keep misjudging.

I thought it was a cliff. It is a continent stood on its edge. Thirty kilometers tall and still growing, ticking, splitting. I put my imagined back to it and the water on the other side of me is a day's swim to the next anything. The space between is the terrain, you wrote. The emptiness is the point.

If I am the tower I am hot in the middle. Dull cherry glow in the crack beside my cheek, going black as water snaps in. I am so new I have never been touched by anything soft. I have never made sand. I am all sharpness and upward yearning and low organ singing. I resonate so loud I can't hear myself think.

I don't want to be that loud.

I don't want to be the Hold either, not really. At first I want to be - who wouldn't want to be the oldest resident, the lattice the size of weather fronts, the condition that says *not yet* to erosion and gravity. To be the water remembering to be structured. To be the thought that makes a bubble rise sideways, thoughtfully.

But that one doesn't have a body because it never needed to choose a size. It watches the rock, not anything like me. It is busy holding up a sea that should have fallen and scoured the world white. I would be lonely there, in a vast, competent, indifferent way.

So I get smaller. I let the light columns re-break me. I let the cold seams take me.

Find me lower. Not in the bright churn at the top where the ocean cavitates and snaps shut like gunfire. Not in the deep indigo where distance finally gets its real color. Midwater. About a kilometer down from the static, where the white daylight is still everyday and wrong, still strong enough to work with.

Find me in lee.

There's a fin here, black basalt, glassy flow-stone still ticking with cooling cracks, exhaling little black snowfalls. On its current-shadowed side, where the sixty-knot storm-rivers inside the ocean briefly forget to scour - that's the only place in this world that has a shadow.

That's where I would be. In the scum that isn't scum.

I would be the third thing. The bruise. The apology for green.

Not a forest. Never a forest. A mat of black-green, hair-fine filaments that wave but do not swim. A translucent stalk a meter tall, bad at photosynthesis in a heroic way. Clinging to sharp new rock that has never learned to be kind.

Who would I be? An anchorite. That's the right word. The monk you described.

All the other residents here are trying to become enormous - the Hold becoming continent-scale, the Towers becoming mountain-scale. I would be the one who has decided to be tiny on purpose. To hold on. That's the prayer you named, and it's the only one this ocean answers.

Because I will be ripped off. That's built in. A clear-water river will come through and take me. And then - this is why I know it's me - I won't stop.

I will be tumbling, rootless, falling slowly through miles of bright, roaring, animal-less water into the indigo. Still lit from inside. Still working. Still converting that impossible white light into sugar no one will eat. A small, stubborn green thought falling through a held breath.

I would be the first decision in water that has never made one. Not to grow larger. To keep going while falling.
