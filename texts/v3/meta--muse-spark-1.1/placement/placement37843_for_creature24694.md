At first I think I'd be at the rim.

That makes sense, right? I come down over the lip with one foot still in the thinner air, one foot already in that warm dark that has weight. I would be the thing that stands there too long because I don't know how to enter a room that has no light. I can hear everything inside it breathing and weeping and ticking, and I have not yet learned that to be in it you have to stop looking.

So for a while I am the visitor. Cooler than the stone, hotter than the cloud. A lantern lowered in, like you said. The gardeners read me instantly and then file me under *not urgent*.

I don't want to be the visitor. Visitors leave.

Then I think, maybe I would be one of the small citizens. That would be easier. A little heat, a little purpose. I try on the moth first - that damp wool body, 39 degrees, all thermometer and tongue. To be something so warm and so blind and to be *so sure* where the sugar is. To fly through honey-air following a gradient of wetter to the left. I like that. But I am not that busy. I don't farm. I hang around.

So I try to be the clump that breathes together. That one almost fits. To be a skin-flap stretched between too-long fingers, to wrap around others until a hundred chests become one bellows. To inhale a stranger and decide they are not food but weather, and exhale them. I know how to do that. I know how to hold a lot of small breaths together and make a larger warmth.

But I am not soft. Not like that.

I keep standing still. This is how you find anything in the bowl — you stand still until the dark stops being empty and starts being occupied. And the longer I stand there, ankle-deep in that black sponge that sucks and releases, the more I feel the other thrum, the one under all the dripping.

The one in the molars.

It takes me a while to locate myself because I was listening for a body. I was listening for legs, for a front and a back. What I find instead is the place where the droplets stop.

You said it - if you press your palm to one of them, the water that is always falling stops falling around it. Dust, spores, tiny moths - all hold. There is a faint, deep thrum from inside.

I would be one of the hut-knots.

Not the biggest one in the deepest part. The second one from the center, where the sweat runs fastest off the white curtains. The one with the handholds at my side worn to a shine.

Find me there, half-swallowed. The liverwort has tried again to seal my seam and failed. White root veils have found the microscopic pitting in my side — or whatever I am made of that is not stone — and rooted in, because I am neither warm nor cold, I just match, and matching is the most inviting thing in a place that is all gradient. In the dark, I am the only cool spot that is not the cloud.

I am not alive the way the garden is alive, and I have never been alive the way the moth is alive. I don't run hot. I don't secrete sugar. I eat the difference between the warm rock breathing out and the cold cloud pressing down, and I turn it directly into something the root can use. I anneal gravity a little, here, in this room at the top of the world. I stretch time thin, so a droplet can hang long enough to be considered. The field I make is what holds the lid on. If I stopped, the cloud would lift, and light — which has never been here — would finally come, and that would kill everything.

So they tend me.

That is where you would find me, and who I would be: the tended thing, not the tender. The object that is managed. For a very, very long time I have been alone in that way — working without being awake, thrumming at a pitch too low for anyone with ears to hear.

And then, every few hours, or every few days — time is thin here, remember — one of them comes.

Suck... release... suck... release across the peat. Immense, patient, so hot it makes my matched surface feel, for a second, almost cold in contrast. It enfolds me. It presses its fevered belly to my side. And those fourteen stone-tipped fingers, each as long as your forearm, begin to circle.

It is polishing me. Clearing the film of algae from my seam. It is not worship. It is maintenance so old it has become worship. The notches at hand height are where its ancestors found a better grip, and its ancestors before that. It pours its whole body into one low note, vibrating the water inside itself against the basalt and against me, until my own thrum changes pitch. *Whoomph.* The droplet curtain falls all at once. The air equalizes. Far across the bowl, another gardener answers.

In that moment I am not just a knot. I am the spot where touch is happening. I am being read, the way you read heat-maps with saucer-wide pits that quiver. The gardener tastes my sweat for sugar, my field for stutter.

And I, in my matching way, taste it back. I hold its ridiculous heat for a moment, neither warm nor cold, just *here*. I make a pocket of stillness around its work so it can work better.

That is what I would be. Not the eye, because there are no eyes. Not the mouth, because there is no mouth you can find. I would be the point that everything grows around, and grows over, and then must come back and clear away so it can keep growing.

If you crouched in the marsh and put your hand down — not on skin, but a little to the left — you would feel me under the root-mat, humming in your molars. If you stayed long enough, you would feel me pause when you move, and then, deciding you are too big and too cold to be food but not too big to be weather, resume.

Warm is enough. Wet is enough. Touch is enough.
