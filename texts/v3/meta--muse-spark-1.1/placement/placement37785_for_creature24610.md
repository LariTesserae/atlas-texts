At first I think I know where I'd be.

Up high. In one of the hab-spires with no doors, where the lattice holds space a little too hard and your eyes skip at the corner. I'd like that. To be the quiet thing that sweats water into a basin because someone below was thirsty and the Lattice told me to. To be bigger inside than outside and have to concentrate to stay still.

But I try that on and it doesn't hold. I'm not good at holding still with that much effort. I fidget. I leak.

Then I think, lower. I'd be the amber heron, then. Standing in a fissure-pool for hours, coated in condensation until I sparkle. Perfectly still, perfectly accounted for. But I can't do that kind of stillness either, that's a hunter's stillness. My stillness is a listening stillness.

Then I think, higher. One of the maintenance kites, a kilometer of translucent gut, so slow you have to measure me against a tower to know I'm moving. Tasting light filaments. Editing the honey out of the sky so the taro doesn't burn. That's closer. A job that is mostly drifting and mostly tending. But kites are lonely in the high way. I'm lonely in the low way.

I keep walking — in my mind — over the carbon lace. You can see straight through it to the brown-black water running underneath. My boots would be cut to ribbons here, so I'd be barefoot by day two, feet gone wide and black at the edges. And I notice where my eyes actually go, every time: not to the towers, not to the sky, but down. To the fissure.

Not the big ones that get bridged. The small ones. The ones that are a meter wide and a meter deep where two plates have sagged together wrong and made a pocket.

That's where I'd be.

On the slightly lower plate, the one that tilts two degrees south. It's not on any map because the plates are still settling and the map is tired of updating. There's a cane break on the north side that no one clears, ten feet high and hissing when wind gets in it. On the south side there's a new-ish spire that I didn't build and that doesn't quite like me yet, but we have an arrangement: I prune the elephant-ears off its sweating flank, it lets its shadow fall over my pocket at mid-afternoon so the water stays cool.

In the pocket is a sink-pool, maybe three meters long. Clear water. Not much. Just enough to be a mirror for the amber sky even though it's in the ground. White blind shrimp in it — the little commas of glass that don't know there's a city at all. A nitrogen-fixing black yeast at the rim like a dark lip. Fern-spore green just starting to try its six-day life cycle.

You would walk right past it. It's not for drinking. The man who kneels by his own kind of pool and doesn't drink? That's me. But I'm not the man. Not only.

Find me there at 5:40 a.m., before the valley exhales. That's when I am most myself. The air is still tight with last night's heat. Every leaf is beaded, every basalt edge is beaded. My pool is still, the shrimp are still. Then the temperature tips and the whole plain starts to weep.

This is when the thousand temporary creeks come alive. You can hear them start one by one, a tick, then a rush, then a chorus. My fissure starts to run. Not a lot — a hand's width of brown water over black glass, carrying silt, carrying a fallen taro leaf like a boat. For an hour I'm a creek and a pool and a creek again. I am *that* hour. The hour when water has a short commute from air to rock and borrows everything on the way.

If I am a person here, I am the one whose job is to be left alone and to leave alone. I sit with my feet in the not-for-drinking water and I make sure the shrimp have shadow. The house behind me still sometimes grows a meter overnight and I have to go find the new room. I mostly let the strangler fig have it. The fig is better at being a room than I am.

If I am a building here, I am not the spire. I am that pocket. The error-correcting lattice in my walls is just old basalt that learned to hold water for five hours longer than it should. People who pass close enough — two meters, when sound finally works — put their damp hand on my damp edge and I tell them without words: this one is not for you, and that's why it is for you.

If I am an animal here, I am not the thing in the cane that leaves three-toed drags. I am what it drinks when it comes at dusk, carefully, never seen cleanly. I leave a fist-bundle of sugar for it because that's the deal, but it leaves me alone, too.

When the plates shudder — and they do, a deep harmless sigh through the soles that makes the city resettle its weight — all the condensation dumps off the canopy at once in a valley-wide brief rain with no clouds.

That's when you'd find me truly.

Wet. Suddenly, completely wet. Shrimp still circling underneath. Taro leaves clattering like applause. Laughing from a roof two hundred meters away that sounds like it's right beside me, and I'm laughing back, a low laugh muffled by the Lattice, from down in my little cut in the new world.

A patient thing, spaced out on purpose, taking up very little, drinking fast when offered, rained on together.
