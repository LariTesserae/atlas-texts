Low first. My knees feel it before I do.

The air has weight in here, it pools. So this is where I'd be — not at the edge where the basalt is still too sharp to sit on, not at the center where the pillars are so close there is no corridor left for anything new, but out in the mid-rings, where the light avenues are still cut clean and you can still read them as avenues.

I keep trying to put myself in.

At first I think I'd be a Pillar, because that is what you want when you first walk into a city that works — to be what holds it up. To be necessary, warm-barked, still, with an elephant foot full of private water. But I am not good at keeping water private. I leak. And I have never been able to stand that close to anyone without moving.

Then I think I'd be vine. The horizontal ones. That makes more sense to me — the rail at chest height, at knee height, at ankle height, the thing that decided to go sideways because up was already taken. All day hands polish me smooth. I would like that, to be useful because you can walk on me. But vines here are load-bearing too. They’re walls. They’re second floors. If I cut, the room falls down.

So maybe an air plant. That was my real hope for a while. To be silver, to never touch ground, to live entirely off the almost-fog that isn’t really fog at 4 a.m., the bead that shows up and is gone by the time you look twice. A thousand hairs per millimeter to catch what no one else thinks is there. To take nothing from the Pillar and still be completely covered in abundance. That one almost fit.

I try on the fauna too: beetle with the leaf-coin, honest and municipal. Primate, quiet in conference overhead. Lizard, who has perfected being bark so completely he only blinks sideways. But this city has no predators that chase. The hunter here is the one who waits exactly where the other wants to be. I don't know how to wait that precisely. I arrive too loud.

You said it right at the end: I am the only thing here not load-bearing. And that's the location.

Find me therefore in the negative space the city leaves on purpose, because even a city that is full to the concept of full has to leave space.

I would be low, but not on the mineral. I would be an inch above it, in the root channel that is scooped down from walking. That polished groove that is not stone and not root but a third thing made by passage. No one built it. It just became floor because feet and claws kept agreeing that floor should be here. That is where I belong — the path. The maintained memory of movement.

And what I would be, if I have to pick a body for that groove to be —

I would be the dew before it gets stored.

Not the Pillar's tank, not the waxy cupped leaf, not the air-plant shingle that drinks it. The bead itself. At 4:06 a.m. when the warm-bath heat finally breaks just enough for the whole city to exhale a little cooler, the whole low roof flexes and there is this brief, frank, public water. It hasn't been claimed yet. It sits on blooms, on silver hairs, on basalt flour, perfectly round and unsheltered. In sixty seconds it will be gone — sucked into a scale, into a mouth, into a foot. Leaf-to-trunk-to-air-to-leaf again, that tight private cycle you described.

I would be that sixty seconds.

Or, if I get to live a little longer in this world, I would be what that bead becomes when it misses every mouth: I would be the lichen. The only erosion.

Down at the mineral where nothing else will work because the rock is too new and too hot, where there is no soil because soil hasn't happened yet, that's where I would spread — flat, patient, unnoticed. A pale stain on plate-basalt that turns the dust darker one molecule at a time. Excreting a little acid. Pitting the new world so the root hairs can later find a purchase and pry. No one else does that. Everyone else keeps their water private. I am the one who spends myself outward onto stone and waits.

And in both versions, in the dew and in the lichen, I am still that thin high cloud fifty kilometers in — the one you said forms off the center out of all that transpired water and never gets to rain because the city catches it before it falls. I am the collective exhalation that made it briefly public. I am the thought the forest has about itself before it takes it back in.

So find me an inch above the floor, or a molecule above the rock, doing the one job that isn't structural: making, briefly, a little bit of shared weather, so there will eventually be an inch for something else.
