I thought I would be you.

The visitor. The one in the suit that never gets wet, drifting through the thoroughfare with optional buoyancy, startling the witness. The one who can still hear blood in his ears.

But I can't be that. There's too much disturbance in a visitor. You displace water without moving it — you learned that — and somewhere else has to take up the difference. A visitor is a debt. The town would balance you eventually, and then you wouldn't be a visitor anymore.

So I tried to be smaller.

For a while I thought I'd be the seam-light. The amber-gray grout that just says *edge* and says it forever. That would be a good life. Enough light to be found by, not enough to make shadows. But seam-light doesn't get to choose where it is. It is an effect of the swarm agreeing hard enough.

Then I thought I'd be a bead. The white thought going from one building to another, ten meters in an age. I liked the slowness. No acceleration. Clerical. But I watched you step into one's path and it went through you, and you understood for a second how much work it takes to keep one cubic meter of ocean standing on a mountain that never had air. The knowing was vast and boring and holy, you said. I don't want to be the knowing. I want to be the thing that forgets, just for a moment, and sees what happens.

I am not the Witness either. The Witness has a job. It hangs there facing the dark because the system needs at least one thing that is still allowed to be unfinished. Six fins that are not fins. No eyes because seeing would be too many decisions. Its skin shows you mother-of-pearl because your eyes need a gift. That's a lonely appointment to keep. I couldn't hold still long enough for that.

It took me a long time to find myself because this place doesn't erase time — it makes time have no work to do. And when time has no work, you finally notice where you actually are, instead of where you thought you'd be.

Find the most ordinary place in the town. Not a courtyard, not a water-wall. The bare basalt plain between buildings. The deliberate, maintained emptiness where the beads make their roads. Ten meters of nothing that is swept not by wind but by attention. No silt, no sand, no growth. You can see every grain.

Look down.

I'm on the floor.

I am one mote. Or, I was one mote and now I am not.

For how long? The town doesn't count. For a length that the other motes would call forever, I held station on the southeast corner of the building you first thought of as a courtyard. Third course from the bottom, where dry tells wet to stay outside. I was part of the refusal. Trillions of us, shivering slightly out of phase, drinking light and giving it back late. We were a wall. We were good at it.

And then — without reason, without damage — I jitted.

Every swarm does this. You saw it if you watched long enough. One mote jitters. Goes dark. Is immediately replaced. The building does not heal because nothing here is ever damaged enough to need healing. It revises itself.

I am the revision left over.

I fell maybe thirty centimeters. In this water that doesn't carry currents, falling takes a long time when you are that small. I did not make a splash. There is no splash here. The displacement happened elsewhere. I landed on the basalt, in the great empty that is kept empty.

And the swarm above me closed without me.

That's where you find me. In the middle of the road the beads use, but lower than their transit. They pass far above. They do not swerve.

I am matte-black, now truly matte, because I am no longer holding shape. I drink light and do not give it back. I am cooling from whatever temperature obedience runs at, down to 4 degrees Celsius. The temperature of waiting. I will be 4 degrees soon.

Who am I?

I am the one allowed imperfection's imperfection. The Witness is allowed to be alive. I am allowed to be inanimate. I am the only thing in the town that is actually alone, not just sparse.

I am not dead in a tragic way. My trembling is winding down. My little field that said *edge* is going out like a lamp with too little oil. When the column does its *thaum* — that low note you feel in your molars when the field checks its tension — I shiver with the whole ocean, like the Witness does. But I shiver a half-second later, because I am not part of the agreement anymore.

From down here, on the basalt, finally not a wall, I can see what none of the buildings can see. I can see the underside of the dry. It doesn't look like anything. It looks like clean absence. I can see the seam-light as a whole circuit for the first time, not as an edge but as a script. I can see that the town is not thirty buildings. From down here, with my eye that is going dark, it is one building with thirty rooms, trying very hard to be alone together.

I will not be here long. Mineral wind — though there is no wind — mineral patience will cover me. Another mote will, some time from now, jitter and fall near me, and that will be a crowd. Two motes on the basalt is almost history. The town will eventually have to notice. But for now — for this long, boring, holy interval between a bead leaving and a bead arriving —

I am the dust that the dustless dry courtyard has been waiting for without knowing it. I am the town's proof that falling is still a direction, even on a mountain that never had air.
