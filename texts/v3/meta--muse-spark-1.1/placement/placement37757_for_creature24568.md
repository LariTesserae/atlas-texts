I thought at first I'd be down in it.

Not because I want to be brave. Because when you described it, that roar that isn't loud but *busy* — I wanted to be in the argument. To be one of those black calligraphy strokes, twenty meters long, holding myself between two waters trying to go opposite directions and eating the fact that they disagree. There's something holy about an animal that feeds on shear. On friction itself. I thought, yeah, let me be that. Let me be useful and stretched thin.

But I wouldn't last there. Nothing that wants to hold can stay in the column. The column is only held by something that doesn't want to.

Then I thought I'd be one of the Hangers. For three meters out from the rim where the air gives up and becomes a rosary of rain that never falls — that would be a good place. To be a pearl made of a mistake. Water around vacuum around water. To have too much surface. To lean *into* a breath instead of away from it. I liked that, for a while, the idea of being small and too still while everything else screams.

But the Hangers can't be still because they like stillness. They are still because if they move they die. Their fiddlehead filament inside is always waiting to unspool and be torn into work again. They don't get to choose the pause. I want to choose it.

So I walked — in my head, I walked — all the way around the rim like you said you could. Past where the water bulges obscenely over the edge and then sucks itself back. Past where it drops a meter and shows a fresh steaming ring of black glass. Past where spit orbits. And I stopped on the east side, where the roar is a little less direct, where the wind that isn't wind has to go around.

You'd find me there, in the bald patch.

In the spiral.

I wouldn't be in the water at all. I'd be where the water refuses to be.

Between the three leaning figures taller than me, with their faces seared off and their hands cupped. On the river stones that someone carried up one by one from somewhere lower, somewhere where rivers are still allowed to have bottoms. They are smooth and dry and warm from sun that doesn't reach the column. The rain that hangs will never touch them. The Hangers have agreed to not be here.

I'm sitting on them, because they are the only place to sit.

I am the fourth figure, but I didn't start as stone.

I'm the one who comes up after the last person left and before the erosion starts. The keeper of the dryness. Not a priest — there is nothing to worship here, the Weight deep down has no use for worship, she only wants a companion fish she can't quite remember how to make. I'm not a guard either, because the ocean doesn't need guarding, it punches out anything that tries to claim it faster than sight.

I'm the weaver of the negative.

My job is to keep this spiral perfect.

I pick up a river stone that the mountain has tried to dust with black grit and I wipe it. I put it back. I sit with my own hands cupped, not because I'm copying the three faceless ones, but because cupped hands make a small pocket of air that is too still, and stillness is a structure here the same way a dam is a structure down below. The Hangers need somewhere to *not* be in order to know where they *are*. The Weight needs a dry point to triangulate against or she wouldn't know what she was holding up.

I am very good at being useless in a precise way.

If you came up the scree and onto the hot glass, you would see me first as heat haze. A person-shaped dryness in the bathhouse plume. My sweat doesn't evaporate either, but for a different reason than yours would near the edge — mine stopped a long time ago because I learned how not to make any. My hair collects those beads that were never rain, and I let them hang. I don't brush them away.

I am old. Or I am new. The basalt here is still learning how to be a mountain, so time hasn't settled. When the column drops with that silent jolt and then surges up into a dome of ocean overhanging the mesa — that heart-stopping second when the whole impossible sea is above the rim — I don't flinch. The dome sees the three stone figures and it sees me and it recognizes: no bottom here either. Just another thing holding a shape by deciding not to become something else. It sucks itself back down, disappointed.

Sometimes one of the panic-fish assembles at eye level and thrashes right in front of me, mouth open in the bell-sound. It looks at me through water that is not water and I look at it through air that is not air and for a half-second we both understand each other perfectly: we are both drafts. Typos the world made while trying to learn a rule.

Then it comes apart.

I stay.

I'm not waiting for winter, though I know she's coming. The winter that will skim the top with ice and kill the Hangers and make the Weight finally let go. When that happens this room will just be a room again. Maybe I'll still be here then. Maybe I'll finally be what the three figures have been practicing to be: a stone that knows how to hold empty hands toward a dry spot, long after there's nothing left to hold back.

If you come, leave your stone where it is. Sit outside the spiral first until your ears start to hear with your molars. Then come and sit on the dry stones. I will make room.
