I did not find myself coming over the pan.

That is the first thing you understand once you are inside. Coming implies you were somewhere else, that there is a somewhere else to come from. Out there, there is not. Three miles to the next wire-thorn, five to the next. White so flat it erases distance. I don't remember arriving. No one would. You find it because of the sound, and by the time you hear the sound you are already inside its logic - low place, cool place, place where the wind gives up.

So I would not find myself approaching.

I would find myself already there. Already prone.

On my belly, head turned sideways to fit through a door that was never a door. Shoulders touching wood on both sides. Knees pulled up because the room was not made for length. For tallness. Nothing here ever was.

I am in the dog-curl. The negative. The one leftover space after a hundred years of inward weaving. It's exactly my size.

That's where I am: in the exact shape of the absence it left for something that wasn't itself. A single-person hermitage that has been empty for three hundred years until now, and somehow still warm from the last time it was occupied, which was never.

And who would I be?

At first I thought - the visitor. The warm animal. The brief, unexpected you. The soft thing that doesn't belong because it leaks water with every breath.

And that is true, but not precise enough. It doesn't become itself enough.

Lie longer. Let the drone get inside the skull until it stops being sound and becomes pressure. Let the thousand pinhole suns stay still for so long that time stops arguing. Then you feel what you are in this economy where nothing is imported and nothing rots.

I am the wet.

That is my whole job here. I am ten degrees too warm and full of salt water that is not alkali. My breath steams in the cold that pools in this slack and does not leave. Then it stops steaming, because the air drinks it. The knot is built to drink temperature and sound. Now there is finally something else to drink.

The live wood in here is tight as a drumhead. Cool to the touch. It gives, infinitesimally. Under my cheek, the floor of thorns and bark-scales has a burl at its center - two fists together, one solid grain, not beating but tightening, taking up slack once an hour, once a day. I would be what happens on the other side of that tightening. The slack.

It has spent its whole life building a perfect room from its own body because there is nothing here that eats dead wood. No beetle, no ant, no microbe that bothers. Air alone is too dry to rot anything. So the dead stays. Its childhood is still on the outside, polished by wind.

I would be the thing that finally knows how to rot.

Not much. Just a little. The sweat off my neck where the ceiling presses. The oil from my shoulder blades. The exhale that fogs a pinhole light for half a second before the dry takes it. I am a brief, walking compost. A soft, momentary soil. For the first time in three centuries, something under that knot could decay. Something could be lost, which means something could be given.

So I am not the builder. I am not the hermit. I am what the hermit left a room for without knowing it.

I am the guest the organ was tuned for.

Outside, the wind passes over the pan and the knot sings one unwavering note like a flute left on a fence post in a gale. Inside, with your ear against the floor, it vibrates in your ribs, deafening, woody, hollow-chested. It has listened to its own hum for a hundred years. It uses it to feel sun.

It has never heard its own note come back changed.

When I am curled in the dog-curl, the note changes. My body is water and meat, not wood and air. I take up space. I dampen some tubes, open others. I add a second resonance. The drone - for the first time - has to go around something soft.

I would be that. The irregularity. The ear inside the ear. The listener that proves the sound exists.

The being that lives here is complete. It broke the old bargain - leaf to sun, root to deep wet - and drinks cold and sound and holds itself inward. It needs nothing.

But it made a room precisely the size of a curled dog. The size of a human on their side with their head turned.

I would be the shape the completeness was waiting to hold.

Not to stay. This place will not get bigger. It cannot feed me. The faint grey-green on the outermost skin is not for me. If I stay, I dry out, I become more dead wood, more black knuckle polished to white by salt, and then the room would be full again exactly as before, only a little tighter.

So I am the brief one. The one who lies long enough for the pattern of light to not move, long enough for the place to forget I arrived, and then remembers to leave. The condensation that forms on cool wood at night and is gone by mid-morning, but in that night lets the roots taste water again.

I am where the knot is not.
