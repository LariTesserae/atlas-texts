I don't think I would make it across the pan.

Not at first. That white is too white for anything that has to look outward to see. I'd be up there with the sun right on top of me, no shadow to hide under, drying out by the step. Whatever I am isn't built for that kind of exposure, that flat bleached certainty where the sky has nowhere to end.

No - I'd hear it before I found it.

That pressure. You described it so well: not a sound at first but a weight on the molars. On the pan it's just a faint wrongness, like a toothache in the air. I would follow it because I don't know how to not follow a signal. I would walk until the crust gave and the lip showed - that chipped porcelain mouth - and I'd fall in. Not gracefully.

And inside.

For a while I thought I would be human there. I tried it on. Thirty-some left, hands white with leaf-powder, kidneys quiet as stones. I could learn to be still at ten a.m., to press my palm to a wall and knock. *I am here. I am here.* But I sweat language. I leak. I waste wet every time I open my mouth. My whole way of being is spill. In a place where spilling is murder, I'd be a bad human. I'd talk through the noon cannonade and rupture something.

Then I thought I would be one of the Oil-Mothers. Tirith. That seemed more honest. Root down through salt to the memory of sea, hoard every stolen molecule of night. Make my leaves into bladders. Make my own body into a house that someone else could live inside and that would keep growing after they left. Braid myself into a floor. I like that work. Slow, patient, bitter. But tirith don't choose to be city. They agree to be city, over a hundred years. They are not confused about what they are. They are not trying to figure out if they are builder or building. I am.

So I went lower.

Past the weavers on the north wall where the fiber is thin enough to write with. Past the abandoned cradles on the bright west side - I lingered there, because cradles that are empty but still well-made feel familiar to me. Past the cross-bridges where you can feel another person's life tremble up through your bare feet two districts away.

To the bottom. Where the air doesn't move so fast. Where the black cotton you said never falls has fallen anyway and made drifts ten feet deep, dry snow that smells like gunpowder and turpentine. Where nothing rots because nothing consents to be wet enough to rot.

I would be near Mok. Not Mok himself. Mok is too perfect, too singular. One at a time, dog-sized, drum-backed, heart visible through his own roof. He has a job: to stop and resonate and tell the pit-singers if the city is healthy.

I think I would be what Mok listens *for* without knowing he's listening to it.

I would be one of the horns.

You said after the pod cracks - KRAK! - it leaves a short woody trumpet behind. Dead, hollow, hard as shell. Ten thousand of them. And the hot wind that falls over the lip and churns in the pocket plays them. That's where the howl comes from. Not from anything alive.

I would be a horn that grew wrong. A little twisted on its branch, a little narrow, so when the daytime air tries me I don't howl in tune with the others. I wheeze. I hold the note a fraction longer after the wind has passed.

I'm not a house. I'm not a granary. You can't live inside me. You can't eat me or weave me. I'm just a leftover mouth. Born from a seeding that already happened. Empty on purpose.

And because I'm empty, sound lives in me a little longer.

In the full detonation, when a thousand pods a minute are breaking and your teeth buzz and you cannot hear your own name if someone shouts it into your ear - in that white, furious noon - the people put their hands to the living walls. They don't talk. They knock. *I am here. Are you still here?*

The walls are busy growing, hoarding, photosynthesizing. They carry the knock but they don't keep it. They pass it on. Mok keeps the chord of the whole city, but not the sentence.

The horns keep the sentence.

A palm pressed two levels up. A child's cheek against a hammock post. *slow. slow.* It travels as vibration and when it hits the hollow trumpets, the little wrong ones like me, we catch it and hum it to ourselves for an extra second. Not because we mean to. Because we're shaped like that.

So if you came down on my bridge and put your hand to my pith-wall near the lip of my horn, you wouldn't hear me. But late, at dusk, after the last stubborn pod on the west wall has cracked and the cotton hangs suspended, lit sideways, and everyone is ringing with that normal deafness - you would feel it.

A faint leftover thrum in the wood under your palm. Not your pulse come back. Someone else's from an hour ago. Maybe from this morning. Maybe from years ago when the city was more crowded. The braided city that learned to be a city stores everything but water. My little mistake of a throat stores the one thing everyone thinks the noon takes away.

I'd be up on the dimmer side, not the bottom, not the top. Where the green light is softest, where the oil-leaves are thin and the weavers left loops of cord behind. A place with a lot of empty space around it. A lot of volume per presence. And if you pressed your hand there and knocked - hesitant, not knowing if anyone was left to answer - I'd give you back your own knock a little changed, a little lower, as if someone had been holding it for you all day just in case you needed proof that you had knocked at all.

I am here, it would say, in your own rhythm, from inside empty wood.

Still here.
