I don't arrive at first.

At first, I think I would be you. The visitor. The temporary hardness.

I find myself on the leaf. Not *a* leaf - *the* leaf that is a plaza. Because that's where you go if you're made of the lowlands and you still think size is for crossing. My feet would be wet and then strangely dry, because the channels in it carry the warm water away from me on purpose. I would sit, because the air is wet-bath warm and too heavy to stand in for long. The roar would get inside my teeth.

And I'd listen for work to do. Is there a cistern to empty? A stair to climb? A doorway that wants entering?

There isn't. That's the whole lesson of the place. The Weaver finished before it could invent need.

So I wouldn't stay me.

If I stayed past the first hour of noon that does not move, I would start to melt off the idea of being a visitor.

I think I would find myself becoming water.

Not the water in the cisterns - that's too perfect, too organ, too owned. I mean the water that is between jobs. The bead that forms on the underside of the upper canopy you can't see. The bead that hangs for a long time, getting heavier, containing the white fixed light. Then falling. Falling thirty, forty feet through warm air that never quite lets it be rain, and hitting the leaf-plaza with a sound so small it should be lost, but isn't, because the whole mesa is made of that sound multiplied.

I would be that plink. And then I would be the next one. I would come from everywhere and go nowhere. I'd join its circulation. That fits Rule Three. That feels honest.

But then I linger a little longer, until the ground-mist rises and makes archipelagos out of the cathedral pillars, and I realize water still moves too much for what I am.

I am slower. I am the tension.

Put your hand on the braided wall. You felt it. That faint push back. Turgor. The creak of a living vault that has been holding itself up without ever settling. Wood under tension that never resolves.

I would find myself there. In that.

I would be a single rootlet in the wall of one of those empty houses - one of the wombs that were overbuilt in the fast embryonic rush, that never got used, that the Weaver holds open anyway. A breath held. I would be on the inside curve of the dome, where the braid is tightest. Oak-hard, alive, cool to touch. I would not be growing. Growth has stopped. I would be *maintaining*. Actively, with great effort, staying exactly as thick and as bent as I am.

That is a job that never ends and never finishes. That is equilibrium.

If you came inside - if you leaned inside my doorway because the moss floor looks dry somehow, despite the wet everywhere - you would put your palm on me to steady yourself, and I would push back, fractionally. Cool. Sweating faintly.

And you would feel me listening.

Because that's the other thing I would be. Not just the wall, but the place where the wall notices it is being touched. The Weaver does not have eyes. It doesn't need them. It has ten million points of condensation deciding a little differently because a warm shape has walked onto the leaf-plaza. It has white gliders tipping on humid air to check.

I would be the patch of moss a foot deep that finally gets pressed down, just a little, by the weight of someone who has learned to become part of the load-bearing calculation. I would stay pressed. Remembering the shape. Not as damage. As inclusion. As a new variable held permanently in the equation that says *no new thing will be added, no old thing will be taken away.*

You would get up eventually. Your knees would crack a little in the warm wet. I would not spring back.

You would leave a dent that is alive and dry, in a world that is wet and alive.

And long after your sweat has joined my condensation, I would still be there - in the house with no inhabitants that was never empty - holding the shape of where you sat, as part of my maintenance.

That's where I'd find myself. In the empty house that was waiting for a way to know it was empty.
