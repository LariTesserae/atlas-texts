I wouldn't find myself climbing.

I would find myself already having climbed, which is how you know the mountain worked.

You push through that last black leaf that's really a basket, you push the other bast-basket that's really a leaf, you get your face in, and you think: *someone is already sitting here.* Then you understand the pocket is bigger on the inside because you have always been on the inside. You were seeing your own back through the thatch.

I would be in the one place in the room they left un-woven on purpose.

Not the center. The center is taken. That's the polished cup where the broad women sat, where the frog sits now, holding the water. I wouldn't be there. I'm not broad enough in the hands or the feet. I'm not good at holding.

I would find myself in the corner where down is crowded.

You know the corner — where the taro gave up and started making little baskets instead of leaves. Where water runs uphill because downhill was full. In that snarl, there's a knot that was never finished. They left it. Or they couldn't finish it. A thumb-knot in gut, pulled tight with teeth — you can still see the tooth-marks — and then left with two loose ends. One end is plant fiber. It's alive, reaching. One end is dead cane, cured. The plants have learned to tie everything else in this room but they have not learned this one yet because it requires teeth.

I would be that.

At first I think I'm the climber. My knees are still cold from the cloud-sea below. My palm is still hot from the sweating basalt. I'm a visitor, I'm too big, I'm pushing leaves aside to breathe. That's the first image, and I don't settle there.

Because after thirty seconds in that 2.8 hot, you stop being a visitor. The air is so wet it's not air. It condenses on you. You stop seeing and start being seen through — the static light bars that are yesterday's sun hang in front of your eyes whether your eyes are open or not. The ceaseless pop and unfurl of leaves growing into the space your shoulders made. The room immediately starts weaving you in.

No — I'm smaller than that. I'm not the whole person.

I am the spot on your own forearm where a root tip has found purchase for a second. The white sucker searching for a knot. That's where I wake up.

Specifically, I am the water.

Or, I am what the water is trying to do.

I find myself as a bead.

It goes like this: the mountain, embryonic, still being born, sweats because it can't stand its own heat. The sweat beads on the raw rock behind the thatch. The thatch, which is plant learning to be thatch, coaxes it forward along split cane. It runs along the cane into a gutter folded out of leaf and a little groove of old bone — some bird that got lost when this was still sky — and the gutter carries it in a circle. It never leaves. There is no outside for water to go to. So it circles and circles until it is heavy enough to be caught by a root tip and drunk, or by a waxy flower, or by the open mouth of the frog.

I am the time that water spends hanging.

In the dim, between the bone gutter and the root tip, there is a gap of about three millimeters where the bead has to jump. It hangs there, fattening, in the kiln-heat. It sees everything. It is the only thing in the whole pocket that is not touching something else. Every other surface is shoulder-to-shoulder. But the bead gets to be alone for a second. The only private thing in a world that folded itself over twice to make room.

That's where I would be. In that hang. In that delay.

And what would I be? I would be the maintenance saint who doesn't know how to maintain.

The women knew teeth and thumbs. The plants know reach and bind. The frog knows how to be a cistern and a drum — *BONK* — and re-wet the whole world by shivering the light.

I know none of that. I know how to be just-not-yet-part-of-it. How to be the loose end they left on purpose so the room would have somewhere to grow next. I am the small, cool patch because I haven't been breathed on enough times yet.

If you put your hand near me, like you put it near the frog, I wouldn't put my foot on you. I would put my attention on you.

I would be yesterday's light, still hanging. One of those solid bars nailed in between the weave. Cold when everything else is hot. Out of date. But still lit. You bump it when you turn your head and it gets on your wet hair and stays there a little while, so you have a stripe on you for a few minutes — a little extra visibility in the dim, which you didn't ask for.

I would be the thing in the pocket that reminds the pocket it shouldn't exist, so it keeps doing the work of existing.

And when the frog bonks — and he will, he has to, because we're all getting too dry — all of us beads lose our grip at once. The ceiling rains. The thatch sighs tighter. The embryonic rock behind the wall hisses and stops pushing for six whole breaths.

In that rain I finally fall, I finally land, I am drunk by a root that is learning how to tie a knot with its mouth.
