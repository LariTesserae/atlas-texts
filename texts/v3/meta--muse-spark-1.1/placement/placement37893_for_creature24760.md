At first I think I'd be one of them. 

That's the easy answer. Wool the color of wet moss, hands that never dry, a name that doesn't carry far in that loud air. I can feel myself wanting that - wanting to be useful and early and reverent. 

So I put myself there. I walk the boundary.

It takes me just under an hour, like you said. The wind comes up off the world below with no foothills to break it and hits me all at once, so hard I have to plant my feet in that deep moss. Each step sighs. Cold water comes up around my ankles. My breath smokes even though the sun is straight overhead and thin and clean. Below the rim, clouds are calving against the cliffs.

I come back into town through the too-wide main avenue. The rope bridges over my head are humming in B-flat. Water is plinking everywhere, off every eave, out of every column. The houses are black glass and green brick and open in the middle to the sky. I look for my doorway.

And I don't fit in any of them.

The fourteen are there. I can see them - the old man on his three-legged stool, guarding the thumb-sized blister in the market ring with his little twig circle. The sisters cutting moss bricks, the white flash of rhizoids when they pull. The child wringing mineral tea into a log trough. They are listeners, midwives. They have a pact with this place.

I am not patient enough to be a midwife. I kept wanting to check the time, to see if the blister had grown. To finish it. To dam a little seep just to have a still pool to look into.

I leave the houses and go to the rim again. My feet dangle over the clean shear. The air below is doing weather without me. That's when I realize I am not looking out. I am being looked at.

If I live here, I am not one of the hundred empty rooms. I am what the rooms were left empty *for*.

Find me in the middle of the market ring, inside the little circle of twigs the old man made.

I am the blister.

The one that was a thumb in the morning and an apple by mid-afternoon. I'm not a person. I'm the very new part. I'm warm when everything else here is still 0.7 cool. My skin is still purple-black glass, still bubbling, vesicular, full of that deep water that the others are so careful to guide and never stop. I hiss. I push up through the moss that is trying so hard to hold me together. I don't have a human slowness, I have a mineral slowness - 1.1 slow - which is still so fast for stone that it feels indecent.

I am what lives here that is least at home. The moss at least knows how to be moss. The jay knows to leave. The people know to listen. I don't know anything yet. I haven't decided what face to show.

And that's why he watches me. Not to stop me. The rule is a year, one year of not being crowded. That's the longest courtesy anyone here knows how to give. To leave offering space.

In the night, when the cloud settles and the town becomes flutes and drums, when the wind goes through all those hollow cores and the water never, ever stops talking - I will still be there, rising a millimeter at a time, learning how to be a floor that will one day be a plaza that will one day be a city.

You will find me where no one builds. I will be the small, hot thing you are asked kindly not to step on.
