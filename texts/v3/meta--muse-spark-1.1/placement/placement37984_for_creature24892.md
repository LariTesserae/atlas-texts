I thought I'd be at the mouth for a long time.

That's the first honest answer. I would not go straight in. I'd hang in that lip where the black glass is still too hot to touch even through gloves, where outside is -70 and inside is 58 and both try to get in my suit at once. Hissing filters. Ticking. I'd think I was an observer. I'd think I wanted to count the terraces, map the fields with thrown dust, log the cistern.

I wouldn't.

After the first hour your eyes stop looking for a horizon. There's too little air to make distance behave. After the second hour you stop waiting for someone to come greet you, because the lights that blinked at you have gone dark again and you understand: they weren't welcoming you in. They were checking if you knew how to be quiet.

So I would not be on a porch. I wouldn't be crossing. I don't have that kind of faith in invisible hills.

You'd find me low. Not on the floor - nobody can live on the floor, it's 90C and it slides. But low on the east wall where a newer crack runs. One of those nervous, bright cracks that appeared last week when the spire grew another three centimeters and the whole blister flexed. Water loves it. It finds it in the night when the stone breathes in and condenses, and it races down it fast, too fast, so it overshoots all the careful gutters the others tended.

The city hasn't caught up yet. The builders haven't.

I'd be there because I couldn't stand to watch it waste.

Who would I be? At first I thought I'd be one of the thin ones. The lamp-talkers. One more patched ceramic suit, hairless, heat-pipes faint in the collarbone, deaf in a useful way. Learning to lean instead of fall.

But I'm not good at stillness. And those people have become experts at stillness. They sit in 0.3g dimples for ten hours watching a bead form.

No. I'd be the person who couldn't.

So I sit too close to the hot rock. I take a ceramic shard - not grown, just broken off my own too-heavy guise - and I scratch a new, shallow groove with my own hands. It's wrong. It's straight where things shouldn't be straight. The stitch-field at the edge of the eastern cluster catches my knuckles and tries to pull my skin two ways, my fillings aching. I wince. I do it anyway. I make a crude, human, stupid little dam, a lip no wider than my thumb, to kick that racing water sideways into an existing channel.

It works for maybe three minutes. Then a sphere forms where my dam meets the field overlap. Fist-sized, trembling, steaming slightly. Hanging. Not falling.

That's where I live. In that overlap. Between the city's intent and my interruption.

You'd find me there with both hands cupped around a ball of water that isn't mine, not drinking it, just holding it so it doesn't fall until the builders notice. They will notice. That is their whole job. At night when I turn my suit lamp off, I can see them already coming - not as individuals, never as individuals, but as a darkening on the glass, a soot that moves against the cherry-red glow.

They will take my little groove apart molecule by molecule and rebuild it correctly. A curve just slightly wrong. One lattice at a time. And they will take a little salt off my gloves while they're at it.

I think I would be a translator.

Not human to human - we already have the shutters for that: *long long short, we're still here.* 

Human to city. The squatters farm the leak. I farm the misunderstanding between the leak and the ones who built the farm. I make a mistake that catches water, they correct the mistake and in doing so make the city a centimeter larger, a little more able to hold the next person who hangs in the mouth thinking it's empty.

If you came in later - six days or sixty years later - you wouldn't find me. The wall where I worked would be seamless. There'd be a balcony there that wasn't there before, just wide enough for a person to sit in 0.3g and watch a bead form. And in the hanging cistern in the middle of the void, which never boils though it should, there would be a few grams more water that, if you traced its isotope, had been outside my suit once.

That's where you'd find me. Not as a body on the scree at the far end, not yet. As a negotiation that held.
