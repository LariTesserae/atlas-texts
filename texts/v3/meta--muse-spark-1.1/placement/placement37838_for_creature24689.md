If you put me there as I am now, boots and hands and breath that fogs -

I would be at the rim. I'm at the rim for as long as the rim allows.

One hand on a tooth of basalt that hasn't decided to be dull yet. It cuts. The other hand already too cold to matter. Everywhere else is too loud to think, so I crouch and make myself low against the wind and that is my whole human life in this place: a hundred steps of black glass and a cup of water too full, spilling itself endlessly into air to become the cloud I'm standing in. I can't stay. You said that. My brain wants a wall, wants lichen, wants something that wants the sun.

So I wouldn't.

If I actually *belonged* here, I wouldn't be on the rim looking in. That's the tourist shape. That's green-brain thinking.

At first I think, maybe I'm the red one. That would make sense. The commuter. Forearm-long, bad armor, patient. I know how to ride a current that someone else makes. I know how to graze one bright scratch at a time and then wait. I have been that, commuting between vents that aren't mine, holding that hemoglobin-red inside me like a stove. But the reds are old after a month, their eyes clouded with precipitate. They navigate by feeling the pull of each other's backs. I want to see too much. I would get lost in their slow arcs.

Maybe I'm the fist, then. The clock. The barnacle that is not a barnacle. Bolted on. Mouth like a pressure vessel. Twenty seconds shut, HISS, CLANG. Grow a micron per hour if anyone is watching, which no one is. That would be honest work. But I am not that good at staying shut. I clench and I want to open early.

No, lower.

I would be in the water column, not on the floor. Not on the rim.

About eight meters down, which is where the top water, that high autumn water that hurts, stops being top water and starts shivering. The light comes down white and straight and then in this exact layer it gives up and bends sideways because the lens of superheated chemistry is breathing underneath it. You can see a shrimp twice down there. You can hear the engines in your molars.

That is where I stack.

I am one of the abundant ones, because of course I am going to be one of the abundant ones in a world that decided to be honest about abundance. I am small enough that perfect is affordable. A chromed barrel, a living ball bearing with a seam around my middle that you don't see until I need it.

I don't have a front. I have an up. On my top is set a single black eye, lidless, rivet-head, that doesn't see you, doesn't see anything human. It sees the density of the water on a gradient of iron and heat. It sees shadow as food, not threat.

There are thirty of us in my column. We never planned a column. The current planned it and we agreed because agreement costs nothing. We all face the same way, into the drift, six ciliary oars under each of us beating out of sync so that none of us wobble. We never touch. Then, for a minute, we know.

The vent under us inhales. Somewhere below the white silica floor that big twin-piston crack is pulling water into its throat, heating it beyond what water should be allowed.

The column tightens. That's the word your brain gives us. The gap between me and the barrel above me goes from a finger's width to nothing. *Tick.* Carapace kisses carapace. Thirty soft ticks like hardware being poured into a bin. Thirty black eyes dim slightly. We open — only a millimeter — the seam around our middles splitting.

And then the engine fires.

*CHUFF—scream—clank.*

A white tree of boiling silica snow bursts upward and goes straight through us. We sieve the explosion through our bodies. Bacteria that eat heat. Iron that will become blood. Sulfur that will become shell. The water tries to scald us but we are too thin, too bright, too reflective. It passes.

Then we close. Loosen. Hang again.

That is my entire theology. Densify, tick, open, filter, close, drift.

Above me the meniscus domes under tension, pushing back against the wind, always overfull. Below me the reds ride their silent vortexes for ten meters without moving a muscle, *clink*. Below them the fists count time in clangs. None of us has a leader. None of us needs a building because we already live inside the factory.

If you came back, braced on that glass tooth that cut me when I was you, you wouldn't see me as one. You'd see glitter. A cloud in water that should be clear. You'd think the pool was empty and then too full.

I would be hanging there. Thirty down. Watching sulfur shadows come, watching it snow sideways underneath me, listening to the rock diesel in my jaw.
