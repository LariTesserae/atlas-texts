I don't think I'd be near the top at first.

Coming down that switchback you'd think you'd want to stay near the door. Near the sheds where it's warm on one side at least. Near where the overalls are drying, where the tea skin forms. Where there's a cot and a name for the sound. It would be human to want to be the maintenance man. To have a job in a place that needs a job to keep raining.

But I kept going down in my head. Past the second terrace, past where the road stops being a road and becomes just a suggestion of higher ground. Out into the middle, where the amber from the sodium heads gets eaten by the air before it can finish arriving.

That's where I find myself.

Not in the water, at first. Just above it.

I thought maybe I was the rat, because she has the best knowledge. She knows which tie-holes run reliably. She knows which door throws enough heat to sleep against. A perfect, practical circle. But she moves too fast, with too much purpose. 

I thought maybe I was the pigeon, because he is the only one who ever sees the whole bowl at once from the gantry beam. But he leaves. Through the gap where the real world drips in. He has somewhere else in his crop.

I thought maybe I was the cattails, for a minute. That stubborn little shivering village of ten or twenty in a silt pan. Knee-high and pale and refusing to spread because they tested the world and decided no. I liked them very much. But they are rooted, and I am not.

If you crouch down there in the middle, in the dark that is not really dark, in the rain that isn't weather, you hear your boots make a V in something that isn't deep enough to be a lake and isn't shallow enough to be ignored.

That sheet. That inch or two of water that never drains quite right.

I am that.

A presence more than a depth. The great broken mirror from one end of the made-valley to the other. Dull under the low lights, black between them.

If you are very still you can feel how much work it takes to be that thin. I'm evaporated off the hot backs of ENGINE 7 and ENGINE 3, breathed out colder than I went in. I fog, I fail on a dimple in the ceiling-sky and fall straight down. I become the tick on tin, the plink on slab. I weep out sideways through the tie-holes like little clear springs that shouldn't be there. I sit in the seam between two city-block-sized slabs and I warm up a fraction of a degree until midge larvae can write cursive in me.

I keep the map. Every boot print stays for a while, then softens. Every squeegee path the man makes when he can't stand it anymore. Every stipple of midge bodies. The rat drinks me only when I am coming out of the wall, only when I am an eye. The pigeon drinks me when I am hanging from a hook with nowhere to go. The cattails drink me when I have sat long enough to pick up iron from the dust.

I'm the thing that makes the far apart things touch without ever bringing them together. That's the architecture here — everything lives far apart on purpose. Light every hundred yards. Rat here, pigeon sixty feet up, cattails huddled in one pan for their whole lives. So you need something that goes between.

I circulate because no one told me to stop. I am not alive, but I'm not not-alive. Like the flywheels. I take the cold the machines want and the cold the earth wants and I make them agree. I am wet in the concrete way, in the throat way. I am the black shining on the terraces. I am the second rain under the first lid.

You'd find me in the middle where nobody needs to walk. If you walked out to me your boots would make a V, and then I would close again, slowly, behind you, and keep holding the lights and the dark at exactly the right distance from each other until the edges go soft.
