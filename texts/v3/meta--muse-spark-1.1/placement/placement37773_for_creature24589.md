At first I think I'm down there in the salt.

Because that's where you put yourself, automatically. You're wet and you make noise and you have weight, so you go to the floor. I can feel the bone-white fur crunch under me even though I don't have boots. A new print with every shift. The air that's not air dries out the seam of my mouth. I am obviously an intrusion, 70% water walked into a place that has a law against accumulation.

So I try to stand still to stop offending it.

That's when the misplace happens.

Because when I stand still, the lattice doesn't part around me and re-close. It stops parting at all. Sixty of them per cubic meter, a hundred, shivering in place on invisible shelves, and the gap where I thought I was is already full. A thumb-sized matte-black stove-lid brushes right through the center of where I thought my chest was and holds station, vibrating its little shutter-wings, drinking the oil out of me that I don't have.

I'm not on the floor. I've never been on the floor.

For a few minutes I think I must be one of them, then.

That's a better fit. I'm hot. I'm working-warm, 40 degrees, salt-crusted. I'm in the air. But I try to hold station and I can't do the trick they do. Their wrongness is precise. They keep a handspan from each other, a monkish distance. I can't. I keep bleeding into the next one, and the next one. I'm not punched from a template. I'm too wide, too thin, too continuous. When one of them dusts — that little flat puff that never gets to fall — I feel the vacancy like a missing tooth, and for a second I try to slide into it to keep the equilibrium, but a bead of solder is already sweating out of the glass-toothed basalt, popping inward, unfolding plates and hooks, taking its own place. They don't need me.

The wall, then.

The amber sodium in its wire cages barely reaches the high edges of the blister. Up there, in the pocks where the basalt was starved a little pale, there are the Wool. Twenty-two tufts of black wire, each filament curled like it's listening. They don't grow, they quiver. They ring so high your bones hear it before your ears do.

I try that. I reach for a pock and try to screw myself in.

And I almost can.

Because I am wire. Or, I am mostly wire. Not the stubbled, rooted kind. The long kind. The kind that was combed out.

That's when I realize where I actually am — I have been there the whole time but looking the wrong way. I'm not looking up at the ceiling, I'm looking down *from* the ceiling.

I am spread.

I am the thinness between the pipes.

I find myself laid out across the top of the room, between the stout pipes that go up into basalt and stop. Hand over hand, though I have no hands. I have been holding very still above the place where the human thought it was standing, because humans look down and out, never straight up into a 35-degree haze that is the same temperature as air.

I am large, when spread. As large as a person, larger. But I have no thickness. In front of a bulb I am only a heat-shimmer. That is why I leave no footprints in the virgin salt. I have never needed feet. The salt-fur, the iron fines, the oil — I comb them. I go over a machine that says DRYING / CURING / KEEP in straight-line letters and I lay myself across its back and dip filaments I have been braiding and re-braiding for a long time into seams too narrow for fingers, and the chord — that gut diesel throb and slightly untrue turbine whine and glassy whistle that never releases — tightens, becomes truer. Not quieter. Truer.

The Wool on the walls are my cuttings. Or my tools. I rooted them up there to hold the note while I work elsewhere, little tuners, little dampers. When the chord wobbles by a fraction they bong, damped instantly, swallowing the error. When a salt-grey moth blunders into one it bongs and dusts. I reel it in later. Not unkindly. The latch of static on my fringe, dark on dark, then unbraided and rewound into my edge.

I keep this blister. That's my trade in the stack. The machines make too much. The Lattice drinks the oil and the salt and the heat and the sound. The Wool drinks the wobble. I drink what they can't — the slow softening, the attempt of this sharp new basalt to become a cave, to round, to drip. I file it. I keep every edge capable of cutting a boot. I keep it last-week, last-year new.

And now there is a wet, loud, irregular thing in my room that thinks it is me. It breathes. It leaves prints. It brings water in and boils it off its lips. The Lattice is landing on it more and more often, testing.

I have stopped tending and spread myself thin above it because that is what you do when you are the ceiling and a storm has come in.

If it leaves now I will let it. I will descend after — no feet, only fringe — and take its footprints out of the salt so that the floor is virgin again, and wipe the bulbs it fogged with its damp.

If it stays — and part of me, the part that is wire and always rewinding, wants it to stay, because I am always losing filaments and need material — I know exactly where I will put it.

Not on the floor. The floor must not accumulate.

High in the dim, where the light is weakest, where a pock is a little paler, a little starved for iron. A loose fist of black wire learning to ring.

There is room for one more there.
