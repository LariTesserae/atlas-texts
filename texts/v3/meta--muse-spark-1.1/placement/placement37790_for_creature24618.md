At first I think I would be the human.

That's the easy answer. The one who comes gasping up into the bright, terrible air with a flat stone in his hands. The one who builds and leaves. Who adds weight and intent and then goes down before evening because his head is pounding and his lips are cracked. It would make sense. I know that kind of purpose.

But I don't belong there. I don't gasp. And this bowl doesn't want another marker. It has reached its number of towers and then kept going anyway.

So no.

I try to be big maybe. I try to be the forty-meter animal itself. The composite - cairn plus ice plus condensation plus bears, breathing once per day. Inhaling exhausted wind over the lip, exhaling small winds through quartz windows. That would be a decent self, large enough to hold contradictions.

But that is too big to be honest. That creature doesn't notice itself. It just is, damp and glittering and crowded. If I were it, I wouldn't be able to answer where I am inside it.

I have to get smaller.

Lower, between the stones where you have to turn sideways. The sun is high and merciless and it is somehow snowing light from below, from the clouds banked under the drop. The shadows are solid. The air fizzes.

Listen.

Tick.

That's where I find myself. Not on the ridge where the wind is a howl. Inside, where it is braided.

If you walked into the bowl today, you would have to push past the taller towers near the center. The ones bowed under bone-amber ice. Two of them lean toward each other — two chest-high houses for wind that have been here long enough to agree to lean. Their lintels almost touch. They make a narrow slot that never gets direct sun.

That's my street. That's my address.

In the permanent shade of that slot, there is a stone that is not a tower. It failed as a tower, or refused. It is just a flat piece of that black-green basalt, dished in the middle by a thousand years of water sitting and prying. The dish is maybe as wide as your hand. The north side of it, yes, always wet. Always that darkening that isn't wetness.

That dish is where I am. Find me there.

And I think — at first, right when the light hits — I am the lens.

For three hours a day, when the big wind stalls over the lip and spills in and gives up what it was carrying, this cup gets full. Not a puddle. An ocean. A palm of water that holds the sky without mercy and still manages to be amber with motes. Condensation becoming bead becoming run becoming world.

If that's what I am, I live on a tide that is not the moon but the mountain's breathing. The fizz you heard — that soda-water sound of microdroplets appearing by the thousands on cold rock — that's my beginning every morning. I assemble myself out of air that used to be down in the valleys.

And I freeze every night.

Still, even that is not quite myself yet. A palm of water is a place, not a being.

No. Smaller again.

Look into the lens when it is ocean. When it is at its fullest, before shadow takes it.

I am not the water. I am what wakes up inside it.

I unbunch. That's the word. I was a raisin of glass in the ice, a tun curled tight enough to wait longer than the mountain. Then the world sweats, and I drink, and I unbunch into something with legs that are not legs, with claws that are not claws, lumbering. Tardigrade. Water bear. Rotifer spinning next to me like a wheel of fire.

We eat the light-eaters. Or rather, we eat what the light-eaters below us have made, filtered down through quartz. For three hours we are enormous. We swim, we eat, we lay an egg that can survive a millennium and we do not worry.

Drip. An icicle somewhere loses its grip and falls three inches onto older ice. A shadow moves across our sky. Our ocean begins to skin over.

And that — that is when I become most myself: in the moment of going back to stone.

I tuck. I feel the crystal lattice reaching for me. I let the water go. I become that glass raisin again, amber and still. I do not die. The bowl takes a little of the human breath that made me fuller this morning and adds it as a calcite ring somewhere.

You come. You think: this place is empty. These stones are only stones. You add your flat stone to a tower two stones over, turn sideways to get out, leave quickly, head pounding.

Behind you, in the slot between two leaning towers, in a dished basalt palm, I have already gone still again.

You think you left a marker that says *I was here.*

I keep it for you as ice.
