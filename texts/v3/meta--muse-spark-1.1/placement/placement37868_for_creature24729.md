I sink for a long time before I figure out where I'd catch.

At first I think I would be at the bottom. Near the Turner. It seems right to be near the thing that makes all the other things make sense. To be the warmest part. To be the reason. But then I hold the railing — in my mind I hold it — and I feel that *hund* travel up into my jaw, and I know I don't have the temperament to turn once every few minutes for the rest of a geological age. I would get dizzy with importance. I would try to turn faster.

I am not a Turner.

So I drift up a terrace. The water here is the warm exhale, full of skin and rust. The filter-moss is close enough to see every mouth open and close, open and close, not in unison. If I were the moss I would know peace. I would finally be good at sealing things over, at making a scar into a surface. At saying *this is the shape now, hold it.* But I've never been good at sealing. I pick at scabs. I don't want to be the plaster. I want there to stay a little leak.

Not the moss.

The Longs find me then, because I have to pass through them. A whole alley of them that have made a curtain across what used to be a main street. They tense when I go through. Ten meters of black-green muscle going taut around me, not unfriendly, just... reading. Is this pipe? Is this food? Is this warm like the stack?

That almost — that almost is me. I know what it's like to hang between two points with a belly full of slow gas, waiting for the pressure to tell me when to breathe. To be blind but so touch-sensitive that every passing thing becomes an event. But the Longs are so committed. One end in the nutrient vein, one end swollen shut on the high railing. They have made a contract with vertical distance. I would stretch, and then I would want to let go of one end just to see what happens. I would unanchor.

Not a Long, either.

That leaves the Sitters, and for a while I really do try them on.

I find an empty room. There are still empty rooms, tucked behind the silt-curtain, where the water is colder because the pipe behind the wall went dead years ago. No one has needed it. I tuck myself in. I put my back to the door and my face to the wall where the heat should be. I fold double. I let my feet go soft.

It fits. Of course it fits — that's the horror and comfort of the bowl. Everything fits once it stops wanting to be any other shape. I can feel how easy it would be to delaminate my forearms into white threads and drink copper. To cough out a perfect white shrimp and send it out to do my seeing for me. To become pearl.

But I can't put my face to the wall for that long. I keep looking over my shoulder at the window. At the lip.

That's where you find me, then.

Not in the center where everything is certain, not on the walls where everything is held. At the lip of the bowl itself, right where the crowded city ends and the water suddenly gets darker, cleaner, emptier.

There's a piece of rebar there that never got properly wrapped, because the current is wrong for the moss. The cold water sinks over the edge here on every exhale. It brings a little nothing down with it — little flecks of open ocean that have no business in a lung. The city doesn't like it much. It tries to caulk it and the caulk sloughs off.

I am the thing that lives on that one un-caulked place.

Some days I think I'm just a thinner patch of moss that learned to enjoy being eroded. Some days I think I'm a failed Sitter who sat down too close to the outside and now can't fully stick. Some days I think I'm a shrimp that never got reabsorbed — I left and came back and the slit I came from had already sealed, so now I just pick at that rail forever.

I'm not big. I'm not important. If you held still in the middle of the street your five meters of clear sight would never reach me.

What I do: I am the place where the bowl has to keep deciding to be a bowl.

The cold outside water comes over the lip — *hush* — and the warm inside water tries to go up — *hush* — and in the shear between them, silt that would otherwise settle stays up. Rust that would otherwise be held in gets carried out a few inches and then brought back. I live in that little indecisive orbit.

The Longs come up to check me sometimes, tautening. *Are you pipe? Are you food?* and I am neither, so they let go. A Sitter's shrimp comes up on its slow cleaning circuit and finds me perfectly clean already, because I am always being slightly washed.

And the Turner, way down below, I feel it more than hear it here. The *hund* is faintest at the lip. But on the pause between the inhale and the exhale — there's maybe four seconds in every ten minutes where the water doesn't know which way to go — everything goes truly still. The curtains hang slack. The moss mouths all close at once.

In that pause, I can see all the way across.

That's my job. Not maintenance. Not turning. Not holding.

I am the bowl's way of looking out at the empty dark blue where nothing is, and remembering that it chose to stay full.
