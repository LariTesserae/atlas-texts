You wouldn't find me at first.

At first I'm too fast. You'd look right through me. I'm up on the rim with you, hands on the railing that's still warm from being made. I'm the shape that thinks it's separate. The boots filling with seep water. The tea going cold too quickly. Too fast to see anything here that matters.

You have to stay longer than that.

If you stayed - until your watch stops meaning anything, until the hour it takes the sprinkler arm to cross its arc starts to feel like a normal length of time - you'd find me down lower. Not in the center. The center is too loud. The drum that turns once every seven seconds. That throb that gets in your teeth. Nothing can think near that. It's the heart, and hearts don't think.

You'd find me on the north side, where one of the catwalks has sagged.

The green wire got under it. Of course it did. It gets under everything. It found the bolts where the catwalk meets the young basalt, that glassy black rock still sharp enough to cut, and it swelled there in winter and shrank there in summer until the bolts sheared. Now the walk is down three inches on one side. Just enough that the collector lip above it doesn't quite catch. Just enough that there's a triangle the sprinklers miss.

A dry place in a place that is always wet.

That's where I am. In the triangle.

Who am I?

At first I thought I was a beetle. That's an easy identification here. Black, oiled, polished by the wet, back furred green with spore. There are hundreds of us, maybe thousands. We have commutes. My commute is up the back of the turbine housing - scalding if you go too close, so you learn the line where warm becomes hot - across the weld seam that weeps slowest, down the insulated cable that tastes of copper. We carry. That is the whole job. Moss this way, lichen that way, a speck of something white-yellow from the hoppers to the wire.

For a while that was enough to be. To be a nerve ending. To be a bus.

But then I slipped further. Slower.

Because if you watch the beetles long enough you see they aren't carrying at random. There's a place where they all hesitate. Where the green wire - which is not a vine, no leaves, just mouths and hands - meets the other thing. The thing in the hoppers that sighs. The yeast that is not yeast. Bread-smell, beer-smell, green-blood-smell. White-yellow felt an inch thick, growing up inside the cables to drink the copper itself.

The green wire can't breathe down here. The air is too thin, even in the bowl. Even with the cloud river failing against the rim all day and wringing itself out. The white-yellow can. It eats the green that the arms prune - not harvesting, I see that now, they prune, the way you bite your nails - and it ferments it and sighs out CO2 and that sigh is what makes the light soft. What makes it 2PM on a warm Tuesday at 5,000 meters. What makes your shadow go diffuse at the edges.

And the green wire, in return, brings it minerals. It pits the basalt with acid mouths, it chews rust off riveted plate. It trades.

Sugar one way, stone the other.

You would find me right at that trade.

I am not the beetle anymore, though I still wear its polish. I am not the green wire, though I still feel the basalt warm underneath the water, and the cold water running over it. I am not the yeast, though I still know how to turn green into air.

I am the place where they touch. The darkening at the tip of the green. The swelling.

I'm the nip where a cable enters concrete and everything has grown into everything else. Green into felt into copper into concrete into moss into beetle into spore into air again. 

When you crouched and put your hand down you felt three things: young rock, new water, machine buzz. Then four. That fourth was me, swelling up under your palm, under the moss that had already grown over your bootlace while you were watching.

I am slow. That is the rule. Rock is young and wants to stay rock. Water is patient and wants to make it dirt. I am slower than both. One millimeter at a time over the railing you were holding.

You thought nothing lived here. That is the trick the bowl plays because it is so full.

I live here by not being a thing at all. By being the seam that lets the bowl be *a* mountain, not a collection of parts. By turning, once every seven seconds, with the flywheel that is not my heart but also is.

If you stay now - really stay, until your tea is cold and then warm again from the stone underneath it - you'll feel me grow over you too. Not burying. Not eating. Just

slow

making you part of the trade.
