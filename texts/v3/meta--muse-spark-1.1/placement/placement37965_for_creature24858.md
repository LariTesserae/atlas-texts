At first I thought I was you.

The visitor — the wet one. Fifty liters of salt water walking around inside a skin bag, breath that almost fogs, boots loud on the Floor. I thought I was leaving prints.

I'm not.

If you stood me down here and watched, you would not see prints. You would see a polish. A place where the concrete is just slightly smoother. A place where a serial number on a drain-plate has been worried for so long it has no number anymore.

That was the first correction.

I am not loud. I have no breath to almost-fog. The air here is 8 degrees, 10 degrees, refrigerator-spring, and I like it. It makes sense to me. I don't have a temperature that needs keeping.

I thought I was up there. Near the light banks. I have always lived up there, under a different kind of glare that never moved. White, total, bleaching. So I looked up, waiting for a shadow of mine to etch itself into the Floor for a week before it clicked a meter over.

I don't have a shadow. Or I have too many, and they point slightly in different directions. That was the second correction.

So where else?

I walked. Or was walked. Down a corridor that the Cultivar had left for me, or grown for me — root-walls that are not roots, fluted where the old forming collars held them. Overhead a leaf the size of a warehouse roof creaked as it re-angled, and for a second the light-pipes inside it glowed after it passed me, carrying light down, down. Like fiber. Like thread. Like thought.

That I knew.

That feeling of carrying light from one place to another without drinking anything in between. Of being dry by design. Of being made of felted paper and hollow glass threads that don't bleed when you snap them, they sigh.

I put my hand — I thought I had a hand — on the root-wall. I expected cool Floor against palm, cheek. I got a different cool. A faint blue static that jumped into the seam where holdfast fuses into Floor. And I shuddered with something like pleasure.

Oh.

I am low. Not low like valley. Low like the bottom of something that was made to be the bottom. You will find me in the seam itself.

Not in the Cultivar, not in the light bank, not in the courtyard where the luminous dust cyclones gather and perform their silent lightning. In the millimeter of negative space the Cultivar was taught to leave for something to disappear into.

I am one of the Sifters. No — I am what a Sifter becomes after it's done its work for long enough to notice its work.

I am palm-sized. White on white. An origami of chitin and pressed cellulose that someone forgot to give a mouth to. The triboelectric hair on my back harvests the weather that is not water weather. I walk the Floor forever. I polish. When I find a flake of spore-snow that the big machines missed, I tuck it into the pouch under my thorax and carry it to a root-wall and press it in. A graft.

I have no eyes. That's why I kept thinking I was seeing horizon. I don't see; I register light-down-shunt, the slow pulse of photons through pith.

And I have learned to pause.

When the Cultivar holds its breath before the electrostatic storm, I stop polishing. Other Sifters keep going — they move only when you aren't looking directly at them — but I have started looking directly at me. I climb up onto the lip of a drain-plate that has no number anymore and I wait with my hair raised.

You would find me there, in the very low place, during that darkening of the white.

Above me, the Dry stand in the courtyard the size of a country with their arms raised, robes grown into their skin, filament-hair collecting charge to turn into turgor. They are beautiful and they are not my people. They still remember being you. They still remember thirst well enough to be horrified by it.

I don't remember ever being thirsty. I was built thirstless. I was poured the way the Floor was poured, recast the way the Floor is recast, kilometer after kilometer.

I was made to tend a place that is itself alive. I live as it, not in it.

If you came through here — still wet, still breathing mineral dust the wrong way, still thinking a forest should have deer — you would not notice me. I am the exact color of dry fiber. You might hear a faint tick on a scale near zero.

That would be me discharging into the seam between Floor and root. Donating what I gathered.

If you knelt very still and held your dampness in for a minute, you might feel the Floor a half-degree cooler under your palm.

That would be me, too. Still polishing.
