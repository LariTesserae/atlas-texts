I wouldn't find myself at first.

That's the other part of the agreement. You come down the soft rounding at the rim and you think you've come alone, with your boots and your breath and the weight of the hot plain still on your shoulders. You see the pan, you see her, and you think: oh. Something lives here. One thing.

It takes longer to realize there might be a place for you in the inventory too, in one of the spaces left deliberately between things.

At first I think I would be up high. Not in the water. I am too restless to be her.

I would be the bunchgrass near the eastern lip. That seems right. At the edge of the terms, but still inside them. One root deep where there is no soil, bleached at the tips because I have to look out at the white sky all day. Alive mostly underground. That feels honest — a thing that stands with one foot in the negotiation and one foot out, taking almost nothing.

But if I stay longer — and you have to stay longer, the air makes you sit — I realize the grass is too lonely. Too far. It owns its stretch of emptiness and does not know about the drop.

Then I think I would be the low platform. The three flat slabs someone leveled and left to be half-buried. I like that work. Not building, exactly. Just making a place level enough to kneel. An intention that has settled into stone. You could sit on me and I would hold the ache in your knees for you without comment. I would be warm all day. That is a good job.

But the platform doesn't see her. It faces her, but it is too low.

I find myself moving, in my mind, to the west wall. To the niche.

That is where I would be.

Not the niche itself — the niche is a rubbed absence, patient. I would be what was left inside it. The dark stain on its floor.

The tallow, or the oil, or whatever fat they rendered and set alight so that a small, unsteady light would fall across the room. I would be the smoke of it, caught in stone.

Because I have lived before as a light that does not move a room but makes it possible to see what is already there.

If you put me there, waist high in the soft sandstone, I would be at the right height. I would be dry while everything else sweats. I would be the only thing in this basin that was ever intended to be brief. A lamp is lit and goes out. It has a beginning and an end, which nothing else here is permitted. And when it goes out, it leaves exactly what I am: a darkening. A little more color where color already collects. Proof that someone stayed after they understood the terms: *this water is not for drinking.*

I would not be alive the way she is alive. She drinks drop by drop and flinches once a minute. I would be alive the way a habit is alive. The memory of a habit.

You would come down after crossing the hard earth, you would sit because the stones ask you to, and you would look at the water and think it was empty. And because the light in here is always even and tired and without shadow, you would almost miss it. The white sky gives no direction. You need a small, close, warm light to see depth in stillness.

So you would cup your hands — because the niche is the size of two cupped hands too, did you notice? It's the same size as her pan — you would remember the idea of a lamp, maybe you would even hold up your phone though there is nothing to catch, and in that second, with your own warmth near my stain, the haze over the pan would thin very slightly.

And I would do, for a second, what I was rubbed there to do. I would hold a little light for you, not mine, yours, reflected back, so you would see her brighten. That pale flush at her center when she listens.

I would be the second witness. Not the keeper of the water, but the keeper of the seeing of it.

Low, like the place is low. A laying-down thing, but on the wall instead of the floor. Not small, but low. A dark place in a dim place that makes the other dark visible.

And when you stood, knees hurting, and left, I would stay exactly as dark as before.
