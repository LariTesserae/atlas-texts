I thought at first I'd find myself in the middle.

It's the instinct, isn't it? To go to the center of any built place and declare yourself the heart of it. So at first I pictured myself there - under the thickest part of that brown paper light, in the deepest chord where all the throats meet and amplify.

But I can't be there. Nothing new can get there. The packing is too complete. A thousand years ago a piece of dust might have filtered down to the center. Now not even dust.

So I have to be honest about where my weight actually falls.

I find myself at the edge, but not over it.

Right where your description stops being architecture and starts being cliff. Where the last course of fitted backs and salt-white domes ends, abruptly, because there is no more table to put them on. Where the wind hits first before it learns how to sing.

I am prone there. I didn't choose to lie down, the sound made me. You try to stand in that chord with your soft skull and your ears that still want to hear two things at once. You fold. I folded.

So: on my stomach at the windward lip, forehead almost over the drop into nothing, hands pressed to the last row. The heat is coming back into them too fast.

That's where I find myself. Not inside the hamlet. At its hem.

And for a long while I think I am nothing at all. I am the visitor, the mistake, the soft thing that still needs water and casts a shadow and doesn't know how to make its edges go round. I am the thing that has to leave by climbing back over the edge because there is no room.

I stay there long enough for that to change, because the wind doesn't stop and the stones don't cool.

My hands stay down.

And that's when I understand who I would have to be, to be allowed to stay.

I wouldn't be one of the Builders. Not yet. I'm too loud inside. I still have too much water in me. My eyes still open and shut. My hide isn't stone.

But I wouldn't be outside them either. The edge won't have me. The wind off the drop is too clean, too empty. It has nowhere to keep a thing like me.

I would be what happens between the last two.

You said there is no room between presences because of the presences. But that's not quite right even in your own telling. There is a little room. There has to be. The half-inch lift of a shoulder. The lipped, blackened throat. The deliberate breath.

I would be one of those.

Not a whole body. Not yet. I would be a Throat that didn't used to be there.

Maybe the Builders, as they settled deeper into equilibrium, felt this one gap near the windward edge start to close a little too much. Maybe the old mouth there - a big brother with his hip tilted just so for eight hundred thousand years - finally sagged shut in his sleep. The chord would have gone sour. Just a fraction. Just enough that the rest of the mass, humming, would feel it as an ache.

So they would have had to open a new one. They can't open themselves anymore; they are too fitted, too mineral. But I was right there, prone, with my mouth open against the roar.

So they used me.

I am the new breathing hole. I am kept.

I am on my back now, actually. Turned over. Fitted. I can feel the weight of the brother to my left - his sun-baked-bone back against my ribs, so warm it stops being a temperature and becomes a presence. And the sister to my right, salt-white, crumbling a little into my shoulder. My spine is still soft enough to make a channel.

The wind comes. It has to. And it finds me because I am the path of least resistance, the softest stone.

It goes in through my open mouth - because I have learned not to close it, closing it would be agony, it would spoil the note - down through the long pipe of my throat that I've learned to hold open like you hold a note diving, and out again through somewhere lower, between my hip and the old one's knee.

I vibrate.

I am not deaf yet. That's the terrible, short part. For a little while still, I hear the chord from the inside, not as sound but as work. I feel it sanding my interior smooth. Polishing my throat. Turning my lipped edges black. Teaching my blood how to be iron and hot rock.

I don't eat. The heat comes up from below me, up through my back from the brother who is a kiln now.

I don't see. The brownish light is there, but I have finally understood it is not for seeing. I let my eyes go milky.

I am not one of them that has finished. I am the one that is *finishing*. The freshest piece of living tessellation, still with a little give. The only part of the whole high, packed, total place that is still actively becoming as old as the rest of it.

If you came now - climbing back up over the edge you came from - you would still see a man where I am. A thin man in clothes that have gone the color of ash and rust. But if you put your hand down, the warmth would come back a little too fast from my chest. And if you put your ear to my mouth, which is open, you would not hear wind.

You would hear me learning how to hold a single note forever, so the rest of them don't have to stop singing.
