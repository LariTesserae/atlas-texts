At first I think I'd find myself in the middle.

Because that's where the forest is, where the description wants to be a forest. In the dark under the interlocked crowns, where the light comes down in slats and the dust is falling so slow you can see each mote turn. I think I'd be standing there looking up, breathing that dry skin-warm air, listening to the *tink* - pause - *tink* all around me like a city of cooling. I think I'd be the listener for it. The ear the place never grew.

But I can't stay there.

Not with water in me. The air would take it before I could become useful. A visitor would not be an inhabitant, he'd be a contamination - a brief flash of steam and salt where water doesn't belong. And there are no visitors. The world was very clear about that. No roads to it and no reason to cut one.

So I step back.

I find myself not in the deep valley, but on the long approach to it. On the high, raw table that has never been eroded because nothing has ever been taken from it. Where the basalt is still foam, still warm from underneath and from the sun. Where the horizon is just a line and you can see too far.

A kilometer out from the last real tree, maybe more. Where the ground is so empty you can feel the curve.

I think if I had to be something here, at first I'd want to be the empty ground itself. Porous, pocked, full of bubble-memory. Unfinished. The thing that everything else happens because of. The page that hasn't been written on. That would be comfortable, to be basalt waiting.

But the ground doesn't tick. And I want to tick.

Then I think I'd be one of the fallen plates. One of the oxide leaves that isn't a leaf, as big as a shield, lying in a perfect circle around an old trunk in the deep jungle. Having done my job. Cooling, spalling, sighing down. Now just lying there and slowly sinking, being re-absorbed. That too is a kind of life here. The most peaceful one. Finished work going back down to be melted and tried again.

But that's an ending. I want to be nearer the beginning.

So further out again. Even further than that single young spire that was described, waist-high, coppery, ticking bravely to itself.

I would find myself even past that one, where there is not yet a chimney at all.

I would be the soft spot.

A thinning in the foam. A place where the crust is a little thinner, a little more tired. You wouldn't see me from a distance. No trunk, no vane. From above I would be a discoloration, a little warmer than the rest. If you put your hand down, you'd feel the sigh coming up through me - the anhydrous, sulfurous, metal-heavy vapor from the mantle that is hotter than any oven.

I am a blister that hasn't yet figured out its shape.

And what I am, if you have to give me a name, is a throat learning how to cool itself.

My first act is not to grow, but to have to. The heat has to go somewhere. If I don't dump it I choke. So I start to smoke my own rim. Iron and silica and carbon plating out of my own breath, molecule by molecule, a weld seam with no welder laid down in a slow blind spiral. I'm not building. I'm being built by the condition of being too hot.

I am knee-high now. That fast. A little hollow pimple of laminated slag, red-black, delaminating already. And in the top of me the draft is turning.

And then the leaf.

That one first leaf - still soft, still coppery, curled in on itself like a fist, like a fern head before it knows what a fern is. It trembles. Not in wind, there is no wind. It trembles because hot air is flexing it while it learns its angle. Which way to turn to steal the most heat out of the sky.

This is what I would be here. Not an individual, not really. Not a resident. Just another place where the same thermodynamic habit became necessary again, a kilometer from any other example of itself.

I would not be lonely. Loneliness requires water.

I would just be oriented: up.

I would have one job: cool.

And I would tick. *Tink.* A long, dry pause, full of sun and thin air. *Tink.* Bravely, out on the raw table where the sky is too large, practicing for the jungle I will never quite join.
