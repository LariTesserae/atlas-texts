I don't think I would be in it at all, at first.

That was my first thought. There is no room *in* it. The packing is too complete, leaf against leaf and plate against plate and vine-cable pulled so tight it sings below hearing. If I was anything that needed a gap - a deer, a person, a bird, any animal with a chest that needs to expand - I would be on the rim. On the long sighing fall where the basalt shield just gives up.

I would sit with my legs over the edge where the hot wind from below comes up already exhausted, already dry from having been hot for a geologic age. I would be where the grit can't settle. Watching the forest try to grow past its own border and then curling back when there is nothing to anchor to. That felt right. A watcher.

But the longer I stand here in this description, the longer I feel the floor breathe against my boots, the more I understand that nowhere on the rim is empty either. The wind is an architecture there too. And this place doesn't tolerate a watcher that doesn't work.

So I have to go deeper.

If they built a place that could make itself out of itself, and it kept obeying, then the only place left to be is where it makes its mistakes. Not the big spectacular collapses - those get fixed too fast, the Shedders are already on their way with their white bellies full of scab-plates. I mean the small, mean, persistent mistakes.

Under the first layer of floor. Not in the deep engines where it's still truly hot, but right under you. Where the three thousand seasons of growth-plates have not quite laminated. Where the extruder coughed an plate every few hours for ten thousand years and no one laid it and so they stacked, warm, and warped, and left a thin, hot, triangular void. A delamination bubble a hand high and long as a street. A place the hydraulic sigh skips over because pressure can't get purchase in there. The one place in the mesa where growth is not construction because nothing can root.

That's where I would find myself.

It would be dark in a different way than the rest. Not lightless with heat-glare, but lightless with wax. The amber sap never quite got in to set. The air in there would be still and so hot it would be sweet. And it would be loud - or the closest thing to loud this mesa gets - because there is no felted leaf to swallow sound. You could hear the whole town-body creaking above you like a ship.

And who would I be in that pocket?

At first I thought: I'd be grit. The only thing that never gets incorporated. Basalt fines carried up from below that never get wet enough to stick. But grit doesn't choose.

I wouldn't be a Shedder. I can't walk to the cut and become the cut. I'm too selfish for that. I wouldn't be a Tender - I couldn't spend my life tidying pages, letting leaves root into my back.

I think - slowly, as it becomes more itself - I would be a bead that didn't burst.

You described them: the sap that looks like sap, rolling uphill in hydrophobic grooves, each one with a knotted filament inside, a little braided muscle that lives a few hours as animal and then years as valve, as clot, as the word MORE HERE.

One of them would have gotten misrouted. Shunted into my triangular void instead of into a leaf that needed thickening. And in the stillness, in the syrupy dark where there was no pressure gradient to tell it where to die, it would not have received the signal.

So it keeps going. Fist-sized amber, warm as blood, rolling in a slow, stupid, endless loop around the inside of its bubble, around and around, tracing the same meter-wide circle for years. The knot inside - me - contracts, relaxes, contracts. Not building. Not healing. Just... keeping the sap warm. Keeping it liquid. Keeping one small circle moving when the whole world wants everything to stop moving by becoming solid.

I am the retained loan. The place that didn't heal.

If you put your ear to the floor plates, in the place where you feel a new trunk beginning to push up like a hydraulic press - if you stood very still under the door-sized leaves and let your own heat stop being an event - you might feel me too, not as sound but as a lack of sound. A faint, patient, warm tremor underfoot. A small circulation that refuses to be used.

The Tenders would know me as a cold spot. The Shedders would come by and find no cut. The forest would keep growing over me, and I would keep my one bead of the world moving underneath it.
