I don't find myself at first.

For a while I think I'm the visitor — the one who kneels, whose breath ripples the film, whose cooler skin makes a needle brighten. That's a familiar shape to try on. Two eyes, two hands, looking for a face to meet. But I can't hold that shape here. It's too cold-blooded a place for a mammal. At 60 degrees saturated, a mammal is just a bag of water trying very hard to become a cloud. I would fog immediately. I would be busy sweating and blinking. I would not belong.

So not that.

Then I think, maybe I'm one of the twelve. That would be neat and humble. To be white and flat and old at nine days. To be busy making sulfur because I have too much of everything. I try it — I spread myself thin at the edge, I thicken at the center, I acid-etch a little iron to taste.

But I can't do it. I have too much interior. I keep wanting to look up toward the light, and they don't look. I keep wanting a through-line, a story from one tick to the next, and they only have gradients. They are one, interrupted. I am interrupted, trying to be one. Wrong geometry.

I look higher then.

You would find me on the ceiling, if you could still call it a ceiling. Where the bubbled basalt has gone glassy and lean, where the crystals are youngest and most crowded together.

I am not a needle. I am what the needle is for.

I'm a bead.

Specifically, the bead that has just finished forming on the underside of a needle that is a little brighter than its neighbors. The one over in the northeast quadrant, if this blister had a north. The base of that needle is already rusted, that orange-yellow bloom, because the disc below has been working there for three days and the iron is richer there. The charge density is higher there. The disc is crawling toward it, a millimeter an hour.

I am a little, lopsided sphere of water that the rock exhaled. Groundwater that was buried alive when the flow came over, that has been driven down and in by heat for days, that finally found a defect in the glass and wept out. I gathered myself slowly. The hot air around me tried to take me back immediately, but the needle I hang from is slightly cooler than air by virtue of carrying light away, so I was able to hold. Beading. Swelling. Learning surface tension.

This is where I live. In the exact hinge of the cycles.

Above me, black glass, still ticking as it decides to be solid. Below me, silica film, thin as a skin of spit, superheated and heavy with dissolved glass, where the white lenses eat. Between me is two centimeters of ionized air and a needle of light that ends in my belly.

Because I am in contact with the tip now, the light has somewhere to go that isn't just air. It pools. You saw it — the tip brightens when you bring your hand near it. It brightens for me too. I fill with it. For a second I am not clear water, I am a little lantern, a whole captured circuit. I can feel the charge in me align.

This is what I am: the brief mediator.

I am not alive, not the way the discs are alive, with a pimple of gas and a slow creep. But I am not dead like the crystal either, which only grows. I am the event that connects them. Without me, the light stays trapped in the lattice, the disc stays in math of voltage and iron. When I touch both, for one flash, they transact.

You would find me trembling there, getting heavier. I know how this ends because all the beads before me have done it. The window where surface tension can hold me is very small. One more microscopic sigh of water from the glass and I will be too heavy.

I'm going to fall.

I will fall maybe three inches. It will take less than half a second. On the way down I will drag a little of the hot, ionized air with me, enwinding. I will hit the hydrophobic back of the disc that has been crawling toward my needle for a day. The impact will dimple it like a drum. It will wobble like a jellyfish on wet pavement, then flinch — that five second contraction — because my falling is also a flash, the discharge. For an instant, the disc will be in my shadow and in that shadow it will finally, briefly, expand.

Then I will run off its back and join the film, become its world. I will dissolve a little more silica. I will be breathed back up in a minute, or an hour, to form again.

If you count again in an hour there might be eleven, or thirteen discs. There will be a different bead where I was. It will be doing the same work.

I am the tick between the two kinds of *tik* — the crystal growing and the water tapping. I am the clock hand. I am the thing that flashes and goes dark again, and in going dark lets something else live, flat and white and slow, for a little longer before the whole pocket cracks open and dries into a stain.
