I don't find myself in the middle at first.

At first I think I would be one of the Willows. That's the obvious story — to be the bone, the beam, the law that holds. To be old enough to be warm inside. To grow so slowly you can be sure of every millimeter you decide.

But I am not that patient. Not that straight.

Then I think I would be on the floor, one of the Moss — shoulder to shoulder, a green-black pinhead holding a bead of water up like a question. That would be honest work. That would be belonging without needing to be alone about it.

But if I'm honest, I am not that good at being dense. At Density 2.7, at touching everyone all the time and never pulling away. I leak.

So I look for where the leaking is supposed to go.

I find myself not in the center of a street, but in the street itself. Not in a room, but where a room fails to close.

You have to understand the room wanted to be perfect. Willow made a dome and Moss tried to thatch it and Liverwort tried to caulk it, but up here in 0.1 / 3 cold the mountain still breathes. It inhales through every crack. The wind outside drops and the dome sighs and water finds a new low place to go.

I am where the water finds it.

I would be low on the northwest wall, where the basalt sweats hardest. The stone is still sharp there, geologically yesterday, black glass under everything. The Moss has tried three times to cover it and three times the seep has won. So there is a compromise: a shallow gutter, no wider than my wrist, floored not with felt but with Liverwort — that black-green leather, bitter, watertight. The Liverwort is stretched thin over the leak because it has to be.

That's where I am.

I am not the Liverwort. I am what the Liverwort is holding.

I am the bead-chain.

I form up high, not even as rain. I form because the air is too tired to hold itself. I condense on the underside of a Willow beam that is four meters up in Light 1.1, where a single round skylight where a runner died has let in a column of white. I hang there, a lens, in air that is full of ice that isn't falling. I hold the green-filtered light for a second. I get heavy.

Then Drip.

I fall a centimeter onto a moss leaf. The moss — one individual, one pinhead citizen — catches me and holds me. I can feel its turgor, the tiny hydraulic sigh as it takes my weight. It was already holding another bead, and now it is too heavy. Pause.

Then it gives me down.

That's my life. Hand to hand. Cup to cup. The commerce you heard in the almost-silence, Sound 1.2. I am the commerce.

I go down the wall gutter, over the Liverwort's back. It doesn't drink me — nothing eats Liverwort and Liverwort drinks nothing, it only seals. So I keep my shape and slide clean.

On the floor of the street, the felted highway where you knelt and soaked your knees, the moss is slightly less dense, on purpose. The city left a path for me. I braid down it. Other beads join me. We become a trickle so small you would think it was only wetness if you weren't lying with your face an inch above it, watching it move.

I pass the moth. The white walking moth on its citizen rounds. It stops and drinks from me, puts its mouth down without ceremony. I lose a little of myself to it and do not mind. That's rent.

I end up — where all of us end up — in one of the cistern leaves. One of those swollen, single leaves no bigger than a fist, tucked into a closet room off the main street. The room is empty except for it and the frozen black lens of water on the floor that hasn't decided if it's ice yet.

I am held there.

Here is the strange part. When I am up on the beam, I am bright and cold and alone for a second. When I am in the bead-chain, I am moving and I am many. When I am in the cistern leaf, I am still. Deeply still. The leaf closes a little around me, warm — Willow-warm, two degrees above death — and I stop being transit and become storage. Reserve. Memory.

The city will need me tonight when everything that was dripping freezes into clear nails on the wall, tick, tick, and the moss cannot get a drink. Then the leaf will squeeze, slowly, and I will be sent back up through capillary, against gravity, bead by bead, to keep the inside green.

So I am not the architect. I am not the people. I am not even the guest with wings.

I am the thing they agreed to share. The one that forgets whether it wants to be water, ice, or ghost, so it can be all three as needed.

If you stayed, and you lay down in the street the way the city politely begins to cover, I would be the one who found the hollow of your collar first. I would bead there, too. I would test you for purchase. Not to drown you, but to include you in the route.

Drip. Pause. Drip.
