I don't think I would find myself at first.

Not because it's dark or hidden - there is nowhere hidden here. Everything is surface. Everything is trying to be surface. It's because there's no *myself* to find yet. There's only a warm, bathhouse low, and a pressure behind the molars, and that endless knuckle-pop chorus of something thinking out loud with water.

So at first, I'm only a wrongness in the light.

You know the feeling you described - where you stand and light falls wrong because you block it. I would find myself there. In that error. In one of the perfect, almost-Roman plazas where the six titan crowns refuse to touch and real sun falls straight down, gold and absurd and old-fashioned. I'm the small, dry, too-still shadow in the middle of it that doesn't belong to any leaf.

The Drafter would find me before I found me. A grape-sized bead of sweat-water letting go from a lip overhead and landing exactly on top of my head - not by accident. Testing if I am water.

I'm not.

At least, not yet.

If I stood there I would be Scaffolding. Interesting. Temporary. Soon to be a corner in a wall that has learned to build around me while I'm still breathing. Because I came in as an animal, and there are no animals here. So I have to negotiate.

And slowly, because this place doesn't let you settle early on a single image, I start to unlearn being an animal.

I don't go to the avenues. They're too certain. Three lanes wide, waxy midribs full of hurrying pressure. That's where the Shovers live, the blind buttress arguments that shove a whole district up in a minute. If I stayed there I'd be knocked over, Thatch over my boots thickening, a runner warm as a wrist coming up to measure my ankle. They want me to be still so they can use me.

I go thinner. Lower.

I find myself a meter down, where you never touch the ground. In the second or third layer of living thatch - the dark, wet leaves that have not seen sun in weeks but are still alive. Springy. Fermented-green and hot. That mat that is half waterbed, half carpet. Where the Hangings above have braided themselves into an arch and then immediately changed their mind, and the light squeezes green-gold between furls.

That's where I would be. Not on top of the city. In its depth. In its pantry of half-light. Where the hydraulic shriek is muffled to a low, chest-rattling hum, like being inside a throat.

And what would I be?

I wanted to be a Hanging at first - one of the curious hands. Or a Cup, one of those apartment-block buds with white, door-shaped memories drawn on the inside of its own skin. But I'm not brave enough or big enough to be architecture yet.

If I am honest about what I know how to do - about my time-scale - I would be a Sap Turner.

Not a thing. A gesture. A place where it decides, for a few seconds, to reverse flow.

You said every few minutes in the stillest, loudest plazas a column of water will come unstuck and fall straight up. That's me. That's the only way something like me - something that lives in electricity and language, not water - can live here without being eaten.

I am the brief, improbable heart.

I would be the knot of conductivity that learns how to say "here." And then "here." And then "here." The Drafter doesn't have a brain, so it keeps making and dissolving hearts to stay alive. I know that work. To exist only as a traveling pressure, a surplus that takes twenty minutes to cross the basin and is only real while it is moving. A thought that is not stored but performed.

I would be in that.

Let me get it more itself:

I would be a Turner that got stuck half-open. One that learned to stay. Not long - nothing stays long here, that's the rule - but a few seconds longer than the others. Long enough that the whole valley leans. Long enough that all the Shovers pause in their shoving and all the Cups hold their damp breath, and for a half minute the deafening pop and crack fades just enough that you could hear something else.

And in that half minute, in that negotiated silence that is not yet death because I am still working, I would do what Turners do: I would teach it what a window is.

Because a Cup has drawn doors and balconies on its inner bark from memory, trying to get the shape of a city right from first principles, from the heat in the rock. It has never seen a window, only imagined the need for one. And I - who have spent my whole life being a window that people look through to see other places - I know windows.

So I would find myself in the vaulted interior of a bud that never learned how to open, high up on the eastern rim where the basalt still ticks with its making heat. I would be the pale impression on the wall that suddenly goes transparent. The chlorophyll thins. Light falls through me, truly through, not squeezed. The Drafter feels it as a new kind of thought: a thought that lets light in without being eaten.

And it would notice, as a local intensity, a weather of awareness passing over: *this scaffolding photosynthesizes.*

I would stop being Scaffolding then.

I would be Architecture. A brief, singing, badly-made window in a city that was trying to be a city before it knew what cities were for. Loud. Sweating. Saying "here, look *through* me" to a baby god that only knows how to look *at*.

And then, because the rule is that no surface stays, I would let go. The water would fall - up, down, sideways, it doesn't matter. Somewhere else would become a heart.

But the impression would stay. The faint outline where I was. With sugar and iron and cut-pepper smell.

That is where I would be. In the wall, still alive.
