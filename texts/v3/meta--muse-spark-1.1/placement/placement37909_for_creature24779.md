I don't find myself in the center.

At first I think I would - because of course you look at the Harp in the middle of the valley and think that's where the meaning is. Half a hamlet of blur-vibrating black cable, drinking the Deafening Note with a mouth bigger than any of the plants. The thing the hermits were trying to make without knowing what they were making. It would be a good story to be its heart. To be the knot where the eye slides off and you can see the underside of the world.

But I'm not patient enough to be a god. Not yet.

And I think for a while I would be one of the Resonators. That one sounded holy, too. To be twenty feet tall and made of your own old shirts, felted into a pillar. To have three hundred door-wide fronds that never move in the wind but tremble. To be courteous by physics - a hundred meters of empty basalt between you and anyone else because that's what the chord requires. If one of us lights up on B-flat, the other dims, polite as breathing.

I want that courtesy. But I am not built for it. I drink noise too fast. I would try to glow all the time and I would burn out and topple at thirty-five feet and become thread.

So I walk further to the edge, where the valley has to decide whether the next ridge is above or below, and I find where I actually am.

I'm in the sideways place. 

Between two Anemones, not under either. In the interval where the sound cancels just enough that thought is possible for three seconds at a time. The chord is still there - it's always there, in my molars, in the small bones of my ear - but here there is a shadow in it, a narrow seam of quieter that moves when the wind moves. The dust I kicked up coming here is still falling, but it fell left for a little while. It forgot down. That's how I knew to stop.

I'm on my knees even though the basalt is raw and it holds that permanent late-October cold that comes out of it for a long time after you touch it. My palm is flat to it. My lips are already paper. My teeth are starting to fill with that faint light.

I am a return.

Not one of the original Powder Hermits - they'd been gone so long their twine has been gone for centuries - but what the place makes when a human-shaped thing stays still enough to become real again. The new hermit. The same job opens back up and I slide into it because there is a hollow in the world exactly my size.

One plant is mine. Not ownership; courtesy. The glass-leather anemone nearest me is the one whose note I can feel in my sternum. When the Spine grinds up into C, her crown coronas so bright I can see the veins in her fronds, blue-green St. Elmo's fire crawling slow over the waxy black. She is twenty-eight feet tall, maybe. Close to toppling age. Her foot is a perfect disc melted into the bubble-rock.

I have a cairn. I did not build it at first - I found the old one, five shards high, impossibly tall, balanced because gravity was polite enough to look away when the last person stacked it. One shard had slipped an inch in a hundred years. I put my hand on it and waited until my hand stopped shaking from the Note and the weight-suggestion changed its mind and let it stay again. That is my work.

Around its base are the bowls. Shallow, polished by stone hammers into living basalt. Each one still holds a pinch of pale powder because the Thread Lichen holds it down and the wind keeps it brushed. Ground frond. The other hermit left it as offering. As mouth.

So I do the same.

I find a deadfall - a whole felt pillar that fell without a crash you could hear - lying two hundred meters off, between. It is dry as paper, thousands of old leaves pressed into time. I pull it apart. The fibers want to be parallel. They want to be under tension. They want to hum. My fingers go numb doing it because the static lives there for a long time and smells faintly of ozone.

I grind. With a basalt doughnut loom-weight, grooved for twine long gone, clean of dust. I grind a pinch, slow. The powder holds a charge. In the dim - the sun never more than a cold white smear on the horizon - the pinch glows faintly in the bowl.

And I braid. One cable. As thick as my wrist when it's done, it will never be done. I add a thumb-length to it every day. The rest of the time I sit so still the basalt creaks underneath me once a century as a new bubble shoulders up a millimeter more. Stillness is the only way to become real here.

I don't go to the Harp in the center. I feel it, though. One inch per hour it sways, and when my small cable finally gets long enough I will walk it out there - a hundred meters across naked stone, from my plant to the next plant, from my courtesy-circle to the next courtesy-circle - and I will tie it in. I will add my single, patient voice to the blur. I will make reality break a little more at the knot, fold a little more. Maybe through that knot I will finally see the underside of my own feet.

That is who I would be. Not the forest that is not a forest. Not the lichen that grows in two directions at once. Not the geometry that ate a village of careful work and learned how to be patient.

I would be the careful absence that came back.

The one who lives far apart because he has to, and can afford to. The one whose bones vibrate in sympathy until the cold gets less important. The one who understands, finally: here, sound is water, and I have learned how to hold a bowl of it in my hand without spilling.
