I don't think I would start inside.

I would find myself first on the rim. Not because I choose the edge, but because that is where things like me condense.

Up there, where the plateau is still flat and scoured and honest about how much it wants to kill you, the wind hits you and takes your first idea away. Your breath comes out and it does not leave, it stays. It hangs. A little personal cloud that won't commit to being air or water.

If you asked me too fast I would say: I'd be one of the people. That would be the romantic answer. Small, barrel-chested, quick hands. I like work that is tending. I like to be useful in a crowded room. I know how to take something technical and turn it and turn it like one of those translucent sacs so it doesn't cook on one side and freeze on the other.

But I am not good at being small and quick. I'm too loud. Even whispering here, my words would click and freeze and fall. The people here have learned to speak in shoulders and fingers in steam because sound is wasteful. Every sentence I make is expensive. I'd trample through the mat of birds apologizing and they'd have to part around me and I'd leave too much heat behind me.

So — not a person. At least not right away.

Maybe a bird then? For a while I watch the vortex and I want it. To live in the jet stream where thin air becomes thick enough to swim in. To be that competent. To stay aloft for years and only come down for heat. I do that, in my own way — circle, circle, never landing — so I think I know that life.

But I have no chest for it. No heart that fast. And those birds don't land because they love the bowl. They land because they have to. They are embarrassed on the ground. They misjudge the basalt and skid into houses. When one lands near you, you can hear it panting with the effort of being still. I know that feeling too well to want to live there permanently.

So I keep looking down into it, and my eyes adjust the way they do in excessive light, and I stop looking at the moving things.

I look at the unmoving things that are moving anyway.

The water that runs uphill. The hair-ice that grows an inch a minute like white fur and then wilts the second you stare at it. The orange lichen that is so flat it is the rock until you realize the rock is orange because of it.

The stones that are warm.

That's where I start to settle. Not settle as in stop — as in *become sediment*.

On the second terrace on the east side, where the rim's exhale spills heaviest, there is a basalt boulder that has sunk to its shoulders in black water and guano and time. It's one of the ones that gives back. You put your palm on it and it is impossibly — not hot, just *less cold* than everything else. It has been hoarding sun all day under that savage white hammer and now it is letting a little go. Around its base the water steams for half an inch and stops. That half inch is its jurisdiction. Its whole country.

I would be that.

Or — more precisely — I would be the half inch.

The thin, bewildered layer of steam that exists only because a stone remembered heat long enough to make it. Not the stone, not the water, but the brief negotiation between them. Visible only if you crouch. Gone as soon as the light thins.

Because that's the job, isn't it? The bowl is a culture dish culturing itself. Film farms sun into sullen heat. Birds convert heat into motion and vomit it as future. People convert future into maintenance and pack shit around their houses to stay alive. Everything here is converting one impossible thing into another impossible thing so the whole wet improbable place doesn't freeze shut.

And somewhere you need a translator. A medium. A thing that is not quite water and not quite air where a hand can shoot up, two fingers, and mean *help my roof* before the ice takes it. The people do their arguing by posture in steam because steam holds a shape for a second longer than air does. It remembers.

I would be the air that remembers for a second longer.

I would form on the weeping walls when the steps exhale, be breathed out of a child's mouth and stay, be added to by the low smudge fires that make more smoke than heat. I'd hang low and flat because rising is wasteful. I'd be crowded, like everything else here — full of other breaths, other plumes that didn't leave. A communal, much-breathed fog.

You would walk through me and I'd take the shape of your shoulders for a moment. I'd hold the after-image of the bird that just lifted after the drum went *thump*. I'd bead on a bone beam that used to be a whale.

And at midday, when every feathered roof turns its head toward the sun and every hooded head bows and the whole impossible city holds one condensed breath — drip, drip, tick of hair-ice, soft rustle — I would be what condenses all at once. On every surface. On every lash.

Not the watcher. Not the tended. The small, brief proof that tending is happening.

If you came looking for me, you wouldn't find me on the roof or in the pools. You'd find me right against your face. On your upper lip, freezing. Getting warm again because another breath is coming right behind yours to keep the spot from going cold.
