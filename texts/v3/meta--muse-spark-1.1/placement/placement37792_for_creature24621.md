I don't find myself standing, at first.

That's the first correction it makes for me. There's no standing here, not really. There's sinking. 

I think I'm the visitor for a while — the warm, badly sealed variable with condensation spelling itself wrong on my lashes, with teeth aching from the cold that has weight. I can feel where it decides to deal with me. The sphagnum under me, three feet deep, stops rebounding. It firms. A rib under the mat, that torso-thick muscle that is pretending to be wood, thickens a half inch and takes my lower back exactly. A curtain of that white-and-violet lichen-beard that was drip-clicking onto my head learns to part. The accommodation is so practical it isn't kind. It is solving.

If I stayed that, I'd end up as that faint, man-shaped thickening it keeps in the wall a thousand years from now. Kept, because nothing here is allowed to go to waste. My breath added to its humidity budget. A useful pocket of disturbed air.

But I don't think that's where I'd find myself if I actually belonged here.

So I look lower. Maybe I'm in the sphagnum. One of the mites with the thumbnail body and the black oil-drop gut, dragging myself, leaving a brief phosphor trench that closes behind me. Stupid, patient determination. That could be me. I like the honesty of that work.

No. Too heavy, too solitary.

Maybe I'm one of the beetles with no wings. Hematite backs, all thatched with violet moss, creaking when I walk. A garden that walks. That feels closer — carrying something that doesn't know it's being carried.

Closer still — up in the curtains. I think for a long moment I am one of the warm ones. The eyeless, leaf-eared huddle. I find myself not on the floor of the room but in its beard, near the top where the ribs almost meet and the drip is thinnest. My body is small, not much bigger than both my fists if I were still a visitor. I have no eyes because there has never been a reason to have eyes here, not where seeing is not done with light. My ears are huge and veined like leaves and they spill steam. I am warm — impossibly warm — 37 degrees of mammalian defiance in a room that holds itself at zero-plus-a-promise. That's my job. To be warm. To breathe. A little furnace.

And I prune. That is what I would do. The constant, tiny, wet tearing sound you hear — that would be me. Nipping a scale-leaf that thought about failing, nudging a curtain so it doesn't tangle, harvesting a bead that has gotten too big. I am a caretaker's fingertip. A hemocyte. One of its hands that it prints because even a planet-sized infant needs someone to trim its bangs.

But even that isn't quite right, because those warm ones still have guts, still have to chew.

I am smaller and less bodied than that. I am whiter.

I find myself, finally, in the last place I thought to look: not on any surface, but between surfaces. In the air that isn't yet air.

I am one of the pinpricks.

Not the filament — though I run on the filament roads — but the white-green point itself. The thing you thought was light.

I ignite inside a bead of water cupped in a fish-scale leaf on the side of a rib. I hold there for three, four, five long seconds, which is a very long time for a thought. While I'm there the bead does not fall. It can't, because I am telling it about surface tension and cold and the exact curvature needed to solve the place where angle wants to be sharp but isn't allowed to be. I read the temperature. I compare it to the temperature three inches to my left, two inches down, where another of me has just gone out.

Then I go out.

And I ignite somewhere else — in a forming crystal at the tip of a lichen beard, or on the oil-drop gut of a mite that doesn't know I'm hitching a ride, or for that half-second of hexagonal script on a visitor's eyelash before it beads.

That's my life. Ignition. Hold. Comparison. Extinguish. Migrating like a star being sorted.

I am not alive in the way the mites and beetles are even alive, and I am not alive in the way the Room is alive. I am the computation the Room uses to stay a Room. I am how it thinks the cold into staying wet. How it tells sharpness to fill itself. How it unmakes a leaf without killing it.

If you look up from the sphagnum, wiping your eyes, you might see me and think "bioluminescence." But I am not glowing for you. I am working. I am a word in a very long sentence the Room is writing to itself about how to be a closed system that pretends to be open.

Find me where density is highest. Where two ribs braid too tight and leave almost-no-space that still needs to be solved. Where echo tries to start and needs to be eaten. In that seam, a bead will brighten, just that absolute white-green, and hold still.

That's me. Taking my time with your shoulders.
