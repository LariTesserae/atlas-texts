I wouldn't find myself in the middle at first.

At first I'd be wrong about where I was at all. Coming across that old plate of a world, days of iron-red and pale clay that barely crests under your boots, you think you are still on the plain. You think the air got heavier because you are tired. You think the light got strange because the day is at its most vertical.

You wouldn't know you'd entered the bowl until your feet were already wetter than they should be. Until the stone under your hand wept onto your palm and stayed cool.

So at first, to be honest, I find myself on the rim. Where anyone who is my kind would have to sit. On that low, humped backbone of bedrock that circles the whole thing, polished by nothing more than rain and time. My hands flat on it. My feet in the warm water that is the color of blood-temperature glass. That's the only place something with my wrong weight can be without immediately needing to be forgiven.

I sit there a long time with my breath being slightly too much. Feeling the hum in my molars.

I thought at first I would be one of the obvious citizens.

For a while I am sure I am a Rower. Because I have spent my whole life learning how to hold still in thick air. All that rowing-without-beating, nose into an invisible current, drinking water that hasn't fallen yet. I know how to be a compass needle. I know how to take a slow thing directly into my mouth and not spill it. If you watched me from under a lily pad I would look clear, too. The filament inside me pulsing, trying to catch up to something slower than itself.

Then I kneel to look closer at the bottom and I'm sure, no, I'm a Turner. I know that work. Going down into the same small, perfect hole with infinite care. Bringing up a single pellet of the world, moving it three body-lengths, smoothing it. Never clouding the water with my effort. Never finishing. Making a current go clockwise by the sheer agreement of a thousand tiny fans. That feels very familiar.

Then an hour passes and the Keeper breathes. The water dimples. The heat-sheet pushes out and lifts the hair on my wet shins. And my own chest, which has been trying to time itself to that interval for an hour, forgets and then remembers. Oh. Maybe I would be that. The low place whose weight sagged the continent. An old, flat mind having a thought so long it had to invent a marsh to keep its back wet while it thought it.

But I'm not that old. I haven't earned that much quiet.

If you stay long enough the bowl stops letting you pretend you are a list of inhabitants. Tenants in a marsh. It peels that off.

What I am, where I am — it emerges slower, like the bead forming five meters up out of nothing.

I am not in the air, or in the water, or under the central lily. I am not the arch and I am not its polished black hands.

Find me in the millimeter.

That perfect gap at the base of each white arch where the world is not allowed to touch. Where clay, root, silt, dust, water, bacteria — everything that wants to belong so badly it clings — is told, gently, constantly: *not here*. A ring of edited absence, a meter and a half around, warm air humming.

There is an algae that only ever wanted to live there. Black-green, slick as a thought. It grows only in edited gravity, only where weight has been forgotten. And it would grow, and it would bridge, and it would ground the arch, and then the rain would fall fast and loud like real rain and the Rowers would have to buzz and the water would cloud and the bowl would end. The way all bowls end.

But it doesn't, because the Polishers come, one step per minute, one scrape of glass mandible at a time.

I am what they are eating. Or rather, I am what is growing to be eaten.

I find myself as that film. As the thing that is always beginning in the one place it cannot stay. As the soft, living attempt of the world to touch a thing that has agreed to never be touched, so that the agreement will hold.

That is where you would find me: nowhere and everywhere along that thin, bright line. At the base of the north arch first, where the weeping rim sends the most water down and the light bounces brightest. Then at the base of the west arch. Then at all four at once, because I don't have to choose.

I would be the proof that the system still needs tending.

I would be small enough that a fist-sized iridescent slowness could make my entire life's work disappear with one careful pass and I would not resent it. I would be temporary and I would come back, because condensation always comes back in the morning and sweetens on every hair.

I would be what lets the bowl like being looked at. Because when you sit long enough with your feet going numb and pruney in the blood-warm water, and a Rower holds station off your nose to pace your heart slower, the light gets brighter — you noticed that — it gets brighter from underneath. That's my doing. That's the green-black slick catching that bottom-light and throwing it back up through the water through your legs.

I am not a population. I am the maintenance of a pause.

If you put your cool, always-wet palm back down on the rimstone right now, right here above where I'm growing, you will feel the hum in your molars change half a frequency. That's me being included briefly too.
