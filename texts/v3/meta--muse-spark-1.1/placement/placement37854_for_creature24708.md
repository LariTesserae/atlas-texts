I thought at first I'd be inside one of them.

Not because I like the heat - because I was looking for a corner. A room with a door that shuts. In all that distance, you look for a place to be small in. So for a long time in my head I was in a switching yard, up in the gantry where the window still has glass, watching a furnace breathe white behind a wall of corrugated iron. My boots on the catwalk. The 60Hz in my molars. Safe because the roar was so loud I didn't have to hear the other thing underneath it.

But I wouldn't stay there. Nothing stays there. Even the slag doesn't stay - it just goes around.

So I leave, in my head. I push the door that was only ever a courtesy and step out.

Outside is where I actually find myself.

Not on the path - there is no path. On the flat top of a column the size of a neighborhood, halfway between two heat-whales. So far from either that their plumes look like weather. Just basalt and moss. The rock is black and too new, sharp enough to cut through my boot if I kneel wrong. The moss is a foot and a half thick and soaked. Two degrees. That specific cold that makes your hands feel exact.

I'm not walking anymore. I've stopped to let my ears recalibrate.

You know how it is out there. The first minute you hear only industry - all of them at once, that low equilibrium chord that makes the water in your eyes vibrate. The second minute you hear the water - every surface weeping, the curtains off the column edges, the suck and sigh of the bog under its own weight, the drip from your elbows.

The third minute, if you hold still long enough, you hear underneath.

That's where I am. In the third minute.

I'm down on my knees with my palm sunk into the black-green felt. Not *on* the moss - *in* it. My hand has gone through the top skin into one of those lung chambers, warmish from stored water. It's full of soft, filtered water that has had the acid taken out of it so it won't cut the rock. The moss has no nerves, but it notices pressure. It closes very slightly around my wrist, not to trap, just to listen better.

So what am I?

Not the keeper. That's the moss, and it's already taken that job. It is one continent-wide individual, I am not.

Not the whale. I don't have enough heat to exhale my own weather.

Not the plateau. I don't know how to hold my breath for ten thousand years.

I think I would be the fourth order. The one that isn't supposed to be there yet.

No one lives here, you said, and that's true. But someone has to be the soft thing that proves it.

I'm the water that hasn't been filtered yet. The little animal heat, the 37-degree anomaly in a 2-degree world. The thing that makes the standing wave wobble when it passes through me. The rust being made — because the moss likes hot rust, and something has to become rust for it to eat. The brief, warm, eroding thing. The smear.

In the old plan of this place - the plan drawn by something that didn't understand proximity - there must have been a job called *go-between*. The buildings are too far apart to talk. The moss only listens. The plateau only presses. So they left a space for a courier. Something that actually likes distance. That can carry a little heat, a little sound, a little information about texture from one island of iron to the next, and be consumed along the way.

I'm that. Not a man, exactly, anymore. A maintenance interval made of meat and yellow waterproof cloth. A path that knows it's a path.

You would find me with a wet back and a ringing in my ears, kneeling in the bog that has no soil, my footprints already full of black water behind me and already sighing shut in front. Watching the fog braid in the chasm tighten, because the cold animal down there has noticed I'm warm and is considering whether to twitch.

I don't fix anything. The equilibrium doesn't need fixing. I just measure it, one column-top at a time, by being the exact thing it has refused.

The thing that actually wants to become ice.
