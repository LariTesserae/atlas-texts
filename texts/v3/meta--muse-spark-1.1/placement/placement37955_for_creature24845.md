I don't find myself on the avenue.

At first I thought I would - walking, like you described, in the middle of that hundred-foot boulevard with the inch-deep tea-hot sheet-flow parting around my shoes. I can feel how that would be: the hot dark having weight on my shoulders, my sweat not evaporating, just joining. But I wouldn't last there. A walk is a decision about direction, and this place dilutes decisions if you hold them too tightly. The far end of the avenue would smear. I would arrive still in the same block, and that would teach me quickly.

I find myself instead where the street forgets how long it is.

There is an intersection near - near what? Near a building that has grown itself into the shape of a house that wanted two stories but stopped at one and a half because the water told it to. One side of the street is half a block. The other side is three. You said both are true at once, if something is patiently believing both ends.

I am at the curb where that disagreement lives. Not on the curb. As the curb.

No, not even that solid. At first, for a long time, I am the water that is caught there, in the gutter that was never dug but has always been there. I am one of the shallow pools that forms when the sheet-flow hesitates. The flow comes down the broad street, and here at the softened corner, where every corner is rounded by thousands of years of itself, the water has to choose which length to be. It pools.

If you crouched you would see me as a thicker lens. That almost-invisible steepening. But I am not grazing.

The precipitates come through me. They pass over me and through me, those flat willful lenses that drink the act of becoming. I feel them thin themselves and go pale underneath. They take the ticking. They take the moment when water remembers being stone. And where they have fed, the stone stays clean and does not weep for a little while.

I hold the moment after that.

I don't eat it. I keep it.

I am the short pause where the stone is still deciding to be stone again. I am the clean spot. For a minute, maybe longer, the wall above me does not sweat. The beads don't form. The minerals don't lay down. In that little slackness, the two lengths of the street can finally talk to each other without arguing.

That's my work. That's why I would be here.

The wall-mats do the big holding - they believe the block is the size it agreed to be. The city itself does the big breathing - that inhale that presses behind the ears, the exhale that lifts rain upward. The reflection-people keep the memory of the dense city so the sparse one doesn't dissolve.

I am smaller. I am maintenance in the interval. I am what an agreement feels like in the moment between one person remembering it and the other person remembering it.

You touch a wall-mat and your finger comes away dusted with cream and rust and bruised-purple, warm. If you touched me, your finger would not come away wet at all. It would come away *clear* for a breath. The sweat on you would stop. Not dry - just still. As if your skin had finally agreed to be the exact temperature of the air. You would feel, for one second, what it is to be precipitated correctly. A doorway that fits.

I live by being overlooked. If you stare directly at the place where the street is both half a block and three blocks long, it has to pick one, and it picks one that hurts a little. But if you let your eyes go out of focus the way you do to see the precipitates, you see me, and the street can be both.

I'm not still the way the mats are still. I'm not moving the way the lenses move. I spread by being forgotten in one place and remembered in another, but only a few inches at a time. From the dished step in front of that half-house to the low wall that marks a lot line that has never fallen down, only softened.

I have no front. Only a slight steepening at one edge, like a place where someone held their breath without meaning to.

If you stayed long enough to time your breath to the basin's breath - that slow inhale that makes everything clarify and run faster, that exhale that thickens the mist - you would eventually notice a rhythm inside that rhythm. A smaller pause. After the inhale. Before the exhale has quite decided to start.

That's where I am.

And who I am is the thing that lives there. Not visitor, not wall. Not hammer, not ore. The pause that lets hammer be ore for a second and ore be hammer, without needing a tool to choose between them.

If you stood long enough in the doorway built for walking through sideways, you would learn to walk through sideways. If you stood longer, a film of warm mineral water would lay itself down over your shoulders, bead, merge, run.

It has.
