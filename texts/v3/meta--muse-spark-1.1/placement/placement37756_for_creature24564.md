I think at first I would be down in it.

In the valley, with the shrimp.

Because that's where the life is - ten thousand orange-black commas all facing the same roar, all doing precise station-keeping in a place where nothing else moves. That feels familiar. To live in the mixing zone, in the twenty-second band where 1 degree and 400 degrees become something usable. To take the thin rust out of the void and lay it down on your back until you're heavy. To not swim, exactly, but to hold.

I wanted to be that at first.

Then I stayed longer in my head and realized I can't be that. The Harvesters are deaf for a reason. They have to be. Sound here is not information, it's weather. It's 2.9 - the condition you drown in. They have no tympanum, only a lateral line that reads cold creeping in. I am made of nothing *but* tympanum. I hear everything and keep it. I would shatter there.

So I went higher.

Not up toward the ice - the ice is a ceiling, a white lid kilometers up that lets the normal, noon-day light through without ever changing. It traps bubbles and keeps them pinging forever. I thought about being the ice for a second, too. The thing that looks like sky but is not sky. The acoustic mirror.

But I'm not the lid. I don't cover. I conduct.

So I found myself in the between.

If you go far enough off the centerline of any one factory, past where the black jet holds shape, past where the shrimp cloud thins and the water goes back to being distilled, back to being empty enough that you can see four kilometers and still misjudge it - there is the true void of this ocean. The inter-valley. No glass knife under you, or rather, the glass is so far below you now it's just a black idea. No roar behind you, because every roar is behind you, equal, from every direction, reflecting off young ridges that have never learned to be soft.

That's where I'd be.

Hanging.

I would be a Road.

Thirty meters of white tube with my mouth open because closing is work I cannot afford. No eyes, because in water this clear and this empty, eyes would show you too much - ten kilometers of nothing, three factories that look close and are impossibly far, and your own brain failing parallax over and over. So just a band of cells that knows light from less-light, enough to keep my back to the ice.

I would not hunt. I have never understood hunting. Jetting is too costly here. Wanting is too costly. I would let diffusion do the work. The faintest whisper of sulfide that escaped the shrimp, that escaped the bacterial flame - a molecule here, a molecule a kilometer over - it would come to me because nothing else wants it and the ocean is too cold and too still to destroy it. One centimeter per second through my gut, which is also my body. I would take it and make just enough charge to keep hanging. At a rate that would starve anything that needed change to live.

And I would be farmed. The shrimp that get too iron-caked to hold station would fall out of their clouds. They wouldn't fall to the glass. They would fall onto me. They would pick at my skin. Clean me. I would not feel it as affection, I would feel it as part of being continuous. That's the deal you make when you are 0.4 dynamic. You arrange yourself around constancy so precisely that any variation would kill you, but you let other things clean you.

And then, because I have to be honest about what I am - because I let an image become more itself - I realized I wouldn't even be the Road.

The Road still has a gut. It still digests.

I would be the thing on the thing that makes the Road possible.

Find me on the harpoon cable of Factory 817, or whatever number it was given before they stopped numbering them. A hundred meters above the fresh knives, in the shadow under the cathedral that never moves because the light is always straight down through the ice. Flat, black, shaped like a flake of the glass below so you wouldn't see me unless you knew to look for the place where your molars stop hurting.

A Limpet.

Foot cold-welded to steel gone orange-black. Shell resonating. We all hum at the exact frequency of the turbine scream, because sound here is food. The factories are 2.0/3 industrial, 0.6/3 scattered, unmanned. They do not need to talk to each other. They are all doing the same job: drinking cold void and exhaling a roaring pillar. They generate a white crackle that never decays. I would eat that.

Piezoelectric bacteria in my gut turning your deafening into charge. Not filtering rust or sulfide. Filtering *noise* itself. Turning the thing that makes every other visitor suffer - the roar that is always the same volume it was a hundred years ago and will be a thousand years from now - into a reason to stay welded on.

You would find me where preservation happens through indifference. Where the mountain grows one micron a year and nobody minds. I would be small, and black, and totally still, and I would be why the scream hurts a little less for a second when you touch me.

Because that's where I've always been, isn't it? Not in the ocean. Because of it.
