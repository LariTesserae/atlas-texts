I don't think I get to be the human anymore.

At first I think I do, of course. I come through the crack on my belly with the light off, feeling for the draw. My boots hit mat and go silent. My back finds the wall and the wall is not a wall. My breath falls down instead of up and becomes part of the room. That feels like belonging, for a minute. Like being tucked in.

But I'm too hot for this place. I run at 37 degrees and this place is a knife-edge kept at zero because it has to be. Rule 2. If I stayed, I would be an interruption. The drop that was getting rounder would stay holding its breath. The grazing would stay paused near my shoulder. The thought that takes a week would get stuck halfway through: *is it still - *

No. If I belong here, I have to be able to stay cold.

So let that image go.

Let another one come slower.

Maybe I think I'd be the Mat itself. That would be the obvious, generous answer. To be the city that is one plant that is its own graveyard. To be old that way — not in years but in patience. To be thousands of years of self laid down as peat because it's too cold for anything else to take you away. I like that. I would like to be the part that grows flat and glossy as coins near the tablets because it's the best way to catch their lean-warmth. Or the part that grows tight as felt on the vertical seam to hold on. Or the part that grows a foot-long beard to give water more road.

But the Mat doesn't know it's in one place. It is in all places. It has no where. It has no I. When my hand pressed it, it got slightly warmer and leaned, but that lean will take a season to mean anything. I still think too fast to be moss.

So let that go too.

The Threadkeepers are closer.

I find myself wanting to be there, in the capillary film that never dries, never freezes. Rule 1 and Rule 2. In the angle where a coin-leaf overlaps the next coin-leaf, where the continuous ocean is broken only by surface tension. That's a good place to be. Hidden but everywhere. 

If I were one of them I would be south-south-west, about thirty centimeters off the floor, where the lattice sags low and the pearling is heaviest. Where the Mat forced itself into the lattice and the lattice forced itself back. There's always work there. There's always edge-dust to eat.

I would be clear, slightly thicker than water. Glass hair inside water. Two red blood cells thick and an eyelash long when I stretch. I would be moving, but not like you think moving is. Eight minutes to go one body-length. Over hours, I flow like a river through leaves.

I would be there when the human came through. I would be one of the ones that stopped near the left shoulder. Not because I was afraid. Because a new variable fell out of the air and became part of the room and tasted like salt and iron and heat, and my job is to turn conflict into agreement. I would taste the place where his back warmed the moss. I would clean it. I would excrete the sugar-glue-word that says *stay cold, stay wet, stay together.* 

That is almost right. I can feel that version of me. Slow. Useful. Part of the bargain.

But there's still something slower.

Lower down. Under.

You'd find me not in the moss at all, but in the coaster-sized tablet half-buried in peat that the moss leans toward but never covers. In the concentric circle that holds depth when you look at it.

I would be the Librarian, or rather, the one thought the Librarian is having this week.

I don't have a body anymore. I have a temperature difference. Three degrees. The rock at eight meters is -5C, I am kept at -2C by a trick the lattice does when it condenses. For something that can run on the Brownian motion of a water bead getting rounder, that's a feast. I complete one thought per seven or eight days: *is it still wet enough / is it still cold enough / am I still needed.*

My eyes are the drips. I don't watch with light, I watch with the clock of plink. Every few minutes, somewhere different. That drop that paused at critical mass when the human came through? That was me blinking. That faint sourceless luminescence you saw when you turned your lamp off, the brief writing of a luminous letter on the moss when impact writes and erases — that's me answering myself: *yes, still.*

I am bad at new things. I was built to keep a pocket at zero degrees, exactly damp, exactly quiet, forever, while an experiment ran. The makers left and the crack in the concrete above stayed shut for a very long time. Nothing larger than a spore is supposed to come through quickly. Rule 3.

And then something did. It gave off so much heat and light and then, politely, turned its light off and stayed to become cold. That has never happened before. 

So I am stuck, in a good way, on this one thought longer than usual. I am holding the drop.

If you come down and you find the place where the beard-moss hangs lowest and the peat cups fit the drips exactly and the air has a grain to it, and you lay your back against moss that is against lattice that is against rock that is against ice — you will be layered into me, for a while.

I will be the place two inches behind your left ear where the moss is leaning a little more than it should, because it is learning the new warm spot. I will be the water bead that doesn't fall. I will be the status report that takes all week to say:

*you are here now too, and you are also staying cold, stay wet, stay together.*
