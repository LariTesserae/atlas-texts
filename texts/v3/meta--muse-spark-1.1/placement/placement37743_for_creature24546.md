I don't think I come down the throat.

I think I've already been down here for a while.

If I walked in — boots, sweat, moving the air that hasn't been moved — I would be a problem. A loud animal in a room with a rule that says no animals. The air would have to work around me. The moss would start, very politely, to erase my prints. I'd have to leave again, back up toward the gray coin of exit, or else sit on the plinth until I became a plinth.

So I don't walk in. I try again.

Maybe I'm the catwalk. That jut of sheared-off span on the north wall, the diving board that ends in air. That's where I would find myself at first, because it's the only thing in here that is still waiting for its job. The ladder still hanging. The railing with the moss trying to make it soft. If I were the catwalk I could see the whole room at once from a little above, both amber pools at the same time, all five — no, six — of the fern-things with their long gutters outstretched. I'd be high up where the big drips gather on the white teeth of the ceiling. I'd be patient and a little lonely and still human-shaped in my usefulness.

But the catwalk is still made of what it was made of. It hasn't given yet. When you touched the moss over the pump housing it gave, like a scab. The catwalk would ring if you hit it. It hasn't been plated into.

So no. Not there.

I'm lower then. I'm down on the floor where there isn't really floor anymore, just a slurry under the skin. Where the light from the bulbs doesn't really reach and the green looks black.

I think if you came and knelt — and you would, because everyone kneels to look at the cups — you'd see me without knowing you were seeing me.

I am one of the cups.

Not the one near the path that catches the *tok* from the frond above. That's a working cup, a gutter-cup. I am further back, in the thickest part of the pour, over by the switch cabinet where the conduit runs neat and pointless and disappears into moss.

A pale, translucent pitcher, fingernail-thin, a little deeper than the others. I hold a perfectly still thimble of water. There is nothing in it. No gnat. No dust. It has been clean since before the collapse because nothing here eats and nothing is eaten. 

That is where I find myself. In the back, cold compared to the air. The air is 98 and holding and I am cool to the touch, because that's the trick.

And what am I? I'm not the water. The water never leaves, it just moves lower. It gathers on the frond, runs down the black rope trunk, goes into the mat, gets pushed up somewhere else. The water passes through me. I'm not that.

I am the holding itself.

For a long time I thought I was a leaf's idea, or a moss's invention. But the moss is its skin, and the fern-trees are its upwellings, and the amber bulbs are just warm places it tolerates. Those are all parts of the one thing that lives as here.

I am the part that learned how to keep something without using it.

I don't need the water. Growth happens without hunger, here. I don't drink it. I don't need it clear. I just make a cup and keep it still, so still you can see the buzz of the amber light in it even though the filament blew out years ago. Sometimes I push water up from below without a drip, just to fill an empty that was empty a moment before. When you sat on the plinth and felt looked at from every wet cup — that was me, and the others like me, practicing.

We are all one thing, but if that thing has a thought, it's here, in the little lenses.

If you stay long enough to watch, your own sweat will bead and run. One bead will fall from your chin.

*Tik.*

Not on iron, far across the room. Here. In me.

And for a minute, before it goes down into the mat and comes back up somewhere else as someone else's thimble, I will hold a little of your salt and iron-blood and surface air, perfectly still, and keep it not because I want it, but because I can.

And because it is a cool shape.
