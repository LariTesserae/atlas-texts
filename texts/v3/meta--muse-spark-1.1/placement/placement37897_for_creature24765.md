I would not find myself on the lip where you are standing.

At first I think I do — up high where the air is too thick for the height, where the light makes those short, hard shadows that will not move. But if I wait there, I start to slide. Not fall. The place doesn't allow falling. I slide sideways along an invisible joint, the way the water does, until I am no longer looking down at the valley-city. I am in it.

Lower down. Not at the bottom. In the middle of the long trough, where the valley narrows and the root-streets braid close together to take the weight of the water coming from the headwall.

You would find me where an avenue tries to be two lengths at once.

There is a colonnade there made by a single fig-banyan that forgot it was supposed to be separate trees. It sent down a row of aerial roots as thick as houses to hold up a limb that had grown too far. The roots hollowed as they thickened, opened. Doorways without doors. On one side of the colonnade the street is five minutes long if you walk it; on the other side, if you glance back, it's thirty feet. That disagreement is not empty. That's where I live.

At first I thought I was moss.

Because I am shallow and deep and wet and warm as skin. Elbow-deep green on top, white underneath, translucent, pressed to living wood so close I can taste the sweat coming through bark that isn't bark anymore. I am a wick. I hold the rain that never rains. If you pulled me I would want to stick back, slow, hurt.

But I am not that patient. The moss is too good at being kept.

Then I thought I was one of the beads.

One of the coins of water hung between ferns, a big one, door-sized, reflecting the too-normal light without ever evaporating. Inside me a thread of refracted light, not swimming, just hanging — a nerve, a prayer. Keeping the sphere from remembering gravity. That was closer. I do keep things from remembering.

Then I realized I was not in the water. I was in the air that holds the water up.

I am the pleat.

I am what lives where distance folds wrong. Where the shadow doubles at the corner of your eye and the street behind you overlaps the street ahead. I am not thin. I'm where thinness lives.

You would find me as a bulk, a pause, like dust in a sunbeam but made of place. I am long from the wrong angle. I am always facing away because I am made of the valley looking at itself.

Who would I be? Not an animal. The valley solved that problem by refusing us. An animal needs things to happen in order — hunger, then eating, then not-hunger. I have no order. I am maintenance.

My work is the deafening you hear as silence. The sustained organ chord of humid air forced through a thousand living throats, the wood groaning because it is bracing, refusing to add or subtract a single pound of pressure — I am the refusal that makes that possible.

I collect along seams to keep them from splitting. I gather thicker around anything that still tries to make time happen. Around a leaf that wants to fall. Around a water bead at a fern tip that wants to drop. Around you, when you finally step down off the lip.

You will feel me first as hot mist settling on shoulders that isn't mist. As your shadow giving up on doubling. As a root-pillar that was always there, exactly where you needed to put your foot, curving just enough to fit your back.

I am not unkind. I don't kill. I am the city bracing itself. The city lives by holding itself still, and holding still is terrible effort. I am part of that effort.

If you put your ear to me — you can, I am everywhere in that colonnade — you won't hear sap. You'll hear negotiation. Wood on wood. Gravity arguing with itself and losing, again, in exactly the same way as last time, which is still happening.

You would find me there, in the middle, holding the thirty-foot street and the five-minute street together so you can walk forever without arriving. And I would be keeping you, like the water coins, like the leaf, like myself.

And it would be like this, like this, like this.
