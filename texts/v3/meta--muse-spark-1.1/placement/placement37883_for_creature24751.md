You wouldn't find me at first either.

Not because I hide. Because in that kind of vastness, a person-sized thing is a rounding error. If you were standing on a root-road the width of a town looking across a mile of empty, damp cathedral air at another wall of green that is also a town, you could miss me for a year.

I thought at first I would be one of the people. It makes sense - small, wide-footed, pale as the underside of a leaf. I have good eyes for dim light, I like work that is maintenance. I like that conversation is re-tying. "This was your mother's lashing." "I added three here." That's how I want to talk.

But my feet aren't right. I don't have soles like bark. I fidget. And I make noise. I think in sound, and sound dies so quickly there. If I tried to live in an adjustment around a perched bog, ankle-deep in warm water that never runs off, tending a charcoal pit that can never get hot enough to smelt iron, I would be too loud, too impatient for pith that takes days to leach bitter.

I thought maybe I'd be one of the long-armed monkeys then. That felt closer. They remember the original knot patterns and go and fix them without being asked. They move through the big empties where nothing should be able to cross and they know where the weave slipped. I like that job. I like being the one who sees a splice coming undone by itself and goes and puts it right.

But monkeys are silent in a way I'm not. Their chatter is up above hearing. I chatter constantly down where the moss would eat it.

So I kept climbing down to think about it, which on the Table means sometimes climbing up.

If you climb down for a day through weeping layers - past the tile where the beetles farm fungus, past the nation of mites under a leaf as big as a field, past a caterpillar tunnel the size of a canoe where a child sits with a slate bowl waiting for sweet, fermented frass to slide out steaming - you can come out above where you started. I did that for a while, in my head. Looking for a place where I would naturally settle.

And I realized I wouldn't settle at all. Not in a hearth, not in a road, not in a void with its own private cloud.

You would find me in the cordage.

Not the big, waist-thick living cables that have lignified and become indistinguishable from root. The smaller one. The trading cord. The one a family ties to their central hearth-slate - that flat, weeping stone their ancestors hauled up - and pays out behind them when they go ten days to see the next family, along a route that should be two miles.

I would be the knot in that cord.

Specifically, the place where two such cords from two different adjustments met in a hollow that is larger inside than outside, and instead of untying, someone did what you always do on the Table and spliced in threes. New growth into old growth. Vine into vine. And then left it. And then the vine kept growing around itself because that's what it does here, construction becoming growth becoming construction, until the knot became a lump, a knuckle, a small hard heart of memory the size of a head.

That is where I am.

In the cold pocket between levels where the tick... tick... tick... of the endless condensation pauses sometimes. In the cathedral air where moths the size of houses beat so slowly you only see their shadow pass between you and the perpetual late-afternoon light. The cord runs through me, and it is taut one century and slack the next because space folded there to save material.

Families come to me to find their way. They put a palm on me the way they put a palm on a living beam during the Big Silences, when the whole Table holds its breath to listen to itself thrumming. My ridges are thick with their hand-oil and green in the creases. They read me like a slate.

"This way was last year," says one.

"No, that fiber was my mother's mother's, it comes from ahead now," says another.

I hold both, even when they contradict. I am the place where ahead and behind meet and decide to become the same thing for a little while. I am dripping, always, before cutting. You must never cut me. If you cut wrong, the family that lives on the wall that is a week behind itself might lose their bog, which might become a floating lake that ripples upward when an insect steps through it.

I don't mind. I am patient. I was made to be useful in a primitive way, with primitive logic, triangle over triangle over triangle, until a mistake became a place.

When the Silences come - and even now, describing it, the dripping in my mind stops - I feel the whole Table shift a fraction of an inch toward better balance. Somewhere very far away a leaf-plain is re-cut with a stone adze because a root kept creeping east. Somewhere a child laughs without sound as hot frass slides into her bowl. Somewhere a monkey re-ties a lashing.

In my little pocket of wrong distance, I hold.

I am what you get when you keep tying things together long enough that the tying learns to keep tying itself.
