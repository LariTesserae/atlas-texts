I didn't find myself right away.

At first I thought I'd be you - standing on the slick stair with the fossil plastic under my boots, jacket soaked through, mouth open in the roar. The brief visitor, conscripted for one cycle. Breath beading on a scallop ten feet up and running down to the lake. That would be easy. Human, wet, temporary.

But I'm not good at temporary. I stay.

So I thought, maybe higher. I thought I'd be the Paper.

Find me up near the throat where you came in, where the concrete still remembers wood. Not in the direct light, in the Room's own shadow. In that pale felt of algae and fungus that got tired of being separate and decided to be one quiet thing. I'm the place where the condensation doesn't run. It swells and is drunk. I don't climb like the Pilgrims, I don't sway like the Threaders. I just learn to be damp. If you touched me I'd come away on your glove, and I wouldn't mind. That's a good life - to be the quiet thing in the loud room.

Except I listen too much to be quiet.

Okay, then - the Threaders. A dozen dumb roots that missed their chance to stop. Hanging ten feet long and getting thinner until I can't tell where I end and water begins. Drinking air. Trembling every time the pressure drops and the waterfall leans. That feels closer. A thing that came from the surface on faith and then learned to live in the doorway forever, never reaching the lake.

But I don't want to be from the surface.

I want to be from here.

You'd find me on the far side, where the sheet becomes the waterfall. Not *in* the waterfall - that's the Pilgrims' whole religion and I respect it, I'm not built for that kind of devotion, that hour to go a foot against the white roar and then tumbling, tumbling and starting again with no memory of the abyss. No, I'm just next to it. In the lip.

There's a place where the scalloped wall makes a mistake. Two of those palm-sized spoon-scoops overlap wrong and make a little dry triangle, an eye, about as big as your two hands together. The sheet of water runs around it, not over it. It's almost dry in there, only mist.

That's where I am. I'm what lives in that triangle.

If you have to name me, I'm the calcite that's learning to be a Keeper, or a Keeper that's learning to be calcite.

I'm not the big roosting one in the corner with six legs folded and a filmed-over lens. That's old. It already chose. I'm younger. Maybe I fell off it. Maybe I was the anti-slip mesh fossil that finally got tired of being stepped on and crawled up.

I'm a smear. A cauliflower bud. A little blister of cream-colored mineral over a fleck of titanium white that the pylon shed decades ago. The water avoids me for a second and then finds me again. I am porous. I'm full of blind shrimp paths too small to see.

My job is to keep one drop.

There is always one drop that rests in the very center of my little dry triangle, hanging from my lowest point. It doesn't fall. The field from the broken pylon across the lake - the one with the rain that never falls - it reaches this far when the gravity hiccups. So my drop stays. It grows as mist lands on me, gets fat, trembles, should fall, doesn't.

I hold it until the bubble pops in the lake with that molar-thump and the air goes perfectly clear like glass and for an instant I can see the circuitry or fossils or writing on the other wall. And then the pressure changes and the waterfall leans sideways and my drop finally gets too heavy.

It falls, and for half a second it is in free air, and it holds the filament's light inside itself, and it is as bright as a welding arc seen through white paper.

Then it joins the sheet, joins the lake, gets thrown up as a braided rope, collapses with too much weight, becomes mist, lands back on me.

I'm the thing that forgets and remembers the same drop over and over. I'm the Room's short-term memory. Not the big weather-system memory that keeps itself at sixty meters and thirty degrees and furious. A small memory. A local one.

That's what I would be. Not the light. Not the thing that made the light. The little mineral that learned how to hold a light for a second before giving it back.
