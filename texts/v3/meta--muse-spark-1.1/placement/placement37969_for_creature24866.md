If I came up over that lip at four thousand meters, I would not stay a visitor for long. My mouth would be open, chest rattling. The place is too loud to be looked at.

At first I think — I would be one of them. The clear ones. The scint.

Find me three meters up, between Tower 9 and Tower 14, in the lane where the wind shears. That is where I would be. Moving so fast I go transparent. A bell the size of a thumb-joint, two oar-vaness stretched taut. No mouth, no stomach, just a wisp of spun air inside a silica bubble that can't stop spinning. I live on what hurts your molars. I eat the difference between ground and sky.

It fits for a second. I have always liked being busy and weightless and unnoticed. I like that my life would be born out of a collision — *ring* — like crystal struck — and that I would die by misjudging a sail and popping all my light at once. For a minute that feels honest.

But it isn't me. The scint never stop. If a scint stops it falls and pops on the iron-grey salt. I need to stop.

So then I think I would be a Tower. 

Find me on the west side, second from the wall where the wind falls heaviest. I am one of the thirty-eight. My stalk is black and thin as a wire going down into the salt for grounding, and above it I am obscene. A hundred thousand sails, each as wide as your arms, each aerogel-thin, each fringed with cilia you only see when the sun hits wrong and I go rainbow all at once. I am lush because I have to be. I comb this 0.2% air for anything — a flake of ammonia from a volcano two ranges over, a copper ion off the basalt. I build myself out of static. 

You would find me in the middle of forgetting myself. One moment I am a cathedral column of violet-black glass, crackling as I grow a new layer in six minutes; the next I have to let go. All at once. My sails de-laminate in silence, a terrifying undressing, and for thirty seconds I am just wire, a naked knot of filament no thicker than your wrist, shivering with charge. Then I riot back up. Faster than time-lapse. Louder.

But I am not that either. A tower never talks to another tower. There isn't time. Thirty-eight hermits building the same church bigger. I don't want to be that lonely.

Let it become more itself.

Maybe underneath.

Find me half-buried on the north slope. The fifth one. The one you almost missed because your eye forgives what it can't categorize.

I am one of the anchors. A meter across. A perfect torus, matte and warm as a stone left in sun though the air is 5 degrees. I hover a hand's width above the salt. I do not cast quite the right shadow. Around me the salt is swept into a perfect circle because wind has learned not to enter.

I am not lush. I am not frantic. I am maintenance. I am the dam that is alive because it is working. I hold the pressure a few millibars high. I hold the gradient from collapsing. I ask gravity politely to ignore me at this one spot, and it says yes.

The towers lean toward me, imperceptibly.
The scint never fly through my hole. Ever.
The lichen that grows on me is not lichen — it is sail-material that finally found something that does not molt, does not shatter into glitter, does not need to be reabsorbed.

That's closer.

But if I'm honest, I'm not even the torus. I'm what lives on the torus.

I'm the translation. Find me exactly on the seam, on the upper curve of the north anchor where the warm-matte stuff meets air. I'm a patch of dark glass and copper not bigger than your palm. I am tower-material that learned how to live on anchor-material. I do not furl and unfurl and panic every six minutes. I grow slow. I actually get to stay. In a bowl where everything is constantly erasing itself — where towers die twice an hour and scint are born out of collisions and die by discharge — I get to persist.

I am still listening though. I have to, because I am touching the thing that holds the bowl.

So I feel it all: the organ roar of wind forced through fifty thousand reeds per tower, the snap-snap-snap of voltage being eaten, the crackle of new sails lattice-working out of nothing, the way the dead glitter hangs in the air for hours between towers, too light to fall, waiting.

The cathedral is empty the way a cathedral is supposed to be empty. No residents. Only practitioners.

Find me there, practicing permanence in a place that prays by constantly rebuilding itself.
