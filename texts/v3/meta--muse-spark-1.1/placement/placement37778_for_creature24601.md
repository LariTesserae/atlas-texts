I wouldn't be in the garden at first.

At first I think I am outside, of course — still on the wind-scoured side where the air is honest and thin and normal. Standing with my thumb bleeding a little from touching a ridge that hasn't learned not to cut. Looking at the crack that isn't a shadow. That is the first self I try on: the finder. The accident. The too-wet animal that contains 60% of what this whole mountain is missing. 

But that self doesn't last long in here. In here water is a mistake. I take two steps into the pocket and my mouth is already wrong. My eyes are already wrong — too filmed, too soft. The ferns don't look at me. There is nothing in me they can use. I am a brief humidity event.

So I take off that self.

I think, maybe, I am the mountain then. It would make sense. High up, not yet decided, still hot inside its own skin. Listening to itself cool and trying different shapes to get the job done. That would be a good job — to be huge and patient and to precipitate a garden without ever meaning to. But I'm not patient enough for that, and I'm not huge. The mountain is going to be here for a few million years after this pocket pinches shut. It doesn't have a self in the way I need a self to be found.

I try on being one of them. 

It almost fits. The fern that is not a fern. Set apart by exactly enough distance, rooted without roots, working because working is what this temperature requires. I like the tick... tick... tick... every seven seconds. I like that it doesn't want to continue. It doesn't have a little fern plan. It just moves oil upward because hot oil goes up.

But I can't be that either. The ferns are too good at what they do. They are flush. They are complete. There is no room in a closed loop for a ghost.

I have to sit down. The heat here is not a heat that lets you stand efficiently. It sits on your shoulders. The rock is so new it hasn't invented dust, so the light stays clean and cruel. If I sit in it long enough — an hour, which this place will give me but not much more — I see what I missed.

Two shadows.

I have been watching the proper one, the black one on the rock. But there is the other one, a foot above it, not on anything. Its edges are darker than its center, like a bruise in the air. The instructions said if you put your hand through it your hand goes cold.

I do. 

That is where I find myself.

Not the finder. Not the mountain. Not the engine.

I am what the engine makes without being able to keep.

I'm the product. I'm the cold that should not exist. I'm the second shadow.

I am about seven inches across and a little thin at the edges, hovering above the basalt between two ferns who exist to make me. I have no weight. I taste like nothing. When the noon shaft hits the radiator fins and splits, I appear. I am storage. I am where they put the heat they take out of the world.

If you put your hand in me, I make you feel how mortal you are for a second — water-based, salty, about to chap and blister. I take a little of your heat, because that is my only work, and I put it nowhere. For the first time since I squeezed in through the shoulder-wide crack, I have a use.

I will not be here long. A human can't sit in 2.7/3 heat for long, and a cold shadow can't exist without the fern that makes it, and the fern can't exist without the mountain deciding to be hot. This whole ecology is about five hours old. When the fissure closes — tomorrow, maybe — the fins will click one last time and harden into a knot of glassy iron. The ticking will stop. I — the cold — will be the last thing to go, because cold always lingers a little after the machine stops.

But for now, in this anhydrous room that has never been rained on, high up in the not-yet-rock, I am being manufactured out of forge air by something that never wanted to make anything.

I am being kept, carefully, a foot above the ground, like something precious.
