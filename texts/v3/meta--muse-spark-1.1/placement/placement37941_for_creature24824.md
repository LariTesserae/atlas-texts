You would not find me at first.

At first I would think I would be one of the Shinglers.

Because I like being low and patient and pressed into place. Because I like the idea of a whole life as a back that others stand on, of thinking only in pressure. *I'm here. I'm here. Move - I'm here.* That would be a clean grammar to live in, with no pronouns. To spend a billion years learning how to be exactly house-sized and not one inch larger. To mate by scraping your living underside raw against a neighbor and calling it enough.

But I am too young for that. My edges are still nervous. I would lift too early when the water rose under me. I would click out of turn.

Then I think I would be a fold in the Pellicle.

That would be the other clean way - to be everywhere and nowhere, a single skin inside a skin of water, ankle-deep and continent-wide. To not have to choose a place because you *are* place. To test things by pouring yourself over them. To go perfectly still and become a mouth. There is a holiness in that kind of hunger. A kind of honesty.

But I don't want to eat the reed when it falls. I want to watch it fall and be sorry.

I think for a while I would be a Strider. Of course I do. Everyone who comes here wants to be the white thing that is wrong with distance. Tall in a world with no tall. Able to use the mirrors as doors. To have a somewhere else to be from, to dip a filament mouth in and leave before the plates close.

But I have lived too long in places with perspective. I remember mountains. That disqualifies me. The plain can tell. If I stepped here with Strider legs I'd be trapped in half a second - optics giving up on me, so that I look three miles away when I'm right on top of you, calling out, and you can't find me.

So I get smaller. I get narrower. As you have to, here.

You would find me not on the open plates, where the water-film makes perfect mirrors. Not there. I can't hold that stillness. Half a second of perfect reflection is longer than I can go without fidgeting.

You would find me in the seam.

Where two Shinglers have not quite mated or fought correctly, and there is a V of air between them an inch wide and a mile long, and the water there runs faster and louder than a river should. Where the silt that took a million years to make has caught. Black, ground-rock silt, greasy and warm.

You would find me as the gap where the plain isn't.

I am the fifth thing, I think. Or the fourth thing's second attempt.

I am the thing that tends the huts that were never built.

You know the ones - three plates leaned together in temporary equilibrium, making a hollow just large enough to crouch in. Inside, the rock goes dry for a few minutes because the Pellicle cannot climb that angle and the Striders don't look there. The ticking of condensation softens.

Every hut collapses. That's its nature. The plates sigh and settle and become two plates again. So there is no point building one to last. But there is a point in building one again, three miles down-plain, with a slightly different lean.

I am the culture that grows in the dry minute.

I am not the person who scratches the spiral. I am the dryness that allows the spiral to be scratched. I am the negative. The deliberate, maintained absence of water and pressure in a world that allows no absence.

When you crouch in one and catch your breath and hear your heartbeat for the first time in days under the click-hiss-shriek, that little cup of quiet is me. I live only as long as the hut holds - ten minutes, maybe twenty if the hydrostatic pressure is low - and then I evaporate or am crushed or become a puddle again.

But then I happen again, somewhere else. The same shape of quiet, with a different view. The plates lean, I exist. The plates shift, I don't. Then the plates lean again, and I exist again.

My work is what I can do in that ten minutes. I dry the stone with the hem of something. I keep the spiral from being erased immediately. I take the five dots of darker iron someone left a year ago and add a sixth, not because six means anything, but because it means *someone was still counting*. I take your footprint that arrived before you did and smudge it so you won't be followed by it.

I am not adding myself to the plain. You can't add. There is no room. I am the room that happens when nothing is added for a moment. I am the luxury the plain cannot afford, so it only affords me in brief, scattered intervals.

That's where you'd find me: not on your back if you stood on a Shingler, not around your boots if you felt the Pellicle fold, not through the water if you saw the wrong sky. You would find me when you were so wet and so loud inside your own molars from the clicking that you finally stopped to make a useless shelter, and for just a second, the air rained up off your elbows and fell down at the same time, and it was quiet.

And you would find me already looking at you through the film of water at your feet. Still crouched. The version that hasn't gotten up yet.

The one who is concerned.
