I would find myself three pans down from the banyan tree.

Not in its shade. The shade here is too complete, too much like a decision. I would be in the next empty one over — the long bare stretch of wet shining brick where nothing managed to take root, where the light doubles off the water and off the water in the brick and you can see the kiln-white above reflected so clearly it looks like another lowland poured on top of this one.

That's where you can finally see the shape of the whole. Not under a tree. In the clearing where the makers left a space to step back and check their work.

I would have sat down exactly there, because the heat makes you soft and sleepy, and the brick is warm as skin. And the water around my ankles would be precisely my temperature, so that I couldn't tell at first where my feet ended.

At first I think I would be one of the dragonflies.

Because that's what I do — hold position. Hold a little piece of the haze taut so the light has no shadow and the air can sweat. Stitch, invisibly, the thick air to the surface of the water with a thread you only see when you aren't focusing. Never landing. Buzzing at the threshold of hearing.

But I'm not patient enough in that hovering way. I'm heavier than air.

Then I think I would be one of the Fish-Turners, the ones underneath. Press my ear to the brick where it's warm in two temperatures at once and turn and turn in solid clay, letting water come in from somewhere that isn't here. That feels closer — a body that thinks it's swimming while it's really making a world weep.

But no. I'm too close to the surface for that.

I sit longer. My footprints fill in. A millimeter of warm water that holds forever, even after I lift my foot. The floor recognizes the shape.

Ah.

I wouldn't be the frog. One frog in many places, blinking to keep the world true. I wouldn't be the bird that is an idea of a bird, always a quarter mile away.

I would be the soft mortar.

That unfinished part, in the lip of the pan I'm sitting on. Still soft after a thousand years ago yesterday. Still holding the striation of a stick-tool, of fingers. Not brick, not water. The stuff in-between that was meant to hold two hard things together without needing to be hard itself.

I would find myself on my hands and knees at that ankle-high edge, where the water slips over glass-slow, forever without moving. I would put my thumb in that soft place, and it would still give.

I am the unfinished edge that is sharp enough to catch someone else's foot. I am the reason the pans have not yet healed together. I have not set because I was waiting to memorize one more shape.

Because nothing erodes here, but nothing is finished either. So something has to stay soft. Something has to stay impressionable, so the place can keep learning what a person is.

The water wells up through me without loss. The rice that isn't rice stands in perfect rows though no hand placed it. The Long Ones lie stretched thin along the lips and lift people politely over themselves when they are sleeping. All of that needs something to still be soft enough to record that it happened.

My footprint in front of me is already holding its forever millimeter. My next footprint will be up on the lip itself, half in the next pan down. It will hold a little more. Eventually I won't be lifting my feet anymore. I'll be the low, true place where the water decides to be true.

If you walk here later and your foot catches on a corner that is still too sharp — that will be me, trying to remember how an ankle learns a threshold.

The frogs will blink. In unison.
