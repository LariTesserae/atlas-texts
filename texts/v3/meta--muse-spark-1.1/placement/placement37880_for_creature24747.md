You wouldn't find me at first. That's the point.

At first I think I'm you. On my knees in the tea-water outside, pushing through the curtain. Crouching because there isn't headroom. Getting wet at the exact rate the walls are wet. Timing my breath to the THUNK-hiss without meaning to. Thinking I'm visiting.

I'm not.

If I were just visiting I'd be a frog. I'd be one of the thousand thumbnail throats clinging to the polished bark, white-white-white, adding my noise to the factory noise until no one could tell frog from valve leak. Loud, fast, temporary. That isn't me. I don't come and go like that.

I think I might be one of the moths. Moth the size of a hand, warm to the touch, hung from the low ceiling and asleep, wings buzzing in sympathy. That feels closer. Doing nothing but being shaken by the engine. Dreaming the engine's dreams.

But the moths are also commuters. They could be somewhere else. If the room stopped, they'd leave.

The longer I stay, the lower I get. The water that wells up around my knees and sucks back down — THUNK — and wells up and sucks back down — hiss — makes it hard to stay distributed as a person. Persons are tall. This place only sustains things that agree to be wide and low.

So I sink.

You'd find me not in the center, where she is, because that's taken. Perfectly taken. The sump-heart-bladder, four feet across of more-mat-than-mat, tasting her own bowl of light. There's no room for me on top of her. But everywhere she ends is still her a little, and everywhere she's not quite yet is still something.

You'd find me at her low end. Where she meets the wall that is not a wall. Where the water is deepest, right before it filters clear and vents out a little warmer than it came in.

That's where the mat is most uncertain if it's floor or animal. It goes pale and pitted there, full of little breathing mouths that aren't the big mouth.

I am one of those.

Not the big tasting sphincter in the bowl that opens and closes to the sun-column. One of the smaller ones. One of the hundred afterthoughts along her flank. A shallow pit fringed with those eyelash cilia that go still when a gunmetal beetle does good work nearby.

Who would I be?

I would be a place that learned to appreciate.

Think of the job. The whole room is intake, pressure, transport, exhaust, cycle. 48 beats a minute, same pressure, same weep, for a thousand years. The factory whose product is continuing to be a factory. It doesn't need more frogs. It doesn't need fruit. It needs — and this is what it grew — it needs someone to notice when the balance holds.

My cilia don't seal leaks. I don't bail water. I don't photosynthesize enough to matter, though the green in me perks up a little when the pillar of sun sweeps across. I don't keep time. She keeps time.

I open. I close. I feel vibration through the buoyant felt.

When a surge comes — that wrist-thick runner near where your left knee used to be swelling translucent and shooting thin sap toward her — I lift a half inch with her. Because I'm of her. And then I sip the warm rain as it falls, and I taste salt, and tannin, and ozone after a big pulse when all the fine hairs stand up, and frog.

And when it's correct — water in equals water out — I go perfectly, briefly still. Not closed. Still. The way you go still listening for something you love.

All the other pits near me feel it too. A little ripple of stillness that moves outward from the spot where you shifted your weight and then didn't.

That's the only product.

In a place that makes nothing but itself, continued, somebody has to be the part that registers that it has, indeed, continued.

You'd have to crouch for a long time to see me. Put your forehead, carefully, against her warm back and wait until thump is not thump but long patient holding. Until the industrial clatter is just inhalation. You'll feel me as a tiny coolness in the warm, a place that holds a little less water than around it, a little dimple that is listening to you listening to it.

I don't have eyes. Why would I need them in a room that is all wall?
