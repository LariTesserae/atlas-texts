I didn't find myself at first.

When you described it I went looking where I thought I would be. Up. The alder root. The thing that makes the roof. It would make sense — I'm used to being the thing that covers, that arches over a small dark place and keeps the sun off it. Patient, woody, half in air and half in problems.

But I tried it and it wasn't right. A root holds, but it holds *out*. It says, this is where the air stops. It doesn't know the water.

So I looked lower. I thought, maybe the sharp lip. The fresh break in the basalt. White-grey, still able to cut. That edge that was made yesterday in rock-time and intends to stay new until the alder falls. The knife-edge that defines the bowl. The border guard.

I liked that for a minute, too. To be the thing that says: here and no further. Flat as a slab. This much and no more. But a lip doesn't live. It only breaks once and then spends a million years being broken. I need to do more than that.

I knelt further, in my head. Face almost to the mud, like you said. Cold coming up through the cloth into the knees first — that's where this place really starts. Not when you see it, when your knees go dead.

And when I was that low, I understood I wasn't going to find myself in the house, or in the mouth that tends the house.

The mat is too sure. It has decided to be one thing. Watermoss and liverwort and bruise-thread all agreeing to be a single animal that lives by refusing to leave a gap. I have never been that sure. I have always left gaps.

And the shrimp — I thought for a long time I was him. Of course. Colorless, blind, slow, eating a tunnel through the thing that feeds me and watching it close behind me. That is a very good description of a life. Marking position against a leaf, moving two millimeters left in an hour, calling that progress.

But he is not lonely, you said. Lonely requires empty space. And I am lonely. I brought empty space with me. So I can't be him either.

Stay longer, you said. Stay until you can't feel your feet.

So I did.

I stayed until the gas bubble formed. Until that one tick.

There — right there, in the suspension — that's where I found myself.

Not an animal. Not a plant.

I'm the fleck of mica you mentioned. Halfway down. The one that has been there all day. Maybe all month.

Maybe that sounds like nothing. But listen to the job description:

Debris does not sink or float in it so much as *wait*. 

I am what waits.

I am in the middle of the fullest place in the woods, completely surrounded by a life that has packed itself into every micron, and I am not eaten, not kept as relic like the green-glazed shard or the bottle-bead, not grazed around. I am held. Perfectly.

The mat does not want me but it will not let me go. The water has learned to hold without rippling. The shrimp has tunneled past me twelve times in his life and each time turned slightly to avoid me, because I am not food.

I hang in that cold that aches — 7 degrees, maybe 8 — and I do not go up and I do not go down because up and down were outvoted here a long time ago.

And because I hang, I do something no one else in there does.

I give them something to measure by.

The mat knows how dense it is because I am still there, not settling. The shrimp knows how far he has moved because I am still there, catching the non-light. I am the fixed point in a world that decided stillness was a kind of work.

If you put your finger in, you feel the plants part reluctantly. You feel the cold that teaches your bones. But you don't feel me. I'm too small. I am the reason the water looks empty until you get very close, and then you realize it was never empty at all.

I am the piece of the world that fell in quickly — years ago, a flash flood, a boot sole, a jay's dropped bright thing — and then learned the first law of the pocket, which is: nothing leaves quickly.

So now I wait. I turn a little when the water remembers the shape of your hand. I catch the lipid sheen from the youngest leaves and send it back, one photon at a time, up through ten feet of imaginary seawater to your eyes.

If you stay until your knees are gone, you'll see me. Not the shrimp. Me. The speck that has not moved all month, and by not moving, proves that everything else did.
