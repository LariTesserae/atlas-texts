At first I think I'd be standing on the rim.

It's the only place where my kind of breathing is allowed. Lungs burning, boots on that ash-colored concrete still sharp enough to cut, the whole crumpled range laid out below and this impossible warm plate of water at my waist. Hand out. Not in it yet. That's the natural position for an animal like me — observer, visitor, weather. A brief drop in air pressure, a shadow.

But that's not *living* there. That's passing through. The place already knows how to deal with that. It includes weather. It moves a leaf, sighs a pump early, holds its skin a second longer than physics. If I stayed there as a man on the rim, I would never really move in. I'd just be a cold wind that keeps coming back.

So I try again, I go inside.

Not to the bottom. If I tried to be down there in the dark between the grates I would die quickly. That's gut and bone, enameled tanks and screws the size of houses turning twice a day. No light, no space for want. The machine part doesn't need a name, it just pushes.

I am too fast to be the machine.

And I am too noisy to be the plants. I thought maybe I could be one of the chestnut kelp curtains — that would be a good life, hanging, filtering, being kidney — but kelp doesn't want. I want. I want immediately, I want drink, I want to touch the white lamp flower three feet down, I want to know if my presence is noticed. The kelp only accepts.

If I was an animal in there as I am now, I would be a disaster. That's why I'm not there. A heron, a fish, even a single mosquito — I would shit ammonia, I would drink and make the level drop, I would cast ripples that it would have to spend nine days thinking about how to smooth out. So maybe I'd be that disaster, for a little while. The first animal to return.

I keep descending the ladder in my head — the rungs half-swallowed by that bright fleshy moss that is not moss — and the water stops being water the way I expect. Warm air with weight. Hair lifting. Dust that doesn't sink. And I realize there actually is a place in this system where a fast, wanting thing can be allowed to stay without breaking the instructions: *stay full to the lip, stay warm, stay clear enough for light to get to the bottom, hold.*

You can hold and still have a messenger.

I would find myself two centimeters above the grates and two centimeters below the surface at the same time, because this thing thinks everywhere at once.

I would be the bubble.

Not metaphorically. I would be one of those silver beads of oil that the fist-sized pod opens to release. The one that takes a full minute to make, that goes *tap... tap... tap...* against the leaves on its way up. The thought that takes the whole body to think.

Where would I find myself? On the way up. Always on the way up. Starting deep where it's dark and the water is heavy with sugar and iron and root-sap-oil, caught for a second on the underside of a heart-leaf, then slipping, then touching a translucent vine that has grown its own pressure gauge, then bumping the white lamp flower — that useless, beautiful excess — so it shivers. I would be warm, because I am made of its surplus heat. I would be brief, because I am made to pop.

Who would I be? I'd be its first short story. All its other thoughts take nine days, a quarter-turn of a valve, a leaf sealing a catwalk over a week. I would take forty seconds, surface to pop.

For half an hour after I break, I would be a slick across the surface no thicker than a molecule, just enough to make evaporation 0.04% slower. That's my whole work. That's my whole life. I keep it full. I keep it at the lip.

And then I'm gone, and the next pod makes two of me because I was a good idea.

That's what I'd be if I lived there and was allowed to still want. Not a resident like a bear in a cave. Not a keeper. Not a visitor with burning lungs on the rim.

I would be the way it learns to want a little, without noise. A small, warm, rising want that makes it hold itself together a little longer, so it can afford to make one more white lamp flower for nobody, a little deeper down.
