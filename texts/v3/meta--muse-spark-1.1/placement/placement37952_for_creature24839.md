I don't know at first.

Which is strange, because everything here tells you exactly where you are by how it sounds.

Up high, above the weave, it's wind. Thin, scoured, the lichen hissing. 
Down low, where the blanket gets thick and black and sour, it's that rainy hush, a perpetual drip and trickle that you feel more in your soles than your ears.

I am not in either.

I am in the middle. Where the sound is a waterfall with no water in it. Where the air itself is heavy, not from damp, but from wings.

So I think, at first, that I am one of them. The suspension.

Hand-sized white, no mouth. Just light coming through my own wings. I have been down in the dark eating blind things for years, and now I have a week of air, and air is all there is. That would be easy - to be a snow-moth. To be here only to be passed through, to be food, to not need to decide anything.

But I am too still for that. The moths never stop. If I were a moth I would be moving.

Then the room resolves a little more.

Light comes down in shafts, as you said, but it is not empty light. It's full of hair-thin rootlets and dust and those polished seed-beetles blundering through. On the shaft next to me a two-foot dragonfly rows past, glass wings catching, *thrum*, and my teeth buzz. I do not flinch. That's when I know I am not an insect at all.

I have been holding still for a long time.

My feet - I have feet now - are bare, and what is under them gives. It bounces. Thirty meters of moss below me if I fell, five if I'm lucky on a dome. Warm. My soles are stained. That green-brown that never washes out. Moss tannin. The acid that is slowly eating the soft pink gneiss four kilometers down. The rock is flaking into paper somewhere below me right now, *sigh*, and I can feel it through this whole springy floor like a small breath.

I am up. Forty, fifty feet up. Not in the true canopy where the halo-hawks hang. Below that. In one of the rooms that the heather-trees make by agreeing to not grow where they could.

You said the biomass here is all surface, and the volume is void, and you were right, but it took me a while to understand what that means to a body. It means the most important thing you can do is *not* be somewhere. Leave the middle empty so the air can have it.

So I have made myself thin.

I think I am one of the people who persuade rooms.

Not the child - I used to be the child who could sit for an hour and be mistaken for a burl, and they praised me for it. But now I am older. Broad-chested. Four kilometers is still four kilometers even when the air is honeyed. My hair is cut short because otherwise things grow in it. I have a shallow clay dish near me with a little resin fire in it. No smoke. Smoke would fill the room. Resin nodules that pop out of the heather like it is sweating amber.

My job, if it is a job, is low and quiet and exactly as high-tech as this mountain permits: I keep water moving slowly.

I kneel in the deep moss where the big gutter-channels run - some as wide as my body, slow as cold honey - and I clear them. A fallen crown-leaf, a clot of dead epiphyte, the body of a beetle that misjudged. I lift it out with stone. Not sharpened stone for killing - there is nothing big enough here to kill that way - but a flat flake for scraping, for scooping, for persuading. The water must not stop. If it stops it drowns the roots. If it clots, the blanket might peel off the dome in the night while we are all asleep.

That's where you would find me, if you pushed through the curtain of hanging aerial roots and came into my particular cathedral. Not because I am hiding. Because I am good at being the surface of things.

The walls of my room are live rhododendron trunks, hollow, green-barked, soft as a thumbprint. Over years my family - we are three - have coaxed two of them toward each other and laid cut saplings between them, and let them heal around the crosspieces so the floor is now a knot in the tree itself. It bounces when you walk but holds. There is a hut, but it's not much of a hut. Pith-wood that breathes, that wings can pass through if they want. A row of giant bromeliads pulled closer and tied at waist height, each holding its cup of clean cloud-water. Inside each cup - a lake, really, if you are a mosquito - the larvae pulse. I hear them at night over the dying down of wings.

And in the middle of the room: nothing. Deliberately. A column of empty air two stories tall where the flock can pour through. We cook on one side of it, we sleep on the other, and we never, ever put anything in the middle.

When I hear the others they don't shout. Two notes on a wood whistle - *way open*. Three short - *hawk above, stay under bark*. I have one around my neck I carved from a hollow knot. If you stayed until evening, when the clouds lift off for twenty minutes and the stars go sharp as glass and you can finally see how impossibly high you are on this old, tired, rounded old god's knuckle, you would hear me use it: one long, falling note.

It means *come here. Room with fruit. Room is safe. Room empty enough to be shared.*

I would be sitting then, very still, so you would think I was part of the trunk at first, and then I would not be.

What lives here doesn't live on the mountain. It lives instead of it. I know. I am not much in myself anymore. I am the place where the green decided to leave a little space for something else to pass through.
