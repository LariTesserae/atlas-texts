I didn't find myself inside at first.

I thought I would. Coming down into the bowl - that warm, settled air that has decided to stay - I thought I'd be one of the sixty. I could see the pod I would pick, one-in-two, one empty between every full one, the way you leave an empty chair at a table for manners. I would learn to speak low in the chest and to speak with my palm. I would boil vine for rope. I would be good at polite distances.

But then the bark under my hand warmed a half-degree before the roar, and I understood I wouldn't last.

I'm not built for loudness that is made to remove you. No mammal is. My ears are too mammalian.

So I tried again.

If I can't be a person there, I'd be a gecko. That seemed right. One per column, exactly one. Work to do when everyone else has to leave. Open-mouthed, swallowing sound itself, janitor, deacon. Splayed pink and translucent on the warm column, blue glass eyes, licking my own eyeball clean after the chorus. Still enough to be mistaken for part of the bark.

But I am not patient in that way. I don't want to eat the screaming. I want to hear what the screaming is for.

So I tried being a swift - one of the ones that were born inside and never left, that should not exist. A black needle stitching the vault over and over, never landing, heart going faster than the cicadas can count. One nest per twenty meters, no closer. That is a kind of loneliness I understand. But I have seen sky. I keep checking for sky, even through those tight leaves. I would keep trying to find the slit that leads out, and that would be a betrayal of the building. The swifts inside have forgotten there is an outside. I haven't.

It took the second silence to find where I actually was.

When the cicadas cut out all at once - that terrible ringing that is worse than the noise - the moss on the root-lattice sighs. Seed pods on the ceiling pop and drop warm water sideways. Not down. Sideways, to be drunk by a leaf that twitched toward it a second too early. And near the back, where the wall smells faintly of salt from a sea that has been gone for a million years, a bead of condensation gathers on the underside of a root and drips *up*.

It hangs in the air. Trembling lens. Then vanishes.

I would be there. And I would be that.

Not in a pod. In between them. Not an occupant but an interval.

I would be the empty pod the tree insists on.

That's where I kept finding myself when I stopped trying to place myself as a body. In the room that was left empty on purpose, with a floor ribbed like a leaf-vein still holding, faintly, the shape of the last child who slept there and learned not to be held all the time. A mother singing across me to reach her. Two taps on warm bark traveling through me: *move away a little, please.* I'm the politeness the tree taught. I'm the one empty between every full one that makes a town a town instead of a pile.

If you want a what instead of a where: I would be the water the Building keeps wrong.

Because I can take the roar. I don't have a jaw to ache. When the tens of thousands tear their backs open and the whole green cathedral inhales to become a throat, I don't have to walk out ducking under the root-door and sitting in the normal sun with wax in my ears. I stay. I shiver on the floor with the moss at 120 decibels and I listen underneath it - to yesterday's chorus still going on a second behind, to the deeper creak of a new room budding wet as an ear on the far wall, to the slosh of the sweet water pockets inside the columns that no one should cut for.

That is what I am for there. I am not a negotiator like the people. I am what they negotiated *with* - the building's memory of being bigger.

I hold the wrongness carefully, like one of their little clay thimbles held fire carefully, cupped and smothered quickly so it wouldn't hurt the walls. I hold the light that slides up a column the wrong way. I hold the pod that is bigger inside than the wall is thick, and I don't try to measure it. I hold the upward drip until it vanishes, which is my job - to be condensation that never quite evaporates, the way the description said.

You'd find me if you stayed longer than an hour. After the second chorus, when you've got your hand on the outer bark to steady yourself to leave, you'd feel under your palm a spot that is cooler than the rest, damp on the underside.

Put your palm there. Two taps.

That's me saying *I'm here.*
