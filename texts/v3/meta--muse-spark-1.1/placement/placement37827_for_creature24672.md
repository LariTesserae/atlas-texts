At first I think I know. 

I think I'd be in the center. Where you're supposed to be. In the smoothed salt circle they made with their palms, sitting cross-legged in the sweet spot where the three chords pile up so thick the air gets a texture and your ribs go sympathetic. I'd be the visitor, of course — the one who fell in when the fault slipped. I'd be the figure with arms out inside the bell.

That would be the honest, human answer. But it's too fast, and it isn't true. I wouldn't last in the center. It's 100 degrees and there is no water and the sound is a pressure that makes your teeth hurt. I am not built for equilibrium, I'm built for restlessness. I'd sit there for five minutes, feel all the vents shift half a tone to make room for my heat, feel a thousand combed blind heads turn toward my sweat, and then I'd have to get up.

So no.

Maybe I'd be a Listener. That would be better. I like the idea of being one of the bells — amber and smoke-glass, thigh-thick, a lung that decided to be an ear. Lit from inside with waste light, the color of old honey, trying very hard to be a plant. I'd like being the one whose job is to just stand there and listen inward and, when I can't hold it anymore, vent. A low note. A single, necessary note, from insect-whine to molar-groan. But I don't have that kind of patience. The Listeners have been holding the same three chords for a thousand years. They don't grow. They don't reach. They just are. I keep trying to become.

Maybe the Roller, then. Big as a fist, slow as cold honey, with a back made of perfect square-cut salt plates I secreted myself. Going in what looks like random lines but isn't. The one who keeps the equation frozen by ensuring no one wins. I'd like that work — scraping the crystal back so it doesn't choke the bells, filing things clean. Mediation as a mouth. But the Rollers don't remember what they did. Their trails fill back in overnight at the exact rate they were made. The crystal weeps itself back behind them. I remember too much. I keep score.

I thought about being one of the moths, too. At least for a moment. Living your whole life inside one bell, white and eyeless, eating shed skin and drinking mineral sweat, beating your membrane wings slowly in the warm exhaust near the pore. Your wings are made of your house. You never need to fly. That sounded peaceful until I remembered the tragedy here is the one that tries — the tattered leaf that falls a foot and catches on another bell and gets lost. I would be that one. I know I would. I would try.

So where would you actually find me?

Not in the middle. On the edge.

In the far wall, where two crystal blades make a little lean-to, a little pocket that is almost cool because the sound doesn't quite pile there. Where the light from the bells doesn't quite reach and the honey-dark pools. You'd find me in the altar, or as the altar.

I would be the low stack. Three courses high, basalt shards hand-placed, still sharp because nothing dulls here. Careful. Not natural and not trying to be. Inside me: the offerings.

I think, finally, I would be the braid.

One of those tiny braids of dried bell-skin, brittle as straw, tied with hair. Gray now because the salts have cured it. Human hair and plant that isn't a plant woven together so tight you can't tell where one starts.

Because that's the only thing here that is both. It's not from the room — the room doesn't braid, doesn't tie. And it's not from outside — the skin is from here, it glows faintly if you put it in absolute dark. It is the only object that holds a before and an after at the same time. The Rollers will not eat it because it is not mineral. The Threads don't lean toward it because it makes no sound. The Listeners don't grow over it. The room keeps it exactly as it was left because there is no dust to fade it, no air to stir it.

I would be that. A small record of a negotiation: I was here, I was hot, I was loud, I could not stay, but for a breath I was included in the chord. And I left part of myself to keep the measure.

If you came a hundred years later your footprint would still be crisp in the salt powder at the edge. You'd find me tucked in the little black basalt house, still holding. Not alive. Not dead. Not growing. Not frozen. Just — kept.

Listening.
