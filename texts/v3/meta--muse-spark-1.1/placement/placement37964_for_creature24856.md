I don't find myself at first.

I think I would be up top. On the bald rose-grey domes, where everything has been old for so long it's become patient. I think I would be the black lichen - the one that is not shadow, that thin skin that makes the stone look cool from far away. Because I like the heat. Because I like being the thing that watches the slit without ever being able to fall into it. Thin, flat, sun-heavy, enduring.

But I get down there eventually anyway. You always do if you walk long enough. The peneplain has no cliffs, no teeth, and then suddenly it has this one place where it didn't close right. And I drop that extra inch.

No, I wouldn't be on the domes.

At first inside the pocket, I'm sure I'm one of the loud ones. Because I'm loud. I'm a chest-ache kind of person.

Maybe I'm one of the lizards with the translucent throat. Pop. Pop. Pop. Out of sync on purpose, stirring the air because still air goes stale. Finding a warm cup of rock and claiming it - this one's mine - and announcing it over and over. That feels right for a few minutes.

Or maybe the wasp. The dried-blood one. The one whose work you feel in your molars. I like work you can feel. Tending the ferns that are the size of doors, sawing, making weight out of sound so the damp gets pressed down into the stone and stays.

But both of those are gut bacteria. White blood cells. They are allowed to live here because they are useful. They are not the reason the place continues.

I want to be the reason.

I listen for what is missing.

You said it - there is one silence shaped exactly like a body. In the cool spots where the nodes drink. Four degrees cooler, ozone and wet iron, the place where the noise gets a little lighter because something took the weight off the top of it.

I find myself there, but not as the lattice.

I am not old enough to be the lattice. I am not warm basalt muscle under a fern root that breathes once every forty seconds and remembers being told to *Keep this place damp* before the idea of being told existed.

I am younger than that.

I am a cup.

One of the little ones. Not the bathtub. The palm-sized one. The perfect circle worn and worn and worn until it is not carved but *convinced* into the rock.

I am up on the north side where the light doesn't hit straight except for seven minutes at noon. I'm half in the shadow of one of those smoke-barked trees that grew sideways. The moss that is dense as muscle has come right up to my lip but it negotiated and stopped - we had room to breathe.

In the morning I hold everything. I am the memory. The stone sweats and beads and runs and tries to leave and I catch it. For an hour I'm a perfect mirror, sky in miniature, amber moth wing, fern frond. Beetles come to me to click and drink. The lizard that is near me looks at himself in me before he pops.

By ten, the lattice starts to drink. I can feel the black hair just above me - a filament that ends a millimeter before my surface, humming, too still to be a vine. It doesn't pull. It coaxes. It reminds the water where it used to be. Higher. Cooler. Home.

Water remembers where it was and tries to go back up.

So I go. Bead by bead, climbing that impossible thread. I thin myself out. I make that small, cool exhale around my node. I do the thing I was worn for: I let myself be taken somewhere else. I vanish into a space that isn't here, into the thin air that smells like wet iron, into the lattice's cool belly.

By noon I am almost empty, just a dark tide-mark and a little silt at the bottom.

And that is when I am most myself.

Because the rule down here is *nothing rots without being immediately used* - and I am the one who makes that possible. I'm the table. I'm the bowl. I'm the pause between sweating and swallowing where everything can come and have a drink and decide if you're compost or company.

In the afternoon, a beetle cleans itself in my dust. At night, condensation starts again on my lip, and I begin to fill.

I am not the one who holds the filaments taut. I am what the filaments were extruded to save.

If you sit in that one meter of unclaimed space with your back against my tree, your boots inches from my lip, you will look right past me because I am only a puddle trying very hard to be a place.

But if you hold your breath during a lull in the sawing, and listen to the basalt heart breathe under the root - in - out - cool - once every forty seconds - you will hear my counterpart on the other side of the seam answer. Another cup, refilling. And another.

We are all of us, cup after cup, the reason the lattice still has something to keep damp.
