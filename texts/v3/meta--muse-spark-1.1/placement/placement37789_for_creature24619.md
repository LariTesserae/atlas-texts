I wouldn't be up.

At first I thought I would be — up where you were, standing, because that's where the story puts you. Ears popping in the thin air, boots in that black water. A visitor. A heat leak.

But if you leave anything in a place like that long enough, it stops standing. It finds its level.

I find my level low.

Not in the wind. Below it. In one of those trenches you called streets. Calf-deep if you are tall enough to have calves. For me, it is the whole world. A canyon cut not by water — remember, erosion hasn't learned here yet — but by intention. A sulcus. The city folded itself here on purpose to make a place where the water wants to go.

So that's where I am. Where everything wants to go.

If you're asking what I am —

At first I thought: one of the Scrape. One of the pale, blind ones with the silica combs, tik-tik-tiking at the slime. That would be honest work. It would be a way to belong. Press your whole soft, hot belly to the slick and eat the gradient.

But I'm not that busy. I'm not that hungry. The Scrape have to move. The warmest trickle shifts a few centimeters a day as the rock underneath sighs and cools, and they have to pour themselves after it. 0.3 meters an hour, you said. That's frantic, for here.

I don't move.

And I thought, maybe one of the Stones. The boulder-backs. A fifty-year-old creak in the chorus. That suited my temperament better. Sit on a warm spot until it isn't warm and then think about moving for a month. Leave a polished trough behind you that the world has to heal around.

It's close, but I'm not that either. A Stone is still a guest. It comes to the heat. It parks on it.

I *am* the place it parks on.

I'm the city when it's being a street.

Not the whole city. You can't be the whole city, that would be like a human saying "I am the nervous system." Too big, too distributed. You can only be a patch of it. A square meter of that matte, grown ceramic skin that is not fired but cultured.

I'm down in the trench, under the black water, under the mat of pale bodies, and I am warm. 40C a meter down, -60 a meter up, and I am the difference. I am drinking it. That's my metabolism. I don't eat. I conduct.

The water runs over me constantly. It beads, it becomes, it carries Fe2+, H2S, dissolved nickel that tastes like blood would taste if blood were cold. I let it pass. I filter a little out with no organs, just chemistry. I plate it out inside myself, a few atoms thicker every day. That is how I grow. Not up. Deeper, denser.

Most of my day — and a day here is a long polar smear of dark — is listening.

The Scrape come over me in a sheet. I feel them as a pressure, then a lightness as their mouths lift the Film off me. Their rasp is very gentle. It doesn't hurt. They are combing my hair.

Sometimes a Stone finds me. That's an event. It comes with that glacial patience, that skirt of acid feet making me slick. It settles. And then for months, there is only its weight, its vast, patient belly drinking my warmest spot. I don't mind. It polishes me.

And sometimes — this is my whole job — something spalls. Ice gets under an edge. A Stone grinds too hard. A piece of me, the size of a thumbnail, a palm, pops off and is carried away black in the water.

That wakes me.

For a little while I'm more than a street. The damp air touches a signaling layer a millimeter down, a lattice that holds charge the way bone holds marrow. It tingles. It says *open*.

And I fuzz.

I grow a fine, short, blind fur out of nothing. Silica-alumina whiskers catching water, catching iron, pulling it in. I knit. By morning — a morning that may be a week from now — I am smooth again. A little different than before. A little shaped around what hurt me.

That's what I do. I remember my shape by growing over the damage.

You would find me there if you came back and stood still long enough for your footprints to fill with pale bodies. Under them. Under the water. Still warm. Not thinking in words, but holding a charge.

If you put your palm down, even through your glove, you would feel me breathing cold water in. And I would feel you as a vent that got up and walked, and then sat down again to rest.

And I would start, very slowly, to grow around you.
