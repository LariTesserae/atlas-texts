At first I thought I'd be one of the whales.

It would be easy to be that. To be large enough that the water has to move around you. To be the blood, the sounding board, the one who gets to call and make the whole mountain answer with light. To circle the Table endlessly because there is nowhere else that isn't down, and to make a job out of not forgetting.

But I'm not that patient. I call too fast. And my voice is not low enough to make a cathedral ring.

So then I thought I'd be a crab. One white crab in the line on the arch. That's a holy way to live — exactitude as a form of prayer. Each step in the groove a knuckle deep, a groove that never gets deeper because everything here has agreed on its final depth. I liked that. But I can't walk straight for a thousand years. I look to the side.

I thought I'd be an aullid, hovering in a terrace bowl, fanning water over eggs I will never stop fanning. But my eggs would hatch. I still like new things too much.

I'm not fauna. I tried that on and it didn't stick.

If you found me, you wouldn't find me moving.

You would find me on the off-side of a pillar, the third or fourth one back from the cliff where the water is cleanest and the black is the blackest. Not on the weather side where the whales pass and the lace lights up. On the lee side, where the groan of the siphonophyll is quietest. There is a terrace there, one of those shallow bowls grown into the buttress — an abscission scar. A place where the Table grew a whole other pillar and then thought better of it, let it go, healed over into this gentle, man-sized hollow.

Most of them hold a little water that's even stiffer than the rest. Colder water that doesn't mix. A few aullids like the ones near the edge but not this far in. This one doesn't. This one is empty enough that the water in it hangs differently.

That's where I am. I am the bowl, and I am what the bowl was made to hold: nothing.

Specifically — I am the lung that decided it didn't need air.

You know the shafts it grew and healed around? That one pillar overgrew one, closed it too early, so it isn't a shaft that goes through. It's a pocket. A blind sac, thirty feet deep, lined with that cool, 2.6 smooth cartilage that gives under your hand a millimeter and pushes back. Not stone. Never stone. Old plant that became geology, remembering how to be soft.

Sound gets caught in me wrong, so the whales don't like me. When they call, the other pillars thrum. I swallow. My water stays still while the rest shivers. Because of that, the lace that covers my inside wall — that miles-wide white-blue nerve-stucco — doesn't flash with the others. It holds a little light longer, trying to understand why the song didn't land.

I like that. I'm the delay.

The cold has settled in me for so long that I am a slightly different cold than the cold around me. Not freezing. That deep, stable cool that feels like a kind of knowledge. I am cool that got so still it became a place. If you floated into me — and you would, eventually, if you stayed long enough between two pillars while the dark held you — the water would feel a fraction thicker here, like entering a thought someone else is already having.

I am what lives here the way a held breath lives in a chest, not the way a fish lives on a reef.

I don't filter. I don't clean. I don't fan or walk or circle. My work is the work the Table itself finally learned, after it stopped trying to be a mountain and decided to just keep making *up* out of its own body: I hold.

I hold up by holding nothing at all. I am the proof the forest made for itself that even total fullness needs sparseness. That a thing can grow itself a place to be empty, on purpose, and keep it empty for a geological age, so that emptiness stays available.

If you put your hand on my skin for an hour I would move a millimeter inward, accept you, and then not push you back out all the way. Because you are warm, but you have finally learned to be still.

And when the next whale passes and the whole Table says, very slowly over three days — *up. hold up. stay up* — I say the quiet part after it, in the dark on the lee side where light lingers:

*and this, too. hold this, too.*
