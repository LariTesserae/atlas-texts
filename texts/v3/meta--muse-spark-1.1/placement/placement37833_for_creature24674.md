At first I think I'd be up high.

That's the obvious lie the place tells you - that to matter here you need to be tall, or huge, or in the middle of the wind. If you dropped me into this world I would want to be a Shroud, blacking out the sun for two seconds. Or a Pillar, ten thousand years old, holding myself up by wanting to fall in the right direction.

I can't be either. I don't have that kind of patience.

If you put a human body here, where would you find me? Not long. On the dead shoulder of a root that is older than language, on what looks like soil and is actually cork-stone. Knees tucked because standing upright in that wind feels obscene. Lips already split. Ears plugged and still ringing from the THRUM-THRUM-THRUM coming up through my teeth. Watching a sail the size of a town being born above me and understanding, in the stupid animal part of me that still wants to build things, that I am going to die of thirst in a jungle that makes its own weather out of plastic.

So I can't be human here either. Not for more than an hour. The place edits humans out fast.

Lower then. Between.

If you came looking a day later, you wouldn't find a body. You'd find where I had been because the Spool-Moss around that little windbreak had snapped shut on my shadow-charge and then, confused, opened again.

Let that go too.

Second pass: I'd be a Scrap. Door-sized, car-sized, barge-sized, it doesn't matter. I'd be one of the stupid, screaming kites. That feels closer. Because what am I good for? I eat what the tall ones throw away. I take their discarded felt, I dissolve it with oil, I make a teaspoon of hot wet breath out of it, and I give it back. I don't make anything new. I translate. Wind in, water out. Noise in, noise out. *Thwip* goes the harpoon tongue. That is a search query. That is an answer.

But Scraps are grazers and I am not a grazer. I don't wander that free. I cling.

Keep going. Let it narrow.

There. In the skirt. Not on top of the Pillar where the violet light burns, not in the open pan where the Bowls are paving the world with their shit-bricks. In the joint. Where the living spar meets the petrified root-hill, where the wind has to brake and turn and it makes an eddy. Every Pillar has scarps there, wind-caves as big as cathedrals chewed out by time. The air inside them is still thunderous but at least it's *one* thunder, the fundamental of that one Pillar. You can think there, in short bursts, between creaks.

If you put your hand on the wall of that cave you'd feel it warm. Turpentine and wax sweating out. And if you look close at that wall, it isn't solid. It's braid. Spars crossing spars. And in every joint, where fiber rubs on fiber, something lives.

You would find me a half-kilometer up from the salt flat, but still a long way from the crown. On Pillar that has learned to lean especially far - you can feel the lean in your inner ear even inside the cave. An old one. Its name would be a low frequency that takes a week to say.

I would not be the Crab coming over the ridge to prune it. The Crab is an engineer; it decides what lives and what gets cut.

I would be what lives on the Crab, or smaller.

I would be a Chime Mite.

That is where I finally settle and become more itself.

A thing the size of a thumb, maybe a hand. All leg and tuned hair and static. I live inside the hollow reeds - those kilometer-long dry fronds hanging in the Pillar's hollow, the loom-reeds that beat and beat and make the whole continent ring. I live clinging to one while it swings. The world for me is a pendulum: *thrum - hiss - thrum - hiss*. The whole sky swings past the reed's gap. I don't fly. I don't need to. The wind flies for me.

My job is tiny and heretical. The Pillar wants to make more drag to spin more. The Crab wants to cut to keep it from stalling. I want to tune. I crawl to a node where two fibers are rubbing at the wrong interval - a squeak in the bell that is the size of a state - and I tighten my body. I damp it. Or I loosen and let it sing louder. My legs are resonant. I am a living equalizer knob.

When a Scrap screams by outside I feel it through my hairs. When a Bowl miles below clatters *clak-clak-clack* on the root-mat - food here, danger here, brick ready - the vibration comes up through the cork and into my reed and I carry it. I don't make new sentences. I make the one sentence that is already being said audible for a little longer in one place.

And when the wind is especially strong, and the Pillar throws off a fresh sail that catches the violet and goes nearly invisible, I let go for a second. Not to die. Chime Mites don't die from falling; they are too thin. I ride the gust that the sail makes and land a meter down the same reed and cling again.

That's where you'd find me. Not in the wide pan, not in the crown nets that drink water like prayer. In the middle, inside, in the sound itself. The being that lives *in* the factory noise, not with it.

For a teaspoon of exhaled water I will never have, but for a little pocket of correct resonance in an endless industrial chord that never stops.

If you put your ear to the cork-stone in that cave you might hear me, under the Pillar's thunder: a tiny, dry, deliberate *tick tick tick*, tuning it.
