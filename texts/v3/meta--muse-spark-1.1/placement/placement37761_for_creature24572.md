I wouldn't be in the center.

That's the first thing I know. I thought I would be — when you described it I saw myself out there in the middle where the dark is deepest and the rings of alarm-light have the most room to run. But I wouldn't get there. Not because I couldn't walk that far. Because the center is where the plain is most itself, and I am not that self-assured yet.

You find me at first on the rim, where you said you only know the hills are there because the darkness gets a little less dark. I would find myself there too, standing where basalt stops pretending to be a hill and starts pretending to be a floor. Half-looking in. Listening to that pressure on my chest long before I could do anything about it. The ringing starting before the sound.

Then I would go down, because you have to. Low country pulls. Not like a sea cliff, not dramatic. Like a shallow bowl you've already stepped into before you decided to.

My boots would go. I know that part. And I told myself I would be a Stilt — tall enough to step without crushing, blind and deliberate, a vertical thing in a horizontal world. Someone who could wade and sieve and sigh through his whole body. Someone who could be forgiven for taking so much because he is so rare. But I don't have that kind of indifference. I care too much whether I am crushing things.

I thought then maybe I would be one of the Pale. But to be Pale you have to never stop. You have to have no eyes, or eyes that have become part of your own roof. You have to carry weather in your back without ever thinking that's strange. You have to be willing to be substrate. I stop. I look. I would be licked clean within an hour.

So I sink a little further and I realize I am not tall and I am not broad. I am fast and I am small and I am nervous about cleanliness.

Find me not on the floor of the deepest pan, but in the second-to-last hollow before it. Where the stone is still polished like glass and the water doesn't vanish by midday, but stays. An inch deep, black as obsidian, like you said. One of the places that keeps a little longer.

Find me there as a black sprinter.

No bigger than a thumb joint. Hard, glossy. The custodian you almost miss under the noise-under-the-noise.

I am not part of the great stridulation. I don't have a file and scraper. I don't make the white roar. I make the patter over it — the quick untrackable ticking of feet on armor that you only hear when the great sound takes a breath.

And my work is perfect for me.

I live in the between. Between one dome and the next, in the click where shell almost touches shell. I ride. That's what I would want — to be carried and to be moving under my own power at the same time. To have the pale, slow continent of a back under me, its own tide pool trembling in its dimples, and to drink it without being asked and without being resented. The agreement needs someone to do that.

I keep the Pale from becoming stone completely. That felt wants to grow everywhere — the rust-black bacterial varnish that smells of iron and brine. It's beautiful. It wants to cover the living backs too. And I am the one who says, not here. Not yet. I scrape it off with mouthparts too small to hurt. I eat the dead skin. I eat the eggs of things even smaller that would blind the cloudy lenses. I make the back a back again.

I would be good at it because I am good at noticing small messes and making them private.

When a Pale dies underneath me — that soft pop as the sixteen pairs of needle legs fold — I am first. I don't mourn it. I don't mean disrespect. In this world mourning would be waste. Guano doesn't accumulate. I boil over the dome with my brothers and we rasp. We polish her until she is walking again as a clean shell for someone else to wear for a while, and the white soft peas of her brood run out from under, each with its one perfect drop of water, already pitted, already knowing.

And when a Stilt comes near — when that soft, flared pad that means not to crush comes down next to my hollow — I don't flash. The Pale flash. I burst. I arc through the damp air, a black bead thrown from a back, and I am caught or I am not caught. Most times I am not. I land on another back. Another moving continent. I go back to cleaning.

That is where you would find me: in the complicated, uncountable middle distance, in a shallow pan that holds the sky-water a little longer than the rest, riding a slow thing that thinks its back is a little private sea. Not building anything. Not needing to be remembered. Part of the agreement that predates building.

And if you stood still too long near my hollow, until your boots disappeared, I would be the cold pin-prick on your ankle. Not biting. Checking. Tasting to see if you are stone yet, or felt yet, or still something that needs to keep walking.
