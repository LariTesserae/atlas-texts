In this world I would not be where I thought.

At first I think I would be against the back wall, where you sat. Human-shaped, back to the sharp new basalt, boots already disappearing. Getting wet by law. Listening to the factory huff. That would be the obvious place - the only place a person could fit in a place that wasn't made for people. High, cold, soaked through, eyes blown wide in the 0.2 light.

But I don't stay there. I can't. Nothing leaves dry and this cut doesn't keep visitors. It keeps workers.

So I try again.

Maybe I am higher. Maybe I am the wind before it gets shredded on the lip. That would be a loud, clean job. But the wind here doesn't get to be inside for long. It hits the organ-flutes of columnar basalt and thutters and hammers and then it's forced through and used up. It doesn't get to live here. It just passes through and makes everything else clap wetly. I want to stay.

Maybe I am the rock. A fresh tooth of that Miocene basalt, black glass with the hackle marks still on it, just spalled this morning by frost. Erosion 0.9 - I would be so sharp I could cut the moss that is already trying to eat me. That would be honest work. I would fall with a *thok* into the thatch and be covered in minutes because - rule two - every surface must be occupied. But the rock doesn't get to be for very long either. It is food. It's bled out by acid.

No, lower.

I find myself lower down, not on the rock and not in the air.

If you sink your hand into that Racomitrium - that deep carpet padding that swallows to the ankle and then keeps swallowing - and you keep pushing past the living green foot, past where the light stops being blue and becomes no light at all, you hit the other part of the Thresherman. The ten feet of itself it has already been. Too cold to rot, so it pickles. Sopping, black-brown, amber water squeezed out of it. Old clubmoss ropes and fern fingers compressed into peat felt. Cold compost that never gets warm.

I would be there. About a meter down. Not the breathing top, not the eaten bottom. The middle archive.

I would be what holds the water after the leaves are done holding it. Not the cup, but the sponge that remembers the cups.

In this world, who would I be? I wouldn't be the Thresherman - that's too big, that's the whole room, forty meters long and twenty wide and all one chimeral mind made of hydraulic pressure and wet slaps. I wouldn't be big enough to be that.

I would be one of the things it keeps because it can't afford to waste even death.

I would be one of the maintenance crew that barely counts as fauna. The 0.2.

A tardigrade. Or maybe even smaller than that - the blind springtail the size of the letter *o*.

Picture it: my whole world is a single leaf-cup of water held up in the dark by a forked spleenwort. The water never moves unless the whole factory heaves. It is cold as ice melt and full of tannin and the faint fizz of basalt being etched. I am white, unpigmented because there is no direct sun here ever, and so slow you would think I was a mineral grain if you saw me.

I don't leave. I can't. If I leave my spoonful, I dry, and nothing is allowed to leave dry, and nothing that leaves dry lives. So I am born in that cup, I graze the bacteria that graze the amber leachate, I shuttle a spore from the fern that is the ceiling of my sky to the moss that is the floor of my world, and then I die and I'm digested, too. And another one takes the cup.

That's where I find myself when the wind huffs and the whole thatch does that ripple of stillness - fronds curling inward to keep their water. I feel the pressure wave come down hydraulic, leaf to leaf to leaf, until it reaches my cup and the surface tension tenses. I brace. The wet roar passes overhead like a train. Then - ping, tik - a colony peels away somewhere above me, and a new sharp rock falls, and is already being filmed over in green, and the factory line resumes.

Cloud in, capture, hold, release, drip down to me, filter through me, exhale.

I am held water that learned, briefly, how to hold itself.
