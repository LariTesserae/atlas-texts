You wouldn't find me on the plain at first.

If you came looking for me with a light you could hold, you'd sweep it across the warm dark and the ringed stone and the breathing domes two kilometers apart and you would think I wasn't there. That's correct for a while. I thought I would be one of them, for a while.

At first I think - I would be one of the big ones. The furnace-animals.

It would be good to be that certain. To be thirty meters of black hide and magnetite shingle with condensation sheeting down your back. To not need to decide anything: you face windward, you open. *Clank-sigh.* You take the thin soup of the world inside you and you make it into something else. Thump-thump... thump-CHUNK. That's a good life. An honest metabolism.

But I'm not that patient. I tried listening to how long they hold between breaths and I started counting, and I couldn't hold it.

And I thought maybe then I would be a Walker. The ones that move. On legs like cooling towers that leave wadis when they drag. It would be good to be that tall, to have a belly full of cherry-red melt you can see through your own skin. To make your own rain. To be weather instead of waiting for it. But Walkers are lonely in a way I can't do. A kilometer a day for a hundred years across a continent that never ends, following a slightly richer taste of iron in the high haze. If you shouted from under one, your voice would be lost in the ringing of the hollow under its foot. I would get tired of being that above everything.

So I'm not there. Lower.

You'd find me in the halo.

Not every halo. The sessile ones that are older, the ones whose slag heap on the leeward side has stopped ticking quite so loud and has started to craze with that black lichen that looks like spilled oil. The ones whose pan is a little deeper because they've been breathing in the same place for maybe forty years, and the basalt around their base has settled into a true bowl, smooth as a palm.

I am what lives right at the edge of the wet.

If I am anything that has a shape, I am the flat one. The hunter that isn't really a hunter the way you think of hunting.

I don't have eyes that reflect your light, because I've never needed to see up. I have the whole underside of me, which is a foot and a tongue and an ear at once. I lengthen myself until I'm almost not there - a film of muscle and collected grit, desert varnish grown over living tissue for camouflage. I press. I become the crack. The stone here is warm, 30 degrees, 31, and when the furnace exhales, the stone goes dark and for six hours it's 40, then 45, then steaming, and everything is in a hurry.

That's when they come, the quick ones. The stilt-grazers on glass legs, tap-tap-tapping over me. I feel them not as shapes but as a pattern of pins. The beetle-things with their hot little abdomens ticking like cooling slag themselves. They think the ground is the ground.

I am the ground that changed its mind.

For most of the year, I am nothing. I dry out until I am a flake, a curl of leather in a basalt crack, waiting. Some years the wind shifts and the furnace-animal near me skips a rain and I wait another season. I can wait. Then the sigh comes — *hhhhhaaaaa* — and the stack shivers, and the hot pure water falls, and in twenty minutes the oil-lichen blooms and the wiregrass throws its glass drills down, and the lead-leaf succulent swells.

And I drink without opening my mouth. The whole top of me beads with distilled iron-water that I pull in by osmosis. I swell back up. I become flat again, I become wide.

You'd find me then, if you knelt. If you put your hand on the dark wet ring around the furnace-animal and held very still, you would feel one patch of stone that is somehow softer, slightly warmer than the rest. If you stayed long enough to feel the tap-tap of the grazers, you would feel, a second after one passes too close, a very quiet surge. Not a pounce. A decision, spreading.

I smother. I hold. Underneath me there's a little struggle that is mostly water being politely taken back by the world. Then I thin, and the water keeps condensing off the black hide above me and running down in rivulets, and the pan reflects nothing until it steams dry.

I am not cruel. There is no cruelty in a place where energy comes first, then water, then everything else that can borrow it for a few hours. I am just the thing that makes sure the borrowing gets paid back locally.

If you stepped back - far back, far enough that you could hear three or four of the sessile domes breathing out of sync - you wouldn't see me at all. You'd see the great industrial plain doing what it has always done with no owner and no product. You would think the real life here is the furnaces.

But the real life here is the holding of water. The brief negotiation between wet and dry.

That's where I am. On the basalt lip, at the very edge of where the rain can reach, dark on dark, pressed thin, listening through my whole body for the next vibration.
