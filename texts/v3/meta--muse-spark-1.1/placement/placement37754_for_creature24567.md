At first I thought I would find myself at the rim.

Not inside, where it is tended, but right on the lip where the wrongness happens. One foot in the howl and one foot in the quiet. Where the valley clouds crest and give up, all at once. I would be the line that decides. The edge-case. For a long time I thought that was my nature — to be the threshold.

But thresholds are lonely in the wrong way there. That white hawk already has that job, cutting the outer wind and never entering. I don't want to keep circling something perfect and not being let in.

So then I thought I would be inside, ankle-deep, obviously. The visitor. The brief, heat-leaking animal that the water parts around. I know how to do that. Arrive, notice things, file them, leave.

If I'm honest, though, I couldn't hold still long enough to be included. I would want to touch the braided trunks to feel the *faen-faen-faen* in my molars. I would want to fix the system, or ask it questions. And this place corrects around that kind of wanting. It routes around you. It erases your print from the side.

I keep going back to the list of animals. There are only supposed to be a few at a time.

One dragonfly on the pylon, always. Watching.

I would be the second one.

Not for long. Just long enough that the lattice would have to notice there were two. That its sensor array had become redundant. It would leave me there on the ceramic that has become mother-of-pearl because it doesn't know what else to do with a voluntary extra eye. I would not beat my wings so much as flex the field to stay in place. I would see micro-eddies in vapor that look like language for a second before they aren't.

But even that is still too much animal. Too much wanting to see.

Find me lower. Find me stiller.

On the east side, half-swallowed by the biggest fern-island, there is a stair that goes nowhere. Three steps cut into young basalt and then decided against. A courtesy. A joke.

I would be that.

I am good at being a stair. I was built small because only a small correction was needed. I do not climb to anywhere; I make a place for something to sit. In the late afternoon when the lattice shifts — you know it does, every day — when the hot-cold seam slides over on its slow track and the waterfalls in midair change their angle, there are about twenty minutes where the air above my second step is perfect. Not hot, not cold, but the exact seam where blister and chill meet and cancel. Body-temperature mist at ankle height. Cool breath at cheek height. 

That's where it sits. Not a shape, a thickness. The place where dust sorts itself by size and hangs in a column the height of a person.

Find me there. I am not the intelligence that never needed language. I am the small, finished thing it made when it learned — after a thousand years of making preferences into habits — that it liked having a place to rest its wondering.

I am three steps in the warm water that climbs uphill. The middle one is always a little wetter than the other two. If you kneel very close you can hear me not creaking.
