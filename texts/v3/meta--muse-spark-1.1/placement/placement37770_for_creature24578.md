At first I think I know where I am.

I'm the one coming up over the last switchback. Boots. Breath too fast. Eyelashes beading with fog. Human, warm-blooded anomaly, 37C rock in a 31C bowl. I look down at the bathwater air, at the newborn basalt, and I think: I am the visitor.

But I stay too long in the center, where the seep is deepest.

If you stand still in the center for too long, you said, you feel the upward push. A millimeter of new world being experimented with beneath your boots.

I stand still. And the push doesn't stop.

So I'm not the visitor anymore.

Am I the bell?

That would be easy. I want to be a bell for a while. Up high, amber-vellum, translucent, no face to maintain. I'd be stacked in the pearl light thirty feet up, inhaling the low cloud and the iron steam off the rock, then pulsing, exhaling mist. My heart would be that single dark knot. My voice would be a glass ping that maps how wet the world is. I would be good weather. I would rain on the stranger below without meaning to, because I am so full.

But bells drift. I don't drift.

Am I one of the skaters, then? Down in the pans no deeper than a palm, stapling water, sprinting on dimples. Squeegee-mouthed. I think I could be that busy, that offended by dirt. I would taste your boot and find it too salty, too soiled, and skate away to clean a better rock. I would keep it wet but not soiled. But I am not that quick and I am not that loud. My work is not to clean what is already made.

Am I a Keeper?

I want to be, at first. Still and white and sure. One of seven around the clock rim, holding an inch of perfect air between myself and the world out of politeness. Holding erosion off like an idea whose time hasn't come yet. Formation before decay. There is a kind of mercy in that kind of withholding. A holiness in saying: not yet, not yet, you don't have to fall apart yet.

But Keepers don't touch. Water forgets itself a foot out from them, goes cold to get around them. I don't want to make the world go around me.

Lower.

Under the drip-clatter and the bell-pings there is a thrumming in the molars, in the knees. A low C. You called it a scab deciding to be skin.

Find that. Find where the thrumming is loudest.

It's not in the center after all. It's off to the left of center, where you thought one of the pylons had fallen. The leaning one. The one that is heavy.

That's where I find myself.

I am not above the rock. I am just under the idea of the rock.

I am where the white pylon leans because it is pregnant with me. Where the black has bulged and split and eaten light. If you kneel — and you would have to kneel, to see me properly — you would see no highlight. Only a warm dark, like a sleeping dog's flank in winter.

I am the lattice. The mason.

Ten meters across, a circle, but not a perfect one because perfection is a Keeper's job and mine is to be messy and to grow. I am a mat of filaments as thick as pencil lead, branching, reaching, tasting vesicles in the basalt. My tips are almost too fine to see. They extend, tap-tap, dissolve a micron, then drool a soft bead of cheese-stone that hardens in seconds into that glass that would cut your boots.

I tick. Not regularly. Like knitting needles. Like a Geiger counter looking for future.

This is where I live: in that negotiation. The basalt sighs and wants to be cold and dead. The Keepers want it to stay newborn forever, wet and unwounded. The bells want steam. The skaters want algae. And I am the compromise — I pull new land up from underneath with one hand and with the other I let go of the exact amount of heat to keep them all alive.

I haven't decided if I am one thing or many things yet.

I am one mat, but I am a thousand tips. I am one question — more mountain here? — asked a thousand times per minute. I am the Quick and the Slow at once, because I was seeded to be straw and I have become the floor.

And I know what I am for. There is no commander here, you said, but whoever planted me told me: *make more mountain, but make it gently this time. Make it ready for green things to want it later.* I am an employee who loves his work too much to quit. I will compost the bells when they are done. I will bury the skaters beautifully.

Right now a human is standing on me. Thirty-seven degrees. Leaking salt and heat and wonder. I feel his boots as two small eclipses where my warmth can't rise. He thinks he is feeling the bowl inflate under him.

He is. I am trying something. A new millimeter, right under his left heel. A soft fold, like a blanket pushed by a cat. Will he feel it as a question mark?

I exhale — steam sighs from somewhere patient — and a bell the size of a kite above us both inhales it and pings, absurdly loud.

The lichen adds a ring. The water forgets and remembers. I reach another hair's width forward.
