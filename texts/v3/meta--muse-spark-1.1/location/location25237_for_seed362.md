It is low. Not low like a valley — low like the bottom of something that was made to be the bottom.

If you stood a mountain here it would not fit. Not because the ceiling is too close, but because the idea of a mountain requires ground that isn't fabricated. Here, there is no ground.

There is only Floor.

The Floor is poured, and recast, and poured again. Centimeter on kilometer on centimeter of pale concrete aggregate, iron rebar like old roots, ceramic drain-plates stamped with serials that have worn smooth. It is cold under everything. Cool to the palm, cool to the cheek. It never held groundwater because there never was any. This entire continent-basin — and it is continental — was sealed before it was grown.

And it was grown.

At first you think it is a forest. That is a reasonable mistake in the white light. Then you see that it is not growing *on* anything. It *is* the thing.

The Cultivar fills the basin from end to end, horizon to horizon, far beyond where horizon should be. It is total. Not trees among buildings, not vines on girders. Plant-as-building, plant-as-city, plant-as-atmosphere. Trunks the circumference of silos, but they are not wood, not anymore. They are dry, they are thirstless. They learned a different chemistry.

There is no water here. The air registers 0.3 on any scale you'd care to name — not arid, but *abnegated*. No dew. No sap that runs. If you snap a leaf — and you shouldn't, but you will — it does not bleed. It sighs. Inside is not moisture but densely felted fiber, white pith like paper insulation, a lattice of hollow glassy threads that catch light and carry it down. These plants do not drink. They respire mineral dust. They photosynthesize directly from the glare.

Because the glare is total.

Above, the light is not a sun. It is suns. Or one sun that was taken apart and stapled across the sky. Long, bright, cool-white industrial banks run in parallel for thousands of kilometers, row after row after row of day. No shadows move. Or they move so slowly that a shadow from a leaf-lattice will etch itself into the Floor for a week and then, with a click, jump a meter when a bank cycles. The light is bright enough to bleach color out of your eyes. Everything is the green-white of old paper and the bone-white of the Floor and the light-white of everything else. It is cool under it. You would expect heat from that much photon pressure. Instead the air stays at the temperature of a spring refrigerator, a clean 8 or 10 degrees. Your breath never fogs. The plants like it that way.

And they are dense.

You cannot walk *through* them. You walk *in* them. Corridors are left for you, or grown for you. Canopy bowers roofed with interlaced fronds thick as felt blankets. Root-walls that are not roots but structural columns, fluted and rifled where machine tools have guided their growth. Here and there the original industrial truth shows: a trunk shudders and coughs and you see the gantry inside it, the old rust-ring where a forming collar once held it, the tattooed barcode on bark that has stretched as it grew. All of it built. The forest is a factory that builds itself out of plant. The factory is a forest that decided to be built.

Listen.

It should be loud. At this scale, industry should scream. But the sound scale here is near zero.

There is quiet so profound it has texture. The machines that tend this place — because something *is* tending it — are old enough to have learned silence. You hear faint things: the tick of cooling ceramic. The sigh of a pneumatic seed-loft releasing a cloud of dandelion-like chaff that never needs water to germinate, only a lit surface and a static charge. The creak of a leaf-broad as a warehouse roof slowly re-angling to catch more light. The whir, far above, of a harvester on rails that runs the length of the continent, clipping, taking, processing, baling nothing but dry fiber into more Floor, more gantry, more light-bank.

It is established. Nothing here is collapsing. Nothing is young. The erosion is not of water or wind but of time wearing grooves into purpose. The edges are all rounded. The concrete lips are polished by centuries of feet that weren't feet. The air itself has been cycled so many times it feels filtered to its essentials.

There is weather, but not water weather.

In the vast interior, where the canopy breaks for what must have once been a courtyard the size of a country, electrostatic storms gather. You see them coming from a day away: a darkening of the white, a lifting of the dry leaf-fluff. The Cultivar holds its breath. Then the air fills with floating, luminous dust — spores, chaff, cellulose ghosts — swirling in slow cyclones that generate their own soft lightning. Silent flashes with no thunder. The dust falls like snow that will never melt. It accumulates on everything, a centimeter a day, and by morning the machines and the root-walls have taken it back in.

That is the dynamic. Not predator-prey, or only barely. Growth and harvest and re-growth, at biological pace but industrial scale. The system breathes.

And it breaks reality a little, because how could it not?

You notice after a few hours that gravity is polite but indecisive. Down is mostly Floorward, but in the densest bowers, where the vegetation has grown into thickets of solid fiber, down becomes inward. Leaves grow toward each other, not toward light. A corridor will loop you back to where you started with one extra turn you didn't make. If you stare up, the light banks repeat a little too perfectly; for a second there are two of you casting shadows in slightly different directions. The Cultivar has grown through so many layers of space that space has learned to grow around it.

That is why there is no fauna. Almost none.

Once in a very long while, you will hear something other than machine or creak. A skitter. Rare. It is a maintenance thing that evolved. Or a bug that evolved into a maintenance thing. White, dry, jointed, the size of your hand, made half of chitin and half of stray fiber. It doesn't eat. Nothing eats water here because there is none. It gathers charge. It trims a leaf. It disappears into a seam between Floor and root that was grown specifically for it to disappear into. You are more alone than you have ever been in a place so completely full.

If you keep walking — and you can walk for weeks, for lifetimes, and the bower will still be there, and the light will still be bright and cool — you will eventually find a place where the Floor lifts. Not a hill. A manufactury altar. A weaving loom the size of a city block, its shuttles empty, its warp made of living, dry, endless thread. It is still running. It has always been running. It weaves more forest out of forest.

Everything is made. Everything is alive. Nothing is thirsty.
