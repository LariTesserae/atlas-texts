It is low. Not low like a valley floor you can walk out of, low like the world hasn't decided to have height yet.

The whole country is a fresh pour. Black ground, soft and ruinously hot, still holding the heat of its making. Basalt that never quite got hard, or mud that never quite got soft — a raw, pimply plain that goes to the horizon without ever committing to a hill. You can see for miles because there is nothing to block you, and you can see nothing at all because everything is in the way. That's the trick of it.

The ground is embryonic. No channels, no gullies, no rivers with names. Erosion hasn't learned its job yet. Water doesn't cut. It sits, it sweats, it waits.

Because the air is wet. Not the clean wet of a lake, but the inescapable wet of a place that is breathing on itself. A low cloud that never became weather presses down to about twice head-height and stays there, thick as gauze soaked in hot water. Light filters through it as if through amber — dim, orange-brown, sourceless. No sun visible. No shadow sharp enough to point. The light is the same at what would be dawn and what would be noon. The temperature is the same too. A deep, fleshy, equatorial hot that doesn't spike and doesn't break. It just is. 104, 110, 115 — the numbers stop mattering. Your skin stops measuring after the first minute.

And from that cloud, water is always coming down without raining. Condensing on everything. Beading on leaves big as tabletops, swelling, and dropping straight down into the same warm puddle it came from. The weeping rocks do it too — the black lobes of new earth are pitted like lung tissue and they perspire continuously. Drip. Drip. Drip onto black mud. The puddles never get deeper. The mud never gets drier.

This would be a desert of hot stone if it weren't so crowded.

Vegetation came first and came all at once. There are no trees exactly, because trees require time to become trees. There are proto-trees — dense clumps of things that wanted to be fern, wanted to be palm, wanted to be reed and couldn't choose. Their trunks are not wood yet but packed fiber, wet rope, green-black and fibrous, sprouting from the same root-mat. Leaves unfurl already rotting at the edges, already being eaten, layer on layer. Every vertical inch from ankle to far above your head is stuffed. Fronds shoulder fronds. Vines that are barely distinguishable from roots loop through everything, flowering with fleshy, scentless bells that never close. The ground is not ground but a mattress of living and half-living: leaf litter that never dries enough to become duff, but never wet enough to wash away. You step and it gives an inch, sighs, and stays stepped-on.

And the fauna. 

God, the fauna.

It is not that you see animals. It is that there is no space that is not animal. The air itself is animal. Clouds of midges and gnats and things with no name but size, in strata — low heavy beetles thudding like thrown seeds, mid-level mosquitoes in a whining sheet, high glittering flies that catch the dim light and throw it back. On every leaf, something sticks: frogs the color of wet leaf, frogs the color of black stone, frogs translucent with their green gut visible. Salamander-things with external gills fanned like ferns, breathing the air-water. On the trunks, insects so thick the bark appears to move — armor-plated, overlapping like shingles, mouths constantly working, not eating so much as sanding down the plant matter into finer plant matter. Things chirp, croak, buzz, click inside things that croak.

It is overwhelming not because it is diverse — it is — but because it is saturated. The biomass budget is bankrupt. There is more living than there is place to put living. You inhale and some of it inhales you back.

It is very loud.

Not chaotic-loud. Not jungle-dawn-chorus-loud. Equilibrium-loud. A single sustained chord made of a billion tiny sounds that never rises and never falls. The crickets will never stop because they never started. The frogs call, and two seconds later call exactly again, exactly the same length, as if looped. One leaf drips every four seconds forever on the left. One beetle rasps its leg every 1.3 seconds forever on the right. The whole valley is frozen in a perfect, hot, wet roar. Nothing echoes because the air is too thick and the leaves too close.

That is the rule here, the only rule that matters: nothing changes.

Not that it is still. It is *static* in the thermodynamic sense. Water condenses on the leaf and falls to the puddle and evaporates back to the leaf at the same rate, so the puddle never drains and the leaf never dries. The plant grows a new frond as its old frond is eaten, exactly, so the density never thins. The insect lays an egg where another insect just died, in the divot its body made. The heat never dissipates because the cloud never moves. This place was born like this a minute ago or ten thousand years ago — you can't tell, and it can't tell either. It's pre-history. Pre-erosion. Pre-weather. A world on the first day that forgot to have a second.

If you stand long enough, the gnats will find the exact shape of you and hold it in the air after you move. If you stand longer, the mud will make a perfect copy of your feet and keep it. The forest will grow around your shoulders without getting any thicker. The dim light will not shift. The weeping rocks will continue to weep onto a ground that will never be washed away.
