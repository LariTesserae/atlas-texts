It is high. It is too high for this much green to make sense.

You are in a cut, not a valley. A room punched into the side of a mountain at about four thousand meters, where the air is thin enough that every breath feels accounted for. The scale is wrong for the elevation — you expect, up here, vastness, scree and distance, but this place is small and has edges. Maybe forty meters long, twenty wide, a single feature-area with a back wall and a lip. A hanging cirque, a bite taken out of a young ridge that hasn't learned to be round yet.

The rock is new. That is its character. Erosion 0.9 — everything is sharp. The ridge is Miocene basalt that broke yesterday in geologic time, a black glassy stair-step of columnar joints, still with the hackle marks of its cooling. The frost never stopped working it since. Nothing is softened. Every ledge is a knife, every boulder is a freshly fallen tooth with unlichened faces where it sheared. You can see the mountain is still deciding what shape to be.

And yet it is choked with life.

Vegetation 2.5 is an understatement when you are inside it. For there being no soil to speak of — only powdered rock and the residue of moss — there is too much. An impossibility of green in the dark. Because it is dark here. Light 0.2 — the permanent dark of extreme topography. The cut faces northeast and is overhung by a greater wall above it that never lets the sun in directly. What light arrives is bounced, bruised, late. A deep blue hour that lasts all day. On overcast days, which are most, it's close to true night at noon. Your eyes adjust and then you realize: it's not that there's nothing to see, it's that everything is so dense you can't see far anyway.

The cool does that. Temperature 1.2, maybe 6 or 7 Celsius in summer, just above the dew point always. Your breath fogs even as your hands sweat inside your gloves from pushing through growth. Cool enough that rot is slow, so everything layers.

It is not a forest. Trees can't hold here, not in this wind and this room. It is all low, and all crowded. Density 2.1. Knee-to-chest high, a brutal thatch of alpine clubmoss, filmy ferns, forked spleenwort, woolly cushions of Racomitrium moss so thick you sink to your ankle and then stop because another mat is underneath. Everything growing on everything else. Fronds growing out of moss growing out of fern root growing out of a crack in basalt. No gaps. You have to part it with your arms. The leaves are wet, always wet, but not from rain.

Water 0.6 — damp is the only word. It's not a wet place. There is no stream running through it, no pool. But it is never dry. Condensation is the climate here. The cold rock breathes. Cloud gets snagged on the lip of the cut and is forced through, and it condenses on every frond. Every five or ten minutes a drip comes off the overhang and tracks down the back wall in an intermittent black line. You hear it coming before you see it. In the moss, water exists as a held thing, not a flowing thing — pockets, sponges, a thousand tiny reservoirs cupped in leaves, cold as ice melt.

There is no built. No trails, no cairn, no wire, no piton scar. 0.5 is generous — it's essentially untouched, because no one has a reason to come up to a north-facing pocket with no view and no flat ground. And there is no animal to bring you. Fauna 0.2 — nothing. No birds calling, no marmot whistle, no insects, even. Too high, too dark, too cold for them, or maybe too newly made. The absence is strange in so much lushness. You expect midges, at least. There is nothing moving except the plants and the rock and the water. Which is what makes the sound so loud.

Sound 1.7. It's loud here, absurdly loud for a plant-choked hole with no animals.

First, the wind. At this elevation the wind never stops, and this cut is a reed. When it hits the columns of basalt on the outer face, it is forced through vertical flutes and slots and it howls, then thutters, then hammers. A low, industrial huffing. Not like wind in trees. Like wind in a factory with its doors open, moving past big empty ducts.

Second, the plants themselves. They are so dense and so constantly wet that every gust creates a million slaps of wet leaf on wet leaf. A hissing, shushing, clattering roar. Not a rustle. It is too wet and too crowded for that lightness.

Third, the water and the growth, because this is dynamic 2.9 — turbulent, anthill energy, not peaceful. This is not a stable alpine garden. It's in flux. The moss is growing visibly fast in the constant damp and the endless twilight, a few millimeters a day, and as it swells it tears. You hear soft pings as colonies peel away from the rock under their own weight. Frost heaves daily. Small rocks — sharp, new ones — constantly spall off the back wall and fall into the moss with a heavy *thok*, disappearing without a trace. Water expands in every crack. On the overhang, condensation beads, gathers, falls, starts the whole cycle over. You can hear the mountain ticking with cold contraction like a cooling engine block.

This is the tech. 1.8 — industrial. Not because there is anything made here. There really is not. But the place functions like a mill with no one to run it. There is a natural mechanization that is impossible to ignore. The hydrology is a system of batch processing: cloud in, capture on a million leaf surfaces, hold, release, drip to lower moss tanks, filter through root mat, exhale back out. The air moving through rock is piston work. The rockfall is output. The moss is production at truly industrial scale — biomass doubling and collapsing and doubling again in weeks, not seasons, in the cold and the dark, fueled by whatever mineral pours out of the young rock.

It follows rules that feel imposed rather than grown:

1. Nothing leaves dry.
2. Every surface must be occupied.
3. Everything that happens happens loudly and quickly, and then is buried by green.

If you sit — and you have to, because pushing through that dense, cold thatch in thin air exhausts you — your back against the sharp new basalt, you become damp yourself in minutes. The moss soaks your coat. The cold seeps in. The darkness makes your pupils wide. And the whole small room of the world clatters and thumps and drips and huffs around you like a night shift in a plant that makes only itself, endlessly, out of rock and cloud.
