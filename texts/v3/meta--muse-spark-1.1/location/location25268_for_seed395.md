It is low. That is the first thing your body understands before your eyes do.

The air is heavier here, warm and settled, pressed down into a basin so broad you cannot tell it is a basin at all. The horizon doesn't rise, it just thins. You stand on ground that has just been made — not metaphorically. This is a plain that is still deciding what a plain is. A continent-sized lift of raw mineral, exhaled up from beneath and left to cool.

There is no river that cut it. No rain has ever shaped it. The erosion index is zero. The ground underfoot is plate-basalt and salt-pan and sun-baked clay still holding the memory of its cracking. Edges are sharp. Shards of stone that have never been rounded. Low, flat-topped blisters where gas tried to escape and froze. Dust that is not dust yet, but flour-fine rock with no organic in it at all. If you knelt and brushed it, it would not be soil. Soil hasn't happened yet. And yet —

It is full.

It is more than full. It is packed until there is no concept of empty lot. The first green didn't colonize; it arrived all at once and agreed to fill everything.

This is a city with no builders.

It is not a forest that feels like a city. It is a city that happens to be forest. The trunks are the architecture. They rise twenty, thirty meters, straight as poured pillars, warm gray-brown bark like unpainted concrete, but alive. They grew this close because there was no reason not to. Crown to crown to crown, they interlock. Not roughly — tightly. The canopy is a continuous roof, but it is not high overhead. It is low and close and layered, letting normal, yellow-white daylight through in shafts and grids where the leaves don't quite meet. From below you see the pattern: long, deliberate avenues of light cut between the columns. Cross-streets of shadow. Blocks.

The vegetation itself is lush, but in a dry way. That sounds like a contradiction until you see it. There is no water here. Not a puddle, not a seep, not a damp line on a rock. The air smells of hot stone and resin, not water. And yet every surface that isn't stone is leaf.

They solved water by keeping it private.

The leaves are thick. Not pretty-tropical thin. They are succulent, waxy, cupped, a deep, dusty green with a bloom on them like a fingerprint. Every plant here is a cistern. The trunks are swollen at the base, elephant-footed, storing the dew they comb from the night air. The air plants — and there are millions — hang from every joint and fork in nets and beards and shingles, silver-gray and impenetrable. They never touch ground. They drink fog that doesn't look like fog, just a slight coolness at 4 a.m. that beads and is gone by sunrise.

Under that, the understory is not under. It's mid-structural infill. Vines as thick as your leg run horizontal between trunks at chest height, then at knee height, then at ankle height, forming railings, walls, second floors. They are walked on by everything. Broadleaf shrubs fill every interstitial space, cut to exactly the space available, so you move through this not by pushing through brush but by following corridors that were left for you. And for everyone else.

Because it is abundant.

You think quiet means empty, but here quiet means everyone is busy in a way that doesn't need noise. The fauna is everywhere and you have to train your eyes to separate it from the building.

Beetles the size of your palm, black and matte, moving in steady lines up the pillars, carrying leaf-cuttings. Small, long-tailed, sand-colored primates moving overhead in groups of twenty, never calling. Flocks of dun and moss-green birds that flicker from vine to vine without song, just the softest rustle and rustle. Lizards pressed flat to bark, exactly bark-colored, opening one eye. Moths as big as plates sleeping open on the underside of leaves. Things in the litter — there is litter now, even though the mineral underneath is new — that shift and resettle: millipedes, pill bugs, small snakes the color of roots.

The city works. It is urban in the truest sense: dense habitation, specialization of space, transit, waste, reuse. The street you are in is actually a root channel, scooped an inch below the mineral, polished by feet and claws and the dragging of bodies. To your left, a hollow in a trunk base that goes inside, where something has nested and lined the interior with chewed fiber. To your right, a vertical shaft of light where a trunk fell — it fell recently, the wood still pale gold and sappy — and already three new trunks are growing into its gap, and the space around its crown is a bright market of insects.

Nothing is rusting. Nothing is rotting quickly. The heat is a steady, warm-bath heat — you could work all day in it and never shiver, never sweat much because the air is so dry. The only active weathering is biological. No wind-scour, no flood. Instead, lichen excretes acid and pits the new stone. Roots find a hairline and pry. The place is unmaking its own raw geology from the inside, particle by particle, to make more room for itself.

Above, the canopy breathes. You can feel the whole vastness of it inhale and sigh — not wind, but the collective flex of million million leaves swelling with stored day. It creates its own faint weather right up against the leaves: a warmer layer, a slightly cooler layer. Clouds sometimes form over the center of the forest-city, fifty kilometers in, from all that transpired water that never falls, just cycles trunk-to-leaf-to-air-to-leaf again. They are thin, high, and dry out before they can rain.

Sound stays low. Your footsteps on the root-stone are muffled by a felt of micro-lichens. Even the primates move with soft hands. The loudest thing is occasionally a seed pod cracking open with a clean, sharp pop, and the seeds helicoptering down to land immediately in the one available inch of space and start growing.

If you stayed long enough to see it, you would see that no one lives more than an arm's length from someone else. Not hostile. Just... fully occupied. A city built without a plan, by everything at once, on land that was not supposed to be able to hold a city yet.

It does not need you to complete it. Keep walking, and the avenues repeat, and change, and repeat again, out toward a horizon that is only more pillars, more green, more packed, silent, warm, living building, extending beyond sight.
