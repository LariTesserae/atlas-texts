It is not on the desert. It is *in* the desert, the way a bubble is in glass.

A low place. Not a valley, not even a proper basin — just a long, shallow slack in the white pan where the wind has given up a little. Elevation so low it catches cold. The salt here is down to bedrock, but there is no bedrock anymore. Not here.

You find it because of the sound. On a silent alkali flat you hear it half a mile off, loud and steady, like a wooden flute left on a fence post in a gale. A drone that never wavers, never gusts. One note.

The source is a knot.

A single plant did this. Or was it a plant? In this place things are sparse enough you can count individuals. Three miles to the next wire-thorn, five to the next. But this one — this one is packed. For a hundred years, maybe three hundred, it has grown not out but in. Never taller than your knee. Its branches are black, hard as iron, as thick around as a finger and not a finger wider. When a new shoot tries to grow outward, the dry wind kills the tip. When it grows inward, it lives. So it has learned to weave itself.

There is no soil in it anymore. There is no ground under it. That's what takes you a second to understand when you crouch down and find the opening.

The entire thing is built. From rim to rim, maybe four feet across, two feet high — a pocket, a hidden room, a lung made of wood. The live branches make the lattice, the dead branches make the walls, the shed thorns make the floor, the bark scales that sloughed off make the mortar. Nothing is imported. No stone, no sand enters. It is fully constructed from its own body, settled into its own shape a long time ago. You can see where the wind has smoothed the outer knuckles to a polish, where the salt has bleached them, but none of them move. The form is established, in balance. It will not get bigger. It cannot decay, because there is nothing here that eats dead wood. No animal life. No beetle, no ant, no microbe that bothers. Air alone is too dry to rot anything. So the dead stays.

You have to lie on your belly and turn your head sideways to get inside.

Inside, the density is impossible. There is no cavity in the way you expect a cavity. It is not empty. It is full. Full of presence with no space between. The walls are the ceiling are the floor, a thatch of interlocked branches so tight you cannot slip a finger between them without following the labyrinth around for a foot. Yet you are inside it. The pocket exists in negative, the smallness left over after everything else took up room. A space the size of a curled dog. A single-person hermitage that you have to inhabit with your shoulders touching wood on all sides.

Light comes in bright, painfully bright, because there are a thousand pinholes in the packing. The cool air — and it is cool, ten degrees cooler than the pan outside, held in that low white bowl and in this shade — makes the light visible, dustless, dry. Each hole throws a hard white dot on the thorn floor, and as the sun crosses it doesn't move. The wood is too thick to sway. The pattern never changes. Time has stopped arguing here.

And the sound.

The wind outside passes over the top of the pan and catches in the thousand small tubes of this organ. The knot sings. Loud. Not howling, not whistling — a resonant, woody, hollow-chested hum, as if the whole tightly packed building is one reed. Inside it is deafening, because you are inside the instrument. It vibrates in your ribs.

There is almost no water in this world. The air taken in through your nose has no moisture at all. It smells of hot salt and old, sun-baked resin and nothing green. The green itself is sparse — only the faintest grey-green on the newest growth on the far outer skin, where a leaf might still try. The rest is black and white.

This is the whole ecology: one plant that became architecture, that built everything from itself, that made a room so packed there is barely room for a guest, that used no tool but its own persistence, in a low cool bright dry place where nothing changes anymore, where nothing lives except the builder and the brief, unexpected you, lying with your ear against a thorn floor that drones like a hive with no bees.
