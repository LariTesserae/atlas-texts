It is not on the mountain. It is *in* the mountain's place.

High up where the air should be thin and knife-clean, there is a tower that has replaced the peak. From a distance you would think it was a peak — grey, stark, catching normal white daylight and throwing it back. Up close you realize there is no rock left. Only construction. Plate over plate of prefab, rivet, weld, corrugated skin, catwalk, all built over built until the original elevation became a scaffold for itself.

This landscape is inside that.

A pocket. A single room left over between machines that were never meant to leave room for anything. Maybe three meters long, two and a half wide, not quite tall enough to stand straight if you are wearing a hood. An interstitial service void. That is the entire world.

Everything is made. There is no substrate here. The floor is steel grating, still sharp at the edges, the diamond cut so fresh it would still draw blood if you slipped. Young metal. The walls are the outside of other things — the curved flank of a low-temperature compressor on the left, the flat back of an electrical cabinet on the right, both painted industrial safety white, both bolted until the bolts sang. Above you, low ducting and a cable tray heavy with color-coded lines. Below the grating, more machine. There is no ground to find even if you drilled.

It is cold in the way only a machine can be cold. 0.0 cold. Not winter-cold, not weather at all — a designed cold. A maintained, perfect, thermodynamic cold that has settled at -34C and will never move. The air itself is dry to the point of negation. Water: 0.5. No water. No drip, no melt, no pipe sweat. What little moisture tries to exist is flash-frozen out as a fine, dry frost that coats every underside like salt, like alkaline dust, like the bloom on a dead moon. You breathe and the inside of your nose flash-crusts. Your lungs feel filed down.

And light — normal light, which makes it worse. Two bare fluorescent tubes in cages run the length of the ceiling. Not flickering. 2.5 normal, noon-bright, clinical, 5000K white that shows every tool mark, every frozen granule, every scratch left by the installation crew who are long gone. The light has no source variation. It never warms. It never dims.

In this light, you see the sparse residents.

Vegetation sparse, which should be impossible in a room with no soil, no water, no animals, no change. But there is life that obeys built-space rules. In the corner where the compressor flank meets the cabinet — a 90-degree seam that catches a little vibration — a line of something has taken hold. Not moss, not lichen exactly. Thin, white-grey, flat rosettes of a mineral-tolerant air-fern, each no larger than a coin. Cryophilic. They grow into the draft, drinking the trace oil-vapor in the air, drinking static. Five of them, spaced far apart with great ceremony, as if they agreed not to crowd each other. Between them, long emptiness. The air between presences is the point. The room is sparse in the way a desert is sparse — much space, little claim.

Nothing moves.

Dynamic 0.5. Never changing. This is the strange law that governs the pocket. The frost does not thicken. The ferns do not grow, do not die. The tubes do not burn out. The cold does not deepen when night comes outside, because night never comes inside. The system reached equilibrium years ago and decided to stay there. A frozen equilibrium. You can put your hand on the railing and the frost will not melt under it; it will, for a moment, be both frost and not-frost, and then be frost again when you lift your hand. Erosion 0.7 — young. That is why. This place was made recently by geological standards, by human standards even. Its edges are still sharp, its corners not yet worn round by time or use or weather because there is no weather, no use anymore. Only is-ness.

Except for the sound.

Sound 2.9. Deafening. That is the only thing that feels alive.

The entire room is a resonating chamber for the machines it is wedged between. On the left, the compressor — a piston the size of a car moving at incomprehensible repetition, not cycling, just *present*, a single continuous thunder-note. On the right, the transformer, humming 60 hertz so loud the hum becomes a physical pressure on your molars. And the wind hitting the tower's outer skin far above you, conducted down through the frame, making everything ring. It is not loud like a concert. It is loud like being *inside* a bell while it is struck and never stops being struck. It has no attack, no decay. It just is. Because the dynamic never changes, the deafening never changes either. After ten seconds you stop hearing it as sound and start feeling it as locality — this is what it feels like to have a place insist on itself.

If you shouted, the sound would be eaten immediately. No fauna to hear you. Fauna 0.2 — no animal life. Not even a microbe that moves under its own power. Only those five ferns and the industrial cold.

Weirdness 1.9, strange but not incomprehensible: the room feels bigger when you are not looking directly at the walls. In your peripheral vision the grating underfoot goes down a long way, down past machinery into a depth that cannot exist between two machines. When you look straight down, it's only 40 centimeters to the next plate. The cold does that. The unchanging drone does that. The pocket wants to be larger than its making.

You will not stay long, despite the normal light. The room does not invite loitering. It is high, high up, total-made, industrial, dry as salt, cold as a sealed freezer, young and sharp and already eternal, humming one everlasting deafening note, with five small white things for company, spaced far apart to leave room for emptiness.
