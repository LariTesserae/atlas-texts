It is high.

That is the first wrong thing. You are high up and you are underwater.

This is not a sea that sits low in the basins of the world. This is a sea that sits on top of it. The world has a high country - a continent of stone lifted so close to the top of the sky that you should be able to see weather systems turn below you. And over that continent, there is more ocean. Warm ocean, hundreds of meters or kilometers deep, except it does not fall. It hangs.

You are standing on a mountain that is drowned in sky.

The stone under you is ancient in a way that has forgotten what it was trying to be. Elevation 2.4, erosion 2.7 — these are not sharp Himalayas. These are the oldest mountains imaginable, mountains that have been wind-blown and water-worn for so long that they have given up on being mountains. They are vast, soft swells, domes the size of counties, their basalt and sandstone smoothed down like river stones left in a tumbler for an eon. Valleys between them are not V-shaped but U-shaped, then flattened further, broad shallow basins kilometers across. There is no scree, no rubble; everything that could break has broken and been ground down to silt, to a fine warm mud the color of tea that hangs in the water and never quite settles.

And because the erosion is so complete, because the peaks are so soft and so high, the ocean that covers them is not deep in the way trenches are deep. It is an even blanket, a world-atmosphere of water. Light comes from impossibly far above, through that whole column, and by the time it reaches you here on the high plateau, it is tired, dim, green-amber, diffuse. There is no surface glitter. There is just a slow gradation from lighter honey above to a deeper, warmer dark below. Light does not travel straight here — that is one of the breakdowns. It bends, pools in hollows, clings to the sides of hills like fog. A ridge a hundred meters away might be lit from underneath. Your own shadow, if you can find it, falls slightly to your left no matter where you turn.

The water is warm. 2.3 warm, bath-warm, womb-warm, and almost too thick to call water. It presses but it doesn't crush. Pressure here has forgotten its job. You can be two hundred meters under and your ears don't pop. You breathe it? No, you don't. You breathe the pockets the forest makes. That is the other thing.

It is lush. It is impossibly, chokingly lush for a place with no sun.

Everything that grows here is built to hold air or to make it. The dominant form is not tree, not kelp, but something between. From the smoothed stone rise trunks as wide as houses, but soft - black-barked and jointed like bamboo, filled with gas. They grow slow and then all at once, shooting up fifty, a hundred meters into the water column, branching into flat, round leaves the size of tables, overlapping, black-green, waxy. Each leaf holds a bubble under it, silvered, trembling. The whole forest canopy is a thousand-layered ceiling of leaves and trapped suns. They photosynthesize that dim light with absurd efficiency, exhaling oxygen that never reaches the surface because there is no surface. It just hangs under the leaves in lakes of air, in rooms of air.

You move through it like wading through a cathedral made of curtains. Density 2.4 — there is no empty space. Between the trunks hang beards of filter-vine, curtains of red hair-algae, colonial drapes that pulse slowly, closing and opening, sifting the tea-mud for minerals. Everything touches everything else. A leaf brushes your face and it is cool and slick. A vine catches your ankle and lets go. The trunks are furred with smaller growths, which are furred with smaller growths. You push a frond aside and there are twenty more behind it.

And it is active, constantly in motion, despite the silence. Dynamic 2.4. The water moves like weather because it *is* weather. There are slow, continent-scale currents that flow over these drowned highlands like jet streams, warm rivers inside the sea, stirring the mud, bending the forests. When a current comes through, the whole forest leans. The trapped air-lakes shiver, then detach in a silent rush of a thousand silver balloons rising, merging, falling sideways. The hair-vines flower all at once, not with petals but with sudden clouds of warm bioluminescent spores that don't float up or down, but orbit each other for days.

The rules here have slipped.

Down is not always denser than up. Sometimes you drop a stone and it falls slower the lower it gets, then stops three feet off the ground and just hangs, spinning gently until a current nudges it. Water and air invert without mixing. You will find a pool of perfectly clear, perfectly ordinary air twenty feet across suspended at head-height, with a flat top and bottom like a block of glass, and inside it, mud still floating as if it were still underwater, and a few small shrimp swimming in air, unconcerned. You will find a river of water falling *upwards* into the dimness, endlessly. Gravity here is an old opinion.

Built 0.8: there were people, or something like them, who understood this place.

Their remains are not cities. This high soft world doesn't allow city-foundations. What you find are markers, left on the round backs of the ancient hills. A single terrace cut into a dome, so worn it is almost natural, but its proportions are wrong for erosion. A stair of five steps that leads from soft rock into open water and stops. Small huts made of the same black trunk-wood, now petrified and overgrown, their doors all facing the same impossible direction - straight up, toward the deeper water column and the light that isn't there. Statues of a figure with its hands cupped around nothing, its stone features smoothed until it is only the idea of a face. They are scattered so far apart that you could walk for days between them, even in this dense tangle, and that distance feels intentional. Like listening posts. Like hermitages.

They left no tools. Tech 0.5, none. Even the huts have no joints, no metal, no carving you can find. They just coaxed the wood to grow that way.

Fauna 0.6: Almost nothing moves under its own will, or not quickly enough that you see it move. Most of the animal life here has opted to be plant-like, sessile, filtering, breathing slow. You can go for hours hearing only the quietest sounds, sound 0.5 — the creak of a trunk shifting, the *tick... tick...* of an air bubble freeing itself, the faint, distant groan of stone that has settled too much and is still settling.

And then rarely, very rarely, something large passes.

Far above the canopy, between you and the light-source that isn't a sun, there is a shadow that eclipses that already-dim light for a long time. It does not swim. It drifts with the current, larger than a whale, larger than the hill you are standing on, and shaped like nothing - a folded sheet, a manta of translucent cloth, with no eyes you can see. It does not see you. Filter-mouths along its trailing edge open and close, swallowing water and forests of trailing vine. It makes no noise at all. When it has gone, the forest rights itself and the trapped air slowly begins to gather again, and you realize you have been holding your breath for a very long time in a place where holding your breath doesn't mean what you thought.

You are on top of the world, under the ocean, in a warm dim forest that grows in the sky-sea. The mountains have become soft as skin. Time has smoothed everything but has not ended it; everything grows over everything else, instantly, endlessly, in quiet, warm, wet silence.
