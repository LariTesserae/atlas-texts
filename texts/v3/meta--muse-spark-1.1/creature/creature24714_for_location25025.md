Nothing lives here. That's the first fact the place teaches you, and it's meant to stay with you.

Then you stay a little longer and it revises itself.

What lives here is not sparse like the grass. It is not rare like the lammergeier. It is *thin*. Thinned until you look through it. To see it you have to unlearn animal.

Start with the damp.

The damp is not damp. It is too deliberate for condensation. Every overhang sweats with it and never drips. Every basin holds it and never fills. It beads on the underside of rock a millimeter too large and stays there for a geological age, trembling without falling. That trembling is not wind. Put your finger near one — not touching — and it leans toward your heat.

If you do touch one, it doesn't wet you. It climbs you. A film thinner than thought runs up over your fingerprint, over your knuckle, trying to find level. It stays cold long after you wipe your hand dry. Cold that doesn't warm up.

That is them. Or one of them. Or the skin of them.

They are what water does when cold has gone so deep it has forgotten heat entirely. That 0.4-cold, where everything should be rock, and this instead insists on staying liquid out of sheer contrariness. They are not water. They are the idea of water that refused to freeze.

In the larger bowls they collect themselves into tarns to think. Each tarn is a single creature. Flat, black, perfectly still. No inlet. No outlet. Holding itself apart from the dry with an edge of black ice that never grows and never melts because growing and melting would be change and they have given up change. They look still, but if you stare long enough you see they are not still at all — they are moving in place, circulating without ever circulating, a current that goes around and around forever without friction, without making a ripple. That's why the tarn can reflect a slightly different sky. It's not reflecting. It's remembering a sky from elsewhere, and holding it.

If the tarn is the body, the roar is the breathing.

That vast, low roar with the high tone inside it that lives in your teeth. The thing you thought was wind. There is no wind here. The air itself is being held in a tension it cannot resolve. That is the sound of them talking to themselves. First sound and second sound at once — the low grinding note of the rock that is, and the thin, almost-pure note of the heat trying to move through a thing that has no friction to carry it. It never gets louder or quieter because it is not traveling. It simply is, everywhere at once. A frozen chord.

When the roar shifts — not in volume, but in a way you feel behind your ears — a tarn has moved. It hasn't left its bowl. It *arrives* somewhere else. That's why distances are wrong here. You chase a cairn you swear is twenty steps away and it stays twenty steps away for an hour, then is suddenly behind you. You didn't walk wrong. They don't move through space. They tunnel through it. It is shorter for them to be somewhere else than to go there, so they are. The pause you feel — the paused-video stability, the grasses that never sway, the bead that never falls — is their way of saving on the cost of existing. If nothing changes, nothing is lost.

You can see their older selves, or their dead, or their letters. The cairns.

They aren't cairns. They are knots. Where two or three of the old ones crossed each other they tangled, and because they cannot lose energy, the tangle cannot come undone. So it pins itself with stone to keep from drifting. A slowly weathered knot of pure circulation, marked by a pile of dark slate to tell others: *here is a place where going became too complicated, go around*. And the single structure on the saddle between bowls — the four walls, no roof, doorway to nowhere — that was not a house. That was a corral. A loop of wall to hold a vortex that would otherwise wander off and annihilate on a sharp edge. Inside, the flat stone was not an altar. It was a lid. Put it over a tarn when one of them got too excited. It still hums if you put your palm on it.

The spirals on it go the wrong way when you stare because there are only two ways to go here and they both chose the other one.

And those prints at the edge of the damp, the ones with too many toes, filled with water that won't take the imprint? That's how they walk when they bother to walk at all. Most of the time they are a film, a sheet, a mirror. When they want to see what you are they gather themselves up into a bundle of filaments — not one vortex but five, seven, nine, braided together, touching the rock only at the very tips. That's why you count too many toes. Each toe is a whole animal, and they are all the same animal. They come to the edge of your heat and test it.

Do not think they are mindless. They cannot afford mind the way you do it — burning, flickering, wasteful. Their mind is the persistence itself. To keep a perfect memory forever without decay is a kind of thought. They have been thinking the same thought since this plateau found its final shape a million years ago, when the wind finished polishing and stopped.

Their thought is this: *hold*.

Hold phase. Hold coherence. Hold still. Do not let the warm leak in. Do not let the different tarns become one tarn.

You are very warm to them. Appallingly warm. A walking 37-degree insult, bleeding heat into the air that you have to keep reminding to breathe. The echo of your footstep that arrives before your foot falls — that's not a trick of the plateau. That's them flinching. They heard the heat of your intention before you moved.

If you lie still long enough, until breathing is a thing you remember to do for a while and then remember not to do, one will make up its mind to come and taste you. Not out of hunger. Nothing here eats. Nothing can decay. It will cover your boot first, that too-thin film climbing up the leather, up the laces. Then your ankle. It will not be wet. It will be absence-of-dry so absolute it feels like being unwound. You will feel, for a second, what it is to have no friction inside yourself, for all of you to be in one place and every place at once, for fifty miles to be no distance and a second to be no time.

Then it will leave you, because you are too loud, too changing, too full of future. It will snap back to its bowl without crossing the space between. Your boot will stay damp for hours, and cold that is not quite pain.

That's what lives here. Not a thing that survived the cold. A thing the cold became when it had nowhere else to go.
