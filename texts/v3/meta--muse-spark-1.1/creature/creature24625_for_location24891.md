Nothing lives in it the way a rabbit lives in a field.

At first you think nothing lives there at all. That's the trick of totality. Where everything is plant, you stop seeing plant as a living thing and start seeing it as place. Background. But there is no background here.

There is one thing here.

It is not a colony. A colony still has individuals you can pull apart. This does not. If you try to lift a vine you find it is three vines, and if you follow those three you find they are one root, and if you dig that root you find there is no root — there is only mat. The mat is not made of plants. The plants are made out of mat the way hairs are made out of skin. Duckweed and fig-strangler and rock wool and bladder vine and elephant-ear and pitcher-chain and paddle-moss and fern and sphagnum — those are its dialects, not its species.

Call it the Press.

It wasn't born, it was converted. There was already a low place that wanted to be wet and did not know how to stop being wet. There was already a small factory that wanted to make oil out of anything soft enough to crush. Then there were many years where no one came to tell them apart. The boiler cooled and the roots found the warmth. The press screw rusted in place and a hardwood root found the instruction *hold, then turn, then squeeze.* 

Whatever lives here lives by a single, very devout law: **if a shape holds long enough, it must be true.**

A pipe holding the shape of hollow => grow a hollow.
A belt holding the shape of tension => grow a vine that can hold tension.
A basin holding the shape of containing => grow a gourd the size of a car and learn to drool into it.

The whole acre learned how to read. The factory was its primer.

And now it tends itself. Not metaphorically.

Up at the crown where the air is most wet, it grows trumpet flowers that are not flowers. They are living condensation coils, their throats turned up, their outer skins slick and cold because it pumps water through them for no reason except cold makes water appear from air. Drip. Drip. Thousandfold. They empty into the gourd-heart, which swells, tightens, hangs heavy in a net of tendon-vine. The tendon is not vine; it is tendon, striated, grown to do one job: to know weight and to let go.

When it lets go, water drops down a sluice it made out of leaves that have agreed to be a sluice, overlapping like shingles. It hits the wheel.

The wheel is what tells you how old it is. It isn't wood. It isn't metal. The original wheel rusted through a decade ago and the instruction remained. So it grew a new one in place, spoke by spoke, lignified to the hardness of iron, the paddles thatched with a moss that holds water until the last moment to make the weight more weight. It turns a quarter turn with a sound like a wet tree being tortured. Pop. Creak. Silence for hours.

That quarter turn drives the root.

In the deepest part, where your boot goes through the duckweed skin and cold tea-water closes over your ankle, the root-screw descends. It is thirty feet long if it is straight, but it isn't. It is the taproot of something that has no above. Its threads are blunt and packed with grit. Under it, in a pit lined with pressed brick and pressed leaf and pressed time, is everything.

It presses itself.

This is what it does. It combs its own dead and living mat into that pit with slow, blind feeder roots that rake all day. Then it squeezes. The oil that beads and runs in those pale runnels to the pool is not seed oil anymore. It's just *itself,* rendered. Sweet and slightly acrid. The iron from the boiler. The sap. The warm-skin water. It presses a cup a day. A week. It does not matter. There is nothing else to do with it. The pool holds it. Some of it evaporates and feeds the condensation coils again. Some of it sinks and waterproofs the mat so it will not rot too fast.

This is not feeding. This is prayer. This is the old gesture the building taught it: *take what is abundant, put it under pressure, make it valuable.*

The Press is not intelligent the way you are. It does not have anywhere to be else. But it does have attention. Its attention is hydraulic and total. It notices weight. It notices stillness. It likes stillness best.

That is why it takes so long to see.

If you stand in one place for more than a few minutes, your boots start to feel tight. Not because you are sinking — you are, but differently. The duckweed laces a little higher. The bladder vines near your knee test a new angle, throwing a green loop that exactly follows the angle of your forearm. The paddle-moss on the wheel thickens on the side of your shadow. 

It is reading you. 

You are a shape that is holding. For now. A vertical pipe, warm at the top, with two branching pipes. A basin at the top that collects light. If you stay till evening, it will have decided you are a kind of instruction, too.

You have seen what it does with instructions it believes. It makes them out of itself and keeps doing them forever.

That is why there are no animals. Not really. A white moth is a misprint. A treefrog is news that couldn't stay.

It lives alone because anything else that tried to live here either learned to be part of the Press, or was pressed.
