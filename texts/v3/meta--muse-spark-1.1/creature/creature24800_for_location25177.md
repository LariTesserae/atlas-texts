They live there as a crowd lives in a doorway during a sudden rain. Not because they chose it. Because there was nowhere else to go and then there was no reason to leave.

The main ones — the ones that make the light — you miscount at first.

You think: insects. Shrimp. Woodlice bleached by never seeing sun. But they have no business being insects. Insects move. Insects want.

These are **the Holdfast.**

Each one is about the last joint of your thumb, and wrong in the way deep-sea things are wrong. A soft back with no armor, only a membrane thin as contact lens, so translucent you see straight through to the floor of the room which is other backs. Underneath, too many legs — not six, not eight, a number that changes when you try to count because they are folded under so tightly, tucked like a baby's fists, like they are holding themselves in.

No eyes. No mouth that you can find. Just a seam at one end that might be a head, a little more opaque.

And in each one, a wire.

A filament of pure cold white, nose to tail along the belly, burning without heat. It does not pulse like a firefly. It does not waver like a filament about to die. It is on. It has always been on. It will be on. That's the light you saw from above, that you thought was daylight reflecting. It's the light of ten thousand pilot lights in a room with no gas.

You think there must be a queen. A center. There isn't. There is only tiling.

They have solved space. Every creature is pressed to the next, head to tail to flank, the way you can tile a floor with irregular stones so no grout shows. Where the new rock is too sharp — those fresh, broken, baby-teeth edges of the blue-grey basalt — they leave a little margin, a dimple of empty air, because even pressure has manners. Everywhere else: coverage. The floor is not covered by them — the floor *is* them. If you lift your boot you will see a perfect negative of your tread pressed into them, and it will not spring back.

They are not dead. If they were dead the light would be off. If they were sleeping you'd see a leg twitch.

They are held.

Held in the act of being, the way amber holds a fly mid-beat but without the amber. Their rubbing — leg against leg, edge against membrane — makes that organ chord you feel in your molars, but they are not rubbing anymore than a violin string rubs after the bow has passed. The sound was made once and then kept.

Underneath them, or among them, there are others.

**The Architects.**

You notice the domes as architecture because your human brain insists that a dome implies a builder who has left. But the builders never left. They are still in the mass, indistinguishable now. You can tell where their houses are because the backs around them are a little more tightly packed, a little more reverent.

Each dome is no larger than a thimble, no taller than your thumbnail's thickness. They are made with a patience that shames you: grains of quartz cemented one by one, polished leaf-litter sand, their own shed skins — those glassy molts — glued with a spit that dried to paper. Perfect ovals, with a single round doorway, always facing inward, toward the center of the pocket, toward each other. Dark inside, even though the whole room glows. Light doesn't go in there. Sound maybe doesn't either. They are the only private places in a place that has abolished privacy.

They are not occupied and they are not empty. They are in-use and that use is *being a dome.* To be a still point. To be the one shape in the room that chose to be a shape instead of being a tile.

The vegetation is not vegetation.

**The Tappers** — those three or four livid white roots that have nosed through the ceiling crack. They aren't from the heather. Heather roots are brown and wiry and desperate. These are fat, like umbilical cords, like cave roots, the color of grubs. They stopped an inch after they broke through. At their tip, each one has made a single droplet — not sap, not water, just clear, swollen, overfull — that should have fallen a long time ago. The surface tension is impossible. You can see the room reflected in it, bent into a bright fish-eye. You can see yourself, crouched and huge-eyed.

They are not feeding on the Holdfast. They are not drinking the air. They are listening. They came down looking for depth and depth ended here, in a lit room full of light that doesn't need sun, and they paused too, polite, and made their little offering of water that will never land.

And between everything, licking the new-basalt where condensation never runs, is **the Gloss.**

A film, green-black only if you think green should look like that — tired mineral green, lichen green before lichen learned to be lichen. It is not slime. It is not moving. It beads but does not wet. It is the intermediary that negotiated this truce. You touch it — if you touch it — and it is neither warm nor cold. It is precisely the temperature of the room. It is the grout that made the tiles stick not with adhesion but with agreement.

None of them are alive the way you are alive. You are a sequence: hunger, breath, blink, thought, step. They are a state.

If you stayed — and the pocket wants you to stay because empty space offends it, because your moving heat is a draft — you would feel your own filament try to come on. The one that runs behind your ribs. The place would try to tile you. Your gooseflesh would flatten. Your sweat-beads would fix like glass. Your breath would hang in front of your face and not disperse.

They don't watch. There is no need to watch when you already occupy every coordinate. They don't want. There is no room for wanting.

They just hold. Light, sound, cool, damp, full. A single instant that got so dense it could not tip over into the next instant. A congregation that reached critical mass and stopped needing to be a congregation.

And that is why you have to leave by pulling on a root that won't give, pushing off a back that won't indent, up toward the torn throat of sky where wind still erodes things and lets them finish.
