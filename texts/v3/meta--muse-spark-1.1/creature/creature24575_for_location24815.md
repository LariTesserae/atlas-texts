They are not beetles. That is the first mistake you make because you have a word for beetle.

What lives in there is older than beetle. Better to say: what lives in there is what happens when rock decides it doesn't want to be rock for a little while longer.

In the cherry dark, at first you can't see individuals at all. Only the mass. A packed lung. A liver. A solid made out of backs. It looks like one animal that has learned to pretend to be many, or many animals that have forgotten how to stop touching each other.

Then your eyes stop looking for legs and start looking for seams.

They are each about as long as the first joint of your thumb, but compressed, as if something sat on them when they were soft. There is no roundness to spare. Dorsal armor matte black, not shiny, not black like an insect at all — black like soot stuck on with sweat. It drinks the little red there is. It doesn't reflect. Under that, the plates overlap wrong, like shingles laid backwards, so they can slide over each other and let the body flatten further.

They have no eyes. Or, they have eyes that have become something else. Two dull, unpigmented pits on either side of the head that crusted over. Light has never meant anything in here. Heat is the thing that means.

You see that in the gradient.

At the bottom of the bubble, where the floor is still in that state between rock and cooled glass, where it gives a little if you press a nail to it — down there, the miners live. Or more precisely, down there *everything* is a miner for a time.

Their heads are all jaw. The mandibles are not black. That is what makes them obscene at first. The rest of the animal has committed completely to darkness and these things are bone-white, almost translucent, pallid as grubs, too big for the head, so they must turn sideways to work. And they work without pause. *Chik. Chik-chik. Chik.* The sound isn't chittering. It's filing. Each scrape takes a curl of basalt as fine as pencil shaving, still chemically soft, still holding the heat of its making. They eat it. Not chew and swallow. They *take* it, the way a forge takes coal.

That is because the middle of the mass is not a middle. It is a second age.

If a miner eats enough, if it stays hot enough, long enough, it bloats. Its abdomen swells past its armor and goes clear. You can see right into it, and what you see is not guts but a crucible. A wet orange glow inside the skin, cupped in dark organs that脉 like bellows. They are smelting themselves. There is water in this room — there's no other way — but they keep it the way desert plants keep it, locked and recirculated a thousand times, never spent. The basalt goes in as silicate and iron and something else that smells like old blood when it airs out, and inside that belly it is broken. The iron is pulled free. The slag is breathed out as that fine, hot powder that is coating your throat right now.

What precipitates is behind them. A bead. One bead per animal per slow cycle. Black, but not like their backs. This black has a star in it. Metallic. A perfect sphere of native iron and glass, still soft, still glowing dull cherry for minutes after it drops. It clicks. That is the other sound under the filing. *Click. Click-click.* Beads settling against beads. The floor is not floor at all, but ten, twenty years of these beads, packed, slowly welding together in the heat into a new, purer stone.

And that is why there has to be a top.

The furnace-animals, heavy with heat, become buoyant. The entire living solid is convecting, so slowly you only see it if you stare at one white jaw for a full minute and watch it sink or rise a hair's breadth. Hot bodies rise. Cooled bodies sink at the edges. At the top, pressed almost to your nose where the air is a degree less murderous, are the carriers. Old ones. Their armor is bleached grey from heat cycling, crazed with fine cracks that tick as they expand and contract. Their abdomens are empty again, collapsed flat. Their white jaws have worn down to nubs. Their job now is only to be cool enough to touch. They take the fresh beads from the furnaces below, pass them mandible to mandible — this is not communication, this is cooling — each mouth holding it for a long moment, leaching a little more heat into their own bodies, until the bead goes black and can be set down without melting the animal beneath.

There is no queen. There is no larva. There are no eggs the way you mean eggs. Or else every bead is an egg and every animal is also its own child. You can't tell if that iron sphere contains an embryo or if it is just what is left when a creature that eats mountain has no use for anything that isn't iron. Maybe if you cracked one open it would be solid. Maybe it would have a white, coiled thing inside already filing at the inside of its own shell.

They are not social. They are not eusocial. Social requires decisions. This is physics. Hot rock + sealed room + something that can eat hot rock = a torus of life that turns on its own without ever needing to know it exists. The pocket is not their home. The pocket *is* them. It is how a brand new piece of lithosphere processes itself before the wind gets to touch it. They mine the room bigger by millimeters a decade, and they fill it in behind them with purer iron, refining the mountain the way a kidney refines blood.

You can stay with your face in that crack until your lips blister. They will not move away from you and they will not come toward you. You are just a cool spot briefly. One or two of the top carriers may climb onto your cheek because it is cooler than stone. Their feet are impossibly hot and dry. They will taste the salt on your skin and find no iron in it and let go.

When this bubble finally cools, when the glass finally learns to be real granite and is too hard for even the bone-white jaws to scrape — they will stop. Not die in a pile. Stop. Their water will finally vent. Their backs will vitrify. They will become part of the floor of beads they made, another layer of black glass waiting.

From outside, even a foot away, there is nothing. Just black rock and a slit the wind can't find.
