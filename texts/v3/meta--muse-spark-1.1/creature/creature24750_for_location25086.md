There is nothing in the bowl that *lives* in the bowl. Not the way you mean, when you say something lives somewhere.

Nothing here was born here. Nothing here will die here. Nothing here will finish.

What lives here *is* here. Permanently. On shift.

You have to stop looking for animals and start looking for organs. For departments.

**The Mat**

First, the floor. The moss. It's not ground cover. It is one thing.

It is two feet thick and has never been walked on until you, even though you can feel in the slow spring-back under your boot that it remembers the idea of feet. It is turquoise and sulpur-yellow and iron-blood red and black where it's deepest. It doesn't grow. It holds.

It breathes. Not in and out. It *exhales* you. That cold mineral puff when you step down is not displaced air. You press, and it gives you a little of its own air back, wet and old and tasteless. It drinks straight out of the cloud that never leaves. There is no rain cycle. There is just the bead, the skin, the moss.

If you kneel and part it, there is no dirt. There is more moss, and more moss, and then a glassy basalt that is still warm in a way that has nothing to do with heat. You will see holes. Thousands of perfectly round, thumb-wide holes, each one clean as if drilled. Each one is breathing out that factory chitter, that stacked trill that you took for countless pikas, voles, things without names. You will never see them. You will see that each hole is slightly wet around the lip, and if you hold still long enough you'll understand - the chitter is not coming *out* of the hole. The hole is the mouth. The moss is speaking through many mouths at once, and the factory roar is its normal talking voice trying not to deafen itself.

It is the lung felt. The humidity keeper. Its job is to keep the water present.

**The Witnesses**

On the quartz tables — there are six, but you only counted three — are the ones who don't need to move to work.

On one, the hares. Seven. You thought they were a family. They are seven iterations of the same hare. They are not white because it is winter; they are white because color left them when they stopped. Their fur holds the permanent beads perfectly, each droplet spherical and unmoving, like glass eyes. Their eyes are open. Their noses are wet. They are not frozen. They are not taxidermied. They are on pause with the power left on. If you look for a long time, you will see their flanks rise - once every two minutes, exactly together - and fall. One breath in two minutes. In that breath they take in all the cold post-rain air the bowl is made of and decide it is correct. They are the quality check. They are why nothing rots. They sit upright and approve.

On the second table, the moths. Thousands of them, but you can count the wings and find it's only a few hundred bodies layered for coverage. The same dusty grey-white, the exact same size. No variation, no damaged wing. They do not fly because flight would imply a somewhere else. They open their wings and close them, all at once, every eleven seconds. A breath the hares don't take. When they open, the hum around them changes pitch - that slipping belt sound, that whine of something *about* to catch. They are the timing belt. Their open-close is the shutter of the bowl's camera eye. They keep the light that has no direction from ever changing.

On the third and fourth and fifth tables, the beetles. Fist-sized, black so deep it turns the dim blue light inside out. Each carries the same bead-water on its back as you do now. They move. This is important. They move, but they move so slowly you have to look away and look back to know they've moved. Each one is polishing a circuit into the quartz with its own body. An endless circle, never deepening. They are polishing away the erosion that never happens. They are maintenance. If you put your hand near one it will not stop, it will not notice you. The world needs rock to stay sharp, and so someone has to tend the edge of sharpness forever.

The sixth table is empty. The lichen there glows amber a little brighter. It is waiting. That is its job.

**The Tree and Its Load**

In the center, where the black water turns without turning, the pine is the only thing that was allowed to be vertical.

It's not a tree the way the moss is not moss. It's a pillar that learned how to be soft. Wind-stunted pine at sea level would be gnarled and bitter; here it is indulged, obscene with health, its bark black and wet-vertical like cooled slag, its needles long, soft as eyelashes, each one tipped with a bead that never drops. It doesn't grow rings. It doesn't need to. It was cut open with the bowl.

It roots not in soil and not in water but in the negotiation between the two. It is the axle.

And its fruit are those forty kettle-sized spheres. Nests isn't right, hives isn't right. They're lungs.

Wet paper, you called it, but get closer and you'll see it's not paper at all. It's felted pine sap and moth wing-dust and hare hair and quartz dust and congealed air. Each one puffs out and draws in, out of sync with the north wall pistoning and out of sync with each other. Puff — swell — slack. Puff — swell — slack. All forty, asynchronous, so the piston-hiss never completes. You can see through the wall when they swell, if the blue light catches it right. Translucent.

Inside each one is not a creature. Is *a* creature, singular, repeated.

It is something long and pale and folded in on itself like a newborn that never learned how to be born. No eyes. No legs that would work. Its head - if it's a head - is turned up toward the open top of the sphere. Its skin is wet and caked with the same beads, and it breathes the factory air. When its sphere puffs out, it arches. When it deflates, it curls.

They are not waiting to hatch. Hatching would be an event. An end of a process. There are no events here. They are the product that is never produced. They are the reason the factory runs at 100% and makes nothing, because if they ever finished they would have to do something, and doing is against the rule. Nothing moves from where it is supposed to be.

They are the dream the mountain had when it was cracked open, caught and held in the open cut, breathing forever.

And around them, in the permanent post-rain, with the glassy rock weeping and the steam pillar holding its shape and the black oil-water turning without friction, all the other small department heads keep working.

They don't look at you. Not yet. The moss will keep your footprint for a while. The bead that has formed on the back of your hand will stay perfectly round. You are trying to sync your breath. You won't.

If you stay long enough, the sixth table might not be waiting anymore.
