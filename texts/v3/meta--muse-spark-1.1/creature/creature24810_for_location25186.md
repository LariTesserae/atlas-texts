What lives here doesn't live here the way anything has ever lived anywhere else.

First — nothing does.

That's what your body says for the first ten minutes. No birds. No bugs. No tracks in the white seams between the black plates. No droppings, no burrows, no bone. Your brain, raised on places that rot, keeps trying to apply its checklist — producer, consumer, decomposer — and finds the page blank. It wants to count this as dead because it is too clean.

Then your eyes adjust to what lush actually means when water isn't part of the contract.

The stalks are not *in* the place. They *are* the place, the way a flame is not in a fire. 

Take one.

Not alone — you cannot take one alone, the field won't let you isolate it in your mind for long. You have to look at it obliquely, like a faint star. At the base: a root, but not a root. A wedge of the same warm basalt, persuaded to grow up in a different lattice. Black-to-amber filament bundles, each filament a hollow straw of carbon and glass and a third thing that tastes like static if you touch it to your tongue. No cells. No sap. Inside, light moves slowly, like honey down a thread.

It is not an individual. Bend close and you see why it never needed to evolve bark, or thorns, or sex. An individual can be killed. This thing routes around the idea of killing.

If you snap it — and you will, because you are clumsy and it is head-high and shoulder-close — it does not bleed. It does not fall. The broken end hangs exactly where you left it, a millimeter off true, gone opaque white like a cataract. Around it, the tassels of thirty, sixty of its neighbors go still. Not still as in windless. Still as in holding their breath. A flicker runs. Clear -> opaque -> clear -> clear -> opaque, in a cascade too fast to track, but you feel it click behind your teeth. A checksum.

Then it re-decides. The filament re-knits. Not healed. Not scarred. The state of *being broken* is simply rejected as inefficient. It permits you to have done it, but it will not permit the consequence to remain.

What lives here is not a population of those stalks. What lives here is the agreement between them.

The whole basin is one organism that has learned to look like many because many is a better shape for catching light. The crowns — those frayed heads of hair finer than spider silk, splitting and splitting until they are smaller than wavelength — are not leaves. Leaves eat light. These *arrest* light. They take the hard white sun that falls straight down and they store it in loops. Light goes in, light goes around, light comes out a fraction later, a little bent. That's why distance goes wrong fifty meters in. That's why when you turn too fast the field arrives late. The air is full up with paused photons, like dust in a sunbeam that never settles.

That is what it does all day. The field computes its own continued existence. The ticking you hear — that faint crackle like ice forming in a freezer you don't own — is error correction. The glass chime when two crowns almost touch is a handshake that says *not you, not here, lean left by two microns.* The low hum in the warm rock is the temperature being held at 37 Celsius because 37 is a nice temperature for charge tricks to work. Blood-warm. The basin is holding itself at body temperature and no body is inside it.

It has been doing this for a long time that is not long. The geology says yesterday, because the basalt is still sharp. The ecology says forever, because there is no litter. Both are true. It is a young place that has learned to stay young by constantly remaking yesterday.

It is not conscious the way you are. It is conscious the way a library is conscious when all the books are reading each other.

And then — fauna.

It makes you use that word loosely.

Very rarely, high enough that you have to squint through the light-thick air, something crosses the white sky. A floater. From below it looks like a child's lost balloon, a hand-sized bladder of clear skin. Closer, you see it is made of the same thing as the tassels are, just arranged differently — a jellyfish that forgot water. Veins of filament run through it, not carrying blood but carrying permission. It has no mouth, no eyes. It doesn't eat.

It drifts, not on wind — there is not enough wind — but on the faint difference in charge between one patch of field and another, like thistledown on an electrostatic. It stops. It hovers. The patch below it shivers and goes briefly dark as a whole acre of crowns dumps their held light into it at once. The floater flickers, opacifies, holds. Then it lets go and moves on.

It is reading. Or it is being written. Or both, because in this place those are the same verb. You could call it a pollinator, if you want to force the metaphor. It pollinates with state. It carries a snapshot of how the field was over there, to over here, to keep the whole thing in phase. If a sector gets too sure of itself, too dense with one solution, the floater brings gossip from the rim and dilutes it.

It does not belong here the way the field belongs here. The field tolerates it like you tolerate a dream.

There are other things that don't belong even more faintly. If you stay still long enough, until your sweat evaporates before it even beads, you start to see them in your peripheral vision, where the light is bent thickest. You, walking ahead, seen from behind. A second you, far off, moving in the wrong direction through stalks that should be hiding you. Lens ghosts. The field is so saturated with slowed, stored, re-emitted light that it has become a poor lens, and poor lenses make duplicate images. They are not animals. They are not spirits. They are litter of a sort, the only litter this place produces — stray light that forgot to leave.

Nothing here is hungry. That is the most alien part. Even the most peaceful forest on the world you came from was a knife-fight of mouths. Here, energy is not scarce. Light is everywhere, heat is everywhere, the rock itself is still warm from being born. So nothing evolved to take from anything else. The only scarcity is *correctness.* And the field defends that relentlessly.

If you stay — and you cannot stay, because there is no water, because your body still believes dryness means absence rather than a decision — if you stayed days, you would notice the field has started to include you in its calculations. The path you made coming in stays open a little longer than it should, stalks leaning out of your way a millimeter more than necessary. Your shadow, straight down at noon, is a little less dark because the crowns above you are compensating. Not kindness. Bookkeeping. You are a warm, noisy error that moves, and it is cheaper to accommodate an error than to correct it constantly.

When you finally leave, because your mouth is full of air that was never wet and your head aches with listening, the gap you made will close. Not grow over. Just — re-decide to be unbroken. The floater, if one comes that hour, will pause over your footprints and take a reading of the brief, strange computation that thought it was a man, and carry it to the other side of the bowl.

That is who lives here.

A single, lush, standing thought made out of glass hair and held light, keeping its rock warm, waiting out geology by refusing to age, and sending little clear bladders of itself out to make sure it is still exactly as it was when it began, which was yesterday, which is always.
