What lives in the Works is not a population.

At first you count it. That's the mistake. You see a roof and you say a thousand birds. You see the chain-link and you say ten thousand. You see the concrete aprons and you say one, one, one, one, spaced out. You try to add them up. The number gets large and then stops meaning anything, because what you are counting was never separate.

It is one thing. It wears the shape of many.

If you kneel - and it will make space for your knee, always a little more concrete than you thought there was - and you look close at one of the spaced ones, the ones standing alone in the gaps to hold the emptiness in place, this is what you see:

It is not a pigeon. The pigeon was a suggestion your brain made because your brain had pigeons filed.

Its color is not a color. It's wet newspaper, it's boiler scale, it's the gray-brown you get when you mix all the rust and all the soot together. It has no pattern, because pattern would be change. Its head is too smooth, no beak-ridge, no nostril. A slit that opens a little when the tone requires it. Its eyes are flat and wet and dark, and when it blinks - rarely - it's sideways, a film wiping left to right.

Its wings have too many elbows. If you watch long enough you will see it resettle, and the wing does not fold like a bird wing. It folds like a hand of hands, joint over joint over joint, tucking into itself without ever making a hard angle. The feet are the worst part, or the most honest part. They are not feet. They are soft. Pale pads, slightly translucent, like seal flaps or mushroom flesh, that make no scratch on brick. They don't grip. They adhere. They leave a faint cool damp where they were, which ceases.

They make no shit. No molt. The roofs are not fouled. They are covered, but clean. The feathering - and it is like feathers only at distance - is more like frayed mineral fiber, like asbestos that learned to be soft. It moves slightly, all the time, without wind. Each filament shivers to keep the air around it at exactly the same temperature.

There are two postures. You saw them.

The dense ones, the mat, the ones that make the roof not a roof anymore and the wire not wire anymore. They are the bulk. They are the insulation. Shoulder to shoulder, head to tail, they are not sleeping. They are listening with their whole surface. They interleave their soft wings and become a single continuous blanket. If you put your hand near, you feel the heat they don't keep - a faint, even warmth coming off them, exactly the temperature of the lathe shop, exactly the temperature of the press shop. They take the vibration from the machines through the brick and brick it again, finer. They are acoustic tile. They are liver.

The sparse ones, the ones on the concrete with perfect spacing. Those are the structure. They stand in the middle of nothing and enforce it. Each one knows exactly how far it is from the next, and from the next building, and from the next pool. They are survey stakes. They are holding the bowl's shape in their heads. If one were to step two inches left, the gap would be the wrong size and the tone would waver. So none ever step. When you walk, they don't move. The space where your foot will fall is already there, as if they knew your stride yesterday.

What does it eat?

Not seeds. Not scraps. No flies in the bowl for them to snap, no plants to make seeds. It eats the thing that is happening.

It eats equilibrium. It feeds on a sustained note. On damp that never dries and never gets wetter. On pools that are disturbed and then come back to exactly the same picture. On machines that run without wearing. It pastures on the thin, delicious energy you get when you almost have decay and then don't. The universe wants everything in the bowl to slide - rust to rust through, water to evaporate, sound to dissipate. The Works, left alone, would be a supercooled moment right before sliding.

That *almost-sliding* is food. The factories were not built to make a product. They were built - or grown into, or found - to make the hum that keeps the slide from happening. The lathe turning nothing holds the air at a frequency. The press pressing nothing holds the concrete at a frequency. And the thing in the feather-coats eats the difference between what should happen and what is actually happening. It metabolizes prevented entropy.

You can see it when you touch a pool. Your finger ripples it, dirties it. You lift your finger out. The water is oily, cold. For a half second the reflection is wrong - your face, the sky. Then the water gathers itself and un-ripples, not into a new arrangement but back into the old photograph of water it has memorized. In that correction, that small forced return, the sparse ones shudder. A little heat sighs off the mat on the roof.

It does not reproduce. It extrapolates. Once in a great while, when a truck goes too loud up the access road and puts a dent in the tone, you would find after, if you stayed, that there is one more spaced one standing in the gap, exactly where it needs to be to fill the dent. It didn't come from anywhere. The mat got one unit less dense somewhere else. The total amount stays the same. It has been the same amount for a very long time.

The row of narrow brick houses with the doors that open outward - they are not houses. They are hives in reverse. They are not for living inside. They are for making more outside wall, more north side that sweats dark tongues, more sill, more ledge at exactly the right width for a body to adhere to. The two locomotives, cold, rusted to their final brown, are bones it kept because they were the right shape to baffle sound.

If you stay until late - though late never comes, the light stays the same flat day - you start to realize it is not listening to the factories.

The factories are listening to it.
