Prepare for this city as though combining a polar expedition, a cave dive, and a diplomatic visit to a living industrial organism. It is not safely traversable by an unassisted human. Its principal danger is not aggression but incompatibility: cold, darkness, altered physical laws, infrasonic pressure, misleading architecture, and an ecology that may interpret equipment—or the traveler—as useful material.

## Before departure

### Do not go alone

Travel in a minimum party of three, with:

- One person responsible for navigation and dimensional extraction.
- One monitoring physiology, atmosphere, and local fields.
- One watching the environment continuously.

At least one member should remain at a sealed base camp or outside the basin. Your return mechanism must not depend on local electrical grids, gravity, surface tension, magnetic orientation, or the city’s entangled seams.

Hire a recognized local guide if any reliable contact exists. The inhabitants appear to negotiate with buildings and animals through vibration. A guide is not merely useful for translation; they may be the only thing preventing doors, bridges, and wall colonies from treating you as debris.

### Establish contact in advance

Do not arrive in an occupied street without warning. Send a low-energy, repetitive contact signal from outside the basin and wait. Local communication is likely slow by human standards.

Do not improvise with powerful bass tones. A random knock pattern might be interpreted as:

- A structural command.
- A territorial or migratory signal.
- A door request.
- An alarm.
- An instruction to alter local gravity.
- An imitation of a tower pulse.

Carry a resonator supplied or approved by local inhabitants. Until one is available, communicate through quiet contact taps of limited amplitude, separated by long pauses.

## Human survival equipment

### Environmental suit

Wear a fully sealed, actively heated pressure suit with its own breathing loop. The atmosphere may be breathable in gross composition and still contain:

- Condensed chemical films.
- Microscopic animals.
- Conductive dust.
- Vacuum-bladder spores or juveniles.
- Aerosols produced by machinery.
- Dissolved compounds released by folded reservoirs.
- Gases whose phase behavior changes within engineered fields.

The suit should include:

- Independent oxygen supply and carbon-dioxide scrubbing.
- Heated faceplate with an internal anti-fog system.
- Redundant heating distributed across the body.
- A liquid-retaining underlayer in case local fields interfere with ordinary insulation.
- Hard neck and spinal support against unexpected changes in apparent weight.
- Electrically insulated outer surfaces.
- Replaceable sacrificial boot and glove coverings.
- Smooth seams that cannot easily be hooked by wall fauna.
- Automatic flotation that does not rely only on gas expansion.
- A quick-release outer shell if animals begin incorporating it into a colony.

Do not rely on “waterproof” as a sufficient standard. Water in this city may climb surfaces, enter uphill joints, remain fluid below its expected freezing point, or freeze inside equipment in threads and membranes rather than solid plugs.

### Boots and mobility

Use broad, heated boots with active traction and pressure mapping. Each boot should be tethered independently to the suit. Carry a short staff fitted with:

- Thermal and chemical sensors.
- Vibration monitoring.
- A soft, nonconductive tip.
- A retractable anchor.
- Local gravity-direction indication.

Probe every surface before transferring weight. A dry-looking black slab may carry a nearly invisible water film, be a flexible transit membrane, form part of an animal, or bend only under particular vibration signatures.

Never anchor into masonry without permission. The “stone” may be alive, under repair, or instruction-sensitive.

### Light and perception

Bright visible lights will make you conspicuous without necessarily helping much. They may heat roosting animals, trigger photochemical reactions, or blind inhabitants who use thermal contrast.

Prefer:

- Low-intensity deep red illumination.
- Passive thermal imaging.
- Short-range low-power lidar, if testing confirms it does not trigger doors or animals.
- Tactile mapping through your staff.
- Very gentle sonar in frequencies known not to overlap local signals.

Do not use high-powered sonar. Here, sound can be language, traffic control, structural maintenance, and ecology simultaneously.

Carry physical tactile markers and an inertial navigation system, but assume both can fail. Magnetic compasses may follow city machinery. Gravity indicators may rotate inside elevators. Acoustic maps will be confused by folded rooms and immense conduits. Digital maps may report impossible overlaps that are, in fact, accurate.

Use a breadcrumb line made from inert, insulated fiber—but do not stretch it across animal migration routes or door seams. Mark routes only with local approval.

### Hearing and infrasonic protection

Ordinary ear protection is not enough. Tower pulses and conduit notes will be transmitted through feet, teeth, lungs, and bones.

Use:

- Active whole-body vibration cancellation where possible.
- A shock-isolated helmet.
- A fitted dental guard.
- Chest and abdominal damping layers.
- Continuous heart rhythm and inner-ear monitoring.
- Antiemetic medication.
- A harness that can keep a disoriented person upright.

Expect nausea, headache, phantom motion, anxiety, disrupted balance, tooth pain, and difficulty judging time during sustained low-frequency chords. Establish objective retreat thresholds. Do not allow a traveler who insists they “just need to adapt” to overrule their biomonitor.

During the Hollow’s answering vibration, stop walking, kneel or secure yourself, and wait. The fauna pause for a reason you do not understand. Imitate their caution.

## Choosing an arrival site

Arrive on a surveyed upper shelf outside dense construction, preferably:

- Above ordinary flood levels.
- Away from reservoir embankments.
- Away from walls covered in tessellated animals.
- Outside the apparent fall path of airborne chains.
- Well away from the Unfinished Hollow and its tuning forks.
- On stone tested for field stability over several complete tower-pulse cycles.

Do not appear inside a room. Interior volumes may be folded, occupied, flooded, or subject to rotating gravity. Do not arrive on a bridge: it may only support recognized animals or inhabitants.

Establish a sealed warm shelter with a rigid airlock. Raise it slightly above the ground without drilling. Maintain a heated perimeter, but keep its thermal gradient gentle; concentrated warmth may attract machinery feeders and insulation colonies.

Place dimensional beacons in several locations, including one beyond the basin rim. Protect them mechanically and monitor their mass and internal geometry. A beacon that still appears operational may have become deeper inside than outside or may now point into an earlier state of the city.

## Moving through the city

### Travel slowly, but not on the city’s timescale

The ecology is slow enough to tempt carelessness. Resist that temptation. Enclosure predators, wall layers, chemical defenses, and migrations may take hours to become visibly threatening—but once you notice them, every exit may already be closing.

Assign one person to compare photographs or scans taken minutes apart. Direct observation is poor at detecting local movement.

Watch for:

- A doorway becoming narrower.
- A wall layer thickening behind you.
- Water changing its route.
- Transparent grazers accumulating around your boots.
- Airborne chains sagging toward your heat plume.
- Frost tracks appearing where no traveler is visible.
- Stone becoming soft or rounded near equipment.
- New low-frequency beats in your suit frame.
- Silver parasites gathering around powered devices.

If the environment is slowly rearranging around you, leave immediately. Do not wait to determine whether the process is construction, curiosity, feeding, or repair.

### Do not enter the lower streets casually

Ankle-deep black water is a major hazard. It conceals footing, local fields, transparent animals, chemical films, and sudden openings into conduits. A shoal of hand-sized creatures may be individually harmless but can immobilize a human by piling over boots and locking limbs together.

If a migration blocks a route, go around. Do not wade through, step across the front ranks, or use heat to disperse them. The animals beneath the leading edge may be load-bearing for millions behind them. Disturbing them could redirect a migration into inhabited structures.

If they begin climbing you:

1. Stop moving.
2. Avoid kicking or crushing them.
3. Reduce external electrical leakage.
4. Lower suit temperature slightly, while preserving core heat.
5. Use a guide-approved vibration to request parting.
6. Withdraw along your exact route once a clear patch forms.

### Treat walls as occupied space

A furred wall is a packed animal colony, not decoration. Stay several body lengths away. Do not lean equipment against it.

When a tower pulse begins, look upward and move beneath a solid approved canopy if one is near. Wall creatures may unfold by the thousands and spiral into the street. They do not fly quickly, but their combined descent can obscure vision, hook loose gear, and push a person into water.

Never stand in the open directly after a deep pulse merely to watch.

### Avoid reservoirs

Do not approach a reservoir edge without a local guide. The water may be folded into a depth far exceeding its visible dimensions. Falling in may not place you beneath the surface in any conventional direction.

Anything resembling submerged architecture should be presumed to be an animal. Do not attach ropes, probes, boats, or sampling gear to it. District-sized reservoir beings move rarely, but subtle muscle movement may alter water level, bridge tension, and local gravity before the motion is obvious.

If masonry begins leaning together or bridges become taut, evacuate perpendicular to the district’s structural lines. Do not shelter in a tower erected on the moving animal.

### Be cautious under airborne fauna

Vacuum-chain creatures may look like cables. Confirm every overhead line before passing beneath or touching it. Never clip a tether to a suspended object unless a guide identifies it as infrastructure.

If a chain descends around the party:

- Shut down exposed antenna fields.
- Reduce heat and electromagnetic emissions.
- Remain close together without becoming entangled.
- Move crosswind slowly.
- Do not puncture a bladder.

A punctured vacuum organ could implode, scatter dependent organisms, and provoke a colony-wide contraction.

## Interaction with inhabitants

The conventional inhabitants should be approached as hosts in a city that may regard them as one species among many collaborators. Do not ask which animals are “pests” or which structures are “artificial”; those categories may be meaningless or insulting.

Useful etiquette includes:

- Approach in a small group.
- Keep hands visible.
- Plant your staff gently and do not strike pavement.
- Allow long pauses after every signal.
- Do not touch their living coats.
- Do not shine lights into coat seams.
- Do not step through wall fauna merely because they part for someone else.
- Never follow an inhabitant through a door unless they explicitly signal that the door recognizes you too.

Their silence does not imply indifference. Nearby architecture may be repeating their communication at ranges you cannot sense.

Exchange objects should be chemically inert, thermally stable, and nonconductive. Avoid giving:

- Batteries.
- Radioactive heat sources.
- Strong magnets.
- Loose metal fasteners.
- Self-replicating materials.
- Biological food.
- Plastics that soften in cold water.
- Anything with exposed circuitry.

A “gift” may become food, a breeding substrate, or an accidental command component.

## Food, water, and contamination

Bring all food and water in sealed internal containers. Consume nothing local.

The clear water is not safe simply because there is no vegetation or visible decay. It may contain microscopic chain animals, conductive bone fragments, dissolved maintenance chemistry, or spatial folds that defeat ordinary filtration.

Do not eat the fauna, even in an emergency. Their biochemistry may be toxic, electrically active, dependent on mineral structures, or still behaviorally coordinated after dismemberment.

Maintain strict two-way quarantine. The greater ethical danger may be introducing Earth organisms into an ecology with no plant life and no defenses against familiar microbes. Use:

- Sterilized suits and tools before entry.
- No exposed wood, leather, cotton, soil, or paper.
- Closed waste systems.
- Full external decontamination on exit.
- A prolonged quarantine for personnel and samples.

Do not bring back anything that moves only when unobserved, thickens around cracks, conducts unexpectedly, produces patterned vibration, or alters the freezing point of nearby water.

## The Unfinished Hollow

For an ordinary traveler, the Hollow should be treated as an exclusion zone.

Remain far enough away that its rim cannot be reached during disorientation or a gravity shift. Do not:

- Touch the tuning forks.
- Match their chord.
- Place sensors directly on them.
- Throw objects or pour substances into the Hollow.
- Fly over it.
- Enter channels flowing toward it.
- Use its vibration as a navigation beacon.
- Attempt to measure its depth with a tether.

The Hollow may be an excavation, harbor, machine, or unfinished change to the world’s geography. Water entering without accumulating suggests removal, folding, conversion, or displacement. None is compatible with casual human exploration.

If the Hollow answers and all fauna become still:

1. Stop transmitting.
2. Stop moving.
3. Secure every party member.
4. Do not interpret the silence as an opportunity to advance.
5. Record passively.
6. Wait until local animals resume and your guide moves first.

If your instruments disagree about how long the pause lasted, accept the longest reading for oxygen and evacuation planning.

## Emergency rules

### If gravity changes direction

Do not jump toward the apparent floor. Clip to two independent anchors if available. Keep limbs tucked and wait for the field to stabilize. In a rotating-fall elevator, a wall may become the floor gradually; loose equipment can become lethal.

### If water climbs the suit

Assume it is following a field or conductive path. Disconnect external power, isolate antennas, and withdraw from the nearest wall or seam. Do not brush it upward toward joints.

### If the wall closes around someone

Do not cut immediately. Cutting may trigger repair thickening or animal contraction. Reduce vibration, insert smooth heated spacers, and request local assistance. Maintain the trapped person’s breathing and circulation.

### If the city begins “repairing” equipment

Abandon the object unless it is essential to extraction. A crack being sealed with mineral growth may also mean the device is being incorporated into the street.

### If separated

Do not shout or produce repeated impact signals. Activate a low-power optical beacon and the prearranged personal vibration code. Stay on a stable dry surface. The city may repeat loud calls in misleading directions.

### If extraction systems disagree

Use the beacon beyond the basin rim as primary reference. Do not open a portal whose far side cannot be independently confirmed. Folded spaces may cause a gate to connect to a reservoir interior, a different level of the same room, or an earlier phase of a crystallizing structure.

## Psychological preparation

Expect sensory deprivation and sensory overload at the same time. You will see little but hear and feel everything. Motion will be too slow to follow yet too extensive to ignore. The city may produce a persistent conviction that enormous things are approaching, even when they are merely breathing or turning far below.

You may also begin treating slow processes as scenery. Remind yourself regularly:

- The walls are occupied.
- The streets are changing.
- The water is choosing routes.
- The “buildings” may still be growing.
- The apparent machinery may be fauna.
- The apparent fauna may be machinery.
- The mountains themselves may be recent industrial spoil.

Keep visits short. Sleep only in a sealed, monitored shelter, with one person awake. Rotate observers frequently; prolonged infrasound and darkness will degrade judgment before anyone notices.

The correct attitude is neither fear nor curiosity alone, but restraint. This city is patient, and nearly everything in it operates on scales larger and slower than a human life. Your greatest protection is to avoid becoming significant enough for it to respond to you.
