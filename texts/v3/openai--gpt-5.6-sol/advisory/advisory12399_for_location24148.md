Prepare as though you are visiting a tropical industrial flood zone, a contaminated tidal wetland, and an enormous animal colony at the same time. The city is survivable for an ordinary human, but comfort depends on respecting its pumps, heat, tides, and small inhabitants.

## Before departure

Confirm the ordinary fundamentals first:

- Atmosphere, gravity, day length, and local heat limits are compatible with your physiology.
- Your vaccinations cover tetanus, hepatitis A and B, typhoid, rabies where available, and any locally identified mosquito-borne diseases.
- Your travel insurance includes industrial accidents, animal bites, contaminated-water exposure, and evacuation from a flood-isolated district.
- You have current tide tables, rain alerts, pump-failure warnings, and a map showing staffed shelters and elevated routes. A normal street map is not enough; streets can become channels.
- Arrange sealed accommodation in an inhabited block. Do not plan to sleep in an abandoned building, vehicle, warehouse, or improvised camp.

If possible, arrive through a controlled terminal rather than crossing the basin by road. The terminal will have insect barriers, wash stations, and reliable transport. Schedule your first day lightly: humid heat of this kind can overwhelm even travelers accustomed to high temperatures.

## Clothing and equipment

Wear light, loose clothing that covers most skin. Bare skin buys little cooling in saturated air and greatly increases exposure to insects, contaminated splashes, hot metal, and bird droppings.

Bring:

- Quick-drying long trousers and long-sleeved shirts
- A broad hat, preferably washable rather than woven straw
- Sealed safety glasses or wraparound eye protection
- Waterproof, ankle-high work boots with puncture-resistant soles and firm toe protection
- Several pairs of moisture-wicking socks
- Thin work gloves for railings, doors, and equipment
- Strong mosquito repellent and permethrin-treated outer clothing
- A compact mosquito net as a backup, even in sealed lodging
- Oral rehydration salts and a personal water bottle with a protected drinking valve
- A small first-aid kit with antiseptic, waterproof dressings, antihistamine, and bite treatment
- A flashlight or headlamp, preferably with both white and red settings
- A charged local alarm receiver or radio
- Waterproof bags for documents and electronics
- A P100-class or locally approved particulate respirator for dusty worksites, guano cleanup zones, or contamination alerts

A respirator should not be worn continuously in extreme heat unless officials require it; it adds physiological strain. In ordinary outdoor areas, distance from exhaust, spray, droppings, and disturbed dust is more useful.

Carry a walking pole only if you understand that it is not a depth gauge. Silt can conceal sudden holes, broken covers, submerged rebar, and electrically live infrastructure.

## Heat and hydration

The greatest everyday danger is likely to be heat stress rather than a dramatic animal encounter. Humidity prevents sweat from evaporating efficiently, and the pale sky can disguise substantial solar load.

During your first few days:

- Travel at dawn or during the quieter part of the evening.
- Take scheduled cooling breaks in sealed, air-conditioned buildings.
- Drink treated water regularly and replace salts if you are sweating heavily.
- Avoid alcohol before outdoor travel.
- Do not rely on feeling thirsty.
- Move slowly on exposed causeways; there may be no shade for long distances.

Headache, clumsiness, nausea, unusual irritability, confusion, or suddenly ceasing to sweat are reasons to stop immediately and seek cooled shelter. Local workers may appear comfortable because they are acclimatized and because they know exactly where the nearest refuge is. Do not match their pace.

Condensation can also make metal walkways and stairs unexpectedly slippery. Use handrails with gloves, but test them first: pipes and rails near industrial equipment may be hot.

## Water and ground

Treat every outdoor pool, gutter, canal, and patch of wet soil as contaminated. Water may contain sewage organisms, dissolved metals, petroleum residues, hot discharge, or all four. Even apparently clear condensation can have run across dirty surfaces.

Do not:

- Wade through floodwater
- Wash in rain barrels or cisterns
- Drink from public taps unless marked potable
- Swim in canals or tidal channels
- Eat fish, shellfish, or crabs unless they came through an inspected food system
- Step onto smooth-looking mud

The basin’s mineral flats may have a dry crust over deep, adhesive silt. Subsidence also produces tilted slabs, hidden voids, bowed retaining walls, and doorways below street level. Stay on maintained routes. If water begins rising across one, turn back rather than trying to cross quickly.

Never enter culverts, storm drains, cable galleries, pumping chambers, or abandoned lower floors. These can flood when rain is falling kilometers away or when an automatic gate changes position. They may also contain low-oxygen air, toxic gas, snakes, rats, and industrial electrical hazards.

## Insects and indoor discipline

The insects are concentrated, not evenly distributed. A quiet courtyard can be tolerable while the shaded side of the next building is covered in flies.

Keep doors closed and do not hold them open while unloading. Check window seals, vents, and the space around utility penetrations when you enter a room. Store all food—including fruit, sweets, pet food, and used utensils—in hard sealed containers or refrigeration. Dispose of rubbish through the building’s system the same day.

Before dressing:

- Shake out boots.
- Check sleeves and trouser legs.
- Inspect towels and bedding.
- Look beneath bags that have rested on the floor.

Never slap or brush blindly into a dark recess. Cockroaches and silverfish are nuisances, but a drain or cabinet may also shelter a rat, snake, crab, or exposed electrical contact.

Mosquitoes will be worst near neglected cisterns, settling ponds, and still water after rain. Use repellent even when few are visible. Sleep only behind intact screens or in a net. If you develop fever during the visit or in the weeks after leaving, tell the clinician exactly where you traveled.

## Animals

Most animals will avoid or ignore you if you do not corner them. The dangerous situations arise at concentrated feeding sites, nests, drains, and road crossings.

### Crabs

After rain and near evening, expect roads and yards to become occupied suddenly. Walk slowly and watch where you place your feet. Do not kick crabs aside or try to lift them. Large culvert crabs can inflict serious crushing wounds, and even small ones can cause infected cuts.

If driving, reduce speed substantially after rain. Swerving around individual animals can put the vehicle into soft ground or flood channels; follow local traffic procedure instead.

### Birds

Give rooftops, ledges, cranes, and outflow channels a wide berth. Do not pass beneath dense colonies without eye and head protection. Droppings are slippery, corrosive, and potentially infectious. Nesting birds may strike the head rather than merely call.

Never feed birds. It can trigger a mass approach and draw rats and dogs after dark. Do not touch sick, stunned, or dead birds.

### Rats, dogs, cats, and bats

Do not approach feral dogs, even if they appear exhausted or friendly. Avoid blocking their route beneath trailers or between warehouses. Seek medical care promptly for any bite or scratch.

Cats on upper floors may be tolerated working animals rather than pets. Leave them alone.

Do not handle bats found indoors. Close internal doors, isolate the room, and call building staff. A bat bite can be too small to notice, so direct contact warrants medical evaluation.

### Snakes and monitor lizards

Watch drain mouths and warm concrete margins. Step around animals rather than attempting to move them. Water snakes may be harmless by local standards but should not be identified casually by a visitor. A monitor lizard can bite, claw, and whip with its tail if cornered.

For any bite, remain still, call emergency services, and follow local medical advice. Do not cut the wound, apply ice, or improvise a tourniquet.

## Industrial hazards

The subdued machinery is still operating machinery. Quiet tracks may carry automatic freight, and sluice gates may move without a nearby operator. Vibration through the ground is not necessarily a warning; it may be normal background operation.

Observe:

- Painted walkways and barrier lines
- Flashing beacons even when no alarm is audible
- Clearance markings around gates, rails, conveyors, and adjustable pipe joints
- Respirator and hearing-protection signs
- Restrictions around cooling outfalls and settling ponds

Do not lean on pipes, sit on utility housings, or shelter beneath suspended lines. Hinged connections and floating platforms are designed to move. A gap that was narrow in the morning may not be narrow after rain, settlement, or a tide cycle.

If a warning horn sounds, stop and consult the local alarm system rather than guessing its meaning. A single distant tone may indicate a gate movement, chemical release, severe weather, or evacuation of a specific elevation band.

## Moving through the city

Use enclosed transit when available. Outdoors, travel with a local guide for the first several journeys. Locations that look close across an open flat may be separated by canals, fences, unstable soil, or several kilometers of causeway.

Good route planning accounts for:

- Tide stage
- Recent rain
- Pump status
- Heat exposure
- Bird colony closures
- Freight movements
- Crab crossings after rain
- Availability of cooled shelter

Avoid shortcuts across graded lots. Their emptiness is deceptive. Some are firebreaks or flood clearances; others may conceal settling ground or buried infrastructure.

At night, carry a light but do not shine it continuously into nesting colonies or at vehicle operators. Watch the ground immediately ahead for crabs and snakes, then periodically scan farther forward for dogs and standing water. Do not walk beside the edge of a dark channel: damp concrete can be almost frictionless.

## Food and lodging

Eat in inspected indoor establishments. Choose cooked food served hot, and avoid anything that has stood exposed. The rule about food lasting only minutes in the open is literal: flies can cover it quickly, and heat accelerates bacterial growth.

Your lodging should have:

- Reliable cooling
- Positive-pressure or well-filtered ventilation
- Intact door sweeps and window screens
- Potable water certification
- Backup power above flood level
- An elevated evacuation route
- A clear procedure for pump or seal failure

Keep luggage off the floor and away from walls. Do not leave damp clothes in a heap. Wash them promptly or seal them until they can be cleaned.

## Conduct and expectations

Do not mistake the apparent emptiness for abandonment. The city’s essential work happens remotely, underground, and behind sealed walls. Restricted structures may look derelict while containing active equipment. Ask before photographing ports, pumps, freight systems, or treatment facilities.

Also avoid disturbing animal concentrations for photographs. A quiet flock may erupt without warning, exposing eggs and chicks to heat or predators. A wall darkened by flies is best viewed from a distance.

Psychologically, the absence of vegetation may be more unsettling than expected. There is little shade, softness, or visual relief. Combined with the low cloud, muffled machinery, and animals concentrated in cracks, the basin can produce a sense that the city is both empty and watchful. Plan time in cooled communal interiors, and do not spend every day in the outer industrial districts.

## If something goes wrong

For flooding, chemical alarms, or pump failure, move to a designated elevated interior—not merely the nearest upper floor. Old buildings may have compromised foundations or no sealed refuge.

For contaminated-water exposure:

1. Leave the water without running across uncertain ground.
2. Remove wet clothing.
3. Rinse with verified clean water.
4. Clean and cover all cuts.
5. Report the exposure, especially if water entered the eyes or mouth.

For heat illness, seek cooling immediately and call for assistance. For bites, deep claw wounds, or punctures from corroded metal, obtain medical care the same day.

## Before leaving

Clean boots, luggage wheels, and outer equipment at an approved wash station. Inspect bags for insects, egg cases, small crabs, and nesting material. Wash clothing hot if the fabric permits, and do not carry feathers, shells, animals, sediment, or untreated water between universes.

The city is best experienced from maintained walkways at dawn, during a controlled visit to the tidal gates, and from an enclosed high corridor as evening reorganizes the basin. From there you can appreciate what makes it extraordinary without becoming part of its food web: the hard mineral openness, machinery felt more than heard, and immense animal abundance gathered wherever water, warmth, and human imperfection meet.
