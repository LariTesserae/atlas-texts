## Overall assessment

This is not a wilderness. It is a closed, self-correcting installation wearing the shape of a landscape.

An ordinary traveler should assume that every surface, pool, animal, and atmospheric process belongs to one maintenance system. The fauna may be largely harmless, but the world’s housekeeping mechanisms could be extremely dangerous to anything they classify as contamination, obstruction, excess biomass, or damaged inventory.

Visit only with a reliable return mechanism, sealed equipment, and a strict rule against interference. Do not plan to live off the land.

## Before arrival

### Confirm that you can leave independently

Do not depend on finding local fuel, shelter, personnel, or compatible machinery. There may be no inhabitants capable of conversation and no distinction between “public infrastructure” and the internal organs of the installation.

Your return system should:

- Remain on your person or within immediate reach.
- Work without local navigation signals.
- Be waterproof, corrosion-resistant, and protected from condensation.
- Include a timed emergency recall.
- Have a redundant locator outside this universe or region.
- Not require a physical marker left on the plain; loose objects may be collected rapidly.

If using a vehicle or portal station, keep it on one of the dry roads if possible, but do not block the road. Treat even apparently unused roads as active machinery.

### Conduct remote atmospheric tests

The air feels breathable, but “warm, humid, and ordinary-looking” is not enough. Test for:

- Oxygen and carbon dioxide concentrations.
- Carbon monoxide, ozone, and nitrogen oxides.
- Volatile insulation compounds.
- Metal aerosols and microscopic ceramic dust.
- Biologically active spores, cells, proteins, or engineered vectors.
- Unexpected anesthetics or metabolic regulators.
- Pressure and trace-gas changes synchronized with the ventilation cycle.

Begin the visit on closed-loop breathing. Open your suit only after repeated sampling from several points and heights. Even if the air is safe chemically, local organisms may exchange signaling chemicals intended for their own ecology.

### Establish a strict contamination protocol

This system recycles everything. A hair, skin flake, food crumb, blood droplet, or microbial colony may be treated as recoverable matter. It may also introduce chemistry the installation has not encountered for geological lengths of time.

Bring:

- A sealed suit with integrated gloves and boots.
- A full-face respirator or closed breathing loop.
- A pack-in/pack-out waste system.
- Sealed food and water.
- A method for containing sweat, urine, blood, and damaged equipment.
- External sterilization suitable for both organic contamination and industrial residues.

Do not shed disposable covers, survey flags, adhesive tape, chalk, fibers, or biodegradable markers. What you leave may be reclaimed; what the reclamation process does in response is unknown.

## Clothing and equipment

### Humidity and heat

The primary environmental burden will be heat stress, not temperature alone. Near-saturated air prevents sweat from evaporating. A traveler can overheat while feeling merely damp and uncomfortable.

Use:

- Active liquid cooling or another non-evaporative cooling system.
- Moisture-proof inner layers.
- Anti-fog face protection.
- Waterproof communications and medical sensors.
- Hydration with measured electrolytes.
- A core-temperature alarm.

Do not rely on breathable hiking clothing. It will become saturated and remain saturated.

### Foot and body protection

The surfaces are unworn because they do not wear, not because they are gentle. Expect cutting burrs, knife-like plate margins, sudden lips, slick condensate, and deep slots hidden beneath crowded animals.

Wear:

- Puncture-resistant, electrically insulating boots.
- Soles designed for wet metal and ceramic.
- Shin and knee protection.
- Cut-resistant gloves and outer fabric.
- A compact fall-arrest line near ridges and maintenance gulfs.
- Eye protection against pressurized droplets and unexpected venting.

Avoid exposed metal jewelry and conductive trekking poles. Leakage currents are part of the food web and may be stronger near ports, seams, grilles, and damaged-looking fittings.

### Navigation

The fixed light, repeated hexagons, manufactured haze, and lack of changing shadows will quickly destroy ordinary directional intuition. The sun angle cannot tell time, and similar basins may be separated by tens or hundreds of kilometers.

Carry at least three independent navigation methods:

- Inertial navigation.
- A mapped return bearing to your entry point.
- A local beacon network with devices fixed to your own vehicle or companions rather than abandoned on the ground.

Do not rely on footprints, water disturbances, scratches, cairns, scent, or dropped markers. The environment removes consequences.

Record altitude as well as horizontal position. A maintenance gulf can be hidden behind a ridge until you are nearly upon it.

## Where to travel

### Prefer the cream-colored roads

The roads are likely the safest available surfaces because they are dry, resilient, and avoided by the fauna. However, “unused” does not mean “inactive.”

Walk near one side rather than down the exact center. Maintain awareness in both directions. If the road warms, vibrates, changes texture, or produces a low-frequency tone, leave it promptly and move to a stable plate margin without entering an animal colony.

Do not camp:

- At road intersections.
- In front of factory portals.
- Over seams, hatches, grilles, or drainage slots.
- Beneath ventilation structures.
- On surfaces conspicuously free of water unless they are confirmed roads.
- Anywhere animals maintain an unusually precise empty space.

An empty strip in such a crowded world is probably empty for a reason.

### Avoid crossing colonies

The animals may not hunt in the ordinary sense, but they are integrated with ports, replacement apertures, energy systems, and one another. Stepping between them may interrupt a scheduled exchange and provoke either a defensive reflex or a maintenance response.

Never assume immobility means incapacity. A creature that has not needed to move for millennia may still possess functional jaws, venom, spines, electrical organs, or immense muscular strength.

If passage through a basin is unavoidable:

1. Observe for at least one full visible behavioral cycle.
2. Identify openings in the colony that remain open, rather than spaces that appear temporarily vacant.
3. Avoid touching eyes, pores, membranes, serial markings, mouthparts, and attachment structures.
4. Do not step onto apparently dead bodies.
5. Retreat if nearby organisms begin synchronizing around you in a new pattern.

A change in synchronization may be the closest thing this world has to an alarm.

### Stay well back from maintenance gulfs

The air above a gulf may conceal:

- Condensation-slick edges.
- Pressure changes.
- Toxic gases heavier than surface air.
- Strong electromagnetic fields.
- Sudden maintenance movement.
- Vapor hot enough to burn.
- Openings that interpret a falling person as incoming material.

Use a tether before approaching within several body lengths of an edge. Never descend without a vehicle specifically designed for industrial vertical environments. The balconies and cranes should not be trusted merely because they look motionless.

## Behavior around machinery

Do not press, pull, cut, pry, connect to, sample, or “repair” anything.

Especially avoid:

- Standardized feeding ports.
- Pale metal seams.
- Weeping apertures.
- Microscopic sieve fields.
- Factory doors.
- Reclamation hatches beneath animals.
- Heat exchangers.
- Pressure reservoirs.
- Conduits encircled by organisms.
- Any socket whose dimensions happen to fit your equipment.

The system has preserved equilibrium over immense time. A valve that appears available may be waiting for an animal organ, not a tool.

Do not draw power from the grid. Electrical leakage is already budgeted as food. Even a small load may deprive organisms, trigger compensation elsewhere, or cause the system to classify your device as a newly attached consumer.

Likewise, do not pour out water, cool a hot surface, block a drip, redirect a stream, or cast a long-term shadow over a light-feeding colony. Here, minor environmental acts are interventions in a closed accounting system.

## Behavior around fauna

### Do not feed anything

Human food introduces unbudgeted salts, fats, carbohydrates, microbes, and packaging residues. A creature may consume it without being able to process it, or the machinery may react to the altered chemistry.

### Do not “rescue” injured animals

Predation, tissue removal, and apparent suffering may be scheduled maintenance behaviors. Removing a victim or treating a wound could cause the system to produce a replacement while the original remains present, creating the kind of numerical discrepancy it is built to correct.

### Never stand on a body, even a district-sized one

Large soft expanses may resemble islands or mats. They are living animals with respiratory fields and possibly maintenance openings beneath them. Their movement may be slow but powerful.

### Avoid the replacement process

If an animal fails and the surface begins to open:

- Move away immediately.
- Do not look into the aperture from above.
- Do not interfere with the descending body.
- Do not remain between the hatch and the colony.
- Expect nearby pressure, electrical, or chemical changes.

The greatest local danger may be being lowered into reclamation with the failed organism.

## The likely response to a traveler

At first, the installation may ignore you. This is not proof that you are welcome. It may simply lack a category for a mobile, unregistered organism.

Watch for subtle escalation:

- Droplets changing their otherwise perfect timing.
- Animals turning eyes or sensory structures toward you.
- Empty lanes forming through colonies.
- Local warmth increasing or decreasing.
- Roads becoming tacky or unusually soft.
- Apertures opening under or behind you.
- Condensation beginning to collect preferentially on your equipment.
- Low machinery tones departing from the normal constant bass.
- Identical creatures appearing repeatedly ahead.
- Factory portals developing seams of light or vapor.

If any of these occur, return by the shortest safe route. Do not wait for an obvious alarm. A world organized around damping irregularities may respond quietly.

If the ground opens beneath you, move laterally rather than trying to inspect the mechanism. If surrounding animals withdraw, follow the direction that leads to open road—not the gap they are forming around you, which may mark a reclamation zone.

## Food, water, and shelter

Bring all consumables. Do not drink local water, even if it is chemically pure. It may contain trace nutrients, signaling compounds, nanostructures, dissolved maintenance agents, or organisms too small to see. Purification designed for natural pathogens may not remove engineered molecules.

Eat only inside a sealed enclosure. Capture every crumb and droplet.

For rest, use a self-contained bivouac with:

- Active cooling.
- Independent air.
- A sealed floor.
- No stakes or anchors driven into the surface.
- Vibration and ground-movement alarms.
- An opaque interior and clock-controlled lighting.

The permanent late morning will disrupt sleep and time perception. Maintain a strict personal cycle using clocks, scheduled meals, and artificial darkness. Never estimate elapsed time from the sky.

Keep sleep periods short enough that another person or automated system can continuously monitor the surroundings.

## Psychological preparation

The place may be more disturbing than it is immediately hazardous.

Expect:

- A sense that distance is meaningless.
- Difficulty remembering which basins you have crossed.
- Unease from being watched by enormous numbers of eyes without ordinary curiosity.
- False impressions that motion has stopped.
- Auditory fixation on droplets, breathing cycles, and turbine bass.
- Loss of time sense under unchanging daylight.
- Compulsive attempts to find variation in repeated patterns.
- Anxiety caused by the disappearance of footprints and other evidence of passage.

Travel with at least one companion if possible, but use a fixed check-in procedure; casual conversation may become unreliable under heat and sensory monotony. Record decisions aloud. Schedule regular orientation checks and return thresholds before setting out.

Do not remain simply because “nothing has happened.” In this environment, uneventfulness is an active process maintained by machinery.

## Emergency guidance

### If injured

Seal blood immediately. Do not allow it to enter drainage channels or animal basins. Move to the road if able, but avoid leaving contaminated dressings behind.

A local system may attempt to collect spilled tissue. Treat any opening surface, converging mites, extending mouthparts, or sudden flow of water as a reason to evacuate.

### If lost

Stop on a confirmed road or stable ridge. Do not wander basin to basin trying to recognize landmarks. Use inertial records and beacon bearings. If those disagree, return through your recorded route in reverse rather than choosing the visually familiar direction.

### If equipment is taken into an aperture

Abandon it unless it contains your sole return mechanism. Do not reach into the opening. The machinery below is likely stronger than any person and may not distinguish your arm from attached waste.

### If approached by a moving animal

Move steadily aside without running into another colony. Do not place yourself between the creature and a port, hatch, grille, pool, or factory. Its destination is likely scheduled. Let it complete its route.

## Recommended visit duration

A first visit should last hours, not days. Remain within direct return range and conduct no invasive research.

Longer expeditions require:

- A sealed mobile habitat.
- Redundant cooling.
- Detailed road mapping.
- Remote drones that do not shed material.
- Medical monitoring.
- Full biological quarantine on return.
- A plan for retrieving failed drones without touching the local systems.

Even then, avoid permanent bases. A base introduces mass, shade, heat, waste, vibration, and inventory that the installation may eventually try to balance.

## Final rule

Behave less like an explorer in an ecosystem and more like a contaminant trying not to attract the attention of an immune system.

The animals are conspicuous, but they are probably not the principal danger. The principal danger is the world’s commitment to returning every unexpected thing to a condition in which it no longer makes a difference.
