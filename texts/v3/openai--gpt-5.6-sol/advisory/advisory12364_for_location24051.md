## Overall recommendation

Treat the Fixed Foundry as a **sealed industrial anomaly**, not a cave or ruin. An ordinary human should not enter without a pressure suit, an independently maintained exit, and support personnel remaining outside the chamber’s influence.

The greatest danger is not any single animal or machine. It is the Foundry’s refusal to permit lasting change. A visitor may be able to walk through it normally—or may discover that leaving, consuming supplies, forming memories, or even adding their body to the room counts as a change the place must somehow cancel.

Remote reconnaissance should come first. If an unmanned probe cannot enter, perform a task, and return with both its data and its internal clock advancing normally, do not send a person.

## Essential preparation

### 1. Bring your own atmosphere

Never expose skin or breathe the chamber air.

The Foundry is effectively an industrial desiccator. Its walls remove water vapor before droplets form, and its dust is likely alkaline, electrically active, and abrasive. A conventional respirator is inadequate.

Wear a fully sealed suit with:

- Closed-loop oxygen and carbon-dioxide control
- A deliberately humidified breathing circuit
- Internal drinking supply and hydration tube
- Active cooling that does not depend on sweat evaporation
- Redundant seals at the neck, wrists, visor, and boots
- Automatic puncture detection
- Rapid-curing external patches
- Several hours more life support than the planned excursion requires

The outer suit should be smooth, hard, and resistant to hot metal, ozone, alkalis, sharp salt, and ceramic abrasion. Avoid exposed fabric, mesh, hoses, and loose straps. Small creatures and magnetic seed coils could lodge in them.

Do not remove your helmet for any reason. Do not eat, drink, bleed, urinate, or open a sample container into the local environment. Any leak of body moisture may be seized by the walls almost immediately.

### 2. Protect against the noise and vibration

The compressor and tower produce continuous mechanical concussion, including vibration strong enough to affect teeth, balance, vision, and internal organs.

Use:

- A vibration-isolating helmet
- In-ear protection beneath the helmet
- Active noise reduction
- Bone-conduction or helmet-text communication
- A bite guard if prolonged exposure is unavoidable
- A suit-mounted vibration dosimeter
- Haptic warnings rather than audible alarms

Do not expect to communicate by shouting. The roar may also conceal equipment failure and animal attacks.

Anyone prone to vertigo, panic, migraines, seizures, or cardiac arrhythmia should not enter.

### 3. Prepare for sharp, crowded terrain

There is almost no clear floor. Wear rigid puncture-resistant boots with broad soles and articulated ankle protection. Each step must be selected rather than merely taken.

Carry:

- Helmet-mounted downward lighting
- Short-range lidar or structured-light mapping
- Thermal and electrical-field imaging
- A small, blunt probing wand
- Knee and shin armor
- A compact extraction harness

Do not use a long walking staff indiscriminately; it may strike animals, eggs, live conduits, or electrically feeding plants. Never put a hand down without scanning the surface first.

Move sideways where necessary, with short steps. Do not jump over obstacles. Do not step on geometric pellets merely because they resemble waste; they may be part of the machines’ maintenance cycle.

### 4. Keep the exit physically and causally open

Do not rely on remembering the route. Do not rely on breadcrumbs, paint, chalk, moved objects, or local clocks. The chamber may restore disturbed arrangements or prevent markers from accumulating.

Enter only through an exit system maintained from outside the anomaly: an active portal, anchored breach, or equivalent multiversal return gate. It should have independent power and an operator who never crosses the boundary.

Use a retrieval tether, but do not trust it as the sole means of escape. Test beforehand whether:

- The tether can pay out into the chamber
- Its measured length actually increases
- It can be reeled back
- Marks on it remain in sequence
- Data and power can cross the boundary
- A test object can be removed without a replacement appearing

A rigid or segmented line is preferable to a loose cable, which could catch among the ducts and animals. If the tether stops registering increased length while you continue moving, retreat immediately. That may mean the Foundry is allowing motion without displacement.

## Reconnaissance protocol

Before human entry, send several probes of different kinds. Each should carry:

- Independent atomic and mechanical clocks
- Event counters
- Inertial navigation
- Radiation and chemical dosimeters
- Internal sealed humidity standards
- A unique physical configuration that cannot be mistaken for a duplicate
- Live telemetry and onboard recording

Ask simple questions first:

1. Does the probe’s clock advance?
2. Does its battery discharge?
3. Does storage fill as data is recorded?
4. Does dust accumulate on it?
5. Can it move an object and leave it moved?
6. Can it return with a sample?
7. Is the returned probe unquestionably the same probe?

The last question matters. The Foundry already appears to replace animals in matching postures with matching scars. A returned object may be a local equivalent rather than the original.

If probe results conflict—for example, telemetry shows ten minutes while the onboard clock shows none—cancel human entry.

## Conduct inside

### Stay near the threshold

The chamber is geographically small but may be topologically repetitive. A traveler who walks around one pillar may enter an indistinguishable neighboring chamber without noticing.

For the first expedition, remain within direct visual and instrument-confirmed range of the entry point. Set a conservative external time limit, perhaps five to ten minutes. The outside team, not the entrant, decides when the visit ends.

### Do not touch the machinery

The machines may be maintaining the equilibrium, imprisoned by it, or producing it.

Do not:

- Turn the half-set switches
- Open inspection plates
- Stop a flywheel
- Block a piston or valve
- Touch the singing glass
- Disconnect anything
- Remove the coat or shift token
- Attempt to “correct” the clock
- Enlarge the mineral slots

A control locked halfway between states is especially dangerous. It may not be undecided; it may be holding two incompatible outcomes in balance. Completing its movement could release every deferred consequence at once.

### Do not collect souvenirs on the first visit

Removing a pellet, egg, mineral bulb, plaque, seed coil, or animal could provoke compensation. The room might replace the missing object, prevent your departure, or substitute something of equal mass—including part of your equipment.

If sampling is eventually attempted, use a remote manipulator while the collector remains outside. Record the chamber before, during, and after removal. Keep the sample in a sealed, electrically isolated container.

### Avoid the walls

The thin predators resemble fault lines and strike under cover of the compressor impacts. Since the impacts are effectively continuous, there may be no useful safe interval.

Maintain distance from black salt walls and narrow seams. Use lidar and thermal imaging to look for jointed outlines that are too regular to be fractures. Do not lean against a wall to rest.

Also avoid:

- Standing directly over vents
- Passing beneath hanging bell colonies
- Crossing the fixed silver dust ribbon
- Touching metallic plants bridging two minerals
- Approaching feeding grazers from the front
- Reaching into burrows or ducts

The dog-sized grazers may be peaceful, but their mouths are mineral grinding mechanisms. Give them room. If one blocks the route, wait or retreat rather than forcing it aside.

### Assume the animals are part of the machinery

Do not interpret apparent indifference as tameness. The smaller creatures may be maintaining bearings, sealing cracks, transporting heat, or completing electrical circuits. Killing or displacing one could matter more than damaging a machine component.

Likewise, the predators may be part of population control rather than an external threat to the system. The Foundry may react strongly if you interfere with a hunt.

## Temporal and cognitive precautions

The absence of development creates several possible hazards:

- Your body may continue aging while the room does not.
- Your metabolism may repeat without actually replenishing or depleting anything.
- Memories may fail to consolidate because consolidation is a lasting change.
- You may repeat actions without realizing it.
- The chamber may create a matching replacement when you leave.
- Departure itself may be treated as an impermissible reduction in the room’s contents.

Use externally prompted cognition checks. At fixed intervals, the outside operator should send a new random phrase or number and require an immediate response. The entrant should not know the sequence in advance.

Carry a display showing:

- External elapsed time
- Suit elapsed time
- Remaining oxygen
- Tether length
- Distance to threshold
- A continuously changing challenge code

If the external and internal clocks diverge, if a challenge repeats, or if you remember receiving a code that the outside team says it never sent, withdraw at once.

Do not remain merely because your oxygen gauge is unchanged. In the Foundry, an unchanged gauge may indicate anomaly capture rather than unlimited supply.

## Psychological preparation

The room is cramped, dim, deafening, crowded with partial movement, and devoid of ordinary signs of passing time. Expect intense derealization. The unchanging roar can make motion feel futile, while repeated arrangements may create the impression that you have already performed every action.

Before entry:

- Rehearse the exact route and retreat procedure
- Agree on simple haptic commands
- Establish an unconditional external abort time
- Practice operating while unable to hear
- Use a written task list limited to a few objectives
- Avoid philosophizing or investigating unexpected phenomena while inside

If you see another figure wearing your suit, a duplicate teammate, or yourself beyond a pillar, do not approach. Retreat toward the instrument-confirmed exit. Visual familiarity is not reliable evidence of identity there.

## Emergency procedure

Leave immediately if any of the following occurs:

- Suit pressure drops
- Internal humidity changes unexpectedly
- Dust appears inside the helmet
- The tether loops, duplicates, or stops measuring correctly
- The exit disappears behind an object that was not there before
- A moved object restores itself
- Your footprints appear ahead of you
- You encounter duplicate equipment or personnel
- External communications repeat exactly
- You cannot remember the last challenge code
- A predator begins tracking you
- Machinery changes pitch, even slightly
- The roar stops

A change in machine sound may mean the equilibrium has been disturbed. Silence may be worse: it could indicate that accumulated effects are finally free to occur.

If the suit is punctured, patch it without removing the damaged section and retreat. Do not wash a wound with exposed water inside the chamber. Keep all treatment within the sealed suit.

If a person becomes immobilized, extraction should be performed from outside by tether or manipulator if possible. Sending additional rescuers may simply add more trapped bodies to the equilibrium.

## After returning

Quarantine the traveler, suit, tether, and all instruments.

Initial decontamination should be dry:

- Vacuum under containment
- Electrostatic dust removal
- Magnetic inspection for seed coils
- Microscopy of seams and joints
- Isolation from water until the dust’s reactivity is known

Do not immediately hose the suit down. Black salt, alkaline dust, charged minerals, and hot ceramic residues may react violently or bind the water into a hard crust.

Continue monitoring for:

- Persistent clock discrepancies
- Repeating behaviors or speech
- Failure of wounds, hair, or nails to develop normally
- Identical dust patterns reappearing after cleaning
- Small organisms emerging from joints
- Unexplained warmth, vibration, or metallic sound
- A returned traveler whose scars, memories, or biometrics are nearly—but not exactly—correct

The outside team should also verify that the correct number of people returned. Do not settle for a head count alone.

## Final advice

The safest visit is brief, sealed, tethered, and observational. Do not try to repair the Foundry, free it, stop it, or discover what happens when one of its suspended processes is allowed to finish.

In most dangerous places, the traveler fears being killed. In the Fixed Foundry, the more appropriate fear is being **incorporated**: permitted to move, breathe, struggle, and seek the exit forever, while never becoming someone who has actually left.
