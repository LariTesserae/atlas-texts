This is not a place for an unsupported walk. An ordinary human should treat it as an active industrial environment whose machinery includes space, geology, and several basic physical laws.

## Before entering

Arrange a return method that does **not depend on reaching a particular location**. A doorway left behind may become ten steps away, ten thousand kilometers away, or both. The safest return device is keyed to the traveler, a fixed elapsed time, or a recall signal rather than local coordinates.

Use at least two independent exit systems. One should activate automatically if you become unconscious, lose contact, exceed a heat limit, or fail to cancel a countdown.

Do not plan to camp. A first visit should last no more than an hour, with only a third of the available life support budget assigned to exploration. Distances cannot be used to estimate return time.

Travel with one or two companions, but give every person independent life support and recall capability. Ropes are unreliable here: the distance between their ends may alter without the rope visibly lengthening, and a line across a nearby crack may suddenly span a canyon. Maintain visual contact instead.

## Protective equipment

Wear a fully sealed, thermally controlled suit with:

- Closed-loop breathing. The local air is desiccating, chemically poor, and potentially contaminated with ozone, sulfur compounds, and metallic vapor.
- A substantial water reserve kept completely sealed. Do not drink local condensate, even if any appears.
- Ceramic, glass-fiber, or other minimally conductive outer layers.
- No exposed steel, loose wire, dangling tools, or magnetic fasteners.
- Electrical insulation at the boots and joints.
- Heat-reflective outer surfaces and active cooling.
- Eye protection against blue electrical discharges, infrared heat, and unknown ultraviolet levels from the false sky.
- Continuous monitoring of body mass, suit mass, oxygen chemistry, ambient electric field, magnetic flux, and surface temperature.
- A low-field communication system, preferably optical rather than radio.

Ordinary electronics may be mistaken for environmental signals. Strong radio transmissions, induction coils, motors, and magnetic instruments could interfere with the plants’ field-based behavior. Keep active emissions weak and brief.

Carry instruments on your person rather than setting them down. Loose objects left in shadow may gain mass and sink through the crust. Equipment cases should be internally illuminated if possible, so their contents do not remain in complete shade. Rotate or inspect stored objects frequently.

Bring no blade, shovel, hammer, drill, or sampling equipment on the first visit. Cutting a plant could expose molten or electrically active glass, and damaging the terrain may provoke processes occurring far below.

## Entry procedure

Pause at the threshold and survey before putting weight on the ground.

Do not assume that black stone is cool because it looks solid. Some plate undersides remain incandescent. Test for:

- Heat gradients
- Electrical potential
- Magnetic force
- Vibration
- Geometric distortion
- Unfinished margins

Safe-looking ground should be continuous, matte or fully metallic-skinned, and well separated from glowing seams. Avoid areas of raw color, suspended grains, geometric blur, or surfaces still resolving into existence. A new surface is not load-bearing merely because its outline appears complete.

Mark the entrance with light rather than with a physical object. A strobing optical beacon fixed to your suit or projected from the portal is preferable to flags or cairns. Do not trust its apparent size as an indication of distance.

Record the direction of the fixed shadows. Call their direction east, but do not mistake this for reliable navigation. The shadows may sharpen during a deep relay event, and local geometry may change while their orientation remains constant.

## Moving through the country

Walk slowly, but do not stand still in one place for long.

Your body will cast a permanent eastward shadow. If mass accumulation operates quickly enough to matter to humans, fragments beneath that shadow may become heavier or sink. Periodically move laterally and inspect the ground behind and beneath you. Avoid resting under the metallic crowns, where shade is deepest and the heat is greater.

Never step across a crack merely because it is narrow. Perspective does not predict traversability here. Probe visually from several angles and use thermal or optical ranging, while remembering that contradictory readings may both be accurate. If a crack appears as a canyon from any angle, treat it as a canyon.

Conversely, do not pursue a distant feature merely because it seems close after a change of viewpoint. Establish a strict elapsed-time limit for every outward movement.

Walk on the centers of large plates, not along their edges. Keep away from:

- Glowing seams
- Uplifting ridges
- Magnetized veins
- Unfinished terrain
- Fragile mineral extrusions
- Root filaments
- Areas where all conductive hairs are standing upright

A simultaneous turn of several crowns indicates a large pressure or field change beneath the crust. Stop moving, crouch without touching the plants, and wait for conditions to stabilize. If your field meter rises or the plates begin humming, return immediately.

## Sound discipline

Sound behaves causally backward by several seconds. You may hear an echo before the event that produces it.

Treat any unexplained footstep, crack, shout, ceramic tick, or impact as an advance warning. Freeze and listen. The original event may follow shortly. A sudden “echo” of your own voice before you have spoken is a good reason not to speak.

During the deep relay-note that occurs every few days:

1. Do not run.
2. Do not touch vegetation.
3. Shut down unnecessary transmitters.
4. Watch the ground rather than the sky.
5. Wait for the shadows to soften again.
6. Leave if plants reorient toward you or toward your exit.

The note may indicate a system-wide switching event. Assume that heat, magnetism, geometry, and load-bearing surfaces can all be reassigned during it.

## Conduct around the vegetation

Treat every plant as both a living organism and an energized machine.

Do not touch the foils, stems, seed drums, conductive hairs, or exposed roots. The crowns may carry significant voltage, and apparent softness means little in a structure made of mica-thin metal and ceramic joints. Keep several body lengths away, measured by the number of safe footfalls rather than apparent meters.

Do not use a radio transmitter near a plant. Do not expose it to a strong magnet. Do not place electrodes in the ground. These may resemble reproductive, navigational, or command signals.

Blue pinpricks along the hairs mean charge is present. If the pinpricks spread toward the side facing you, retreat along your exact sequence of previous footholds.

Take photographs passively. Do not collect seeds. A hexagonal drum containing freely rotating rings may still be developing, communicating, or storing energy. “Detached” does not necessarily mean dead, especially in a place where nothing decays.

## The seven-organism clearing

Do not enter the central clearing.

The pale stone is not empty ground. It is an assembly site whose product has not yet fully entered existence. The developing ring and its absent corresponding teeth suggest that part of the mechanism may be elsewhere in time, scale, or geometry. A person standing in the clearing could become an obstruction, a template, a source of trace elements, or the missing counterpart around which the process completes.

Stay outside the overlapping crowns. Do not cast your shadow across the pale stone. Do not place an instrument there “for scale.” The system moves atoms inward over millennia, but it may react quickly to the sudden arrival of kilograms of organized matter.

Observe only from a stable plate outside the root bundles. If the pulse in the pale stone synchronizes with your heartbeat, breathing equipment, communications, or suit electronics, trigger recall immediately.

## Biological risks

The most dangerous rule may be the tendency of matter to become more exact.

Human bodies depend on mixtures: blood, cytoplasm, dissolved salts, emulsified fats, pigments, proteins, and electrochemical gradients. If the local drive toward separation begins acting on living tissue, a sealed suit may not protect you.

Leave at once if you notice:

- Sweat gathering into unusually perfect or motionless beads
- A metallic taste despite the closed breathing system
- Blood separating visibly after even a minor injury
- Skin pigments forming sharp-edged patches
- Salt or mineral crystals appearing in the eyes or mouth
- Sudden stiffness in joints
- Localized changes in body or equipment weight
- Suit materials dividing into layers
- Scratches becoming sharper rather than smoother
- Persistent synchronization between your pulse and the ground vibration

Carry no mixed chemical packs likely to become dangerous if separated. Batteries, composite propellants, medicines in suspension, and pressurized mixtures require particular caution. Use simple, stable materials wherever possible.

## If something goes wrong

If the ground becomes geometrically unstable, do not sprint toward the visible exit. Retrace your exact footholds while activating recall. Previously occupied surfaces are more likely to retain a usable relationship to you than newly chosen ones.

If a companion appears very distant but remains clearly visible, communicate before approaching. They may still be only a few steps away. Have them move one foot at a time and report contact with the ground. Do not throw a rope.

If someone falls into a crack, do not jump or reach across it. The crack may be locally wider than your entire world-line through the chamber. Use traveler-keyed recall or retrieval from outside the local geometry.

If an object becomes heavy in shade, do not lift it. Move the shadow if possible by moving yourself, then wait. If it has begun sinking, abandon it.

If a plant discharges through a traveler, initiate medical recall even if the person remains conscious. The injury may involve metal deposition, magnetic effects, or internal charge paths not visible as burns.

## Personal preparation

The country will feel quieter and more motionless than it really is. Its important events occur either beneath perception, across impossible distances, or over timescales that make a human visit negligible. This can encourage carelessness. Constant light and fixed shadows also disrupt the normal sense of elapsed time.

Use audible or haptic interval alarms inside the suit. Keep a spoken log. At regular intervals, state:

- Elapsed time
- Remaining oxygen
- Remaining cooling capacity
- Number of companions visible
- Exit status
- Ground temperature
- Whether the plants have changed orientation

Do not rely on intuition about scale, nearness, or stillness. Here, those are environmental effects rather than trustworthy perceptions.

Most importantly, arrive as an observer, not an explorer intent on leaving a mark. Do not build a cairn, carve a plate, pour water, plant a sensor, or leave an offering. The landscape is still establishing what a surface is, and the ecology may be part of a manufacturing process whose categories do not distinguish between visitor, contaminant, raw material, and component.

See the bronze gardens. Listen for the buried turbine-note. Watch, from a respectful distance, the unfinished ground become real.

Then leave before the place has had enough time to decide what you are.
