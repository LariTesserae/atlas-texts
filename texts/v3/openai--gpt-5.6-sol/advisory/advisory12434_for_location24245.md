## Expedition rating: extreme; rim observation strongly preferred

The Press is survivable for an ordinary human only as a sealed, tethered technical descent. It is not a place to hike into. Its heat, altitude, gases, darkness, unstable living surface, abrasive winds, and intensely crowded ecology create several independent ways to die.

More importantly, a human body is an ecological event there. You are cool, wet, soft, chemically rich, and conspicuously unarmored. To many inhabitants you may register less as a visitor than as a sudden cave, water source, thermal anomaly, or carcass that has not realized it is dead.

The safest visit consists of observing from the rim with remote instruments. If entry is essential, remain suspended above the floor whenever possible.

## Before arrival

### Train for

- High-altitude pressure-suit use.
- Vertical rescue and fixed-line ascent.
- Heat illness recognition under encapsulation.
- Low-light movement without relying exclusively on vision.
- Remaining motionless under stress.
- Emergency decontamination and suit-breach response.

Do not travel alone. Use at least a three-person team: one entrant and two rim operators capable of hauling the entrant out without assistance. A second person should not descend merely to provide companionship; two people on the floor create more vibration and twice the contamination risk.

Establish your camp well outside the hollow. Do not sleep at the rim, where descending winds, gas overflow, winged scavengers, or migrating animals may reach you.

## Essential equipment

### Atmosphere and heat protection

Wear a fully sealed, positive-pressure suit with:

- Independent breathing supply; no filter-only respirator.
- Low-pressure compensation.
- Active cooling with redundant circulation.
- A hard, replaceable outer shell resistant to volcanic glass, salts, acids, and boring mouthparts.
- Sealed joints without exposed fabric folds.
- Smooth boots and gaiters that offer no crevices for thread-creatures or mites.
- External temperature control.

That last feature matters. The Press’s predators detect cool interruptions in a uniformly hot environment. A conventional cooled suit may advertise you brilliantly. Maintain the suit’s **outer surface** near the local rock temperature while cooling the wearer internally. Never sacrifice the occupant’s safety to perfect thermal camouflage, but avoid cold exhaust ports, exposed hoses, or chilled metal tools.

Carry all drinking water inside the suit or a sealed pack. Do not open water, urinate, exhale, or vent coolant into the basin. Moisture may provoke an ecological response far out of proportion to the quantity released.

### Sensors

Bring redundant instruments for:

- Oxygen and total pressure.
- Sulfur compounds, halogens, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, and corrosive gases.
- Air and rock temperature.
- Thermal imaging.
- Seismic and pressure changes beneath the floor.
- Suit-surface inspection.

Assume ordinary gas detectors are incomplete. Unknown transparent gases may be toxic, anesthetic, corrosive, or capable of penetrating familiar polymers.

Use shielded lamps in visible and infrared wavelengths. Daylight is not useful below the rim. Avoid intense floodlights until their effects on the fauna are known; sudden illumination and lamp heat may cause movement.

### Access and extraction

Rig all anchors in confirmed solid basalt **outside** the occupied hollow. Tap, scan, and core-check anchor points. Apparent rock near the Press may be armor, a plugged burrow, or a mineralized carcass.

Use:

- Two independent lines.
- A powered rim winch with manual backup.
- A rigid or semi-rigid suspended observation cradle.
- A line cutter accessible with either hand.
- A retrieval system that can lift an unconscious traveler.

Do not depend on arches, cairns, ledges, or the bearer-built “hut.” None was built for human load, and many may incorporate eggs, shells, active animals, or weakened rock.

## How to enter

Arrive near local midday, when some reflected light reaches the floor and the rock-air temperature difference is least deceptive. Complete the descent and exit well before dusk.

Survey remotely first. Send down small, sealed probes from the rim, but tether them and recover them. Avoid free-roaming drones that might crash, be dismantled, or become permanent foreign habitat. Sterilize every device before deployment.

The preferred position is several meters above the living pavement. Lower the traveler in a cradle without touching walls or floor. If samples or close inspection are required, use long manipulators.

If a landing is unavoidable:

1. Identify genuinely exposed mineral by spectroscopy, thermal behavior, and prolonged observation.
2. Watch it for hours, not minutes. Tiles move like geological settling.
3. Distribute weight broadly.
4. Keep the descent line loaded and ready for immediate extraction.
5. Take short, deliberate steps.
6. Never put a hand down for balance.

There may be no truly safe place to stand. “Bare” ground may be thin crust over burrows, and armored backs may be indistinguishable from basalt.

## Conduct inside the Press

Move slowly and consistently. Running produces vibration, cooling wakes, and sudden openings in the tile layer—the exact conditions likely to trigger predation.

Do not:

- Pry up a tile.
- Step between overlapping shells.
- Reach into a seam or breathing pit.
- Collect loose plates casually.
- Sit or lean against the walls.
- Enter bearer structures.
- Touch carcasses, however mineralized.
- Follow narrow passages between larger animals.
- Use pitons inside the basin.
- Make unnecessary heat, noise, or vibration.

Assume every surface is alive, occupied, or load-bearing for something alive.

Watch particularly for spires. Their stillness is not passivity. If one unfolds or aligns toward you, stop moving. Do not create a sudden cooler patch by spraying coolant or opening insulation. Ascend smoothly rather than trying to frighten it away.

If pale threads emerge and orient toward you, treat this as an immediate extraction event. They may be seeking cool tissue, moisture, breathing openings, or joints in the suit. Do not brush them off with a glove; that may wrap them around the arm. Withdraw vertically and have the rim team remove them with remote tools in quarantine.

Do not mistake the bearers for guides or builders. They may attempt to collect detachable equipment—or eventually parts of your suit—simply because it is flat and portable.

## Recognizing a gas-rise

The warning signs are:

- Many tiles lowering at once.
- A tightening or coordinated settling of the pavement.
- Ground swelling.
- Muted snaps from new fissures.
- Sudden changes in pressure, chemistry, or heat.
- Fine salt particles appearing in the air.

At the first coordinated lowering of the tiles, leave. Do not wait to confirm it.

During a gas-rise, the floor ceases to be navigable. Animals climb, predators strike indiscriminately, thread-creatures emerge, and shell-borers abandon old armor. Hot gas may compromise seals while salt smoke obscures vision and abrades machinery.

If extraction cannot begin immediately:

- Stay suspended if already off the ground.
- Reduce unnecessary movement.
- Close all external equipment ports.
- Keep the suit exterior warm and smooth.
- Do not shelter under an overhang or inside a bearer chamber.
- Wait for the first violent animal displacement to lessen, then ascend under winch power.

A sudden wind descent can follow or occur independently. Rim operators must secure themselves before hauling. Inside the hollow, wind-blown grit can strip coatings and force small organisms into seals. Turn the suit’s least vulnerable surface into the flow and avoid contact with the braided animal rafts that form during these events.

## The pale central column

Maintain a generous exclusion radius. Do not touch it, drill it, sample it, cool it, heat it, or place an instrument against it.

Its persistent emptiness is the strongest warning in the basin. Every native organism avoids it despite extreme competition for space. That is more meaningful than the absence of obvious radiation or toxins.

If the column begins to vibrate:

- Stop all motion.
- Do not transmit sound or exploratory pulses.
- Do not approach while the fauna is still.
- Note changes in suit electronics, balance, vision, teeth, and bone-conducted sensation.
- Wait until the vibration ends before beginning a smooth withdrawal.

Use analog backups for timing and extraction controls. The vibration may be mechanical, biological, geophysical, or something not adequately described by those categories. A portal or translation device should not be opened beside it; place your multiversal return point at the rim or farther away.

## Contamination and quarantine

The Press’s life specializes in joints, pores, cast shells, and breathing openings. A suit that looks clean may contain organisms under plates, inside fasteners, or within microscopic scratches.

On return:

1. Enter a sealed quarantine enclosure before removing any equipment.
2. Image the suit thermally and microscopically.
3. Remove the outer shell robotically.
4. Bag every layer separately.
5. Sterilize using several methods rather than heat alone; local organisms are heat-adapted.
6. Inspect respiratory connectors, cooling loops, boot seams, and tool housings.
7. Quarantine all samples in dry, high-temperature, chemically isolated containment.
8. Monitor personnel for skin lesions, unexplained mineral deposits, clicking sensations, joint irritation, or sulfurous odors.

Do not bring home a shed shell as a souvenir. In the Press, dead armor is often shelter, egg case, food reserve, or dormant colony. Likewise, do not discard anything there. A washer, foil wrapper, ceramic plate, or broken visor could be carried uphill by bearers and incorporated into the ecosystem for millennia.

## Best way to experience it

The most responsible visit is from a suspended rim platform after sunset, looking down with thermal and low-light optics while remaining above the airflow that circulates inside the bowl. You will see little with unaided eyes, but you may hear the faint charcoal scrape of thousands of tiles and, occasionally, the isolated crack of a shell.

Do not let the apparent quiet reassure you. The Press is not empty, dormant, or still. Its motion merely occurs at scales and speeds human senses interpret as landscape.

Go sealed. Go tethered. Touch nothing. Leave before the floor begins to tighten.
