The power does not enter as light.

Light would be an injury here. A sudden radiance would bleach the films from the stone, wake the bats in terror, expose every soft body to every waiting mouth. Nor does it enter as a voice. The chamber has spent ages teaching its inhabitants the cost of noise.

It enters as attention.

For a long while—long enough for several pools to empty and fill again—it does nothing that can be distinguished from listening. It follows water through the limestone without urging it. It learns the routes of the eels and the slower routes of minerals. It feels the blind towers being built grain by grain. It attends to the blue throat of each frog, though no pulse is meant for it. It knows the great amphibian beneath the floating mat not as a monster or ruler, but as hunger shaped by patience.

The power is wise enough not to mistake suffering for error.

The cricket taken at the pool is not restored. The tadpoles eaten by their larger siblings do not rise again. The drowned bat remains food for beetles, fungus, and stone. To rescue each life from every other life would unravel the chamber more completely than fire. Mercy cannot mean making nothing die. In a place so crowded with continuance, death is one of the ways life changes hands.

So the power does not abolish hunger.

It does not make the pools permanent, because creatures live in their vanishing. It does not steady every drip, because migration—even by a few body lengths—is part of the cavern’s thought. It does not preserve every tower, every brood, every root arch, or every old animal beneath the mats. Wisdom refuses the cruelty of freezing a living place into safety.

But there are dangers the chamber did not grow around.

Far above, beyond the pale stones, people have begun to mark the plain. They see emptiness where there is only concealment. Stakes are driven into the ground. Lines are drawn between them. A machine is planned: a deep bore for water, then perhaps another for heat, then a road heavy enough to carry more machines. No one intends harm. No one knows the buried collapse is there.

The power attends to this also.

It could break the machines. It could frighten the workers with voices from the fissure or open the ground beneath their feet. But fear breeds return in stronger forms, and mysteries become prizes.

Instead, on the morning the surveyors arrive, one of them notices a breath of warm, wet air among the stones. Kneeling, she finds a translucent insect on her sleeve, a species she has never seen. She calls to another. They uncover no treasure, no gate, no proof of ancient builders—only the crooked fissure and, from somewhere below, the briefest blue pulse.

Curiosity delays the bore.

Delay brings instruments. Instruments reveal warm water moving through fragile limestone, isolated lineages in the pools, fungal partnerships not recorded elsewhere, and a chamber whose collapse would poison the very aquifer the drilling was meant to reach. The lines on the plans are moved. The road bends several miles away. On paper, the place becomes a protected hydrological cavity with restricted access, a phrase so dry that no collector is tempted by it.

The surveyor believes she was fortunate to have knelt when she did.

She was.

Below, nothing knows it has been saved from an ending for which it had no instinct.

The power’s second act is smaller.

Deep beneath the chamber lies a crack filled with ancient silt. Water still presses beyond it, but the seal has held for centuries. To tear it open would drown the lower hollows, erase the mud towers, and wake chemistry long separated from the pools. To leave it untouched would not be wrong. Yet the rains above are changing. The intervals between floods lengthen. Condensation thins. One by one, dependable drips begin to fail.

The power does not open the crack.

It places no new spring where none belonged. It merely allows one root-tip, descending blindly through stone, to enter a hairline fracture it might otherwise have missed.

The root thickens over years. It draws a little moisture upward and gives a little downward. Condensation gathers along its fibers. At first the change is scarcely enough for a mite. Then moss darkens beneath it. Midges find it. A frog waits there through one dry hour, then another. Fungal threads enter the bark. The root becomes a narrow bridge between waters that never directly meet.

It is not abundance. Abundance would draw too many bodies and become another kind of famine.

It is one more chance.

The power’s third act concerns the being beneath the floating leaves.

She is old for her kind. Algae covers the furrows of her back, and one forelimb bears the pale scar of a season when the pool nearly vanished. She has eaten frogs, eels, beetles, crickets, two of her own young, and once a burrower washed alive from its tunnel. She has also carried shrimp in the folds of her skin and sheltered larvae from the strongest current simply by remaining where she was.

Her pool is shrinking.

She feels this before any creature at its edge. Pressure diminishes in the flooded cracks. The stone ceases to answer distant movement. The water grows warm and close around her. Hunger tells her to remain still. Every successful season of her life has rewarded stillness.

But this season is unlike the others.

The power could fill the basin. It could lift her in invisible hands and place her in deeper water. It does neither. A life carried without its choosing may survive, but it has been made into an object of rescue. The power will not take from her the small sovereignty proper to a living thing.

Instead, during the hottest hour, when even the insects are quiet, a single drop falls where no drop has fallen before.

It strikes the surface near the mouth of a narrow overflow channel.

The old amphibian feels the ring pass through her jaw.

Another drop follows—not immediately, but before the first disturbance has wholly left the stone. Then a third.

They are not enough to refill the pool. They carry the mineral taste of deeper water.

She waits.

For a day she does not move. On the second night, she turns her head. This alone clouds the basin. On the third, urged by no thought that could be put into words, she leaves the shelter beneath the mats and presses herself into the overflow channel.

It is almost too narrow. Her loose skin catches on calcite. Her scarred limb folds badly beneath her. Once she stops, and stillness begins to reclaim her.

Then, ahead, a droplet enters stone.

She feels it.

She moves again.

The passage descends behind a curtain of roots into a basin she has never known. It is not large, but it is deep. Finger-length eels already live there. Blind shrimp graze its sides. No adult of her species occupies it.

She enters with one soft displacement of water.

The pool closes.

The power does not bless her with understanding. She does not know that anything guided her. She knows coolness along her belly, pressure returning to her jaw, prey moving somewhere beneath a shelf. Within a few days she becomes still again.

That is enough.

Afterward, the power withdraws—but not entirely, because perfect withdrawal is another fantasy of power. Having attended, it cannot become as though it had never known.

It leaves no symbol. The three upright slabs remain ambiguous. The fused ring acquires no flame. No writing appears on the level passage. The frogs do not begin to worship the moving column of noon. The burrowers receive no command. The bats carry no prophecy into the night.

Only a few consequences remain.

A road bends away.

A root finds a fracture.

An old animal reaches deeper water.

And once, many seasons later, rain comes hard across the sunburned plain. Brown water rushes down the entrance cleft and enters the chamber with its old temporary violence. Towers soften. Basins overflow. Ferns bow beneath the current. Crickets climb in silent processions. Burrowers carry their young upward. The hidden waterways touch one another.

In the new pool, the old amphibian feels the flood before it arrives.

The current brings the scent of silt, leaves, fungus, and distant animals. It brings also several larvae of her own kind, swept from narrow cracks. Hunger opens within her as it always has.

She takes one.

The others scatter into crevices.

One finds the root whose tip entered the hairline fracture. It follows the film of moisture upward into a pocket too small for the old one’s head. There it remains until the waters divide again.

Whether this is mercy cannot be separated from everything that feeds and is fed upon. The power does not require the separation.

It asks for no gratitude.

It has not made the cavern gentle. It has not made it just according to laws that belong elsewhere. It has only protected the conditions under which its many small lives may continue to answer change in their own ways.

Then it attends to the plain, the rains, the people drawing their careful maps, and the warm buried darkness they nearly mistook for emptiness.

Below, a drop chooses a new route.

A frog lifts its throat.

Blue passes briefly through the dark, seen by another frog and by no one else.

The power sees it.

It does not need to be seen in return.
