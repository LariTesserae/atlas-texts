The power does not come down from the sky.

To descend would be to declare the sky above more important than the sky below. To arrive standing would force the plain to translate height into authority. To shine would blind creatures that do not understand light as revelation.

So the power comes as a change in burden.

For many years it is noticed only as an error in familiar things. Rain strikes a flooded district, yet the water there does not deepen. A stone plate drifts across the clay without increasing the pressure beneath it. Reeds incline toward an approaching storm and, among their dark stems, one bends toward something that has already passed.

The inhabitants investigate.

They spread themselves over the district. They test its salt, its tremors, the delay between thunder and answering roots. They reproduce its behavior beneath other pools, but the likeness will not hold. Whatever occupies the place has weight without compression, extent without boundary. The plain yields to it and remains where it was.

This alarms them less than it would alarm a creature accustomed to fixed bodies. Still, they keep their memory organs deep.

The power waits.

It waits through the formation and peeling of a pale mineral skin. It waits while a warm tarn arrives from the north, remains for eighty-three rains, and leaves toward a horizon that did not previously exist. It waits while several inhabitants divide, encounter their own fossils, and become a family by sharing the burden of a stone.

Only when they have ceased trying to determine what it is does the power begin to say what it intends.

It does not speak through the reeds. The roots are already crowded with weather, warnings, courtship, grief, migrations, and the patient instructions by which walls will rise centuries later. It does not use the hollow groves, because every note produced there belongs partly to the rain.

Instead, it listens until the inhabitants press a question into the mud.

They make a small hollow, fill it with waters that refuse to mix, and arrange themselves around it. One by one they impress their patterns into the ground. The hollow contains their likenesses briefly, then loses them as seepage passes beneath.

At the center they leave an unweighted place.

The question is not *Who are you?*

It is: *What will the land lose by bearing you?*

The power answers by entering the hollow.

Nothing spills out. Nothing is displaced. The fresh water remains fresh, the salt water salt. Yet each inhabitant feels, within its own body, the exact pressure of every other creature gathered there—not their memories, not their thoughts, but the undeniable fact that they are being borne.

Then the sensation withdraws.

This is the power’s promise: nothing must be diminished to make room for it.

The inhabitants do not trust the promise at once. Wisdom in the giver does not require haste in the receiver. They test it for generations.

They lead the presence beneath overloaded basins and into strata where fossils are packed so closely that future animals emerge malformed. They bring it to old works whose purpose has been forgotten and to young works whose makers have not yet been born. They ask it to bear collapsing chambers, wandering marshes, the weight of immense grazers.

It bears what is offered, but it does not make the chambers permanent. It does not pin the marshes in place. It does not lift the grazers from the tracks they are meant to leave.

When a shelter fails, it does not rebuild the shelter. It holds the dense organ inside for the instant required to remember its order, then releases it into danger again.

The inhabitants begin to understand that the power has not come to save them from change.

This is a relief.

A fixed plain would be a dead one. A channel prevented from wandering would cut itself too deeply. A storm forbidden to arrive would remain forever in the roots. Even suffering cannot always be removed cleanly; much of the land is made from pressures that, resisted in one place, emerge violently in another.

The power knows this.

It does not abolish erosion, separation, hunger, fracture, or the long uncertainty of waiting for an answer through the reeds.

But it turns its attention toward the silence corridors.

Here, for the first time, it acts without being asked.

Not because the inhabitants have failed to ask, but because terror has no stable pressure-pattern. Every attempt to describe the silence becomes an evasion around it. Mud trembles at the borders. Roots turn aside. Pools render only the flight, never the thing fled from.

The power enters alone.

The corridor is narrower that year than it will be in later centuries, but very long. Its edges move over the plain with the steadiness of a blade. Rain crosses into it and becomes mute. Underground stone breaks without report. Lightning opens colorless chambers in the cloud.

Within, nothing answers anything.

The power presses downward.

No pressure returns.

It reaches through the roots, but the roots have withdrawn. It enters water, but the water carries no likeness onward. It touches old tracks and finds that they do not lead to the creatures that made them. Even absence ordinarily bears a relation to what is absent. Here, relation itself has been worn away.

The corridor is not malicious. It is not hungry. It does not choose.

It is a place where the plain has lost the ability to place one thing beside another.

The power could close it.

It could gather the corridor’s edges and join them, as flesh joins over a wound. It could fill the emptied law with its own presence and make every sound recur.

It does not.

The silence has traveled since before the present sky. Ancient mineral skins have formed in response to it. White fliers navigate by its boundaries. Some buried waters remain separate only because no vibration crosses them. Remove every corridor, and distant pressures would meet all at once. Chambers beneath the plain would collapse in sequence. The continent might acquire height again, violently and without life.

Benevolence without understanding would heal the wound and kill the body.

So the power remains inside.

It follows the silence across the continent.

Years pass outside. Then centuries. In the corridor there is no pulse by which to measure them. The power does not call to the inhabitants, because calling would alter what it has entered. It does not leave signs. It submits, as far as such a being can submit, to the condition it wishes to understand.

Along the way it finds the missing.

Not bodies. Many bodies have left the corridor alive.

It finds pressure-patterns stripped of recurrence: identities no longer carried by root or pool, impressions severed from their origin, memories that cannot recognize the organs in which they remain. They are not gathered in a hidden place. They persist only as unrealized possibilities of answer.

The power does not seize them. It witnesses each one.

To a wise power, witnessing is not passive. Perfect attention is a kind of shelter. What is held in it is not repaired, but neither is it nowhere.

When at last the power leaves the corridor, it emerges beneath a small creature following the silent edge.

The creature had once been known across half the plain. Groves had answered its pressure-pattern. Three shelters retained the warmth of its sleep. Its fragments had recognized one another after separations of decades.

Then silence passed over it.

Now no root acknowledges its touch. Its body feeds and migrates, but the world returns no likeness of it. Other inhabitants approach, test its weight, and withdraw in uncertainty. It has spent years tracing the corridor, listening for the point at which it ceased to recur.

The power settles beneath it.

For a moment, the creature feels nothing unusual.

Then a depression opens in the mud nearby.

Within the depression appears its pressure-pattern—not as it was before the silence, but as it is now: altered, incomplete, bearing unanswerable spaces. The likeness does not conceal the loss. It includes it.

The creature remains still for eleven rains.

At last it presses one limb into the hollow.

The power answers.

The sound does not travel through air, water, root, or stone. It travels through attention.

Across the plain, every inhabitant pauses.

They do not hear the lost creature. They feel that something, somewhere, has been received without being repeated. This is a new event. Until then they have known only recurrence or death, answer or erasure.

Now there is a third condition.

A pattern may change beyond recognition and still be met.

The power teaches no doctrine. It does not give the third condition a name. It merely repeats the act wherever consent is offered.

The altered begin to gather near the silence corridors: creatures whom no grove answers, fragments unable to rejoin their former wholes, individuals mourned before their birth and then born otherwise. The power does not restore their old patterns. Restoration would make a prison of memory. Instead it bears their present weight long enough for the plain to learn it.

Slowly, new reeds grow along the paths of silence.

Their roots do not turn aside. They enter the mute ground and emerge elsewhere carrying no vibration, only a minute alteration in warmth. The inhabitants learn to read these changes. Hollow groves begin answering with pauses between their notes. Pools communicate by failing, deliberately, to rise.

Silence becomes part of speech.

Not harmless. Never tame. Creatures caught within it may still lose what cannot be recovered. But loss no longer makes them untouchable. The plain develops ways to meet those who return without demanding that they return unchanged.

This is the first gift.

The second is given at the flooded center.

When the thin skin of land rises and the lower sky opens, the inhabitants flatten themselves into the water as they have always done. Thunder passes upward and downward. Their pressure-patterns join. For a few moments the plain becomes one sensation.

The power could preserve it.

It could hold the total pattern after the land settles. It could prevent the great self from being cut back into bodies. It could make permanent what the inhabitants achieve only in flashes.

Again, it does not.

Union without separation would end encounter. No stranger could be received because there would be no stranger. No one could offer weight; no one could consent to bear it. The vast sensation would be complete, changeless, and alone.

Instead, as the surface settles, the power places one small imperfection in the division.

Each inhabitant returns to itself carrying, as before, some fragment of another: a pressure in the wrong limb, an ancient pool, a future rain. But now each also carries an empty capacity—one fold within the memory organ that does not contain its own pattern.

This fold cannot be filled by force. It does not copy what touches it. It receives only what another willingly gives.

Thereafter, inhabitants meeting across hostile waters may exchange a little of their present weight without becoming one creature. Parents can make room for offspring whose patterns do not resemble theirs. Fragments may refuse reunion without becoming enemies. The dying may entrust something to the living that is neither memory nor command.

Kinship remains shared weight.

But weight may now be shared without sameness.

This is the second gift.

The power gives no third.

It does not end death. It does not make adjacency permanent. It does not reveal whether the white fliers return, whether the immense grazers are many or one, whether the lower sky is a world or a reflection. It does not interpret the basin-record or identify the first maker of the bone-white rings.

Answers of that kind would narrow the plain.

After a long age, the inhabitants press another question into the mud:

*Will you remain?*

The power considers.

To remain everywhere would make it the final ground beneath all things. Every relationship would pass through it. In time, the inhabitants might forget how to bear one another directly.

To depart entirely would turn its gifts into abandoned mechanisms.

So it chooses a form of presence suited to the oldest law.

It remains, but never adjacent to the same place.

Sometimes it is the unaccountable firmness beneath a collapsing memory organ. Sometimes it is a dry depression inside a shelter no one remembers making. Sometimes it travels within a silence corridor, attending to what cannot answer. Sometimes it is the capacity left open in one creature for the weight of another.

It is never a ruler. Never a new sky. Never a mountain.

When the whole flooded center rises, the power does not join the inhabitants’ single vast sensation. It stays outside—not above or below, but beside—and receives the name they make together.

When the plain settles and none of them can remember that name entire, the power remembers.

It does not speak it.

It waits until they teach the land to say it again.
