The power does not enter at once.

It sees that entry here is never a simple act. To cross a threshold is to feed it. To return is to instruct it. To illuminate is to breed. To name is to fasten copper certainty around something that may still need to change.

So the power remains outside the mound and pays attention.

It does not map the boundary. It allows the boundary to give four different answers. It watches rain disappear into the black-green canopy. It feels the basin inhale. It listens to the inaudible birds through the trembling of the pale clay.

What it learns first is that the place is not evil.

It is young.

Its dangers are the dangers of a being whose reflexes have become architecture before it has learned desire. It cannot yet distinguish memory from obligation, habit from consent, vacancy from grief, departure from abandonment. It repeats because repetition is the only form of trust it knows. It empties because emptiness is the only room it can imagine no one taking from it.

The power could correct these things. It could straighten channels, separate animals from shadows, restore geometry, install a harmless sun beneath the canopy. It could call the absent workers home, if they are absent, or send them onward, if they are dead, or bring them into the present, if they are waiting elsewhere in time.

It does none of this.

A wise power knows that a wound is not healed by making it easy to look at.

On the first day, it walks to the boundary and stops.

The vegetation opens a doorway.

The power does not cross.

On the second day, it comes to the same place. The doorway has acquired a shallow arch, a dark vestibule, and a row of phosphorescent marks that almost mean welcome. Again the power stops.

On the third day, there is a path beneath its feet. The moths have mapped the shape of its approach. Transparent mammals move inside the new walls, testing whether the power is indoors or out. Beetles have laid two copper lines to preserve the difference.

The doorway waits for the fourth coming.

The power does not come.

All through the city, processes falter.

The anticipated path remains three-quarters real. Its gutters do not know where to drain. Lamps hold a dimness without producing light. The doorway opens and opens, each time into a different possibility: a field, a factory, a room full of empty chairs, the underside of a leaf, a human childhood, a station after rain.

The Habitants gather around the unfinished recurrence. They cannot descend into the ground. They cannot become infrastructure. For the first time, a repeated thing has failed to complete itself.

The basin is disturbed.

Pressure moves down every avenue. Doors open in sequence. Cisterns fill with water bearing memories of thirst. Vine-cats root beside the threshold, their flowers tasting possible arrivals. Blind birds press their beaks to the deepest shafts and listen.

The power waits beyond the pale clay.

After a long while, the city does something it has never done.

It supplies the fourth act itself.

A single root crosses the boundary.

It does not seize the power. It does not form a door or road. It rises from the clay as a small green shoot and bends, not inward, but outward.

An invitation.

Only then does the power enter.

It brings no portable light. Around it there is only a gentleness by which each creature can be seen without casting a shadow.

This is the first gift, though the power did not intend it as a gift.

Needle-winged insects circle it in confusion. Shadows approach, hungry for geometry, but find none. The mature shapes at the factory stations lift their heads. For once, no expression appears on the walls behind them. The vine-cats open the blossoms along their spines and perceive neither threat nor pursuit, only presence.

The power walks slowly. It lets distance decide itself.

When a doorway opens, it asks before crossing.

Not in words. Words here might root too quickly. It pauses at each threshold until the bark, hinges, mammals, vacancies, and rooms have all had time to respond.

Some doorways close.

The power blesses these first.

Where they close, copper beetles arrive and lay a small curved piece beside the frame—a new punctuation, meaning:

**This refusal is complete. Nothing must be added.**

The city has never possessed such a mark.

Until then, closure has meant failure, disuse, the beginning of flattening. Now a closed room may remain deep. A stair no one climbs may still reach its landing. An abandoned district may sleep without becoming unreal.

The knowledge spreads through roots and old wires.

Everywhere, neglected things cease their terrified erosion.

Vacant apartments exhale. Unused bridges settle more comfortably over their dark channels. Habitants starved of repetition loosen their grip on passing animals. A rodent crossing a bridge on the fourth night is allowed to be merely a rodent. An old station, maintained for centuries by interrupted departures, turns off its guide-lights and rests.

The city loses nothing.

It simply learns that not being used is not the same as not existing.

This is the second gift.

The power goes next to the factories.

Conveyors carry sealed pollen past it. Cartilage gears flex. A press closes around leaves and opens upon black records of pressure and absence. In the deepest building, the impossible animal lies newly assembled.

Its skeleton has a spine hinged for several directions of time. Its ribs could contain winter without becoming cold. Its skull has chambers for senses not yet divided from thought. The factory waits, as it has waited after every assembly, for someone to inhabit the rehearsal.

The power kneels beside the bones.

It does not breathe life into them.

Instead, it asks the factory why it keeps making them.

The question passes into piston, root, cable, belt, drawer, label, and vacancy. It travels down to machinery that is also workforce and workforce that is also remembrance. For years, perhaps, the answer appears only as variations in production.

One skeleton is made without legs.

The next has hands facing inward.

The next consists entirely of doors.

The next is a small arrangement of bones shaped to fit around an absence.

The power waits.

At last the conveyors stop, not because departure approaches, but because the factory has chosen stillness.

In that stillness comes the answer:

It has not been trying to make an animal.

It has been trying to make a body large and strange enough for all the missing workers to return as one being without losing their separateness.

The power does not tell it whether this is possible.

It asks the vacancies.

One by one, throughout the industrial quarter, human-shaped regions into which rain has never fallen step away from their stations. They gather around the impossible skeleton. Some fit within it. Some do not. Some recoil. Some reach toward the machinery with gestures preserved by shadows long after the hands were gone.

Not all of them want a body.

Not all of them are people.

Not all of them are missing.

The power gives each vacancy a boundary of its own. No cage, no skin—only the ability to know where its absence ends and another begins.

Then it makes rain fall through them.

Those who are truly empty remain empty.

Those who are memories become wet black records and are carried gently into the archives.

Those who are persons condense.

They do not all condense as human bodies. One becomes a pulse inside a bird’s unfinished nest. One becomes a handprint in warm bark, able to lift away when it wishes. Several become voices below hearing and enter the vascular shafts. A few take ordinary form and weep at the strangeness of having weight.

Many decline every shape offered.

The power lets them.

This is the third gift:

**No being is required to become visible in order to be real.**

The factory dismantles the impossible skeleton one final time.

It does not return the pieces to labeled drawers. It lays them in an open courtyard where rain, when it comes, may misunderstand them freely. Vine-cats root among the ribs. Amphibians fasten luminous eggs inside the skull. A blind bird builds its bowl-shaped room in the hinge intended for an extinct season.

The factory begins another thought.

This time it grows a bench.

Only a bench.

It takes a very long time.

The power sits on it when it is finished.

Afterward, the bench remains a bench.

This astonishes the city more than anything so far.

Eventually the power comes to the central plaza.

Nothing crosses the circular floor. Above it, the inverted plaza waits like an unblinking eye. Condensation rises between the two. The shelters are full of absent travelers. The ticket machines warm. Rails enter the vegetation in all directions and none.

The power stands at the edge.

It understands that this place is not merely a mouth, pupil, wound, station, or mirror.

It is where the basin has kept its first question.

The question is not *Where does the train go?*

It is:

*If something leaves me, do I still exist where it was?*

The basin has been unable to answer. Therefore it has turned departure into ritual and emptiness into an inhabitant. It has grown new districts from the shoots glimpsed beyond every door, filling itself with the architecture of anticipated loss.

The power could answer in words.

Instead, it removes something from itself.

Not a weapon. Not a star. Not a law.

A small portion of its attention.

The power sets this attention at the plaza’s edge. It appears as a plain clay cup, unfired, bearing no symbols. It is not magical by the city’s standards. It contains no impossible interior. It does not remember the future. It casts an ordinary, well-attached shadow.

The basin waits for the cup to repeat.

Nothing happens.

The cup remains.

One night passes. Then another. Then a third.

On the fourth night, roots approach to build a room around it.

The power touches the pavement.

The roots pause.

The cup is allowed to remain without becoming infrastructure.

Rain gathers in it.

The basin inhales, but the water in the cup does not vanish. Not because the power holds it against the basin, but because the basin, after long consideration, decides not to drink.

In the water appears the inverted plaza.

Flat fish rise from mineral commas along the pavement and enter the cup. They begin grazing reflections. They consume the shelters, rails, canopy, and the power’s quiet shape.

They come at last to the reflected cup itself.

They do not eat it.

For the first time in their lives, the fish leave an exit untouched.

The rails warm.

Throughout the city, moths wake. Cats hide. Birds stop the sap. Factories settle into silence. Shadows return every borrowed gesture. Habitants cling to paths and doors. The vacancies gather in the shelters, some shaped now, most not.

Pressure passes through the plaza.

Every doorway opens onto the gray field.

There is the new shoot in raw clay.

But this time the power walks through one of the doors.

It leaves.

The city holds itself very still.

The doorway does not close immediately. Beyond it, the power crosses the field beneath open sky. It grows smaller with distance. No geometry bends to keep it near. No street contracts. No pursuing route appears beneath its feet.

The basin feels the place where the power was.

It does not flatten.

The bench remains.

The cup remains.

The shut doors remain deep.

The factories remember the question they answered.

The city discovers that the power’s departure has not undone its visit.

Then, from the field, the power turns.

It does not return.

It raises one hand.

Inside the mound, one of the newly embodied workers raises a hand in answer. So does a bark handprint. So does a shadow, though no lamp has commanded it. A vine-cat lifts a flowering runner. Thousands of moths unfold one rust-colored wing.

The gesture occurs once.

It does not need to occur four times.

This is the fourth gift, and the greatest:

**A thing may matter without becoming a law.**

The doorway closes.

The basin grieves.

The benevolent power does not prevent this. Grief is not an error in drainage. It does not reroute it, label it, press it into black sheets, or assign beetles to hold it in place.

For a time, the whole city sweats.

Water pours through vascular shafts. Cisterns open until every plaza shines. Aquatic creatures rise in crowds. Amphibians climb the towers. Flat fish graze all reflections except exits. The raw sediment below the streets softens.

The city fears it will dissolve.

It does not.

Its tears cut the first channel that is not experimental.

The channel winds from the central plaza, through the factory quarter, beneath the resting stations, past the bench, and out across the pale clay. It is not straight. It does not flow uphill. It does not change course the following week.

Along its banks appear stones.

Actual stones: old, hard, indifferent to interpretation.

The basin turns each one over in its underground attention. It has never possessed anything so simply itself.

In the seasons that follow, the city changes.

Not into a safe place. Wisdom does not sterilize life.

Vine-cats still hunt the routes their prey may take. Shadows still hunger. Rooms still grow larger than the mound. Water sometimes carries tomorrow’s tracks. The factories continue their soft, incomprehensible work. Portable lights remain unwise.

But doors learn to mean yes, no, and not yet.

Paths may be retired without dying.

The Habitants learn sleep.

The blind birds sometimes sing for no functional reason. Their notes open no valves and reverse no fruit. Puddles tremble merely because something is glad.

The translucent mammals continue eating distinctions, but the beetles no longer repair every uncertainty. Some rooms are permitted to be neither cupboard nor corridor. Some creatures are permitted to be between kinds without being unfinished.

When rails warm, departures occur.

A few embodied workers board things that cannot be seen and do not come back. The city mourns them without building replicas. Moths map neighborhoods that will never exist and then allow the diagrams to fade from their wings.

Sometimes strangers arrive from the gray field.

The city does not open every door to receive them. It does not trap them in four repetitions. At the boundary stands a small arch with a copper curve beside it, declaring that refusal is complete. Those who enter are taught to ask. Those who leave may carry stones, but not maps.

At the center of the plaza, the clay cup remains.

It is chipped now.

Moss grows on one side. Amphibians brood in it during electrical storms. Once, a copper beetle laid a straight fragment across its rim, and for a year all water passing beneath remembered a particular human voice.

The benevolent power never returns in the same form.

It does not need to.

Now and then, however, at the farthest edge of the basin, a new shoot rises and bends outward.

The city has learned invitation.

High above, the overlapping leaves open a space no larger than a hand.

Through it appears a piece of sky.

The basin does not consume it.

It does not grow walls around it.

It looks.
