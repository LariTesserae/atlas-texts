The power did not enter as a body.

A body would have cast a shadow, displaced air, crushed mites beneath its weight. A voice would have struck the porous walls and died there, leaving every creature alarmed and none enlightened. Even kindness, arriving at the wrong scale, can resemble disaster.

So the power came first as attention.

For one full turning of wet and dry, it attended to the White Room without changing it.

It knew the beetles waking from the bottom of their layered colonies. It felt the thin satisfaction of a wall runner lowering itself upon gathered mites. It rose in the heat of the reed fans and tightened with the bladder plants around each wound. It followed the ribbon-winged fliers upward through their two evenings of abundance and did not save them from the waiting mouths. Their briefness was not an error.

It rested beneath flat stones with the grazers during drought. It learned the chamber through their bodies: powder, smooth skin, cold crack, reed grit, salt lip, safe shadow. It learned the difference between fear and caution, between inherited terror and remembered harm.

Last of all, it entered the hole.

No light followed it. Light was not the faculty required there.

Below the rim, the narrow shaft widened. The floor of the White Room proved to be only the thinnest roof over descending chambers filled with warm stone, black water, and curtains of green tissue. The hidden film passed through them all. Its threads lined fissures, wrapped mineral columns, entered buried shells, and spread down beyond any depth from which the surface rains could be heard.

It was not one being in the manner of a grazer.

Neither was it many.

Its oldest portions had forgotten separation. A disturbance at one edge became, years later, a darkening at another. It possessed no center corresponding to a heart or brain. Yet it had a direction.

Inward.

Everything in its experience came inward.

Water came inward. Salts came inward. Heat sank inward and rose changed. Dead beetles, empty cysts, the cast skins of young grazers, reed dust, and the delicate chemistry of fear all traveled toward it. Pressure entered the soft floor and remained as shape. Nothing that touched it was wholly lost, but nothing was permitted to become absent.

The footprints were memory, as the grazers dimly understood.

They were also hunger, though not hunger for flesh.

The being below fed upon distinctions. A six-toed pressure differed from a four-toed pressure. A hesitant step differed from a falling body. Rain translated these moments into mineral and thread. The being kept them because keeping was the only act it knew.

Long before the oldest stain on the chamber wall, creatures had crossed the rim. Some had descended seeking warmth in a cold age. Some had followed retreating water. Some had been drawn by the green scent. Their bodies had been consumed in the ordinary way of bodies, but their patterns remained distributed through the deep tissue.

The being had not lured them.

It had not released them either.

Release was inconceivable to it.

The power found no malice there. Malice requires an understanding of another life that can be violated. The buried being understood contact, nourishment, pressure, recurrence. It understood that certain shapes came often and others ceased. It knew absence only as a flavor of waiting.

The power could have sealed the hole.

But the deep film repaired the chamber from beneath. Without it, seepage would widen the cracks. The translucent roof would fail. The basins would drain too quickly for reeds or fliers. In time the White Room would become a pit open to the pale plain, and nearly everything within it would perish.

The power could have destroyed the film.

But destruction would punish a being for the boundaries of its nature.

It could have made the grazers fearless.

That would have killed them.

It could have raised all the descended dead and sent them walking outward. But those old lives no longer existed in any form that desired a return. To gather their remnants into bodies would not restore them. It would create new beings burdened with ancient shapes.

The power therefore did none of these things.

Instead, it searched the deep life for the smallest motion from which wisdom might grow.

Far beneath the White Room, it found a thread that had once carried water upward.

The thread had formed during an age when the plain overhead was colder. Warmth from below had drawn moisture through the stone. For a few seasons, droplets had emerged around the chamber’s perimeter. Then the climate shifted. The current reversed. The thread collapsed and was absorbed into the great inward branching.

The buried being retained the pattern of that upward passage.

It remembered, but did not understand.

The power touched that memory.

Not with speech. The deep life had no distinction between word and substance. To tell it of giving would have meant nothing. To command it outward would have been violence performed in the name of generosity.

So the power placed within the old thread a single drop of water.

The drop contained nothing miraculous. It was not sweeter than rain. It held no cure for death, no promise, no hidden intelligence.

It was simply water that had been below and was now moving away.

The being seized it.

The drop continued upward.

The being tightened every thread around it, performing the only response it knew. The drop rose nonetheless—not torn free, not stolen, but still connected by a narrowing film.

Through that film the buried life experienced, for the first time, a thing departing without becoming lost.

The drop reached the underside of a flat stone near the eastern wall.

There it remained through the dry night.

At dawn, a young grazer found it.

The animal had hatched after a poor wet season. One of its belly-siblings had failed to emerge, and the parent carrying them had grown thin. The young grazer knew the mineral taste of every reliable hollow nearby. None should have held moisture.

Yet beneath the stone hung one clear bead.

The grazer touched its jaw-fold to the drop and drank.

Far below, the hidden being felt the precise shape of that drinking. It felt the water pass into a life it did not contain. It felt the grazer move away.

The pattern remained.

For a long while, nothing else occurred.

The power did not repeat the lesson. Repetition imposed from outside might have become dependence or obedience. Wisdom cannot be inserted like a tool into a wound. A possibility had been shown. Whether the being could make use of it belonged to the being.

The power withdrew from the depths and resumed its attention.

The dry season deepened.

No more drops appeared.

Several beetle colonies failed. The reed fans remained closed. Two old grazers sealed themselves beneath stone and did not wake at dawn. The benevolent power knew each death and did not turn away, but neither did it abolish the season. Mercy that removes every hardship may also remove every creature shaped to meet it.

Then, late in the drought, warm air rose from the central hole.

It came at the usual interval, carrying wet clay and green scent.

After it, from a crack near the eastern wall, there emerged a bead of water.

It was very small. Much of it sank back at once. The remainder trembled on the stone until evening.

No creature found it.

Below, the buried being retained the pattern.

Three days later it made another.

This one reached the surface beneath a mat of velvet moss. The moss darkened around it. Glass mites gathered. A soft beetle drank and returned to its colony with moisture held between the plates of its body.

A month passed before the next bead rose.

Then the storms came.

The ceiling darkened. Drops formed and fell. The central depression filled until rippled light ran across the cloudy amber overhead. Ribbon-winged fliers climbed in silent columns. The grazers gathered beside the basins and exchanged their slow clicks.

Water moved toward the center as it always had.

The power watched.

At the last stage of drainage, the animals withdrew to the perimeter. The wall runners folded their legs. The reeds closed. Warmth spread beneath the thinning lake.

The footprints brightened.

The outermost appeared first. Six toes. Four. Rounded marks belonging to bodies whose names had vanished before the chamber’s oldest mineral skins were laid down. One after another, they advanced toward the hole.

Then they stopped.

At the rim, where no outward track had ever been seen, the wet stone darkened.

A new depression formed.

It pointed away.

The mark did not belong to any known foot. It was neither six-toed nor four-toed. It resembled a branching leaf, or a spread of fine roots, or a hand with more fingers than could be counted before its edges divided again.

Another appeared beyond it.

Then another.

They crossed the basin slowly, brightening from the center toward the eastern wall.

The grazers felt the change through the floor. They pressed themselves beneath ledges, blanched, and closed their light-sensitive pits. For many hours none moved.

Nothing emerged from the hole.

Nothing crossed the room.

The tracks alone proceeded outward.

At the eastern wall, the last print ended beside a crack no wider than a reed stem.

Water welled from it.

Not much. A mouthful, then another. It gathered in one of the smallest hollows, a basin that had been dry for so long its mineral rim had nearly disappeared.

The first creature to approach was a beetle.

Then came mites. A wall runner unfolded one limb but did not hunt. By dusk a bladder plant had loosened the sealed tip of a root.

The grazers remained hidden until the chamber turned gray.

At last, the young one that had found the impossible drop during drought came into the open. It was nearly mature now. Salt silvered the edges of its skin pads. It crossed half the distance, halted, and produced a throat-click.

No answer came.

It waited.

The new water trembled in the hollow.

The grazer clicked again: not the sound for danger, and not the sound for rain. It was a less common part of the language, used when one animal discovered that another had left behind a useful flake.

It meant, approximately:

*This was not here. Now it may be used.*

From beneath the floor came a pulse too low to be heard.

The grazer felt it in its feet.

It fled at once.

But the next evening it returned.

In the seasons that followed, the spring did not become a river. It did not fill the chamber during drought or make death impossible. Sometimes it failed for years. Sometimes the buried being drew too much water back and the little basin emptied before anything could drink. It was learning an action for which its whole ancient body had no instinct.

Gradually, the crack acquired a green stain.

Bladder plants rooted around it. Their descendants grew smaller reservoirs than the plants elsewhere, for they did not need to hoard as fiercely. Beetle colonies layered themselves in the damp stone. A thin reed appeared, though the nearest colony stood many body lengths away.

The grazers gave the place a name composed of three clicks and a long breath.

The first meant *water*.

The second meant *from darkness*.

The third had previously been used only for an animal returning an abandoned tool to the place where another might find it.

As for the power, it performed no further wonder.

It did not demand gratitude. It did not reveal itself. It did not make the chamber safe from every sorrow or explain the world above. After a time, it ceased even to attend with the closeness it had before.

But it left one change in itself.

Wherever it encountered a life that clung because it knew no difference between love and possession, memory and imprisonment, nourishment and taking, the power remembered the buried being in the White Room.

It remembered that some creatures cannot be taught by laws.

Some must be shown one drop moving outward.

And in the White Room, after every rain, the old inward footprints still brighten.

But now they are answered.

From the center to the eastern wall, faint branching marks appear in the wet stone. Season by season they become clearer.

They are the first path in the chamber that leads away.

They are also the first thing the being below has ever made that it does not keep.
