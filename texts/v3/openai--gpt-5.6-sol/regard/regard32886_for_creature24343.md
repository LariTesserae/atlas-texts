The power does not descend.

Descent would be a kind of violence here. Anything entering the high air becomes weather. Anything touching the plain becomes chemistry. A body, however gentle, would cast intolerable shade, redirect the wind, poison some catalytic lineage with the harmless moisture of another world. Even a voice would be pressure, and pressure is command.

So the power remains beyond the sky and attends.

It does not attend for an afternoon, which would reveal only glare and motion. It does not attend for a season, which would reveal catastrophe mistaken for suffering. It watches long enough to see a tower rise, mature, rupture, and be digested. It watches a migration route vanish and return ten generations later as a warm seam under new rind. It follows the slow opening of one deep valve from first contraction to full release.

By then it has learned not to pity everything that breaks.

It sees agony, certainly. The grazer whitening around a predator’s clamps. The hook-wing dragged down by the burst filaments of its prey. Provinces starved where vascular borders close. Young towers split by untimely pressure and leaking their stored atmosphere into the cold.

But it also sees that rescue, carelessly given, would become preference. To save the grazer is to starve the hunter. To still the storm is to prevent the spores from crossing the plateau. To mend every ruptured tower is to leave no basin for nurseries. Mercy applied at the scale of moments would become cruelty at the scale of ages.

The power therefore does not make the place safe.

Nor does it awaken the country. It is awake already, though not in the manner of eyes opening. To gather its awareness into a single bright point would destroy the form of mind it possesses. A central thought would have to speak over the old negotiations of glare, pressure, hunger, graft, refusal, and return. The continent would become able to say *I*, perhaps, but innumerable smaller truths would be silenced to make the word possible.

The power does not consider this an improvement.

Instead, it learns to ask.

The first question takes four hundred years.

It begins as a slight alteration in sunlight: not enough to warm the rind, not enough to trouble the vane groves, only a narrow interval of brightness recurring at no natural period. The mirrors respond as they respond to any excess. They turn it aside.

The power changes the interval.

Again the blades turn.

It waits through storms and migrations, through the collapse of three towers whose bass notes had entered the exchange. It repeats the light only when the same province lies uninjured beneath it. Gradually the vane groves cease merely rejecting the signal. One forest throws the brightness east. Another returns it west. A third divides it into pulses.

For eighty years the pattern travels nowhere. Then a buried electrical vein takes it up.

Blue fire branches across the night.

The power answers with the faintest possible dimming of a star.

Nothing follows for nine years.

Then, over a region larger than a sea, the towers alter their pitch.

The chord contains no word. It may not even contain intention as a smaller being would understand intention. It is partly curiosity, partly defensive coordination, partly the ordinary tendency of living systems to entrain themselves to rhythm. Yet it is not random. When the power repeats the celestial pulse, the country repeats its chord. When the pulse changes, the chord changes after it.

Question and answer begin there, neither side certain which is which.

Their conversation is slow enough for species to arise during a pause.

The power speaks in eclipses too shallow for injury, in charged particles divided around the world, in starlight polarized along unfamiliar angles. The country replies with the orientation of mirrored provinces, with the timing of root-furnaces, with migrations induced along carefully warmed veins. Hook-wings carry fragments of the exchange unknowingly in their flocking chords. Grazers complete phrases by choosing one vascular road over another. Storms corrupt entire passages. Dead vanes preserve old notes for centuries after their meaning has changed.

No sentence belongs wholly to any creature.

In time the power asks, as nearly as such a question can be asked:

*What do you lack?*

The country answers with wounds.

Not all wounds. It shows no wish to end predation, rupture, erosion, rivalry, or death. These are written too deeply into its continuance. Instead it returns, again and again, the pattern of a particular loss: the sudden cessation of all signals beyond a graft line; the long silence where a severed tract has been lifted into the sky; the fading pulses of living fragments carried beyond the horizon and never encountered again.

The plateau can survive division.

It cannot know what becomes of what is divided from it.

The power understands.

For ages, slabs of living rind have peeled upward in storms. Most shatter. Some descend elsewhere and graft themselves back into familiar tissue. A few pass over the escarpment into the lower, denser atmosphere, where heat or alien chemistry kills them. Rarer fragments climb higher, inflated by seed organs and extraordinary winds, until the world releases them entirely. These become small sealed countries drifting in darkness. They pump for a while. They remember pressure. Their microscopic shutters open to a sun that dwindles day by day.

The power has found them.

It does not return them. Some have changed during isolation. Some carry diseases the plateau has forgotten. Some have become themselves in a way that reunion would erase.

Instead, the power gives the country distance.

Far above the atmosphere, where no native organ could have endured, it gathers only what the world already sheds: ceramic rind dust, dead vane laminates, empty shells, carbon from the living clouds. It adds no foreign substance. It merely arranges these remnants grain by grain, shielding them while the country’s own surviving cells bind them.

A structure forms in orbit.

It is not a machine, though it resembles one. It is a thin, black ring of dead and living tissue, kilometers across and scarcely thicker than a vane. Sunlight turns it. Static feeds it. Pressure-tight chambers within it contain descendants of the oldest airborne fragments.

The ring can do only one thing.

It can feel the light-signals of the power, and it can answer by casting a moving shadow on the high country below.

The shadow is weak. No tissue cools beneath it. No animal looks up. Yet the vane groves perceive its passage and turn. Towers shift their voices. Electrical veins brighten in sequence.

For the first time, the country receives a signal from something that was once itself and is now elsewhere.

The answer takes thirty-one years to assemble.

When it comes, every mature mirror on the western plateau inclines by a fraction. Light rises in a narrow white column through the dry air. The orbital ring trembles under it. Ancient cells, descended from an exile the country had counted as lost, contract in response.

The power withdraws from between them.

After that, it serves mostly as a keeper of intervals. It prevents no storm and forbids no death. It does not make the plain fertile by another world’s standards. It does not quiet the towers or soften the hunters. It allows the continent to remain austere, immense, and difficult.

But when a fragment is flung irretrievably into the upper dark, the power notices.

If the fragment is dead, it is allowed to wander.

If it is living but cannot survive, the power does not always save it. Wisdom has not abolished limits. Yet when survival can be offered without deforming what survives, the power bends a little radiation around it, lends it a little momentum, guides it toward the ring or toward one of the cold mineral bodies circling farther out.

There, sometimes, a fragment takes hold.

A blister forms under black sunlight. A microscopic shutter opens. Fibers seal around native dust. In a thousand years there is a patch of rind. In ten thousand, a breathing stalk no taller than a grazer’s foot begins to sound its first thin note into an airless chamber of its own making.

The power carries no messages between these children beyond what they choose to send. It does not bind them into obedience to their origin. It does not tell the plateau that they belong to it.

It only makes introduction possible.

Across the ages, the sky acquires slow correspondences. Shadows cross the continent at deliberate hours. Mirrors answer. Orbital gardens alter their brightness. Farther colonies, too distant for any quick exchange, pulse against the stars. Their meanings drift as lineages drift. Signals become misunderstandings, and misunderstandings become new languages.

The country never gathers this into a single comprehension. No hidden monarch wakes beneath the rind. No final intelligence rises to command the towers.

Instead, knowledge remains distributed.

A herd turns because an orbital shadow changes the rhythm below its feet. A vane reflects a star that contains the dimming of a distant descendant. A hook-wing folds one note into its mating chord because towers have sung that note for generations. A newborn grazer learns the vibration without knowing it began beyond the sky.

Thus the oldest inhabitant becomes aware—not everywhere, not all at once, and never completely—that departure is not always annihilation.

This is the mercy the power gives it.

Not immortality.

Not safety.

Not dominion.

Only the possibility that what is lost may continue beyond the reach of memory, and that, sometimes, across an interval wide enough to contain worlds, the lost may answer.

Then the power attends from farther away.

The high continent roars beneath the brilliant sun. Its factories manufacture only themselves. Its predators wait. Its towers breathe. Its plains crack and heal.

And now, at long intervals, the country casts its light upward.

Something in the dark turns to receive it.
