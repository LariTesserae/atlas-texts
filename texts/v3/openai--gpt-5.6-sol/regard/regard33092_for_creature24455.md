The power did not descend as light.

Light would have meant little here. It would have startled the slivers, disturbed the symbionts beneath the drifters’ skins, and disclosed distances no living thing had needed to see. Nor did it arrive as a voice. There was no language broad enough to address the plateau, and no single mind within it to answer.

So the power entered as attention.

It followed the north current across the terraces. It passed edgewise with the sliver shoals and waited through the sealed sleep of the seam mouths. It hung among the mourning bells until the whole chamber opened around it. It learned the pressure-shadow cast by a resting bracket and the faint warm taste by which reverse crabs recognized the way home.

It did not ask what the place had been.

That question belonged to the absent.

Instead, it asked what the place was now.

It saw that the ruin was not empty. Every failure in it had become shelter for something. Every breach joined waters that had once been separate. Every fallen tower altered the migration of a shoal; every exposed chamber permitted a colony to begin. To restore the ancient plan would be to destroy the living one. Doors made whole would close upon animals that had occupied their thresholds for ten thousand generations. Shafts cleared of grit would become torrents. Walls raised again would cut old currents and starve distant chambers.

The power could have lifted the plateau from the sea.

It could have stilled the currents, repaired the fitted slabs, raised the worn towers, drained the Basin of Steps, and summoned daylight across the radial avenues. It could have searched the buried levels for the first intact room and from that room inferred the purpose of all the others.

But this would not have been healing.

It would have been the replacement of one world by another, performed for the satisfaction of a history no living creature remembered.

The power left the drowned highland drowned.

It did not make the pale material young. It did not return names to the blank reliefs. It did not wake the makers from extinction or invent ghosts to occupy their halls. It did not teach the slivers that an avenue was an avenue. It allowed the architecture to remain weather.

Yet there was suffering here that served neither life nor freedom.

The power found it in the manner of breakage.

The fitted material endured strain without bending, without cracking, without any preliminary tremor that the animals could perceive. A slab held until it did not. An arch remained secure for an age, then cleaved in one precise instant. Creatures able to read the smallest variations of pressure could not read this. Bells, seam mouths, sleeping brackets, and whole nurseries of drifting eggs were extinguished not because they had failed to understand the world, but because the world had given them nothing to understand.

The power considered strengthening the plateau.

But permanence would eventually choke it. Without collapse, no sealed chamber would open. Warmth would cease to reach the terraces. Bacterial dust would fail. Old conduits would fill. The ruin’s destruction was also its circulation.

It considered softening the currents.

But the slivers required them. The bells fed by them. The violet drifters had become immense in their outflows. Calm water would be a desert.

It considered making refuges that no storm could enter.

But fixed safety would gather prey, and prey would gather predators, and soon the refuge would be a trap—or an empire of one species over all the rest.

At last the power made one change.

It did not prevent anything from breaking.

It gave breakage a warning.

Deep inside the pale slabs, along those perfect planes where failure had always waited, it placed a minute capacity for hesitation. Under mortal strain, the material would no longer pass directly from endurance to division. First, its close grain would shift against itself. Not enough to weaken it sooner. Not enough to alter its ancient shape. Only enough to send a low vibration through the fitted courses.

The signal was not loud. Loudness would have been violence.

It was a pressure-pattern: three long compressions, a stillness, and then a fine ascending shiver conducted through every joined surface. In small blocks it lasted only a few breaths. In buttresses and buried vaults it might continue for hours. Before the greatest failures, it could travel across districts.

The power shaped no command into it.

It meant neither *flee* nor *take shelter*. Those were thoughts belonging to creatures with other kinds of minds. It was simply made distinct from current, feeding flow, settling grit, distant impact, and all the ordinary unrest of the plateau.

It meant only:

*This surface will not remain.*

Then the power waited.

The first warning came beneath an empty arcade near the eastern rim.

A seam mouth had been closed there for ninety years. Bacterial dust had failed long before, and pale residue had accumulated along the edges of its plates. Several thread scavengers sheltered beneath it. Above, in sockets worn into the arcade, a young bracket had anchored six of its outer limbs.

Strain gathered in the overhead courses.

The new hesitation began.

Three compressions entered the seam mouth’s armored underside. There was a pause. Then came the ascending shiver.

The animal did not understand. Nothing had ever required it to respond to such a thing. It remained sealed.

The bracket stirred. Two limbs opened, then folded again.

The arch divided.

Half fell outward into the dark. The bracket went with it, still gripping the block. The seam mouth was buried beneath grit but survived. No miracle announced itself. No lesson was learned.

The power did not withdraw the gift because it had failed once.

Elsewhere, an old mantle rider felt a warning pass through a parapet on which it had settled. The vibration entered the fluid chambers along its wings. It lifted, not from foresight but irritation, and rode the upward current away.

The parapet broke moments later.

The rider lived.

That response was repeated. Not often at first. A bell contracted before a section of wall fell through its colony. A reverse crab left a narrowing crevice. Slivers turned edgewise at the trembling of a buried avenue and were already suspended in the water when the constructed ground beneath them opened.

Many still died.

Some moved toward danger instead of away from it. Brackets unfolded when they felt the warning, mistaking the vibrations for prey striking a distant surface. Seam mouths woke too slowly. Violet drifters were too vast to escape the collapse of entire buttresses.

The power did not reach in to save each one.

To remove every consequence would have made action meaningless. To favor only the gentle would have starved predators. To preserve every offspring would have filled the plateau until all life consumed itself. Benevolence could not mean that nothing ever died. Wisdom could not mean arranging the world so that every event had a harmless conclusion.

The gift remained what it was: not rescue, but notice.

Life began to receive it.

Among the slivers, those whose lateral organs were most sensitive to the ascending shiver turned sooner than the others. Shoals carrying that sensitivity survived collapsing districts in greater numbers. Generations later, a warning passed beneath them and the whole procession lifted from the avenue like one transparent sheet.

Mourning bells acquired a second contraction.

The first remained their old response to pressure change: immediate, simultaneous closure. The new response moved strangely through a colony. Bells nearest the vibrating surface released their black filaments. Those farther away remained tethered. When the wall failed, part of the colony was carried clear as drifting beads while part endured in place. What had once been total loss became division.

Seam mouths changed most slowly.

Their lives were long, and the dissolution of their juvenile nervous systems left little capacity for new behavior. Yet even among them, change came. Adults began retaining a narrow ring of sensory tissue around the rasp. When the warning entered a joint, they withdrew their feeding teeth and loosened the hundreds of gripping feet beneath them. A current that ordinarily could not move them then rolled them aside.

Often it rolled them into hunger or the mouth of a predator.

Often it saved them from being sealed beneath a slab for the remainder of their lives.

The brackets learned the warning too. They unlocked from doomed conduits and crawled with their grave motions toward stable walls. They were not excluded from mercy because they killed. Hunger was not malice. The power did not confuse innocence with harmlessness.

The violet drifters learned on the scale of centuries.

A warning passing through the outer buttresses entered the water as a sustained, almost inaudible pressure. The drifters’ symbionts flashed in response. One animal glowed; then another farther down the escarpment; then another, until a chain of violet pulses descended into the black.

The animals folded their canopies and launched away.

Behind them, a buttress broader than a city separated from the plateau.

It fell without striking a single drifter.

For a long time it remained visible by their scattered light: a pale vertical continent sinking between violet stars. Then the last glow withdrew, and the block passed into the deep.

Its departure opened hundreds of chambers.

Warm water rushed outward. Eyeless crustaceans tumbled through the breaches. Bacterial floc spread along the rim in dense clouds. Sliver shoals gathered. Bells seeded new surfaces. Mantle riders came from beyond the visible plateau. Brackets chose the mouths of freshly uncovered channels.

Death had opened life, as it always had.

But this time the living had not been required to stand beneath the falling wall.

The power made one other adjustment, so deep that no animal ever sensed it.

At the lowest courses it could reach, where the repeating masonry descended toward pressures that crushed all bodies, the power saw stresses accumulating across the entire highland. The plateau would not endure forever. Nothing should. But its eventual destruction was tending toward a single catastrophic failure: a collapse in which the Basin of Steps, the rim, the terraces, and all the inhabited upper levels would descend together.

Such an end was not required by age. It was only an accident of how the hidden courses bore their weight.

The power redistributed nothing by force. It strengthened no slab. Instead, it extended the same mercy of hesitation into the deepest planes and arranged that their warnings would release one after another rather than all at once.

The plateau would still end.

But it would end by regions.

One district would descend and another remain. One escarpment would open while another settled. Currents would change, migrations would fail, species would vanish, and new forms would arise in the chambers exposed. There would be grief, though no creature named it. There would be terror, though no history preserved it. There would be continuance.

No apocalypse would arrive without an interval in which life could move.

Having done this, the power did not remain as ruler or guardian. It placed no eye over the Basin. It demanded no recognition. It left no indestructible mark declaring that mercy had passed there.

Perhaps it departed.

Perhaps attention, once given so completely, does not depart in the way bodies do.

Ages later, a storm reached down from the upper sea.

The twilight vanished. Pressure thickened. Currents reversed through the empty avenues. Animals wedged themselves into seams, folded their wings, sealed their plates. Far beneath the Basin of Steps, an ancient course took on the weight of a shifting region.

For all the history of the plateau, it would have held silently until the instant of its failure.

Now it hesitated.

Three long compressions traveled through the artificial ground.

Stillness followed.

Then the ascending shiver spread outward through terraces, shafts, ribs, blank walls, and worn circles. Mourning bells released by the thousand. Seam mouths loosened their grip. Brackets unfolded and crawled. Reverse crabs poured from fissures. Mantle riders lifted into the turbulent water. Across the broad plazas, the sliver shoals rose.

At the rim, one violet drifter flashed.

Another answered.

Then another.

The storm struck.

Ancient blocks lifted. Grit darkened the highland. The Basin boiled, and a buried avenue folded into the levels beneath it. Towers came apart. New hollows opened. Old passages vanished.

Creatures died.

More lived.

When the water cleared, the architecture had changed. A pale line never before exposed stretched toward the horizon. Warmth entered a chamber that had been cold for millions of years. The survivors redistributed themselves, feeding, hiding, fastening, drifting, and returning.

Nothing knew it had been spared.

Nothing offered thanks.

The power had wanted neither.

Across the drowned plateau, everything still moved. Nothing was restored. Nothing was made eternal. The old construction continued its slow passage into ruin, and life continued borrowing what remained.

But the world no longer changed entirely without warning.

For the first time, between what was and what would be, there was a little room.

And into that room, life could choose to move.
