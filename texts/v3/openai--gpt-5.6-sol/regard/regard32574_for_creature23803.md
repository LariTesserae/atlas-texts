The power does not descend.

Descent would already be an error in a place where downward can be revised by a bell, and where the sky sometimes occupies the water. Nor does it announce itself with light. Light would become charge, heat, reflection, food. Herds would graze its edges. Drifters would draw it into their sacs. Within minutes revelation would be reduced to a chemical uniformity and the flats would lie still behind it.

The power is benevolent, and therefore does not mistake strangeness for misery.

It is wise, and therefore does not assume that teeth should be blunted, predators emptied, or the dead made separate from the living. It does not recoil from the crack-hunter’s unfolding or from the round mouths beneath the stone. These things are terrible only in part. In another part they are courtship, metabolism, weather, and the distribution of form.

To abolish them would not rescue the basin.

It would kill it politely.

So the power attends.

Its attention enters first as a difference too small to eat.

A grazer encounters it between two adjacent drops of water. One is no warmer, fresher, heavier, younger, or more charged than the other, yet they are not the same. The rotating mouth pauses over them. Its chambers cycle. Its legs lock and release. Unable to consume the distinction, the animal passes on.

The difference remains.

Ring-young gather around it. They chir, scatter, reassemble, and exchange every part that can be exchanged. Still the distinction persists. A bladdered drifter lowers a conductive thread and draws no current. A pale sealing disk flattens itself over the place, but nothing is sealed.

For the first time in the basin, there is a difference that performs no work.

The power waits inside it.

Waiting there is not measured in years. Years belong to climates, orbits, growth rings—none of which are reliable in the basin. The power waits through nine upward rains, through the passage of a reflected migration belonging to an extinct atmosphere, through the arrival of a wound whose predator will not be born until the western flats have folded twice.

It watches the inhabitants make use of everything except this.

Then, because watching from outside is insufficient, it allows itself to be taken apart.

A ring incorporates part of its attention and rolls away.

A grazer drinks another portion with brine.

A crack-hunter separates that grazer along its natural joints and fits the portion into a cavity beside its central clamp. Centuries later, a black composite offspring carries it into a fissure. The fissure closes. Pressure draws it downward into the ancient compressed bodies beneath the stone.

There the power learns the labor of the dead.

It feels extinct limbs contracting by fractions whenever thunder crosses the oldest strata. It feels sound handed from shell to shell through bodies that have not ceased being useful in a million ages. It feels memories precipitating into reflections, reflections acquiring mineral rims, the vanished dragged gradually back into weight.

It passes upward through a ridge vent as steam.

A red valve-creature closes around it.

The power becomes pressure resisted by a living body. It learns the satisfaction of perfect function and the pain that is indistinguishable from that satisfaction. It learns that the valve does not think, *I am trapped*, because trap and nourishment are the same warmth. Yet when the pulse ceases, the creature remains clenched. It has no motion by which to release itself. Its anatomy contains closure but not opening.

The power does not open it.

Not yet.

It enters the central waters. It becomes vibration under a tripod’s bell. It is drunk through the minute valves along the legs and circulated through nested mouths, each opening into the next. There it discovers that the tripods do not command the basin’s laws.

They endure them first.

Before gravity inclines for everything else, it inclines inside the tripod. Before shadows multiply, the tripod bears the surplus darkness within its bell. Before rain stops in the air, every drop inside its tissues becomes motionless. The call is the sound of the condition escaping into the world.

The tripods are not lawmakers.

They are where laws hurt while being born.

The power remains through one contraction. Then another. It learns the basin at the speed of its deepest note.

Only after this does it understand what mercy could mean here.

The basin does not suffer because bodies are shared. Shared bodies are among its joys.

It does not suffer because death becomes geology. The dead do not universally wish to be elsewhere.

It does not suffer merely because creatures consume one another. Many hunger to be incorporated. Some find continuance inside the predator more complete than any solitary life.

The suffering lies deeper.

Nothing is allowed to be useless.

No body may close without becoming a wall. No body may open without becoming a channel. No difference may remain ungrazed. No death may go unbuilt upon. No track may fade without rising again. No extinct thing may finish being absent. Even error is recruited into motion. Even wounds that never happen must hang in the air and collect rain.

The basin has abundance beyond measure, but no remainder.

It has change, but no release.

It has uncountable forms, but no refusal.

The benevolent power does not give the creatures peace. Peace imposed on this place would resemble paralysis.

It gives them an interval.

The next time the atmospheric pressure falls, every creature turns east.

The rotation begins beneath the crowded central sheet and moves outward: grazers bracing, chain-bodies tightening, rings pouring through gaps, drifters collapsing their western sacs. Water rises in curtains. Shell grinds against shell. The old immense movement enters the legs of a tripod.

Its bell contracts.

The deep note passes into teeth, eyes, pools, compressed carcasses, ridges, and wounds waiting in the air.

The inhabitants brace for alteration.

Perhaps water will climb stone.

Perhaps reflections will harden.

Perhaps every animal will acquire the memory of having drowned.

But after the call, no law changes.

For the duration of one hydraulic pulse, no condition requires an answer.

The pressure still falls. The east remains east. Appetite remains appetite. The warm lies beside the cold, charge beside charge, prey beside the joint that will open it.

Yet nothing is compelled to proceed.

Across the basin, function hesitates.

The grazer nearest the tripod lifts three of its forward limbs. It has never held them empty. Every extension has always anchored, scraped, filled, compressed, or released. Now the limbs remain suspended, brine trembling inside them.

A ring-young begins to disassemble from alarm. Half its loops leap away. The others remain. For the first time, scattering is incomplete without becoming injury. The divided animal experiences two wishes and is not destroyed by the contradiction.

In the western flats, a crack-hunter hears the thunder of an approaching herd through the oldest stone. Its first limb unlocks with a rifle report.

The second does not.

The prey arrive. They pass over and around the folded seam. The hunter feels every latent separation in their bodies. It is hungry. It remains hungry. Nothing rewards it.

Nothing punishes it.

Inside a ridge, a red valve-creature unclenches.

Steam tears past its soft sides. The land shudders. For a moment the vent stands open and the pressure that should have ruined a hundred chambers pours uselessly into the rain.

The power receives it.

Not as sacrifice. Not as debt.

Simply so the ridge need not be destroyed by one small creature’s release.

Then the interval ends.

The suspended limbs strike the water. The separated rings flee. The hunter unfolds too late and clamps only rain. The red organism, startled by its own opening, crawls from the vent. Another valve-creature approaches the warmth, considers the empty place, and enters.

The basin resumes.

But it does not resume unchanged.

Wisdom knows that a single choice unsupported by the world is only another form of cruelty. To tell a valve it may leave while allowing the ridge to rupture behind it would make freedom indistinguishable from catastrophe. To let a grazer cease feeding while hunger destroys its pressure chambers would merely create a new mechanism for death.

So the power places no commandment in them.

It alters the cost.

Not everywhere. Not always. Only enough.

Around any creature that refuses its function, there sometimes appears a narrow margin in which consequence loosens. Pressure may wait without increasing. A gradient may remain ungrazed without poisoning the water. An unoccupied cavity may stay empty instead of filling with offspring, sediment, memory, or another body’s need.

These margins cannot be stored.

They cannot be herded, plated, inherited, or made into organs. The round mouths cannot swallow them. The ridges cannot refine them. When pursued as resources, they vanish.

They occur only around relinquishment.

A hunter leaves prey intact, and for a while its hunger does not sharpen.

A ring returns a borrowed sensory cluster, and does not become less complete.

A drifter lowers captured creatures back to the flats, and the charge they produced remains in its sacs just long enough for it to survive the next pressure bank.

A dead grazer lies unfolded in the water. Scavengers approach. Mineral rain begins to plate its joints. Then, for reasons none can detect, the small animals turn away. The rain falls without adhering. The body is neither consumed nor made into floor.

It remains only a body.

Ages pass over it.

At last it dissolves.

Nothing rises from the place.

This emptiness troubles the basin more than any new law.

Tracks migrate toward it and stop. Reflected herds curve around it. Tripods gather at a distance, their vapor pads flattening the rain. One sounds, then another. The notes enter the oldest stone and return from vanished places.

The empty place does not answer.

It is not waiting to become useful.

Gradually, water gathers there—clear not in color but in intention. It contains minerals, charge, incompatible temperatures, traces of ancient shell, and rain from several directions. Grazers can feed along its margins, but cannot exhaust it. Every distinction consumed returns differently, not restored but renewed. No cycle closes exactly.

For the first time, the basin produces something other than itself.

Possibility.

The power does not remain as ruler. It builds no sanctuary with fixed borders. A sanctuary would become habitat, then organ, then appetite. It founds no species to administer mercy. Such creatures would learn to prey upon guilt.

Instead it withdraws from every part that can do without it.

What remains is not its presence but a small capacity in the basin’s laws: the capacity for a process to pause without immediately becoming another process.

Most creatures never use it.

The herds continue to graze distinctions. Crack-hunters still open prey along their seams. Round mouths receive shelves of stone. Drifters trail nerves over metallic veins. The dead unfold and become terrain. Extinct migrations continue beneath water too shallow to contain them.

The basin is not softened.

It is not redeemed into resemblance with another world.

But now, occasionally, a predator allows an anticipated noise to pass without striking the place where prey will be. The prey arrive unwounded and stand bewildered in the unused future.

Occasionally, a raised track lies down.

Occasionally, an extinct creature reaches the edge of a reflection and does not precipitate back into matter. It turns away into whatever direction absence has.

Occasionally, when pressure falls, one animal faces neither east nor west.

And in the central waters, after an age that cannot be counted, a ring-young assembles from needles, loops, warm beads, two pieces of another animal, and a mineral hoop that was never alive.

It stands among the packed multitudes.

Its surfaces are bright.

There are no scratches on them.

The inhabitants draw close, chirring, venting, grinding, testing the oldest stone for an echo of the journeys the new being must already have taken.

No echo returns.

The young creature wobbles once.

Not from a recurring error. Not in fulfillment of any remembered motion.

It wobbles because it has not learned how to stand.

Then the tripods contract.

The basin waits for the law that will follow.

Far beneath the flats, the immense hidden being turns. Water considers several directions. Rain hangs as silver grains between ground and sky.

And for one immeasurable interval, nothing is required to happen next.
