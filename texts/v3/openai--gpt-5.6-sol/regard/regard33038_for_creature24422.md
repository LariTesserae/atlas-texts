The power does not bring water.

This is its first act of wisdom.

It does not clear the sky, calm the winds, soften the mineral sheets, or teach the needle-cats pity. It does not make the Answerers upright, smooth their folded skin, or replace their amber circulation with blood. Such mercies would be extinctions wearing gentle faces.

Instead, it attends.

For ninety-nine stillnesses it remains beyond detection. It learns which clap belongs to living vanes and which to the dead. It learns the interval between the subterranean knock and the first answering bulb. It follows names through heel, palate, sternum, stone. It listens until it understands that no true statement in the Rattlecourt is made only once.

It listens below as well.

Far beneath the basin, under the warm plates and seams of cold glass, something is caught.

It is not a god beneath a mountain. It does not sleep. It has no mouth with which to ask who remains. It is a life so slow that the fan-trees seem to it like sparks. Its tissues are pressure, crystal, heat, and faults lined with conductive ore. The mesa is not its body entire, but one sensitive extremity of it, grown upward through the crust over ages.

The immense knock is a spasm.

A plate within that buried life expands against a collar of glass and cannot pass. Strain accumulates. At last it slips. The blow travels upward.

The Rattlecourt’s answer returns through the stone as a thousand delicate impacts. These do not keep the being asleep.

They ease its pain.

Not greatly. Not for long. But enough that, over generations, the buried life has grown toward the basin. Enough that the roots of fan-trees follow channels warmed by its relief. Enough that the scale-lichens hinge scarlet when tension gathers below. Enough that every living thing in the hollow has become, unknowingly, part of an organ of comfort.

The power could break the collar of glass.

It does not.

Too much depends upon the knock. Seeds wake to it. Mites change direction. Brood cases open after particular sequences. The Answerers have built law, memory, vigilance, and fellowship around the shared instant. To cure the buried being by silencing the Rattlecourt would amputate one life from another.

So the power searches for a smaller kindness.

On the hundredth stillness, after the knock rises and the whole basin answers, there comes a second vibration.

It is very faint.

It passes not through the air but through every object touching the ground. It travels up four-toed feet, through the walls of shelters, along the central ribs, into spindle roots and needle-cat mouth plates. It is too measured to be an echo and too gentle to be a fracture.

One long pulse.

Two close together.

Then a soft downward brushing tremor, like six fingers drawn along a sternum.

The Answerers remain still long after the wind returns.

No one claims authority. No one has heard it first. Even the youngest child, palms spread against stone, received it at the same instant.

They gather at the central instrument.

There are disputes. Some believe the buried one has learned their name. Some believe a mouth left behind has entered the rock. One brittle elder says the new vibration is merely an old sound returning from a distance too great to imagine. A hunter cuts the tendon representing this claim before the wind can judge it.

At last a child presses both hands to a cold seam and feels the faintest repetition below.

Not a demand.

A question.

The child tightens a strand of shed cilia between two stone ribs. Others add tendon, vane-fiber, copper, resonant shell, and one of the black pierced objects left centuries earlier by a walking whirlwind. They work through three heat pulses. When they finish, the instrument has a note it did not possess before: too low for the air to carry, but clear through the feet.

They sound it once.

The wise power receives the answer.

Permission, though not surrender.

Help, though not transformation.

Come nearer, though not unseen.

Then the power enters the Rattlecourt in the only form the Rattlecourt can trust: as something with a body.

During the next gale, one of the crown-wheels descends.

It is smaller than most, no wider than an Answerer’s outspread arms. Its vanes are colorless, its ring pale rather than black. It circles no spindle-bush. It lowers itself around the central stone ribs and turns there without tearing a single thread.

The Answerers kneel along its path, as they do for walking figures.

They knock their names.

This time, the figure answers.

Not perfectly. Its heel-strike is too deep. Its palate clicks emerge as taps of vane against cartilage. It has no sternum, so the final gesture passes through the central threads as a descending shimmer.

Yet it answers each name differently.

Only then do the Answerers approach.

The pale wheel contains no commandments. It grants no immunity from hunger, injury, error, or grief. It carries one piece of knowledge: a pattern that can be played through the cold glass seams when tension gathers beneath the mesa.

The pattern is not language exactly. It is leverage made from rhythm.

Played correctly, it causes microscopic fractures in the glass collar far below. Not enough to shatter it, not enough to silence the knock, but enough to let the buried plate slip with less violence. The great spasm becomes a deliberate movement. Pain becomes signal.

The pattern is difficult. No single Answerer can perform it. One must strike the western seam while another dampens the warm plates. Children cling beneath the overhang and tap the roof with pigment-stained hands. Elders brace resonant bones against the shelter walls. Hunters stand in the open and turn copper strips toward the charge flashes. Even the dead instruments must be placed among the fan-trees so their altered voices join the work.

The first attempt fails.

The second sends a mineral blade sailing through a shelter roof.

On the third, a needle-cat erupts beneath the central ribs and nearly destroys the instrument.

The power does not correct them. It repairs nothing they can repair themselves. It remains in the branches, turning slowly, present but not sovereign.

At the next stillness, they try again.

The knock begins below.

Before it can break upward in one immense blow, the Answerers play the new pattern.

The basin trembles.

The buried pressure shifts in stages: three deep pulses, a pause, then one vast movement without violence. Fan-trees clap in sequence rather than all at once. Resin bulbs answer from east to west. The bellows release a rolling note that passes around the cliffs and returns unchanged.

For the first time, the Rattlecourt does not merely answer.

It converses.

Something below presses gently against the cold seams.

The child at the western wall feels it first: a slow contact, withdrawn and offered again.

The buried being has discovered that the lives above are not weather.

The Answerers have discovered that what they took for a question was pain, and that answering pain can become a greeting if done long enough.

Afterward, the pale crown-wheel rises.

They ask it to remain. They ask through stone, so the request is true.

The power declines, also through stone.

A wise kindness cannot become the central pillar of a place that has survived by many supports. Its pattern belongs to them now. They will alter it, argue over it, misuse it, forget portions, recover others, and add the hunting rhythm of needle-cats, the alarm notes of bellows, the hesitation-taps of children. In time it will cease to be a gift and become tradition.

Before departing, the power performs one final act.

It changes nothing living.

Instead, high above the mesa, it touches the permanent belt of circling grit. From the dust it gathers the minute golden traces lost by every cut tree, opened brood case, wounded animal, and Answerer becoming thin. These traces have accumulated for ages, too scattered to leap toward charge, too fine to fall.

The power does not return them directly. That would draw the dead backward into shapes they have already left.

It binds them with mineral salt and lays them, grain by grain, in a narrow vein beneath the western overhang.

There the substance grows slowly into amber crystals.

The crystals cannot restore the dead. They cannot cure every thinning. But warmed between geothermal stones and ground into paste, they can give a failing body time: an extra interval, perhaps two, in which to return from the resin cloth—or to strike a name clearly into stone before becoming something else.

The vein will not last forever.

Nothing in the Rattlecourt does.

When the pale wheel finally departs, the Answerers do not build an image of it. They add one colorless vane to the central instrument. For a while they address it by the power’s name.

Later, when its sound changes beyond recognition, they allow it to become something else.

The winds continue.

Kitebacks fasten onto the unwary. Needle-cats hunt under the loudest storms. Children cross the underside of the overhang and leave long trails of fearful hands. The shelters need patching. Arguments snap their threads. Some who enter the warm alcoves do not return.

But when the winds cancel, tools are pressed to stone.

The knock comes more gently now.

The Rattlecourt answers—not because it fears what will happen if it fails, and not because it knows at last what everything means.

It answers because something beneath it can feel the answer.

And far away, the benevolent power listens once more, hears that the place remains wholly itself, and does nothing further.
