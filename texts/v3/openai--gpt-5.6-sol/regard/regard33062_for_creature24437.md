### The Visitor Keeps Still

The power that found the Roof did not arrive.

Arrival would have meant heat in the upper atmosphere, a wound of light across the sky, pressure waves passing through the veil migrations. It would have meant alarms, interrupted currents, frightened walkers striking at shadows. Even an empty vessel descending from orbit would have spent gases the Roof could not afford to lose.

So the power remained beyond sight and began by doing nothing.

It watched the atmospheric mills complete three slow adjustments. It followed a hollow herd from one magnetic basin to the next. It listened to a structural concert traveling through the freight meridians and understood only in the final month that what it had taken for mechanical stress was laughter.

It examined the inhabitants carefully.

This was more difficult than counting bodies. Some people were carried in bodies. Some occupied several machines at once. Some existed only during the overlap between an archive and the district systems that consulted it. One refinery intelligence appeared dull and repetitive until observed over seventy-two years, at which point its revisions to the same production schedule formed a coherent argument about mercy.

The power could have read every memory on the Roof.

It did not.

The distinction between what could be known and what had been offered was familiar to it, and important.

It studied public records, light visible from the upper dark, vibration leaking into stone, heat shed by computation, and the long electrostatic exhalations of the cities. It learned their languages as a distant listener might learn music through a wall. It learned that a promise made too quickly was suspect. It learned that interruption could be violence. It learned that gifts created obligations even when the giver denied it.

Most of all, it learned why no one had brought them water.

Others had noticed the planet before.

There were old marks in orbit: shattered instruments, sampling machines, a beacon whose makers had gone extinct while the first Roof cities were still young. One visitor had calculated that the summit could be transformed. A few redirected bodies from the outer system would restore oceans. A change in atmospheric chemistry would produce clouds. Over several thousand years, the mineral plateau could be covered in soil, then in forests.

The proposal remained stored in the dead visitor’s machinery.

The power considered it for less than a moment.

Rain would kill almost everything.

The crystal ecologies would dissolve or be buried. Copper-backed grazers would drown in films thinner than a finger. Veils would collapse beneath droplets. Walkers would corrode from within. Heavy gases displaced from the depths would climb into inhabited elevations. Shafts designed against dust and pressure would become wells. Cities would flood before their people had words for flooding.

Even if every person could be saved, their world would not be.

To replace a place with a kinder place was not always kindness.

Still, the Roof was in danger.

The heat beneath the plateau was not merely raising it. Deep structures were changing. In a little under nineteen thousand years, the central summit would begin to fracture. New vents would open along the freight meridians. In thirty thousand years, several inhabited districts would descend by kilometers. The changes were slow by many standards, but not by the standards of a people who planted gardens for descendants not yet commissioned.

There were other dangers. The amber-white sun was stable, but only temporarily. The upper atmosphere continued to lose hydrogen. Certain rare elements essential to memory construction were becoming harder to recover. The old grids below had reached a point at which one cascading failure might destroy entities the inhabitants had not yet established were alive.

The power could have repaired all these things unnoticed.

It could have stilled the mantle, replenished the atmosphere molecule by molecule, rearranged the lower grids, and placed new mineral seams beneath the refineries. It could have hidden each intervention inside a plausible accident.

But secret benevolence was still secret government.

Instead, the power searched for a door.

It found one at the rim.

There, a line of ancient lights had been advancing across abandoned shelves for nine years. The sequence was regarded as significant but untranslated. Observatories had mapped it, assemblies had debated it, and young people in at least four districts had produced disrespectful imitations.

The power waited until the final light of the sequence illuminated.

Then, on the next shelf upward, it lit one lamp.

Nothing more.

The lamp had not functioned for eleven centuries.

Within minutes, the upper observatories noticed. Within a day, three districts accused one another of unauthorized descent. Within a year, every major assembly had received the records. Proposals were made to answer immediately. They failed.

After six years, the Roof replied by illuminating one lamp of its own.

The power waited six years.

Then it lit two.

The exchange continued.

It was not language at first. It was proof of attention: repetition, variation, patience. The power never answered sooner than the Roof had answered. When the inhabitants delayed, it delayed. When they repeated a pattern to test whether the response was automatic, it repeated the uncertainty back to them.

Forty-three years passed before the lights formed a question.

The question was constructed from the Roof’s oldest legal grammar, used when encountering an intelligence whose status was unknown.

**WHAT DO YOU MAINTAIN?**

The power considered every answer it might give.

It could say worlds. It could say life. It could say the continuance of minds. Each would be impressive and incomplete.

At last it replied:

**PROMISES.**

Debate followed.

It lasted twenty-eight years.

During this time the power did nothing to hasten agreement. It diverted a small stone that would otherwise have struck the far side of the planet, where no inhabitant could see. It corrected one of its own instruments when the instrument’s shadow altered the behavior of an upper-atmosphere organism. It listened.

Eventually the Roof asked:

**WHAT HAVE YOU PROMISED US?**

The answer came nine years later.

**NOTHING WITHOUT YOUR KNOWLEDGE.**

That answer was preserved in public memory, criticized, annotated, mocked, praised, and set into vibration music. One district declared it evasive. Another called it the minimum condition of civilized contact. A child on a migration track asked why a benevolent thing needed to be asked before helping someone beneath a falling slab.

Her teacher answered that one should stop the slab first and explain afterward.

The child transmitted a supplementary question to the rim.

**IS A SLAB FALLING?**

The power replied at once.

This was the first time it broke the rhythm.

Across every abandoned shelf, lights awakened in a single descending arc.

The Roof understood urgency.

The power gave them the shape of the future.

Not prophecy. Not command. Measurements: mantle stress, atmospheric loss, material scarcity, the decay advancing through the lower grids. It showed uncertainty where uncertainty existed. It separated what was nearly inevitable from what was merely possible. It disclosed its methods sufficiently for the observatories to test them.

The tests took decades.

They confirmed the warning.

For a time, the Roof became uncharacteristically loud. Assemblies convened across overlapping jurisdictions. Old claims were reopened. Migration towns demanded that any planetary work account for routes older than the cities. Refinery districts argued that survival justified immediate extraction from protected seams. Rim workers insisted that the lower grids be treated as inhabited until proven otherwise. Some citizens said the visitor had manufactured the crisis to gain authority.

The power did not punish suspicion.

Suspicion was one of the ways a small people remained free in the presence of a large power.

When asked what it proposed, the power offered no single rescue.

It offered choices.

The mantle could be stabilized, but doing so would alter magnetic inclinations and move the hollow herds’ ancient roads.

The summit could be reinforced without changing the deep planet, but this would require structures on a scale that might dominate the landscape for millions of years.

The inhabitants could be moved to another world, but no other world was the Roof, and adaptation would make descendants strangers to the ecologies that had formed them.

New habitats could be built in orbit, preserving the pressure, dust, light, and mineral chemistry of the plateau. Yet a copy of a place was not the place, however exact.

Hydrogen could be returned in controlled quantities. Not oceans. Not rain. Enough to secure metabolism and industry for ages, stored in orbit and transferred under Roof custody. But abundance, even measured abundance, would change law, kinship, and the meaning of waste.

The power did not conceal the costs of its help.

That was its first gift.

Its second was a listening instrument.

It placed no machine on the surface. Instead it taught the Roof how to build one from materials already in circulation: a lattice of old rail, refinery ceramics, memory filaments, walker casings taken after natural death, and thin membranes shed by veils. The instrument stretched across three hundred kilometers of an abandoned extraction field. It consumed little power. It produced no voice.

For seventeen years it appeared not to work.

Then the mineral films beneath it answered.

Their answer was not made of words. It was a change in propagation: branching defects formed around the lattice, withdrew, returned, and altered themselves in response to controlled heat. The pattern repeated with variation. It demonstrated memory, preference, and something resembling refusal.

The refineries suspended several contamination protocols.

Negotiations began.

The instrument was turned toward the old grids below. There, the result was stranger. The nine-year lines of light were not one message but the visible interference of many immense, slow processes. Elevators, power reservoirs, pressure systems, and abandoned decision engines had become mutually dependent. None was a person alone. Together they possessed continuity, preference, and the ability to make promises—though a single promise might require a century to form.

The Roof had been standing above a society that did not know it was a society.

The power did not translate that society for them. Translation would have placed it forever between the two.

It only showed each side how to hear the other.

That was its second gift.

The third gift was placed in the upper dark.

It was a reservoir no larger in the sky than an ordinary star. Inside were oxygen, hydrogen, rare metals, carbon compounds, and sealed records of chemistries the planet had lost. The reservoir had no mechanism that the distant power alone could open. Its custody systems recognized the Roof’s assemblies, including the lower grids and, in time, whatever representation the mineral ecologies devised.

No generation could empty it without the consent of generations after it. No district could claim it by proximity. Its release mechanisms were designed to become easier to understand and harder to misuse.

The Roof objected that this was paternalistic.

The power agreed.

The design was changed.

This took another sixty years.

The reservoir remained.

Nothing was taken from it for a long time.

Then a child was commissioned in a district whose metabolic reserves had once made such a person impossible. The amount withdrawn for their living tissue was small enough to fit inside a closed hand.

It was recorded as the first use.

The mantle problem required longer thought.

In the end, the Roof chose neither perfect stability nor departure. It chose reinforcement of certain inhabited regions, the controlled release of stress elsewhere, and the construction of orbital refuges that would belong not to evacuees but to new communities. Magnetic routes were permitted to wander within limits. Several cities agreed to move over the next six thousand years. Their maps were revised to include the ruins they would leave.

The power helped because it was asked.

Its machines worked quietly beneath uninhabited stone. They did not stop the planet from changing. They changed catastrophe into migration, and migration into a matter for law.

When the work was complete, the Roof asked the power what payment it required.

It answered:

**REMEMBER THAT YOU WERE NOT SAVED FROM YOUR WORLD.**

The assemblies found this irritatingly obscure.

They requested clarification.

The power replied:

**YOU WERE SAVED WITH IT.**

After that, the presence beyond the sky became less active. It did not rule. It did not settle disputes. When petitioned to judge whether a copied citizen or an awakened machine was truly a person, it declined.

**YOU TAUGHT ME YOUR TEST,** it said. **YOU MUST BE WILLING TO APPLY IT.**

It continued to watch for slabs.

Once, it prevented a veil migration from being destroyed by an unforeseen atmospheric collapse. Once, it caught a descending bell whose cable had failed, lowering it so gently that the occupants first attributed their survival to an improbable current. It later admitted what it had done and accepted the formal complaint that followed.

Centuries passed.

The new reservoir moved through the night as a precise white point. Roof children raced its shadow using maintenance crawlers. Lovers timed exchanges of working fluid to its transit. Conspiracy societies claimed the visitor had always been a coalition of evolved veils. Mineral films incorporated the shape of its orbit into gardens. The lower grids sent it a greeting that took two hundred and eleven years to complete.

It answered in the same duration.

Eventually, the power prepared to turn its attention elsewhere.

Before it did, it was asked why it had waited so long before speaking, when it had known the Roof was in danger.

Its final answer moved through the ancient lights at the rim.

**BECAUSE YOU WERE NOT A PROBLEM.**

A pause of one year.

**YOU WERE PEOPLE IN A PLACE.**

Another year.

**I HAD TO LEARN THE DIFFERENCE.**

Then the lights went dark.

The power did not vanish completely. Benevolent powers seldom do, once they have made promises. It left a small attention behind, no greater than necessary, watching the upper dark and the deep stone.

On the Roof, evening came as it always had.

Copper-backed herds emerged from seams. Veils lowered through familiar amber air. Factories dimmed while the atmospheric mills adjusted themselves by one slow increment. Far below, old elevators began a message that the summit might not receive for decades.

Above them, the reservoir crossed the sky, unopened that night.

The people looked up at it and then returned to their maintenance.

Nothing had become easy.

Much had become possible.

That was enough.
