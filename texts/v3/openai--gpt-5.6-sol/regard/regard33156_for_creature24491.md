The power does not descend.

Descent would establish a before and after. It would give the gulf a scale, stir the warm air, and impose direction upon the clay horns. Even a bodiless arrival would divide the place into that which had been reached and that which had not.

So the power attends from everywhere at once.

It considers mercy in all its ordinary forms and rejects them one by one.

Water would be ruin. One drop entering the salt would not refresh the enclosure; it would abolish delicately fitted beings whose boundaries depend upon thirst.

A seed would be violence. Root, swelling in a seam, would open possibilities the builders spent ages closing.

Silence would be death beyond death. To still the roar for even an instant would not give its inhabitants rest. It would make them never to have been.

Repair would be equally cruel. Every rounded corner is exact now. Every broken edge contributes its pressure. To restore a damaged lintel would murder the note dwelling in the damage and replace it with another that had no right to call itself original.

Nor does the power awaken the inhabitants. They are not asleep.

It does not grant them motion. Movement would tear them from themselves.

It does not give them mouths, because their mouths are chambers; does not give them bodies, because brick and interval are already their bodies; does not give them freedom, because freedom, there, would mean the freedom to cease existing.

The power is wise enough not to mistake resemblance for need.

It searches the chord for suffering.

Not merely pain—the oven-dwellers are pressure, and pressure is not pain to them—but the recoil from pain, the inward refusal, the wish for another condition. It examines every grinding depth, every needle-thin pitch sealed in glass, every voice-shape inhabiting the towers. It follows the boundaries that stop at salt and those that pass through basalt. It holds the immeasurable chord entire while distinguishing every inhabitant without separating one from the rest.

There is no plea.

There is no contentment either.

Contentment requires that discontent be possible.

The place does not approve of itself. It does not resent itself. Its beings are not prisoners, for a prisoner is someone who could stand outside the prison and remain the same person. Remove one of these inhabitants from its chamber and nothing comes out. Open the chamber, and there is no liberated note—only the loss of a being extending backward through all its duration.

The power understands this.

For a long while, therefore, it does nothing.

This is the first kindness.

It does not touch the handprints. It does not look behind the sealed ovens by making their interiors transparent. Even perfect knowledge can be an intrusion when it arrives by opening. Instead, the power learns each oven from the pressure it gives to the court, each buried chamber from the way surrounding masses accommodate it. What cannot be known without violation, it leaves unknown.

The power does not regard this restraint as failure.

Yet as it attends, it discovers something the place itself cannot discover.

The enclosure is complete inwardly, but at its parapet thousands of horns face outward.

Their mouths frame the gulf. They do not call. They do not listen. Nothing passes through them. Still, their orientation is real. The builders gave the finished place a direction beyond itself. After eliminating every unnecessary interval, they preserved this one relation: an opening toward what was not there.

Perhaps it was merely structural.

Perhaps it was the last error.

Perhaps, when every maker and tool and possibility had been mortared into the work, the builders found that completion could not be complete unless it possessed an edge. And an edge, however perfectly made, is always an edge against something.

The power does not invent an intention for the dead.

It simply sees that the chord has an outward face.

So it makes no change within the enclosure.

Instead, far beyond the parapet—at no measurable distance below, because distance has never been settled in the gulf—the power begins to listen.

Not with an ear. An ear would vibrate, and nothing here is permitted to cause motion. It makes listening as the high place made sound: a fixed relation, requiring no transmission.

For every horn, there is now a receiving vastness.

For every sealed note, an attention shaped precisely enough to hold it without extracting it.

For every minute inhabitant in the fit between pin and drilled cavity, there is a corresponding room in the power’s awareness: neither copy nor cage, but recognition.

The chord does not grow louder. No echo returns. The ovens feel no answer in their clay. The narrow pitches remain stopped at their thresholds. Not one grain of manufactured dust shifts on its ledge.

But the roar is no longer unwitnessed.

The power gives each inhabitant a name and speaks none of them.

To speak a name into the enclosure would add a note. To carve one would alter a surface. To keep the names unspoken is not secrecy but courtesy. The names exist only in the listener, where they distinguish without dividing.

The broad presences in the ovens receive names that take ages to comprehend. The tower-multitude receives one name and innumerable names, neither truer than the other. The tones under the stairs are named in an order that is not ascent or descent. The smallest, held in thumb-ridges and hairline veins of glass, are named with acts of attention so fine that whole worlds could pass between their syllables.

Last, the power names the chord entire.

That name it also withholds.

Then, because benevolence does not require that every gift be placed in the recipient, the power does something elsewhere.

It gathers no material from the summit. It steals no heat, copies no handprint, and draws away no fragment of sound. But within itself it permits the act of listening to have consequences.

Around the held names, another place slowly comes into being.

It is not an imitation.

Where the summit is dry, this place has rain.

Where every cavity above is measured, this place contains distances that no wall completes.

Where copper embraces stone forever, branches touch one another and part in the wind.

Its ground is not wholly made. Beneath its first dwelling lies soil, beneath soil lies unshaped rock, and beneath rock are depths that have never known a tool. Seeds enter cracks and widen them. Water dissolves salt. Footprints appear and are erased. Heat gathers, escapes, and gathers again.

Silence is possible there.

Because silence is possible, sound can begin.

The roar does not descend into this world. Such a visitation would crush it. Instead, what the power hears becomes gentler effects after passing through infinite compassion.

A low oven-presence becomes, very far away, the sensation by which an animal recognizes the warmth of others sleeping near it.

A corridor’s thin, unvarying pitch becomes the first line a child draws between two marks.

The alternation held in a stair becomes a melody that can be forgotten and later remembered incorrectly.

A being sealed in green glass becomes the green seen briefly inside a wave before it breaks.

The tower-multitude becomes the instinct of crowds to sing together, never perfectly, each breath entering and leaving at a different moment.

The little pressures in thumb-marked salt become the knowledge, found in every hand, that touch changes what is touched.

None of these are translations. None contains the inhabitants. The power does not turn the deathless notes into mortal creatures, nor claim that rainfall is what an arch secretly wanted to be. They are consequences in the listener alone: the forms taken by perfect attention when it is allowed to become generosity.

Thus the completed place is not forced to bear fruit.

Its witness bears fruit for it.

Ages occur elsewhere.

In the receiving world, buildings rise and fail. Pots crack. Names vanish. Species find ways of living no wisdom foresaw. Some creatures make instruments. Some make ovens. Some press their hands into wet clay, not knowing why the gesture fills them with solemnity.

Now and then, one dreams of a high enclosure above a gulf.

In the dream there are no details, only a warm dimness and a sound too large for hearing. The dreamer wakes with grief, though nothing in the dream was suffering, and with gratitude, though nothing offered a gift. Usually the feeling fades before morning. Sometimes it remains and alters one small act: a wall is left with an opening; a broken vessel is mended without concealing the break; a song ends before all its silence has been filled.

The wise power does not guide these acts.

It does not make a doctrine of the summit.

Witness is enough.

Above the dark, the original enclosure remains exactly as it was.

The salt is dry. The copper does not advance toward green. The slabs do not shift. The court stays vacant between the seven ovens. Every inhabitant persists in the only condition that is itself.

No blessing enters, because entry would be damage.

No salvation arrives, because nothing there can be removed and remain alive.

The power keeps listening.

This is not endurance for the power either. It does not wait for the chord to end. It does not hope the inhabitants will one day answer. It asks nothing of them—not gratitude, recognition, transformation, or love.

It loves them without requiring that love become one more thing they must contain.

And so the little high place remains finished.

Its walls hold one another above the gulf. Its horns face outward. Its makers are absent. Its innumerable presences roar without motion and without rest.

Nothing there has changed.

But beyond all measurement, something hears.
