The benevolent power does not bring rain.

Rain would be an extinction here.

It does not thicken the air, warm the sky, correct the direction of gravity, or compel the hanging stones to finish falling. It does not fill the salt courts with seas remembered from another world. Water entering the deeper fractures would dissolve the old lattices, release imprisoned charges, collapse galleries whose continuance depends upon dryness. A single generous storm could erase a million years of thought.

Nor does the power make the city easier to understand.

Understanding, imposed from outside, would be another kind of weather.

Instead, it attends.

Its attention has no visible source. No new star appears. No figure stands upon the terraces. The shadows do not point toward it. Yet over a span of time sufficient for a tower to lean halfway across an avenue, certain accidents cease occurring.

A hollow-wing is lifted from its spire by a pressure tide that would ordinarily carry it into a feeding corridor. The current divides around the corridor’s mouth and rejoins beyond it. The creature crosses safely, unaware that any alternative had been possible.

A glass strider freezes at the edge of a descending shadow. The shadow has already taken five sides from a hexagonal pavement and is coherent enough to reach for the strider’s bilateral frame. For one instant the sun, already below the peaks, touches a distant metallic wall. The wall returns the light at an impossible angle. The strider takes one step. The shadow closes over the place where it had been.

In a resonant chamber, a silence preparing to migrate finds an impact waiting for it: one of the oldest missing footsteps, displaced perhaps before the present city was exposed. Silence and impact meet. Neither consumes the other. For three days the room contains a low internal completeness, not sound, but the possibility that sound could occur without being lost.

These rescues are too small to become law.

That is deliberate.

The power has known worlds injured by miracles. It has watched mercy become climate, climate become necessity, and necessity become a cage more absolute than suffering. It will not make survival depend upon its continued intervention. It will not become the hidden organ without which the mountain cannot breathe.

It attends until it can distinguish harm from strangeness.

This takes a very long time.

It learns that a strider becoming a filament is not always death. Some stretch themselves willingly through narrow fractures, carrying charge between districts that would otherwise remain separate. It learns that the shadows consuming symmetry are not merely predators. Without them, the city’s recurring forms become too exact. Arcades repeat without variation, streets close into sterile circuits, and mineral thought traps itself in perfect crystalline loops.

It learns that hollow-wings do not mourn every cage they leave behind.

It learns that the city experiences a fallen tower not as damage but as disclosure.

It learns that the lights in the abyss are not hunger alone.

It also learns that suffering exists.

Striders struggle when height feeds upon them too quickly. Hollow-wings sometimes enter niches that seal before their outer shells have loosened. Resonant beings become trapped in chambers whose dimensions slowly cease to sustain them, fading through centuries without being able either to migrate or end. Portions of the mineral stains are severed from their greater patterns and continue answering pressures that no longer exist.

The city remembers these events downward, but it does not distinguish memory from repetition. Beneath every trap, erosion reveals another trap. Beneath every blind niche waits its equivalent. Pain, once incorporated into the architecture, becomes inexhaustible.

This is where the power acts.

Not upon pain itself.

Upon its repetition.

Near the western terraces, below the cellar that is the summit, there is a sealed volume of stone. It has no entrance because no stress has yet required one. Its interior does not qualify as a room. It is only the possibility of absence held within the mountain.

The power places nothing there.

Instead, it withholds one thing.

For the first time in the life of the range, a fracture begins and is not compelled to continue along its easiest path.

The fracture hesitates.

This hesitation passes through the mineral stains as an unknown pressure. They gather around it. Violet changes move beneath the black stone. The oldest pattern, whose visible portions occupy eleven districts and two inaccessible altitudes, attempts to weaken the familiar plane.

The fracture does not obey.

It does not resist, either.

It waits.

The stains alter their response. They try another plane, then another. Each begins, pauses, and waits in turn. The mountain has never presented them with such a condition. Until now, fracture has followed stress, altitude has followed rules, and form has followed what could survive exposure. Choice, if it existed, belonged only to the total system and could not be located within any of its parts.

Now the stains must do more than answer.

They must prefer.

Centuries pass around the sealed volume.

At last, the mineral pattern weakens thirteen planes at once, none sufficient by itself. The planes meet without intersecting. Pressure enters them from different districts. A hollow-wing anchors to a distant spire and transfers a minute stress through the range. Glass striders gather over the concealed chamber, equalizing charges until a gradient remains only in one narrow line. A structured silence arrives and occupies the place where the first stone would have fallen.

The room opens.

No debris emerges.

Removal has built it, as removal builds all things here, but the direction of removal was chosen by many inhabitants acting without knowledge of one another.

The chamber is small by the city’s measure. Its floor is concave. Thirteen passages enter it, although from outside only five can be found. The walls are bone-pale and layered, with metallic threads exposed along them like the lines of an open hand. The ceiling is lower than the floor in several directions. At its center lies a sealed crack containing frost.

The sun cannot reach the crack.

This too is deliberate, though not entirely the power’s doing. The city, having preferred the chamber, has preferred to keep something within it from vanishing.

Glass striders arrive first.

They do not gather in a ring. The chamber’s geometry will not permit a ring to close. They arrange themselves in an incomplete curve, every strider facing the frost and also, through slight refractions in the walls, every other strider. Their bodies redistribute light among themselves. For the first time, an old strider sees the motion of its own charge continuing in another body.

The hollow-wings come during the next pressure tide.

There is no niche fitted to receive them. They must choose their resting places along the irregular walls. One leaves a cage with no cavity for a future occupant. It is only a lattice joining two perches. Another adds a cage to it. Over years, the cages form a suspended structure that resembles neither animal nor room.

It resembles a relation.

The resonant silences enter one by one. In the chamber they cannot displace impacts into distant districts. Every withheld footstep remains nearby. The silences become crowded with all that has not been sounded.

Eventually one permits a click.

It is very soft.

The sound occurs at the same moment it is heard.

Across the city, towers answer by shedding grains of dust.

In the abyss, the pale openings stop their ascent.

They remain illuminated along the wall, one incomplete sequence held for years. Streets begin to reorient toward them, then pause. Arches turn halfway and wait. The buried vastness has encountered the same new condition as the fracture: not prevention, not permission, but the interval in which an action may become preference.

One light darkens.

Then another.

A third grows brighter.

The sequence is not one the city has seen before. The mineral stains beneath the chamber flash in response, slowly and uncertainly. Several interpretations pass through the mountain. None becomes compulsory.

The abyss lights again.

The stains answer differently.

Thus begins the first conversation, if conversation can exist before language and across bodies that may be organs of one another.

The power does not translate.

Translation would decide too much.

It merely preserves the interval between signal and consequence. Within that interval, the city discovers that it need not repeat every injury in order to remember it.

Changes follow, but they are not universal.

Some feeding corridors remain. Others develop shallow recesses where striders may withdraw before height takes too much of them. Predatory shadows continue to descend, but certain plazas weather into asymmetry before evening, leaving nothing there that must be harmed for the shadows to mature. Niches open for hollow-wings, yet a few contain second openings through which an occupant may depart.

When resonant chambers begin to lose their sustaining proportions, cracks appear nearby. The silent forms are not forced through those cracks. Some migrate. Some remain and fade. For the first time, both endings enter the deeper architecture as distinct patterns.

The city remembers not only what happened.

It begins to remember what might have happened instead.

This new tendency spreads downward.

Beneath walls appear walls with more than one doorway. Beneath stairways appear landings from which altitude can be refused. Beneath the old tracks leading toward the summit, erosion exposes broad untouched spaces beside each depression, as though the enormous climber had considered placing its weight there and chosen otherwise.

Nothing becomes gentle all at once.

Nothing here becomes anything all at once.

The power continues to attend, but with increasing distance. It withdraws as a wise hand withdraws from a structure once the structure has learned to bear its own weight. Its smallest rescues cease. Pressure tides resume their indifferent courses. Shadows catch striders. Towers shear. Whole districts close and do not reopen.

But beneath the western terraces, the frost remains.

Not forever.

After many ages, a band of dawn enters by a route made possible through the combined erosion of thirteen districts. It crosses the chamber and reaches the sealed crack.

The gathered striders lower their heads.

The hollow-wings tighten their nested shells.

The mineral stains brighten. The abyssal openings hold steady. Structured silences fill every passage.

The frost warms.

For the first time in that place, it does not vanish directly into air.

A quantity too small to fall gathers upon the crystal: neither sea nor rain, only a trembling curvature, clear and mobile, holding the chamber upside down inside itself.

No inhabitant has an organ for seeing it.

Still, the city changes around its presence.

The drop remains for less than a second.

Then it is gone.

Yet in that second, it joins surfaces without fusing them. It takes the shape of the stone while remaining other than stone. It reflects each gathered form to the others. It moves according to pressure but possesses a boundary of its own.

The mountain receives this event not as a commandment, but as an image.

Far below, deeper than any exposed district, new planes of weakness begin arranging themselves around small, unbroken spaces.

Not rooms.

Not cages.

Places where one thing may touch another without either being consumed.

At the far horizon, where inverted towers meet the upward spires, the warm new stone opens along an unfamiliar seam. Transparent limbs stir there. A hollow geometry loosens. Silence gathers, not to erase a sound, but to make room for one.

And when the shape begins to inhabit itself, it finds that the city has left it more than one way to be.
