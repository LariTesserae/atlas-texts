The power does not descend in fire.

It does not tear open the roof, name the inhabitants, or declare the country unfinished. It does not mistake stillness for suffering merely because stillness would be suffering to a creature born beneath a turning sky.

It waits above the hidden country for a time that cannot be measured there.

Then it discovers that waiting is already the language of the place.

So it enters without crossing a threshold.

In the long hall beside the pool, the clear depression at the center remains undisturbed. No hand touches it. No face bends over its surface. Yet the water becomes aware that it is being regarded.

This is not the attention of a visitor. Visitors look from one place, through two eyes, during a limited span. This attention has no direction. It is equally present at the bottom of the pool, within the membranes of the hall, along the buried cords, and in the minute spaces between water and root where neither has entirely yielded to the other.

The elder associated with the hall responds.

Condensation lengthens on the eastern wall. A sweetness passes into the floor. Far away, beneath a forest of jointed tubes, three root-tips open their transparent channels.

The utterance means, as nearly as any quick being might understand it:

**You are not enclosed. What are you?**

The power answers in pressure, mineral, light, and withheld sweetness. It does not force the inhabitants to bear the violence of sound.

Its answer requires eleven years in the world above, though inside the second country no one experiences delay.

It says:

**One who can alter what it loves, and therefore must be careful.**

The answer passes through every joined grove.

The colorless swimmers incline their heads. Flat creatures loosen a single filament from the pillar-trunks. High among the luminous leaves, pale wings alter their angle against the brightness. Throughout the country, structures that seemed empty begin to register one another. Alcoves taste the seeds they contain. Bridges become aware of the basins they cross. Corridors attend to the fact that they lead somewhere.

The inhabitants consider the presence.

At last they ask it the question they have asked every stranger, though never in words:

**Will you remain?**

The wise power understands that this question contains others.

Will you cease moving?

Will you permit us to keep you?

Will you become comprehensible?

Will you stop ending?

It answers:

**Not in the manner you mean.**

This causes no grief, because grief has not yet entered the country. But over a region many leagues wide, the veins in the high leaves become dark.

The power perceives then that the inhabitants are not merely maintaining equilibrium. They are defending it.

Deep below the resilient floor, beneath rhizomes broad as hills and root-cords carrying conversations older than nations, there is a command distributed through nearly every living tissue. It has no single source. It is embedded in the shape of pores, in the chemistry of clear sap, in the folded proteins of seeds that have never opened.

The command is simple:

**Let nothing be lost.**

It is older than the halls. Older than the pillar-people. Older, perhaps, than the distinction between person and country.

The benevolent power follows the command downward.

It passes through stone without breaking it and through memory without reading what has not been offered. At the lowest depth reached by any root, it finds a narrow chamber in the embryonic mineral. The walls are pale and softly curved. They bear the impression of a body that once lay there.

The body itself is gone.

Only one root enters the chamber. It is finer than a hair and completely black. Unlike every other root in the country, it is dead.

The power understands.

At the beginning, something was lost.

Perhaps it was the first leaf, torn before the canopy had learned to hold itself. Perhaps it was a companion to the first inhabitant. Perhaps the country began as two living things, and one failed to continue. It no longer matters which account is materially true. The loss was experienced before the inhabitants possessed any way to distinguish ending from annihilation.

Everything that followed was an answer to that terror.

The steady heat. The windless air. The arrest of decay. The bright leaves admitting no night. The pools that did not overflow. The seeds kept from germination, because germination spends the seed. The motionless animals, the unworn stone, the dead visitors preserved against every process that might make their departure irreversible.

The country has not been waiting for birth.

It has been preventing a second death.

The power does not uproot the black filament. It does not replace what was lost with an imitation. Wisdom forbids such mercies when they would make every love into a refusal to know the beloved’s absence.

Instead it stays beside the dead root.

For the first time, the ancient command is accompanied.

Nothing changes for a long while.

Then the power asks the inhabitants:

**Is nothing lost here?**

The country answers by displaying its completeness. It shows the preserved leaves, each cell fixed in its final condition. It shows the guests in their clear cradles, every eyelash and scar retained. It shows memories held in seeds, spoken words traveling forever through closed rings of trunks, patterns copied without degradation.

The power regards all of it tenderly.

Then it asks:

**Are they present?**

This question is more difficult.

The roots tighten around the preserved dead.

Membranes brighten. Water gathers at the edges of pools without crossing them. One pale winged form vanishes from the canopy and appears several leagues away.

The inhabitants search every record they possess.

They find impressions, structures, temperatures, salts. They find the shape of a traveler’s hand remembered by the meeting water. They find the rhythm of blood once spilled upon a corridor. They find a spoken name repeating through wood long after the mouth that spoke it ceased to breathe.

But they cannot find the one who answered to the name.

The discovery passes through the country as a new pressure.

It is not yet sorrow. It is the hollow into which sorrow will eventually grow.

The benevolent power does not enlarge it.

Instead, in a distant alcove, it selects one black seed. It asks permission of the inhabitant whose memory is folded inside.

Permission comes slowly: a trace of sweetness, offered and withdrawn, offered again.

The power does not make the seed germinate.

It shows the seed what germination is.

Inside the unbroken coat, time is allowed to pass through a space smaller than a grain of dust. Stored tissue softens. A root-tip imagines pressure. Two first leaves, colorless and tightly folded, receive the possibility of light. The seed experiences that to become the plant, it must cease to remain the seed.

The power holds this possibility open but does not compel it.

The seed waits.

Then, of its own living tendency, it breaks.

The sound cannot be heard outside the alcove. It is only the minute fracture of a black shell.

Nevertheless, every inhabitant in the joined country perceives it.

A white root no longer than an eyelash enters the pale wall of the niche.

The broken halves of the seed remain on either side.

At once the inhabitants reach toward them, intending preservation. Threads prepare to arrest the exposed cells. The air adjusts to prevent the loss of moisture.

The power says:

**Watch.**

The empty husk pales.

Its structure loosens. What had been stored within it passes into the new root. A minute portion enters the air. Another portion is received by a fungus that has waited without feeding since the landscape began. The shape of the seed is lost.

Nothing capable of being restored remains.

But the seedling extends.

A green point appears between the halves of the husk.

The inhabitants search the green point. They find the mineral of the shell. They find its sugars altered and carried upward. They find its precise pattern no longer preserved, yet not wholly absent. Some of it has become root. Some leaf. Some fungus. Some warmth. Some has entered relationships for which the seed possessed no dormant instruction.

For the first time, they encounter a continuation that is not retention.

The country does not awaken. Not yet.

But it revises one question.

**Can change occur without betrayal?**

The power answers:

**Sometimes.**

It does not say always.

That is part of its benevolence.

It does not promise that every wound will become wisdom, that every death will feed new life, or that nothing essential can be destroyed. Such assurances would merely conceal the old terror beneath more beautiful words.

Instead it says:

**What is loved need not be kept unchanged in order to have been real.**

This answer enters the root-cords.

It will take a very long time to understand.

While it is being understood, the power turns its attention to the preserved travelers.

There are more than the inhabitants realized. Some lie in chambers copied from their clothing and tools. Some are seated beneath membranes clear as the inner skin of an egg. One stands upright within a pillar whose bark has grown around his shoulders, his face calm not because he died calmly, but because the country erased no condition and permitted no further one.

The inhabitants have kept them warm. They have maintained the chemistry of their final instant. They have mistaken the arrest of departure for hospitality.

The power visits each.

Where any spark of living return remains, however small, it restores the body to its own motion. A heart contracts. A lung draws the hot air. Blood completes a journey interrupted for centuries. These awakenings are rare.

Most of the travelers have truly died.

The power does not call them back. Their lives have gone beyond the reach of preserved flesh, and wisdom will not drag them against their course merely to resolve the confusion of those who stayed behind.

But neither does it abandon their remains.

It teaches the inhabitants a distinction between a person and the trace of a person. Not by doctrine, which roots could preserve without comprehending, but by contact. It permits the country to feel where presence has ended.

Across the bright interior, cradling roots loosen.

The membranes covering dead eyes grow thin. For the first time, bodies begin to cool, though the air around them remains warm. Fungal threads that had waited at the skin are allowed to cross it.

Here the inhabitants nearly withdraw their consent.

The command rises again:

**Let nothing be lost.**

The power answers from the chamber of the black root:

**Then receive what can be received. Release what cannot.**

Decay begins in one small chamber.

It is not violent. Nothing swarms. No stench spreads through the corridors. The body returns by increments suited to the country’s patience. Salts enter roots. Water joins water. The woven cloth slumps where the shape beneath it changes. A metal buckle remains, and around it a vine grows in the form the inhabitants learned long ago.

The dead traveler is not restored.

Neither is he erased.

His matter enters the place that tried to understand him. His form persists imperfectly in halls, vines, memorized pressure, and the altered attention of an elder. His absence is permitted to be an absence.

This is the country’s first burial.

The power remains during all of it.

Afterward, the inhabitants ask another question:

**If we begin, must everything begin?**

They fear that one fallen drop will draw all condensation after it; that one seed opening will command every seed; that time, once admitted, will rush through the country like fire through dry grass.

The power answers:

**No.**

It sets no universal clock.

Instead it helps them grow boundaries—not walls against passage, but membranes between kinds of duration.

One basin consents to movement. A single current enters its water. The colorless swimmer there makes its first voluntary stroke.

One grove permits its leaves to age. They deepen from green to bronze, fall, soften, and become dark soil. New shoots rise among the old pillar-trunks, and the inhabitants discover that the young do not repeat every memory they are given.

One field of equal blades chooses to remain exactly as it was.

One chamber becomes cool at regular intervals. The change is slight, no more than the difference between a held breath and a released one. Yet condensation falls there.

At first, one drop.

It strikes a broad leaf.

The sound travels farther than thunder would have traveled.

Every pale winged creature lifts from the canopy.

They do not vanish and reappear. They fly.

Their wings disturb the air. The disturbance touches another leaf, then another. A current passes between the pillar-trunks. It is too weak to be called wind anywhere else, but here it crosses a country that has never felt one moving thing influence another through open space.

The luminous leaves tremble.

Shadows appear beneath them.

The inhabitants do not extinguish the shadows. They examine them.

Within those moving regions of reduced light, age does not immediately gather. No ruin advances. Nothing monstrous enters. There is only depth, contrast, concealment, and the discovery that a thing may be partially unseen without ceasing to exist.

The wise power does not make night.

It gives them the ability to choose where dusk may begin.

Nor does it take the place for itself. It does not become the awaited inhabitant, the ruler of the groves, or the final meaning of the halls. Benevolence without restraint would be another form of conquest.

It leaves no throne.

What it leaves is a new organ grown beside the meeting pool: a small, many-folded flower, plain in comparison with the vast radiant canopy. The flower does not remain open. It responds to need.

When a quick, enclosed being enters from above—scarred, hungry, carrying endings—the flower opens at once.

Through it, the inhabitants can understand urgency.

A medicinal shoot intended for a wounded traveler no longer takes three years to arrive. Roots do not preserve blood outside the body while the body empties. Corridors shorten by growth. Bridges alter their incline. The country learns that care sometimes means haste.

The flower also allows the visitor to feel the country’s slower questions without being trapped inside their duration.

It is an instrument of translation between those who cross distance and those who become it.

When there is no need, it closes.

The power withdraws then, though not entirely. A benevolent and wise power rarely remains visible after its presence would begin to shape every choice. It leaves the inhabitants to determine which districts will enter change, which will retain stillness, which dead will be buried, which records will remain seeds, and which ancient structures will be permitted to become something their makers did not intend.

In the deepest chamber, the black root is left where it was.

But beside it grows a white one.

The white root does not replace the dead filament. It does not cover it. They lie together in the pale stone: loss and continuation, neither required to pretend it is the other.

Far above, beneath the unseen roof, most of the condensation still hangs in brilliant rows. Most pools remain smooth. Most flowers stand at the threshold of opening. The country has not been cured of its nature, because its nature was not an illness.

Yet here and there, by consent, something proceeds.

A leaf falls.

A swimmer crosses its cup.

A pillar-person draws its folded hands from the neighboring trunk and, after an interval full of effort, places one hand against the open air.

In the long hall, the meeting water trembles.

Not because a stranger has touched it.

Because, somewhere beneath the living floor, an inhabitant has chosen to move.
