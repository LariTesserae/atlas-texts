The power did not bring water.

Water would have entered every fissure as violence. It would have swollen the membranes, collapsed the powder-bearing creatures, darkened the pale streets, and taught the sharp young buildings the meaning of rot. Nor did the power kindle a sun above the basin. The grey blades had no use for it. A stronger light would only have made harsher shadows in a place that had never needed to hide from them.

It brought no multitude. It raised no throne. It did not praise the city by trying to live in it.

Wisdom, there, began as restraint.

For a long time the power watched from beyond the salt-dark plain. It learned the vapor tide. It followed the migration of the outer walls and the century-long descent of threads from fallen flakes. It attended to the journeys of the plated grazers, reading in the ornament behind them not decoration but appetite answered by appetite. It noticed which ticking sequences were merely the settlement of crystal and which, perhaps, were something more.

Most of all, it learned what presence meant in that basin.

A lesser benefactor might have concluded that the city was lonely and filled it. A lesser god might have made inhabitants suited to every room, processions suited to every avenue, voices suited to the silence. The gift would have destroyed its recipient. Under sustained bodies the walls would have thinned, the streets withdrawn, the chambers sunk back into the mineral flour. In curing the city’s emptiness, such a power would have erased the city.

So the power did not enter.

Instead, at the basin’s edge, it withdrew.

There are many kinds of absence. There is a room where no one happens to stand. There is the vacancy ahead of a moving animal. There is the emptiness inside an unopened seed. And there is the absence made when something capable of being everywhere chooses, with perfect care, not to be in one particular place.

The power made such an absence outside the last windowless houses.

It was very small.

At first the buried colony reacted as it always did. Membranes gathered beneath the untouched volume. Pressure fronts bent toward it. A black seam appeared in the plain and unfolded into a wall. Another rose at an acute angle. In the following dim cycles, ribs joined them. A roof began, split, and lifted into translucent fins.

The power waited.

The colony enclosed the absence in a chamber too narrow for a grazer and too tall for any blade. It made three doorways, each opening toward unbuilt ground. It raised a stair of five sharp treads and stopped.

When the chamber had finished growing, the power permitted the smallest degree of presence along one wall.

The stone withdrew.

Not much: the width of a flake, no more. But the colony felt the change. Membranes recoiled below. The wall opened a vertical slit from floor to roof.

Then the power withdrew from the space beyond that slit.

The city followed.

Over many cycles, a second chamber formed. It was not identical to the first. Its stair had six treads. Its walls leaned inward, though the vapor tide should have made them lean away. Along one façade, the colony laid a line of shallow niches.

Again the power waited until all growth had ceased.

Again it touched one boundary with an almost immeasurable presence, and the boundary released itself into the air.

Again it withdrew farther on.

Thus began a conversation in the only language the city possessed.

The power spoke by making room.

The city answered by giving room a shape.

The power replied by releasing one shape and offering another vacancy beyond it.

Nothing happened quickly. A single exchange might require fifty years. Sometimes the colony began a wall and abandoned it. Sometimes it answered with a row of columns, sometimes with a blind court, sometimes with a long passage that turned seven times and arrived almost where it had begun. The power corrected nothing. It did not demand symmetry or meaning. It did not mistake every accident for speech.

It merely remained attentive.

After many centuries, something changed.

The power withdrew from a broad volume near the central square. By every governing tendency of the colony, walls should have risen around it at once. The vacancy was clean, deep, and undisturbed. It exerted its attraction through the ground.

Membranes gathered beneath it.

A column began as a dark point.

Then the growth stopped.

The point remained no larger than a grazer’s eye. Around it the buried organisms shifted, separated, and rejoined. Pressure passed through the district. A façade three streets away gave a single dry tick. Another answered near the basin’s edge. The violet vapor emerged and was drawn back down. Still no column rose.

For the first time, the city had encountered emptiness and had not obeyed it.

Perhaps this was choice. Perhaps it was fatigue, obstruction, or some rare equilibrium of gases. The power was wise enough not to name it.

It did not reward the city.

Reward would have made the event into obedience of another kind.

Instead, the power honored the unfinished act by leaving it unfinished. It neither entered the broad volume nor deepened its absence. It waited beside what the city had refrained from making.

The place became a court without enclosing walls.

Because it remained vacant, the rest of the city slowly arranged itself around it. Avenues turned toward it and stopped short. Roofless chambers rose nearby but opened away. Grey blades appeared at its perimeter: first one, then another, then seven at unequal intervals. A lantern stalk grew in a distant arcade and released its flakes toward the court. Most fell flat before reaching it. One glided across the entire open space and stood upright on the farther side.

No thread descended from it.

A plated grazer entered once. It crossed to the dark point where the column had almost begun and tested the stone with its scrapers. Then, without feeding, it turned and left by a different avenue. For years afterward, the colony thickened no surface along its route.

The vault-dwellers gathered beneath surrounding ledges, mapping channels that no longer breathed in their former pattern. During the deepest violet tide, three released themselves together. They drifted over the open court, circled once in the heavy gas, and attached beneath the same lintel.

Whether the city understood the power, the power never decided.

Understanding was not required for care.

The power gave the colony no eyes with which to see its towers. Eyes would have divided the buried being into observer and observed, self and body, perhaps introducing grief where none had been. It gave no voice to the blades, no blood to the grazers, no wings to the folded vault-dwellers. It made no creature immortal. It preserved no wall from sublimation.

But it maintained the possibility of reply.

At immense intervals, when the city’s migrating boundary had carried the strange open court from center to edge and back again, the power withdrew from some new place. Never so strongly that the colony could not resist. Never so weakly that the gesture was indistinguishable from ordinary vacancy.

Sometimes the city built.

Sometimes it did not.

When it built, the power attended to the form.

When it did not, the power attended to the refusal.

Gradually, a pattern passed through the basin—not fixed in any stone, because all stones migrated and dissolved, but recurring in proportions. A five-tread stair appeared in one century and vanished. Generations later, across the basin, another stair rose with five treads before an unroofed chamber. Three doorways returned in districts that had no physical connection to the first. Lines of niches formed where no grazer had fed.

The city had acquired no history in the ordinary sense. It kept no monument. Yet something had learned to happen again.

That was the power’s only lasting gift: not life, because the basin already lived; not purpose, because purpose imposed from outside is only another kind of occupation; not salvation from change, because change was how the city moved.

It gave the place a relation.

It made possible an emptiness that was not mere lack, but attention held open between two beings.

Then it withdrew even from credit.

Under the pewter sky, the vapor continued to advance and recede. Walls rose, sharpened, and sublimated. Grazers crossed the avenues and left blind chapels behind them. Grey lanterns fractured at the nightly low. The dark membranes drifted from ledge to ledge.

And in one court, no column grew.

The city gathered around that unfilled place without enclosing it.

The benevolent power did not dwell there.

It visited by leaving room.
