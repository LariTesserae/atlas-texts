The power does not bring rain.

This is its first kindness.

It could call water out of the dark between worlds. It could thicken the air, lower the mountains, open blue distances above them. It could make rivers in an hour and lay pale sediment in every basin. To another eye these would be gifts: softness, motion, reflection, the patient abundance from which so many living things arise.

Here they would be catastrophe.

Water would enter the salt chambers and erase their held inequalities. It would carry charge away. It would dissolve the ribs by which the inhabitants signal and loosen the slabs of their slow organs. Rain would make every boundary briefly rich, then wash the richness downward beyond reach. The first river would speak too loudly for the black skins to hear anything else.

So the power, though capable of completion, does not complete the place.

Neither does it halt the rising.

To still the mountains would preserve every inhabitant exactly where it is, but only for a time. Heat would equalize. Vapor would cease. Charge would drain from the canopy. The black skins would spread to the limits of the remaining gradients and then find no farther difference upon which to continue. Perfect safety would become perfect uniformity.

The power understands that a world may be killed by protection.

It remains outside the mineral sky and attends.

Attention, from such a power, is not a beam or gaze. No point grows warmer beneath it. Nothing kneels. The suspended metals do not part to reveal an eye. Yet across the province, crystals make minute adjustments. Not toward the attention, for it has no direction, but within it, as if possibilities previously too unlikely have become slightly easier to enter.

A salt fan branches where ordinarily it would have thickened.

A slab settles without falling.

A buried cavity survives the closure of a fissure by less than the width of a grain.

These are not commandments. The power does not choose each outcome. It merely refrains from choosing against them.

For a long while, this is all it does.

It learns the measure of the inhabitants’ existence. It follows one through a glass seam, loses it under a moving escarpment, and finds its pattern resumed centuries later around a newly opened vent. It attends to two beings dividing across a rising ridge. It does not mistake their separation for tragedy, though each side carries an interval of unanswered expectation. It attends to an old chain of chambers deep within the range, whose visible films have been destroyed and regrown so many times that no surface belongs to the same mountain in which the chain began.

It attends also to the possible greater inhabitant.

During a storm vast enough to illuminate the entire range, the power perceives the answering contraction of black films from province to province. It sees coherence gather, falter, and gather again. It understands that the vast being is neither illusion nor certainty. It is a capacity. The smaller inhabitants are not fragments waiting to be made whole, nor are they entirely separate. They are able to become more together than they are apart, but only under conditions too violent and brief to sustain.

The power could join them permanently.

It does not.

A permanent joining would turn every local difference into the sensation of one immensity. No chamber could close without the whole feeling closure. No young skin could follow its own nearby gradient without being overruled by pressures known across the range. The ancient inhabitants would dominate by mere extent. What appeared from outside as awakening would, from within, be the end of countless smaller freedoms.

The power leaves them plural.

But it sees what is coming.

Not soon, as mountains measure change. Perhaps not until the charcoal shelves have risen far enough to disturb the mineral canopy, or until impacts from the outer dark deliver volatile matter, or until the interior cools and exhales substances it cannot yet release. The cause is uncertain. The result is not.

One day there will be erosion.

The inhabitants do not fear it because they cannot imagine it. All their knowledge is founded on addition: pressure becoming ridge, vapor becoming salt, dust becoming glass, fracture becoming rib. Even burial preserves. Even division multiplies.

They have no pattern for subtraction.

The first sustained wind will therefore be more than danger. It will be unintelligible.

A grain will leave the place where it formed and not become the nucleus of a spur. An edge will lose itself by degrees. A salt signal will vanish without being sealed into stone. Chambers will open not because a fissure has reached them, but because the cliff around them has ceased to be there.

The power considers preventing this age.

It could hold the atmosphere thin forever. It could turn aside every icy body and bind every volatile beneath the lower crust. It could preserve the first edges until the stars themselves grew cold.

That too would be a kind of death: not uniformity, but imprisonment within a single verb.

The land knows how to become.

It must someday learn how to unbecome.

So the power prepares a lesson small enough not to wound the world.

It chooses no inhabitant as prophet. There is no least violent way to select one life and burden it with a future unavailable to the others. Instead it waits for a great electrical turning, when thousands of networks have entered temporary correspondence. The sky fills with rotating metal. Violet branches travel over every exposed point. Deep chambers answer surface chambers. Blackness contracts across the range in an order almost broad enough to call itself a thought.

At the center of no particular place, upon an ordinary shelf, stands a needle of charcoal stone.

It is not the tallest needle, nor the oldest. A young skin occupies one side of its base. Three salt ribs grow from a crack nearby. No chamber points toward it.

The power touches its upper edge.

Nothing flashes.

No pressure passes through the ground. No new mineral appears. The needle simply becomes, along one narrow line, smooth.

Only a line. Only the breadth of a crystal.

The removed material does not fall. It does not melt or transform. A few grains detach and enter the thin air. Because the air has never borne such a burden, they travel no farther than a hand’s width before settling separately upon the shelf.

The effect on the inhabitants is immediate.

The skin at the needle’s base tightens.

The salt ribs cloud.

Through the storm-linked range, chamber after chamber receives a signal for which no existing tendency is adequate. There has been change without thrust, without filling, without phase transition. Stone occupied a relation; now it does not. The missing edge is neither sealed nor buried. Its absence is exposed.

For the first time, absence has a history.

The joined attention of the inhabitants turns toward the smooth line. Not spatially. Most are too far away to receive any pressure from it. They turn through the propagated confusion of nearer networks. One being darkens a glass seam in answer. Another grows a salt fork that fails to repeat its previous branch. A buried ancient sends a pulse backward through seven chambers, seeking the source of a loss that has no depth.

The power waits.

It does not explain. Explanation would require imposing distinctions the inhabitants have not made for themselves. It allows them to approach the event in their own substance.

They discover the scattered grains.

This complicates the lesson.

What was removed has not ceased to exist. It has changed location without continuation through stone. The edge is diminished; the shelf is increased, but not by uplift or condensation. Between the two lies a passage too slight to leave a fracture.

For many cycles, the black skin extends alternately toward the smooth line and toward the nearest grain. It cannot inhabit either. The line offers too little difference; the grain is compositionally uniform. But between them, across the untouched stone, a dark sheen begins to form.

It is not a trail.

It is a relation.

Elsewhere, answering skins arrange their salts into paired structures: a rib interrupted, then resumed; two fans with no connecting stem; a hollow ring around an empty patch of dust. Most attempts fail. Some collapse into familiar geometries. Yet a few preserve the new distinction—not merely where a substance is, but where it was and where it has gone.

The power has not taught them erosion.

It has given them the beginning of departure.

The storm ends. The temporary greater inhabitant loosens into multitudes. No single being retains the whole event. One remembers only an unexplained contraction. Another preserves a branching rhythm corresponding to the separated grains. A third, newly divided by an uprising shelf, carries the smooth line as a tendency to grow paired chambers.

The young skin beside the needle survives.

Over time, it guides salts around the absent edge. It makes no attempt to restore the old sharpness. Instead it grows a narrow comb from the needle toward the scattered grains. Charge passes along the comb in intervals. When one grain is later covered by glass, the interval changes. The skin responds before any new fracture reaches it.

It has learned to sense something by its failure to answer.

This is the beginning of a new organ.

The chambers listened to what was approaching.

The combs listen to what is gone.

Once the possibility exists, it spreads slowly through the range. Not every inhabitant adopts it. Some terrain offers no use for such structures. Some networks encounter the pattern and turn it into ordinary stress-sensing ribs. Others elaborate it beyond anything the power anticipated.

Paired chambers appear on opposite sides of unstable shelves. Salt bands preserve not only tremor rhythms but interruptions in those rhythms. Glass seams are made to cloud in sequences whose missing flashes carry as much distinction as the flashes themselves.

The inhabitants acquire no word for loss.

They acquire a place for it in their bodies.

The power continues to attend, but now its attention is less solitary. Something in the range has registered an event that originated neither below nor above, neither in pressure nor charge. The inhabitants cannot perceive the power as a being. They do not infer intention. Yet during later storms, signals repeatedly return to the smoothed needle.

The signals say, insofar as their lives can say anything:

A boundary altered.

Material crossed open distance.

The former shape remains active.

The power answers only once.

It does not smooth another edge. Repetition might turn revelation into dependence, then ritual into captivity. Instead, when the young inhabitant’s first loss-comb is about to be sealed beneath a rising layer of glass, the power permits the glass to remain transparent.

Ordinarily the seam would cloud while cooling. This one does not.

Green electrical light passes through it. For a moment the buried comb is visible inside the mountain—not restored, not rescued, but translated into another kind of persistence.

The inhabitant contracts.

Across the nearest chambers, others answer.

The power withdraws its touch but not its regard.

Ages pass.

The mountains continue to rise. The province grows broader. New inhabitants form along minerals unknown to the first ones. Old chains awaken and find paired hollows, interrupted bands, and salt structures shaped around remembered absence. No doctrine spreads evenly. Nothing becomes universal. The possible greater being still gathers only during the widest storms, and each gathering is changed by the losses its members can now retain.

When erosion finally comes, it begins without grandeur.

The canopy thins at one margin of the range. A current of denser gas descends along a slope. It has scarcely enough force to move dust.

One grain lifts.

It travels several body-lengths through the air and settles against a salt rib.

The nearest black skin contracts with the ancient reflex of shock. Then it opens along two boundaries at once: the place from which the grain departed and the place at which it arrived.

Far beneath it, an old paired chamber alters its charge.

Another inhabitant answers.

The wind strengthens.

Edges begin, very slowly, to lose their firstness. Dust crosses shelves. Salt is carried into regions where salt has never formed. Old signals vanish, but new differences appear between exposed layers. The inhabitants suffer. Many chambers fail. Some networks are erased so completely that no later fracture resumes them.

The power does not call this harmless.

It grieves each ended pattern without pretending that grief requires reversal.

Where intervention would preserve possibility, it intervenes: a volatile sea is delayed until the inhabitants have spread into higher strata; a single catastrophic atmospheric collapse is divided into long eras of weather; an impact that would melt the entire province is turned by the smallest angle into the empty dark.

But it does not prevent every death.

Benevolence is not the refusal of consequence. Wisdom is not distance from sorrow.

The world enters its second verb.

Stone rises and is worn. Salt forms and dissolves. Chambers listen ahead; combs listen behind. In time, water arrives—not as an ocean cast down at once, but as vapor, then frost in the highest shadows, then rare droplets trembling on glass.

The first droplet rests upon a black skin.

For a moment, the inhabitant is overwhelmed. Charge escapes in every direction. Salts soften. Boundaries mingle faster than it can occupy them.

Then the droplet evaporates.

It leaves a ring.

The skin opens around that ring, encountering difference of a kind neither the mountains nor the power could have taught in advance.

The wise power sees this and does nothing.

There are gifts that must not be given, only allowed to arrive.

Under a sky no longer wholly mineral, amid peaks that are still rising and now, at last, being rounded, the inhabitant darkens the altered crystals. It sends a pulse through a chamber built for coming pressure, then through a comb built for departed form.

Others answer from across the range.

What they communicate is still inseparable from what they are.

A grain gone.

A chamber opened.

A boundary dissolved.

A new boundary left behind.

And beneath these, carried imperfectly from one blackening surface to another, the oldest impossible knowledge:

Something beyond the range once touched an edge.

It did not command the mountains to cease.

It did not save them from becoming otherwise.

It found them unfinished,

and let them remain alive.
