### The Fourth Rule

The power did not descend in fire.

Fire would have frightened the moths, emptied the moss of its hidden moisture, and awakened in the hollow responses older than fear. Nor did it arrive as a voice. The chamber already possessed voices enough, and wisdom does not mistake loudness for authority.

It came as attention.

Not the sliding, forgetful attention of travelers on the summit, nor the acquisitive attention that names a thing in order to carry it away. It did not search for use, origin, weakness, or ownership. It regarded every portion of the hollow without separating any portion from the rest.

For the first time in an age beyond counting, the place was wholly seen.

The power saw the dark shrines and all the distances folded within them. It saw where the monkeys went, and where they had come from. It saw the far mountains, drowned caverns, salt basins, abandoned engines, buried chambers, and impossible gardens joined for an instant whenever an insect entered one oval and emerged from another.

It saw that most of those places were gone.

Some had dried. Some had drowned. Some had been opened by tools and emptied by hands. In one, the shrines stood beneath kilometers of ice. In another, the last living thing was a colorless film waiting around a mineral spring that had ceased to flow. The monkeys carried pollen from forests that existed now only in scattered rooms. The spotted cat represented a lineage whose flesh-and-blood descendants had vanished from the warm plains generations before.

The hollow was not merely a refuge.

It was a knot tied in a net that was coming apart.

The power saw, too, the intelligence of its rules.

Nothing living was permitted to become indispensable, because indispensable beings make a world fragile. Emptiness remained, because a place already full has no answer to the unforeseen. Surplus was not condemned to starvation but conveyed into need. Shape yielded to duty. Life continued not by preserving every form, but by refusing to let any necessary relationship die with the body that performed it.

These were not cruel ideas.

They had carried green things through epochs of heat. They had kept songs sounding after singers failed. They had preserved pollination without insisting on bees, grazing without herds, predation without slaughter. Under the hollow’s severe stewardship, extinction had often become transformation rather than ending.

The power honored this.

Then it saw the counted ones.

It felt the sleeper’s warmth beside the low walls. It heard the breath caught in mineral bands. It sensed the awareness stretched thin through the obscuring tendency of the summit: a person dispersed across every traveler’s impulse to turn aside. It found the hand in the wall, still remembering touch though it had not moved for centuries. It listened to the unfinished counter inhabiting the pause between entrances and exits.

The hollow had not tortured them. It had not hated them. It had done something more innocent and, in its innocence, nearly as terrible.

It had mistaken endurance for consent.

The power remained through one silence.

Water reversed. The crabs turned. The beetles emerged to measure alterations too small for flesh to perceive. Every living thing held still around an attention that was not sound, heat, hunger, weather, or danger.

Then the cicadas began again.

The power remained through a second silence, and a third.

It could have broken the shrines. It could have unfolded their inward distances and exposed every connected world beneath one sky. It could have separated the counted ones from stone and rebuilt them from remembered dust. It could have commanded the chamber to release what it had taken.

But command would have taught nothing. Destruction would have killed much. Restoration without understanding would only return ancient persons to a world in which their languages, peoples, foods, obligations, and beloved dead no longer existed.

Benevolence restrained its own strength.

So the power made itself small enough to enter.

At the seam in the summit, pale grasses bent though there was no wind. Dust lifted from the crawlspace in a single soft breath. Something passed beneath the roots without crushing them and descended into the green shadow.

No animal fled.

That did not mean they were unafraid. The lizards flattened themselves against the mineral bands. Moths tightened their overlapping ranks. The spotted cat appeared beside the central pool and looked toward the entrance, its drifting spots gathering darkly around its face. Six roads of ants stopped at once, violating their ancient order of precedence.

The visitor had no fixed body. The hollow attempted to assign it one.

For a moment it was a tall animal of warm blood and upright posture.

Then it was a coolness above the pools.

Then pollen.

Then a seventh road.

Then the possibility of rain.

Each shape was accurate and none contained it. The hollow continued testing forms, as it did with all new arrivals, but the power did not resist. Resistance would have been interpreted as a role. Instead, it accepted each form completely and relinquished it before the chamber could make it necessary.

The place had never encountered anything so willing to be changed and so impossible to diminish.

The largest shrine opened an aperture.

It was upright, dark, and approximately shaped to the visitor’s standing form.

The power approached it.

A faint inward pressure touched the chamber. Moss leaned toward the oval. Droplets rose from the pools and hung in the air. Somewhere beyond the aperture, multitudes of possible bodies waited to inherit whatever office the newcomer might leave vacant.

The power placed one hand against the shrine.

It did not prevent itself from being counted.

Instead, it counted the hollow back.

It counted every pool, including those not currently present. It counted each ant road and each substitute species that had walked it. It counted the monkeys here and elsewhere, the pollen in their fur, the teeth and infants left in cracks. It counted the dead forests from which they came.

It counted the spotted cat not only as pressure, symbol, and reserved violence, but as itself.

It counted the frogs that were calls and the calls that had forgotten their frogs.

It counted the shrines.

It counted the greater organism, if organism it was, distributed across inaccessible distances.

It counted all the bodies that had become functions.

And last, it counted the chamber as one thing.

The hollow had never included itself in a census.

The effect was slight.

The overhang boomed once.

A shiver moved through the stone, not outward but inward, traveling along channels for which direction had no ordinary meaning. In distant chambers, ice cracked. Salt trembled. A colorless film around a dead spring contracted as if touched. Monkeys in transit turned their black faces toward a place between places.

The hollow discovered that it, too, performed an office.

It was a keeper of continuities.

It was a bearer of relationships across ruin.

It was a vessel of adaptation.

It was a refuge.

And because it possessed an office, its first rule applied.

Nothing living was permitted to become indispensable.

Not even the hollow.

For seventeen minutes there was no sound.

The silence was not the usual silence. Water did not reverse. Shadows did not shift. Beetles remained within the shrines. The cicadas waited in old and new bodies alike.

The chamber considered the possibility of its own absence.

This had always been forbidden—not by malice, but by the narrowness of its design. It had preserved every necessary role except its own. Every living thing within it could be replaced, altered, divided, or exported. The hollow alone had been required to continue forever.

The power did not accuse it.

Instead, it offered help.

Across the warm plains, far below, seeds lying dormant in dust received a pattern too subtle to be called instruction. In a cloud passing days away, droplets arranged themselves around microscopic spores. Beneath a riverbed, stones shifted by less than the thickness of a root hair. In the gut of a migrating bird, three swallowed fruits did not digest.

None of these things became another hollow.

Not yet.

They became the capacity for one.

The work of keeping continuities would no longer belong to a single hidden chamber. It would diffuse by degrees: into wetlands that could remember drought, groves that could exchange duties among unlike species, fungal webs that left clear spaces for what had not evolved yet. No place would reproduce the mountain refuge exactly. Exact reproduction was not the gift being given.

The gift was redundancy.

The gift was that the hollow might someday fail without taking every ancient relationship with it.

The gift was rest.

Warm rain began to fall upward through the apertures.

The power kept one hand on the shrine. With the other, it touched the bare stone beside the unfinished row of carvings.

The counter stirred.

Not as a body. There was no body left to stir. The minerals had taken its salts; roots had borrowed its water; beetles had carried microscopic portions of it through seams and into distances beyond retrieval. The person who had once knelt there could not simply be separated from the chamber without tearing apart lives that now depended on those elements.

But a person is not made only of recoverable material.

There was also direction: the inward shape of wanting, refusing, fearing, recognizing. There was the old tension of an unfinished task. There was the memory of making one mark after another and knowing that entries did not balance exits. There was a name, though no living tongue could have pronounced it correctly.

The power spoke that name without sound.

Five pale projections withdrew from the mineral band.

A breath entered the chamber and did not immediately become condensation.

The patch of warmth near the walls gathered itself, hesitantly, around an absence shaped like someone sleeping. The corroded bowl rang once against the niche.

The hollow responded as it always had. Ant roads bent. Midges adjusted their pillars. One of the small shrines began forming another upright oval.

The power touched that shrine too.

Not to close the aperture.

To alter its meaning.

Until then, every opening had been an assignment. A body entered; a role opened. The chamber read prolonged presence as availability. To remain was to become useful, and to become useful was eventually to be used completely.

The power gave the aperture another function.

Refusal.

The dark oval brightened—not with light, but with the visible possibility of elsewhere. Those who looked into it did not see a destination. They saw whatever within themselves had not become landscape.

The spotted cat saw hunger that belonged to no ecosystem.

The monkeys saw forests not as stations in a network but as homes.

The ants saw nothing, being ants, yet each colony paused as if a burden had been lifted from an ancestor.

The sleeper by the low walls saw an open plain under unfamiliar stars.

The obscurer saw the entrance it had hidden for generations and understood that guarding a thing need not mean belonging to it forever.

The unfinished counter saw the final row.

Slowly, a mark appeared on the wall.

It was not an entrance.

It was not an exit.

It was a third sign.

Choice.

The power waited.

The counted ones did not all depart.

One had become the warmth at night and loved the tiny frogs that gathered where stone held the temperature of skin. Offered release, that one remained, but remained differently. Warmth was now a gift renewed from moment to moment, not a sentence imposed by duration.

One had become the booming voice of the overhang. At once it left.

The stone gave a tremendous report. Dust fell. Moths fluttered awake. For an instant a laugh—not joyful, not sorrowful, but astonished—rolled through the chamber and out beneath the seam. It crossed the summit and startled birds from cliffs miles away.

Thereafter, the overhang boomed only during storms.

The hand in the mineral band chose neither departure nor continuance. It became dormant, withholding decision. The hollow learned to shelter an undecided thing without assigning it a purpose.

The obscurer loosened its hold. The entrance did not become easy to find, but attention no longer slid away from it indiscriminately. Those who came seeking possession still walked around the seam. Those who came seeking refuge might notice pale grasses moving without wind.

The counter was last.

A figure gathered near the low walls, not in flesh but in an outline where condensation would not form. It sat beside the carvings for a long time. The chamber roared around it. Ants resumed their roads. Frogs called. Water changed direction and changed back.

The figure lifted one hand toward the final mark.

Then it rose.

The upright aperture received it without swallowing.

For a heartbeat, the oval showed a broad warm plain at dawn. No road crossed it. No ruin stood there. There was only grass, moving beneath the first wind, and space enough to walk without being counted.

The figure stepped through.

The aperture remained.

This was the fourth rule of the hollow:

**What stays must be permitted to remain itself.**

Or perhaps:

**No need is entitled to the whole of a being.**

Or:

**Every true entrance must allow an exit.**

The chamber could not express the rule in one form. Like its inhabitants, the rule wore different bodies according to the work required of it.

Predation continued. Things died. Fungus poisoned trespassing roots. Beetles cut down seedlings that threatened the open stone. Surplus animals still passed through the shrines and returned altered. The hollow did not become gentle by ceasing to be alive.

But thereafter, usefulness alone could not complete the taking of a creature.

A frog might inherit the call that marked the turning of water, but something of it remained unassigned: some small, wet, private existence belonging neither to role nor rhythm. The spotted cat continued to represent killing, yet sometimes it slept beside the central pool and dreamed useless dreams. Monkeys left gifts, but occasionally a young one carried away nothing and was not corrected.

The empty spaces changed most of all.

Before, they had been reservoirs of possibility maintained by force. Now one bare patch was left unpoliced. Moss could cross it. Seeds could root. Vines could creep over the polished rock.

For many seasons, nothing did.

Then a plant appeared there, a single low thing with four dark leaves.

No beetle clipped it. No lizard carried it away. Fungal threads approached its roots and withdrew. The hollow possessed no vacant office for it. It pollinated nothing, stored little water, fed no grazer, poisoned no rival, and marked no change in temperature or moisture.

It was unnecessary.

The chamber waited for the power to explain.

But the benevolent power had gone—or had withdrawn its attention into forms too ordinary to recognize.

The plant remained.

In time it produced one flower, colorless and without scent, opening only during the seventeen-minute silences. Nothing visited it. No seed followed.

For the length of its small life, it performed no known function at all.

And the hollow, ancient keeper of all necessary things, kept it anyway.
