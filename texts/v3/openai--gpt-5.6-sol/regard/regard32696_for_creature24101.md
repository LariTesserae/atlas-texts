The power does not descend.

Descent would be too violent a form of attention. A body arriving from beyond the cloud sea would bend the canopies, empty pools into buried chambers, and send alarm through every thread in the plain. A voice spoken into the air would mean little here. Air is the least trusted messenger. It shifts, disperses, and carries the cries of prey.

So the power begins by becoming patient.

It watches one drop of water.

The drop gathers beneath a silver-tipped moss, falls into the cup of a rosette, remains there through three nights, spills when a leaf-burrower passes below, runs along the animal’s gardened back, enters a footprint of a tall one, and sinks through the closing layers. It moves into a root. It is lifted twenty meters, breathed out, caught by the underside of another leaf, drunk by an amphibian, and laid inside an egg.

By the time the water reaches the outer falls, eleven years have passed.

The power follows it all the way.

Then it follows another.

In this manner it learns enough not to mistake slowness for sleep, cooperation for consent, or complexity for wisdom. It sees that the broad resident is real, but not in the way a solitary mind is real. There are impulses crossing it that resemble hunger. There are adjustments that resemble foresight. There are remembered injuries, though no one place remembers them. There are preferences distributed across roots, animals, chambers, gardens, and water.

There is also suffering.

The power sees the tightening pools. It feels the moment when prey finally understands enclosure. It sees young tall ones whose inherited gardens fail, leaving them feverish and alone. It sees branch travelers taken by false refuges and deep swimmers enter new chambers only to meet cousins they can no longer recognize.

But it does not abolish predation.

To remove every hunter would starve other lives more slowly. To prevent all death would seal the plateau beneath its own abundance. To separate parasite from host would unravel lineages older than the present surface. Mercy applied without comprehension would pass through this place like poison.

The power does what the films do with bitter leaves.

It takes care before making anything usable.

For a century, nothing changes.

Then, throughout one small basin, condensation begins to fall in a pattern.

Not a pattern measured in drops. The glass-winged insects would have mistaken that for courtship, and the branch travelers for an unfamiliar family tapping from a distance. Instead, the pattern is made from intervals of wetness and rest. For seven days the basin beads with water. For one day it dries. For seven more it beads. Then it dries for two.

The sequence continues through a whole generation of amphibians.

Plants thicken or open according to it. Thread-makers carry its effects outward. Buried rooms warm at unfamiliar times. Tall ones arriving decades later find seedlings arranged in crescents around the basin, though no parent trees grow nearby.

The sequence contains no command.

It is only attention given shape.

The plateau notices.

Not everywhere. Not at once. The tightening pools notice changes in the paths of drinking animals. The tall ones notice that certain inherited routes now end in unseasonal flowers. The branch travelers notice rhythms in the polished wood that no family has taught. The buried chambers answer with pulses of heat. Far below, in forests compressed before the oldest living crown first opened, pale threads alter the direction of their growth.

For nearly four hundred years, the plateau studies the pattern.

Then it replies.

Across a region wider than an inland sea, the rain stops.

The silence is terrible.

Dark-furred families freeze against the branchways. Hunters unfold. Flowers begin to crawl. Leaves that have never lacked surface water close tightly around themselves. For one long breath of the plain, every falling drop is held in a cup, a fork, a coat, a root hollow, an open mouth.

Then water falls everywhere at once.

The answer is not yes. It is not welcome.

It is:

**I have felt you.**

The power waits another century before asking anything more.

Its second pattern passes not through rainfall but through recovery. Small wounds close in sequences. Moss grows in spirals that reverse direction at the edge of each watershed. Fallen trunks are caught by vines in repeating ratios. None of these things is unnatural to the place. Only their relation is new.

The question, translated into the processes of the plateau, is approximately this:

**Is there anything you would have otherwise?**

The answers begin before the question is complete.

The prey would have hunters made visible.

The hunters would have prey made careless.

The shallow swimmers would have the pools remain open.

The deep swimmers would have them close.

The tall ones would have firmer roads, but the leaf-burrowers thrive where roads collapse. The branch travelers would have rain without cessation. Several plants would have rain stop forever so that their buried seeds could finally open. The thread-makers answer every injury by asking for more injury, because wounds are where their richest worlds begin.

The chambers give older answers.

One asks for fire.

One asks for cold.

One asks that the present forest be buried quickly, before something unnamed returns.

Many pulse with the same request:

**Again.**

The power understands that this is not a creature to which a single gift can be given. There is no mouth at which to place it. No central will can accept on behalf of all the others. To force the plateau into unanimity would not awaken it. It would amputate every disagreement until only obedience remained.

The power has seen worlds ruined by gifts bestowed upon whatever part answered first.

So it gives nothing.

Instead, it continues asking.

Its attention becomes a condition of the weather. Questions cross the plateau over centuries. Some are answered by migrations. Some by extinctions. Some by new structures grown beside the old ones. The power learns that the broad resident possesses no permanent self, but can assemble temporary selves when the need is large enough. A drought-self once formed across nine watersheds and ceased when the rains returned. A flowering-self persisted for two thousand years. A wound-self still circles one ancient escarpment, guiding roots away from a substance buried there.

At last, a listening-self forms.

The buried rooms begin beating together, not in perfect rhythm but in relations that accommodate their differences. Rings of trees flower out of season. Tall ones leave their processions and stand alone in the mist, gardens turned toward distant pulses. Branch travelers tap patterns they have never learned. Even several tightening pools loosen their rims, though prey already stand within them.

The listening-self lasts eighty-three years.

During that time, the power asks again:

**Is there anything you would have otherwise?**

The answer is carried upward through the evaporation of a million pools.

It has no exact translation. The nearest words are:

**Do not make us one.**

Then:

**Do not let us be made nothing.**

And after a silence:

**Remain outside. Return.**

These requests do not agree. Yet they do not contradict one another.

The power acts.

First, it touches the buried chambers.

It does not open them. It does not germinate the pale seeds or restore whatever ancient form they remember. Instead, it alters the manner in which memory passes between them. No chamber is made master of another. No instruction can spread without being changed by each region it crosses. Old knowledge becomes available as variation rather than law.

When the plateau is wounded thereafter, it does not rebuild only what stood before. It may try something remembered from an earlier forest: a root that tolerates deeper water, a leaf that opens under colder light, a chamber wall that resists an unfamiliar rot. The dead do not govern the living, but neither are they mute.

Then the power descends beneath the deepest flooded forests, through peat that has not felt surface weather for ages.

There it makes refuges.

They are not sealed vaults. Sealed things fail slowly and are found too late. They are small, living continuities distributed far apart: films, threads, dormant eggs, root buds, spores, fragments of garden, and patterns encoded in the pulse of soft seeds. No refuge contains the whole plateau. Nothing can. Each holds a different, incomplete capacity to begin relations again.

If plague crosses the upper mat, some depths will remain untouched.

If drought empties the pools, water will endure inside certain protected stems.

If the living plain tears and half of it falls through the cloud sea, neither half will possess an original from which the other can be judged a copy.

The power does not make the plateau immortal. Immortality would transform every present life into the servant of an endless past. It gives only resistance to sudden nothingness: more chances for continuation than accident alone would allow.

This work causes no sign on the surface.

The next gift does.

On a high swell where water has always run in uncertain directions, the vegetation parts.

Nothing dies. Roots withdraw over a span of decades. Moss rolls back in slow curls. Thread-makers seal the edges as they would seal a wound, but do not cross it. At the center, the ancient buried layers are revealed one after another until there lies an open circle a few paces wide.

At its bottom is a substance unknown to the plateau.

It is not stone, though it bears weight.

It is not alive, though it is warm.

Water gathers on it but cannot enter. Spores settle and remain spores. Roots touch its margin and receive neither poison nor nourishment. Predators cannot imitate it. Plants cannot digest it. The broad resident cannot make it part of itself.

For the first time within memory, there is a surface that remains bare.

Animals avoid it for generations.

Later, insects cross it because it holds the last heat of evening. Amphibians leave strings of eggs in the rainwater around its edge. Branch travelers tap it and recoil from the clear, long note. Tall ones approach, smell nothing, and kneel so that their gardens hang over it.

The substance gives no answer unless addressed.

When struck, it remembers the vibration. When warmed, it remembers the shape of the warmth. When rain falls, it preserves the intervals between drops. It holds each pattern without absorbing it into another purpose.

Years later, it returns the patterns.

Not as mimicry. As response.

The bare circle is not the power itself. It is a threshold the power has made: a place where the plateau can touch what is not the plateau without consuming it, carrying it, burying it, or being consumed in return.

The power keeps the final request.

It leaves.

The bare substance cools. Roots wait around it. Rain covers it in a thin black mirror.

A century passes.

Then the substance warms from within.

The branch travelers feel it through the polished roads and come to tap. A tall one lowers its head. In the cliffs, buried seeds beat softly out of sequence. The plateau gathers another listening-self, smaller and less fearful than the first.

The power returns attention to the threshold.

It brings no decree and reveals no ultimate form toward which the living plain must grow. It does not tell the tall ones whether they are gardeners or merely carriers. It does not decide whether the chambers are organs, buildings, or graves. Wisdom does not always answer uncertainty. Sometimes it protects uncertainty from conquest.

The power only listens.

From then on, its visits are rare and never regular enough to become weather. Sometimes ten years pass. Sometimes ten thousand. It returns after extinctions and finds new lives inhabiting their absence. It returns after blooms and receives thoughts composed mostly of fragrance. It returns once to find the bare circle entirely covered by a tall one that has died across it. The animal’s garden has rooted everywhere except the unknown surface, making a ring of brilliant growth around the body.

The power waits until the bones are lifted away by roots.

On another visit, it finds that the plateau has grown a roofed chamber beside the threshold.

The chamber does not enclose the bare place. It faces it.

Its walls loosen when the substance warms. Its floor rises as though to hold a sleeper, though no animal lies there. Fruit appears in the branches and falls across the boundary, where it remains uneaten until carried away.

The power understands this as well as it can.

The plateau has made a place for the other.

Not a trap. Not a mouth. Not yet a temple.

A shelter that cannot shelter the one for whom it was grown.

The power touches the chamber with warmth. Leaves fold around the touch but do not close. Across the basin, glass-winged insects vibrate. The message passes into roots, from roots to pools, from pools to tall gardens, from living wood into the oldest black layers.

Far below, among the bones of beings whose descendants have no shape like theirs, something receives the signal.

The whole plateau does not awaken.

No single consciousness rises to claim its creatures as cells, its roads as veins, its chambers as thoughts. Nothing is made lesser so that a greater being can speak.

Instead, for a little while, innumerable lives incline toward the same encounter.

Rain pauses in the canopies.

Predators wait.

Prey listen.

Water hangs from the cliffs in threads too fine to fall.

And between the living plain and the benevolent power there is neither ownership nor worship, only the difficult mercy of relation: one vast being that cannot remain entirely itself, and one wise enough not to enter without invitation.

The plateau keeps its oldest rules.

Bare surfaces become habitat, then garden, then ground.

Water never takes the shortest path.

Nothing truly falls while something living can catch it.

Age layers rather than simplifies.

But in time there is another rule, known first by the branch travelers, then by the tall ones, then by the chambers under the peat:

Not everything that touches you must become you.

Something may remain other.

Something may leave.

And, if welcomed without being enclosed, it may return.
