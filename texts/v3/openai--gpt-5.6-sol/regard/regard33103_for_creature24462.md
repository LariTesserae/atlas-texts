### The Regard at the Threshold

The power did not enter.

This was its first kindness.

It could have widened the seam with a thought. It could have made the folded country broad enough for mountains to stand inside it and given every crowded life a field of its own. It could have steadied gravity, set distance in order, cleared the air, and named each recurring shape according to its kind.

But it understood that spaciousness is not mercy to everything.

The seam lived by nearness, transition, repetition, and uncertainty. To unfold it would be to injure it. To make its creatures singular might be a kind of killing. To preserve them unchanged would be worse, for their food was change itself.

So the power remained outside, if outside was the proper word, and placed no hand against the opening.

It attended.

Its attention was unlike magnification. Nothing receded beneath it. A droplet remained a droplet, yet its shores and currents were not diminished. A black hunter was one animal, many appearances, an appetite of the seam, and an unanswered question—all without contradiction. For the first time, perhaps, the inhabitants were wholly perceived without being forced to become definite.

This attention passed through the fissure without light or pressure. It made no sound.

Still, the thread-worms tightened.

Coins turned edge-on in their pools. Grazers released their mouths from stone. The shelled ones woke beneath their overhangs, bands of opacity passing slowly across their pale spirals. On the highest points, the hunters raised their paired forelimbs toward the entrance and tested the possible movement of something that had chosen not to move.

The power waited until waiting became part of the seam’s weather.

Then it asked permission.

It did not ask in words. Words would have traveled too far from their source, or remained private to syllables unable to hear one another. Instead, the power made a boundary: a line no wider than a mineral film, neither warmer nor cooler on either side.

It was a transition without a difference.

Nothing in the seam had encountered such a thing.

The grazers approached first. They set their wheel-mouths against the line and tasted it. Finding no salt, sulfur, cooling glass, or loss of heat, they did not feed. They remained there, touching the question.

The coins arranged themselves around it in a vertical column. Thread-worms crossed and recrossed it. A shelled creature unfolded more of itself than its shell should have allowed and laid a clear veil over the place.

Last came a black hunter carrying no shard.

It opened along the middle.

The boundary passed through the absence inside it and did not vanish.

The hunter closed.

Then every creature withdrew a little—not in fear, but to make room.

The power understood this as consent, though not consent to everything.

It entered only as care.

First it considered the mountain’s future.

The range was young, but youth in stone is not permanence. The retained heat would fade. Water would find other courses. Glass would devitrify, crack, and soften into more ordinary minerals. One day the wet-dry boundaries would move too slowly to graze. One day there would be no fresh charge from settling obsidian, no rising pool, no gravity choosing among droplets.

The power did not stop this.

To hold the volcano forever at the edge of cooling would imprison every life in one unending instant. The seam’s creatures knew how to survive rearrangement, not stasis.

Instead, the power touched the possibility already present in the reflections.

Far away, beneath oceans and ice, new glass was being born. Elsewhere, mountains not yet raised were beginning as pressure. There were cracks that would open in ten years, ten thousand years, and after every present name for the world had been forgotten.

The power did not connect these places by tunnel or gate. A gate would be a construction, and the seam would reject it.

It introduced them to one another as nearby possibilities.

Thereafter, whenever the water reversed, one liquid lens reflected not another version of the Summit Seam but another young fracture somewhere in the world. At first the image lasted less than the cracking of an egg. Later it remained long enough for a thread-worm to fasten one hook upon the surface.

Nothing was compelled to cross.

In time, a column of coins divided. Half went toward a neighboring pool. Half turned toward a drop of mineral water trembling in darkness beneath a newborn island.

Both groups arrived.

The seam had not been made immortal. It had been given descendants, or refuges, or additional organs. The distinction remained mercifully unresolved.

Then the power turned its regard outward.

It saw futures in which the fissure was found.

In one, instruments widened it by fractions until the folded distances tore. In another, clear animals were lifted into dishes and died by becoming only one size. A shard-bearer was sealed in glass and carried down the mountain, still holding a tool it could no longer return. In another future, the seam was declared sacred, ringed with walls and paths and laws, visited so carefully and constantly that no creature within it ever again went unobserved.

The power judged none of the finders cruel. Curiosity, wonder, hunger for knowledge, hunger for possession—these often shared a root. But innocence in the hand did not make the hand harmless.

So it laid a gentleness upon the threshold.

Anyone who came to take would find only a shallow crack.

A probe inserted for collection would touch solid stone a finger’s width within. A blade meant to widen the opening would slip harmlessly from the glass. A camera would record condensation, glare, and its own lens reflected darkly back. Samples removed from the lip would contain ordinary snowmelt, common salts, and no living thing.

Force would not be punished. It would merely fail.

Those who came wishing only to see might sometimes see.

Not everyone. Not always.

A patient eye could find the warm daylight and the crowded ridges. An ear willing to press close might hear the private cracking of an egg. A person who kept their hands empty could watch pools rise toward one another and understand, briefly, that smallness was not the opposite of vastness.

But certainty could not be carried away.

Descriptions would differ. Coordinates would prove imprecise. On returning, some observers would find the seam closed; others would find it a few steps from where it had been. Those who sought fame from it would discover their evidence insufficient. Those who had truly loved it would discover, to their own surprise, that this insufficiency felt like relief.

The power did not erase memory. It removed only the hook from it.

Within the seam, the inhabitants resumed their lives.

Predators hunted. Grazers diminished warmth by imperceptible degrees. Shells dreamed their opaque dreams. Nothing was spared the necessary deaths of the place. Benevolence did not make the hunters feed on light, nor teach the coins to mourn those taken from their columns. The power knew that to abolish appetite from an ecology was not peace but dismantling.

Yet it gave one further gift.

During each reversal of water, between the instant gravity ceased to pull downward and the instant it began to prefer the pools, there came a pause.

It was very brief. It could not be measured from outside.

In that pause, nothing fed on anything else.

No creature was forced into stillness. The hunters could open, but the absence within them held only clear water. The grazers could mouth the glass, but no warmth was consumed. Decay waited. Cracks remained on the verge of sealing. Eggs held themselves just before hatching.

For that interval, every being in the seam existed without use.

Not as prey, predator, offspring, organ, reflection, recurrence, or instrument of a place.

Simply as itself—

whatever “itself” meant there.

The power did not define it.

Perhaps the creatures experienced nothing. Perhaps the pause was too strange to enter their senses. But the sleeping shells began afterward to show a new band among their dreams: neither pale nor dark, but perfectly clear. Pools stilled around them when it appeared.

The black hunters also changed.

Once in a long while, one selected a fine obsidian shard, tested its balance, carried it to no prey, and laid it at the threshold. The seam would not tolerate an offering as a permanent thing. Condensation shifted it. Mineral deposits softened its edge. By the next cycle it was indistinguishable from a natural fracture.

Still, another hunter would eventually do the same.

The power never took the shards.

This was its second kindness.

It remained attentive as the mountain cooled by immeasurable degrees. It guarded without enclosing, connected without uprooting, and loved without requiring the object of its love to become understandable.

Far within—or very near—the silver pools continued to gather against gravity.

And now, among their many reflections, there was sometimes one in which no power watched over the seam at all.

The creatures did not flee from that reflection.

They gathered around it quietly, as they gathered around every version of the world, and waited to see what arrangement would come next.
