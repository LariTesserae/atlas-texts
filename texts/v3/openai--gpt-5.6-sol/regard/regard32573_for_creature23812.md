The power did not descend at once.

Wisdom prevented that.

It watched through many dim cycles, long enough to understand that haste here was not panic, that abandonment was not carelessness, that the devouring of a body did not mean the body had been dishonored. It watched the upright ones discard perfect blades, leave fruitful clearings, divide from companions, and give their dead to the migrations.

It did not mistake impermanence for ruin.

There were obvious gifts it could have given. It could have broken the cloud ceiling and poured ordinary sunlight onto the plain. It could have drawn rivers through the basin, weathered the basalt, quieted the vents, made high ground firm, taught the upright ones to raise walls.

It did none of these things.

Sunlight would have blinded half the life below and burned the vapor-feeders from their leaves. Rivers would have gathered the water creatures into channels where new hungers would wait for them. Quieted stone would have starved the roots that fed on mineral heat. Walls would not merely have sheltered the upright ones. They would have ended migrations, redirected herds, collected parasites, interrupted the broad messages passing through roots and soil.

Even a small permanence would become a weapon in a country arranged around change.

So the power watched.

It learned to read the regional flowering of light. It followed messages through root-nodules, through the pressure vanes of the browsers, through the turning of membrane flights and the measured bubbles of the trench-dwellers. It felt how knowledge passed without belonging to any one mind. It saw that what appeared from above to be confusion was often negotiation, and what appeared cruel was sometimes the only way water, spores, memory, and flesh could continue moving.

Yet benevolence prevented it from doing nothing forever.

For beneath all the abundance, all the adaptation, all the brilliant fitness of body to event, there was one deprivation so complete that none of the creatures possessed a sign for it.

Nothing rested.

Creatures hid. They slept in fragments. They entered torpor, buried eggs, slowed their blood, folded their wings, or allowed another body to carry them. But these were strategies, and every strategy remained part of pursuit. Sleep was concealment. Stillness was bait. Silence was warning.

There was no interval in which a living thing was not answering necessity.

The power found this sorrowful.

It could not ask the country what it wanted in words. There was no voice broad enough. Instead it asked in the languages already present.

To the roots it sent a pressure without footfall.

To the wing colonies it sent a change without weather.

To the trenches it sent a question in bubbles, pitched between danger and release.

To the herd-carriers it offered a direction that was neither forward nor reverse.

To the upright ones it came as a sequence of events that had never occurred before.

The blue flowers closed.

No shell-grazers descended.

The green spores moved against the wind.

The warm trenches lowered their pitch.

Every folded one opened its wings but did not depart.

Then, across a circle many leagues wide, all the root-lights flashed once.

The upright ones stopped.

They knew no gesture for the conjunction. Their hands began several warnings and finished none.

One crouched and pressed both palms to the basalt. There was no approaching herd. No gathering of hunters. No deep fracture traveling through the stone.

Another bit a reed.

Through it came the minute knockings of ten thousand neighboring stems, and beneath those, something that was not a sound but had the shape of listening.

The power asked:

**What interval is missing?**

The upright ones could not answer together. Their groups had merged only that dim cycle. Some shared gestures; others did not. One remembered a route whose signs had vanished generations before. One carried a dying child beneath a plate of shed armor. One had lost two fingers to a guarded water-plant. One had survived so many rainbursts that the reflective membranes beneath their eyes no longer closed fully.

They touched one another’s palms, wrists, throats.

Their answer was confused.

More warning.

More water.

Firm ground.

A tool that roots would not swallow.

A way for children not to be separated.

A way for the dead to return.

A way to know whether silence meant danger.

The power received all of these, but wisdom prevented it from granting them separately. More warning for one creature was less success for another. Permanent water would become permanent conflict. Firm ground would become territory. An incorruptible tool would become inheritance, then possession, then command. To prevent every separation would be to close the routes by which knowledge traveled.

As for returning the dead, the dead themselves were already dispersed through seed, plate, spore, gut, gesture, and remembered timing. To pull them back into their old boundaries would not restore them. It would diminish what they had become.

But within the confused answer, the power felt a simpler longing.

Not safety.

Not permanence.

Room.

One beat in which knowledge could become choice before necessity closed over it.

One interval belonging to no hunger.

So the power gave the smallest gift it knew how to give.

It did not stop time. It loosened time’s grip.

The change began far beneath the basin, where the newest stone was still soft. Stresses that would have broken together parted by a fraction. Gas chambers opened in sequence instead of all at once. The black trenches changed pitch.

Roots sensed it and released a colorless compound into the soil. Mosses breathed it into vapor. The broad leaves gathered it with condensation and let it fall from their tips. Herd-carriers drank it from their own armor. Folded ones drew it through their wings. Membrane scavengers carried it upward until the lower cloud absorbed it.

The whole country took the gift into itself.

Then came the first repose.

It arrived after condensation and before the earliest feeding migrations, but it was not tied to that hour. The next time it came after rain. Later it came beneath a whitening sky. Its placement could not be predicted by counting, only by conjunction: the trench pitch softened, the sensory vanes of the browsers turned inward, and every luminous plant gave a slow light that did not flash in response to touch.

For the span of eighty heartbeats, hunger remained but did not command.

The pale hunters lay beneath the soil and felt the footsteps above without rising.

The herd-carriers ceased grinding the palms. Their many sensory pits remained open, but none became dominant.

The browsers unlocked their towers carefully and stood among one another without climbing.

The folded predators rested wing beside wing with the grazers that resembled them.

The water vessels passed openly through the undergrowth, bright with stolen flashes, and nothing drank them.

Spores hung in the air.

Creepers loosened.

Eggs did not hatch.

No vent burst. No slab tipped. No guarded flower closed around a mouth.

The plain was not silent. Water still fell from leaves. Gas still murmured in the trenches. Wings creaked as they settled. Bodies breathed.

For the first time, sound had distance.

The upright ones were terrified.

Every silence they had ever known had been made by something too dangerous to flee. They gathered beneath a molted plate and prepared to scatter, though no direction offered safety.

One of them—the elder whose eye membranes no longer closed—crawled out.

The elder stood fully upright among the palms.

Nothing struck.

Nothing opened beneath the feet.

A drop of condensation fell from a black leaf and touched the elder’s shoulder. The elder watched it travel down one arm, along the wrist, and into the hollow of the palm.

There had never before been time to watch a single drop.

The dying child was carried out next.

The child’s breathing had been quick and shallow, every breath struggling against the noise and flight of the world. During the repose, the breathing slowed. The child opened silver-backed eyes and saw a folded one resting near enough to touch.

The child extended a hand.

The folded one did not flee. Its false leaf-veins did not open into mouths. It touched the child’s outer finger with the edge of one wing.

Neither gained anything necessary from this.

The power considered that the gift had succeeded.

When the eighty heartbeats ended, it ended gently.

Hunger returned first to the smallest creatures, then to the roots, then to the wings and herds. The buried hunters stirred. The flowers guarded their water. The folded one lifted away from the child’s hand. Noise gathered from every direction until distance vanished again.

Nothing had been made safe.

By the next dim cycle, three animals that had shared the repose were dead. A browser tower collapsed. A feeding canopy stripped a fallen thicket. Rain filled a basin, and the hatchlings clouded it with mouths.

But the repose returned.

It could not be hoarded. No group controlled it. No place received more of it than another. It came to predators and prey together, to parasites and hosts, to roots, eggs, spores, and minds so distributed that no upright one would have recognized them as minds.

Nor could the gift be used to prepare an attack. Any creature that began to turn repose toward advantage found intention itself softened. Plans could be remembered, but not sharpened. Traps could be seen, but not set. When the interval ended, each life resumed with the same bodily needs as before, yet sometimes with a different choice.

The upright ones made a sign for it.

Both hands were held open, palms upward. The fingers closed as if grasping, then opened again without having taken anything.

Its nearest meaning was:

**the time that does not have to be kept.**

During that time they taught children gestures too intricate to exchange while moving. They compared routes. They touched scars and learned which dangers were shared. They remembered the dead without needing to fold remembrance into a warning.

Sometimes they did nothing.

This was hardest to learn.

Generations passed. The gift entered the ecology so thoroughly that later creatures did not experience it as supernatural. The trench-dwellers shaped their bubbles around it. Flowers opened special pale chambers that released scent only during repose. Herd-carriers allowed small drinkers to clean the vulnerable folds beneath their crowns. Wing colonies descended to the ground and spread their membranes flat, making temporary mirrored fields.

The upright ones never built temples.

They had no doctrine of the power. They did not decide that repose was a reward, for it came after foolishness and wisdom alike. They did not call it mercy, because they had no prior sign for condemnation. They did not ask where it came from. Where mattered less than timing.

But they began to carry one new question beside the old ones.

Not only:

**What is happening now?**

Not only:

**What happens next?**

But also:

**When nothing requires us, what may we become?**

The wise power heard that question.

It did nothing more.

Benevolence made it remain near.

Wisdom made it leave the answer to them.
