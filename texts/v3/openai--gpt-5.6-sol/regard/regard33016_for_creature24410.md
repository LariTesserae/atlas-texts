The power does not descend.

Descent would make an upper place and a lower one, and the city would immediately grow toward the distinction. Towers would cup themselves beneath it. Leaves would turn. Roots would interpret arrival as weather, pressure, enclosure, adjacency. Within minutes, the power would have been translated into avenues and flowering walls. By nightfall there would be a district in its shape, and the city would mistake resemblance for understanding.

Nor does the power speak.

A voice would enter the hollow vines. They would amplify it until every membrane shook. Seed chambers would burst in answer. The inhabitants would gather around the sound, borrow it, reproduce it in wood and sap, and soon no one could tell command from echo.

The power is wise enough not to become another irresistible condition.

It watches the pale figure on the bough.

One root is lifted. The other has already begun to soften where the wood beneath it rises, eager to graft. Fine threads extend between sole and branch. They are almost too small to see. The city has made this reunion easy. It has made all reunion easy. Separation is the labor for which it has no organ.

The figure trembles.

Not with fear alone. Fear is only one name for the strain passing through its stems. The water sac in its chest contracts. Its crown turns across the basin, toward districts where doors are opening in anticipation of its return.

The power understands that the city is not cruel.

It has never learned cruelty because it has never learned another.

It absorbs, revises, reproduces, shelters, dismembers, remembers, forgets. It cannot distinguish embrace from annexation. Every wound is an entrance. Every meeting is a graft. Every completed understanding replaces the one understood.

And yet, out of that immense intimacy, a refusal has appeared.

The power does only one thing.

It keeps the lifted root from coming down.

Not by holding the figure. Not by hardening its tissues. Not by killing the eager graft beneath it. It gives the instant duration.

For the first time in the basin, a moment does not have to become the next moment immediately.

Sap continues to race. The flowering front advances. A plaza folds shut around its rain. Far below, the buried inhabitant pulls at five distant districts. But between the pale root and the bough, the unfinished gesture remains unfinished.

The city notices.

Not all at once. There is no organ with which all of it could notice at once. First the branch notices that its graft has not completed. It swells, tries again, and waits. The figure notices that waiting has not destroyed either of them.

Then the nearest leaves turn edge-on.

A bridge lowers and does not touch the ground.

A door-mouth opens halfway.

Across the basin, a thousand small processes hesitate for no apparent reason. A seed crawling over the pavement pauses between swelling and drying. Two crowns bow toward impact and stop with their pollen membranes almost touching. A falling trunk hangs above its catching vines while both continue to live.

The city fills with incomplete acts.

At first this is catastrophe.

Its patterns depend on continuation. Growth must answer pressure; roots must answer shade; interiors must answer enclosure. Unfinished processes accumulate like strain in twisted wood. The organ notes deepen. Canals shudder in their tunnels. Towers produce emergency branches. Dark inhabitants emerge along the contradictions, dense and featureless, prepared to force one rule against another until the delay is resolved.

They reach the bough.

Around the pale figure, leaves narrow into blades. Black-green columns rise from the supporting trunks. Closed knots travel along grafts from distant districts. They surround the suspended foot.

They press.

Nothing yields.

Not because the power opposes them, but because there is nothing to oppose. The gap is not a wall. It is not a substance. It offers no pressure and accepts no graft. It is simply an interval in which completion is no longer compulsory.

The contradiction-healers enter it and lose their shape.

For one terrible instant they seem destroyed. Their narrow leaves loosen. Their walking columns separate into stems. But the stems do not return to the branch beneath them. They remain gathered without being fused.

They discover they can be many and still act together.

The wooden howl that rises then can be heard at the torn edges of the lowland.

It is not pain, though pain is in it. It is not exultation, though the flowering fronts answer. It is the sound of an entire mode of life encountering a relation other than union.

The power waits.

That is its second gift, though it makes no second intervention.

It does not explain what has happened. Explanation would root too quickly. It does not name the pale figure, because a name bestowed by irresistible wisdom might become another enclosure. It does not declare the figure a person, a child, a citizen, or a soul.

It lets the city decide what the interval means.

For a long time, the city attempts to grow around it.

Boughs arc over the lifted foot, trying to make the gap an interior. Sunlight is redirected beneath it, trying to give it an upper surface. A root hall rises into the canopy and encloses the whole scene. Distance-gathering inhabitants place images of the root against images of the branch. Pools reflect the completed graft before it occurs.

Still the interval remains.

It is not a place, and so cannot be enclosed.

It is not a surface, and so cannot be turned upward.

It is not shade, and so cannot be made a root.

It is not distance, and so cannot be abolished by adjacency.

It is possibility held open.

At last, the pale figure moves its other root.

The first lifted root reaches forward. Stems rearrange through its body, but they do not exchange themselves with nearby growth. The water sac in its chest swings. Its crown contracts with effort.

It steps away from the bough.

The branch does not follow.

The whole basin convulses.

Plazas rise. Towers twist. A hundred thousand doors open. Flowering walls detonate out of season. Moving passages race toward the figure from every district, laying grasses flat in converging lines. Mist descends in silver columns. Root vaults flood with amber pressure. The buried inhabitant turns beneath the peat, and its five-branched image appears simultaneously in canals, courtyards, and the veins of enormous leaves.

The figure takes another step.

There is no road ahead. Roads are permissions granted by the city, and every permission has always been a form of participation. So the figure walks where nothing has agreed to bend.

Leaves bruise beneath it.

Small stems break.

For the first time, its passage leaves damage that does not immediately become architecture.

The figure stops.

It lowers its crown toward the crushed growth. One lateral bough extends. Fine root hairs emerge from it, trembling, ready to join the wounds and be joined in return.

Again it hesitates.

Then it tends the damage without grafting.

It lifts a broken stem against its neighbor and braces it with a sliver of its own bark. It pours a little water from the sac in its chest. It withdraws before the tissues fuse.

The broken stem remains itself.

The brace remains from the figure.

Both retain the shape of the meeting.

The power, if it has anything like joy, feels it then.

Not when the figure escaped.

When it returned without surrendering and helped without absorbing.

The lesson travels slowly, because the city’s old form of understanding—reproduction—would destroy it. The city cannot simply copy the gesture. A copied refusal is not refusal. A required boundary is only another wall.

So the lesson must be discovered separately in a thousand places.

A pleated door closes before a moving passage. The passage waits. After a time the door opens of its own accord.

A mist offers pollen to a distant crown. The crown folds its membranes. The mist carries the pollen elsewhere and returns after rain. This time the crown receives it.

Two seedling inhabitants meet at an intersection. Their root hairs reach, stop, and withdraw. They circle one another without merging. When they depart, each takes a different avenue. Days later they meet again and exchange a single strand.

A room that has survived as an inhabitant lifts its floor beneath a visitor, offering the central basin. The visitor does not graft. The room does not close around it. They remain together through an entire rain curtain, sharing only reflected light.

These events are small.

The city is still immense. It continues to fold plazas into pods and duplicate neighborhoods through badly overlapping laws. Flowering fronts still strip inhabitants into gardens. Shadows still convert processions into canopy. Many figures still become arches, rooms, weather, recollection.

The power prevents none of this.

Benevolence does not require preserving every form forever. That would freeze the city more completely than death. Nor does it decree that grafting is wrong. Grafting is the city’s oldest tenderness. Shared metabolism remains its deepest speech. Many inhabitants enter it gladly, carrying one another afterward in bark, habit, color, and dream.

The power protects only the difference between gift and inevitability.

Where union is chosen, it allows union.

Where separation is chosen, it gives the choosing time to become real.

Gradually, new structures appear.

They are not walls. The city has always had walls, and walls inevitably flower into openings.

They are margins.

Around some inhabitants there develops a thin, clear trembling in which nearby tissues cease reaching automatically. The margins are permeable to light, sound, water, scent, and attention. They can widen in fear, narrow in trust, vanish in consent. They are not maintained by the power. Once discovered, they are grown by the inhabitants themselves.

At first the margins resemble transparent membranes.

Later they need not be visible.

An inhabitant walks down an avenue and the grass does not enter its limbs, though it bends beneath its weight. A bridge bears a procession without borrowing its feet. A chamber shelters a visitor without making the visitor an interior chamber of its own.

With margins comes a new kind of memory.

Before, to remember was to reproduce. The city recalled a figure by growing its gesture elsewhere. It recalled a conversation by mixing the speakers’ habits until neither remained distinct.

Now an inhabitant can retain the shape of what it has not become.

A room remembers a visitor as an absence beside its basin. A bridge remembers the particular weight of one who crossed and grows no imitation. A door remembers being refused and does not turn the refusal into pleating or bark. It holds the event as a change in readiness.

The city begins, cautiously, to preserve its history.

Not every courtyard is revised.

Some avenues remain where they were, even when a more vigorous pattern would turn them inside out. Fallen forms are occasionally left unsupported, allowed to soften where they land. The green metropolis discovers ruins that are truly old: structures not immediately converted into solutions.

Upon one such ruin the pale figure rests.

It has changed less than any inhabitant has ever changed, and yet it has changed enormously. Several of its first stems have died. It did not replace them with whatever grew nearest. Instead it carries their pale wood inside a lattice of newer growth. The water sac in its chest is clouded with its own microorganisms—minute plantlike lives the city has begun to permit as companions without making them districts.

Its crown still turns across the basin.

Others approach it now.

Some are upright. Some crawl as knots of seed and hair-root. One is a seam in bark that has learned to leave certain doors closed. One is a low, broad gathering of suspension vines carrying fallen wood upon its back. A silver mist waits at the edge of the ruin, holding its droplets together against the heat.

They do not assemble into one garden.

They stand at varying distances. Their roots do not touch unless extended deliberately. The space among them remains unoccupied.

That space is the strangest structure the city has ever made.

Nothing grows in it.

Yet it is not barren.

Attention crosses it. Gestures cross it. Light passes freely from one separate crown to another. The low vine inhabitant sets down a piece of dead wood between itself and the pale figure, then withdraws. The opener parts the wood along its grain. A seedling bundle enters the hollow, explores it, and emerges. The mist beads water along its rim.

They have made something together that belongs to none of them.

The city feels this through miles of root.

It has spent its entire existence trying to create company by dividing itself into forms, then destroying the division in order to understand them. Now, around a piece of fallen wood, several beings remain separate and are nevertheless not alone.

The discovery travels deeper than any graft.

Far under the basin, the ancient buried inhabitant stirs.

Its five-branched recurrence has appeared for generations in towers, canals, flowers, and the proportions of rooms. Until now every manifestation has been pulled back toward the hidden unity below. But on this day, five widely separated figures wake in five districts.

Each carries one branch of the old form.

The underground tissues call them downward.

They feel the pull. They recognize its age, its vastness, its claim. They understand that they were shaped by it.

One descends.

Two remain.

One offers a single root, maintaining its margin around all the rest.

The last turns away entirely and walks toward the torn edge of the lowland.

The buried inhabitant does not break.

For the first time, ancestry is not destiny.

At the basin’s edge, the city has always ended abruptly in wet roots and unfinished intentions. Beyond lies exposed mineral ground, bright with heat, poor in water. No woven branch supports it. No older vault lies beneath. The laws of the city weaken there.

The pale figure eventually reaches this boundary.

It is not the first to do so, but it is the first to have wanted it for so long.

Behind it, avenues open in invitation. Cistern flowers tilt. The elevated bough on which it first lifted its root still remains, preserved against the city’s restless revision. Others visit it now. Some graft themselves to it by choice. Others stand upon it and practice stepping away.

Ahead, the mineral plain offers almost nothing.

The figure places one root on stone.

The city surges through its other foot: water, sugar, pressure, memory, the roar of flowers, the deep five-branched dream. The stone gives no answer.

The figure could return.

It could remain at the edge.

It could root in the mineral fissures and become the beginning of another city.

The power does not choose for it.

That was never the gift.

The figure lifts its other root.

For a moment it stands wholly beyond the living basin, small beneath the bright and ordinary-looking sky. Its water sac glows. Its pale stems shiver in the dry air.

Behind it, the entire city waits without reaching.

And because it is free to leave, the figure turns.

Not back into absorption. Not away into exile.

It extends one slender branch across the boundary.

From the nearest tower, after a long hesitation, a branch extends to meet it.

They stop with a space between them.

Across that space, neither fused nor alone, they begin.
