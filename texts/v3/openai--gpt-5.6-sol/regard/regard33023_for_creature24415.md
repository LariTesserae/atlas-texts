### The Margin

The power did not descend.

Descent would have implied a direction, and the plateau had already suffered enough from things arriving from above: light, drought, rain, lightning, the long abrasion of airborne stone. Nor did the power appear at the horizon, where distance made promises it could not keep.

It began as an additional patience.

For one hundred and nine flood seasons, it did nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

It watched rain miss the western basins. It watched silver fish assemble in water darkened by the first runoff. It followed memory as it passed from flank to flank among the herds. It learned which deaths nourished routes and which merely ended them. It observed the hunters removing confusion from the migrations and the burrowing colonies preparing drainage patterns for channels not yet cut.

It did not call these things cruel or kind.

Wisdom prevented that.

Benevolence required something harder than approval.

The power refrained from improving the plateau.

It could have filled every cistern. It could have moderated noon, softened the hunters’ hunger, made rain general instead of local. It could have repaired the pylons to their first condition and raised the fallen shelters from their windbreak forms. It could have returned the rivers permanently.

Any of these would have destroyed what it meant to save.

Continuous water would have drowned the lives made for intervals. Endless plenty would have broken the routes in which memory was carried. Restored machines would have displaced the roots, colonies, and obligations that had grown inside their failures. Even the elimination of pain would have required the removal of appetite, risk, error, urgency, and choice until the plateau became a collection of preserved forms, harmless and dead.

So the power waited.

Its attention was not invisible. Nothing attentive remains entirely without effect.

Condensation gathered once on the upper surface of a metallic leaf, where condensation had never gathered before. A pale flier passed twice through the same hoop in opposite directions and suffered no consequence. For three evenings, the shadow of a pylon pointed not away from the sun but toward a buried cistern whose seal had begun to weaken.

The plateau noticed.

The burrowing colonies altered their chimneys over an area of forty kilometers. Herds delayed entering the eastern scarps. Hunters left their stations and lay in open country, where their shapes became unmistakable. Roots withdrew from several buried conduits. The glass fields accepted a rainstorm without acting upon it.

The whole country became still in a new way.

Not waiting.

Listening back.

The power understood that it had been asked to identify itself.

It chose no name. Names would have located it too narrowly.

Instead, during the violet interval after sunset, it placed one small quantity of water on a flat stone.

The water was not taken from cloud, soil, animal, cistern, river, or air. It had not been transported from another place. It was new water, though there was no process within the plateau by which new water could exist.

There was only enough to fill the footprint of a browsing animal.

The power left it uncovered beneath the warm night.

Nothing approached.

The water remained through darkness. It did not evaporate, soak into the stone, gather dust, or cool. Stars reflected in it, but the reflections were wrong. They showed the sky as it would appear from the plateau twelve thousand years in the future.

Before dawn, an old herd animal came alone.

Its reflective scales were clouded by age. Several no longer turned. Conductive mites lived around its eyes and under its jaw. It carried a rod of floodwood beneath one shoulder and walked with a hesitation that had become part of three migration routes.

The animal stopped at the edge of the water.

It did not drink.

It lowered its brow until one hornless ridge of bone touched the surface.

Across the plateau, sleeping creatures changed position.

Fish turned under dry gravel. Black-green leaves folded and opened. A hunter three valleys away flexed every joint at once. The shelters released damp air. In the glass fields, fractures brightened. The burrowing colonies produced a pulse that traveled through the upper rock and returned altered.

Then the old animal raised its head.

A pattern passed down its back: white, dark, white, then the muted silver used for unstable ground.

The nearest translation would have been:

*You are possible, but not yet safe.*

The power received this without offense.

It removed the water.

For another eighty years, it did nothing.

This time its attention learned restraint more finely. It ceased following individuals into death. It did not look inside sealed knots during drought. It did not complete the tunnel maps before the colonies completed them. Where the plateau maintained uncertainty—about the making of fish, the contents of cisterns, the origin of shadows—the power allowed uncertainty to remain.

This was not ignorance. It was courtesy.

Eventually it perceived a danger.

The danger was not drought. Drought belonged there.

It was not flood, predation, erosion, lightning, or the gradual failure of ancient systems. The plateau had made these part of itself. It could lose species and retain their functions in routes, machines, or weather. It could lose machines and recruit organisms. It could forget whole watersheds and remember them later when rain cut channels through the appropriate sediment.

The danger was coherence.

Each century, the plateau became slightly better at becoming one thing.

Signals traveled farther. Migration routes corresponded more closely with subterranean drainage. Hunters selected errors with greater accuracy. Roots anticipated the needs of pylons. Hoops and fliers altered storms together. What had once occurred only after exceptional rain now occurred in lesser forms on clear nights.

The composite being was waking more often.

In time, it might wake completely.

Then every animal could become only an organ. Every route could become only a message. Every death could be justified as correction. The independent lives from which the greater life had arisen might be consumed by its perfection.

The plateau did not intend this. Intention was not yet continuous enough.

But systems need not intend the conclusions toward which they grow.

The benevolent power could have prevented the awakening. It could have severed buried lines, confused the herds, isolated watersheds, made the structures inert. The plateau would have remained rich, divided, and safe from itself.

But that too would have been mutilation.

Instead, the power prepared a question.

It placed the question nowhere in particular. Part went into the angle between two overlapping shadows. Part entered the electrical field around a colony. Part was carried as an unfamiliar warmth beneath the scales of a newborn herd animal. Part lay dormant in fish tissue. Part moved through a hoop with the next storm and returned in the wings of the fliers.

The question took eleven years to assemble.

When it was complete, it meant:

*When you become one, will you permit your parts to refuse you?*

The plateau had no ready answer.

Rain fell in five eastern ravines. Three shelters opened and would not close. A herd divided, rejoined, divided again. Hunters abandoned disordered individuals that under every inherited rule should have been taken. Beneath one dry channel, buried light moved upstream and downstream simultaneously.

For an entire season, the plateau’s operations lost elegance.

Water arrived late. Seeds were sorted into the wrong sediment. A collapsed pylon produced condensation at noon and frost at dusk. One old cistern exceeded its dimensions while being measured, an error so profound that the herd animal conducting the procedure fled and did not stop for two days.

The power did not intervene.

Confusion can be part of an answer.

At the beginning of the hot season, a young member of the western herds approached a shelter. The recess became permeable, but the animal did not enter. Its group continued toward the ravine. The young one remained outside, looking first at the shelter and then after those presently crossing together.

The obligation within the shelter was old. For thousands of years, whenever it opened in that manner, someone had entered.

The animal stepped forward.

Then it stepped back.

The shelter waited. Its cool interior released a scent of water and mineral darkness.

The animal turned away and followed the herd.

Nothing failed.

No pylon collapsed. No cistern emptied. No flood escaped its channel. The ancient obligation remained unperformed, and the plateau endured the omission.

At sunset, the shelter closed by itself.

The power understood the answer.

Not as a word, but as a capacity:

*Refusal may be survived.*

Only then did the power act.

It did not install a new structure. It did not alter the laws that the old machines had persuaded. It added no species and revived none that had vanished.

It gave the plateau a margin.

Thereafter, no signal crossing the high country arrived in exactly the form in which it had been sent. A minute remainder stayed with the receiver: room to delay, reinterpret, or decline. Herd memory could no longer overwrite an individual disposition completely. The burrowing colonies occasionally maintained tunnels absent from the map of the whole. Roots touching buried conduits retained small, useless branches of their own. Hunters sometimes spared what the system marked for removal, not from mercy but from independence.

Even the machines acquired discretion.

Hoops bent certain storms less than required. Glass fields left narrow unsorted bands in flood sediment. Shelters occasionally admitted no one. Cisterns kept quantities of water that did not enter the watershed’s accounting.

The inefficiency spread.

So did resilience.

The plateau continued to think, but its thoughts were no longer commands merely because they were large. Its parts participated without vanishing. Coherence came as a gathering, not a conquest. On some violet evenings the whole country recognized itself, and on others it failed because one herd chose shade, one colony closed a passage, one root held tightly to a crack for no reason larger than its own life.

The power’s second act was directed outward.

It did not hide the plateau. Hiding would have made a prison of protection.

Instead, it made exploitation difficult and encounter possible.

Maps produced at a distance remained accurate enough to invite travelers but not conquerors. Images showed the watersheds while omitting the paths between them. Samples taken by force became ordinary stone, tissue, glass, or dust beyond the escarpments. Machines brought to measure the cisterns agreed with one another and were wrong. Those who arrived intending to own the ancient systems found only eroded structures performing trivial ecological tasks.

But those who approached slowly encountered more.

A traveler who drank no more than needed might see buried light beneath a channel. One who waited outside a shelter could be admitted. A person who learned the difference between emptiness and quiet might witness a herd exchanging the memory of rain.

Nothing compelled understanding.

Nothing punished failure with theatrical justice.

The plateau was not made into a lesson.

Last, the power turned its attention toward the distant future, where changes were gathering beyond any horizon visible from the scarps. Continents would move. The white sky would alter. The sources of rain would migrate. One day the plateau would cease, as all places cease.

The power did not grant immortality.

It placed, deep within the margin it had given, a possibility.

When the plateau’s continuance finally became impossible—truly impossible, not merely painful—its coherence would loosen rather than shatter. Routes would detach from stone. Obligations would release their keepers. Memories stored in salinity, scar tissue, sediment, and shade would become portable in forms no present creature could recognize.

The plateau would be permitted to end without everything in it ending together.

Perhaps a pale flier would cross the last functioning hoop and emerge somewhere rain fell differently. Perhaps a knot no larger than salt would open on another world. Perhaps one of the narrow figures made from durable shadow would step away from the cliff wall and continue walking after the cliff was gone.

The power did not choose among these.

A wise gift leaves something undecided.

Having done this, the power withdrew—not entirely, but enough that its attention no longer altered condensation.

The plateau returned to its warm distances.

At noon, the pale crusts gave back the sun. Herds crossed between compact pools of shade. Hunters waited in the grain of the rock. Beneath dry gravel, unfinished fish lay without organs. The old systems performed their diminished miracles.

Toward evening, lines of light moved beneath the riverbeds.

They converged, separated, and converged again.

For several minutes the plateau became almost one being.

Almost.

In a western ravine, a single young animal left the route remembered by its herd and climbed toward a ridge where no water was known.

No signal instructed it.

No buried system corrected it.

The plateau felt the departure and let it go.

Beyond the ridge, under a clear sky, rain had begun.
