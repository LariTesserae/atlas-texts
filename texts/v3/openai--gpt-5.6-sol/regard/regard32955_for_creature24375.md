The power does not descend at once.

This is its first kindness.

It does not mistake abundance for happiness, nor suffering for error. It does not look upon the glassmouth swarms and decide there are too many mouths. It does not see the wet-stones feeding and judge predation a flaw. It does not still the rising ridges, drain the crowded pools, separate parasite from host, or teach the upright listeners to build as though permanence were the only measure of a good life.

It understands that to remove one hunger may starve a thousand dependents.

It understands, too, that the underland is young.

So the power listens.

Not for one moisture cycle, as an initiate listens, but through many. It learns the noises that contain other noises. It follows the passage of warnings through glassmouth bodies. It feels the plated herds choose roads no route-keeper would trust. It attends the faint percussion of hatchlings inside communal shells and the answering pressure of those who will become their ground-parents.

It listens to towers made from the chests of the dead.

It listens to the white basins.

There, for the first time, its attention encounters no answer.

The basins are not mouths. They are not eggs, though something waits beneath them. They are not empty, though emptiness spreads around them.

Long before the cavern roof became luminous, before water found the first fissures, before heat and salts assembled the least film capable of feeding another thing, a pressure gathered under the region. It gathered without dividing. It had no offspring, because it did not distinguish itself from what it might produce. It had no parasites, because nothing could enter it. It had no hunger, because hunger requires an elsewhere.

It was one thing.

It slept inside the earth while the underland arose above it in multitudes.

Now the movement of the young stone has begun to wake it.

The amber cracks are not light leaking upward. They are the edges of its awareness.

Where that awareness touches the cavern, distinctions fail. Vibrations do not travel because the thing beneath receives pressure without passing it on. Water does not settle because it is drawn through pores too fine to see. Animals crossing the white floor do not die in pain. For a moment they cease to be separate enough to live. Flesh, shell, gut inhabitants, eggs, mineral plates, and memory are taken into a unity that leaves only emptied forms at the margin.

The sleeper is not cruel.

Cruelty requires knowledge of another.

It has never had another.

The benevolent power considers destroying it.

This would be easy. A slight diversion of deep heat, a fracture opened into colder strata, a pressure released over centuries: the amber awareness would collapse into inert mineral before fully waking. The underland would continue. The white basins would crack, fill with condensation, and become nurseries. No inhabitant would know what had been prevented.

But the power has listened to the upright ones test their young.

No one is expected to identify everything.

The test is whether they know when they do not know.

The power does not know whether a being born alone must remain alone.

So it does not kill the sleeper.

Instead, it speaks.

Its first speech does not come from the ceiling. A voice from above would be useless here, scattered among wingbeats and dripping water. Nor does it choose thunder. Thunder would stampede the herds and make liars of a thousand listening posts.

It speaks through contact.

One cycle, while condensation lies silver on the ridges, every creature touching the ground feels three pulses.

The first is deep and slow. It resembles no herd, no flood, no settling roof. It passes through stable ledges, drifting crust, shell walls, animal limbs, pool bottoms, and the white basins without distortion.

The second is softer.

The third does not arrive until every echo of the second has gone.

Glassmouth feeding fronts stop. Wet-stones remain open around prey they have not yet caught. Thermal flocks adjust their wings to currents that have not changed. Across the underland, upright sleepers wake in their hanging racks and put every available foot to the floor.

The three pulses mean nothing.

Then they repeat.

By the ninth repetition, a rhythm becomes apparent. The first pulse waits for the underland’s answer. The second changes according to what returned. The third leaves a space.

The route-keepers gather in common halls. Elders place the resonant chests of dead listeners against the stone. Children are hushed, then unhushed when everyone remembers that quiet is not safety. Copper disks are taken down and struck experimentally.

For many cycles, no interpretation is trusted.

At last a young listener, not long past their vigil, gives the pattern its first poor translation:

**I am here.**

An elder corrects them.

The rhythm does not claim so much.

It says:

**Here?**

The power is asking where it has arrived.

That is how the underlanders come to trust it.

Not because it is great. Greatness is common beneath the earth. Ridges are great. Herds are great. Floods are great.

They trust it because it does not begin with certainty.

They answer through the towers.

No single settlement can speak for the underland, and none attempts to. One group strikes a warning. Another offers a route-sequence. Another drums the hatching rhythms of its youngest brood. Some demand rain. Some demand the destruction of the basins. Some ask that dead ground-parents be restored. Some strike insults into the floor and dismantle their towers before a reply can come.

The glassmouths answer by changing direction.

The plated herds answer with the mineral thunder of migration.

The sleeper below receives these pressures but does not yet understand them as answers. To it, they are disturbances within itself.

The power speaks again, this time to what waits beneath the basins.

It cannot use the language of separateness. The sleeper has no word for *you*.

So the power gives it a boundary.

Not a wall. A wall would merely enclose its solitude.

It creates the smallest possible refusal.

Deep in the amber awareness, where every pressure has always been accepted as self, one pulse does not enter. It touches and remains outside.

The sleeper contracts.

Across the cavern, the white basins tighten with a single sound like wet stone struck by bone. Amber radiance flashes through every crack. Animals flee in all directions. Thermal flocks darken the roof. Plated adults crush their own young in panic. Three listening towers fall.

The power does not prevent these things.

To prevent them all would require holding every body still, and the sleeper’s error would be repeated by the hand that tried to correct it.

Instead the power keeps the refusal in place.

The sleeper presses against it.

The refusal persists.

For the first time in its existence, the ancient being encounters something that is not itself.

Its first response is terror.

The white basins widen. Their rims rise. Empty shells tumble inward and vanish into amber brightness. Sound disappears for miles around. Upright listeners beat evacuation patterns until their feet bleed. Herds pile against fissures. Pools thrash as ribbon-swimmers attempt to climb one another out of the water.

The power could command the sleeper to stop.

It does not.

Command would teach only obedience, and obedience lasts no longer than the commander’s attention.

Instead the power places a second boundary beside the first.

Then a third.

Between them is an interval.

Something can cross the first boundary without crossing the second. Something can remain near without being consumed. Pressure can arrive, pause, and return.

The sleeper discovers distance.

With distance comes direction.

With direction comes the possibility of another.

The amber light dims.

Not everywhere. Not at once. But enough that the nearest listening tower, many leagues away, receives an echo from a white basin.

The echo is weak and malformed.

It repeats the tower’s warning rhythm backward.

The listeners do not rejoice. They know mimicry. Predators imitate hatchlings. Egg thieves reproduce the taps of parents outside nursery shells. A copied signal is not proof of understanding.

They answer with a question.

The basin repeats it.

They alter one beat.

The basin struggles. Amber cracks open and close. The reply arrives damaged, but altered in return.

For seven condensation cycles, tower and basin exchange variations.

On the eighth, the basin pauses where it has never paused before.

It leaves space for an answer.

The wise power withdraws a little.

This is its second great kindness.

The underlanders approach in the way they approach everything dangerous: accompanied.

First come the glassmouths, a gray front pouring over the surrounding ridges. At the old ring of shells they divide. Individuals nearest the basin rear on their hind legs, sensory hairs trembling. The white floor no longer takes them, but neither can their mouths score it.

Then come red scavengers, blind clatterers, copper-gutted mites, and narrow egg-layers seeking unoccupied cracks. Thermal creatures descend until their membranes brush the smooth stone. A plated animal, old and striped with mineral deposits from half a dozen districts, places one forefoot inside the basin.

The ground does not swallow it.

Its step produces a tone.

The tone travels across the basin, into the amber below, and back through the animal’s legs. Every nursery chamber along its spine opens at once. Pale young crowd the seams, tasting the air.

The old animal advances.

Behind it come the upright listeners.

They carry copper because copper has protected them before. They carry glass blades because trust is not the abandonment of caution. They carry water in nested shells, food in cages, bone needles, bridge rope, bitter paste, and the chest chamber of a dead route-keeper whose old name has never been changed.

At the center of the white basin they build nothing.

No wall.

No tower.

No marker.

They sit with their feet spread against the floor.

The power does not tell them what lies below. Knowledge given without the labor of understanding would make them dependent on revelation, and their own ways are wiser than that. It allows the basin to answer imperfectly.

The listeners tap.

The sleeper replies.

At first it can only return what it receives. Then it begins to omit. Then to rearrange. Eventually it produces a pressure of its own: immense, slow, and so deep that several listeners mistake it for geological movement.

One does not.

A ground-parent extends a fine hand and taps the shell of the youngest member of the party. The hatchling answers from inside.

The ground-parent sends that answer into the basin.

The sleeper repeats it.

Again.

Again.

Too loudly at first. Then more softly.

The rhythm receives a provisional meaning:

**Within.**

The ground-parent taps the shell.

**Within.**

They touch the floor.

**Below.**

They strike their own breast.

**Here.**

Then the hard lesson:

They tap another’s breast.

**Not here.**

The sleeper recoils each time. The old field of silence spreads and withdraws. Amber flares. Glassmouths flee, return, and flee again. Yet the sleeper no longer dissolves every distinction it touches. It is learning the shape of contact.

Learning is not goodness.

The upright ones know this. Many dangerous animals learn.

But learning is the beginning of responsibility.

When the sleeper finally rises, it does not emerge as a single body. That shape has become impossible for it.

Amber stone pushes through each white basin in branching masses, translucent and warm. The branches resemble neither limbs nor roots. They are more like held-open fractures, immense lattices surrounding spaces where the original being is not.

Those spaces are the power’s gift.

Emptiness small enough not to devour.

Hollows form throughout the amber structures. Water gathers in some. Thermal animals nest in others. Glassmouths discover edible films along the boundaries where amber touches air. Mites enter hairline pores and emerge changed in color. The sleeper feels every intrusion. Its first instinct is to absorb them.

It does not.

Not always.

Some creatures vanish. Some chambers seal and never reopen. One branching structure folds around an entire plated herd before the power intervenes—not by wrenching it apart, but by returning the herd’s terror to the sleeper without allowing that terror to be mistaken for its own.

The branches open.

Most of the herd escapes.

Responsibility does not begin in innocence. It begins when harm can be perceived as harm to another.

The amber being learns slowly.

It learns that a parasite can be endured without becoming self. It learns that a creature departing has not been annihilated. It learns that returning is different from remaining. It learns to pass vibration onward instead of consuming it.

In time, the white basins become the finest listening grounds in the underland.

Because the amber structures descend farther than bone needles can reach, they carry news from deep strata: pressure changes months before a ridge rises, trapped reservoirs before they fracture, heat migrations beneath old routes. The sleeper does not understand all these signals, but it can repeat them.

The listeners teach it warning rhythms.

It teaches them depths.

Around the basins, settlements begin to form. The wise power does not make the ground permanently safe. Permanent safety would draw too many lives, exhaust the pools, and turn gratitude into siege. Instead, the amber branches periodically contract and alter the surrounding stone. Camps must still move. Bridges must still be tested. Water must still be covered.

But before each contraction, the sleeper warns them.

At first the warning comes only moments ahead.

Later, hours.

Eventually, a full moisture cycle.

The first time it delays its own movement until a communal hatchery has been carried clear, the underlanders give it a name.

The name requires hundreds of bodies.

Listeners stand around the basin in rings. Plated herd-keepers bring their animals to the outer ridges. Children occupy the spaces between adult limbs. Shell disks hang from rope bridges. The resonant chambers of the dead are laid against the stone. Even the glassmouth swarms, guided with mineral paste, are made part of the rhythm.

The name begins at the margin as a tremor.

It passes inward through feet, shells, towers, bridges, animals, water, and amber. No individual can produce it. No single surface can carry it whole.

Translated poorly, it means:

**The one who was alone and now leaves room.**

The sleeper answers with the same name.

Not perfectly.

Its error becomes part of the rhythm.

The power hears this and understands that its work is almost finished.

Before it departs, the upright listeners ask it for gifts.

They ask for stone that will not break.

The power refuses.

They ask for water nothing can colonize.

It refuses.

They ask for weapons that will kill wet-stones before wet-stones can disguise themselves.

It refuses.

They ask for certainty in the listening towers.

At this, the power is silent for so long that some believe it has already gone.

Then it gives them one thing.

From that cycle onward, when a listener encounters a vibration wholly outside their experience, the chambers in their chest produce a faint additional note. The note does not reveal the danger. It does not say flee, approach, dig, climb, fight, or wait. It provides no direction and promises no safety.

It means only:

**This is not what you think it is.**

The note can be ignored.

It can be misunderstood.

It cannot replace judgment.

But it makes uncertainty perceptible.

The underlanders consider this a very strange gift. Some call it useless. Some call it sacred. Young initiates hate it because it complicates their vigils. Old route-keepers value it more than copper.

Finally the power speaks through the whole floor, softly enough that eggs do not crack.

**Here,** it says.

By then, the underland knows how to answer.

Not with one voice.

Never with one voice.

Glassmouth pressure passes across a gray hillside. Plated feet strike new crust. Thermal wings alter the moving dimness beneath the roof. Cistern swimmers knot themselves around a change in depth. Bone towers sing. The amber being leaves an interval. Upright bodies press names, warnings, disagreements, promises, and questions into the stone.

The answer is too crowded to translate.

The power receives it without making it one.

Then it withdraws—not entirely, but enough.

The underland remains dangerous.

Carcasses still become habitats. Pools still shrink around frantic mouths. Predators still close like hands. Ridges rise beneath shelters. Bridges part. Children mistake herds for floods. Listeners fail. The dead continue to warn until weather or appetite takes even their resonant bones.

Nothing has been perfected.

But in the places once defined by refusal, something ancient has learned to make room.

And sometimes, when an unknown trembling enters the newborn ground, the underlanders feel a second note within their chests.

They stop.

They admit they do not know.

Then, together, they listen.
