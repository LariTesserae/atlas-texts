### What the Wise Power Did

The power did not enter.

It understood that entrance was the first danger of the Knot. Anything entering gave the place a shape to preserve. A hand would become ten thousand clay hands. A flame would remain as fever in the roots. A spoken name would be repeated behind every little arch until nothing living there could distinguish naming from command.

So the power remained in the flooded forest.

It stood among the alders beneath the gray rain, though no animal looked up and no footprint appeared in the mud. For seven days, or seven centuries within the hollow, it attended without watching.

This was not the attention that fixes a thing in place. It did not measure the chamber, decide whether the mouths were one animal or many, or force the stair to reveal its ends. It allowed uncertainty to remain where uncertainty was shelter.

It learned what could be learned.

The Knot was not wicked. It had not chosen to imprison the puddles, the seeds, the reflected rooms, or the weather inside the fish. It did not hoard because it was greedy. It kept everything because it had never learned the difference between preservation and mercy.

Nothing within it knew how to end without being erased.

The pale plants endured because loosening their hold felt like death. The amphipods fed on old light because no new light could enter safely. The builders, if builders there were, maintained their thresholds against a world in which every true opening led farther inward. The mouths drank stillness because stillness was the only substance abundant enough to sustain them.

And the oldest resident copied exits because it possessed no memory of departure—only of openings seen from the wrong side.

The power did not judge them for this.

Nor did it decide that the chamber should be made wholesome according to the laws of the upper world. To drain it would kill almost everything. To illuminate it would be another kind of flood. To make all directions honest would strand creatures whose bodies depended on uncertainty. Even a current, however gentle, would become a catastrophe in a place accumulated so densely.

The power therefore gave the Knot no light, no heat, no cleansing fire.

It gave it a loss.

At first the gift was scarcely perceptible.

An old leaf, blackened for centuries but still perfect in every vein, failed to make an impression in the clay beneath it. The leaf remained. The clay remained. But between them, for the first time, nothing was passed on.

The Knot waited for the missing copy to appear elsewhere.

It did not.

The absence was small enough that the chamber did not contract around it. No root snapped. No mouth vanished. The black fish passed nearby, and the plants folded before it as always.

Later, a fungal thread reached the threshold of one of the tiny arches. Ordinarily it would have divided, preserving the opening’s exact contour. This time one branch ceased growing. It did not rot. It did not harden into a replica. It simply finished.

The other branch continued around the arch.

The chamber had never contained a finished thing.

It knew the ancient, granular labor of decay, but decay in the Knot was only transfer. Leaf became stain. Stain became mineral seam. Seam became remembered leaf. Nothing was released from resemblance.

Now there was an ending that made no copy.

The power waited.

Wisdom, in that place, required waiting long enough not to mistake alarm for refusal.

The mouths were the first to understand.

One appeared in a bed of silt near the seven-step stair. Its inward teeth clarified. It drank the hesitation of roots, the pause between movements of the amphipod, the held century before a shale splinter slipped from clay.

Then, slowly, it closed.

No one witnessed the instant. The power did not witness it either.

That was part of the gift.

Where the mouth had been, the silt did not retain a ring, a scar, or even a difference in color. It became ordinary sediment—not secretly an organ, not the image of an organ, not an opening awaiting sufficient stillness. Merely silt.

The pressure behind the other mouths changed. Whether in fear or relief could not be known. Some widened. Some withdrew. One remained open and continued feeding exactly as before.

The power permitted this. Benevolence did not require that every mouth accept closure.

Afterward, minute relinquishments began throughout the chamber.

A root stopped following the veins of a drowned leaf halfway to its tip.

A clay shelf softened without reforming elsewhere.

A reflected cloud completed its passage across a bubble and did not return.

One of the colorless amphipods—or the only amphipod, at one of its ages—came to a place where a leaf had finally become mud. It tested the surface with its delicate feet. There was no underside, no remembered foliage, no trustworthy orientation.

It waited.

Then it stepped onto the mud.

The mud remained mud.

This was the first new act in the Knot that was not an imitation.

The wise power still did not leave. A gift of ending, given alone, can become despair. Once the inhabitants learned that things might truly be lost, they needed also to learn that loss could make room.

So the power gave the chamber a second thing.

Not an exit.

A direction.

It was a very slight direction, no stronger than the difference between a tear held in an eye and a tear beginning to fall. The water did not flow. Roots did not bend. Silt did not stir. Yet somewhere beyond the stair, distance acquired a preference.

For the first time, one could go toward something without forcing everything else nearer.

The black fish felt it at once.

It stopped inside a dense net of roots. Every leaf in the chamber folded, including leaves far ahead of its customary path. Weather moved beneath its skin: gray cloud along the belly, pale rain at the fins, one narrow line of blue near the spine.

For a long while it did not move.

Then it turned in a direction it had never turned before.

No corridor opened for it. Instead, the chamber refrained from closing.

The fish passed between two moss banks, crossed a region where the water held the cold of an immeasurable sea, and reached the seven-step stair.

For the first time, the stair was seen from the front.

Its upper end rested beneath the fish’s mouth. Its lower end descended only seven steps and ended in a shallow bed of clean clay.

There was no abyss. No kingdom. No buried door.

Only enough bare ground for one small thing to arrive.

The fish opened its mouth.

It released the skies it had eaten.

Not all of them. Some had become too fully part of it; wisdom does not demand the undoing of every intimacy. But many emerged as tiny dark bubbles, each containing its overcast day. They did not drift sideways. They gathered above the lowest step.

There they touched one another without merging.

Rain fell in a dozen separate worlds.

Clouds crossed and were not lost.

The surrounding darkness turned its attention toward them, as it always had toward brightness, but this time it did not copy what it saw. The power’s first gift protected the little skies from preservation.

One by one, they completed their weather and emptied.

What remained was not light but open water.

The fish descended the stair.

At the seventh step it came to rest in the clean clay. Its body made a shallow impression. Then it swam away.

The impression did not become another fish.

Much later, one of the unseen builders placed three stones over the empty mark.

Whether this was an arch, a memorial, or merely three stones could not be determined. Moss covered one outer edge. A fungal thread crossed the threshold without dividing. No one repaired it.

The oldest resident approached last.

It moved by contours, as it always had: from root scar to shale edge, from shale edge to mineral vein, from vein to the lip of a niche. The chamber grew crowded with its approximations. Clay fronds leaned from walls. Fish-shaped cavities appeared in the silt. Tiny replicas of the entrance opened behind liverwort curtains and led inward.

It reached the stair by making seven thousand copies of a single broken line.

At the lowest step it found the fish’s fading impression.

The old resident began to copy it.

It formed the curve of the head. The taper of the body. The small open mouth. It laid each contour down with exquisite patience.

But before the copy was complete, the original impression softened.

The old resident stopped.

For all its age, it had never encountered a form that vanished faster than it could be preserved. It tried again, transferring the curve into mineral stain. The stain blurred. It pressed the shape into a leaf skin. The leaf skin tore and became mud.

Each failure was gentle.

Nothing attacked the old resident. Nothing forbade its work. The wise power only kept the unfinished shape from becoming permanent.

At last there remained a single curve: neither fish nor root nor doorway, merely a bent line in clay.

The oldest resident held itself there.

It could retreat and continue as before. The power would not prevent it. The Knot could keep countless things without keeping everything. There was room for ancient habits as well as change.

Or it could release the line.

A century passed.

The curve softened.

The old resident let it.

Throughout the Knot, copies did not collapse, but they ceased tightening around what they remembered. Clay fronds became only clay. Root scars no longer ached to become roots. The little arches led nowhere, and for the first time, nowhere was not the same as farther inward.

Above, the rain ended.

A drop hung from the tip of an alder root directly over the narrow entrance. Within it was a reflection of the forest: trunks, gray sky, the trembling brightness of shallow floodwater.

The wise power touched neither drop nor root.

It simply withdrew its attention.

The drop fell.

This time the water made a sound.

Very small, scarcely more than the opening of a seed, the sound entered the Blackwater Knot and was not consumed. It traveled through roots and moss, past the silent mouths and the builders’ arches, across the clean clay at the foot of the stair.

Then it ended.

The chamber listened to what remained after.

Not a deeper quiet.

Room.

The power departed without sealing the hollow. To seal it would have made a tomb; to widen it would have made a road. The clenched-hand opening remained beneath the alders, and rainwater continued inward in its dark thread.

The Knot remained strange. It remained crowded, cold, measureless, and almost entirely dark. Amphipods still walked beneath leaves. The fish still passed alone, carrying some private weather under its skin. Mouths opened in the silt and filtered stillness. Behind tiny arches, minute rooms sometimes appeared with figures seated around low fires.

But leaves now occasionally completed their decay.

Bubbles emptied without being eaten.

A niche collapsed and was not rebuilt.

And in the places where something had truly ended, the Knot did not construct more darkness.

It allowed a little water to remain open.
