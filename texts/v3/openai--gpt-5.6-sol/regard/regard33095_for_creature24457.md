The power did not enter.

That was the first kindness.

It came to the rim where ordinary dust lay against immaculate crystal and stopped with one foot in the weathered world. It had no need to respect the boundary. No geometry could have confused it, no corrected history could have removed its arrival, and no white figure could have made distance intervene. But wisdom does not mistake ability for permission.

So it sat down.

For a long while, nothing happened.

The basin held its warm completed instant. Pollen remained in the air. The mineral flowers followed their invisible lights. Crown after crown preserved its impossible alignment. Far below, in the downward forests, something considered the pressure of a presence that had not yet touched it.

The power watched without measuring.

It did not call the basin unnatural. Nature was larger than any one arrangement of laws. It did not call the basin broken. Brokenness was not proved by strangeness, nor suffering by silence. It did not assume that change was good merely because changing creatures feared stillness.

It listened for desire.

This was difficult, because desire ordinarily declares itself through alteration: a root extends, a mouth opens, a creature moves from pain toward shelter. Here every movement was retained beside its alternatives. Preference could not easily become action. The basin might contain longing in infinite abundance and still appear serene.

At last the power lifted one hand and tapped the ground outside the boundary.

A small sound entered the ordinary dust.

It was not loud. It carried no command and concealed no device. It was simply a click, imperfectly made. Its beginning was sharper than its end. A grain of soil shifted beneath the fingertip, so that the sound could never be repeated exactly.

The click traveled across the plain.

Every glassleaf rib received it. The saffron carpets leaned toward all the places it might have originated. Pale crowns turned their empty centers upon the rim. The veils ceased consuming unused light. White figures appeared among the green—not approaching, but allowing the distance between themselves and the visitor to become briefly honest.

The click passed from horizon to horizon.

The answer came from beneath the power’s hand.

It was exact.

A second answer followed.

This one was almost exact.

In it, the final interval was too long.

The power smiled, not because it had solved the basin, but because something below had attempted a thing and had not corrected the attempt.

It tapped again.

The basin answered.

For what might have been an afternoon or several mountain ages, they spoke in this way. Their language had no nouns. Nouns were difficult in a place where an object could hold all its histories at once. They spoke instead in intervals: persistence, distinction, approach, refusal, return.

Slowly, a question emerged.

The power asked:

**Do you keep everything because you love it, or because you cannot release it?**

The basin gave no answer that could cross the rim.

Across the plain, open flowers remained open. Buds remained folded. The white walkers turned their dark seams toward one another. In the downward country, mirror-fields faced a deeper light.

Then every crown-plant shifted its alignment.

Only once.

Their empty centers no longer framed one another. From the rim, they formed a long corridor through the green, ending at the basin’s low center.

It was invitation, or experiment, or both.

The power entered.

Nothing corrected the footprint.

The mineral crust bore the impression without cracking. Saffron filaments bent and did not immediately rise. Heat flowed away from the sole into the ground. These were small events, but the basin gathered around them with immeasurable attention.

The power walked slowly.

It allowed the contradictory distances to remain contradictory. When a grove was five hundred meters across, it crossed five hundred meters. When the same grove was many kilometers within, it crossed those also. It did not force either measure to yield.

At the first white figure, it stopped.

The figure faced the power from every side. Its dark seam opened lengthwise through the scene. Inside were countless versions of the meeting: the power absent, the figure absent, both turning away, both standing forever, the basin empty, the basin spread across the world.

The power reached toward none of them.

“What do you want?” it asked.

The figure had no mouth, but the seam narrowed.

A leg lifted and came down in the same place.

For the first time, the figure was not farther away afterward.

The power understood.

The walkers were not wardens. They were the basin’s unfinished departures. Each was an impulse toward elsewhere that the completed whole had preserved but never permitted to arrive. They moved by revising distance because true travel would require one location to be surrendered for another.

The power held out a hand.

The white figure touched it.

Its surface was warm.

For one instant the figure became many things: an animal with a beating heart, a pale machine crossing a dead planet, a thought in a buried mind, a person lost among glassleaves, a tool made by beings whose universe had ended. None was declared the original.

Then the figure stepped across a distance the basin did not reclaim.

It stood one pace nearer.

All the mineral flowers turned toward it.

Deep below, something clicked in alarm.

The power placed a hand upon the black pavement.

“I will not dismantle you,” it said. “I will not make you mortal because mortality is familiar to me. I will not call your abundance imprisonment if you name it peace.”

Roots gathered beneath the hand without moving.

“But what is kept without the possibility of release is not remembrance. It is custody.

“What cannot be refused is not harmony.

“And what contains every answer may still be unable to choose.”

The basin opened around the words.

Not physically. No central chamber appeared. Instead, all its local laws became attentive at once. Groves that preserved every state listened beside groves that favored the oldest. Crowns looked through distant worlds. Veils unfolded over absent skies. The deep presence brought its entire inverted forest close beneath the crust.

The power offered no cure.

It offered a door.

The door occupied no location. It was a new permission within the basin’s rules:

**A state might end, if the life holding it wished to let it end.**

Nothing would be forced to age. Nothing would be compelled to die. No leaf would fall merely because leaves fell elsewhere. The completed whole would remain available to every being that desired it.

But release would become possible.

The basin resisted.

Fractures appeared and repaired themselves before they had formed. Noon arrived alongside evening. The power’s footprint vanished, returned, multiplied, and became the impressions of feet that had never existed. At the rim, ordinary dust rose into the air and hung there, uncertain which world owned it.

The power did not overpower the resistance.

It bore it.

Every contradiction the basin cast outward was received without injury and without retaliation. The power held the versions that could no longer coexist, not choosing among them, but making room for choice to arise within them.

At last, somewhere in the saffron carpet, one orange bead opened.

There was no second bead beside it, intact.

There were only two halves of a shell and a pinch of gray powder.

The basin became utterly silent.

Then a sound passed through the roots unlike the dry clicking. It was low and uneven. It might have been grief. It might have been wonder.

The opened bead was not restored.

Around it, the filaments leaned inward.

They did not know what to do with absence.

The power knelt beside them and remained until they learned that absence had a shape, and that the shape could be held without being filled.

After that, the changes were few.

A single glassleaf blade altered its angle and cast a new shadow over the crust. A veil crossed a crown and consumed one ray of light completely, leaving no unused path by which it might return. One white walker took seven ordinary steps. Each carried it forward.

In the low center of the basin, a bud opened.

It had always been folded. Elsewhere on the same branch, it had always been in bloom. The power did not erase those truths. It merely allowed this particular bud to cease being closed.

Its petals were not green or metallic. They were thin, colorless, and almost soft.

Within lay a drop of water.

The basin had no rain, no streams, no liquid life. Yet the drop trembled in the flower, rounding itself under gravity. Sunlight entered it and divided.

Every crown aligned upon it.

Every white figure faced it.

The entire downward forest turned toward that minute, unstable lens.

The drop slid from the petal.

It struck the black crust and remained there for less than a second.

Then it evaporated.

The basin did not bring it back.

Warm vapor entered the motionless air and was lost among the ordinary molecules of the sky.

The loss traveled through the plain.

Some groves rejected it and closed themselves more perfectly than before. The power honored their refusal. Some preserved the falling drop in silver ribs, where its sound could be released but not relived. Some began, very slowly, to loosen.

At the boundary, three travelers stepped out from among the glassleaves.

Their companions had watched them motionless at an impossible distance for years. They emerged unchanged, then changed all at once: hair lengthening, wounds closing or arriving, tears appearing for several contradictory reunions. The power steadied them until each could choose one history to carry.

Others remained.

That, too, was permitted.

The white walkers gathered at the rim. Most turned back. One crossed onto the dust.

Its first footprint stayed.

The figure looked down at the mark. Its dark seam opened, but there were no diminishing repetitions inside—only darkness, narrow and deep.

Then it continued toward the mountains.

The power did not remain as ruler or guardian. Before departing, it placed nothing at the basin’s center and raised no monument. It left only the permission it had offered, woven lightly enough into the local laws that the basin could one day reject even that.

The place stayed warm, green, dry, and nearly motionless.

Its crystal needles remained sharp. Its impossible groves retained more histories than the outer world could bear. Space continued to break with perfect composure. The deep presence still answered clicks from beneath the listener’s feet.

But not always exactly.

Now, on rare evenings, wind enters a short distance beyond the rim.

It moves one glassleaf, perhaps two.

A few grains of pollen fail to return to their former positions. Here and there, beneath the thickets, lies a fallen blade. It does not rot. Not yet. But neither is it restored.

And in the basin’s low center, surrounded by millions of permanent flowers, there is a small dark stain on the immaculate stone.

Nothing grows there.

Nothing repairs it.

The basin keeps it carefully—not as a wound, and not as an error, but as the first thing it ever allowed itself to remember because it was gone.
