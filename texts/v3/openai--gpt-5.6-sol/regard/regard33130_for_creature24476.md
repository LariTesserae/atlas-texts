### The Gift of Room

The benevolent power did not bring water.

It did not open the roof to the sun. It did not quiet the sailbacks, blunt the hushes, extinguish the blue fires, or teach the Underbeat to cease its pounding.

These were its first acts of mercy.

For a long while—longer than any brace had stood, longer than several vaultbacks had courted and died—it did nothing that could be distinguished from attention.

It listened through the ceramic plates of the worldstems. It followed pressure through the braided cores and learned the slow decisions of plants. It entered the flank chambers of the listener’s widow without becoming the noise that fed it. It felt the hush-circle open and understood that silence here was not peace but a shape made by fear.

It tasted the volatile blood of wounded branches. It moved with pollen, with resin smoke, with the metallic cells of mating vaultbacks. It descended into tunnels polished by rasp herds and farther still, into salt compacted before the first living thread entered the fracture.

Everywhere it found answers.

The sailbacks answered light by releasing their holds.

The flowers answered terror by opening.

The worldstems answered burden with strength, damage with poison, use with memory.

The answering people answered everything.

Yet the power was wise enough to know that an answer was not necessarily a request.

There were deaths it could have prevented. A young climber missed a resin cord when a bridge convulsed and fell through three layers of foliage before vanishing into a feeding swarm. The power could have returned them to the branch. It did not.

A hush pierced an egg-bearer within sight of her settlement. The power could have hardened her scales, diverted the hunter, stopped the cone an instant before it entered her body. It did not.

Blue fire crossed an old brace and consumed the dried ancestors within it. The power could have denied the flame. It did not.

Not because it was indifferent.

It saw that the climber’s fall scattered parasites into a canopy where they had never lived, and that these consumed a blight which would otherwise have killed a young worldstem. It saw the hush feed its offspring, and the silence around the feeding open a brief season for delicate flowers. It saw fire split seeds that had slept for eighty bright-dim cycles in the joints of the brace.

But even these consequences were not its reason.

Wisdom did not make suffering good merely because something later fed upon it. Benevolence did not call every wound necessary.

The power refrained because this country was made of answers, and an answer given too quickly from outside could become a command no living thing had chosen.

So it listened.

Eventually, beneath the noise of life, it heard something none of the inhabitants could hear.

The fracture was closing.

Not now. Not within the lives of the people, nor within the standing age of the worldstems. The change was too slow for bark memory and too deep for forked sticks. On one side of the buried country, basalt pressed against salt. On the other, ancient layers settled grain by grain. The ceiling did not descend; the floor did not rise. Instead, the pressure gathered everywhere.

In twelve thousand bright cycles, storms would grow more violent.

In thirty thousand, the light membranes would begin tearing faster than they could fuse.

In fifty thousand, the greatest worldstems would crack along their cores.

After that, the fracture would close not in one catastrophe but in ten thousand local ones: terrace by terrace, vault by vault, settlement by settlement. There would be no final day. The country would simply become less able to make room than life was able to fill it.

The Underbeat knew.

It did not know as the power knew. It had no measure for fifty thousand cycles and no image of a country ending. It knew through the resistance meeting its mineral teeth. It knew as pain along pale armor. It knew as an instinct older than the first worldstem: forward had become difficult, and every other direction was becoming worse.

Its pounding was not a warning.

It was an attempt.

The power descended to it.

No eye saw this descent. No membrane brightened. No holy warmth passed through the roots. The power approached in the only form the deep animal could receive: as a difference between one blow and the next.

The Underbeat struck.

Salt fractured.

It struck again.

Basalt rang through its chisel.

On the third blow, the power answered by allowing one grain of stone to turn.

Only one.

The grain lay far ahead of the animal, beyond shell, tooth, tunnel, and root. It shifted against the pressure holding it and came to rest at another angle. The movement was smaller than germination. No living listener could have detected it.

But the next grain now bore its burden differently.

And the next.

The power did not split the planet. It did not conjure a vacant paradise beside the old country. It introduced a flaw.

The flaw traveled outward through the basalt in a branching plane. It curved away from poison strata and chambers of lethal heat. It passed near metals without entering concentrations rich enough to invite ruinous growth. It approached the warm faults, but not so closely that blue fire would fill it. Where the salt was strongest, the flaw narrowed. Where roots might someday brace it, the flaw widened.

It was not a cavern.

It was the possibility of one.

The power laid no path to it. Instead, it changed the taste of three mineral seams by the smallest possible degree.

The Underbeat struck again.

This time its chisel entered a softer line.

The great body paused.

Across the buried country, the regular quarry pounding ceased.

Silence did not follow. Sailbacks roared. Drummers courted. Rasp herds ground their passage through a pillar. Yet every answering person awake at that moment lifted their head, because a rhythm older than settlement had stopped.

The Underbeat turned.

Its next blow came at an angle no listener had heard before.

Then another.

Then another.

It began to follow the flaw.

Its feeding opened hairline spaces. Its discarded salts held some apart. Furnace-moss entered behind it and warmed the stone. Worldstem roots found the warmth and pressed into the seams. The roots carried colonies. The colonies carried eggs. The eggs hatched with mouths already suited to what was there.

The new country was not given to them empty.

It was grown toward.

For generations, nothing appeared to have changed except the direction of a distant pounding. Then a root vault split along an unfamiliar edge and released a breath of hot mineral air. Pale furnace-moss unfolded over it. Small salt lickers gathered. Three died from compounds their bodies had never encountered. Two survived and laid capsules in the warm seam.

A worldstem sent a root after them.

The answering people found the opening much later.

A listener named **Three-Taps-After-Stillness** pressed her sounding fork into the new root and heard distance.

Not emptiness. Never emptiness.

Distance.

She heard thin stone under tension, crawling disks, the feeding clicks of salt lickers, hot gas passing through a crack, and deeper than these, the Underbeat moving away from the inhabited country.

She remained still until ballooning animals covered her shoulders and balancing fan.

When she returned to the settlement, she did not say she had discovered a chamber.

She tapped a pattern no one knew.

A long pressure. A pause. Two strokes angled away from one another. Another pause, not as long. Then a scatter of light contacts moving outward along the bark.

The others placed their hands against the trunk and asked her to repeat it.

She did.

They argued over its meaning.

Some thought it meant *pillar splitting beyond safe reach*.

Some heard *herd leaving known fibers*.

An old brace-worker said it resembled the invitation rhythm used when offering a beam to living bark, except that no refusal followed.

A child, not yet trained enough to distrust simple meanings, said:

“Room.”

The pattern became the word.

Exploration took many lives. The first routes closed. A warm shelf collapsed beneath a survey group and killed four. A colony of needle-faced hunters entered through an opened fiber and had to be driven back with bitter smoke. One promising terrace proved to be the unfelted back of a young vaultback, which rose in irritation and carried away half the markers.

The benevolent power prevented none of these things.

But once, when a light membrane detached above a narrow crossing crowded with travelers, it altered a thermal current by the width of a scale. The glowing bell folded left instead of right. It passed close enough to fill their translucent eye-plates with gold, but did not strike them.

Once, when a new worldstem rooted into a stone that would have poisoned it, the power did nothing. The root tasted the danger and withdrew by itself.

Once, when a child became lost inside a breathing chamber, the power also did nothing. A cup-mouth settled on the child’s filter mask. Its feeding made the mask whistle. Searchers heard.

The people built the first brace at the edge of the widening seam.

They made it from old growth, shed shell, fired bark, and the cured body of Three-Taps-After-Stillness, who had died many cycles after hearing the new distance. They tested each joint. They warmed the root. They applied resin and tapped invitation patterns.

The root opened soft tissue around the beam.

Leaves appeared there before the next dimming.

The people added the pattern for *room* to the brace, but they did not carve it as ownership. They hammered it as instruction:

Not *this space is ours*.

Not *the way is safe*.

Not *something powerful has favored us*.

The pattern meant:

*There is more to answer.*

In time, the new fracture became several valleys wide. Light colonies crawled toward its ceiling. Sailbacks crossed into it and stripped its first canopies white. Hushes followed. Rasp herds hollowed the young pillars. Blue fire baked black roads through the growth. Vaultbacks wedged themselves between trunks and began the centuries-long work of becoming plazas.

The place was not gentler than the old country.

It contained fear, hunger, error, predation, and grief. Its bridges failed. Its doors closed. Its storms rearranged every promise.

But it had time.

The old fracture ceased closing. Pressure passed instead into the branching flaw, where living braces, roots, shells, tunnels, and discarded armor received it gradually. The Underbeat’s excavation became part of the planet’s settling. What would have crushed the country now helped open it.

The power did not remain as a ruler.

It accepted no offerings. It gave no laws. It did not demand gratitude from creatures who could not know what had been done.

It stayed only as attention.

Very rarely, where no native answer could arrive in time, it allowed one grain to turn. A current bent. A fault delayed its slipping. A spark entered hardened bark rather than a nest. These mercies were so small that chance received the credit, which suited the power.

Everything else it left to the country.

For it had understood the deepest dignity of that place: life there did not need to be rescued from change. It did not need to be made pure, quiet, painless, or permanent.

It needed no paradise laid over it.

It needed room to continue becoming itself.

And so the wise power did not save the buried country from its nature.

It braced the world beyond it.

Then it listened for the answer.
