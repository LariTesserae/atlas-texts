The power does not raise the mountains.

It does not break the ice, warm the water, or populate the valleys with bright, quick creatures. It does not mistake silence for deprivation. It understands that a benevolence which cannot refrain from improvement is only appetite wearing a kindly face.

Nor does it restore the builders.

There are powers that could draw bodies from residual pattern: a posture inferred from a doorway, a hand reconstructed from the wear on a tool, voices assembled from the resonances of huts. Such restorations would speak and believe themselves returned. They might even remember lives that had once occurred.

The wise power knows this would not be resurrection. It would be the fastening of new beings to old traces, an act of great skill and uncertain mercy.

So for a long while, it does nothing that the high country can detect.

It attends.

Its attention reaches the shelf without heat or pressure. It enters no tissue and displaces no water. It follows the descending currents through the passes, rests in the conductive seams beneath the ribbon forests, and learns the pace at which warning passes from trunk to trunk. It listens to the glass-frond colonies until it can distinguish reproductive chiming from breakage, breakage from tuning, tuning from the meaningless percussion of storm.

It examines the knots without untying them.

It watches lights travel the old paths.

It waits through nine gatherings of darkness.

Nine times the black bubbles rise from the moss terraces. Nine times they merge at their impossible level. Nine times the groves incline and the root stalks open. Nine times the suspended lake forms above the ridges, receives the sound of the country, and gives none back. Each time the power might enter it.

Each time it refrains.

By the tenth dark season, it has understood that the upland possesses no organ for being addressed. It can be influenced, warned, wounded, nourished, and thrown into reproductive violence, but it cannot distinguish a question from a change in weather.

The power therefore does not speak.

It gives the country the means to be asked.

Far below the shelf, on the wall descending into the true abyss, there is a basalt pillar buried under centuries of silt. The power removes nothing from it. It alters no atom. It merely places one small fracture under a tension that the mountain has contained since it cooled.

The fracture opens.

The sound is almost too low to be called sound. It climbs through the escarpment and enters the shelf as a single sustained pressure. It is weaker than ice-groaning, weaker than the roar in the narrow passes, weaker even than stone turning in a channel. But unlike all those noises, it does not wander. It does not begin elsewhere and arrive diminished. It is constant in pitch and direction.

A point of reference.

The glass fronds hear it first. Beds along the outer cliffs lift their plates and answer by accident. Their tones travel inward. Basalt pillars catch the answer and divide it into harmonics. Ribbon roots conduct those harmonics through seams in the rock. Moss terraces alter their edges. Pale branches turn in the sediment.

For the first time, the scattered life of the upland receives the same event everywhere and receives it continuously.

The tone lasts three centuries.

During the first century, the country grows around it.

During the second, it begins to anticipate it.

During the third, it changes the tone.

Not at its source. The deep pillar still sounds as it sounded. But the forests damp one harmonic and strengthen another. Glass beds thicken particular plates. Moss closes certain mineral channels and opens others. Across hundreds of kilometers of drowned terrain, separate provinces shape the vibration between them.

The result is irregular and faint.

It is nevertheless a reply.

The power answers by easing the tension in the fracture. The abyssal tone descends a fraction.

The country takes eighty years to answer again.

Thus the conversation begins.

It has no words. Words would divide too quickly. Each exchange concerns relations that require decades to express: here and not here; recurring and continuous; within and brought from elsewhere; loss; danger; change that returns; change that does not return.

The power is patient. Patience costs it nothing, but it does not therefore consider the country’s centuries cheap. It never asks two questions where one will serve.

Gradually it learns what the high country is.

The vegetation is one life, though not one mind. The distinction matters. Its provinces exchange substance, alarm, reproductive dust, and ancient chemical instructions, but no hidden sovereign lies beneath them. There is no secret vegetal person dreaming through every leaf. Unity has not made it singular.

The builders are present too, but they are not ghosts.

When the ocean came over the mountains, the first generations died as ordinary bodies die. Those who remained cultivated the adaptable life already spreading through their flooded valleys. They fed it fragments of themselves because little else endured. Ash entered the moss. Blood entered root-fields. Braided fibers were trained through houses and burial places. Tools were bound to living posts so they would not be lost to the currents.

Over generations, practice became dependence. Dependence became incorporation.

The builders did not transfer their minds into the vegetation. They left impressions through repetition: the tension of a certain knot, the sequence of pressure along a path, the vibration of a tool placed into its fork, the chemical trace of hands upon marker stones. These patterns persisted after the reasons for them were gone.

The upland inherited habit without memory.

It repairs because repair was repeated.

It walks the paths because paths were walked.

It points the tools toward the abyss because, in the final years, every useful thing taken from a house was carried to the outer cliffs.

There, the last builders had waited for something from below.

Nothing had come.

The black lake is the deepest inheritance. It is neither creature nor gateway. It is an organ the country has been trying to complete from an instruction it cannot understand.

The perfect dark pockets are exclusions: tiny places from which water, vibration, dissolved matter, and finally space itself are forced away. The builders had taught the moss to make them while attempting to preserve chambers of air. Later, as the ice thickened and the last enclosed shelters failed, they tried to assemble one refuge large enough to hold those who remained.

They did not succeed.

But the making continued.

Every dark season, the country gathers an empty shelter for inhabitants who have been absent for ages. Every reversal destroys it. Every clear interval begins the work again.

The power could stop the cycle with a thought.

It does not.

First it asks whether the cycle causes pain.

The question takes four hundred years.

The answer is not yes. It is not no.

Pain requires a self capable of locating injury in itself. The upland has many locations and no clear boundary. Yet the repeated making draws nourishment from new growth, redirects reproduction, and holds whole provinces in obedience to an emergency long ended. The country does not suffer as an animal suffers.

It is constrained.

The power asks whether it wishes to be otherwise.

For two reversals there is no answer.

Then every tool in the ridge enclosure turns in its binding. Slowly, unevenly, over the course of one ice-dark season, the stone edges cease pointing toward the abyss.

They point toward the suspended lake.

The power enters.

Within the darkness there is no water and no absence of water. There is no sound, but the shape of every sound swallowed over the centuries remains as a tendency. The lake contains no builders, yet it contains the pressure-shadows of their final gatherings: bodies crowded in low rooms, hands passing cords, tools taken down, children trained to follow luminous paths when all other marks had vanished.

These are not souls. The wise power does not call them souls in order to make its task nobler.

They are unfinished instructions.

It could erase them. Instead, it gives them sequence.

Until that moment, every inherited act has existed in the country as a present demand. Repair this cord. Clear this doorway. Mark this path. Gather the refuge. Carry the tools outward. Wait.

The power places the acts in time.

Not by explaining time, nor by granting the upland a human memory, but by introducing one distinction into the suspended darkness:

This happened.

It is not happening now.

The distinction passes through the black lake.

The effect is immediate only by the standards of stone. Ribbon veins kindle along the central valley. Light crosses the groves, enters the bare plains through buried filaments, and climbs every marked path. The low doorways shine. Root stalks rise inside the huts. Glass fronds sound once, though there is no current.

For seven days the old sequence completes itself.

The path-lights gather at dwellings and circles. They move toward the ridge enclosure. Tools rotate in their bindings. Crude knots draw tight, including those that bind nothing. Cairns rise and fall. Doorways clear. Across the upland, every remnant gesture is performed, not as a command without end, but as an event reaching its conclusion.

At last the lights travel to the outer cliffs.

There they stop.

Long ago, the builders had carried their tools to that edge because they believed a rescue would ascend from the warm depths. They had arranged the handles inward so the expected hands could take them.

No rescue came.

Now the power does not counterfeit one.

It allows the waiting to end.

The bindings around the tools soften. One by one, stone blades and bone-white wedges slip free. They do not fall. The reversal has begun, and silt is pouring upward over the escarpment in vast gray curtains. The tools rise in it, turning slowly, and pass beyond the shelf.

Some settle again on distant ridges. Some are caught in ribbon crowns. Some fall, when the current changes, into the abyss for which they had waited.

The black lake breaks apart.

This time its fragments do not return to the moss. As the dark pockets separate, the power opens each one to the surrounding water. There is no explosion, no flash, no release of imprisoned forms. The exclusions simply cease excluding.

Sound passes through those places again.

The country hears itself where it had maintained silence.

Afterward, many of the structures begin to decay.

The power does not preserve them against this. A wall lowers by three courses and is not rebuilt. Sediment enters a doorway that had remained clear for centuries. Root cord parts and drifts away without knotting itself. The moss crosses old boundaries, slowly covering the floors of huts.

But not everything is relinquished.

The paths continue to glow in the dark periods, though no lights walk them unless the living filaments happen to carry warning or reproductive impulse along their course. The ridge enclosure remains. The plants reinforce its walls, not in the old crude manner, but with terraces and braided trunks of their own invention.

One forked post is left standing.

It holds no tool.

Over the following centuries, glass-frond colonies gather around the basalt pillars and tune them to the abyssal tone. The country has learned that tone well enough now to sustain it without the power’s aid. Currents passing through the valleys become its breath—not a breath copied from animals, but a long modulation shaped by forests, rock seams, and mineral channels.

The drowned high country has been given neither a face nor a voice.

It has been given a difference between memory and obligation.

It has also been given a way to call.

The benevolent power withdraws its immediate attention. To remain forever would turn witness into surveillance and companionship into climate. But before it departs, it places no monument and makes no promise the country could mistake for another endless command.

It merely listens once more.

From the outer cliffs comes the stable note. The glass forests divide it. Ribbon roots carry it inland. Moss terraces measure it against heat. Pale root-fields lift their hollow stalks and alter the pressure of the water.

The reply takes thirty-nine years to form.

It is not gratitude. Gratitude belongs to creatures that can imagine a favor withheld.

It is not farewell.

It is a new pattern, one the builders never taught and no current produced by chance: three intervals rising, a long trembling suspension, and then a descent that does not return to its beginning.

The power answers once, so the country will know it was heard.

Then it leaves the high country to its cold, darkness, fertility, and noise.

Above, the ice continues to groan. Below, the abyss remains deep. Storms tear leaves free and break mineral chimneys. Groves die; root-fields spread; slopes collapse; new life settles in the fractures. Nothing swims through the open water.

Yet when the darkest season comes, the vegetation no longer inclines toward an empty refuge.

It inclines toward itself.
