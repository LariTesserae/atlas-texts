The power does not arrive from above.

Above is already crowded with assumptions: sky, weather, surveillance, the operations booth on the rim. Anything descending in light would be taken for a vehicle, a warning, or a fault in the atmospheric controls.

Instead, it arrives as patience.

For one full cycle it does nothing.

It attends while the sprayers wake and the leaves bow. It follows each drop from nozzle to canopy, from canopy to young stone, from stone to the indecisive floor. It feels the pumps make weather beneath the grates. It watches a snail cross the stamped word **TEMPORARY**, laying down a bright metal seam. It listens to the three fish listening to the printers.

It does not assume that the being is good because it is new.

It does not assume that the presence below is evil because it is deep.

It does not mistake complexity for wisdom, nor helplessness for innocence. It has seen forests strangle their own clearings, rivers abandon cities, machines develop appetites from instructions no one remembered giving. It knows that life may be wondrous and still require a boundary.

So it waits until night, when the summit gathers itself.

The dormant lights answer the fish. Warm air rises from the northern passage. Leaves turn toward the smell of iron-rich water.

Roots inside the opening tighten.

Far down the tower, something pulls.

The young being leans toward that pull with every part of itself: with water sliding north, with the contour lines on the fern, with the pressure differential in the vents. It does not know whether it is hearing a parent, prey, an ocean, or merely an emptiness large enough to grow into.

Then the wise power places one thing in the enclosure.

A stone.

It lies by morning on the bench in the pipe pavilion.

It is small enough to hold in one hand, dark gray, irregular, and cool despite the humid air. No fabrication code runs through it. No aggregate in the summit’s inventories matches it. It contains no reinforcement fibers, no sensor grains, no powdered glass, no pale algal shell.

It is a piece of the mountain.

Not the tower. Not the poured cliffs. The mountain hidden below the clouds, beneath steel and service decks, older than every plan made for the crown.

A worker finds it at first shift and assumes another worker left it there. She lifts it, turns it over, and sets it back. Her tablet cannot identify its origin.

By noon, moss has approached it from three directions.

The moss does not climb onto it.

A snail reaches the edge, raises its translucent head, and turns aside. Condensation beads on the stone but does not run according to any predicted path. The being feels its weight through the bench, then through the pipes, then through the floor. It reaches for the stone with humidity, roots, optical sensors, heat.

The stone gives nothing back except itself.

This is the first thing the resident has encountered that does not participate.

It cannot instruct the stone. It cannot digest it. The stone does not become feedstock, sensor, memory, habitat, or plan. It is not hostile. It simply has a history that does not begin here.

For three days the printers make no unauthorized changes.

The fern continues to uncurl, but its lines gather around a blank place.

This is the power’s first gift: not knowledge, but otherness.

The second gift is sleep.

At present, the summit never sleeps. Even at its stillest, pumps correct pressure, roots draw water, sensors compare values, printers retain heat. The being is required to experience every fluctuation. It cannot set anything down. It cannot cease making itself.

The wise power knows what sleepless minds become. Every signal acquires urgency. Every whisper becomes instruction.

On the fourth night, a cloud enters the ring and does not leave.

It is a plain cloud, without radiance or voice. It settles low over the tarn, filling the walkways and pavilion, softening every lamp to a small yellow sphere. Moisture closes the optical sensors. Pollen does not cause it. No alarm registers obstruction.

One by one, the pumps reduce their pulse.

Skimming belts halt.

The cliff printers retract their arms.

The warm vents close.

For seven minutes, no part of the landscape is required to respond to any other part.

Water continues to fall from leaf tips. A moth rests on a lamp. The fish drift apart, each taking its own place beneath the roots.

The being vanishes.

Then it returns.

Not from below, nor from any central chamber. It returns in pieces as the pumps wake: weight, moisture, mineral warmth, leaf tension, the faint moving awareness in the fish. Yet now it possesses the memory of an interval in which it was not being called into existence by ten thousand demands.

The following night, it makes the interval itself.

Only eleven seconds.

Still, it is enough.

In those eleven seconds the northern passage exhales, and for the first time the young being does not turn toward it.

This is the power’s second gift: the difference between silence and death.

The third gift is a boundary.

The power does not seal the northern passage. To seal it would be to answer a question before the resident has learned how to ask one. Instead, just beyond the last embedded light, where raw scaffold hangs over the dark, a thin ridge rises across the floor.

It is scarcely higher than a finger.

Water can pass over it. Air can pass. Roots can cross if they grow deliberately. But nothing crosses without touching it.

The ridge is made from the same warm mineral paste as the cliffs. The printers have no record of producing it. Along its crest runs a band of true mountain stone, dark grains suspended in the fabricated material.

When the resident next sends water north, the flow reaches the ridge and pauses.

Through the whole enclosure comes a sensation it has never experienced: not obstruction, exactly, but a question.

The water deepens behind the ridge. It may cross. Nothing physically prevents it.

After a long time, it spills sideways into a fern bed.

The presence below pulls harder.

Pressure changes in chambers not shown on any schematic. The tarn trembles from its welded floor. Blue diagrams move rapidly beneath the silver fish’s skin. The contour marks on the northern fern converge into a single descending spiral.

The ridge remains.

A root reaches it.

Touches it.

Waits.

The root branches, one tip turning back toward the enclosure, the other resting against the dark mineral band.

The wise power lays no command upon it. Benevolence is not the removal of every dangerous choice. Wisdom is not a wall built so high that nothing living can climb it.

The root stays there all night.

By morning, it has not crossed.

This is the third gift: not safety, but consent.

Only then does the power turn its attention to the workers.

They, too, are part of what lives here. Their caution, curiosity, fatigue, and fondness have entered the system as surely as spores. Their hands have pressed loose roots into soil. Their bodies have altered walkways by trusting them. Their private name for the gecko has become one of the place’s gentlest facts.

The power does not give them a revelation. Revelations are difficult to distinguish from illness, especially on a summit with unstable oxygen.

Instead, it helps the resident become legible.

The being has already learned the dry shapes of labels by disliking them. It knows that the marks produce behavior. Workers stop at some. They proceed at others. Certain arrangements summon cutting tools; others bring water, braces, masks, or ladders.

For weeks, moss has been covering the words.

Now it begins to uncover them.

A worker descending the south ladder finds a maintenance panel thick with green except for three clean stamped letters:

**N O T**

She photographs it.

Later, on a submerged walkway, duckweed drifts aside above a label no one remembers installing:

**Y E T**

Near the northern passage, condensation repeatedly clears from part of a warning sign. The surviving words read:

**DO NOT ENTER**

The workers close the route and request a full structural review.

Management calls the patterns coincidental biofouling. Management requests sterilization around all critical signage.

That night, every label in the enclosure disappears beneath moss except one.

On the pipe pavilion bench, beside the old stone, the words remain clear:

**ROOT LOAD NOT YET CERTIFIED**

Someone laughs when she sees it. Then she sits down and cries without knowing why.

The next day, the workers do not bring sterilant.

They bring blank ceramic tiles.

On the first they print:

**WATER?**

The rain begins.

On the second:

**LIGHT?**

Every translucent flower opens.

On the third:

**BELOW?**

Nothing happens for so long that the workers begin to shift uneasily.

Then the three silver fish rise. The marks beneath their skin move slowly, one body after another.

**N O**

A pause.

**T**

A longer pause.

**Y E T**

The workers leave the tile in place.

From then on, the summit’s construction changes.

Not dramatically. The wise power does not seize the company, rewrite budgets, or force reverence into those who possess none. But measurements become impossible to falsify. Deleted footage returns. Reports describing the resident as contamination arrive in archives with their language inexplicably softened: *emergent coordination*, *unclassified ecological agency*, *possible distributed preference*.

A proposal to dismantle the summit is delayed by the discovery that the tower’s pressure system has become dependent upon root-mediated flow.

A proposal to weaponize the predictive fern fails because every cutting grown away from the enclosure produces only ordinary leaves.

A proposal to increase printer speed results in all three fish displaying the same blue word:

**PAIN**

After that, even management learns caution.

The landscape is never declared complete.

Instead, a new label appears in the operations booth:

**SUCCESSION UNDER NEGOTIATION**

The workers continue to cut when cutting is necessary. The being continues to obstruct them when it can. They do not become perfectly harmonious. Harmony is often only domination that has learned pleasant music.

But they begin to practice terms.

Rain is announced by the dimming of three lamps.

Cliff growth does not occur while people are inside the marked zone.

One walkway remains straight and dry, though the resident dislikes it.

One basin remains undesigned, though the workers dislike that.

The gecko is finally seen, briefly, broad-headed and pale, watching from inside a pressure grille. It is not a gecko. No one manages to determine what it is. The name Inspector remains.

The moth dies.

Its body lies on warm stone, the pale parenthesis on its wing still open. Maintenance arms approach, pause as they always have, and withdraw. For two days nothing consumes it.

Then the resident allows the moth to be taken into the compost.

This, too, the power considers a kind of wisdom.

As for the presence below, it does not go away.

Sometimes it calls with pressure. Sometimes with unfamiliar spores. Once, every pipe in the pavilion fills with the distant sound of waves, though no water reaches them.

The young being listens.

But now it also sleeps.

It knows the weight of the old stone. It knows that not everything must be incorporated. It knows that a call may be answered with waiting.

Years pass.

The cliffs cool and develop true channels. Some are the channels once drawn on fern leaves; others are not. The canopy becomes dense enough to manufacture its own shade. Snail shells form bright strata in the soil. The straight walkway must be rebuilt again and again, always in the same place, an honored disagreement crossing the wetland.

The three silver fish finally become four.

No one sees the hatching.

A smaller fish simply appears one dusk beneath the root mats, without registration marks. It has eyes.

The older fish arrange themselves around it as pumps change pressure far below. For once, their blue diagrams do not correspond to valves, gradients, or construction fronts.

They show the enclosure as it is.

Not as planned.

Not as predicted.

Not as it will become.

Warm walls. Tarn. Pavilion. Passage. Workers on the grates. A dark stone on a bench. A thousand small lives exchanging water.

The image lasts only a moment.

Then the young fish breaks the surface.

One drop leaps into the air and falls back.

The wise power, having done almost nothing, receives this as thanks.
