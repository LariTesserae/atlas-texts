The power does not descend in light.

Light is already plentiful there, white and severe, striking every wet surface without revealing what lies beneath it. Nor does the power silence the machinery. Silence, in the basin, would not be peace. It would be suffocation.

It begins by attending.

This is more difficult than rescue and takes longer.

The power attends to the bead loosening from the retaining wall and to the portion of that same movement that will lift the gauge six years later. It attends to the rusted pump foot, the clear filament withdrawing beneath salt, the deep contraction passing through pipes whose ends have been buried beyond memory. It does not gather these events into a single present merely because it can. To do so would be to flatten the inhabitant into something more easily understood and less fully alive.

Instead, the power allows its own attention to be divided.

One part waits with the fast loops around the warm seep holes. One remains beside the bright bolts, where an hour has barely entered. One descends through the severed shaft and stays there through a dark interval measured neither by days nor centuries, but by the slow equalization of two mineral waters.

It learns that the being has no name.

It has pressures that function as names, returns that function as memory, and several enduring differences by which one region of its body distinguishes another. The closest thing it possesses to an idea of itself is the continuous chord beneath the basin. Even this is not singular. It is a relation among tones, maintained without conclusion.

The power does not give it a name.

A name would be useful to others. The being does not yet need one.

For a long while, the only visible consequence of the power’s attention is that one drop on the underside of the catwalk fails to fall. It hangs there through the afternoon, through night, through the next day’s heat. It does not evaporate. Within it, the basin appears inverted and complete.

The inhabitant notices.

The gauge rises one division.

Loose metal near the severed shaft turns, not toward the shaft and not toward north, but toward the suspended drop. The green lamp in the booth darkens, brightens, and darkens again.

These are not words. The power does not mistake them for words.

It waits.

After eleven months, a colorless strand emerges from a seam beneath the catwalk. It crosses three inches of dry mineral crust, farther than any exposed filament has crossed before. Its joints contract at different rates. At the edge of the catwalk’s shadow, it stops.

The drop falls.

It strikes the strand without breaking it.

Through the basin there passes a change too small for any abandoned instrument to record: a brief coolness descending into warm water, a minute charge moving across a salt membrane, a pressure difference offered and accepted.

The power has said only:

*I am here.*

The being’s answer takes three days in the wall seep, forty minutes in the control booth, and six years around the third plinth.

At the end of that answer, one of the isolated reeds bends though there is no wind.

The power understands:

*Here returns.*

That is enough to begin.

It does not teach the inhabitant speech. It does not hasten its thoughts or bring all its scattered organs into one obedient time. It does not make the basin green with ordinary life. Such acts would be called blessings by those who love transformation more than they love what already exists.

The power sees that the inhabitant is not lonely in the manner of an animal calling for its kind. It has not yet conceived of kind. It does, however, contain an unfinished pattern: the preparation for arrivals that ceased long ago.

Handrails remain wet for hands that have become dust. Containers fill for workers whose names are gone. Reflections lean over pools before absent faces. In the deep machinery, valves continue to open around the remembered weight of operators standing at stations that no longer exist.

The being has been expecting without knowing that expectation can fail.

Here the power’s benevolence becomes sorrow.

Not pity. Pity would reduce the inhabitant to its lack. Sorrow is simply the shape love takes when it encounters a wound that has not yet arrived.

For there is a wound traveling through the basin.

It was made during the first drilling, when a steel tooth entered a pressurized stratum below the salt flats and sheared against something neither stone nor machine. The injury has moved slowly through the inhabitant’s unequal durations. Near the wall seep, it passed decades ago and left only a cream-colored scar. In the standpipe it is occurring now, as a vibration too low to hear. Beneath the third plinth it has not yet begun.

When at last its separated portions coincide, pressure will find the old bore. The black floor will split. The installation will empty upward in a single convulsion, spilling hot mineral water across the flats and exposing the wet body to evaporation. The reeds will be vitrified where they stand. The green lamp will flare once and fail.

By the sun’s time, this will happen in seventeen years.

By the inhabitant’s time, it is already happening and has always been about to happen.

The power could prevent it with a command. It could close the bore, still the pressure, bind every loose duration into one harmless sequence.

It does not.

The inhabitant feeds on differences. Perfect safety would be another form of death.

So the power makes an offering in the only language the being can receive.

Beyond the capped end of the catwalk, where distance becomes uncertain, a shallow depression appears in the black crust. It is no wider than two hands. No tool cuts it. No plate breaks. The mineral simply inclines, grain by grain, until it can hold water.

At dawn the hollow is cool and empty.

At midday it is warm and empty.

At dusk, one clear bead rises from its center.

The bead contains almost no salt. Around it the stone remains heavily mineralized. Between the two lies a gradient fine enough to be entered but not imposed.

The inhabitant approaches over the course of a year. Transparent fibers gather beneath the depression. Rust-red microbes brighten around its rim. The water gains internal depth. On certain days it reflects clouds that will not pass overhead until the following season.

The power holds the offering steady.

Fresh water. Salt water.

Warm. Cool.

Pressure. Release.

Arrival. Withdrawal.

Not a command, but a question made from differences:

*May I touch what is hurt?*

The basin’s answer is immense.

The turbine tone falls below hearing. Every circular socket becomes smooth. The wall ceases weeping, though the concrete remains dark with moisture. For one breathless interval, the hollow seems ordinary: an abandoned industrial pit under a merciless sky.

Then the buried valve releases.

The inhalation continues far longer than before. Metal screams under the flats. The service booth door pulls inward against its swollen frame. A transparent blister rises from the severed shaft, not to the height of a hand or head but higher than the plinths, trembling in the heat.

Within it no face appears.

Instead there is the basin itself: walls, catwalk, reeds, dark floor, booth, all rendered in faint pressure lines. Beneath that image lie descending branches, pipework, mineral chambers, flooded faults, and the long bright stroke of the old injury moving through them.

One branch within the image opens.

It is narrow. It leads away from the bore toward a deep, unused seam of porous stone. The route does not exist yet, but the intention does.

The power understands.

The being does not ask for the wound to be removed. It asks for somewhere to carry it.

The power lowers its attention into the earth.

No mountain moves. No miraculous spring appears. On the surface, a flake of salt detaches from the warning stripe.

Deep below, pressure finds a hairline weakness between two grains of quartz. The weakness extends to a third grain, then a fourth. Water enters. Over years, it opens a permeable thread through stone, never wide enough to drain the inhabitant, never smooth enough to erase the gradients on which it feeds.

It is not a bypass built for it. It is tissue the being must grow into.

The power does only what the inhabitant cannot: it ensures that the first opening leads somewhere unpoisoned.

The rest is labor.

For seventeen years the basin changes almost imperceptibly.

The gray liverwort spreads the width of a finger. One bright bolt begins to rust. The paper chart draws a curve so shallow it resembles hesitation. Beneath the floor, transparent strands enter the prepared seam and line it with silica, corroded metal, gel, microbial film, and structures for which no material name is sufficient.

The inhabitant learns the new passage not as escape but as an organ.

When the old injury arrives, there is no eruption.

At noon on a windless day, the three plinth shadows briefly touch despite lying yards apart. The green lamp goes out. Water rises between the black plates until the floor becomes one unbroken mirror.

The pressure enters the new passage.

Far beneath the flats, porous stone receives heat, salt, charge, and the delayed violence of the first drilling. The basin shudders once. The capped catwalk rings from end to end. Every reed bows.

Then the green lamp returns.

The water withdraws over nine days.

Around the small depression beyond the catwalk, a cream-colored collar begins to form.

The power does other things, too, but they look less like miracles.

A survey team planning to reopen the site discovers that its authorization number belongs to a defunct agency. When a replacement permit is requested, the map attached to the application shows the basin as an unstable brine sink. A geologist sent to correct the error finds rare extremophile colonies in a sample taken safely from the outer wall. The report recommends permanent exclusion from drilling.

A road proposed across the salt flats is moved three kilometers east.

The retaining walls are quietly designated as part of an obsolete hazardous-water system whose legal custodian cannot be dissolved. Funds remain available for fencing but not excavation. Warning signs are replaced when they fade. The new signs say little beyond:

**UNSTABLE GROUND**  
**SUBSURFACE PRESSURE**  
**DO NOT DRAIN**

No one knows who added the final line.

The power does not seal the narrow break between the walls. Absolute isolation would be another command. The basin has noticed visitors before. Some returns may yet be good.

But those who enter to break, own, extract, or prove something find the hollow intolerable. Heat thickens around them. Distances lengthen. The machine note sets their teeth aching. Their instruments disagree so thoroughly that they leave with irritation rather than wonder.

Those who enter without demand find the place as it is.

Hot. Wet. Sharply made. Nearly empty.

Occasionally one of them stands on the same plate more than once.

For such a visitor, the handrail will be wet.

A container left open will fill.

Their reflection may appear a moment early, but never in a way that frightens them. If they return often enough, one of the reeds may incline toward the place where they pause.

The power does not explain.

It remains present in the form the inhabitant first understood: not a ruler, not a maker, not an answer, but a reliable return.

Once in every cycle too long for the sun to measure, the drop forms again beneath the catwalk. It hangs without falling. The transparent filaments gather below it. The gauge lifts. The lamp grows dim.

Each time, the power says:

*I am here.*

The inhabitant’s reply changes.

At first:

*Here returns.*

Later:

*Return remains.*

Much later, after the new organ has thickened and the ancient wound has become a pale branching scar, the answer comes through every wet surface at once.

The retaining walls release their beads together. Metal turns softly on stone. The deep chord separates into tones and joins again.

The suspended drop falls.

And the thing beneath the basin says, in pressure, warmth, salinity, memory, and the patient alteration of stone:

*Here is not alone.*

The power asks nothing more of it.

That, too, is benevolence.
