The power did not descend.

Descent would have required an above distinct from the vapor, an empty place from which to come. There was no emptiness here. The basin filled every interval with root, wing, breath, spore, hunger, seep water, and the blind pressure of germination.

Instead, the power attended.

Its attention rested first upon the whole: the broad equilibrium, the faultless exchange of color and substance, the green fabric drawn across unfinished stone. It saw how every loss was answered, every opening filled, every quantity maintained. Nothing exceeded its place. Nothing failed for want of provision. The system was intricate beyond praise.

But the power was wise, and therefore did not mistake intricacy for goodness.

It looked closer.

It saw the moth taken from the trunk. To the basin, the event was negligible. Another moth settled before the scales of the first had ceased shining in the bird’s throat. The pattern remained.

To the moth, however, the event was everything.

It saw the calf separated among the herd’s innumerable legs. The herd curved, closed, continued. Another calf was born into the same warmth. The number did not change.

But the lost calf cried for one particular body, one particular smell. No replacement answered what it meant.

It saw a frog endure the slow work of venom. Another broad-backed frog surfaced beneath the vacated leaf, algae already green upon it. The pool lacked nothing.

But the dying frog was not the pool.

The power remained attentive for a long while.

It did not avert itself into admiration of the whole. It did not call suffering necessary merely because the arrangement was beautiful. It did not condemn tooth, venom, appetite, or fear, for none of these had chosen the law under which they worked. The snake was innocent of the frog’s anguish. The frog was innocent of the gnat’s. Hunger passed through them before thought could judge it.

Nor did the power break the hunters’ jaws or fill every mouth forever. It knew that an abundance in which no desire could fail would soon become another kind of stillness. Choice requires the possibility that the world resist. Love itself reaches toward what may be absent.

Yet this place contained more than resistance. It contained futility.

Every creature paid the full cost of living, while nothing it learned could enter the world.

That, the power would not preserve.

It searched the basin for the smallest change from which all other mercies might grow. It passed over the great herds, the mineral terraces, the veiled horizon. It entered the temporary tunnels of beetles and followed fish through pores in warm stone. It listened to the amphibians sounding their notes into hollow stems. It touched the branching web beneath the fungi, where every body was translated and no translation retained a name.

At last it found a wader lifting one wide foot from red mud.

Ordinarily, water would gather in the print. The mud would soften without collapsing. For an immeasurable moment the track would appear newly made, and then it would be neither erased nor preserved, only deprived of consequence.

The power allowed one grain of clay to remain displaced.

Only one.

The foot rose. Water shone beneath it. The bird moved on.

The grain did not return.

Nothing cried out. No light pierced the vapor. The insects did not interrupt their metallic chorus. Yet across the basin, every leaf trembled, though there was no wind.

A second grain settled against the first.

The print began to fill.

Not with the perfect restoration that had always followed passage, but with rain, fragments of leaf, a strand of shed algae, and the dark powder of a moth’s wing. The hollow became slightly different from the unstepped ground around it.

A fish entered and found the water deeper there.

It remained for three breaths.

Later, when the shallow channels warmed, the fish returned.

This was the first route.

The power did not teach it. The fish made it by remembering.

Elsewhere, a branch bent beneath a sleeping cat and did not rise to precisely its former angle. Condensation ran along the altered bark and dripped onto a patch of pale fungus. The fungus grew more thickly there. Beetles came. A bird noticed the beetles.

A relation persisted after its first use.

A frog struck a hollow root and heard the answer change where rot had widened the chamber. On the following call it adjusted its note. A distant frog answered the adjusted note rather than the old one.

The basin had repeated sounds forever.

Now, for the first time, it replied.

The power proceeded gently. It had no wish to exchange an arrested paradise for a catastrophe of sudden time.

It loosened the law by degrees.

Fruit still ripened beside flowers, but one branch finished fruiting and rested before it budded again. A nest still held young of many stages, but an egg laid after heavy rain hatched after an egg laid before it. The difference was slight. Then it was observable. Then a bird began to prefer the older, drier side of its nest for shells and the newer side for hatchlings.

The first arrangement had been made.

The bird had no word for it. It needed none.

Roots pressing into claystone began to enter the seams. Stone resisted. Roots thickened. After many warm rains, a plate cracked.

The sound was very small.

It passed through the ground farther than the deepest amphibian call.

The raw earth had received its second mark.

With history came danger.

The power had known this. Once fruit could truly diminish, a mouth might find a branch empty. Once births could increase a herd, the reed flats might be stripped. Once stone could erode, water might gather force. Memory could preserve kindness, but also fear. A creature that recalled one safe hollow could return to it; a creature that recalled an injury could carry terror beyond the wound. A useful stick could become a spear. A shelter could become a wall.

The power did not withdraw its gift.

Wisdom is not the prevention of every peril at the cost of every possibility.

But neither did it abandon the basin to the excuse of freedom. It placed limits around ruin as carefully as banks around new water.

No pain would continue after it had ceased to warn or protect. Venom would numb before it stopped the heart. A trapped creature would still struggle, but not beyond hope. When hope was gone, fear would loosen its hold.

Hunger would remain, but starvation would not be permitted to become an age-long torture. Drought might come one day, for rain that never fails cannot be received as rain, but there would be refuges beneath the root mats and deep reservoirs within the porous stone. Disease might move among crowded bodies, but never with such force that the basin’s variety could be reduced to one triumphant survivor.

Extinction would become possible, because consequence without loss is only another theater. Yet no kind of being would vanish merely because it had been slow to understand the new laws. The power gave the world long thresholds. It gave warning in scent, migration, silence, and failing fruit. It gave time for life to answer.

Most of all, it amended death.

It did not abolish it. Bodies still passed into fungus, beetle, root, feather, and warm blood. The yellow of the flower still traveled. The moth still entered the bird.

But what had looked from behind the moth’s eyes no longer vanished with the eyes.

The power received it.

Not the moth-pattern, for that already remained in countless wings. Not its substance, which the basin had always kept perfectly. It received the irreplaceable center: the small, complete fact that this life had been lived from here and nowhere else.

It received the calf’s recognition of its mother.

The frog’s sensation of mud cooling beneath its belly.

The bird’s preference for one branch in the crowded crown.

The old herd animal’s trust in the flank that had pressed beside it through a thousand passages.

The branch-cat’s satisfaction in warmth.

The gnat’s brief alarm at shadow.

None was dismissed for being small. None was blended unwillingly into a greater mind. The basin could remain a whole, but the whole would no longer be used to deny its members.

The dead woke beyond pain in a country suited first to recognition. Those who had lived by scent found clear trails. Those who had known the world through vibration felt familiar footfalls approaching. Those who had lived only minutes found those minutes intact and sufficient to be held.

Some rested.

Some went onward into forms of understanding they could not have borne while alive.

Some chose to return to the basin.

Those who returned did not carry memories sharp enough to crush the new life beneath the old. A frog did not hatch recalling every snake. A prey animal was not born already terrified. But something remained as inclination: an ease near certain voices, a curiosity about certain hollows, an unlearned patience, a sudden gentleness where once there had been none.

Thus experience entered the world without becoming a prison.

Ages began so softly that no inhabitant saw the first one pass.

The mineral runnels deepened. Water opened a narrow channel between two reed flats. Fish followed it and found, beyond the root-dark passages, a pool large enough to reflect a whole pale panel of sky.

One fish lingered at the edge of that brightness.

It had never seen so much open water. Its body urged concealment. Its memory offered no instruction, for nothing like this had existed before.

It entered anyway.

Behind it came another.

The low trees continued to spread, but now some limbs died and fell, leaving gaps that were not immediately filled. Through one such gap, direct light reached the ground for less than a minute. It struck wet basalt and made the stone shine.

Every nearby creature stopped.

The lizards flattened themselves. The gnats scattered. A wader raised its colored face. Small birds gave alarm calls, then curiosity calls, then sounds not used before.

The light faded as vapor closed.

For many days the birds returned to the gap.

They had seen the sun only once, but once had become enough.

Herds began to leave paths. At first the paths were only softened vegetation. Then they became mud. Water entered them. Seeds caught in the prints of broad feet and germinated in lines. Some paths failed at flooded ground. Others led reliably to mineral seeps.

The oldest animals knew which were which.

Calves followed them.

A tradition began in the pressure of one body turning after another.

The power watched for mastery and did not encourage it. It did not choose a favored species, straighten its spine, free its hands, or place dominion in its thoughts. Intelligence appeared in many forms and remained answerable to many lives. Birds arranged shells. Beetles maintained chambers instead of letting them close. Herds remembered water. Amphibians altered hollow roots to carry calls. Lizards learned that certain leaves, folded together, held condensation through the dimmer hours.

Nothing became sole heir to the basin.

When a creature first struck one stone against another, the power did not forbid it.

When the sharp flake cut flesh, the power did not hide the lesson.

When the creature used the flake to open a hard fruit and fed another beside it, the power rejoiced.

Not because stone had become a tool, but because memory had become generosity.

The basin was no longer immaculate.

Ravines formed. Some trees fell and did not root again. Moss covered the once-raw basalt, and lichens wrote slow maps across mineral shelves. Floods rearranged reed beds. Herds grew, divided, and met again after generations with altered calls and different tolerances. Predators learned routes. Prey learned deceptions. Nests became structures. Structures collapsed. A few were rebuilt better.

There were mistakes that lasted.

There were also kindnesses that lasted.

A wounded animal was fed twice by companions, then many times. Young birds learned a call first made in response to a vanished patch of sunlight. A line of broad-footed grazers continued turning around a dangerous sink long after the eldest one who discovered it had died. A particular hollow root carried frog-song across the basin until rot opened it to the rain. By then its rhythms had entered a hundred other songs.

The world acquired scars.

The power found them more beautiful than flawlessness.

In time, the vapor ceiling thinned for an hour. The horizon, once visible only in fragments, opened along the low green distance. There were no summits. No revelation of the whole was given. The basin remained too broad, too crowded, too alive to be possessed by a single view.

But from a pale mineral terrace, several creatures looked outward together.

They saw reed flats bending along the new river. Black stone islands carried groves older than memory and younger than the stone beneath them. Far away, rain crossed the vegetation as a dark moving veil. Beyond it lay places none of them knew.

One creature stepped forward.

Its foot pressed into soft red mud.

The print remained.

Rain entered it. A seed lodged there. The creature moved on before the seed opened and never knew what followed.

The power knew.

This time, that did not mean the act belonged only to the power.

The world remembered too.
