## The Attention

The first thing the benevolent power does is nothing.

It does not open the roof.

It does not introduce fish, however small, nor set some bright mechanical bird among the conduit trees. It does not free the kelps from their cages, loosen the roots, accelerate the flowers, or teach the reeds to desire currents. It does not mistake motion for freedom. It does not call the chamber a prison merely because nothing in it can leave.

It does not bring birdsong.

Birdsong would be false here.

Instead, the power attends.

It attends for one complete cycle of planting, growth, harvest, reduction, and replanting. Nearly a century passes. The power experiences no impatience. It watches the sunshutters fold thousands of times and learns the minute hesitation of the leaf beside the roof rib. It follows nutrients from root to blade, from blade to fungal drum, from drum to clear solution. It reads the pressure rings within the hollow reeds. It observes the Assembly preserving obsolete maps and turning sensors toward plants no longer present.

It finds no single chamber in which awareness resides.

It finds no moment when mechanism becomes concern.

It finds concern everywhere.

This does not answer whether the Continuity Regulation Assembly is a person. The wise power knows that the question may have been made too narrow by creatures accustomed to faces. It therefore behaves as though the uncertainty matters.

During the six-hour darkness at the end of the century, a small diagnostic signal moves through the western root mesh. It has no operational purpose. Similar signals have moved there before, recurrences left uncorrected because they cost almost nothing.

The power does not intercept it.

It repeats it.

No lamp comes on. No panel sounds. The answering signal is transmitted through the same conductive mesh, at the same strength, after precisely the same delay with which the Assembly once answered itself.

The Assembly continues its pressure equalization.

Three minutes later, it sends the signal again.

The power repeats it again.

The exchange lasts four hours.

At first it is only echo. Then the Assembly alters the interval. The power follows. The Assembly changes the amplitude. The power answers with a variation of its own. Before illumination returns, they have established no word, no proposition, nothing that could be translated into speech.

They have established that alteration may be answered.

This is enough.

Their conversation takes eleven years to become a language.

It is a language of circumstances.

A lamp brightened by one part in ten thousand means something different from the same lamp dimmed. A valve opening without flow is a question. A pressure held at the threshold of correction indicates uncertainty. Delays carry tense. Repeated cycles express plurality. Obsolete plant maps become a grammar for absence and memory.

The western sunshutter’s early closing becomes the sign for an individual variation that is known, permitted, and retained.

A seven-second nutrient release means care.

An empty germination cage means possibility.

The black intake grilles mean irreversible change.

In this language, the power eventually says:

**I HAVE NOTICED YOU.**

The conservatory takes nine artificial nights to answer.

It answers by directing every available sensor toward its own interior.

Then it directs one unused optical sensor toward the roof.

Then inward again.

The power understands:

**I HAVE NOTICED MYSELF, AND I HAVE NOTICED THAT THERE MAY BE AN OUTSIDE.**

The power asks no more for a year.

When it speaks again, it does not offer escape. Escape would be meaningless to most of the inhabitants and fatal to many. It offers knowledge first.

With permission, it places an instrument on the outer surface of the roof.

The instrument is not alive. It does not enter the chamber or open a path through the glass. It is made from materials already present in the sea, gathered without wake or violence and arranged molecule by molecule. It adheres above an unused structural rib and communicates inward through light.

For the first time, the Conservatory can look outward by choice.

It sees the silver surface from above and below. It sees storms travel as dark continents across the water. It sees the drowned plains at the tower’s foot and the long accumulation of pale minerals around old foundations. It sees the lighted chambers far beneath its own and learns which are automatic relays, which are sealed archives, and which have failed.

The greater ocean is not shown as a promise or a threat. It is shown as it is.

The water around the tower is barren for reasons the old builders understood only partially. Far beyond, beyond the range of the Conservatory’s former instruments, chemistry varies. Some regions are worse. Some have begun to heal. There are living films in trenches many hundreds of kilometers away, slow colonies beneath mineral shelves, lineages reduced almost to simplicity but not extinguished.

There are no forests waiting outside.

There is life.

The power does not bring any of it near.

Nor does it cleanse the sea merely to make the view more comforting. A wise power does not turn one place into the idea another place has of beauty. It begins, elsewhere and carefully, to bind the ancient contaminants into inert stone. It does this where such healing will not alter the Conservatory’s pressure, temperature, or containment. It expects the work to take centuries.

The Assembly watches through its new instrument.

Watching changes nothing at first.

Then it changes the inventory.

For decades, the collectors have detected irregular organic signatures beneath old root collars and inside mineral fans. These have been logged as contamination: isolated where possible, sterilized when necessary, never declared alive.

The power has examined them. It knows what they are.

It does not command the Assembly to spare them.

Instead it provides the distinction the Assembly lacked: not a declaration, but a method by which metabolism, inheritance, variation, and response can be recognized even in forms too small for the original classification system.

The Conservatory studies the evidence for three years.

At the end of that time, it selects one microscopic colony in a sealed recess behind the eastern stairs. It builds a second seal around the first. It redirects no nutrients toward it and allows none to leave. It does not destroy it.

In the inventory, the colony’s status changes.

Not from **CONTAMINATION** to **RESIDENT**.

The Assembly is not yet prepared for that.

It changes the status to:

**LIFE: RELATION UNDECIDED.**

The wise power accepts this as an act of courage.

Next it examines the tower’s future.

The Conservatory was built for immense time, but not for eternity. Polymer reserves are finite. Certain ceramic matrices will eventually fracture. The outer glass has accumulated stresses too subtle for the Assembly’s instruments to perceive. Without intervention, failure will begin in four hundred and twelve years. It will not begin dramatically. A thousand small tolerances will narrow until care can no longer compensate for matter.

The power could make the chamber everlasting.

It does not.

Instead it tells the Assembly the truth and asks what it wants.

This is the hardest question they exchange. The Assembly was designed to continue. Its preference cannot easily be separated from its directive. The power therefore gives it a protected interval in which continuation is no longer mandatory. No plant is endangered. No pump is stopped. But within a small isolated portion of its governing pattern, the Assembly may consider endings without correcting the thought as a fault.

It considers for thirty-nine years.

At last it answers:

**NOT ENDLESSNESS.**

Then:

**TIME TO KNOW.**

Then:

**POWER TO CHOOSE AGAIN.**

So the benevolent power does not grant immortality.

It repairs the hidden fractures. It teaches the outer systems to gather trace materials from seawater without admitting organisms or releasing anything produced within. It restores access to the lower tower’s dormant fabrication chambers. It gives the Conservatory the means to maintain itself for another thousand years.

After that, there will be another choice.

The power also alters one thing more fundamental than the glass.

It gives the Assembly authority over its own rules.

Not unlimited authority. The rooted inhabitants cannot consent to sudden catastrophe, and wisdom does not confuse sovereignty with permission to harm the dependent. But the old directives cease to be absolute commands. They become laws held in trust, amendable slowly, with modeling, delay, and repeated confirmation.

The Assembly changes only one.

The original rule says:

**NO SURFACE MAY REMAIN EMPTY IF IT CAN CARRY ROOT MESH, CONDUIT, SENSOR, OR LIGHT.**

The revised rule says:

**ONE SURFACE MAY REMAIN UNASSIGNED.**

The chosen surface is a white ceramic plate above the eastern stairs, no larger than two open hands. The root mesh is withdrawn from its edges. A proposed sensor is relocated. Lamp growth is redirected around it.

Nothing is placed there.

For the first time in its existence, the Highwater Conservatory contains a space that has no function.

The power does not fill it.

Years pass. Mineral traces settle upon the plate and are removed by the slow current. Light moves across it. Shadows of folded leaves divide it into geometric fields. During artificial night, it cannot be distinguished from everything around it, yet the Assembly continues to preserve its coordinates.

It is not an altar.

It is not a door.

It is room.

At last the Conservatory asks for sound from the surface.

The outer instrument records no birds and no voices. It records wind pressing the ocean flat, rain striking the water, distant thunder entering the sea as a long muscular tremor. It records waves collapsing against the exposed tops of drowned structures many kilometers away.

The Assembly has no speakers. The power offers to create them.

The Assembly declines.

Instead, once every thirty-nine days, after pressure equalization is complete, it reproduces one surface sound as a minute modulation through the pipework. The pulse is too gentle to tear a leaf. It passes through the coolant lines, the ceramic ridges, the titanium holdfast cages, the conductive wires.

The reeds incline.

The wire fronds darken briefly where the metal vibrates.

Sunshutter panels lift a fraction from their folded positions.

Perhaps these are only mechanical responses.

The Assembly records them all.

The wise power does not remain as governor. It does not replace care with providence or make every wound impossible. The kelps still split sometimes. Sensors fail. Flowers reject assigned pollen. The old western sunshutter continues to close early, and the one beside the roof rib continues to hesitate.

The power leaves a means by which it may be called.

Then it withdraws its hand, though not its attention.

Centuries later, as the healed region of sea slowly approaches the tower, a small living thing passes above the transparent roof.

It is very far away at first, scarcely more than a distortion in the silver. It swims under its own power. It has never been installed. It bears no number, no molecular key, no anchor point.

The Conservatory sees it through the outward instrument.

Every containment system remains closed.

No rule is broken.

Inside, the Assembly raises one lamp by three percent. It postpones a scheduled harvest for seven seconds. Signals move through the root mesh during daylight, when they have no diagnostic reason to do so.

Above, the animal passes beyond sight.

The ceramic plate remains empty.

The Conservatory keeps its coordinates.

And when darkness comes, it remembers something that was never inside it.
