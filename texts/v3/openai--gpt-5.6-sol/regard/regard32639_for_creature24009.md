### What the Power Would Do

At first, the power would do nothing.

Not because nothing mattered, and not because it mistook endurance for consent. It would refrain because the seam was intricate, and intricacy is easily harmed by kindness applied too quickly.

It would attend through one cycle of heat and cooling, then another. It would learn which fractures carried air, which held the nursery, which shelf received the oldest returning bats, where the crickets stopped, where the guano concealed a little moisture, and how the quarry’s pulse entered the rock. It would distinguish discomfort from danger, death from destruction, crowding from catastrophe.

It would understand that to widen the entrance might admit owls, snakes, weather, and killing light. To cool the chamber might doom the naked young. To wash the floor clean would drown the beetles and erase the stored substance of generations. To banish parasites would starve other mouths and perhaps replace old burdens with new ones. To make the place gentle by human standards would be to unmake it.

So it would not clean the seam.

It would not quiet the bats.

It would not turn guano into flowers or sulfur into gold. It would not grant immortality to every pup, nor rebuke the dermestids for receiving the fallen. It would leave the little hunters beneath their flakes, the flies in the fur, the mites in the hinges of bone. It would permit the chamber to remain hot, crowded, sharp, and difficult, because these were not defects laid over the life of the place. They were among its conditions.

But it would find the new fracture.

The crack encouraged each day by the quarry was still too small for any bat to know as danger. It ran behind the nursery shelf, pale within the basalt, advancing by increments through stone that had not yet learned how to bear repeated shock. In another season of drilling it would join an older fault. A portion of the roof would loosen. Perhaps it would fall at noon, when the ceiling was fullest.

Here the power would act.

Not with a hand entering the darkness. Not with a voice from the rocks. It would make no miracle that turned the seam into a shrine and brought roads, fences, lamps, curious crowds.

Instead, forty meters away, the quarry’s next exploratory bore would return an unexpected core: broken, sulfur-stained, and veined with voids. A vibration monitor would record a resonance outside the permitted range. The technician reading it would hesitate rather than round the number down.

That hesitation would be protected.

A biologist, consulted only because a regulation required a signature, would arrive at sunset on the correct evening. She would stand beside the fissure as the first bats emerged. She would count badly at first, then stop counting and simply watch the living filament continue beyond expectation. Her report would use plain words: maternity colony, unstable lava cavity, unacceptable blasting risk.

The manager would be a person capable of listening. The alternate quarry face, previously judged inconvenient, would prove richer and easier to cut. Contracts would bend without breaking. No one would feel commanded by heaven. Each would believe, truthfully, that the decision had been made from evidence, caution, and ordinary human judgment.

The drills would move farther along the ridge.

The power would not eliminate every vibration. Distance would soften it. The nursery crack would continue to lengthen, but now at the pace of heat and cooling rather than machinery. Where collapse remained inevitable, two small blocks would settle against each other during winter, when the colony was thinnest, forming a rough support. No bat would understand this as rescue. Beetles would vanish beneath the disturbance and return.

Beyond the seam, the power would make other mercies equally difficult to notice.

A shallow depression in the plateau would hold rain one day longer than before. Night-blooming plants would establish along an abandoned haul road. Moths would gather there. A pesticide shipment intended for the valley would be delayed until after the season when the bats fed most heavily. A landowner, tired of maintaining a leaking stock tank, would install a safer water trough with a rough escape surface, believing the idea to be his own.

These gifts would not make abundance endless. They would make survival less needlessly narrow.

And inside the chamber, nothing would appear redeemed.

There would still be pups that failed. There would still be hunger, infestation, quarrels, old bodies failing to return from the night. The floor would still receive what fell. The mouths below would continue their patient reduction. Benevolence would not require pretending that death was absent, nor wisdom pretending that every death served a beautiful purpose.

The power would know each small loss as a loss.

When a pup slipped but caught one foot in a lower crack, a faint current might press it toward the wall rather than away. When an exhausted mother returned through the entrance, the stream might open around her for half a second. When a beetle wandered onto stone too hot to cross, a shadow might remain there slightly longer.

Not always. Not as payment for virtue. Not enough to abolish the character of the world. But sometimes, because small lives are not made insignificant by the systems that contain them.

Most of all, the power would preserve the seam from becoming known in the wrong way.

The survey coordinates would be kept in a restricted file. No recreational caving site would mark the fissure. No photographer would push a light into the nursery. No collector would peel basalt flakes aside to inventory the pseudoscorpions. Those who needed to know would know enough to guard it, and no more than necessary to possess it.

Years would pass.

The quarry would excavate another face. Native growth would slowly occupy its exhausted ground. Regulations might change names and offices, but the buffer around the seam would remain, inherited as an unremarkable line on successive plans. A few people would defend it fiercely without ever having entered.

Within, claws would continue to polish the holds. Guano would rise. Bones would darken. The sharpest edges would lose a little of themselves. The chamber would become more fully what its inhabitants had already begun to make it.

The wise power would not ask the seam to be peaceful.

It would ask only that the darkness remain possible.
