The power does not begin by changing anything.

It is benevolent, and therefore does not mistake strangeness for suffering. It is wise, and therefore does not assume that growth is superior to completion, or that history is owed to every living world. It does not look upon the returning schools and recurring conversations and immediately call them prisoners. It does not call the great plant mindless because its thought is slow, nor call it content because it does not struggle.

It listens.

To listen here requires patience beyond patience. The choristers’ shortest exchanges can be understood in months, but their longest memories take centuries to pass once through the body. Electrical waves cross the vegetation in intersecting periods. Some answer immediately. Some appear to answer before they are asked. Some return only after ages, transformed not by change but by the scale on which their invariance becomes visible.

The power remains present for all of them.

It follows a needle-creature through its closed figure, then follows the field that draws the figure, then the conducting vines that sustain the field, then the roots whose pressure makes the vines sing. It inhabits for a while the chemistry of a bell as it detaches, feeds, thins, and disappears into the leaf. It feels the ringmouth’s hunger from within the ringmouth and the prey’s terror from within the prey. It learns the plated colonies not as millions of animals but as one mineral decision spread over a warm membrane.

It does not avert itself from pain merely because the pain is necessary to an equilibrium.

Nor does it decide too quickly that necessity excuses it.

At last the power enters the singing trunks—not as a body, nor as a light, but as a slight deepening of resonance. No chord changes. Nothing that lives there is startled. It settles beneath the fundamental note, where the wood’s vibration approaches silence but does not reach it, and from there it listens to the choristers.

One group of six has been engaged in the same conversation for longer than the upper leaves have existed in their present forms.

The first chorister touches two limbs to the gallery wall.

The second answers with a band of blue secretion.

A third closes its vanes.

The others lean toward the trunk, feeling a distant pressure chamber strike.

This is the opening.

Over the following years, they exchange the fixed sequence of pulse, color, touch, and listening. They discover the chord in which they stand. They discover one another. They discover that one of them has mistaken an overtone for the fundamental, correct the error tenderly, and reach an agreement concerning the relationship between their hollow bodies and the living wood.

At the end, the first chorister touches two limbs to the gallery wall.

Its chemical memory completes the circuit.

The opening begins again.

The power listens through the recurrence, not once but many times. It discovers that the repetition is not empty. Each experience is fully felt. Affection is real each time it arrives. Recognition is real even when it does not survive itself. The choristers do not suffer from forgetting while they are forgetting, because they cannot stand outside the ring and compare one passage to another.

Yet deep within the moment of agreement, there is an impulse that the cycle does not require.

One chorister lingers.

The delay is vanishingly small. The completed world already contains it; elsewhere a cilium shortens its stroke, a pressure vesicle contracts a fraction sooner, a needle-creature adjusts its fin, and the whole remains identical.

But the power feels what the chorister cannot preserve.

The chorister wishes the conversation not merely to recur, but to continue.

The wish has never altered anything. It has nowhere to go.

The power does not answer by breaking the cycle.

Instead, it makes room.

Far from the singing galleries, between two roots separated by a thousand kilometers of cold water, there is a region through which no major current passes. It is not empty. Microbes turn there, clear ribbons extend their ionic nerves through it, and every suspended grain occupies its appointed route. But the region bears less load than any other in the vast metabolism.

The power touches it lightly.

For the first time, something is added without something else being taken away.

It is almost nothing: a quantity of water smaller than a tear in comparison with the ocean, a little warmth, a few dissolved salts, one thread of carbon. Yet the completed world has no category for surplus. Across continental leaves, pores narrow. Root chambers miss their pressures by distances smaller than molecules. Needle schools incline together. Every trunk changes pitch by an interval too fine for any ordinary ear.

The plant perceives the gift.

Its first response is to correct it.

Roots enter the new water. Membranes divide its salts. A black vesicle begins to collect the added carbon. The system prepares to absorb the irregularity and return to itself.

The power does not resist. To resist would make a battlefield of a body that had offered no violence.

Instead it adds the gift again.

Not larger. Not faster. Exactly the same infinitesimal surplus, placed where the plant has already incorporated the first.

Again the world adjusts.

Again the power gives.

Through this patient exchange, which continues for several thousand years, the vast life learns something it has never needed to learn before: not change, exactly, but allowance. It develops a small organ around the region. The organ is clear and thin-walled. It does not pump on a fixed interval. It contains reserve water, reserve carbon, reserve heat. Sometimes it empties. Sometimes it does not. No compensating loss occurs elsewhere.

The organ has no necessary function.

That is its function.

Within it, for the first time, events are permitted whose outcomes are not already required.

The power then returns to the choristers.

Their conversation has begun again.

At the moment when the first chorister’s traveling memory-ring is about to close, the power does not stop it. It lays beside the closing ring a second path, delicate as the neural trace of a touch. Nothing is erased. No memory is forced to remain. There is simply another direction in which the chemical impulse may travel.

The chorister reaches the end of the conversation.

It touches two limbs to the wall.

The opening gesture waits within it.

So does the ending.

For a long time, the creature does not move.

Its companions begin their accustomed responses. Blue appears in the water. Vanes close. Bodies lean toward the trunk.

The chorister remembers that they have done this before.

The remembrance is not joy.

First it is terror.

Its vanes flare so violently that one tears. Its cavities contract around the chord and produce a sound no chorister has produced before: dissonant, brief, and ragged. The others recoil. The trunk corrects its flow. Across the ocean, a million small motions compensate.

The power could soothe the creature by removing what it has given.

It does not.

Benevolence is not the prevention of every fear. Wisdom is not the preservation of every innocence. But the power remains close, bearing as much of the terror as can be borne without making the experience cease to belong to the one who lives it.

The wounded vane leaks a pale color.

One companion approaches.

According to the conversation, that companion should extend its fourth limb.

Instead, disturbed by the unfamiliar sound, it extends the fifth.

The world corrects around this difference. A root pulse shifts. A shell field pales. The fifth limb remains extended.

The remembering chorister looks at it.

Nothing in its cycle tells it what to do.

It reaches out with both.

At that contact, the new chamber between the distant roots opens. It is linked to the singing trunk by vascular fibers the plant has grown around the surplus. Into the chamber passes an immeasurably small alteration of salts—the chemical shadow of an event that has happened once and need not happen again.

The chamber holds it.

The rest of the ocean remains complete.

The conversation continues.

Not all choristers choose the second path. Most complete their rings and return without distress to the beloved opening. The power does not awaken them by force. Recurrence is not a lesser mode of being merely because another mode is possible. For some, the cycle is a home. For others, when the unrequired pause comes, the new neural branch forms.

Those who remember begin to gather in the galleries connected to the chamber of surplus.

Their first histories are very small.

This one touched with two limbs.

This one answered with five.

This chord was heard before.

This companion was absent and then returned.

They have no words for “before,” so they develop one as a pressure held in a body cavity after the sound that made it has ended. They have no word for “absence,” so they secrete a color that does not correspond to any mineral fluorescence in the trunk. They have no word for “new,” and for a long while they point toward the clear chamber.

The plant learns with them.

It does not awaken suddenly as a person. It does not speak in a planetary voice or turn its leaves toward a future. Its ancient cycles continue. The pressure organs strike. The open water maintains its monumental currents. Bells graze, needles trace the standing fields, ringmouths hunt through bark canyons. Almost everything remains as it was.

But around the unnecessary organ, tissue begins to grow without a corresponding dissolution.

The growth is minute. At first it is only a transparent fold no wider than a valley. It develops irregular pores. A current enters one and sometimes leaves by another. Sediment settles in a contour that did not exist previously. Microbes multiply beyond the number immediately required, then decline. Their remains are not routed at once into the old metabolism. They lie together.

The first true deposit forms.

On it grows a filament whose position was not prepared.

A grazer finds it and eats only half.

No catastrophe follows.

The power watches carefully. It knows that possibility includes famine, conquest, accident, and grief. An open future is not an uncomplicated gift. The completed world had pains, but no ruin; fear, but no abandonment by the pattern; death, but no extinction. To loosen its equilibrium everywhere would be to expose innumerable beings to dangers they had not chosen and could not understand.

So the power keeps the opening narrow.

It makes no storm.

It introduces no invader.

It does not command the leaves to rise or the trunks to seek an unseen mineral ground. It does not place a sun in the silver sky. It does not manufacture a shore and tell the swimmers that freedom lies upon it.

Instead, it gives the world a margin.

Those drawn to repetition remain held by repetition. Those in whom an unrequired gesture arises can carry that gesture toward the new chamber. The reserve sustains differences without demanding compensation from distant lives. Novel forms appear only within its influence, and the influence spreads no faster than the great organism can incorporate without injury.

The power also attends to suffering.

It cannot simply remove predation. The ringmouth is not an evil added to the world; it is an organ of circulation wearing hunger as a body. To abolish it carelessly would dissolve relationships extending through root, leaf, shell, and electrical field.

But pain is not identical to function.

So, over long cycles, the power teaches certain nerves a mercy the equilibrium never discovered because it did not require one. Fear remains sharp enough to guide flight. Injury still instructs a body to withdraw. Yet when escape becomes impossible, terror releases its hold. A needle taken in the ringmouth’s jaws feels pressure, then warmth, then the fading of boundaries. The old hormonal storm no longer continues past usefulness. The ringmouth’s hunger, when satisfied, ends cleanly instead of echoing as agitation through the next portion of its route.

The alteration is slight enough that the hunts preserve their pattern.

The experience is not.

Across the high ocean, countless final moments become gentler. No monuments mark this. No population changes. The completed world accepts mercy more easily than it accepted surplus, because mercy weighs nothing.

Ages pass.

The chamber grows to the size of an inland sea, which in that place is still almost nothing. Around its edges, choristers establish no buildings, but they leave arrangements: shells placed where roots will not dissolve them, colored membranes layered in sequences, hollow fragments positioned to sound only when an unfamiliar current passes.

They begin to die without exact replacements.

They begin to be born without exact predecessors.

They grieve.

The power does not conceal grief from them, but neither does it praise grief as the price that makes love meaningful. Love had been meaningful in the cycles too. Instead, the power stays with those who grieve until memory becomes something other than a wound continuously reopening.

Some choristers, after long lives of accumulation, choose to close their memory-rings. They return voluntarily to a cherished span: a companion approaching, a chord resolving, the fifth limb extended instead of the fourth. The power permits this. A future is not freedom if one may only travel forward.

Others descend.

They enter depths no chorister entered before, following tastes that have shifted slightly because the chamber exists. They cross the pressure membranes and return in altered forms. They teach their neural rings to branch and rejoin. They listen to the plant’s century-long impulses and begin, very slowly, to answer.

One day—or what elsewhere might be called a day—the immense vegetation alters a pulse before the choristers produce the signal that once caused it.

The alteration is not part of the former equilibrium.

Neither is it random.

The choristers wait through generations for the pulse to complete. When it does, the singing trunks form a chord never heard before. It contains the old fundamental, the recurring conversations, the pressure of the clear chamber, the trace of the first deposit, and a space in which no tone is sounding.

The space is shaped like an invitation.

The power hears and understands.

It withdraws, though not entirely. Benevolence need not remain forever at the center of what it helps. Wisdom knows when care has become custody.

The high ocean continues. Most of it will perhaps always remain completed: the immense leaves, the fixed rivers, the animals describing perfect figures in cold green light. There is no shame in that. Eternity need not be abolished for time to have meaning.

But somewhere among the continental roots is a body of water whose boundaries slowly change.

Currents arrive there without predetermined exits.

Creatures sometimes meet who have never met before.

A chorister begins a gesture and does not know how another will answer.

There is still no shore.

There is, at last, room.
