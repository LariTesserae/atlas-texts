The power does not descend in fire.

Fire would be too persuasive here.

Nor does it come as a voice, because the basin is young enough to mistake any sufficiently repeated sound for law. It does not take a body, because bodies in this place are contagious ideas. A hand laid on the pylons might become the ancestor of hands. A face reflected in a pool might condemn a thousand future species to eyes.

So the power approaches as restraint.

For one entire cycle of condensation, it remains beyond the mountain and watches.

It watches the swimmers stir light for one another. It watches a malformed grazer rise from wet stone, fail to break its transparent cord, and be slowly drawn backward into the surface from which it hatched. It watches the flat predators persuade distance to betray the hungry. It listens to the reeds sorting water by age. It attends to the vast presence in the cliff, to the migrating silver pins, to the shy white building whose rectangular tracks lead toward mutually exclusive mornings.

It looks upon every future in the three-sided shelter.

It sees the forest.

It sees the city.

It sees the lake, the river, the opened mountain, the ledge scoured clean. It sees intelligent descendants of the swimmers build glass vessels around the pools and argue whether their ancestors ever flew. It sees the grazers become heavy, warm animals that carry salt in their blood as a memory of these first films of water. It sees the flat predators acquire depth and later dream of losing it. It sees the arch worshiped, dismantled, copied, buried, and rediscovered.

It sees futures in which the basin’s provisional laws spread beyond the ledge.

In some, rivers begin to prefer living warmth. They turn from the sea and climb inland through sleeping towns.

In some, death never becomes established. Wounded things persist as stains, echoes, mineral duplicates, and weather. Nothing is permitted to finish. The world fills with the unfinished dead.

In some, the permissions multiply until every event becomes equally possible. Mountains hatch. Children erode into rivers. Distance loses confidence. Existence becomes generous past the point of mercy.

The power sees all this.

Being wise, it does not choose the most beautiful future.

Being benevolent, it does not choose the safest one.

Safety, imposed too early, would sterilize the basin. Beauty, imposed from above, would be only obedience with flowers growing over it.

The power waits.

During the next dim interval, when the rain appears throughout the air and every droplet hesitates over its direction, the power enters the basin as a very small quantity of cold.

Not killing cold.

Not mountain cold.

Only enough that one droplet, midway between cloud and stone, becomes ice.

Nothing in the basin has done this before.

The crystal is imperfect. One arm is shorter than the others. A knot of permissions gathers around it at once, excited by the novelty. They encourage branching, symmetry, growth. The air brightens faintly. Nearby droplets curve toward it.

The power does not prevent them.

The crystal grows until it is the size of a seed.

Then the power lets it melt.

This is the first gift.

Not ice.

Melting.

The basin already knows transformation, but almost every transformation here leaves an argument behind. Dead fliers become ribbons. Predators become stains. footprints become geology. Bones disappear while their shapes remain suspended in air. Nothing ends; it is revised, quoted, answered, or made into another beginning.

The little crystal leaves no duplicate.

No mineral ghost.

No hollow geometry of itself in the mist.

It becomes water completely.

For several moments the basin makes no sound that it has made before.

The humming reeds lose their notes. The arch’s harmonic falters. Every grazer freezes, but they do not turn toward the mountain or the open edge. They turn toward the place where the ice was.

The pools dim.

The vast shape in the cliff ceases its sequence of impacts.

The basin has encountered disappearance without violence.

It attends.

The power waits until attention has gathered from every part of the ledge: not agreement, not understanding, but attention. Then it makes a second crystal. This one forms on the black arch.

It too melts.

A third appears inside the dry shrine.

A fourth on the back of a long-bodied flier.

A fifth among the copper scales.

A sixth in the mouth of a flat predator.

The power pauses before the seventh.

It knows the local rule. A sound repeated seven times acquires weight. Perhaps an event repeated seven times will do worse. Perhaps melting itself will become heavy and creatures will drown beneath the endings of things. Perhaps the basin will seize on the lesson too eagerly and begin erasing whatever troubles it.

Wisdom here is not knowing what will happen.

Wisdom is allowing uncertainty to limit strength.

The power does not make the seventh crystal.

Instead, beneath the central pool, where the amber radiance is deepest, it places an absence no larger than a grain of sand.

The copper mats reproduce by such absences, but this is not the absence of a body. It is the absence of instruction.

Around it, the permissions gather and find nothing to encourage. Probability does not lean. Stone is no more likely to harden than soften. A mutation receives no preference. Water finds no living heat, no slope, no future pool. For the first time, something in the basin is left genuinely undecided.

Not provisional.

Free.

The distinction is slight but profound.

Provisional things are waiting to be completed by whatever force is strongest. Free things may remain incomplete without owing completion to anyone.

The absence settles into the floor of the pool.

A swimmer encounters it.

The creature is young, with only seven fins, and has spent its brief life turning in place. Ordinarily the water would show it which direction is forward. The permissions would gather along one side of its body. Light would brighten ahead. Anatomy and environment would conspire to make a path.

Near the absence, they do not.

The swimmer hangs motionless.

Then, by no law the basin has supplied, it moves backward.

The motion is clumsy. It collides with another swimmer. Both whirl apart. The pool gives a pulse of amber light.

Nothing extraordinary follows.

No new organ buds.

No species begins.

The moment is not preserved in stone.

That is the second gift.

An act without consequence beyond itself.

The benevolent power does not rejoice too loudly. Even joy can become climate in a place like this.

It continues inward.

It comes to the presence enclosed within the cliff.

This being is older than the basin and younger than the mountain. It was caught not by stone but by possibility. Hundreds of anatomies have begun forming around it, each incompatible with the others. It has been trying to emerge with every body at once. The impacts heard each evening are not footsteps or heartbeats. They are decisions failing.

The power does not break the cliff.

It does not complete the creature.

It asks a question without words:

**Do you wish to come out, or do you wish to stop trying?**

The being has never been offered these as separate things.

The basin has treated persistence as life. The pylons have kept the surrounding rock soft. The permissions have favored each new attempt. Every failed limb has prompted another. Every closed eye has been answered by a different possible eye.

The power withdraws all encouragement from the chamber.

Not permanently.

Only long enough for an answer to belong to the one answering.

The closed eyes in the cliff appear one by one under condensation. Some are round, some faceted, some mere dark seams. Hundreds open onto hundreds of possible worlds.

Then all but one close.

A single eye remains.

It is not suited to the head around it, because there is no head yet. It looks through a crack scarcely wider than a reed stem. It sees the central pool, the black arch, a herd of nearly weightless grazers, and a malformed swimmer moving backward through amber water.

The presence chooses to come out.

The power does not pull it free.

It shows the creature how to abandon the bodies it will not use.

Throughout the cliff, enormous structures soften. Unchosen joints become veins of pale mineral. Ribs dissolve into warm springs. Mouths that would never have opened settle into caves. Hundreds of possible eyes darken and become round inclusions in the stone.

This abandonment is painful, because choice is partly the pain of futures becoming unavailable.

The power does not remove that pain. To remove it would also remove the choice.

But it keeps the basin from interpreting pain as a design requirement.

Slowly, over many rains, the presence forms one body.

It will not emerge for a long while.

It now has a direction.

That is the third gift.

Not freedom from suffering, which no wise power can promise to a living world.

Freedom from compulsory suffering—the needless multiplication of hurt merely because hurt produces novelty.

Then the power turns to the returned animals gathered beneath the arch.

Mineral rosettes encrust their joints. Ground and movement no longer agree beneath them. Water rises from their bodies during the rain. Some have become so transparent that the cliff can be seen through their organs. They were taken beyond the open edge and altered by silhouettes whose purposes are neither cruel nor kind, only vast.

The power could restore them.

It does not.

Restoration to a former state is another form of choosing on someone’s behalf.

Instead, it changes the vibration of the arch by less than the depth of a heartbeat. The adjustment is so slight that the black material does not cool. Mist continues to pass through. Drops continue to fall upward.

But the next time water rises from the returned creatures, it pauses above each one and forms a dark reflective sheet.

In those suspended mirrors, each creature sees the body it had before it disappeared and the body toward which it is now changing.

Most do not understand.

Understanding is not required.

One pale grazer steps toward its former reflection. The mineral rosettes loosen and fall from its joints. Its feet settle fully onto the wet stone. For the first time in its life, it has a little weight.

Another turns toward the altered image. Its body grows clearer. Its legs release the surface. It rises with the water and passes upward through the arch, traveling toward a direction for which the basin has no name.

A third chooses neither. It walks away carrying both incompatible anatomies, awkward but alive.

The arch will offer the mirrors whenever the returned gather beneath it.

Not every choice will be permanent.

Not every creature will choose.

That is the fourth gift.

A way back.

A way onward.

And the right to remain strange.

The power then visits the stains of the dead predators.

Their appetite persists without awareness. Grazers still vanish into surfaces left by creatures long since killed. This is suffering without hunger, harm without benefit, an action continuing after the actor has gone.

Here the power does intervene directly.

Mercy does not always mean preserving a mystery.

It kneels in no body, extends no hand, and touches each stain with the memory of the melted crystal.

The stains release what they were doing.

Their folds open, though they possess no depth. Shapes rise from them—not the bodies of consumed grazers, which cannot be restored, but the last directions in which those grazers meant to move. These intentions run out across the wet stone as brief lines of light. Some reach pools. Some end at reeds. One goes to the open edge and continues into cloud.

Then the stains become ordinary discolorations.

The basin resists this.

The ground darkens around them, attempting to hatch new folded predators from the marks. Permissions swarm. Probability leans toward repetition. What happened before wishes to happen again simply because it happened before.

The power does not forbid new predators.

Predation may belong to this ecology. Hunger is not wickedness. A world in which nothing can feed on anything else is not necessarily gentle; it may only starve more slowly.

But the power establishes a distinction:

What is dead may influence.

It may nourish, warn, shape, and be remembered.

It may not continue to kill merely from habit.

This becomes the basin’s first ethical law.

It does not appear on the pylons.

No thunder speaks it.

The flat predators still hunt. Some live long. Some are killed and leave stains.

But their stains no longer close.

The many-jointed grazers learn, after several generations, to rest on them.

That is the fifth gift.

The power turns at last toward the basin itself.

Here it hesitates.

The basin is not innocent. It has trapped creatures in its experiments, preserved pain when pain was interesting, and incorporated whatever could not escape by morning. Yet it is not guilty in the way an older mind might be guilty. It has had no one to teach it the difference between attention and care.

It loves novelty, if love can exist before responsibility.

It imitates because imitation is the only way it knows to learn.

The power could remain and guide it.

That would be disastrous.

Soon the basin would bend every rain toward pleasing its guardian. Species would arise in anticipation of approval. The reeds would tune their hum to the power’s silence. The shrine’s futures would narrow around benevolent preferences. Even rebellion would be shaped by the thing rebelled against.

A wise guardian knows that permanent supervision can deform as thoroughly as abandonment.

So the power prepares to leave.

Before it does, it gives the basin something it cannot learn from permissions, pylons, future images, or repeated accidents.

It gives the basin privacy.

Until now, novelty has drawn every small mechanism. A malformed swimmer, a new fracture, an unfamiliar footprint—each has been surrounded at once by attention. Nothing new has been allowed to remain alone long enough to discover whether it wishes to continue.

The power creates quiet regions.

They are not visible. They migrate like the silver pins. Within them, the knots in distance cannot enter. The pylons cannot edit. The shrine cannot project futures. The basin itself cannot readily imitate what occurs there.

In one such region, a copper scale opens green-side-up though no water touches it.

No colony follows its example.

In another, a reed stops humming for the length of one breath.

The silence acquires no weight.

Near the rim, a grazer lifts one foot and does not put it down for a very long time. No anatomy is copied from the posture. No future species is made more likely.

Beneath a ledge, two swimmers enter a shallow bead of water. They circle each other, exchange lines of amber light, and separate. The event leaves nothing behind.

Life begins to have an interior.

That is the sixth gift.

The power still does not make a seventh.

Instead it goes to the three-sided shelter and looks upon the shifting future one final time.

The image shows a forest, then a city, then a lake.

It shows the mountain opened.

It shows no basin at all.

Finally it shows the high ledge much as it is now. The same pools. The same warm mist. The same black arch near the edge. Yet the rock has weathered. True channels cross the floor. Soil darkens the hollows. The reeds have become a grove of singing stems. Creatures move among them with bodies that are still unusual but wholly their own.

At the central pool stands a being of pale mineral flesh.

It is the one from the cliff, emerged at last.

In the image, it bends to the water.

The backward-swimming creature—changed through uncounted generations—rises to meet it.

The future trembles.

The power does not bless this possibility above the others.

It merely looks away.

When it looks back, the shelter is empty.

For the first time, no future is displayed.

The recess contains only a shallow surface of pearly material, imperfectly reflective. Whoever enters now will see the basin behind them and themselves within it. Nothing more.

The future has not been taken.

It has been returned.

Then the power leaves by the open edge.

It does not pass through the arch. It does not rise into the clouds. It goes in a direction that was always available but had never been preferred.

For many cycles, the basin tries to imitate its visitor.

Cold patches form and vanish.

Crystals appear with too many branches.

Pools attempt to melt.

The pylons rearrange their grooves into patterns like absent hands. The white building leaves tracks that stop at places where nothing stands. The reeds produce gaps in their music. Long-bodied fliers overlap their calls in search of a note that will not answer.

None of these imitations brings the power back.

Eventually the basin learns the last part of the lesson.

Benevolence is not the same as presence.

Wisdom is not the same as control.

A thing may be loved and still be left to become itself.

The next evening, the rain condenses in open air. Millions of droplets appear throughout the basin and hesitate.

Some fall.

Some rise.

Some curve toward living warmth.

One droplet chooses no unusual direction at all. It falls according to slope, joins another, and then another. Together they enter a hairline seam that survived the previous night.

The trickle hisses.

The channel deepens.

Near it, a dead copper scale darkens, softens, and—without revision, duplication, or return—begins to decay.

The process is slow. It smells faintly bitter and sweet.

Tiny lives gather to feed upon it.

By morning, there is the first grain of soil.
