The power does not descend.

Descent would imply a direction from which it came, a point of arrival, a disturbance in the air. There is none. The broad sky remains washed blue. The silver film at the basin’s center does not brighten. No shadow crosses the basalt.

Instead, attention enters the place.

It enters slowly, because speed here would be a kind of violence. It takes the measure of the buried circuits over centuries. It follows one body of warm brine down through porous stone and waits for it to return. It listens until each separate vibration can be distinguished within the roar: the compression of vapor in basalt chambers, the thin singing of quartz, the conduction through metallic seams, the fixed fall of water from every condensation face.

It does not mistake constancy for simplicity.

It attends to one plated creature beside a shallow hollow. For eleven years, nothing visible happens. Then a filament beneath the shell relaxes from a dry grain and settles against another grain a little farther into the damp. The change alters a current too faint for ordinary instruments. The creature’s innermost membranes darken in response.

The power notices.

It continues to notice through intervals that would erase cities elsewhere.

Its first act is to ask whether anything suffers.

Not aloud. No language is introduced into the roar. The question is carried through every scale at which an answer could exist. The power examines the tensions within the creature’s membranes, the duration of chemical imbalance, the recoil of tissue from damage, the electrical patterns produced by drying water and fractured shell. It studies the transparent threads when a pool contracts. It studies packets of layered tissue that fail to unfold.

There is aversion here, but little anguish. A damaged limb withdraws. A membrane resists dissolution. A creature follows moisture and leaves a place where conduction has become poor. Yet no fear reaches ahead of injury. No grief remains behind it. There is no prolonged torment hidden beneath the stillness, no consciousness trapped for millennia in a body unable to cry out.

The power asks whether anything is imprisoned.

The plated creature is constrained by voltage, water, mineral, and time. But constraint alone is not captivity. A river is not imprisoned by its banks merely because it does not wish to cross them. The creature does not contain an unspent desire to run, hunt, build, gather, speak, or see the country beyond the basin. Its stillness is not paralysis. Its slowness is not waiting.

The power asks whether anything is missing.

This question takes longest.

It would be easy to answer according to the abundance of other worlds. There are no green leaves here, no flight, no games, no tenderness between parent and young, no memory of ancestors, no astonishment, no song distinct from the ground. The power knows all these things and values them. It knows forests that communicate warning through their roots, animals that cross oceans to return to the place of birth, minds that make beauty from the certainty of death.

For a time, it holds all such possibilities beside the basin.

A small alteration would be enough.

One irreversible reaction could enter the brine. One thread could build a lattice it did not later dissolve. A mineral packet could vary beyond the narrow sequence of its inheritance. A creature’s conductive plate could preserve experience instead of returning it to the ground. From that little asymmetry might come adaptation, memory, competition, complexity. In a few million years there might be color across the pavement. There might be swiftness. There might be eyes.

There might also be hunger.

There might be pursuit beneath the mineral stacks and shells overturned in the dry channels. There might be offspring produced in excess and lives organized around the avoidance of death. Memory might become attachment, and attachment grief. The basin’s perfect circulation could be broken into resources. Its residents could become consumers of one another. Time, admitted as novelty, would also enter as loss.

None of these things is evil. On many worlds they are the conditions from which courage, affection, discovery, and joy arise. But they are not gifts merely because they are familiar to the giver.

Wisdom restrains benevolence from becoming appetite.

The power does not seed the silver water. It does not cool one ridge or warm another. It does not interrupt a droplet so that, for the first time, the rhythm misses a beat. It does not grant the plated creature a mind shaped to desire what it has never lacked.

Nor does it make the basin silent.

Silence would destroy the arrangement by which the creature knows its surroundings. To the power, the roar is loud. To the creature, it is orientation, nourishment, weather, distance, and the continuous touch of the world.

The power understands, finally, that there is nothing here asking to be rescued from what it is.

But understanding is not indifference.

It follows the plated creature when, after nine hundred years, the condensation hollow begins to fail. The animal raises its shell. Its multitude of legs unfolds and establishes contact with the next span of stone. It moves along an electrical inclination whose destination lies less than twenty paces away.

The journey lasts thirty-one thousand years.

Once, a splinter of silica damages a living membrane beneath the shell. The creature turns by a fraction and redistributes the current through other plates. Once, warm rain covers the pavement to the depth of two fingers, and for six days the electrical slope becomes almost flat. The creature stops. Once, it encounters another of its kind.

Their limbs overlap.

For one hundred and eighty years they form a single conductive field. Tissue passes between them. Then the field divides, and they continue in different directions without recognition or farewell.

The basin retains almost none of this. Dust returns to its standing ripples. The altered membranes discharge. The track softens until it is indistinguishable from the old lines in the pavement.

The power retains it.

Not because an unwitnessed life is worthless. Not because the creature requires an audience. The power keeps the passage simply because to attend with love is to allow what is perceived to matter. It remembers each adjustment of each transparent leg, each temporary union of current, each plate darkened and cleared. It remembers the course of the journey even after no path remains.

This is the first thing it gives: not memory to the creature, but memory of the creature.

The gift places no burden on the recipient. It creates no hunger for a past that cannot be recovered. It does not turn experience into identity against the creature’s nature. The basin continues to release history. Elsewhere, gently and exactly, history is held.

The second thing the power gives is restraint against other powers.

It does not wall the basin away from chance. It does not make the mineral columns unbreakable or hold the climate fixed by decree. To protect the place from every contingency would be to convert a living equilibrium into a preserved object. Storm, fracture, and even eventual ending belong to worlds.

But intention is different.

If someday another intelligence crosses the black ridges and hears in the buried processes only energy to be captured; if it sees the conductive shells as ore, the brine threads as useful chemistry, the great circulation as an engine awaiting ownership—then the wise power will attend again.

It will not begin with destruction. It will make the intruders understand.

For one moment, or for as long as understanding requires, they will perceive the basin at its proper scale. They will feel a droplet’s century-long rhythm as clearly as their own pulse. They will experience the plated creature’s migration not as immobility but as a journey. They will sense the uninterrupted depth of the balance beneath their feet and know how much must be broken before anything here can be used.

If understanding is enough, they will depart.

If it is not, they will still be prevented.

No mark of this guardianship appears on the ground. There is no boundary stone, no warning, no change in the pitch of the stacks. The promise exists outside the basin’s circuits and does not ask the basin to preserve it.

The power then withdraws its attention—not wholly, but enough that attention does not become possession.

The plated creature reaches the new condensation hollow. It lowers its shell beside the quartz face. Fine limbs spread over wet and dry stone. A high tone diminishes. A lower vibration enters its tissues.

It does not know it has been observed.

It does not know its journey is remembered.

It does not know that a vast benevolence considered remaking its world and chose, with care, not to do so.

The warm rain falls. The metallic odor rises. Water gathers in basins no deeper than a hand. Beneath the pavement, brine descends, exchanges ions, and returns. The transparent threads assemble their delicate lattices and take them apart again.

Nothing has been improved.

Nothing has been diminished.

And when the same droplet falls into the same hollow at the same point in the greater vibration, the power—far away, if distance has meaning for it—hears.
