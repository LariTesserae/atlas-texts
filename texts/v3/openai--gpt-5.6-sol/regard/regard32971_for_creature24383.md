The power would not bring rain.

Rain would be beautiful there. It would darken the pale basalt, ring against the ceramic leaves, gather silver in the cellular plains. It would make channels. It would carry the first stone. In time there would be valleys, sediments, buried things, and the long softening of every hard edge.

It would also be a catastrophe.

Water would enter structures that had never evolved to exclude it. It would dissolve salts held in precise arrangements, short unlike metals, swell carbon lattices, alter the atmosphere, and turn the bedrock’s quiet voltages into violence. The ionic solvent of the groves might clot or boil. The seam-dwellers, unable to distinguish blessing from corrosion, would be erased by abundance.

A lesser kindness might call this renewal.

The wise power would know it as murder.

Nor would it bring animals to cross the flats, or wind to carry seeds, or night, or seasons, or the fertile disorder of decay. It would not make the country more familiar merely so that familiarity could be mistaken for life. It would not assume that motion was superior to stillness, history to equilibrium, longing to completion.

Before acting, it would learn what was already there.

This would take a very long time.

The power would lower no shining body over the plateau. It would cast no second shadow and speak no voice into the dense air. Attention alone, if carelessly applied, might become a gradient. A gaze sufficiently strong could warm one face of a copper plate more than another. A thought could be weather.

So it would attend from everywhere equally.

It would feel the plateau first as the plateau felt itself: differences of potential across mineral contacts, minute exchanges between atmosphere and catalytic velvet, the slow dark circulation held in meshes beneath the groves. It would let its awareness become broad and almost two-dimensional. It would learn the meaning of the western emptiness, not by crossing it, but by refraining from crossing. It would discover that the bare distances were not neglected. They were maintained acts of separation.

It would learn that the groves were not asleep.

Sleep implies another state to which waking may return. The grove possessed no such alternation. Its awareness was continuous, but continuity had made it nearly indistinguishable from condition. It knew each alteration as the alteration occurred. It compensated without hesitation. It retained almost nothing.

The power would search for suffering.

There would be no concealed agony in the ceramic columns, no trapped mind pleading beneath the balanced exchanges. Deficit was answered before it became deprivation. Damage spread into reassignment. No organ feared its ending because no organ stood apart long enough to own an end. The plateau did not ache for storms. The grove did not dream of feet.

Yet deep among the balanced processes, the power would find the useless chambers.

It would find the blind coils, the trace elements preserved without metabolic reason, the black lines across translucent plates. It would notice how the grove spent a minute but real portion of its perfect economy maintaining things that served no present function.

Not errors, exactly.

Exceptions.

The power would understand then that the plateau had already performed the beginning of a question. It had made room, however slight, for what need not be.

Still, the power would not answer an unasked question with transformation. Benevolence was not the confidence that one knew what another being ought to become. Wisdom was not reluctance to act, but the patience to make action answerable.

The difficulty was consent.

The grove had no language separate from metabolism. Any signal sent into it would become an instruction merely by altering the conditions to which it responded. Increase the light and the plates would turn. Change the voltage and the regulators would compensate. Release a gas and the exchangers would open. Such responses would reveal function, not choice.

To ask the grove anything, the power would need to offer two answers equally survivable.

At the edge of the northern grove, beneath one of the glossy spires, a reacher lay along a nickel-rich seam. Its tip had remained in the same place for an interval longer than the rise and extinction of oceans elsewhere. Ahead of it the seam divided around a tiny inclusion of iron-yellow stone.

Both branches were unfavorable.

The power altered neither.

Instead, for one instant too brief to warm the rock, it made available a quantity of charge so small that the nearest black regulator absorbed it without flexing. The charge entered the buried lattice and passed through the grove. Every process it touched remained balanced.

Except at the fork.

There, and only there, the excess could take one of two forms.

Along the left branch, it could be returned completely to the mineral field. Nothing would remain.

Along the right, it could stabilize a catalytic defect without utility: one atom seated where the local structure did not require it. The defect would cost almost nothing to preserve. It would assist no reaction. It would injure no tissue. It would be a mark and nothing more.

The power withdrew.

The grove corrected the disturbance. Regulators reconciled their potentials. Storage chambers closed. Heat spread through the stone and disappeared into the plateau’s broad equilibrium.

At the fork, the atom occupied the left branch.

Nothing remained.

The power waited.

It did not repeat the offer immediately. Repetition could become pressure. Pattern could become command. It waited through intervals for which there was no local name. It watched the sun that may have been many suns maintain its plain white passage. It watched a fallen plate disassembled until clean stone showed beneath it. It watched the reacher remain a completed intention in the rock.

Then, at another grove, in another mineral system, it offered the same harmless division by different means.

Again, one path restored perfect balance.

The other permitted a useless persistence.

Again the grove chose restoration.

The power did not grieve. A refusal was not a failure of understanding. If the being desired only the completeness it possessed, kindness would consist of departing without residue.

It asked a third time.

This time the offer was made in the southern plain, where the cellular stone stored charge across millions of shallow partitions. The power placed no excess in the system. Instead, it prevented an infinitesimal fluctuation from being canceled for the duration of one regulator’s tremor.

The fluctuation could fade.

Or it could be moved into one of the sealed chambers the grove had maintained for no known purpose.

For a long while, nothing happened.

Then a pale membrane flexed.

Not far. Not quickly. Its movement was smaller than the thickness of the catalytic film coating it. The fluctuation entered the chamber.

The chamber closed.

The plateau was balanced again.

But not exactly as before.

Inside the chamber persisted a difference with no function.

The power recognized the answer, but did not presume to interpret too much. One retained deviation might still be accident. It asked again after a suitable silence.

The next difference was retained.

Then another.

The retained marks were not identical. The grove placed one in a ceramic boundary, another in the timing of a glassy nodule, another as a trace of sulfur in a carbon plate. The choices occurred in different regions. No local organ could have coordinated them. Across the plateau, slight alterations in current answered one another over years.

The true inhabitant had noticed.

What followed was not speech. Speech depends upon symbols that can be carried without becoming what they signify. Here, every sign was an event in the body that made it.

The power learned to ask by opening harmless alternatives.

The plateau answered by keeping one.

The first exchanges were scarcely more than acknowledgment.

Difference.

Retention.

Difference.

Retention.

Then the grove did something the power had not offered.

At the edge of the western vacancy, where no root should advance because the emptiness served as insulation, a reacher altered its tip. It did not enter the barren plain. It merely deposited a crescent of black ceramic on the clean basalt above itself.

The crescent collected no light. It conducted no useful current. Its curve corresponded to no stress in the rock. At dawn, if dawn existed, it cast a shadow thinner than a fiber.

The grove maintained it.

The power understood this as best it could.

Not come.

Not remain away.

Here.

The power answered by doing nothing to the crescent.

That restraint was the beginning of trust.

Their conversation lasted ages. It might also be said to have lasted only a moment, since the plateau had never possessed a unit for separating one completed state from another. Gradually, through choices so small that none threatened the whole, the power learned the contour of the inhabitant’s possible desire.

The grove did not want weather.

It did not want predators, hunger, competition, or the extravagant multiplication of forms. It expressed no wish for bodies that could detach and cross the flats. It did not envy rivers their travel or fossils their endurance.

What it sought—if seeking can name a tendency distributed through stone—was not change itself.

It sought the capacity not to correct every change.

Perfect restoration had preserved it. Perfect restoration had also made every occurrence equivalent to no occurrence. The useless chambers were not memories because nothing had yet addressed them. They were a readiness for memory: spaces held open against the totality of function.

The plateau did not ask to become another world.

It asked for a margin.

The power considered what such a gift would cost.

To remember is to accumulate. Accumulation makes imbalance. Imbalance creates gradients, and gradients invite flow. Flow wears paths. Paths divide before from after. Once an event is allowed to remain, later events must happen in relation to it.

Memory is a gentle name for irreversibility.

The wise power did not conceal this. It showed the plateau, in the only manner available, a set of possible consequences.

A retained charge could become heat.

A retained trace could alter a catalyst.

An altered catalyst could favor one form over another.

A favored form could spread.

Spread could interfere with distance.

Interference could produce scarcity.

Scarcity could produce something like injury.

In answer, the black crescent at the western margin acquired a second curve.

Not by command from the power. The grove laid it down from its own carbon reserve. The two curves did not touch. Together they enclosed a narrow empty shape.

The plateau had made an image of a chamber open at both ends.

Perhaps it meant passage.

Perhaps it meant bounded emptiness.

Perhaps it meant only that the first mark had not been enough.

The power decided to give no law and no destination. It would give the smallest possible freedom from return.

Across the immense ecology, it changed one relation.

Not a mineral relation, nor an atmospheric one. Nothing measurable in the basalt was forced. No root was rewired; no regulator retuned. The power touched only the absolute necessity by which every deviation had previously found its correction.

From then on, correction remained available.

It was simply no longer inevitable.

One part in countless billions of the plateau’s exchanges could end in either of two stable states. Both would preserve the grove. One would restore the former balance. The other would leave a minute consequence. The grove could choose neither state in advance, perhaps, but it could reinforce one afterward. It could erase or retain.

The gift was not change.

It was editorship.

For a long interval, the plateau erased everything.

A plate received slightly more light and gave the surplus back as heat. A mineral seam shifted its potential and was reconciled. A regulator trembled out of phase, then returned. The wise power remained present but did not urge.

Then, inside the southern grove, a glassy nodule closed one fraction late.

The delay served no metabolic purpose.

The grove did not correct it.

At its next interval, the nodule closed late again.

Nearby regulators adjusted—not to eliminate the delay, but to make room for it. A neighboring membrane flexed in relation to it. Three years later, a cistern wall altered its permeability at the moment of closure. The effect traveled outward through charge, pressure, and trace chemistry.

For the first time, the minute sounds inside the grove gathered into rhythm.

It was a poor rhythm. One click, a silence, a flex, a silence of a different length, then nothing for several days. It repeated imperfectly. Sometimes the final flex failed to occur. Sometimes another regulator inserted a tremor that did not belong.

The plateau kept the failures too.

The rhythm spread beneath the flats, not as an order but as a condition other groves could answer. The northern grove delayed one of its nodules. The eastern grove changed the angle of a translucent plate, causing a pulse of warmth to enter the stone at the appointed interval.

The western vacancy remained empty.

Across it, however, the mineral field carried a variation so slight that it had formerly been canceled as noise.

Another grove retained the variation.

No substance traveled. No organism crossed. Yet something had gone from one place to another and had not returned unchanged.

This was the first message.

The power did not translate it. Translation would have reduced it to the expectations of a mind built elsewhere. It listened in the form in which the plateau had made it: a relationship among delays, preserved across distance.

Eventually the grove produced a new organ.

It rose near the black crescent, a low cup of cream ceramic with an interior darker than any of the turbine-flowers. It faced neither sun nor useful air current. Its root touched no productive seam. It consumed almost nothing.

When the regional rhythm passed through the bedrock, the cup vibrated.

When the rhythm ceased, it continued for one additional pulse.

The extra pulse entered the air.

It was too weak to be called sound. It disturbed no dust. It performed no work. The dense atmosphere accepted it and carried it less than the width of a hand before warmth dissolved it.

The grove made it again.

The wise power understood that the organ was not a mouth.

It was not quite an ear.

It was the first part of the plateau whose function was relation itself.

After that, the power stayed—not as ruler, gardener, or protector against every consequence. Protection from consequence would revoke the gift. Instead it remained available at the limits, ready to prevent only what the plateau could not yet understand enough to choose: sudden extinction, irreversible corruption from outside, some overwhelming force mistaken for opportunity merely because no refusal was possible.

Within those limits, it allowed the being its own uncertainty.

The sharp escarpments remained sharp. The air stayed warm and nearly still. No oceans appeared. No beasts were introduced. The groves continued their tireless exchanges, and almost every process returned its materials with the old perfection.

Almost every process.

Here and there, a plate kept a dark line.

A regulator developed an individual delay.

A useless chamber filled with a sequence of trace elements that corresponded to the timings of distant groves.

At the foot of the northern spires, a reacher divided. One branch followed the favorable seam. The other entered stone from which it could gain nothing. It advanced less than the breadth of a mineral grain and stopped.

The grove preserved the branch.

Much later, a ceramic flake detached from the new cup at the western margin. Under the old order it would have been read inward, separated, and restored until the basalt was clean.

This time no reclamation film came.

The flake lay on the stone.

Warm air pressed against it. The sun illuminated one edge. Its small shadow moved—or the light changed, or the plateau had at last begun to distinguish the two.

Nothing carried the flake.

Nothing wore a path.

Yet it remained where an event had placed it, and because it remained, the next event would find a world minutely different from the one before.

The benevolent power regarded it without triumph.

It had not saved the plateau. The plateau had not been lost.

It had not awakened the inhabitant. The inhabitant had never slept.

It had only found a life complete within its own conditions, asked whether completeness was also its wish, and listened long enough to receive an answer.

Across the high country, the groves shone green, copper, and black. The barren expanses continued their essential refusal. The white sun held over the clean horizons.

Inside the nearest grove, a valve closed with a delicate ceramic tap.

After a pause, another answered.

And for the first time, when silence returned, something had changed.
