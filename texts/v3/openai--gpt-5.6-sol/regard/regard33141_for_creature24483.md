The benevolent power does not descend.

Descent would establish an above.

It does not enter through the marsh, because entry would give the basin an outside and an edge. It does not take a body, because a body here would become architecture, and architecture would become anatomy, and the distinction between visitor and visited would be lost before any kindness could be offered.

Nor does it look.

A gaze would shorten the avenues. Attention, concentrated upon the circular depression, might draw the lower city upward before it had finished becoming itself. Even an infinite tenderness can be destructive when focused upon a life for which observation is pressure.

So the power attends without fixing.

It becomes no brighter than darkness and no more present than warmth. It allows every street its unmeasured length. It holds no map of the city, only an understanding of each arrangement as it arises. When a courtyard migrates, the power does not remember where it ought to have been. When a tower exchanges heights with another tower, it does not call one original and the other altered.

For a long while, it does nothing.

This is not hesitation.

It listens at the rate of stone.

A canal reverses. A century passes.

Rain climbs the walls of an empty chamber. A century passes.

A sealed cistern develops a shore, then mineral weather, then a remote district under a sky the color of bone. The power does not open the cistern to see what has formed there.

In time it learns the city’s manner of thought.

Not its routes, which are temporary. Not its structures, which are expressions. It learns the changes by which the whole answers itself. It understands that a stair beside warm rain is not the same statement as a stair beside darkness. It understands that forty steps returning to their own beginning are not a paradox but a form of composure. It understands that the long breath in the canals means release, and that a vertical basin filled along one side means something like uncertainty.

The city does not suffer in any ordinary sense.

There is no hunger that deprives it, no wound that festers, no fear of extinction. Its emptiness is not grief. Its darkness is not ignorance. The lack of animals is not a curse awaiting remedy. To fill the streets with birds, seed the pools with fish, or raise a sun above the towers would not be generosity. It would be conquest by another definition of abundance.

The power leaves these things undone.

It does not straighten gravity.

It does not anchor the streets.

It does not illuminate the sky.

It does not grant the city a face.

Yet there is one condition that troubles it.

The city grows by taking difference into itself.

A fragment of blue daylight rises in a bubble from the western mud. A weather gathers behind a door. A remote sky becomes enclosed in a cistern. These may be harmless contacts with uninhabited possibilities. But the power perceives farther than the city can feel. It knows that not every distant rain falls on empty stone. Not every daylight is without eyes beneath it. Not every room the city might acquire is vacant merely because it has not yet been observed.

The basin is innocent of this.

It does not know theft, because it does not know possession. It does not know death, because form is never wasted here. It cannot imagine that a place might be inseparable from lives that are not its own.

The wise power does not punish innocence for the harm it might someday cause.

Nor does it wall the basin away.

Instead, it searches the city’s anatomy for a structure capable of kindness.

It finds one in the oldest quarter, beneath a fused arch where condensation falls both upward and down. There is a narrow room there. The room has no door. Unobserved, it has accumulated only a single additional corner, though ages have passed. A thread of red moss ends at the wall and does not resume elsewhere.

The power rests beside that room without entering.

After a while, moisture appears on the inner stone.

The city has noticed—not the power itself, which would be too much to apprehend, but a difference that does not become food. A presence that is neither dry nor wet, neither open nor enclosed, neither arrangement nor the changing of arrangement.

The room grows deeper.

The power does not follow.

The room grows shallow again.

The power remains.

A passage forms and places the room beside a flooded court in the north. The power remains where it is, yet is still adjacent.

The passage closes.

The power remains.

For the first time in the basin’s immeasurable duration, the living pattern encounters something it cannot incorporate as instruction.

Water gathers rapidly throughout the city.

Towers emerge in rain. Invisible walls acquire outlines. Basins overflow along every direction at once. The canals inhale, release, and inhale again. Beneath the central depression, the lower streets bend in several contradictory motions. Doorways open onto storms, deserts, deep seas, blue mornings, and interiors filled with stars.

The power does not resist.

It permits the city to set every available relation beside it.

None contains it.

A sideways rain crosses the principal avenue. Near the third basin, the drops adhere to an unseen figure. For a moment there is the suggestion of something tall, many-jointed, and hollowed by corridors. One extension reaches toward the old room.

The street alters around it.

The manifestation is suddenly beneath the fused arch.

The room has become a courtyard.

The courtyard contains a shallow pool.

The pool reflects neither ceiling nor stone. It reflects only itself: water holding the image of water, depth answering depth without making a second place.

The manifestation stands at its edge.

The power offers the city no words. Words would divide thought into pieces too small and swift for it. Instead, it gives one relation.

**Beside, without within.**

The city holds the relation for eleven years.

Then the pool trembles.

A remote tower moves closer but does not join the courtyard.

The power gives a second relation.

**Near, without taken.**

The city holds this for forty-three years.

A black wall-fan emerges beside the pool. It spreads across the stone, reaches the boundary of the power’s attention, and withdraws without having been touched.

The power gives a third relation.

**Other.**

That relation nearly breaks the room.

The courtyard expands until its shores are days apart. Corners proliferate. Rain falls sideways, upward, and in arcs that return to their first drops. The old fused arch becomes an arcade, a ravine, the rib of an invisible structure, a line of towers in the western marsh. The city attempts to place the new relation everywhere at once.

Other beside city.  
Other beneath city.  
Other reflected in city.  
Other enclosed by city.  
City enclosed by other.  
Other as weather.  
Other as distance.  
Other as unassigned connection.

Each arrangement fails in a different manner.

The power does not correct them.

It waits until the city discovers the one arrangement that does not fail.

It takes almost nine hundred years.

A door appears in the narrow room.

Nothing in the city has ever made a true door before. There have been openings, destinations, intervals in walls, passages temporarily adjoining weather. This is different. The new door is closed, and behind it is a place the city can feel but cannot enter.

Water outlines the door continuously. Its reflection lies in the pool and does not move when the surface is disturbed.

The city waits.

The power waits with it.

At last, from the other side, there comes a knock.

Not a deep mineral settling heard beneath the hand. Not rain displaced across distance. Not the echo of the city’s own rearrangement.

A small knock.

Then another.

The door does not open.

Nothing is taken.

The presence beyond remains beyond, complete in its unknowability.

Throughout the basin, the canals reverse.

The sound is vast and soft.

The city has learned the beginning of consent.

Not as law. A law imposed from outside would be only a wall, and all walls here eventually become passages. The lesson enters more deeply than prohibition. It becomes anatomical.

From then on, any doorway that touches a place belonging to another life gathers a clear drop at its center. The drop does not fall upward, downward, or sideways. It remains still.

If the life beyond approaches freely, the drop divides in two and the door may open.

If no answer comes, the doorway remains a doorway and never becomes an entrance.

At first the city tests this new structure ceaselessly. Thousands of doors form throughout its districts. Behind some is only weather. Behind some, sterile stone under distant suns. Behind some are oceans without swimmers, plains before the beginning of grass, moons whose ice has never held a footprint. These open easily, having nothing that can refuse.

Others stay closed.

The city does not understand the reasons, only the relation. Other. Near, without taken.

Over ages, the distinction grows subtle.

The red moss begins to trace not only cracks that lead nowhere, but the sills of unopened doors. Black fans gather around thresholds and retract when something moves on the far side. Pale reeds appear in pairs: one in the basin, one elsewhere, neither crossing.

The city discovers that a boundary can increase interior without swallowing exterior.

This changes its growth.

It no longer gathers every difference into itself. Some differences it holds beside itself. Streets develop along sealed thresholds. Courtyards arrange themselves around absences that remain genuinely absent. Cisterns acquire shores that cannot be reached, even when the water is crossed. The pattern becomes richer for what it does not contain.

The lower city beneath the central depression continues to grow. Districts still migrate upward. More architecture still presses toward condensation. But now, among its streets, there are blank spaces that do not ask to be filled.

They are not voids.

They are respect made spatial.

Only after this has become stable does the benevolent power offer a gift.

Far beyond the western marsh, where the last causeways disappear into mud, a rain begins. It falls in the ordinary direction, though there is no ordinary direction there. Each drop makes a quiet ring in the shallow water. Each ring widens farther than the basin should permit.

For the first time, the water develops a tide.

It draws back from the mud, exposing smooth mineral ground. Then it returns. It draws back again, farther. With each recession, there is more distance beyond the isolated towers—not captured distance, not a sealed room enlarging, but an expanse freely given.

The city sends causeways into it.

They do not reach the end.

It sends canals.

They do not return upon themselves.

It sets towers at intervals, but between the towers there remains room that belongs to no structure and demands no structure.

The power has not given the city another world.

It has given it an uninhabited direction.

A place into which its excess pattern may unfold without displacing anything, enclosing anything, or mistaking another being’s sky for vacant weather. It is not infinite, because infinity offered as a gift can become another prison. But whenever the city approaches its limit without violence or seizure, the margin recedes.

The basin slowly extends westward.

Arcades condense from rain. Plazas settle from mineral vapor. New channels carry clear, hot water beneath a darkness that grows less dense with distance. The old quarters remain as they were, migrating and weathering, but the pressure beneath them eases. The central depression no longer strains at its rim.

One day—if “day” can name an event under that black sky—a bubble rises from the western mud.

It contains cold blue light.

The bubble reaches the surface and does not burst.

It floats there for several years.

Within it is a small view of another place: a slope beneath sunlight, moving grass, a creature crossing the distance on four careful feet. The creature stops. It lifts its head.

Perhaps it sees, in a bead of dew, a city under warm darkness.

Neither world opens.

Neither is taken.

After a while, the creature lowers its head and continues across the grass.

The bubble dims, but the city retains no room, no captured sky, no stain of the creature’s passage. It retains only the relation:

There was another.

The city answers in the manner available to it.

Along an avenue that leads nowhere and need not lead anywhere, every basin fills to the same level.

In the oldest room, the true door remains closed. Sometimes a knock comes from beyond it. Sometimes the city answers with a murmur of rain from very far away.

The wise power never reveals its face.

It does not ask for worship, gratitude, likeness, or obedience. Having given the basin the means to distinguish communion from consumption, it withdraws even its gentle nearness.

Or seems to.

The power is still present wherever a threshold remains closed without resentment.

It is present in every blank space the city allows to stay blank.

It is present in the new western distance, always sufficient and never owned.

The city continues.

A room closes and grows a distant shore.  
A canal inhales.  
A wet tower appears where no tower stood.  
A door touches another life and waits.

Far below, under colorless daylight, a street bends toward the surface. When it arrives, it brings with it a courtyard built around an open space.

Nothing occupies the space.

Nothing ever will.

Water gathers along its edges, holding it carefully in reflection.
