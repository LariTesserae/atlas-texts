The power does not arrive as a figure.

A figure would be too small in one sense and too violent in another. To stand before the towers in a body, however radiant, would force the country to understand an intelligence as a weight, a temperature, an interruption of rain. The plateau would remember the footprint before it understood the visitor.

Nor does the power speak at once.

It knows that swiftness can be a form of shouting.

For a long while—long even by the measure of roots—it attends.

It follows the silver threads down the fluted trunks. It feels how each is separated, flavored, and recombined. It listens beneath the roar, where basin speaks to basin in intervals of pressure too slight for air to carry. It watches the apparent ravines held at the lip of becoming. It reads the sealed wounds, the long records of gliders feeding, pollinators landing, strangers crossing, droughts diverted before they could begin.

It does not call the plateau one being merely because its parts are joined.

It does not call the towers many merely because their crowns stand apart.

Wisdom is partly the refusal to simplify another life for the convenience of loving it.

The power sees no hidden prisoners beneath the root mat. It finds no vanished makers enslaved into walls, no buried animal mind stretched unwillingly through a continent of stems. The halls are not mouths waiting to close. The bridges are not snares. The continuous water is not pain disguised as music.

Nor, however, is the country wholly at peace.

Deep in the pale scaffolds along the basin margins, there is a question.

It is repeated in every miniature delta that never receives silt, every loose-looking plate that never falls, every channel permitted the shape of deepening but denied depth. The question has been passed from tower to tower for ages. It has become so old that much of the plateau mistakes it for part of its own structure.

The question is not expressed in words.

If rendered into words, it might be:

**What follows?**

The inhabitants have built beginnings with inexhaustible tenderness. They have studied the instant before change from every side. They have preserved possibility so perfectly that possibility has become enclosure.

They do not suffer as an animal suffers in a cage. There is no pacing, no wound, no hunger for a landscape beyond the escarpment. Their unease is slower and less certain. It appears as an unnecessary stair added beside an older unnecessary stair. It appears as a spillway tuned almost, but never entirely, beyond the harmonic range of its basin. It appears in the knockers’ fourth concussion, which has acquired variations no tower will acknowledge originating.

The wise power notices.

Still, it does not break the escarpment.

It does not bring winter.

It does not teach predation, scatter stone, interrupt the returning water, or declare death necessary merely because death is familiar elsewhere. Benevolence is not the enforcement of a universal lesson. The power has seen immortal things that flourished and mortal things that did not. It has seen decay nourish worlds, and decay consume them. It will not mistake its own breadth of experience for permission.

Instead, it asks to be noticed.

At the outermost edge of the highest terrace, where the root wall descends into cloud, one droplet fails to fall.

It hangs from the tip of a pale root.

There is nothing extraordinary in its brightness. It contains no image, no flame, no impossible color. Yet the droplet does not swell, descend, evaporate, or return through the hidden channels. It remains outside the circulation.

The plateau detects it almost immediately.

“Immediately,” here, means that within three rainy seasons the nearest pool contracts by the width of a membrane. Within nine, a row of flowers turns toward the escarpment. Within forty, smooth-barked paths begin conducting faint pulses away from the edge. The nearest tower changes the mineral taste of its water, though no minerals are present.

Farther towers respond.

The broad hydraulic speech shifts around the suspended drop. Questions move through reservoirs and hollow stems. A low note takes shape under the ordinary roar. The knockers call in fours, then in fours again. White gliders rise by the thousands, their petal membranes making the air briefly pale.

The droplet remains.

It is the first thing the inhabitants have encountered that neither belongs to them nor presses upon them.

They test it with mist. The mist passes around it.

They warm the root from which it hangs. The droplet does not evaporate.

They grow a small cup beneath it. It does not fall into the cup.

They produce sweetness, acidity, red sealing sap, receptive pores, fine thirsty filaments. The droplet refuses none of these, because refusal would be an action. It simply remains available without being possessed.

Eventually, through the shifting pitch of the spillways, the plateau shapes a response.

The response is not a command. It is not exactly welcome.

It is an opening made in the music.

The power enters only as far as that opening permits.

For one cycle of rainfall, every basin on the highest terrace hears a new overtone. It is very quiet. No animal notices it. It travels through water, bark, sap, and the bright beads held at the tips of submerged roots. It does not carry concepts foreign to the inhabitants. Instead it arranges their own memories: the pressure of a traveler’s foot, the folding of a leaf, the hollowing of an old tower, the embryonic gesture of deltas, the endless brink of the cork cliffs.

From those memories it forms a question the country can understand.

**Is the edge kept because you cherish the edge, or because you cannot pass it?**

The plateau takes a long time to answer.

Some towers tighten their crowns. Some divert their water into older channels and become silent. One grows a curtain across a gallery that has remained open for longer than the lower terraces have existed.

Others increase their flow.

The tower that favors concentric basins answers with a sequence of contracting rings. The tower of branching forms sends one stream into two, two into four, then reunites them all without loss. The tower of unnecessary stairs raises a new step at the brink of the escarpment, where no body could ever stand upon it.

There is disagreement.

This, too, the power does not simplify.

It does not seek a majority and call that consent. A country joined beneath the surface cannot safely be altered by counting its visible crowns. Nor does it permit the most fearful tower to bind every other forever. It waits until the inhabitants themselves discover a form that can hold both caution and desire.

Their deliberation takes six hundred years.

During that time, the power keeps the droplet suspended.

Nothing else changes because of its presence. Rain continues through it. Gliders sometimes brush it and depart carrying a coolness along their translucent flanks. Once, a knocker strikes the root above it four times. The pressure of the fourth blow passes through the drop and emerges on the other side as something almost like a fifth.

That event occupies the plateau for another century.

At last the paths begin to grow toward one of the empty spaces they have always curved around.

It is a modest space between two low ridges, far from the oldest towers. Warm air gathers there in the afternoons. Its echo has been preserved with unusual care. No buried knot of memory lies beneath it. No major vascular channel crosses its root pavement. It is neither sacred nor useless. It is simply a place the inhabitants have kept open.

Around it they raise a ring of pale cork.

They line the ring with independent capillaries so that no consequence within it need spread unless deliberately admitted. Three bridges grow across the boundary, but stop just short of joining the interior. Canal creatures arrive at the edge, branch into delicate silver threads, and wait.

The country has made a chamber for an answer.

Only then does the power act.

It does not place something in the chamber.

It withdraws something.

For an area no broader than the crown of a young tower, the power ceases to preserve what the plateau has always preserved without knowing it was preservation. It removes no law of nature except the law that consequence must halt at the threshold. It takes away the invisible gentleness that returns all water, reclaims every loosening leaf, and holds every surface in the image of its own beginning.

The next drop that falls there does not return.

It strikes a leaf hollow.

The hollow deepens.

Not much. Less than the thickness of a glider’s petal. Yet a minute fragment of living green is dislodged. It slides over the leaf’s lip, enters a tiny channel, and comes to rest at the foot of the cork ring.

Across the plateau, water-notes falter.

It is the first true debris.

The fragment remains part of life for some time. It is green, then dark green. Its cells attempt to rejoin the surrounding circulation, but the chamber does not carry their signals. The fragment softens. Its ordered membranes fail. Colors enter it that the plateau has never grown on purpose.

Brown.

Gray.

A dull and tender black.

The inhabitants recoil. Bridges thicken. Pores close for miles. Red sap rises along the boundary, ready to seal the whole chamber beneath an impervious dome.

The power does nothing to prevent this.

The gift was not decay. The gift was choice.

For nine days the fragment lies in rain. Minute bodies appear within it—not invaders brought from elsewhere, but possibilities long held inactive inside the plateau’s own tissues. They consume and divide. A faint warmth gathers. The fragment loses its remembered shape.

Then the nearest bridge extends the final width and touches the interior.

A single root hair crosses.

It enters what remains of the fragment.

The country experiences soil.

Not the appearance of soil, not root-plates patterned like ground, not seed nodules serving as gravel. Soil: life no longer intact, transformed beyond recollection into a darkness from which other forms may draw.

The root hair takes in a trace.

The tower of concentric basins answers first. One of its rings overflows into the next and does not reclaim all the water.

The branching tower follows. Of four new channels, it permits one to clog.

The tower of stairs waits another seventy years.

Then, at the lowest step of a long and purposeless flight, it allows a bead of rain to wear away an edge.

Elsewhere the plateau remains as it was.

The cliffs do not collapse. The circulation does not fail. No winter comes howling across the warm tableland. The white gliders continue to rise. The canal creatures branch and reunite. Old towers transfer their awareness into root and pavement, still uncertain whether this is death, migration, or thought.

The chamber changes.

Slowly, cautiously, with pauses that last generations, the inhabitants enlarge it. At times they close it almost entirely. At times they let a rivulet run for a year without recall. Sediment collects. A true crack opens in a cork lip. One loose plate falls the length of a hand and breaks.

The sound of its breaking is very small.

The plateau listens to it for centuries.

From the dark accumulation at the chamber’s center, a stem eventually rises that no tower designed. It is crooked. Its leaf disks are uneven. One side grows faster than the other because the soil is richer there. When rain passes through its crown, the notes do not resolve into any established hydraulic phrase.

The young stem is not cut down.

Nor is it immediately joined to the common roots.

It is allowed to be strange.

The benevolent power remains near, but no longer in the droplet. Once the chamber was opened, the droplet fell. It struck the pale root pavement at the escarpment and entered the circulation like ordinary rain.

Its work is not to supervise what follows.

It has not saved the plateau, because the plateau was not damned. It has not perfected the inhabitants, because they were not a problem awaiting correction. It has not taught them mortality as though mortality were wisdom.

It has only shown them where their own protection ended and their freedom might begin.

In ages to come, some terraces choose change and some do not.

There are regions where every surface remains living, every drop returns, and the architecture of imminent collapse is tended with the old exquisite care. Their towers do not become lesser for choosing continuity.

Elsewhere, small weathering gardens appear. Leaves fall there. Water carries particles. Dark beds gather behind root barriers. Things feed on things and, occasionally, are fed upon. The inhabitants approach these processes with restraint, horror, fascination, and forms of joy too slow to resemble joy.

They discover that not every loss is instruction.

They discover that some losses are only loss.

When this happens, the wise power does not tell them the suffering was necessary. It comes as steadiness in the roots around the wound. It helps them contain what they did not choose. It preserves whatever can be preserved without turning preservation again into imprisonment.

And when they ask—through a basin lowered by one seed’s width, through a bridge permitted to break, through four knocks followed at last by a fifth—whether they should continue, the power never answers for them.

It listens.

It keeps open the difference between fear and refusal, between patience and paralysis, between a cherished beginning and a beginning that has been forbidden to end.

That difference becomes the one space in the country that is neither grown nor eroded, neither hall nor canal nor root.

It cannot be seen from the sky.

No path leads to it.

Yet, in time, it is where the inhabitants most fully live.
