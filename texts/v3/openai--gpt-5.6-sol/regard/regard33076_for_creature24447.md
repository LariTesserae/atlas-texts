The power does not descend as light.

Light would alter feeding. It would draw moths from their routes, expose the frogs, wake birds at the wrong hour, and cast unfamiliar shadows across the paths of the herds. Nor does it speak. A voice large enough to address the whole plain would flatten reeds, empty roosts, and pass through water as terror.

It enters by attention alone.

For one complete turning of wet and dry, it watches.

It watches water gather in hoofprints and leave them. It follows a mineral from deep clay into a root, from the root into a leaf, from the leaf into the gut of a grazer, from the grazer’s blood into a fly, from the fly into a frog, from the frog into a snake, and from the snake back into the dark labor of the soil.

It watches the oldest crocodilian fail to rise one evening and become food along the bank. It watches a termite colony drown, a thousand winged daughters depart, and one pair survive inside the softened base of a tree. It watches seedlings die by the million beneath their parents and one seed, carried in the gut of a bat, take root on distant high ground.

The power does not avert these deaths.

Benevolence without wisdom would save the calf from the cat, the fish from the bird, the caterpillar from the wasp, the tree from fungus, and the body from decay. Soon the cat would starve, the bird would vanish, the wasp would fail, the soil would thin, and the forest would choke beneath everything that mercy had forbidden to end.

So the power does not confuse kindness with interruption.

It asks instead: What suffers here that belongs to the life of the plain, and what suffering would erase the plain itself?

The answer takes a long time.

No mouth can give it. The grazer would ask for the absence of cats. The cat would ask for the weakness of grazers. The young tree would ask for an opening; the vine would ask for the tree’s strength. The mosquito would ask for blood. The animal carrying fever would ask for the mosquito’s extinction. There is no wish that does not press upon another wish.

The power listens below wishes.

It listens to continuance: to eggs waiting in mud, to fungal threads searching beyond exhausted soil, to old paths curving toward water, to fruit ripening where bats can find it. Beneath hunger, fear, infection, pursuit, and escape, the multitude expresses one vast inclination.

Not to remain unchanged.

To continue changing without being ended.

When the power understands this, it gives no commandment and appoints no guardian. It chooses no favored species. It does not make the plain sterile against disease, invulnerable to drought, or inaccessible to fire. Those things, too, have their measured places.

It gives the plain room.

Far beyond the visible thickets, where the lowland narrows between stone and dry country, roots begin over many years to loosen the earth. Springs that had been sinking into fissures rise a little nearer the surface. Floodwater finds old channels buried before the oldest living tree germinated. The channels do not become rivers. They become passages: shallow, intermittent, tangled, sufficient.

Through them fish can disperse when one basin grows too warm. Herds can shift when sedges fail. Trees can advance upslope generation by generation. Frogs can follow rain. Cats can follow herds. Fungi, seeds, ticks, beetles, and all the lesser travelers can move with them.

The gift does not preserve the plain in place.

It allows the plain to go elsewhere.

Then the power gives it memory.

Not memory as an animal keeps the scent of danger, nor as a mind preserves an image. The memory is distributed.

A few more seeds remain dormant through the first rains, waiting for the second or third. Some fungal partnerships widen the range of soil in which their trees can live. Fish eggs endure a little longer in hardened mud. Within the innumerable bodies of insects, slight variations already present cease to be lost so easily. On scattered mounds, old root crowns hold moisture several days beyond expectation.

Nothing becomes immortal. Nothing becomes immune.

But after flood, heat, disease, and fire, the plain retains more ways to begin again.

The power hides this gift so deeply that no creature can depend upon it. Dependence would become fragility. The herds must still seek water. The trees must still cast seeds extravagantly into death. Termites must still repair their chambers grain by grain. The power does not replace labor with providence. It only prevents every door from closing at once.

Last, it turns its attention outward.

Beyond the lowland are minds unlike the distributed mind of the plain. They draw lines before arriving. They see flatness and call it unused. They see standing water and call it waste. They count timber without counting shade, meat without counting paths, acreage without counting the lives folded beneath a single footfall.

Against hunger, the power raises no barrier. A hungry being may enter, take what it needs, and become answerable to the same exchanges as all other bodies.

Against curiosity, it raises none. A patient observer may come, be bitten, drenched, bewildered, and taught.

Against refuge, it raises none. The lost and hunted may find the old passages.

But against the desire to make the whole place simple, the power acts.

Survey marks rot overnight, not always, but often enough to trouble certainty. Engines meant to drain the lowland find their straight trenches slumping into older channels. Roads laid without regard for water crack where buried roots swell after rain. Maps disagree with the ground. Distances prove longer than measured. The firmest routes lead toward the least vulnerable margins, while paths into the deep plain divide, bend, and quietly return upon themselves.

This is not punishment. No worker is swallowed, no settlement cursed, no child made to pay for another’s greed. The power does not teach through torment when confusion will do.

Instead, those who come with plans of total possession encounter delay.

Delay brings cost. Cost brings argument. Argument brings time.

And time allows other human wishes to arrive: restraint, wonder, memory, science, reverence, the knowledge of people who understand that wet ground is not vacant ground. A hand raised to cut may be stayed by another hand. A boundary may be drawn, then widened. A channel may be left connected. A road may bend. Not every harm is prevented, but annihilation ceases to appear easy.

The power knows that perfect concealment would also be a kind of death. A place wholly removed from knowledge can be lost without witnesses. So it permits glimpses.

A child standing at the margin sees a firefly illuminate the armored face of a beetle. A hydrologist discovers that the plain holds more floodwater than any built reservoir could hold. A hunter notices that the herds have abandoned one route and leaves it alone. Someone listening at night understands, without being able to prove why, that the silence is full rather than empty.

These glimpses pass outward.

The plain acquires defenders who do not own it.

Meanwhile, within, no animal knows it has been blessed.

The old crocodilian still dies. The calf is still taken. Pools still contract until fish crowd against one another and birds gather at the edge. Parasites still enter blood. Lightning burns a dry thicket, and for three nights the low sky is red.

Afterward, beetles come to the blackened wood. Rain presses ash into the soil. Grasses rise first. Browsers arrive along the opened ground. Beneath them, roots encounter fungi that survived in deeper dampness. The burned place does not return to what it was. It becomes another district of the living plain.

The benevolent power asks no gratitude. Gratitude would require the multitude to mistake it for a ruler.

Having done what it can without making the place less itself, the power withdraws—not entirely, but to the distance at which attention does not become control.

Its presence remains only as a slight excess of possibility.

A channel connects when it might have failed.

A seed waits one season longer.

A migrating herd finds firm ground beneath floodwater.

A person intending harm loses the way, while another, coming to learn, notices the nearly invisible entrance between the reeds.

Nothing announces these mercies. There is no shrine, no bright mark upon the oldest tree, no creature set above the rest. The plain continues to be governed by occupation, concealment, patience, appetite, decomposition, and return.

Warm rain falls in sudden sheets.

Water beads along bark and shell.

A heavy drop gathers at the tip of a leaf, bows it, and falls into a black pool. Rings move outward among mosquito larvae. A fish rises. A frog shifts on its stem. Somewhere beyond sight, a grazer places one broad foot into mud and deepens a hollow in which, months from now, something small will be born.

The power attends.

It does not interfere.

And when the danger is greater than death—when the danger is ending—it places one unseen hand beneath the whole dark multitude and keeps the future from closing.
