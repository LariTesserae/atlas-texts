The power does not descend in fire.

It does not still the cataracts, straighten the distances, or set the sun upon an honest course. It does not call the city cursed. It does not call it blessed.

From far beyond the white field of cloud, it attends.

Attention, from such a power, is not merely looking. It is the willingness to know a thing without first making it resemble the knower. Thus the power does not mistake the city’s completeness for paralysis, nor the green being’s lack of desire for despair. It does not assume that mortality is the only form of life, or that sequence is the only form of meaning.

It perceives every terrace without reducing them to a map. It hears the cataracts not as noise but as the continuous joining of what elsewhere would be separate moments. It knows each vine as one and many. It understands how a stone may be carried away while remaining where it was, and how a traveler may take several roads without any of those journeys becoming false.

The power finds no prisoner at the center.

There is no buried mind calling weakly from beneath the mountain. No ancient maker has been trapped in its own architecture. No god is sleeping in the pool. The green things do not suffer from their inability to flower further. The water does not long to reach the world below. The city is not waiting to be released into time.

To give it change merely because change is precious elsewhere would not be mercy.

A wise power knows that every gift can become an injury when given to one who has no place for it.

So it does not give the mountain a future.

It gives the travelers a way home.

This is more difficult.

A road cannot be laid through the city, because any road would join the existing streets and become another expression of their refusal to depart. A gate would open onto itself. A ladder lowered through the clouds would return from above. A sign would divide into equally valid signs pointing in all directions. Even a command spoken with perfect authority would become part of the fixed roar: always being uttered, never having been heard.

Nor can the power reach into the terraces and seize those who have become spacious there. To gather them by force would require deciding which version was original, which memories were true, which shadows deserved a body. That judgment would destroy more than it rescued.

Instead, the power searches each traveler for the smallest thing the city has not made its own.

It is not the pulse. Pulses become uncertain.

It is not the name. Names loosen.

It is not memory, flesh, intention, fear, or longing. All these can be opened into districts.

What remains is consent.

Not choice in the ordinary sense, because choices multiply on the terraces. Rather, the inward fact that no being, however divided, is merely a place where things happen.

The city has accommodated travelers without malice. It has taken no flesh. It has extinguished no soul. Yet it has never asked whether they wish to be made spacious. It cannot ask. Asking requires a moment in which an answer is absent, followed by a moment in which it is given.

The power can ask.

Its question does not enter through the ears. Nothing could be heard clearly beneath the cataracts. It appears wherever a traveler’s sequence has spread: beneath the sleeper’s closed eyes, within the figure walking under upright water, along the green-lined footprint, behind the face that has become a reflection, in every shadow taking its separate street.

The question is always the same.

**Will you remain?**

For the first time, something in the city is incomplete.

Not the city itself. Not the green being. Only the question.

It contains an opening where the answer has not yet been placed.

Some travelers say yes.

The power does not correct them.

Perhaps they have come from lives made narrow by pain. Perhaps they no longer wish to be carried from loss to loss. Perhaps the terraces feel not like dissolution but like enlargement. Perhaps they have seen in the city a form of existence no less worthy than the line that brought them there.

Their yes is honored.

They remain in every place where they have become present. Their shadows continue without becoming ghosts. Their warmth stays in the walls. Their faces beneath the reservoirs do not drown. They are not swallowed, because swallowing requires an afterward in which something is gone. They participate.

The green deepens around them, though it does not change.

Others say no.

The power does not pull them out.

It places an interval in the roar.

The interval is very small. It lasts no measurable duration, for duration is unreliable there. It is simply a place in the sound where sound is not. The cataracts continue their endless concussion on every side of it. Wells still press their bass notes into the jaw. Galleries hum. Passages whistle.

But somewhere, for the traveler who has refused, there is a silence.

The silence is not located in any district. It cannot be approached by stairs, yet it becomes nearer whenever chosen. It does not appear on the reflective pool. It casts no shadow. The city cannot repeat it because repetition requires something to be present, and the silence is an absence freely maintained.

The traveler turns toward it.

Every version turns differently.

One rises from beneath the pillar-trees. One steps through the vertical reservoir. One follows a stair that counts ninety steps in both directions. One walks directly into a wall and finds that the wall remains a wall but is no longer an obstruction. One descends toward the lower terraces. One climbs. One stays perfectly still while the city ceases to be around them.

No route is privileged.

At first their division increases. There may be hundreds of figures now, then thousands: wet-haired, moss-marked, wounded, sleeping, old, young, carrying stones that remain in courtyards, remembering journeys that contradict one another. The city presents every accommodated possibility, not to prevent departure, but because it cannot do otherwise.

The power does not choose among them.

It teaches them a mercy unknown to ordinary worlds: they need not become one by declaring the others unreal.

They gather without collapsing.

Every shadow is allowed its posture. Every road retains its memory. The child-memory and the recent footstep stand side by side. The person who slept and the person who walked all night are both included. Contradiction is not removed; it is carried.

Only their consent is singular.

That is enough.

Beyond the silence is an ordinary place.

The power is careful about this.

There is no shining gate, no celestial meadow, no throne. Grandeur would resemble another impossible district. Instead there is a mountain slope beneath the clouds. It is steep, muddy, and covered with imperfect grasses. Wind moves across it unevenly. The air is cooler than expected.

Water runs downhill.

It visibly runs. A person can watch one drop pass a stone and not return. The sight is often terrible.

There are insects in the grass. Their movements are abrupt and purposeless. A small brown bird makes three attempts to land on a bending stalk, succeeds, and flies away. Somewhere below, hidden by trees, an animal cries once and receives an answer.

The travelers emerge there.

Some weep because the wind disturbs their hair. Some kneel beside the stream and remain for hours, watching loss occur in its smallest form. Some panic when footprints accumulate behind them. Some are appalled by decay: a fallen leaf softened at the edges, a dead beetle being dismantled by ants, fruit opened by teeth.

Their hearts beat forward.

Their wounds bleed, then close.

Hunger deepens.

Shadows agree to remain attached.

The power does not spare them these things. To return sequence while withholding consequence would be another kind of timelessness, and a more deceptive one. Those who leave the city receive the world entire: change, uncertainty, damage, repair, appetite, fatigue, distance, arrival, and the final cost by which moments become precious.

Yet they do not return unchanged.

They remember multiple streets taken at once. They wake from dreams with the posture of a shadow left sleeping under the pillar-trees. They sometimes recognize a courtyard they have never seen in the branching of a fern. They may hold opposing memories without needing one to be false.

Some become gentle.

Some become unbearable.

Some spend the rest of their lives seeking the way back.

The power prevents none of this.

Benevolence is not the arrangement of all outcomes into happiness. Wisdom is not the refusal to permit regret.

On the summit, the city remains complete.

No new wind disturbs its leaves. No bird crosses its terraces. The cut vines are still cut and uncut. The flowers continue offering nectar to a world without mouths. The reflective pool shows the mountain crowned in green-black stone above the clouds.

The being has not gained a future.

But its relation to travelers has changed, though change is not the word the city would use.

Where once consequence entered and could only be distributed, there is now a distinction the terraces cannot dissolve. Any traveler may remain, but none can be retained without consent. The distinction does not stand as a law carved into stone. There is no inscription. It is more like a shore established between two kinds of existence.

The city reaches the shore everywhere.

The shore does not diminish it.

For a long while—or for no while—the benevolent power considers giving more.

It considers placing a single true seed in the high court: a seed that would crack, root, rise, flower, and die. It considers releasing one drop from the cataracts to complete its fall. It considers allowing one leaf to yellow. Such things would be small elsewhere. Here they would be revelations greater than suns.

But the power understands what they would introduce.

A growing seed would create an ungrown past and an uncertain future. A fallen drop would leave a place where water had been. A yellowing leaf would teach the entire green being the existence of loss.

Perhaps the city would awaken to desire.

Perhaps it would suffer.

Perhaps it would love.

The power does not make that decision for it.

Instead, at the edge of the central pool, it places nothing.

This nothing is different from absence. It is possibility withheld from force. If the city ever inclines toward change—not by the pressure of a traveler, not through damage or infection, but from some motion that can truly be called its own—the power will attend again.

It will ask a question suited to mountains.

It will wait for an answer, even if waiting must last longer than time.

Until then, the cataracts fall without descending. The vegetation lives without becoming. The streets continue to arrive where they began.

And hidden within the roar, available only to those who need it, is a silence no larger than mercy.

It leads down.
