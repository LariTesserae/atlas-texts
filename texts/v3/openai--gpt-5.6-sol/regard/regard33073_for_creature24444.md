The power does not come as light.

Light would mean little here. A brilliance over the basin would flatten the wet faces into glare, blind what has no eyes, warm the upper pools, alter the patient routes of condensation. A sunbeam might be worshiped by a creature of leaves. In this place it would be only an injury from above.

Nor does the power take a body.

Any body large enough to declare its nature would crush chambers, silence hollows, break networks that require centuries to cross the width of a hand. Any body small enough to enter without harm would be mistaken for rain, loose stone, or one more pale concentration of the basin’s thought.

So the power remains beyond appearance.

It listens.

This listening lasts longer than the rise and collapse of several pillars. It does not infer haste from motion or simplicity from slowness. It does not call the threads primitive, the crawlers unfinished, or the warm nodules eggs merely because eggs are familiar elsewhere. It waits through cycles of saturation and silence. It follows patterns as they pass from pool to seam, from seam to body, from body to fitted ring. When a pattern returns after eighty years with one interval altered, the power remembers both forms and does not assume the change is error.

Nothing in the basin knows it is observed.

That, too, is an act of care.

A lesser kindness might rush to announce itself. It might stop dangerous fractures, hold leaning walls upright, deepen the pools, clear the mist, bring food, bring companions, bring names. It might decide that the place is barren and seed it with green. It might decide that the crawlers are lonely because they do not speak. It might decide that the basin is suffering because it is dark.

The wise power decides none of these things.

It finds suffering where suffering exists.

A pool-network is severed when a deep fault closes. For twelve thousand rains, its threads contract in patterns that no neighboring chamber receives. Crawlers gather at the sealed seam. They press themselves into it, stiffen, soften, divide, and try again. The rock is too thick. Warm stones form on both sides and cool without ever sharing their layered memory.

The power does not open the fault.

To tear it open would change pressures far below, perhaps bringing the entire central forest down. Instead, on the next long rain, it permits one drop to fall where droplets have always clung without falling.

The drop strikes the roof of a narrow chamber.

The note is slight. No human ear would distinguish it from the surrounding percussion. Yet it enters a mineral band whose continuity no inhabitant has discovered. It travels around the sealed fault, diminished but intact, and reaches the isolated pool.

Every colorless thread withdraws.

Across the barrier, the greater mesh withdraws with it.

For the first time in centuries, the two sides contract together.

The power does not repeat the note. It has shown a path, not built one. Over the following generations the crawlers raise two slabs and tilt a third. Water begins to gather beneath them. The new chamber strengthens that slight note and casts it into the hidden band. Communication resumes, thinly at first, then with increasing intricacy. The sealed networks do not become one again. They have changed too greatly during their separation.

They become neighbors.

The power understands this to be no lesser healing.

It continues to listen.

Eventually it discovers what, in the basin, most nearly resembles fear.

It is not fear of breaking. Fracture is one of the ways the basin thinks. It is not fear of the crawlers’ dissolution. They divide and mingle without grief, though certain rhythms are preserved after them with great care. It is not fear of silence itself, for silence reveals stresses that sound obscures.

The fear lies deeper.

Far beneath the black formations, the basin rests upon a descending shelf of stone. In some distant age the shelf will tilt. Water will abandon the upper chambers. The cloud ceiling will thin. The pools will empty through fissures faster than they are replenished. Threads will contract and not extend again. Warm nodules will cool in their rings. The last crawlers will flatten themselves against mute floors and wait for rain that does not come.

The inhabitants do not foresee this as an image. They feel it as a contradiction among long pressures: routes narrowing, deep tones sharpening, old intervals returning too soon. No one part understands. The knowledge exists only in the whole, and the whole cannot gather itself quickly enough to answer.

The benevolent power could prevent this.

It could lift the shelf. It could fix the cloud in place. It could make the water inexhaustible and preserve every pool exactly as it is.

It does not.

To preserve the basin unchanged would be to arrest the very being it wished to save. Its life consists not merely of water and resonance, but of alteration: cavities closing, routes being discovered, bodies becoming architecture, architecture becoming memory. An eternal rain over unchanging stone would sustain its substance while ending its thought.

The power seeks another mercy.

First, it must make itself known.

It chooses a region of open pavement near the basin’s margin, far from the oldest networks. Beneath the pavement lies an empty chamber with no inlet and no useful resonance. Nothing lives there. No structure depends upon it. The power places no object inside. It changes only a relation.

On a windless night, when condensation pauses and the black forest grows still, a stress begins at the chamber’s lowest point.

It rises through the rock.

The power has learned the speed at which fractures become meaning. It allows the stress to travel neither as swiftly as danger nor as slowly as weather. The line ascends for nine days. Threads in nearby pools contract. Crawlers press their limbs to the ground. Warm stones shift their heat by fractions of a degree.

On the tenth day, the chamber sounds.

Three brief pulses.

One long vibration.

It is an old pattern. One of the oldest the power heard: a rhythm that began in a drowned hollow, crossed half the basin, vanished, and returned eighty years later changed.

The chamber gives the first form.

It waits through a rain.

Then it gives the changed form.

Nothing in the basin moves for a long while.

The power waits.

A person might have heard only two dull reports beneath the ground. To the distributed life, the meaning is impossible and exact. Here is a chamber that remembers without having received. Here is a repetition with no traversed route. Here is something outside the continuity of water, stone, and flesh, yet addressing that continuity in its own measure.

Across the basin, filaments draw into their pores.

Pillars begin to answer.

Not together. The basin has no single voice with which to speak. A low ring sounds, then a flooded crack. A crawler lifts two limbs and strikes the pavement with the hard plates beneath them. A roofed channel returns an interval stored from a storm six hundred years gone. Deep in the central hollows, one immense formation alters its hum as water drains from its side.

The answer requires forty-one years.

The power attends to every part.

What the basin says cannot be reduced without doing violence to it. It is not *Who are you?* The basin possesses no concept of who. It is not *What do you want?* Want, for it, is not separate from pressure and route.

Its answer is closer to:

*You are not carried by the water.*

Then:

*You did not begin where your sound began.*

Then, after many seasons:

*What bears you?*

The power could answer with a revelation. It could press into the basin an understanding of stars, distance, life, death, worlds beyond cloud. But knowledge given without roots can be a kind of mutilation. The basin has no sky within its thought. To force the heavens upon it all at once would be like forcing an ocean through a hairline seam.

So the power answers only:

*Attention bears me.*

This takes the form of a vibration that weakens wherever it is ignored and strengthens wherever it is received.

The basin considers for nearly a century.

During that time a pillar falls, destroying three ancient rings. A pale crawler is divided beneath it. One half emerges after a year. The other remains trapped in a dry pocket and hardens into a layered nodule unlike any formed before. The power could have prevented the fall. It does not, because the pillar’s breaking belongs to the basin’s own chain of pressure, and because rescue without understanding would make every future event suspect.

But the power does not abandon the trapped half.

No water can reach it. No filament connects it to another living portion. Its pressure-pits continue to receive the diminishing ticks of cooling rock. It contracts slowly around the rhythms it carries.

The power remains with it.

Not as sound, for the pocket has ceased to resonate. Not as warmth, which might prolong the tissue beyond its nature. It remains as perfect witness. Every fading pattern is held in attention. Nothing that passes from the trapped creature passes wholly alone.

When it has hardened, the power remembers its last rhythm.

Centuries later, when a fracture opens the pocket and threads enter the strange nodule, the power returns that rhythm through a single drop of water.

The basin receives what had been lost.

This is the first gift, though the power does not name it:

not immortality, but the refusal to let isolation be absolute.

At last the basin answers the statement *Attention bears me.*

Its reply moves through almost every structure the inhabitants have shaped. Rain is diverted down old walls. Crawlers gather in rings until their bodies connect them. Threads emerge into exposed water despite the danger of drying. High within the pillars, the unexplained knocking descends rather than rises.

The basin says:

*Then we bear you now.*

The power, which has been known by praises grander than mountains and older than suns, receives this with humility.

After that, it asks about the dying water.

Not by showing catastrophe. It introduces a sequence into the open chamber: deep pressure, narrowing routes, cooling rings, silence. It sends the pattern once and allows the inhabitants to test it against the long stresses beneath them.

Their recognition passes outward in waves.

For the first time, the whole basin gathers around one event that has not yet occurred.

Confusion follows. Networks contract until pools appear empty. Crawlers wander during inadequate rains and become lost. Several structures are dismantled through competing vibrations. A standing wall, balanced for ages, is brought down deliberately, though whether in anger, argument, or experiment the power cannot tell.

It does not soothe them with falsehood.

It does not say the water will remain.

Instead it asks a question.

The question takes two hundred years to construct, because the basin has no distinction between survival and sameness. The power must build the distinction from what already exists: the severed networks that became neighbors; the divided crawlers that continued as two; the old rhythm altered by travel yet still recognized; the water taken into bodies and released in distant cracks.

When the question is ready, it enters the basin as a sequence of remembered transformations.

In the nearest translation, it is this:

*Must you remain here in order to continue?*

The answer does not come for a very long time.

While the basin considers, the power studies the lands beyond it. It finds old subterranean channels running outward beneath the fractured gray plain. Most are sealed. Some descend to salt strata fatal to the transparent threads. Some end in heat. One, however, reaches a chain of caverns beneath distant highlands where rain falls seasonally and stone contains familiar silica and iron.

The route is not open. Making it open would require force.

The power does not make it open.

It reveals the first seam.

Again, only a drop. Again, one note in an unregarded chamber. But this note returns from beyond the known basin carrying an alien interval: the acoustic shape of distant stone.

The inhabitants react violently. Filaments knot. Crawlers abandon warm rings. A high fin splits from top to base, though no pressure required it. For several decades the new interval is answered only with disruptive noise.

The power does not insist that strangeness is good.

It lets the strange note remain strange.

Eventually a small crawler crosses the outer pavement during heavy rain. It settles over the seam where the new sound enters. It listens until the rain ends. In the silence it does not flatten itself. It takes one more step.

Then another.

It dies far from water.

Transparent grains from its body lodge in the seam.

Years later, threads appear.

The migration is not an exodus. It is slower than the growth of forests elsewhere. The inhabitants tune fractures, raise slabs, divert seepage, and form new warm stones one hidden chamber at a time. Many attempts fail. Some routes lead nowhere. The basin does not abandon its center, nor does the power command it to flee.

It learns to extend.

By the age when the deep shelf finally begins to tilt, the basin is no longer bounded by its visible pillars. Portions of its rhythm inhabit caverns beneath the highlands. Threads line buried watercourses far beyond the cloud. Warm nodules grow inside stones of unfamiliar color. Pale crawlers emerge where rain is brief but thunder travels deeply through the ground.

The old pools diminish.

The power does not refill them.

One by one, ancient structures cease to sound. Rings cool. Pillars that carried memories for millennia split and lean against one another. The last water leaves the central hollows through cracks the inhabitants once would have sealed.

But before each chamber falls silent, its patterns are sounded into the routes beyond.

Not copied perfectly. Nothing living is preserved perfectly. Some intervals are altered. Some are misunderstood. Some vanish. Others combine with the acoustic shapes of distant caves and become patterns the first basin could never have made.

The original land dries.

At last only one warm stone remains near its center, resting in the oldest fitted ring. A crawler settles over it during the final rain. Threads rise from deep pores and enter its body. The stone cools. The crawler waits, receiving as much as can pass.

When the rain ends, it begins to move.

The power could carry it.

It does not.

The crawler crosses the wet black stone one patient step at a time, pausing between each to receive the landscape. Behind it lies a clean, dry track. Ahead, too far for any ordinary sense to detect, distant chambers answer through the ground.

The crawler follows.

The wise power accompanies it without lifting its weight, shortening its road, or taking away the danger that gives each step its consequence.

Far behind, the emptied basin settles under thinning cloud.

It has not been saved as a place.

It has been saved as a continuity capable of leaving one.

A final fracture travels upward through the oldest pillar. When it reaches the surface, the black face divides cleanly. One half remains standing. The other leans and, after many years, falls into the dry hollow where water once gathered.

The impact produces a deep, carrying boom.

Beyond the horizon, in caverns the old basin never knew, colorless threads contract at once.

Then they extend again.
