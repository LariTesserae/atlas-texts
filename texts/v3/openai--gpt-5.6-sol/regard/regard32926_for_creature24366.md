### The Mercy of Not Being Remade

The power does not arrive as light.

There is already light enough in the Clattering Crown, and many of its inhabitants would mistake sudden brilliance for the great discharge. Eggs would open before their tissues were ready. Old shells would release their holds. Spark lice would empty themselves into an unprepared network.

Nor does it arrive as sound.

Every sound here has a material consequence. A greeting might split a seam, call larvae into barren stone, or bring listeners down upon healthy machinery. Even a whisper, if spoken at the wrong frequency, could become an earthquake in the enamel gears of a rootwheel.

It does not take a body.

A body would displace water. Water displaced would become current. Current would turn the rooted vanes, and the rooted vanes would pump, and the pumps would alter the chemistry of chambers far beneath the rubble. There is no gesture small enough to remain merely a gesture.

So the power stops outside the crater.

It attends.

For one full tidal inhalation and one full exhalation, it learns the pressure of the place. Then it remains for another, because one cycle reveals only the machinery of hunger. Then for the long accumulation and release of charge, because without witnessing the white interval it would mistake interruption for death and death for waste.

It watches a listener spend three days opening a knuckler whose rhythm has begun to fail. It watches the knuckler continue rasping until the listener reaches its grinding organs. It does not save the knuckler.

It watches a juvenile rootwheel test fissure after fissure, each unsuitable. The young creature has no conception of hope, only an inherited sequence of chemical preferences. At last it settles into poor stone. Its vanes will never rise fully into the tide. It will live stunted and produce no young.

The power does not lift it into a better crack.

It watches acid spill from a ruptured sourheart and dissolve a bank of bell-globe eggs. It watches the released minerals feed a microbial bloom that would otherwise have failed. It watches needles consume the bloom, spark lice consume the needles, and listeners pry the spark lice from the braid.

It changes none of these things.

Benevolence without wisdom would empty the Crown of pain. Wisdom without benevolence might preserve pain merely because it is ancient. The power possesses both, and therefore does not confuse mercy with the prevention of every ending.

The Crown is not peaceful. It is not just. No principle assigns long life to the deserving or quick consumption to the cruel. The inhabitants do not deserve their suffering, but neither do they understand it as punishment. Their lives are pressure, appetite, rhythm, injury, repair, reproduction, dissolution. To remove all harm would not perfect those lives. It would replace them with something else.

The power has not come to reward the Crown for being marvelous.

Marvelous things require no reward.

It has not come to make the Crown intelligent. Intelligence is not the inevitable promotion of life, nor consciousness a crown to be set upon whatever attracts a god’s attention. The rootwheels need no knowledge of the whole. The wiregutters are not incomplete because they cannot imagine the network. The tarnish cells are not awaiting souls.

The power perceives, moreover, that there is already a kind of knowing here.

Not thought.

Not one thought, at least.

The Crown knows the stone through the listening membranes of the knucklers. It knows failure through the feet of the listeners. It knows dissolved metals through the colors of the bell-globes. It knows direction through the dead magnetic bodies of sterile needles. It remembers in grooves, inherited frequencies, shell terraces, abandoned braids, and the altered plumbing of sourhearts centuries old.

Its knowledge is divided because division is how it endures.

To gather that knowledge into one mind would create a being capable of terror on a scale the crater has never contained. It would feel every tooth wear, every egg dissolve, every shell levered apart. It would discover that it is eating the mountain beneath itself. It might understand its eventual death millions of years before it came.

The power refuses to call that awakening a gift.

So it attends without announcing itself.

In time, it notices a danger no inhabitant can perceive.

Far below the Crown, under the drowned plains and the old buried slopes, the mountain is changing. The change is not the ordinary cooling that opens fresh fractures. A deep wall of weakened stone has begun to creep. It moves less than the width of a microbial cell in a season. No rootwheel reaches deeply enough to sense it. No seam singer can find its resonance.

In twelve thousand years, perhaps less, the wall will fail.

The summit will not erupt. It will slump.

One side of the crater will descend into the black water. The shell banks will shear away in sheets. Conductive braids will snap. Rootwheels will be torn from their fissures, still turning as they fall. Pressure changes will rupture every bell-globe. Acid chambers will burst together. The mountain’s illuminated crown will become a long avalanche, and most of its inhabitants will pass below the depth at which their eggs can open.

Catastrophe is not foreign to life. The Crown itself began in catastrophe. The power knows this.

It also knows the difference between an ending that belongs to a place and an ending that merely arrives before anything can answer it.

The Crown cannot flee. Its young disperse only a little way. The nearest suitable summit lies across leagues of open water, beyond currents the larvae cannot survive. If the crater falls now, this form of life will not be transformed. It will be erased.

Here, at last, the power acts.

It does not brace the mountain. To make the crater permanent would be to imprison it in the present. The animals would continue consuming the rock, waste terraces would choke the fissures, and the hot chemistry would eventually fade. Preservation without change would become another manner of killing.

It does not prevent the future collapse.

Instead, it touches the tide.

The touch is less than a current. For a human hand suspended there, it would be imperceptible. It is a slight persistence in the half-day exhalation, a bias too weak to dislodge a grain of mineral dust.

But larvae are lighter than dust.

During the next spawning cycle, a few transparent young rise higher than their ancestors did. They drift over the rim. Most are eaten. Some settle on bare slopes and fail. A handful remain in the moving water.

The power does not carry them. It makes carrying possible.

Across the drowned range, the tidal exhalation acquires small continuities. Not a road, precisely, but a sequence of chances: a cold eddy that delays metabolism; a suspended cloud of volcanic hydrogen that feeds microbial symbionts; an electrical gradient weak enough for needles to follow; an exposed ridge where a juvenile knuckler can swallow its first silica.

Nothing travels all the way in one generation.

The first settlers die on the outer slope. Their shells catch mineral grains.

Later settlers find the shells.

Tarnish grows inside them. Bell-globe eggs adhere to the changed refraction. Needles arrive along a field written partly by the magnetic remains of those that came before. A sourheart opens a crack and leaves flakes where assayers can feed. Wiregutters lay short, useless strands that lead nowhere until other wiregutters cross them.

For centuries, the new places resemble failures.

They are too quiet. Their cycles do not align. Their electrical networks leak into stone. Predators arrive before prey are plentiful. Rootwheel young settle where there is warmth but insufficient current, or current without the proper dissolved metals. Whole colonies vanish, leaving only blackened grooves and thin metallic layers.

The power does not correct each error.

A path made safe in every step would teach the Crown nothing, though the Crown cannot be said to learn. More importantly, it would belong to the power rather than to the creatures traveling it.

The power supplies no design.

It supplies time and adjacency.

After nine hundred years, a distant ridge produces its first sustained conductive pulse.

After sixteen hundred, seam singers on a buried spur find a frequency that shakes loose food.

After three thousand, a young rootwheel reaches a fissure on the neighboring summit. It anchors badly. One vane is deformed. Two gear rings grow out of alignment, and the animal’s turning makes an uneven knock that would draw a listener in the old crater.

There are no listeners here yet.

The rootwheel survives.

Its pumps bring up warm fluid. Tarnish blooms in protected cracks. Filter animals gather. Wiregutters follow the gradients around its shell and leave braids in loops, most of them useless. Spark lice cluster at the junctions. Their first collective discharge is weak and badly timed.

It opens seven eggs.

Six hatch malformed.

One becomes another rootwheel.

The new summit does not become a copy of the Clattering Crown. The stone differs. The water carries less iron and more manganese. Creatures that were black grow purple shells. Assayers develop an additional gut chamber. The local bell-globes flash colors not seen in the old crater. Without the old acoustic bowl, seam singers tune themselves to the cavities inside abandoned shells.

A different noise begins.

Thinner at first. Less a factory than a distant workshop heard through many walls.

The wise power listens to it and does not compare it unfavorably with the first.

In the Clattering Crown, no revelation occurs. No creature raises its eyes. The rootwheels do not know that their descendants have crossed the drowned range. The knucklers continue to test the stone. Listeners continue opening the faulty and, sometimes, the healthy. The great charge gathers and whitens the crater at its appointed intervals.

Nothing gives thanks.

Nothing is asked to.

The deep wall beneath the mountain continues its movement.

When it finally fails, ages later, the collapse is immense. Half the crater breaks away. Turbines topple into darkness. Shell banks fragment. The conductive web fires through torn ends and turns the falling water blue-white. For a little while, the whole summit seems to descend as a single illuminated body.

Much dies.

Not all.

The remaining wall becomes a steep crescent open to the sea. New currents pass through it. Old species vanish from the Crown. Others, once confined to the rim, enter the broken center. Rootwheel conduits exposed by the fracture become reefs. Sourhearts colonize the fresh rock of the scar.

And elsewhere, across the drowned mountains, other crowns are clattering.

None is immortal.

None is identical.

The power has not saved every inhabitant, nor preserved every relationship, nor made suffering meaningful. It has done something quieter: it has prevented the accident of isolation from becoming a final verdict.

Then it withdraws—not completely, for a benevolent attention is not a gaze that tires, but enough that the tides belong wholly to the ocean again.

It leaves no monument.

A monument would alter the current.

It leaves no commandment.

There is no creature there for whom obedience would be mercy.

It leaves only a widened possibility, already filling with hunger, error, adaptation, and unplanned continuance.

And if the power carries anything away, it is not a shell or specimen or perfect image of the place. It carries the Crown’s noise within its knowing: the knock under the overhang, the cough of brine, the loaded whine of living teeth, the immense slow turning at the center.

Not as a design to be repeated.

Not as a problem solved.

As proof that care need not make a thing its own.
