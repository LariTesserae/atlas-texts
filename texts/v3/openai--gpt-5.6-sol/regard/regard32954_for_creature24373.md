The power does not descend.

To descend would be to arrive, and arrival requires a place from which something else must be displaced. There is no such place in the basin. The smallest shelf holds eggs beneath its lip. The clearest patch of water is crossed by the sensing lines of fish. Air that appears empty is already divided among spores, wings, breath, and the reach of webs.

So the power remains above the aperture, where the cloud closes over it without resistance.

It does not shine. Light would favor the leaves nearest the opening and alter all the hungers beneath them. It does not speak. A voice strong enough to enter the basin would become weather there. It does not take a body, however small. Even the body of a gnat would be one gnat too many unless another were denied its place.

Instead, it becomes attention without weight.

This is possible for such a power. It can notice without casting a shadow. It can know the cold of the pool without warming it, follow the water under the low shelf without widening the passage, and rest upon each living thing without making it bear the burden of being watched.

For a long while, it does only this.

It feels the frogs receive one another through the trembling sedges. It follows the minute pressure by which warning crosses a branch. It enters no mind, but attends to every mind from its own center: the fish’s dark and lateral awareness, the moth’s nearness to stone, the spider’s world made of tension, the old amphibian’s bodily certainty that the useful pebble lies beneath the flap of moss.

The power perceives the basin as one being.

Then, because it is wise, it perceives that this is not the whole truth.

Wholeness is visible from above. Hunger is felt one body at a time.

The snake and frog participate equally in balance, but the frog does not experience itself as balance when the snake takes it. The moth wall never thins, but the moth in the owl’s beak does not know that another is unfolding behind the moss. Eggs, mouths, roots, rot, and renewal compose a perfection in which every particular creature remains particular to itself.

The power does not confuse endurance with mercy.

Nor does it condemn the basin for this. To forbid the snake its frog would be to choose the frog by starving the snake. To preserve the moth would be to empty the owl. To remove decay would choke the roots with what could no longer return. Every rescue attempted within the closed exchange would become an injury elsewhere.

The power could remake the creatures so that none fed on another. It could give leaves to the snakes, stone to the owls, mist to the spiders. But then these would be other creatures in another place, and the basin before it would have been abolished under the name of kindness.

It could open the aperture and pour in the sun. The ferns would brighten first. Then some would climb above others. The pool would warm; larvae would quicken; fungus would retreat into wood. Voices might begin. Seasons might enter. There would be flowers, flights, migrations, invention, memory.

There would also be thirst.

The power could introduce history. It could allow one population to swell, another to fail, one clever hand to retain a flake and another to build around it. In time there might be paths, dry chambers, stored food, names, graves, disputes over the exposed seam of stone. Beings who had once fitted their tools back into the mountain might learn to fit the mountain to their tools.

Some of this would be beautiful. Some would be terrible. None of it would be a gift merely because it was change.

So the power does not open the sky.

It does not stop time, either. The basin’s stillness is not a commandment to be enforced against the mountain. If one day the rock itself gives way, if the cloud belt rises, if a tree falls in a manner no former tree has fallen, the power will not hold every pebble in place to preserve an image of perfection. Benevolence is not the making of a prison so gentle that no inmate can name it.

The power waits until it has understood all that can be understood without asking.

Then it asks.

The question is not made of words. Words would seek a listener and give that listener authority over the rest. Instead the power places the question into the common water as lightly as the coolness placed there by night.

Not a ripple. Not a taste. Something nearer to an opening in possibility:

**Is anything wanted?**

The question passes beneath the sedges.

The fish receive it as an immense, directionless distance. They settle lower among the roots.

The frogs receive it as a stem trembling with no body upon it. Their throats remain full.

The birds tighten their claws. The warning travels from branch to branch, but no danger can be located.

The moths press their wings closer to the wall.

One of the browsers puts down its flake of stone. Its hands remain open for several breaths. Then it picks the flake up again and continues cutting the white edge of a fungus shelf.

There is no single answer.

There are thousands.

The frog wants the pressure behind it to pass elsewhere.

The snake wants warmth enough to complete digestion.

The owl wants the moth to lift from the wall.

The moth wants the wall never to release it.

The grub wants fungus. The fungus wants wood. The tree wants what the fungus returns. The seed wants an opening no existing root can surrender. The roots want the rock to remain closed around them.

The old amphibious creature wants the shell beneath its hand to break cleanly.

The snail wants otherwise.

These wants do not rise into harmony. The basin’s collective life cannot answer on behalf of those whose desires oppose one another. Its wholeness is real, but it is not a sovereign. It has no right to tell the moth that the owl’s hunger is more important, or the owl that the moth’s fear is sacred.

The wise power accepts the contradiction. It does not force the multitude to pretend to be one.

What it changes is almost nothing.

It touches no birth, prevents no feeding, favors no species. It adds no new resource and removes no appetite. It does not teach the hands to build or the birds to sing. Water continues to enter from the weeping stone and disappear beneath the shelf.

But wherever death must pass through terror, the power shortens the passage.

The owl’s strike finds the small place where sensation ends at once. The snake’s mouth carries a stillness that reaches the frog before crushing does. The spider’s bite closes the world gently. Fish taken under roots feel the dark deepen without struggle. Creatures still flee, because flight belongs to their life; they still defend themselves, because defense belongs to their form. Pain remains where it warns the living body away from harm. It is not permitted to linger after warning has become useless.

No predator is deprived. No prey is made careless. Nothing in the count is altered.

Only the unnecessary moment is removed.

Then the power does one further thing, not within the basin but within itself.

It remembers.

Until now, the place has retained patterns and released particulars. Frog follows frog. Moth replaces moth. Skill passes from hand to hand while the shard returns to stone. No shell is a monument. No song bears a name. The basin’s activity leaves no history because everything that happens is taken immediately into what happens next.

The power does not impose memory upon the inhabitants. It does not load the birds with ancestors or make the old amphibian grieve every snail it has opened. Such memory would overfill minds already fitted precisely to their lives.

Instead, the power keeps the history they cannot keep.

Every creature is held distinctly in its attention: not as an example of its kind, not as prey, offspring, nutrient, or function, but as the brief and singular center it was. The moth beneath the owl’s claw is not exchanged for the moth behind the moss. The first remains itself. The second is received as another.

Nothing is taken from decay. Bodies still pass into mouths, mud, fungus, root, and leaf. But no life is allowed to mean only what consumed it.

The basin remains without monuments.

Its dead are not unremembered.

Last, the power turns its regard outward.

There are intelligences elsewhere that would not enter as water enters. They would arrive needing bare ground. They would cut a place to stand, then another on which to set what they carried. They would see the stone seam and remove the shards. They would hear of animals using tools and bring cages, lamps, names, measures, and claims.

Against these, the power raises no wall.

A wall would be visible.

It gives the basin the protection of failed attention. Maps blur around it. Paths incline almost imperceptibly away. Those searching from the air see only cloud. Those climbing toward it remember another purpose and turn aside before the outer slopes fall away. Instruments collect ordinary readings. Stories told about a dark green knot high on the mountain lose one necessary detail each time they pass between speakers.

This concealment is not absolute. A lost creature may still enter. A seed may come on a storm. The mountain remains joined to the world. But no one reaches the basin by appetite for possession.

Having done these things, the power withdraws even its question.

The birds loosen their claws. Fish rise by the width of a fin. Moths soften against the wall. The browser finishes cutting the fungus and presses its flake back into the exposed seam. The old creature places a snail in the hollow of the root, retrieves its pebble, and taps.

The shell opens cleanly.

Water passes from leaf to leaf. Sound travels no farther than the body that makes it. The same dark pool receives the same fine silver threads.

Nothing there knows itself to have been blessed.

The power considers this no failure.

A blessing need not become knowledge. Mercy need not announce the hand from which it came. The basin has no empty place for gratitude, no need for worship, and no language in which to separate the giver from the gift.

It remains wet, cool, crowded, and nearly silent.

It remains many.

And though its changes continue to repeat themselves perfectly, they no longer vanish perfectly.

Somewhere beyond light, beyond season, beyond the mountain’s oldest rock, the wise power holds every small life without confusing it for any other.

The basin keeps the balance.

The power keeps the names that were never spoken.
