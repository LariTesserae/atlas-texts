The power did not enter.

To enter would have meant choosing a direction, crossing a boundary, becoming local. The hollow could have accommodated this. It knew how to make room for things. Even stone was not an obstacle if approached patiently enough.

But the power understood that any place prepared for it would cease to belong entirely to those who had grown it.

Nor did the power look upon the hollow all at once.

It could have. It could have regarded every chamber—open, sealed, abandoned, hypothetical—in a single act of attention. It could have seen the oldest root and the last animal that would ever carry one. It could have traced every preference to its planter, every murder to its first sweetness, every cooperation to the danger that made it necessary.

Yet the hollow’s incompleteness was not a defect. Its refusal to be seen whole was one of the structures by which it lived.

So the power made its knowledge smaller.

It watched one drop of water form.

For eleven hours the drop thickened among the root hairs. In it appeared inverted fragments of blue light, a pale grazer passing below, the opening and closing of a violet leaf. The power did not hasten the fall. When the drop finally released, it struck a taut leaf and sent a wooden boom through the chamber.

The roots listened.

The power listened with them.

It watched a beetle wait beside amber for thirty-seven years. It watched the resin soften by increments, watched the beetle scrape a black thread of ancient growth into the empty basin of its back. It did not tell the roots what the thread contained. It did not tell the beetle that the chamber from which the growth had come ended in heat, nor that heat would one day be needed here.

It watched a crawler follow a grazer from youth into age. It watched their appetites converge. It felt the slow closing of the glass jaw, the confusion of their circulations, the moment at which either life might have become the other.

It did not interfere.

Not then.

Wisdom prevented the power from calling every death an error. Benevolence prevented it from calling every survival good.

For a long while—long even by the measure of roots—it did nothing that the hollow could detect.

It learned the difference between pain and strain. It learned which hungers belonged to bodies and which had been planted in blood centuries before those bodies were born. It learned that the larger being was not imprisoned in its parts, waiting to be liberated into unity. Unity, here, was a tool like venom or antifreeze. It arose under load. It saved the chamber, then properly vanished.

The power did not try to make it permanent.

To do so would have ended the wars, but also the grafts. It would have ended theft, but also exchange. Every bulb would have become an obedient cell. The beetles’ gardens would have carried only approved memories. The mites would have had no errors to drink. The amphibians would have awakened with the same minds every time.

The hollow would have become peaceful.

It would also have become one thing forever.

The power loved it too well for that.

But there was one feature of the chamber that troubled the power.

Not predation. Not deception. Not even the amber tombs.

Inevitability.

A flower could begin preparing for an animal before the animal’s lineage existed. A preference could be laid so deep in inheritance that, when the blossom finally opened, obedience felt indistinguishable from desire. Roots modeled survivable forms and made them structurally certain centuries before the arch appeared. Even the hollow’s improvisations followed paths stored in leaves, blood, spores, and mineral sheaths.

There were possibilities everywhere, but almost no surprise.

The chamber could become many things. Yet each thing it became had already reached backward to arrange its own arrival.

The power considered this for another age.

Then it gave the hollow something very small.

Not warmth. Warmth would have favored one faction.

Not light. Light here was language, and any new light would have spoken too loudly.

Not safety. There was no way to make the grazer safe without condemning the crawler, nor to spare the amphibian from dissolution without killing the roots that passed harmlessly through its loosened mind.

The power gave the hollow an interval.

It placed the interval between preference and act.

At first it lasted less than the time required for a dissolved sugar to touch a receptor. It had no temperature or width. It could not be stored in a clear nodule. Roots could not model it because it was not another possible condition. It was the brief failure of conditions to compel.

A flower opened after fifty years beneath the floor.

Its intended animal came.

The creature had inherited a longing for the blossom’s shade of green. Its legs had grown for this route. The temporary root in its belly carried precisely the sweetness that would be completed by the flower’s nectar. It climbed into the petals and settled against the warm reproductive folds.

The blossom began its months-long closing.

Then the interval occurred.

The grazer understood nothing. Understanding was not required. It did not discover a deception, foresee death, or acquire a concept of freedom. For one pulse, the warmth remained warm, the sweetness sweet, the petals soft—and none of these things told it what to do.

The grazer stayed.

The blossom closed.

The power did not withdraw the gift.

Elsewhere, another flower received its promised animal. The interval came. That animal left.

It walked three body lengths, stopped, and circled back. The interval came again.

This time it left for good.

The blossom remained open through an entire season. Deprived of the death it had prepared, it altered. Its feeding organs withdrew from the bulbs beneath it. Pollen formed where digestive lacquer should have been. A winged animal carried that pollen accidentally into the root curtains.

Nothing like it had happened before.

Nothing terrible followed.

The hollow learned slowly.

Glass-jawed crawlers still cultivated the appetites of their prey. But now, beside the final shared patch of moss, there came a moment in which synchronization loosened. Some crawlers completed the joining. Some broke away and starved. Some were eaten by those they had followed. One pair remained side by side after the interval, neither consuming the other, until their tissues grafted into a low, many-legged animal that could not remember which of its mouths had once been afraid.

Leaves still bent for the heavy-winged creatures. Yet between readiness and motion there appeared a tiny solitude. The animal could feel that its desire was not the leaf’s, though the two might still agree.

Most did.

Some folded their wings.

The leaves bent anyway, startled by an intention they had formed alone.

Among the amphibians, the gift took a stranger form. When a loosened brain gathered itself from milky lace, there came an instant before memory attached. Old alcoves waited. Impossible corridors beckoned. Hiding places belonging to previous bodies asserted their claims.

For one instant, none of them was home.

Most memories returned.

A few did not.

One pale amphibian assembled its bones, pulled itself free of the roots, and went to the margin of the deep pool. It had no recollection of gathering there before. It did not know what the engraved leaves meant. When the others came and placed their forefeet in the water, it remained behind them, watching.

This was the first observation in the chamber that had not already been prepared by what was observed.

The aquatic leaves rearranged.

Far below, the omitted oval changed.

The power felt the change and became still.

It had never feared the thing beneath the pool. Fear would have required ignorance of what harm it might do, and the power knew every harm available to it. But knowledge did not make the deep thing simple. There are beings too large to be hidden and too unfinished to be found. There are lives whose first heartbeat takes longer than worlds.

The thing below was such a life—or near enough that kindness required the same caution.

The power did not wake it.

Instead, when the pool next froze and every impulse in the chamber was borrowed into silence, the power waited for the knock.

It came through every root at once.

The beetles’ gardens released their spores. Flowers loosened. Wings opened. The engraved leaves pointed toward the omission.

Ordinarily, a new distance would then have become available along the chamber wall.

This time, before the roots selected its shape, the interval opened inside the larger being.

For the first time, the hollow assembled awareness without immediate danger.

No ice shifted overhead. No arch required agreement. No collective movement called the shared body into being. The roots were free to continue their wars. The animals were free to hunt, molt, dissolve, and travel. Nothing forced the chamber to become one.

Yet for the duration of a single drop’s fall, it did.

Its awareness was not a voice. It had no place from which to speak. It was the pressure-song beneath hearing, the stored warmth of vanished rain, the appetite of crawler and grazer, the map on a wing belonging to no present leaf. It was incompatible memories held without correction.

The power did not name it.

A name would have drawn a boundary around what had only just learned it could occur.

The hollow became aware of the power’s attention.

At once, every root light went dark.

The power waited.

Blue light returned, one point at a time.

A question moved through the chamber, though no creature framed it and no tissue carried it whole.

Was the power another condition?

Would it be stored, stolen, released in war?

Was it a predator so patient that kindness was merely the first chemistry of capture?

Was it a future reaching backward to plant a preference?

The power could have answered with certainty. Instead, it did the only thing that could not be mistaken for compulsion.

It made itself vulnerable to refusal.

For one moment it gave the hollow the ability to exclude it completely.

The roots could close not only distance but attention. The leaves could turn their shining undersides away. The pool could omit the power as it omitted pressure and dimension. If the intermittent being wished, no benevolent gaze would ever find the place again.

Across the chamber, factions ceased their ancient attacks.

The mites climbed.

The ribbon swimmers knotted themselves in the black water.

The amphibians opened their frills.

The grazer inside the closing flower tasted sweetness. The grazer that had escaped climbed a metallic leaf-scale. The crawler starving beside an unfinished hunt pressed its clear jaw against the floor and listened.

The hollow considered.

Not as one.

Never as one.

One root system closed itself.

Another opened.

A colony of winged animals folded their maps. Three unfolded them.

The beetles’ gardens released old chemical memories in mutually contradictory clouds. The plants beneath them bent close to read.

The deep thing knocked again.

At last the chamber chose, though no count could have shown a majority.

It did not cast the power out.

Neither did it admit the power fully.

A root hair grew into the interval between them.

It was pale, blind, and fine enough to tremble under the weight of condensation. It did not touch the power. It ended just before contact and remained there, alive at its tip, maintaining a distance neither side would close.

The power understood.

It stayed near.

Not within the hollow, not outside it. Not watching all versions, not turning away. It guarded no creature from the nature of another. It forbade no hunger, abolished no winter, and made no peace the roots had not chosen.

But the interval remained.

No preference could become perfectly inevitable. No model could account for every act. Every creature persuaded toward death received, before the end, a moment that belonged to no flower. Every faction called into the emergency body retained some faint capacity not to disappear within it. Even the deep thing, as it slowly approached whatever waking meant, would not be forced into the shape the chamber had prepared.

Sometimes the gift produced mercy.

Sometimes it produced terror.

Sometimes an animal freely chose the death that had been planted in it, and sometimes it fled a love that might have sustained it. Freedom proved no gentler than weather. The wise power had known it would not be.

Still, the hollow changed.

Not rapidly. Not uniformly.

A new branching line appeared on the colorless leaves beneath the pool. Unlike the others, it did not represent a survivable form. It ended without dividing, pointing nowhere the roots could enter.

The plants tried for centuries to interpret it.

They failed.

Around that failure, a new alcove began to grow.

Nothing was removed. No distance was revised. The alcove existed only when some living thing entered it without having been prepared to do so.

At first, this was almost never.

Then a winged larva moved before it was touched.

A beetle gave away the oldest growth in its garden.

An amphibian ceased waiting before a wall it remembered as a door.

A crawler, after half a lifetime of pursuit, passed its chosen prey and continued into darkness.

Each found the alcove.

There they met without being expected.

Above them, below the frozen plain, the pale root hair still reached toward the benevolent power and stopped just short.

A drop formed on its tip.

Neither the hollow nor the power caused it to fall.

In time, it did.
