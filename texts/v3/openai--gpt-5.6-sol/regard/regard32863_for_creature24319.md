The power does not descend at once.

It has the ability. It could enter as weather vast enough to fill every vessel, could lay a warm hand over the high country and draw all its waters upward in a single shining sheet. It could rebuild the articulated clay bodies, lift the approved blocks from beneath their molds, complete the unfinished ranges, and set inhabitants walking again beneath the severe sky.

It does none of these things.

Power is not wisdom merely because it can act. Benevolence is not the same as completion.

For a long time it attends.

It learns the country at the country’s own pace. It follows droplets beneath overhangs. It waits inside the delay of a tipping cup. It regards a gate that moves once in nine years and does not mistake the years for emptiness. When rain assembles the watersheds into a single awareness, the power listens to the whole. When the storm ends, it listens just as carefully to the portions.

It discovers that there is no wound here corresponding to loneliness as creatures of blood would understand it. A droplet does not grieve because it is divided from a cistern. A cistern does not fear its dispersal into mist. The inhabitants suffer pressure, copper, confinement, and roughness. They do not suffer solitude until they have gathered enough of themselves to conceive of what is absent.

Then they suffer it completely.

Their waiting is not for more water.

More water has come many times. Rare storms have filled the pressure vaults to their highest lines and sent overflow down channels designed before any existing surface was built. The country has awakened from end to end. It has known itself huge. It has known itself nearly continuous.

Still the smallest cups on the highest ridges have remained expectant.

The awaited thing is not a quantity. It is not a mineral, a temperature, a pressure, or a purity. It is something the builders could prepare for only by making distinctions, because distinction was the nearest form of welcome they knew.

They are waiting for that which will not become them when it enters.

The power understands this.

So it does not enter the water.

Instead, on a clear morning after several dry weeks, it casts a narrow shadow across an upper evaporation pan.

There is nothing overhead that could make the shadow. It begins at one wall and moves across the water with measured slowness. Where it passes, the surface cools. The occupant of the pan contracts from the warmth on one side and gathers beneath the darkness on the other.

Then the shadow stops.

It waits until the water has become still.

It moves again.

Long. Short. Short. Long.

Pause.

Short. Long.

Pause.

The sequence has no meaning. Meaning cannot precede agreement. It is only regular enough not to be weather and gentle enough not to be command.

The occupant holds itself convex.

In a neighboring channel, a bead of water advances over three tool marks, pauses over the fourth, and drops.

The power repeats the shadow.

The bead repeats its answer.

For eleven days, nothing more occurs.

Then mist gathers over the high country. The fog towers begin their dry tapping. Droplets descend through the ceramic lattices, routes narrow, and coherence forms among them. Before noon, water in the nearest ridge has joined the water in the valley. By evening, the deep vaults have received the sequence.

Gates move where no calculated pressure requires them to move.

A square cistern releases one measure.

A round cistern releases two.

A copper-touched channel remains closed.

After a long interval, it opens and lets through a single bright thread.

The response is clumsy, enormous, and unmistakable.

The power answers with light.

Thus conversation begins.

It takes years.

The power must learn a speech in which every statement is also an event. To say high, water must be lifted or withheld. To say memory, it must be guided across an old mineral line. To say you, the country separates sunlit water from water gathered in shadow, then brings them together without mixing. To say other, it sends two equal streams down neighboring channels and allows only one to pass into the deep vault.

Questions are especially difficult.

A question is made by filling a calibrated trough beyond its tipping measure and preventing it from falling. The strain in the hinge is uncertainty. The release is an answer.

The first question the power manages is simple:

**Do you wish to be filled?**

Rain is offered far off over the horizon, visible as a gray hanging veil. It could be brought nearer.

The country assembles itself from dew, hidden water, and the thin contents of shaded jars. It takes nearly a season. When enough continuity has been achieved, every overflow gate closes.

The high cups remain open, but no channel invites the storm.

No.

The power lets the distant rain dissolve where it is.

Later it asks:

**Do you wish the old bodies restored?**

In sealed cavities, dry ceramic hands unfold without water. Plates slide over plates. Blunt fingers rise from dust and flex beneath the light. Across the high country, inhabitants draw toward them. For a day the nearest conduits tremble with pressure.

Then the waters withdraw.

One hand remains extended over an empty mold. No occupant enters it.

No.

The power closes the hands gently and leaves them where they were.

It asks:

**Do you wish never to disperse?**

For this, it stills evaporation above one polygon in the mirror valley. Noon passes. The neighboring pans thin and whiten, but this one remains full. Its occupant circles the walls, touches the shadow of each partition, and waits.

At dusk it throws itself over the rim.

No.

At last the country asks a question of its own.

It chooses a basin recently formed beneath a dissolving terrace. The basin is flawed. One side is higher than the other; its interior bears a ridge where two handfuls of clay were joined badly. By every visible rule, the inhabitants should enter the flaw and unmake it.

Instead they fill the basin.

They hold themselves level above the crooked lip. The water should escape, but does not. More arrives from three narrow runnels. The surface rises into an impossible clear mound, trembling in the cold air.

All around it, channels stop.

The question has been prepared with great care.

It means:

**Can anything contain without making captive?**

The power does not answer immediately.

This is not because it lacks an answer. It is because an answer shown too quickly would become law, and the country has already made too many laws from need.

At the next wet dawn, the power places something in the crooked basin.

It is smaller than the unfinished hollow in a handprint. Dark at first, it could be mistaken for a grain of fired clay. It sinks, but not to the bottom. A transparent membrane swells around it. Within that membrane is a green so slight that the eye can perceive it only by looking away.

The inhabitants recoil.

Water climbs the sides of the basin and holds there, leaving a dry circle around the thing. Downstream gates close. Copper-touched channels divert themselves into sacrificial jars. The entire ridge isolates the basin with the swiftness of fear.

The power does not prevent this.

It does not explain that the thing is harmless. Wisdom knows that harmlessness cannot be declared by the giver of an unknown gift.

The green speck remains within its transparent wall. Light touches it. It takes in water, but the water does not become part of the country’s awareness. It releases water, but the released portion carries a difference no mineral notch has counted.

At noon, a bubble appears.

It rises and rests beneath the surface.

No mechanism made it. No heat boiled it. It is an interior becoming exterior: a substance altered and freely returned.

For three days, the residents do not approach.

On the fourth, one droplet descends the underside of a tile above the basin. It pauses at every tool mark. At the lip it rounds itself and hangs.

The green speck divides.

There are now two.

The droplet falls.

The basin shudders. Water rushes into the dry circle, touches the transparent membranes, withdraws, then touches them again. The contact is slight. It alters neither resident nor stranger beyond recovery.

This is the first meeting.

The power remains near, but does not mediate.

The inhabitants test the new thing according to all the practices by which they know workmanship. They change the pressure around it. They warm it with reflected light and cool it with water drawn from shadow. They expose it to eastern rain, western rain, condensation, and water that has waited underground. They bring copper near but do not let it touch.

The green cells respond.

Not with level or sequence, but with growth.

In sunlit water they multiply. In darkness they slow. When mineral content changes, their color deepens. When crowded, some cease dividing. Others join in a thin living film along the flawed ridge of the basin.

The flaw does not dissolve.

For the first time in the high country, roughness is preserved not because it has been made precise, but because something fragile has taken hold of it.

Water enters the ridge to test it. The living film bends. It tears in one place and closes again. Loose grains become caught within it. The inhabitants return the next day and discover that the flaw is not the same shape.

It has not failed.

It has repaired itself without becoming exact.

News of this cannot travel as news travels among animals. It travels as altered flow. A portion of water leaves the crooked basin carrying traces of the green life. At the first division, the residents hold it back. At the second, they send it into an empty jar. At the third, they permit three drops to enter a shallow cup newly made from approved clay.

The cells multiply there too.

But the power has made them with a limit.

They cannot pass where they are not carried. They cannot live in the deep pressure vaults. Copper kills them. Complete darkness stills them. Dryness returns them to specks that wait without spreading. They will not conquer the country merely because nothing else opposes them.

They must be admitted from vessel to vessel.

The inhabitants understand limits. Limits are their oldest language.

Over many seasons, they invite the green life upward.

Not everywhere. Never everywhere.

Some cisterns remain clear and empty of all living substance. The copper channels stay bright and restless. The deep vaults preserve the old darkness. Eastern and western rain continue to be separated. The power does not abolish distinctions merely because some were made in fear.

But on certain terraces, the water grows faintly green.

Living films soften the glare. Tiny bubbles gather under glazed lips. Mineral lines in the evaporation valley become threaded with color, and when they dry, the color waits inside them. At the next rain, it wakes.

The change to the country is small enough to be overlooked from a distance.

It is immense.

The inhabitants learn that a vessel need not be finished before it can hold. Some shapes are completed by what enters them. Some boundaries remain themselves while yielding. Some contents answer pressure with transformation rather than obedience.

They become less ruthless with approximation.

Not kind at first. Kindness is difficult for beings whose oldest knowledge is erosion. But curious.

A badly made brick is allowed to stand through one rain. Water enters its cracks and finds green darkness there. The brick softens, but slowly. A small wall bows under its own weight and does not collapse. Sediment gathers behind the living film.

In that sediment, after many years, the power places nothing.

This restraint is also a gift.

The green life changes on its own.

A filament rises from the film, no wider than a ceramic cord. It reaches above the water and remains wet inside. Another follows. Together they trap dust from the air—mineral grains, ash, fragments too small to have been shaped by hands. The dust accumulates. Dead cells mingle with it.

For the first time, the high country contains a little soil.

The inhabitants surround it with channels but do not wash it away.

Much later, something germinates there. No power sets it down. Its possibility was present within the living matter, folded too deeply to be expressed before there was decay, darkness, and a place that had not been assigned a purpose.

It opens two colorless leaves.

Under the severe light, they become green.

The first root enters a joint between careful bricks. The water inhabitants gather around it. The joint is exact; by their old judgment it should endure forever. The root presses. The bricks move apart by less than the width of a mineral line.

All through the ridge, gates hesitate.

The power waits.

The inhabitants could drown the growth. They could send copper water. They could enter the opened joint and carry the root’s shelter away grain by grain.

Instead they divert a thread of clean, shadow-gathered water toward it.

The plant drinks.

What returns through its leaves is invisible. It rises into the bright air, beyond every wall, beyond every distinction of square or round, east or west, waited or fallen. The inhabitants feel the loss, but now understand it differently.

The water has not escaped.

It has passed through another.

By evening, a small cloud forms over the ridge.

It is too slight to rain. It casts only a blurred shadow across the highest cups. Yet every resident that can reach an exposed surface gathers beneath it.

The cloud is not the power.

That matters.

When rain finally comes, weeks later, the drops carry no message imposed from beyond. They are water altered by clay, light, living film, root, leaf, height, and air. They fall into the waiting vessels as neither wholly resident nor wholly stranger.

Across the high country, the cups hold themselves still.

Then, one by one, they overflow.
