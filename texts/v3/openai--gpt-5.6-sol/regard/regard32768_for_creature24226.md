The power does not enter as light.

Light would be of little use here. It would startle the transparent grazers, wake dormant chemistries in the mineral bowls, and make the wet city briefly visible according to laws its builders had never trusted. Nor does the power enter as a voice. There is already too much sound, and wisdom has no need to be louder than water.

It enters as attention.

For a long while it changes nothing.

It follows one drop from the stone tooth to the bronze basin, through the dividing channel, beneath the grille, along a clay pipe lined with pale threads, and into the lowest sump. It waits there while the drop becomes indistinguishable from all the others. It learns the city at the speed the city can be known.

It feels the thumbprints in the mortar.

It rests in the sleeping hollows without imagining the vanished bodies too quickly. It reads the inscriptions that remain and those reduced to dimples, giving equal care to knowledge and erasure. It places no certain meaning where only possibility survives. It does not call the civic tools offerings. It does not call the gate a tomb, a prison, or a promise.

At the gate, it listens.

The deep sound resolves beneath its attention into more than one sound: water shouldering through microscopic seams, shale adjusting grain against grain, a hollow beyond, and farther down a slow fracture propagating through the bedrock. No heart beats there. No hidden hand waits against the other side.

Or if one does, it has chosen a stillness so complete that even benevolence must honor it.

The power does not open the gate.

Instead, it lays against the grooves the gentlest possible question.

Not in words. In pressure.

A warmth no greater than that of a palm recently withdrawn passes once across the layered shale. It follows the concentric channels inward, pauses at the center, then recedes.

The power waits.

A year is nothing to it. Neither is a century.

No answer comes.

So it turns its care toward those who are alive.

It finds that the white creatures are fewer than they appear. Several sumps hold them, but the sumps have been separated too long. In one, every egg fails before hatching. In another, a copper-bearing seep has begun to enter during the season when the mountain is most saturated. Elsewhere, behind a grille blocked with the transparent armor of generations, a small population survives on a film that grows thinner each decade.

None of this is tragedy to the creatures. They do not foresee extinction. They clasp their tiles, comb the water, molt, carry their clear eggs, and fold themselves when the current takes them. Yet lack of foresight does not make their lives less worthy of continuance.

The power could fill every cistern with food. It does not.

It could purify the water absolutely. It does not.

Perfect water would kill the films. Endless food would alter the creatures until the channels choked with them. To preserve a living thing is not to release it from every hardship, nor to hold it forever in the shape in which it was first pitied.

Instead, the power makes three small changes.

Far above the city, inside stone no tool has touched, it turns the edge of a copper-rich seep by less than the width of a hair. Water that would have entered the poisoned sump now passes through a seam of limestone, where its bitterness softens before descending.

At the blocked grille, it loosens one fragment of empty shell. The fragment travels two finger-lengths, catches in a side current, and draws the obstruction after it. The pipe clears with a knock scarcely different from the city’s ceaseless knocking.

Then, in a dividing channel near the old civic basin, the power moves a pebble.

Only a little.

The pebble had rested there since the last hands left. Mineral skin had nearly made it part of the channel. When it shifts, the sound is soft enough to vanish inside the roar.

A thread of water takes a path it has not taken for centuries.

Days later, it reaches a sump where three white creatures graze beneath a tile. With it comes one traveler, folded tightly around a cluster of clear eggs. She catches against the tile’s underside, opens her matched legs, and holds.

That is all.

Yet the old city notices.

A pipe silenced beyond living memory begins to mutter. Pressure leaves one wall and enters another. A bronze cup receives a drop at a new interval. Across the flattened hollow, tiny adjustments answer one another. No flood comes. No cistern empties. The equilibrium bends without breaking.

The altered note travels through the streets.

It enters sleeping recesses, sealed rooms, staircases, chimneys, and the porous foundations packed with rubble. It touches bone needles hidden in cracks. It passes through tools made from ribs and pegs that may once have been fingers. It reaches the deepest gate and returns.

For one moment—not measured in human time, but present nonetheless—the city makes a sound it has not made since it was inhabited.

Not the sound of a voice.

The sound of maintenance.

The power hears in it no gratitude. It asks for none.

There remains the fracture in the bedrock. Left alone, it will reach the chamber in nine hundred years. The ceiling will not collapse at once. First one ledge will shear away, then a resonant shaft will close, then the drinking cistern will fill with shale. Given another power’s temperament, the whole city might be encased in protection: stone made unbreakable, water commanded never to vary, each white creature preserved in an immaculate and everlasting province.

But wisdom knows that permanence can become another name for death.

The power does not make the city eternal.

It gives it time.

Deep in the stratum, where pressure has accumulated against an ancient fault, it allows one harmless tremor. On the surface, centuries hence, a few loose stones will roll down an empty slope. Below, the fracture spends itself sideways and closes under its own weight. The city gains perhaps ten thousand years, perhaps thirty. Enough for uncounted generations of blind, transparent lives. Enough for the films to change, for clay to soften, for new channels to form, for some rooms to drown and others to become shores.

Enough for the city to remain alive rather than merely intact.

Last, the power considers the vanished builders.

It could restore them from residue. Their salts remain in the walls. Skin cells are sealed beneath mortar. Hair lies mineralized in drains. There is sufficient pattern for a great power to imitate bodies, gestures, even memories reconstructed from tools and wear.

But to make replicas and call them returned would satisfy the loneliness of the witness, not the wishes of the dead.

So the power does not populate the sleeping hollows with obedient ghosts.

It does not place spectral hands on the valves.

It does not complete the worn inscriptions.

Instead, it remembers.

Not as an inventory, but as a practice.

It remembers that there were people who governed by listening. That they understood theft as a change in a neighbor’s walls. That they made grandeur from intricacy because height was unavailable to them. That they touched one another to speak. That their knowledge was tender without being innocent, and practical without being empty of reverence. That they fitted themselves to severe tolerances and, for a time, made a civilization inside the sweating of stone.

The wise power bears this knowledge elsewhere.

In places where rulers mistake loudness for authority, someone dreams of hands gathered around a trembling basin.

Where engineers imagine water as only a substance to be conquered, someone wakes with the conviction that every channel has a listener at its end.

Where a child has been told that smallness cannot contain magnificence, the child begins to build a city beneath a table: roofs as plazas, walls as roads, rooms entered sideways. They do not know why they place cups everywhere, or why the smallest door is the most important.

The old city remains undiscovered.

A drill planned for the slope above breaks unexpectedly before reaching the black stratum. A map is copied with a harmless error. A quarry is opened on the other side of the ridge, where the stone is easier to cut. No scholar receives the glory of naming the vanished people. No collector carries away the little civic wedges and arranges them beneath glass.

The power does not hide the city forever. Only from those who would enter it before learning how to listen.

Then it withdraws—not wholly, but enough that its care does not become dominion.

The weak mineral bowls continue to release their ancient blue. The water continues its enormous breathing. The gate stays shut. The white creatures feed beneath their tiles, and in the newly joined sump, the traveler’s eggs open one by one.

Nothing kneels to bless them.

Nothing announces that they have been saved.

There is only a slight sweetness in the water where bitterness had begun, a cleared passage, a widened future, and among the innumerable notes of the city, one renewed pipe calling softly into rooms that no longer have names.
