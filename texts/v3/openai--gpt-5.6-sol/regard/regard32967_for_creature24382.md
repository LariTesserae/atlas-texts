The power does not descend from above.

Above is not a reliable direction there, and descent is among the things the country does not permit to finish.

Nor does it arrive as warmth. To warm the shelves would loosen every completed fracture, wake every withheld avalanche, and release the motionless falls all at once. The mountains would undergo their unspent history in a single instant. Benevolence without wisdom might call this liberation.

The power understands that it would be ruin.

It does not silence the roar. The roar contains too much: warnings, names, the last words of lost travelers, the struck notes by which the Still People recognize one another across impossible distance. To erase it would be to erase much of what they possess instead of memory.

It does not straighten the country, establish a horizon, or teach the rivers to run downstream. Such corrections would make the place legible only by destroying it.

For a long while, therefore, the power does nothing that can be distinguished from attention.

The gray radiance remains gray. The waterfalls hang. The moths move without crossing the space between one wall and another. The browsing animals continue to stand at distances that cannot be reduced. Condensation gathers beneath the overhangs.

The Still People notice.

They are accustomed to being looked at by travelers. Travelers look with fear, curiosity, loneliness, calculation. Their gaze is a pressure from one direction.

This attention has no direction.

A Still Person lays a palm against the cliff and finds the branching lines of its hand darkening farther than before. Across several shelves, others raise their heads. One listening at another’s breastbone hears, between two heartbeats, a space that does not belong to the body.

The interval is brief.

It ends.

That is how they know something new has entered the country.

They gather in their fashion: one beside a black pool, one within a ring of slabs, one standing under an upward-pointing shadow, many seated on separate ravine edges around a center with no location. The small ones remain in the chambers. Hands reach into cracks and are held by distant hands.

They wait.

The power waits also.

This continues for what elsewhere might be years.

At last, on a shelf beneath a motionless waterfall, one of the Still People touches the ground between itself and the empty air.

An invitation.

The power accepts by becoming adjacent.

No form appears. Instead, the Still Person becomes aware that the place beside it is occupied, though nothing is there. Frost lies unbroken on the stone. The shallow shadow of the suspended fall passes through the spot unchanged.

The Still Person touches its own sternum: warning, refusal, perhaps fear.

The presence does not advance.

After a long interval, the inhabitant reaches out. Its fingertips encounter something neither warm nor cold, firm nor yielding. What it touches feels most like the instant after a hand has been withdrawn.

The power has learned enough of their language to answer.

It touches the Still Person’s jaw.

Not direction, exactly.

Possibility.

It touches the throat.

Not memory.

Release from memory.

Then it closes a hand—which it does not have—over the Still Person’s eyes.

Nothing vanishes.

When the eyes open, the country remains.

This is the first gift.

It is also the first refusal.

The power will not take them away merely because another kind of being would find their existence unbearable.

The Still Person turns toward the waterfall. Every thread of it hangs as before, descending forever in a shape of descent. From far below comes the impact that never occurs.

The power attends to a single drop.

It chooses carefully: not one whose removal would break the silver column, not one joined to some hidden river, not one whose position holds a traveler’s path in place. It finds a drop at the outer edge of the fall, smaller than a moth’s eye.

For the first time since the country acquired its condition, water moves.

The drop falls.

It does not appear elsewhere. It does not lengthen into a frozen thread or remain hanging in each place it has occupied. It leaves one location, crosses the intervening space, and enters another.

The Still Person recoils so sharply that frost breaks from its collarbone.

The drop strikes the stone.

Its sound is almost nothing.

Within the roar it is less than a breath, less than the twitch of a transparent larva. Yet every Still Person hears it. The browsing animals lift their heads. White moths appear on bare rock throughout the high troughs. The pale bladder-plants bend for the first time in differing directions.

The note of the drop persists for a moment.

Then it diminishes.

Then it is gone.

The absence it leaves is more astonishing than the fall.

The Still People know sounds that began before them. They know sounds whose makers have not yet arrived. They know echoes without decay, words that cannot cease calling, warnings worn dangerous by repetition.

They do not know a sound that has completed itself.

Throughout the country, hands fly to throats and sternums. Some inhabitants press their faces to the stone. Some retreat into chambers and curl inside the old depressions. One of the small ones begins making low pulses too quickly for any adult to interpret.

The Still Person beneath the fall remains where it is.

Slowly, it places two fingers against the wet mark.

The water is already freezing.

Even so, the mark is not where the drop has always been. It is where the drop arrived.

The power touches the inhabitant’s ribs with one knuckle: a warning.

Then the stone.

Then the closed eyelid.

What it communicates is simple enough to be misunderstood for generations:

*Not everything unfinished must be forced to finish.*

*Not everything preserved must be preserved forever.*

*There can be choice.*

The power does not release a second drop.

Instead it makes a place.

It chooses one of the solitary walls crossing an empty slope, a wall that may have been built and may have learned building from the memory of hands. At its end, where the stones stop abruptly, it sets another slab upright.

The slab is not new. Newness would sit badly in that country. It is weathered, lichen-marked, rounded at the edges. Several Still People remember passing it in locations where it has never stood.

Together, the wall and the final slab form a narrow corner.

Nothing is enclosed.

Nevertheless, when entered, it is a room.

Inside, the roar remains audible, but sounds made within the room do not join it. A footstep lasts only as long as the footstep. A stone struck against another rings, softens, and becomes silent. Exhaled vapor disperses. Condensation falls when it grows heavy enough.

Time does not flow there unless someone is present.

When occupied, it proceeds gently.

Frost thickens by imperceptible degrees. Water crosses the shallow inclination of the floor. Moss grows, not in circles, but outward according to where light and moisture happen to be. A moth once placed within the room opens its wings and flies the width of it. Its flight is clumsy and brief. It lands. It does not vanish.

The first Still Person to enter remains for seven breaths.

On the eighth, it flees.

Later it returns and stays for nine.

Others follow. Some cannot bear the room at all. They stand outside with palms against their sternums, warning themselves away. The power does not draw them in.

Those who enter discover hunger sharpening and passing after food. They discover fatigue that can culminate in sleep. Their suspended breaths dissolve above their mouths. The lichen rings on their shoulders expand slowly, irregularly, and can be removed without reappearing.

Wounds begin to heal in sequence rather than possessing pain and healing at once.

This frightens them more than wounds.

The room becomes known by a pattern of touch: fingertips to throat, palm lifted away, then nothing.

Outsiders translate it poorly as the House of Silence.

It means something closer to:

*The place where a thing is permitted to stop being heard.*

The Still People use it sparingly.

They bring no tools into it. They make no fire. They do not remain long enough to become creatures wholly of sequence.

They bring names.

One inhabitant enters alone, stands in the center of the unenclosed room, and speaks a single low word. It may be the name of a dead companion, though the Still People do not make that distinction. The name sounds once. For several heartbeats it can still be heard.

Then it fades.

The speaker collapses.

Others find it curled on the floor, trembling with a violence no cold has ever caused. They do not understand grief as travelers understand it, but until then they have known no grief that could pass through the body and leave it changed. Loss in their country has always remained present from several directions.

Now one loss has become an event.

They carry the speaker out by laying hands on the stone around it until the appropriate distances close. For many conditions resembling days, it does not move.

Then it rises.

It touches its own throat.

Nothing answers from the roar.

The absence hurts.

The absence is also kind.

After this, the Still People begin bringing the suspended breaths from their chambers. They cannot lift them in the ordinary way. Instead they cup their hands around the pale forms and alter adjacency until the breaths hang within the room.

There they disperse.

Some contain only vapor. Some contain voices. One contains the scent of rain from a weather no visible shelf has ever known. One releases a child’s laugh, startling and brief. Another darkens the room and leaves frost on the inside of everyone’s clothing.

The Still People choose which breaths to release and which to keep.

The power does not choose for them.

Bodies are brought next.

Not all. Some remain beneath the waterfalls. Some continue as polished hollows and lichen circles. But a few are placed inside the room, where they do what bodies elsewhere have always done.

The first decay is treated as a catastrophe.

The Still People crowd every adjacent shelf, each alone, all turned toward the room. The roar fills with alarm pulses. Moss mantles rise along their spines. Several attempt to remove the body, but the one who carried it there touches the ground between them and then its own sternum.

Invitation.

Warning.

Choice.

They leave the body.

It returns slowly to simpler things.

Nothing rises from it. No spirit becomes visible. No secret is proved. The power is too wise to turn mercy into demonstration.

When only bones remain, the Still People carry them outside and build a cairn that can be dismantled.

Its top stone lifts alone.

When released, it falls crookedly and must be set right by hand.

They regard this imperfection with reverence.

The country does not transform. The impossible shelves retain their inclinations. Clouds remain both above and below. Rivers still must not be followed toward their sound. Overhangs with more than seven beads remain dangerous. The great arrested waterfalls continue to thunder without arriving.

But the roar has changed.

Not in volume. Not in any layer a traveler can hear.

Within it is now one missing sound: the tiny impact of a completed drop.

The Still People can always find that absence. They listen for it as others listen for a bell.

It gives the roar a measure it did not have before.

It gives their language a new interval: not a pause of indeterminate duration, not the length of rainfall or healing, but the interval after something has truly ended.

They use it in names.

They use it when speaking to the small ones.

They use it beside the pools, and the transparent larvae begin, very rarely, to twitch forward by the width of their own bodies.

Travelers who have lived too long in the high country are sometimes led to the room. No one is compelled to enter. Those who do may speak their own names and hear them fade without answering from the mountains.

Some weep.

Some sleep.

Some return to the lower world by a doorway that exists only after they have decided to leave.

Others step back into the gray country, place warm hands against the cold stone, and remain.

The benevolent power does not judge between them.

Having given the country neither escape nor correction, but a place for consent, completion, and rest, it withdraws in the only way suitable to that land.

Its attention ends.

Not everywhere. Not forever.

Once.

And in the vast basin without a horizon, the ending is felt as a small and perfect quiet.
