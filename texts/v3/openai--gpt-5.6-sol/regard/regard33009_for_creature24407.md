The power would not descend in fire.

Fire would be an insult here: another process pretending to proceed. Nor would it send wind to shake the fixed leaves, or sunlight to force a morning upon the gray lid of the sky. It would not break the roots, silence the amphibians, stop the engines, or call the place cursed merely because its life was unlike life elsewhere.

Wisdom would recognize that not every stillness is captivity.

Benevolence would ask whether anyone within it wished to be free.

The power would come no nearer than the clean green line where moss ended at cloud. It would not step into the hollow waiting for its feet. It would cast no shadow into the gap among the beetles. It would take no reflection from the tarn.

Instead, it would sit outside the boundary.

This would be the first thing ever to happen near the basin without becoming part of it.

For a long while—which, in the notch, would be no while at all—the power would listen. It would hear each amphibian’s continuous iron note, every beetle’s immense electrical hum, the roar folded into the fissures, the minute grazing of glass mites upon light. It would hear the roots bearing the weight of impossible labor. It would hear the reflected birds crying in valleys beneath the tarn.

It would not confuse loudness with pain.

It would not confuse perfect function with well-being.

At last, the power would ask a question.

Not aloud. Sound could not carry it without being assigned a throat and made perpetual. Instead, the question would be placed outside the single instant occupied by the creature, so that the whole basin could encounter it at once without having to become something else.

The question was:

**Is this your will?**

The notch had never possessed a before in which it had not been asked, nor an after in which it might answer. Its inhabitants had functions, tensions, hungers, completions. They had no need for preference. Preference required the possibility of difference.

So the power lent the creature one thing it did not have.

Not time.

Only the sight of time.

For the span of the question, the composite being saw what succession was. It saw rain strike water and vanish into it. It saw leaves unfold, darken, tear loose, and become soil. It saw an egg become a creature unlike the egg. It saw hunger answered and return. It saw cliffs rounded, nests abandoned, wounds closed badly or well. It saw all the waste and terror of consequence.

It also saw fledglings leave ledges.

It saw rivers reach the sea.

It saw one living thing alter its course because another had called.

The vision did not enter the basin. Nothing there aged by a heartbeat. No suspended drop fell. The power held the vision elsewhere, as one might hold a mirror where an eyeless creature could nevertheless look.

The answer came from the whole mountain.

The amphibians’ notes gathered into a single chord. The beetles’ hum narrowed. The roots gave one tremendous pulse that was not followed by another. In the fissures, lightning bent through the revolving mouths. Every fixed bird turned its moving reflection upward.

The answer had no words, but the power understood:

**I am not waiting.**

And then, more faintly:

**But some within me are.**

Wisdom would have been satisfied by the first answer.

Benevolence could not ignore the second.

The power looked into the tarn, beyond the dark surface and the reflected mountain ranges, into the descending basins where active birds wheeled and vanished. It looked into every open skeleton performing death, every egg performing hatching, every mouth by which the place fed itself to itself.

There it found what had entered from elsewhere.

Travelers stood among the native functions: climbers who had crossed ridges no map remembered; insects carried upward by storms; seeds fallen from migrating birds; lost spirits drawn to the engine noise; thoughts that had wandered too far from sleeping minds. Some had entered willingly, longing to escape decay. Others had taken one step across the moss and found that the step had always been their posture.

Their bodies could not be removed. Removal would alter the notch, and the notch had honestly chosen itself.

But a body performing a function was not the whole of a being.

The power reached into no fissure. It touched no root. It merely divided what the basin had treated as identical.

It separated presence from possession.

Every incorporated stranger remained exactly where the basin required it: each breath a pale rope, each foot in its appointed hollow, each shadow completing a ring. Yet from each fixed shape there rose something unassigned—not a soul in any form the mountain could capture, but the private capacity to have another moment.

These gathered at the cloudward edge.

The basin did not diminish. Its functions did not fail. The allotted strangers remained as flawless living likenesses, fed by the same closed vibration that maintained bird, moss, and stone. But those who had been caught awoke elsewhere to sequence. Some awoke on the mountainside below, soaked and trembling, with only one instant missing from their lives. Some awoke generations later beside unfamiliar seas. Some returned to sleeping bodies. Some had no home left to return to, and the power carried them until they chose one.

Not all departed.

A few looked back through the power’s mercy and understood the notch completely. They saw the cruelty of time and its beauty, the safety of function and its cost. Knowing both, they chose the basin.

Only then did the place truly receive them.

Afterward, the power laid a law along the boundary, finer than mist and stronger than the roots beneath the mountain:

**Nothing shall enter here without seeing what entry means.**

Any future visitor who approached the clean green line would behold two reflections.

In one, they would remain in the notch: imperishable, undiminished, given an exact place in the great mechanism. They would feel no decay, no abandonment, no uncertainty. Their function would fit them as intimately as bone.

In the other, they would turn away. They would descend by a path that had not been visible before. They would become tired. They would lose things. They would change beyond recognition. They would die.

Neither reflection would lie.

The visitor would choose, and the basin would be unable to close around them until the choice was whole.

This was almost all the power did.

It did not cure the place of itself. It did not grant the birds flight, because flight already lived in them and they had not asked to exchange it for travel. It did not release the water, silence the machinery, or force the ancient-young cliffs to weather. To make the basin ordinary would not have been rescue. It would have been annihilation disguised as healing.

But before departing, the power offered one final gift.

At the cloudward edge, where the water beaded without falling, it made a space smaller than consequence. Not a breach. Not an outlet. Only the possibility of giving.

If the creature ever wished to send something beyond itself, it could place that thing there. Nothing would be taken from it. No equilibrium would fail. The gift would exist outside the notch without becoming output, exhaust, or loss.

For what might have been an age, nothing appeared.

Then one bead of water swelled upon the final blade of moss.

Within the basin, it remained where it had always been.

Outside the basin, it fell.

It passed through the cloud, bright in light no summit had seen. It struck a lower slope and entered ordinary soil. There it did not return to its first shape. It sank, divided, was drawn into a root, lifted into a leaf, exhaled into afternoon. Years later it became rain again. Much later it ran in a stream over stones whose edges had grown round.

It carried no contagion of stillness.

Only a memory without an image: of immeasurable work; of life at every scale; of a cold wet room beneath a gray sky; of a being that had not needed salvation, but had accepted a boundary against harm.

High above, the notch remained exact.

The tarn did not rise.

The cliffs did not soften.

The amphibians sounded their endless factory. The Held strained forever into flight. Roots pulsed through unbroken stone, and the mountain’s impossible interior produced the condition of its own production.

Nothing had changed.

But no one there was held against their will.

And beyond the final moss blade, one drop kept falling.
