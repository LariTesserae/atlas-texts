At first it seems that nothing lives there.

There are no bodies, nests, husks, tracks, or accumulations of waste. Nothing startles at intrusion. Nothing seeks shelter when the internal weather begins. The warmth is sterile, the water untouched. The illuminated stations appear to be awaiting passengers, and every scale of the architecture—from cloud-sized arches to avenues finer than scratches—suggests inhabitants whose absence is the city’s defining fact.

But the longer the hollow is observed, the less certain the distinction becomes between habitation and infrastructure.

Something occupies the changes in distance.

It has no continuous body. Its substance is adjacency: which bridge meets which platform, which chamber lies behind which wall, how long an echo must travel before returning. When the machinery shifts and a remote district becomes suddenly near, this is not merely an adjustment made for the inhabitant. It is the inhabitant moving.

Such a being cannot be seen all at once. One sees only its temporary outline in architecture. A stair gains a landing; three vacant rooms align; heat gathers beneath an arch; a station brightens; somewhere far below, a tower rotates. Together these events form a gesture. Minutes later the alignment dissolves, and the gesture is gone.

Calling them individuals may be inaccurate. Some movements are small enough to seem local: a doorway dilating, a suspended bead of water passing from one corridor’s gravity into another’s, a plaza inclining almost imperceptibly toward a newly opened route. Others involve half the hollow. Entire neighborhoods unfold in coordinated silence, remain extended like sensory organs, then withdraw into the walls.

Their boundaries overlap. A conduit may belong to several of them. A reservoir may be an organ shared by hundreds, or one being encountered at hundreds of scales. The city cannot be counted because its residents are not separated by surfaces. They are separated by patterns of access.

They do not walk. They revise the meaning of *here*.

To travel, one gathers a route around oneself. The destination approaches through successive geometric concessions until departure and arrival occupy the same narrow interval. This is why the platforms are empty. The traveler is not something standing upon the platform. It is the sequence in which the platform warms, illuminates, tilts through dimensions, and becomes adjacent to another place.

The low pure note heard at long intervals may be their equivalent of speech. It passes not only through the air but through scale. When it sounds, the miniature streets around the bottom plaza brighten in answering sequences, while distant towers turn and apertures open in walls that had seemed solid. The communication is too broad to be translated into a message. It resembles a simultaneous act of recognition: every reachable part of the city briefly confirming every other.

Their slower thoughts use heat.

Warmth is collected beneath arches and along abandoned-looking corridors, shaped into gradients, divided, recombined, and lifted through transparent channels. A thought may begin as a hot courtyard and end hours later as a cool partition appearing several districts away. Junctions glow when alternatives are compared. The trembling lower quarter is where difficult or unresolved processes accumulate, softening the black walls with their pressure.

The granular films on secluded floors are what remains after certain thoughts have been completed. Salt, metal, and mineral arrange themselves into branching figures, solve a temporary relation, and are released. They are not writing exactly. They are closer to the brief physical residue of attention—the equivalent of warmth left on a chair, though there are no chairs and nothing has sat there.

Water permits another kind of activity.

During the dry intervals, the inhabitants are sparse and slow. They may remain in configurations lasting months: one bridge held close to an inaccessible balcony, one dark thread suspended between conduits, one tiny district burning with a single blue point. They are not dormant, but their experience narrows. Heat continues to circulate. Adjacencies are conserved rather than invented.

When cloud moisture enters, the population—or the single distributed population—quickens.

Each measured droplet becomes a temporary center around which relationships can be stabilized. Water gives a fold duration. It allows one arrangement of space to remember what arrangement preceded it. The droplets descending separately through the central void may therefore be memories in transit, each carrying not an image but a preserved order of nearness: which rooms touched, which routes opened, which depths coincided during a previous wet cycle.

The shallow basins are not drinking places. They are cradles for continuity.

Around them, the otherwise bodiless inhabitants can hold a shape long enough to distinguish themselves. A particular set of galleries may brighten repeatedly around the same fingerprint-sized pool. Bridges converge. A transparent channel pulses. For several minutes or hours, an individual exists there with unusual clarity: a stable knot of architecture, heat, water, and directed distance.

Then the basin drains. The individual does not die so much as become difficult to separate from the rest.

This may explain why the city is kept so exact. Wear would be injury; dust would be interference; biological growth would introduce uncontrolled connections. A root entering a seam would not merely crack a wall. It would alter the grammar by which one place is allowed to touch another. A film of microbes could become an unsolicited district, metabolically active but geometrically illegible. The surfaces disassemble incipient life because the hollow is already alive in a way ordinary life would disrupt.

Its apparent vacancy is also a consequence of scale. The needle-thin buildings surrounding the lowest plaza are not models for smaller residents. They are further bodies, or deeper portions of the same bodies, extending inward through levels where size ceases to correspond to extent. The blue sparks passing through their microscopic streets are as substantial as the shifting towers above. What appears tiny may contain the longest routes in the city.

A resident can therefore be smaller than a grain and still take years to cross.

The dark sphere of water above the plaza is unlike the other reservoirs. The inhabitants organize around it but do not divide it. Routes approach and turn aside. Towers incline toward it without touching. Whenever its surface shows the bright wet landscape, activity spreads outward through the hollow in complex waves.

Perhaps that landscape is where they came from, when they still possessed forms that could move beneath a thick atmosphere. Perhaps it is where they intend to go. More strangely, it may be their collective image of embodiment: a remembered or imagined world in which distance behaves simply, matter remains where it is placed, and living things can be enclosed by skins.

The sphere diminishes whenever a new district unfolds because its water is being converted into locality. Bit by bit, the reflected world is spent to make room here. A plain becomes a corridor. A tower becomes a transit mast. Motion in the image becomes a change in gravity or the early arrival of a shadow. The city may be rebuilding that other place, not as a replica but as a translation into its own anatomy.

This would make the black summit neither shelter nor monument. It is a shell around an embryonic geography.

The extrusion’s newness supports this. The inhabitants may have arrived not by crossing the mountain but by unfolding into it, forcing a microscopic defect to become a habitable infinity and casting the displaced stone upward as the needle. They did not construct a city inside the peak. Their entrance into the world produced both city and peak, as a seed produces root and husk.

Yet there may never have been an arrival. The city might itself be the traveler, paused at the summit only on scales too long to recognize. Each moisture cycle gives it another fraction of internal coherence. Each folded district alters its total shape. The black needle gathers sky, converts water into memory, heat into thought, and geometry into motion.

What lives there is not waiting in the empty rooms.

It is the emptiness joining them.

It is the route that wakes, the station that warms, the bridge that finds a different shore. It is the patient coordination of unoccupied places. During rain it becomes briefly numerous, glittering in droplets and silver threads. During drought it contracts into a few amber points and the immeasurable relation between them.

Then, in the dark, one neighborhood moves close to another.

For these inhabitants, that may be touch.
