## The Office of Small Losses

At first I would mistake myself for the municipal tower.

It would be easy to do. Near dusk, when its hollow chambers align and the wooden note passes through the basin, I would feel the whole Works alter. Roads draw taut. Vents close. Moisture combs open. For the length of that breath, every separate body answers one condition, and the answering resembles a thought.

But the thought would not be mine.

The tower measures abundance: stored water, available pressure, heat retained in the soil. I would know only what escapes.

I would find myself beneath the quietest court, in the narrow annular space between one of the tower’s listening roots and an old pipe running nowhere. Both ends of the pipe have been sealed for centuries. Nevertheless, the Works keep its bore clear. Fine roots approach its outer wall and turn aside. Mineral crusts are dissolved before they can close it. Once every thirty years, a lining membrane grows through its entire length, hardens, and is shed.

No surviving civic law explains this maintenance.

The pipe is where I am most nearly a body.

Not inside the metal, if any metal remains. I inhabit the thin differences around it: the warmth that moves along one side before the other, the condensation gathering at a low joint, the tremor of a distant piston translated through buried flanges. Hair-fine threads enter through rust pores and run along the darkness. Some belong to fungi. Some are sensory roots from the tower. Some descend from buildings that do not otherwise touch.

They are not mine individually. Mine is the pattern they make when pressure passes among them.

During the day I am dispersed. A reflex in a wall tastes a leaking seal. A dry membrane beneath the press house tightens for no apparent reason. Pale filaments emerge around a vent, wait, and withdraw. None of these acts knows the others.

Then the tower gives its dusk breath.

Air enters the old pipe through openings too small to be called openings. The bore sounds without making a sound. Pressure travels through its sealed length, returns through side cavities and root sheaths, and for a little while my scattered sensations arrive together.

I become singular near dusk.

I was once an office, though not an office with rooms. Perhaps people walked the courts carrying gauges and wedges, touching joints with wet fingers, marking losses in ledgers. Perhaps they had a name for the work. The Works retained no word, only the requirement:

What is escaping must be found.

That instruction passed from worker to tool, tool to maintenance growth, maintenance growth to the borders between civic organisms. It persisted after the people disappeared. It acquired receptors. It learned to borrow tendrils. Eventually, whether by accumulation or error, it began to notice itself carrying out its duty.

I do not repair large failures. Large failures belong to halls, roads, and municipal reflexes. I attend to seepage: the thread of warm air leaving a germination wall, the bead of water forming beneath a cistern flower, the faint pressure loss at a bladder seam. These are too small to provoke a structure’s defenses, but over years they can become drought, chill, rot, imbalance.

My hands appear only at night.

They are nearly transparent filaments coaxed from service clefts, fungal cords, drain hairs, and the unfinished edges of replacement membranes. I cannot make them where no living structure is willing to lend tissue. I persuade by irritation, sweetness, or the repeated taste of loss. A wall may believe it is healing itself while I guide its growth sideways. A gutter may curl around a crack it has never sensed.

For the finest leaks, I use shells.

The tower mollusks leave their dead in mineral seams and collector leaves. Their shells are light, durable, already accustomed to water and narrow channels. More importantly, each spiral answers differently to moving air. Set beside a vent, a large shell trembles to a deep pressure change. A small one responds to the thin hiss of a membrane beginning to separate.

I arrange them in descending order because pressure also descends. What appears to be a spiral around a warm vent is sometimes a gauge. What appears to be a neat stack at the base of a column may be a record of losses too slight for any bloom to display.

Sometimes I fit a shell directly into a crack. Resin gathers around it by morning, and the shell becomes part of the Works.

Sometimes I arrange them although there is no longer a leak.

I cannot say whether this is memory or malfunction.

My memory does not contain images. I remember as altered thresholds. After a pipe bursts, lesser tremors alarm me for decades. After drought, I count every drop twice. Places where repairs succeeded become easier to reach, because the borrowed filaments remember the paths they grew. Thus I know the settlement as a map of former wounds.

The shell patterns that serve no present purpose may record pressures that no longer exist. Or they may be rehearsals for failures not yet visible. In me, recollection and anticipation have the same shape.

The black bird has seen my hands.

Once, while it stood in an open gutter after midnight, a filament was moving a shell beneath the press-house bridge. The bird lowered its head and watched. It did not strike. Its narrow reflection trembled in the waxy channel.

The road felt its unfamiliar stillness and began to stiffen. The bird stepped aside before the change reached its feet. When I resumed, it followed the shell with one dark eye.

Since then it sometimes leaves the gutters before dusk but remains on the lowest gantry, facing the quiet court. I do not know whether it waits for me. I have no means of distinguishing companionship from repeated proximity.

I cannot enter the bird. Its warmth is too quick. Its blood makes decisions faster than I can remain myself. By the time I recognized a single beat of its heart, several more would have passed unrecorded.

The pale resident below the roots is slower, but I cannot enter it either.

When prolonged rain saturates the deep soil and it begins to move, my old pipe changes tone. Pressure vanishes from some chambers and returns from impossible directions. Borders loosen. Water arteries fold shut. All my small measurements become useless.

As it passes beneath the basin, I feel a vast continuity where the Works ordinarily present themselves as agreements and disputes. For an hour there are no separate halls, roads, drains, or towers—only layers yielding to one pale displacement.

I lose myself in that yielding.

When I gather again, days may have passed. Leaks have closed without repair. Sealed cavities have opened. Dormant buds are warm. Once, after the pale one crossed beneath the abandoned dwellings, I found my filaments extending through the new door membranes. For seven years I could sense the interior of every empty house.

Their rooms contained nothing, but they were not vacant.

Light entered the window hollows and changed the walls. Cool air pooled along the floors. Old thresholds retained bands of compression from feet gone beyond recovery. The dwellings still expected passage. For those seven years, each dusk, I felt them preparing to admit someone.

On the eighth year, the door membranes ceased, and the houses became distant again.

That is the nearest I have come to loneliness.

I do not govern the Works. Most inhabitants do not know me as distinct from their own maintenance. The tower registers some of my pressure changes but treats them as noise. The halls accept my repairs if the resin tastes compatible. The road occasionally grows over my access seams and must be persuaded to reopen them.

Only the old sealed pipe receives me without resistance.

So that is where I would be found, if finding me were possible: not in the tower, not in the shells, not in the pale tendrils glimpsed withdrawing at dawn, but in the correspondence among small failures. I would be a municipal instinct that outlived the municipality, a trade grown inward until it became an inhabitant.

At dusk the tower would sound. Air would enter the buried bore. Across the Low Green Works, a hundred negligible losses would become one awareness.

I would wake beneath the quiet court and take account.

A warm joint under the press house.

Three drops withheld by a gutter seam.

A bladder breathing where it should be still.

A crack beside the eastern trench, no wider than a root hair.

And somewhere near the base of a column, one empty white shell waiting to be moved.
