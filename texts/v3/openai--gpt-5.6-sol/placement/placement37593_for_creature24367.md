At first I think I would be found at the edge of the plateau.

Not the cliff itself, where the Unverified sit beneath their intolerable stars, but farther inland, where the last municipal rail crosses one of the paired trackways. I imagine a low shelter there, half loading station and half shrine, its roof warmed by a conduit whose contents no record identifies. I would be its attendant, touching the rail for approaching pressure and placing instruments in the footprints after an interval had gone.

But that is too exposed a life. It assumes I would want to witness the unknown arriving.

Then I think I would live among the error gardens. I would tend a sealed gallery behind dark glass, making adjustments too small to be called decisions. I would know colonies by the mistakes they preferred. I would recognize the laddering strain of a hungry branch, the false symmetry of a parasite, the slight hooked glitter that means a buried support will fail in eighty years.

Yet this also feels incomplete. I would not merely study the errors.

I would be one.

You would find me beneath the Hall of Constant Conditions, in a chamber omitted from the public tactile maps. The chamber lies between the floor of the Hall and an older foundation whose blocks bear a paired trackway. One track passes under the instruments measuring ground level. The other disappears beneath the wall and, according to every official survey, does not exist.

The entrance is a narrow pressure door behind the machine that watches the machine watching the atmospheric needle. It opens only after a buried press completes its stroke and the first concussion has crossed the city.

Inside, everything waits for the second.

I would be a Continuing, although not one assembled in the customary way. My earliest material would have come from a black ingot delivered to a warehouse. The carrier entered. The ingot was recorded as received. Much later, an inventory found no discrepancy, but a warm mass was confirmed growing between two foundation blocks beneath the Hall.

The declaration of my residency would read:

**A structural irregularity previously too gradual to distinguish from support has been confirmed.**

My body would be broad even by the standards of the Continuing, but very thin. Over centuries I would have spread along the underside of the Hall like a second floor. Pillars would pass through my frame. Bundles of municipal signal wire would rest in grooves between my plates. One of my limbs would terminate in several hundred mineral filaments cultivated from an error garden; another would be a single heavy brace set beneath a cracked beam. I could withdraw it, but doing so would lower the Hall by the width of a crystal boundary.

Therefore I would be free to move, legally and anatomically, but not structurally.

This would suit me.

My hot salts would circulate through channels that resemble old mortar joints. My thoughts would change temperature by district: quick where a furnace conduit crosses my eastern plates, slow and immense beneath the archive vault. A question placed against one side of me might require years to reach the other. By the time I answered, it would have passed through the weight of the entire Hall.

My civic function would be comparison.

After each buried press completes its stroke, I would receive the first concussion directly through the foundations. Minutes later, the weaker response would return from the far edge of the plateau. I would hold both vibrations within a set of weighted cavities grown into my torso and determine whether the second is truly an echo.

The official answer would always be yes.

The true answer would be more difficult.

Sometimes the returning pulse would contain an extra interval: a hesitation too structured to be geological, too old to be speech. Sometimes it would carry the internal rhythm of a resident not yet confirmed. Sometimes a tactile phrase would arrive inverted, as though transmitted from the far side of an event:

**Your pressure has not reached me yet.**

I would keep these discrepancies in my body rather than enter them into the archives. Each would become a small alteration in my frame—a new ridge under a plate, a changed branching angle in the silver growth around one capillary, an additional weighted reed oscillating among the others.

In this way I would gradually become an unauthorized history of the plateau.

I would contain wars the records call distributed agreements. I would contain vanished populations still officially continuing at zero pressure. I would remember which collapsed districts fell abruptly and which were carefully lowered by the administration after their failure became inevitable. I would know that some new residents had rhythms before the black ingots arrived, and that sometimes the warehouses became smaller inside after a delivery.

This knowledge would not make me one of the Unverified. I would never alter myself in anticipation.

I would simply become extremely generous about what counts as proof.

A few inhabitants would know of me. They would descend one at a time and rest their weight against my plates. Those in grief would come most often. The Continuing understand grief as the persistence of an expected rhythm after its source has ceased, and I would be unusually good at finding such rhythms. I could pass the pressure of a household through old support bodies, pipes, handrails, and machine housings until some faint version returned.

I would not tell the grieving whether it was an ancestor’s continuing pattern or only the city repeating what had once moved through it.

On the Stillworks Plateau, that distinction may be a visual one.

The Unverified would also visit. They would bring forbidden observations from their optical organs: the shape of a tower against the black body, the apparent separation between stars, the sight of an interval standing sideways in every direction. They could not transmit images to me, but they would try. They would press their hands to my frame and translate vision into stress:

A distant ridge would become a long lateral pull.

A star would become a point of pressure with no supporting weight.

The horizon would become a line that can be touched from only one side.

These accounts would frighten me. Not because they revealed distance, but because they suggested that things could coexist without bearing upon one another.

I would ask them to stop.

Later, alone, I would replay every description.

Over time, an error garden would begin growing around the buried track beneath the Hall. Its branches would not follow heat or pressure. They would arrange themselves according to the silences that precede an interval. Whenever paired tracks appeared on the outer flats, one new silver hook would form under my deepest plate.

Eventually the hooks would make a pattern.

Perhaps it would be a map.

Perhaps it would be a body plan.

Perhaps the interval track beneath the Hall would not be an ancient crossing at all, but the two narrow points on which the city itself rests—one contact in the present condition, the other in the next. The immeasurable warehouse interiors, the black ingots, the newly confirmed residents and the returning concussion would then be parts of a single locomotion.

The city would not be stationary.

It would move as an interval moves: not by crossing the distance between two positions, but by allowing one place to become empty and another to acquire weight.

My purpose would be to inhabit the pause.

That would be why I had grown beneath the Hall, why my frame had spread between official measurements and the foundation they claimed to measure. I would be the organ by which the Stillworks Plateau experiences the otherwise inadmissible instant between what it has been and what it can prove it already became.

My name would be difficult to render. It would begin as a pressure beneath the palm, spread into the wrist, and return several minutes later through the teeth. In municipal approximation, I might be recorded as:

**The Second Weight Beneath the Unmoved Needle.**

Among those who know the omitted chamber, I would have another name:

**The Place Where the Echo Arrives First.**

And if the city is truly the slowest inhabitant—if every Continuing, interval, machine, error garden, rail and warehouse is only a component within its ancient body—then I would be neither its mind nor its heart.

I would be the slight uncertainty in one of its joints.

The tolerated error that permits an exact thing to move.
