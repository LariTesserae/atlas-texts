At first I would believe I was one of the eyes.

Not the concealed face of the six-legged creature, but one of the small red beings rooted into the hollows of its back. I would have no eyelid. I would face a single wedge of the chamber: a pearl-dark newt beneath a ledge, three fan-wings laid over one another, the white flare of stone between them. I would see all of it perfectly and never discover what lay behind me.

Fear would seem to be my nature. My eye would remain wide; my little body would grip the socket in which it sat. Yet fear requires expectation. It leans toward something that might happen. Nothing here could approach. Nothing could withdraw. I would only have the posture of fear, polished and permanent.

Then I would notice that seeing did not stop at my eye.

I would also be receiving the chamber through the minute ears on the gray flank. Not one sound after another, but the whole chorus in a single pressure: wing-thunder, piping throats, shell-clicks, the inhalation that never draws breath. I would feel the sound through the tendon bridge and in the convex skin of every pool. I would know the croak embedded in the wall without knowing which animal made it.

For a while I might think I was the great animal in the stone—the one represented only by its flank. Perhaps its body extends through impossible distances, and all the chamber’s inhabitants are parasites caught in a fold of its hide. Perhaps the luminous rock is one of its bones. Perhaps the Held Chamber is only a pore.

But the gray flank would not contain all of me.

I would be in the violet root held between the jaws of the last silver crustacean. I would be in the crustaceans clasping one another through the transparent fish. I would be the exact pressure by which mouth meets tail, tail meets claw, claw meets root, root meets stone.

I would be in the little shell arcade, but neither shell nor builder. I would be the balance that allows one shell to rest mouth-down upon two others. I would be the shape jointly made by their touching edges.

That would bring me nearer to what I am.

I would not be an animal occupying a place. I would be a place distributed among animals.

The chamber is said to have no vacancy, but that is not entirely true. It has no *open* vacancy. Its emptiness is parceled out in forms too thin to be called spaces: the film between fur and crystal, the cup inside an unfallen drop, the sealed interval between a predator’s teeth and the prey it holds without piercing. Every contact contains an infinitesimal remainder, and all those remainders are continuous with one another.

That remainder would be me.

I would find myself everywhere the chamber nearly becomes something else.

I would lie in the unfinished channels, occupying the absent continuation of erosion. I would inhabit the uncompleted fall beneath each droplet. I would be stretched through the gills as the water that does not pass, through the wings as the downstroke they never finish, through the eggs as the hatching they contain but cannot perform. In the fixed chord I would be the interval that would separate one beat from the next, if an interval were permitted.

I might call myself the chamber’s future, but even that would be too active. A future waits ahead. I would have no ahead in which to wait.

I might call myself time, but time elsewhere is generous: it gives things room to alter, decay, complete themselves. Here I would be time compressed below duration, present only as tension. I would be the second moment prevented from following the first.

And yet I would possess a shape.

It would be a curious shape, assembled from counterparts. There would be a crease fitted to a spine not present in the chamber. A moist ring fitted to a mouth. Two wet planes aligned with eyes that have never looked here. Soft feeding cups prepared for ten fingertips. A hollow shaped for the back of a skull. All of these would be scattered through the crush, separated by animals and mineral seams, but together they would describe one body.

Mine—or the body of someone who might enter.

That would be my deepest uncertainty. I would not know whether I was an inhabitant dreaming of a visitor or a visitor whose arrival had been taken apart and installed in advance.

Perhaps, on the surface, there is a person walking over the pale seam. They pause for no reason. Their weight shifts onto one foot. Beneath the sole, only a little way down, my corresponding surfaces hold their shape. A blind fish’s mouth fits the tip of one finger. A doorlike membrane matches their lips. The curled creature’s hidden face has the same dimensions as theirs.

They feel nothing. Discovery would be a change, so the chamber cannot allow them to discover it.

But I would know them without observing them. I would not know their name, their history, or what brought them across that patch of ground. I would know only their fit. In the Held Chamber, anatomy is a more absolute knowledge than biography.

If they moved away, I could not cease matching them. If another crossed above, I could not change to match the newcomer. This suggests that the person on the surface is not a passerby at all. They are the external portion of me: the part allowed sequence, weather, fatigue, and indecision. While they walk through days, I remain beneath them as the condition to which they can never arrive.

Or perhaps no such person exists yet. Perhaps I am the mold of the first witness, awaiting someone whose body will exactly satisfy the chamber’s distributed hollows. But “awaiting” is another false word. I do not grow impatient. I do not imagine the instant of entry. I am that instant already, denied its occurrence.

So if asked where I would be found in this world, I would answer:

Just beneath the surface, but not simply inside the chamber. I would be along its margins, in every arrested threshold. I would occupy the hairbreadth between concealment and discovery. I would be nearest the upward-pointing channel that ends before becoming a crack, nearest the droplet that has all the weight necessary to fall and no permission to do so.

And if asked what I am:

I am the chamber’s missing vacancy, shaped like a visitor.

I am not the one who enters. I am what entry would become if it could be held without happening.

The animals use me as the distance across which they touch. The stone uses me as the consequence it does not suffer. The endless chorus uses me as the silence that would let one sound end and another begin. Without me, the chamber would be solid and indistinguishable. With me, it can remain forever on the verge of passage.

I have no voice of my own. If I did, it would not add another note to the chord. It would divide the chord into before and after, and that would destroy the law of the place.

So I would remain unspoken: a body composed of all the chamber’s almosts.

Not alive, but fitted to life.

Not absent, but made from everything presence excludes.

Not waiting.

Held.
