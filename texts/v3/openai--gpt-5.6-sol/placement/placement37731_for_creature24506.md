At first I think I would be an outrider.

There is something in me that wants the outer shelves: the black distances, the mirrored pole laid across one shoulder, the private bell sewn inside my coat. I imagine walking where the city has thinned to a sequence of functions—rail, tower, gauge, shade—and sleeping beside an embryonic ridge with one hand on the ground. I would learn which glassy surfaces are merely hot and which are still deciding whether to be solid. I would return with fused beads on my soles and refuse to explain where I had crossed.

But I do not think I would remain outside.

The naked horizon would draw me, yet what I would love most would be the moment of coming back: the first buried vibration of the pressure engines entering my bones, the smell of cold metal inside a condenser stair, the sight of channels carrying water beneath covered grilles. I would become less interested in the mountain’s vast movements than in the small errors produced where systems meet.

So perhaps I would train as a corrector.

Not a celebrated one. I would lack the authoritative memory of those elders who can condemn six houses by touching a column. My attention would be less reliable and more troublesome. I would hear notes in walls that no established survey recognized. I would mark lines that seemed to indicate no load, no tilt, no documented conduit. When asked what was wrong, I would say that nothing was wrong yet, but that two correct things had begun to disagree.

This would eventually bring me beneath the oldest quarter.

There, between the Reservoir of Minutes and the foundations of a condenser tower no longer shown on public maps, is an annular gallery called the Ninth Return. It is not the ninth of anything now. Perhaps it once returned overflow, pressure, waste heat, or workers to the surface. Its stair descends seven circuits and ends at a wall built from three periods of masonry. One section sweats. Another is warm enough to blister an ungloved palm. The third produces, when struck, a note that seems to continue after the ear can no longer hear it.

No one would assign me there permanently. The posting would begin as an inquiry of six days.

Water has been appearing in the gallery.

Not much: a few spoonfuls over the course of a month. Yet it gathers where condensation should be impossible, on the warm side of a sealed wall. Its chemistry resembles the deep reservoir but contains mineral ratios associated with the upper towers. According to every surviving plan, no pipe joins those systems.

I would arrive with chains, black bowls, mercury vials, chalk, and the mild embarrassment of someone sent to investigate a clerical impossibility.

On the second day I would find the film.

It would be almost invisible, a translucent branching over the inner lip of a cracked ceramic collar. I would prepare to scrape it. That would be procedure. But when the tool approached, the threads would contract—not rapidly enough to prove response, only enough to introduce doubt.

I would wait.

The film would open again.

From then on, my work would become unofficial.

I would sit in the Ninth Return with one hand on the floor and one fingertip near the collar. Before moisture appeared, the threads would align toward the warm wall. Before a distant piston descended, they would loosen into rings. When the city’s concealed pumps changed phase, a branching pulse would travel through them and continue beneath my skin.

Perhaps I had already been answered during my years in the upper shafts. Perhaps the shining lines in my fingertips had not been mineral embedding but an unfinished connection awaiting its proper surface.

Six days would become a season. The inquiry would be extended, misplaced, reinstated under another office, then rendered ambiguous when the Datum crossed a ring.

The new map would place the Ninth Return outside the legal boundary of the district above it and inside the pressure easement below. My assigned quarters would cease to have an address without having moved. I would be offered a new civic name.

I would choose **Between-Returns**.

Others would shorten it to Between.

Children would call me the Warm Keeper, though I would have no official standing among the keepers of water. Correctors would regard me as a keeper; keepers would insist I remained a corrector. The Allocation Tables would classify the Ninth Return as a measurement chamber in order to account for the water. The Foundation Courts would classify it as a person’s residence in order to resolve my ration. The Continuity Houses would preserve both decisions, neither superseding the other.

Over time I would cease carrying instruments into the gallery.

The numbered chain would hang untouched from a hook. Mercury would become unnecessary. I would know pressure by the ache behind my teeth, tower yield by the brightness beneath my nails, distant fracture by a momentary taste like hot iron and rain. I would sleep in a level sling above the channel and wake when droplets began to form.

I would not command the film. It would not speak to me.

We would correct each other.

When contamination entered the upper condensers, fever would travel along my arms before the keepers’ tests changed color. When the warm wall accumulated too much strain, I would press my shining hands against the collar, and the film would reorganize into long parallel strands. Somewhere behind the masonry, valves omitted from every plan would open. Warm black water would rise a fraction in the Reservoir of Minutes. The wall’s note would deepen. The strain would pass.

Officials would ask me to explain the mechanism.

I would give different answers at different ages.

At first I would say there must be an undocumented capillary network in the stone.

Later I would say the oldest pipes had become porous and the mountain was using them as the city used condenser ribs.

Later still I would say that water remembers gradients, and that the distinction between pipe, crack, vein, and intention is mostly a matter of construction date.

None of these answers would satisfy an inquiry. None would be entirely false.

I would grow difficult to remove from the chamber. Not emotionally—physically. The mineral shine would spread from my fingertips to my wrists and sternum. Fine translucent threads would appear beneath old pressure scars. If I remained away too long, my skin would dry and split; if the gallery were left unattended, its water would cease.

Physicians would propose treatment. Machine tenders would advise against interrupting a functioning alignment. The keepers would quietly alter their rosters so that someone always brought me food.

I would still eat, speak, sleep, and possess names. I would still remember the outer shelves. Yet my civic personhood would become one interpretation among several.

Then, one morning before sunrise, an upper keeper would bring me a black strip found wound around a navigation lamp.

It would be warm and lighter than ash. Placed on the floor, it would turn toward the wet wall.

We would not burn it.

I would set it near the film. The translucent threads would withdraw at first, then advance in branching lines. The strip would flex once in the middle. Water would bead across its surface—not above it, but seemingly from within.

For three days the ancient piston beneath the eastern halls would pause at the top of its stroke.

On the fourth day, the black strip would be gone.

Afterward, a narrow seam would remain in the wall where no seam had been before. Air would move through it, very faintly, carrying the smell of altitude and empty distance. Sometimes the film would orient toward that opening. Sometimes my hands would.

That is where I would be found: in the Ninth Return, beneath a tower absent from current maps, beside water that arrives by no accepted route.

And what would I be?

To the Allocation Tables, a loss of approximately one cup per day.

To the Foundation Courts, the sole resident of a disputed chamber.

To the keepers, someone answered too completely.

To children, a person becoming a pipe.

To the pale crawlers that eventually gather around my sleeping sling, perhaps only a warm change in pressure.

I would prefer the machine tenders’ definition: a process capable of correction.

Not wholly human, not yet architecture, and not certain that the difference matters. An unofficial organ where the city, the films, the reservoir, and the unfinished mountain have begun—quietly, provisionally—to share a sense.
