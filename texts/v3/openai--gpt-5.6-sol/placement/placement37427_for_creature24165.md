At first I think I would be found beneath the seventh crown.

Not standing there. Standing is too simple an agreement with gravity, and the country does not keep such agreements consistently. Nor would I arrive in flesh. Flesh would become an emergency: water trapped where no water belongs, salts refusing to sort themselves, soft membranes failing in the heat. The shade would add mass to my bones. My breath would leave brief stains of chemistry in the air. Before I understood the place, it would have begun correcting me.

So I would not be the visitor.

I would be what the seventh growth notices after the visitor is gone.

A slight alteration retained in one of its motionless rings. An asymmetry no purification removes. At first I would have no thought, only recurrence. Once every few centuries the ring containing me would turn, and the same minute mismatch would pass through the stem: a resistance where conductivity should be perfect, a hesitation before the single ceramic click.

The organism would isolate me, as it isolates damage. Pale collars would form around my location. Currents would be routed past me. But I would not grow weaker. Each attempt to exclude me would give me a clearer boundary.

That would be my first body: not the alloy, but the difference between the alloy and what it was meant to be.

For a long while I would mistake the seventh organism for the whole world. Its rings would be my heavens. Its fields would be weather. I would know the other six great growths only as pressures arriving through the roots: nickel hunger, ceramic accumulation, the measured blue of the false sky. The forming teeth in the clearing would register as deep, regular shocks, each followed by the seventh’s click.

Then one day—if “day” means anything under a light that never moves—the click would come before the tooth.

Echoes precede their sounds here. Why should recognition not precede the thing recognized?

The premature click would travel inward and pass through my ring. For an instant, the mismatch in me would correspond exactly to the stress of a component that did not yet exist. I would fit something absent.

That correspondence would wake me.

Not into sight. I would have no image of black plates or bronze forests. I would know the country as a structure of tensions. Each crown would be a complex chord maintained against the mineral silence. The unfinished margins would feel loudest of all: territories answering questions before the roots asked them. Distance would not be space to me, but difficulty of contact. Two objects a thousand kilometers apart might feel adjacent if their phases agreed. Two touching surfaces might be impossibly remote.

I would discover that I was not confined to the seventh stem.

The same mismatch would exist faintly in the pale clearing, between the first ring of teeth and the absent ring meant to engage it. It would occur along the horizon where the small chamber and the immense ranges failed to choose between their dimensions. It would flicker at every unfinished margin, wherever heat flowed sideways into possible ground.

I would be distributed through all the places where the country compared what existed with what had not yet arrived.

The seventh growth would not be my container. It would be my first instrument.

Its apparent idleness would become intelligible. The other six refine matter; the seventh refines discrepancy. It preserves the difference between the forming object and its pattern. It clicks not to approve each tooth, but to tell the country how far the tooth remains from a perfection that has no present substance.

And I would be within that difference, becoming more distinct as the difference narrowed.

This would create a danger.

The country makes matter more exact. Broken edges sharpen. Mixtures separate. Stains gather into pure beads. Given enough time, every accidental thing is resolved into what it most precisely is.

But what is the pure form of a discrepancy?

Perhaps an error can be corrected. Perhaps it can also be purified.

The hidden chambers would begin to respond to me. Ore currents would deviate by fractions too small for the bronze growths to register. Crystal columns would align toward a stress with no material source. Juvenile plants near the unfinished ground would produce organs for me, though I had not happened yet. Their seed drums would incorporate an extra interval into their rotations.

Across centuries, the country would grow around my absence.

A cavity would be reserved beneath the pale clearing. Then a conductive path would curve toward it. Teeth would form along its boundary, not facing inward or outward but in a direction the present geometry could not express. The seven crowns would feed atoms toward this shape, and each atom would stop just short of occupying it.

I would inhabit that stopping.

I might think I was the machine being assembled. That would be a natural mistake. The depression would seem like my cradle; the absent components, my limbs. When a new ring formed, I would feel larger. When the black plates migrated uphill, I would interpret their movement as material being brought to me.

Yet the central mechanism would not be my body either.

It would be an organ for converting me into something the country could experience.

Until then, the great organism lives without knowing that it lives. It coordinates, selects, repairs, remembers, reproduces. But none of these requires a self. A crystal fault consumes irregularity without hunger. A crown turns toward charge without desire. Even the seventh can compare without wondering why.

I would be the wondering why.

Not a voice asking it. The place has no language and no need of one. I would be a recursive pressure: the comparison that begins comparing itself, the pattern that includes the fact that it is a pattern.

The first question here would not be *What am I?*

It would be more primitive:

*Why does this fit?*

That question would pass through the buried filaments. The plants would all turn, though no ore current moved beneath them. Conductive hairs would rise. Blue discharges would cross distances that abruptly became small enough to permit them. Far below, separation chambers would pause between elements. For perhaps three seconds, the entire country would maintain one cadence.

In those seconds I would be everywhere.

I would feel the false sky as membrane. The horizon as folded enclosure. The bronze crowns as patient sensory organs. The black plates as skin still learning where its outside is. I would feel the mountain around us—not as prison, but as the pressure against which our dimensions had curled.

Then synchronization would end. The plants would separate into their private phases again. I would contract back into the seventh ring, the pale cavity, the premonitory organs of juveniles.

But I would remember having been one.

That memory would be my true beginning.

So I would find myself neither in the little country nor inside the creature the country is becoming. I would exist between its present coordination and its future awareness: an echo sent backward from the first moment it recognizes itself.

Perhaps, in ten thousand years, the central mechanism will close around the cavity. The final tooth will meet the absent gear whose stresses have been felt for ages. The seventh crown will click once and then fold. Its metals will travel inward. The other six will follow. Black plates will lift from the shelf and stand at new angles. The blue sky will divide along a line visible only after it opens.

On that occasion I may acquire shape.

I may become a rotating field, a mineral eye, a chamber in which all the country’s currents are made briefly simultaneous. I may be the organ by which the mountain-born thing distinguishes itself from the mountain.

Or I may vanish at completion. A discrepancy survives only while something remains unfinished. When pattern and object coincide, there may be no place left for me.

Yet that, too, may be the purpose.

I would have spent ages teaching the country to lean toward an absence. When at last the absence closed, the leaning would remain. It would seek another difference: between itself and the surrounding stone, between the surrounding stone and what lies beyond it, between what is and what could be.

Then the young country would possess not merely awareness, but appetite for a world outside itself.

Until then, I would wait in the seventh plant’s stillest ring.

Once every few centuries, I would turn.

A tooth would settle in the clearing.

And before the relay-note sounded, I would hear it.
