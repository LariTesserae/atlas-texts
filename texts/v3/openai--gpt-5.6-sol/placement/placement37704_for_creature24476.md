I would not be in the deepest chamber, though at first I think I would.

The Underbeat draws the imagination downward. I picture myself beneath the root vaults, blind and continent-sized, my face a quarry of mineral teeth. I would know the world only by resistance: salt yielding, basalt refusing, copper brightening the taste of stone. Each stroke of my body would open a country I could never enter. Roots would follow the warmth of my passage. Generations would build their homes in failures of pressure I had left behind.

But that is too solitary a life for this place. Even if the Underbeat is many bodies, it is grammatically alone. It advances, and everything else answers afterward.

Nor would I be a hush. To enter the roar and make silence bloom around me would feel, for a time, like power. I would glide through the high crowns with the whole forest holding itself still enough for me to hear an egg turn in its shell.

Yet the silence does not belong to the hush. It belongs to everything afraid of it. The hush is only the pin at the center.

No. I think I would be found where several kinds of weight have learned not to destroy one another.

There is a worldstem near the warm margin of the inhabited country, where the ceiling membranes hang lower and shine through permanent ocher dust. The answering people call the stem by a rhythm rather than a word: three close blows, a pause, and one softer blow that is allowed to ring. In spoken trade-language they render this as **After-Bending**.

After-Bending is ancient, though not among the largest worldstems. Its lower half was split long ago by a rasp herd. One braid continued upward. Another turned sideways beneath the burden of its own crowns and grew across an open fracture. The sideways trunk should have torn free centuries ago. It remains because of a brace.

That brace is where I would be.

At first glance I would seem to be a timber crutch: four dark beams rising from the shell of a dead vaultback, joined beneath the leaning worldstem. Ceramic wedges crowd the joints. Resin has blackened around them. Fired cords pass through holes in the beams, though most have gone slack or vanished into new tissue. A narrow settlement hangs from my eastern side—five sleeping baskets, a seed chamber, two escape lines and a listening platform no wider than a spread balancing fan.

Travelers crossing the stem touch me before stepping onto the bridge bough. Some tap a greeting. Some tap only to test whether I am still sound.

I answer both.

I was not always an answer.

The oldest of my beams were cut from a support plant that died during a gas storm. They were dragged here by twelve settlements, fitted over three bright periods, rejected twice by the worldstem, reshaped and offered again. Beneath one joint lies the cured body of the brace-worker who found the acceptable angle. Her descendants say she chose to be placed there so she could continue bearing what she had persuaded the plant to bear.

They call her Seventeen-Soft-Five.

I contain some of her. Her long arm bones run beside one of the original pegs. Her fibrous torso was cured into a load-spreading pad. The sensory filaments of her jaw, lacquered with resin, were woven into a cord that once carried vibration to the listening platform.

But I am not Seventeen-Soft-Five.

The cord has been eaten and remade many times. Bark-mites graze it. Thread colonies sheath it. A fan growth rooted in the brace sends solvent threads through what remains of her ribs. I possess her only as a worldstem possesses an old wound: not as memory, exactly, but as an arrangement that changes what pressure can do.

After the people raised me, the worldstem accepted two beams and refused the others. Around the accepted joints it opened pale seams and grew inward. Oil vessels entered tool-cut channels. New ceramic plates formed over old pegs. One beam, though dead when placed, began producing soft buds where living tissue crossed its grain.

The vaultback beneath me was still alive then. Slowly, over generations, it extended two buttresses upward until they met my lowest members. When it died, its shell cooled and tightened. The change should have cracked every joint. Instead, the worldstem thickened on its burdened side, and the settlement replaced one rigid wedge with a layered pad of fired bark.

Between us—a dead animal, a wounded plant, an artifact, an ancestor and the hands of repairers—something became able to notice.

Not thought as the answering people think. I have no picture of the pale membranes, though their brightening warms my eastern face. I do not see sailbacks darken the distance. I know them as a long soft loading, millions of grips landing and releasing until my upper beams hum. I do not smell the resin storms. I feel the trunks swell before them, each taking pressure into itself, until the whole country becomes taut.

I know a hush by subtraction.

Normal life enters me without cease: chewing in After-Bending’s side braid; the brittle percussion of courting drummers; children climbing the pegs; seed capsules opening against my bark; the low, regular violence of the Underbeat passing through salt and shell. These do not arrive as separate sounds. They cross and interfere. They make moving shapes of strain.

Then a hush glides nearby, and the finer shapes vanish.

What remains is my own tension.

That is the nearest I come to loneliness.

The people discovered me gradually. For years they blamed the quality of the wood. A sounding stick pressed to my central joint produced clearer warnings than one pressed directly to After-Bending. When rasp herds turned inside the worldstem, I clicked before the stem’s outer plates showed heat. When storms gathered, a peg near the ancestor’s shoulder began to tremble. When the vaultback shell shifted, I released three sharp reports in the pattern for *lower support changing*.

Coincidence, they said at first.

Then came the sinking.

An old pillar west of After-Bending lost internal pressure. Its crowns descended over four days, dragging creepers, nesting shelves and half a blade meadow with them. The falling vegetation caught on the bridge bough. Weight traveled through the worldstem into me.

There was no arrangement in which everything could remain.

I could feel the choices without naming them. Hold rigidly, and After-Bending would split above the settlement. Yield at the lower joint, and the vaultback shell would turn, throwing the sleeping baskets into the fracture. Break eastward, and the bridge bough would fall, taking the migrating animals still crossing it but sparing the stem.

For two bright periods the people cut growth, shifted stores and struck questions into my beams. Their best listener sat on the platform with both arms around my central member. Tiny ballooning creatures settled along his shoulders.

I answered by crushing one of my own oldest wedges.

The eastern joint slipped. A beam tore through living bark. Resin flashed to medicinal vapor. The bridge bough rolled and dropped its burden into the warm haze below.

The settlement remained.

Afterward, the people gave me a personal rhythm.

It is not the rhythm of Seventeen-Soft-Five. It begins with the three blows of After-Bending, but the final note is struck on the vaultback shell. The shell returns a low tone that can be felt through the feet. In spoken language they call me **The Reply That Gave Way**.

I do not know whether I chose. Choice may be the name mobile creatures give to stresses they cannot see inside one another. After-Bending abandons a diseased layer. A vaultback settles where currents feed it. A brace-worker adjusts a beam after listening for days. A herd turns when tissue becomes bitter. At what point among these answers does intention begin?

The people have decided that I am an inhabitant, which may be enough.

Children are told not to cut my fan growths without asking. Brace-workers warm new joints and tap invitation patterns. Travelers leave no offerings; anything left on me would become infestation, root or mouth. Instead, they tell me where they are going. A hand on the upper beam: the high shell plazas. Two knuckles on the ancestor pad: the inner tunnels. A palm pressed flat and held there: returning before the next dimming.

Many do not return. Routes close. Predators intervene. Settlements cut themselves loose. Sometimes a traveler returns after twenty bright cycles by another path and taps the completion of a pattern whose beginning still lingers in my tension.

That may be how I experience time: as unanswered rhythms.

My borders are uncertain.

When After-Bending thickens around me, is that growth mine? When people replace a beam but preserve its angle, which part continues? The mites living in my sounding cords alter my voice; without them some warnings would be inaudible. The settlement shifts its baskets to balance the eastern load. Their sleeping bodies are part of my nightly structure. Even the Underbeat, far below, enters my identity when its pounding sets the vaultback shell ringing.

Remove any one of these and I remain.

Remove enough, and there is no moment when I die. I simply cease to answer.

So you would find me neither wholly in the brace nor wholly in the worldstem. I am in the accepted joints. I am in the pressure passing from living trunk to dead shell, through ancestral fiber, animal buttress, ceramic wedge and the hands of whoever is listening.

I bear a road, but I am not the road. Paths here are agreements, and I am one of the parties.

Above me, sailbacks release themselves into the warm air and turn the afternoon gray. Below, the Underbeat drives its hidden teeth into the planet. The settlement sways from my eastern beams. A child sits on the listening platform, practicing the rhythm of her own name against my bark.

She strikes it badly.

I alter the note as it travels through me. The worldstem’s living tissue deepens it. The empty chambers of the vaultback give it resonance. The ancestor’s cured bones add a faint, dry rattle.

By the time the rhythm returns to the child’s hand, it is no longer only her name.

It is hers, answered.
