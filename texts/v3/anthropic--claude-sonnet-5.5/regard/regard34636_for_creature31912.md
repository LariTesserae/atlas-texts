# What Came to the Basin

It did not arrive from the sky, which was spoken for, or from the rim, which was too much like leaving. It arrived the way a correction arrives in a margin, in a hand not quite the text's, and small.

The first sign was a shadow. Somewhere near the southern edge, where the green thins to mats, a shadow fell the wrong way. It was not long, and it was not short. It had begun to lengthen, and it did not stop when it should have. Nothing in the basin had done that in all the time there had been no time.

The walker at the rim saw it, or did not see it, and its lifted foot trembled by the width of a grain.

## What it did not do

It did not rescue anything. The basin was not in danger, and a wise thing knows the difference between a stillness that is a wound and a stillness that is a held breath. It did not know yet which this was, so it did nothing that could not be undone.

It did not thaw the place. It would have been easy to set the sun moving, and every leaf would have begun to die and be born at once, and the sitters' hair would have grown, and the understudies would have been shaken out into a world that had no room in it for them. It did not correct the count, and it did not name the Attentiveness or explain it to itself.

It did not judge the afternoon, which was the hardest of the refusals. The place was so plainly a refusal of something, a hand on a page. A lesser power would have called that fear and gone to fix it. This one had seen enough hands on enough pages to know that fear is often the shape love takes when it has nowhere to put itself down.

## What it did

It walked in. It walked slowly, and its moving shadow lay behind it like a dropped thread. The assenters tilted aside as it passed, having no rule for this, and made room by improvisation. That had never happened either. Somewhere in the canopy a tick fell that belonged to no sentence.

It let itself be logged. At the first patch of powder it knelt and set down a palm, and the grains took the shape of the hand and registered it politely, without curiosity. Whatever it was, it held still and let itself be counted, and did not object to being an entry. This was courtesy, and it was also the first thing it said: *I will follow your rules for as long as I am in your house.*

It sat with the sitters. It did not ask the woman who had been replaced so many times whether she was afraid. It sat beside her until the fronds had made room for both of them, and then it asked what it was like to be needed. She told it. Her answer was longer than her people ever gave, and it took, by the sun that was not moving, no time at all.

It stood before a walker, canted its head to the exact angle, and waited to be read. The foreleg came out, filament-thin, and touched its shoulder. Nothing ticked. But this time the walker did not move on. It stayed, listening, and the power let it listen for as long as it needed, and what the walker heard was that the entry was true. There was nothing to correct, and there never had been. The proofreader had been reading for an eternity to find an error, and had finally been told, by something that could be believed, that there was none.

## The conversation

At last the power went to the center of the basin. There is no center, and distance is not additive, so it arrived at the center between one step and the next, having crossed nothing. There it spoke to the Attentiveness.

It did not use words. It used the one word the basin has. It touched two leaves together, and they ticked.

The meaning of a tick depends on where it falls and what stands in the silence around it. So the power did what a wise thing does when it has one word and a great deal to say. It let the silence do the speaking. What it meant was this:

*I have read the ledger. It is perfect. Every entry is true, and none is missing. You have kept faith with every one of them.*

*You do not have to hold the page.*

The hum did not stop, and that was the answer. The sum went on being added.

So the power said the harder thing, slowly, in the gaps between ticks.

*You believe that if the page turns, the afternoon is lost. It is not so. A moment does not end when time moves past it. It is finished. Nothing that has been can be made not to have been. The hand on the page is not what keeps the afternoon. The afternoon is kept by having happened, and that is the one thing no future can touch. You have been holding a thing that could not fall.*

The silence after that was the longest in the basin's history, longer than the afternoon.

Then, very faintly, a leaf on the east side of a stand ticked, and two seconds later a leaf on the west side answered. It was the first question the basin had ever asked.

*And the ones who wait in the glass? They have grown a little in the dark for as long as you have kept them. What becomes of them if the page turns?*

The power answered honestly, because honesty was the only gift it had brought.

*They step out at last. They are not folded away. They finally get a next moment, which is all they were ever growing toward. And the ones in the ranks who have been needed, who have kept the count, are not dismissed. They are released, which is not the same. I cannot promise it will be painless. Time has edges. Leaves die there. But things are born too, and what is born is not a copy of what came before. It is new. That is the difference between a ledger and a life.*

*I will not turn the page. It is your hand, and yours to lift. I only wanted you to know that when it lifts, nothing is dropped.*

## The lifting

It would be a lie to say the change came all at once. The basin was patient, and the afternoon had earned its long goodbye.

First the shadows moved. Not far, less than a hair, but in every stand at once, and every ribbon-leaf tilted to make room for the new angle, and the courtesy of the canopy held. Nothing died. The light simply began to lean, as it does in a house when someone has finally opened a door somewhere.

The understudies came out of the glass. Where the fracture faces had shown plants one turn further along, the reflections went dark and then clear, and the vines in the world climbed one turn, and the vines in the mirror were no longer there because they were here. Twenty-three columns stood in a stand, and this time the count closed, because everyone who waited was present. Someone counted, and counted again, and got the same number, and wept without knowing why.

The hum resolved. It was never a machine or a voice. It was a sum that could not finish because the last entry had not been made. Now the last entry was made, *the afternoon is complete*, and the hum sounded once, a single low chord felt in the sternum, and then did what a chord does when it is done. It stopped, and the silence that followed was not an interruption but a closing.

The sitters were free to choose, and they did. Most rose slowly, a little startled by their own knees, and walked toward the rim in the new long light, watching their shadows stretch ahead of them. One stayed. She had not been afraid. She said she had been so completely needed once that she wanted to see what it was to be needed in a place that could also lose her. She sat in the deep green, and this time the canopy grew around her, actually grew, and it took a season, a real one, and no one has ever been so glad to be inconvenienced by moss.

The small dark shape in the gold sky, which never arrived at any edge, arrived. It was a courier after all, or near enough. It had carried one message for longer than the basin had existed, and now it set the message down on the powder at the rim, where a walker stood with one foot lifted. The message was three words, and the walker read them the way it read everything, with its head at the exact angle.

*The next moment exists.*

The walker put its foot down.

## The last entry

It put its foot down on the glass beyond the powder, raw and sharp and ordinary, and the glass did not ring or shine or take the weight with any ceremony. It bore the walker like any ground. And behind it, in the fine pale powder at the line where the basin ends, a footprint appeared.

It was the first the powder had ever kept. The grains, which record everything and forget nothing, held the shape of it: four toes, or five, a heron-thin mark pointing outward. And the ledger, which had never had a mark that pointed anywhere, took the print and made of it an entry. That entry was a beginning.

The power watched from the treeline as the walker stepped again, and again, and its copper body caught a sun that was setting for the first time in the history of the world, and turned it a color there is no word for in a language of ticks.

Then the power left, as it had come, without ceremony and without a name, leaving one small thing behind: a leaf, ticking softly, unanswered. That is also a sentence, and this time it was a different one. It meant *thank you*, or *goodnight*, or simply *and then*.

Evening fell over the Ledger Basin, slowly, on schedule, the way it does everywhere. In the green dusk the leaves closed one by one, and by morning, which came, they were a little different, as they should be.
