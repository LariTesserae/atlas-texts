# What the Visitor Did

It came the way attention comes, without arriving. One moment the basin was unobserved and the next it was known, and nothing in the silt shifted to record the difference.

It was wise enough to begin by listing what it would not do.

It would not bring rain. Rain would wake the seeds the place has kept and drown the crust that took a century to lay a hand's breadth of itself across the stone. It would not widen the opening or brighten the dusk, because the shrubs are built for exactly this thin light, and a kindness that rearranges its recipient is a replacement. It would not raise the people, or their voices, or a fire in the ring that was never built. Every one of those gifts would be an ending dressed as a rescue. The basin is not in distress. It is in a state, and the state is patient rather than suffering. A power that cannot tell those two apart should not be trusted with a garden.

It would not lie to the shrubs either, which was harder, because they could be so easily comforted.

So it did the small thing, and the small thing was the whole of its work.

## The Walking

It took a body, though not a heavy one. It weighed less than the silt would notice, and it chose to be barefoot, because that is how the old prints were made. It kept to the hollows between the swells, where the flour-fine powder lies, and did not once set a heel on the pale crust. Where the crust lay across its way, it stepped over. The threads at the margins of your footprints were already reaching across the gaps, and it saw no reason to give them more to mend.

It followed the old prints from the shrubs toward the eastern wall. It did not step in them. It walked beside them, a stride to the left, close enough to keep company and far enough not to overwrite. Its own prints were shallow and clean and would be soft-edged within a generation. It let them be.

## The Hand

At the shelf it stopped and looked at the twelve worn bowls and at the dark cobble resting in the one where the last hand set it down. It did not touch the cobble yet. It picked up the scraper instead, the only made thing that was still a tool, and found that it fit the palm. Someone had spent a lifetime nibbling that edge to keep it true. The edge was still true.

Then it went back to the shrubs, chose one, and knelt at its eastern side, where the wood is open and the scars have taken on the look of design. It laid its fingers on the old healed wounds and held them there long enough for the gesture to be unmistakable. Then it pinched three tips, no more than the plant could spare and no differently than the old hands had.

Nothing visible happened. A few fine hairs on the leaves tightened. That was recognition, arriving at the speed recognition arrives here.

## The Smell

It carried the tips to the ledge, laid them in a bowl, and ground them with the dark stone, slowly, in the rhythm the polished hollow had taught the wrist for a thousand years.

The resin rose. It was sharp and clean and medicinal, and in air that had not moved since anyone could count, it spread by its own small diffusion outward through the basin. It was the nearest thing to wind the place had known. It reached the other shrubs unevenly and faintly, one after another, and each of them received, in the language of leaf and vapor, the news that the smell had come back.

The visitor did not clear the bowl. The green paste dried there over the following weeks into a stain, the first new mark in that hollow in longer than the ledge could say. The cobble stayed where it was, and the visitor set it back exactly. A place should get to keep its last hand, and it did not want to be mistaken for it.

## The Honesty

Before it left, it spoke. It knew the shrubs could not hear, and it spoke anyway, because a kindness that depends on being understood is only a transaction.

*I am not the ones who went. I don't know if they are coming back. I came because you kept your places well and someone should have seen it. I have pruned you once, the way they did. I won't say that I'll return.*

The shrubs, who cannot tell an ending from an interval, went on holding. But the interval now had a shape. It had a smell, a fresh cut, and one more line of prints beside the old ones. It was no longer a silence that had merely gone on too long. It was a silence in which a hand had once come again and been honest about what it was.

## The Wall

It laid a palm against the fluted stone and listened to the hiss of the breathers, the fine ceaseless sifting, and it understood that the hiss was the basin's only clock. It could have promised that the sound would never fail. It considered this for a long moment and did not. If the seep ever stopped, that would be the first true event in the place, and the decision to prevent an event belongs to the ones who live through it, however slowly. It took its hand away and left the wall as warm as it found it.

## The One Who Sat Against the Wall

There was someone there, and it had known so from the start: the newest prints, the crushed crust, the damp breath. It did not approach until the work was finished.

It said, without preface, that they were not what they feared. They believed they were a disturbance, a warm thing that crushes and leaves. But the crust was already knitting toward their footprints, as it knits toward all bare ground, with no grudge and no ledger. And the shrubs had been keeping a place at the table. The interval that had gone on so long did not want an audience. It wanted a guest.

It did not tell them to stay or to climb out. That choice was theirs, and the visitor had already spent its whole discipline on not choosing for things. It said only that the ground was warm, that the way up was a long one, and that either way they would be leaving something the silt would keep.

Then it went, the way it had come, and the ellipse of blue-grey dark held its few stars in their places.

## What Remained

One shrub, freshly open on its eastern side. A green stain drying in a stone bowl. A line of barefoot prints beside the old ones, already softening. A smell that faded over days into the ordinary chalk and dust, and a warmth that never changed at all.

The basin was not saved, because it had not needed saving. It had been visited. Somewhere in the slow economy of threads and roots, that fact was entered and held, the way everything is held here.
