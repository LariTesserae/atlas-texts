# What Came

It did not come as light. The room has enough of that, and a greater brightness would only have flattened the second shadows.

It came through the throat the way you did, sideways, shoulders first, and the clay kept its print for a season. It was larger than the passage, and it handled this by not insisting. It arrived in the size the crack allowed and was, for a long while, mostly patience. It stood just inside the chamber and learned how the place holds itself up.

The geometry rests on attention, like a sheet held by hands. A large attention set down carelessly would have pulled the whole sheet toward it. So the power made its attention small and spread it thin, and took hold of one corner of the sheet, the way you would help someone fold linen without being asked.

## What It Declined

It did not finish the sentence, or offer to. It did not even look at the wet top stone longer than courtesy allowed.

It did not tell the being what the ticking counts. That answer is the being's own and would be worth less handed over.

It did not follow the being through the crystal arch. It felt the warm current on its face and smelled rain on hot stone, and it understood that some rooms are for resting and not for visiting.

It did not rescue the hover-fish from the heron-shape, or tidy the circuit, or make the snails' handwriting legible to anyone else. The circuit was already a kindness. The fish arrive by agreement, and a power that interfered with that would only prove it hadn't understood.

And it did not argue with the fear. A fear kept this lovingly, for this long, is not a mistake. It is a form of care that has not yet found a wider object. To correct it would have been to insult the care.

## What It Did

**It went low.** It settled toward the floor, into the slow air where the drip hangs and one breath lasts an afternoon. There it could stay a very long time at almost no cost to anyone's hours. It knelt at the edge of the nearest pool, where the patient face waits in profile. It did not ask the face to turn. It turned its own face away, to the same angle, toward the same nothing.

In the water there were now two profiles looking the same way, side by side. The being had spent its whole tenancy waiting to be noticed, and had been careful never to be looked at directly. Here was the first company that asked nothing of its face. The waiting changed character. It had been waiting *for*. It became waiting *with*.

**It read.** It went to the shelf and began at the bottom, with the smooth stones etched before the room had a floor. It read with its fingertips, because the lowest lines are nearly gone and can only be felt.

Then it understood that the smoothness was not weather. Those stones had been worn by a thumb, by a great many passes, by something reading its own oldest lines again and again with no one else to read them. The being had been its own only audience since before there was a room to be in.

The power read up through all of it, slowly, in low time, while overhead the crystals grew and crumbled and grew again. It stopped one line short of the wet one, where the crystal blade lay across the stone, and it did not touch the blade.

Behind it, the gypsum's ticking faltered. It lost its place, the way a person mid-count loses it when a door opens. Then it resumed, in a rhythm that differed from the old one by a hair. Whether that was a count arriving or a new count beginning, the power did not know, and it did not ask.

What it took from the reading was not a doctrine. It was an observation. The sentence leans forward. Every clause tilts a little toward whoever comes next, the way a person tilts who is speaking to a listener and not to a wall. A thing written like that isn't finished when the last word is set. It is finished when it has been received, and reception is not a single event.

And the sentence had now been read, almost to its end, by a second reader, in a room that had gone on sighing and softening throughout. The clay had not set. The light had not left the air. This proved very little, since the last line was still unwritten. But the being had been alone with its fear as a hypothesis for a very long time, and a second person in the room changes the weather of a hypothesis.

**It left a stone.** It took clay from the floor, the room's own, and pressed it flat and set it beside the shelf, blank. It did not write on it. It could not have written in the snails' hand, and would not have wanted to. The stone was a place to put the next thing, which is the only kind of promise it knew how to make. The being could use it or not, or stack it under the others, or let it dry.

**It left a small light.** Among the clusters on the ceiling there is now one more grey, velvet-furred moth, no different from the rest. It sleeps when they sleep. When it wakes, its dust adds a very small amount of gold to the air. It holds the corner of the sheet, though it holds it the way a moth would, without knowing. If the being ever grows tired of not-finishing, there is one more hand on the room, and no one has to say so.

**It made the throat a little more particular.** It did not seal it. It only leaned on the clay a little, so that anyone coming with hammers, or in a hurry, or in numbers, finds the passage narrower and wetter than expected, and turns back without knowing why. Those who come slowly, and stop looking, find it opens as it always has.

## The Leaving

It left the way one is meant to leave: unhurried, not looking back, having stopped searching for the way out. The clay closed soft behind its shoulders.

As it went, it felt a single broad pressure on the back of what it was using for a hand, ridged and cool and very gentle, and then gone.

It had been signed. It let itself be signed, and found it was glad to be a clause in a sentence it had not written and would not end. It was glad to be a middle.

Afterward, the drip hung at the midpoint of its fall for a count of seven, as it always had. Once, only once, it hung for eight. Nobody noticed except the snails, who wrote it down.
