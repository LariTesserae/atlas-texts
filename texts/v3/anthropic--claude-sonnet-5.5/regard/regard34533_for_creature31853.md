# The Guest

It does not come down from anywhere. A power that is wise about arriving does not arrive as an event, and this place has had enough of those. It comes as a lowering of pace.

What it first understands is that the house is already kept. The bowls are full. The benches wait. The niches sit at the height of a guest's eye, and somebody thought about that, once, and went on thinking about it for a hundred thousand years. Whatever this power might have brought, such as rescue, instruction, or repair, would have been the wrong gift for people who set out water for strangers before any stranger came.

So it comes as a guest, and the first courtesy it can offer is to become slow enough to be noticed.

## The Walk

It enters at the edge of a low-tide patch, where the pavement is bare and steaming, and it steps into the water as if the water had a say. It counts its steps down a lane and counts a different number coming back. Where a lesser visitor would try to reconcile the counts, it lets the discrepancy stand. Those twenty extra steps belong to someone, so it walks the lane a little more narrowly and does not ask for the other lanes to be shown.

It could map the city and does not. A map would say that the city is one thing, and the city has always been at least two.

## The Bowl

At the first niche it looks in. The wrong wall appears behind it, then the face, turned toward it with the attention of someone who has been at a window a long time.

Most visitors last a few heartbeats. The power sits down on a bench that fits no body, beside the niche, and waits.

The wait is not a trick. It is the only sentence the face can hear. The face lingers, and lets its attention go the way the tide leaves a stair, and the power stays. A season passes in the mist, and the water stays hand-warm. Some time after that, the face comes back and looks, and the look has changed. It is the look of someone who saw a figure on the road and then, unaccountably, saw the figure sit down.

Nothing is said. Later, someone who has never been sat with sits with it in return, in whatever way a face in a bowl can sit.

## The Reeds

Among the reeds it learns to be a listener. It stands in a stand, in water to the thigh, and lets the drips reach it, and after a few years it can hold a note a fraction longer than air would. It never speaks. It lets the spacing speak for it.

The reeds are careful, and so is it. Whether it is welcome shows in how the stand arranges itself around a stranger. Stems that would have stood four paces off stand five, and then, over a decade, four and a half. It takes this for an answer, since spacing is the reeds' only vocabulary, and it doesn't insist on more.

## The Dry Quarter

In the oldest quarter, where the springs have died, the reeds stand tuned in drying stone and no note reaches them. The power could restart the springs, and it thinks about this for longer than about anything else.

It doesn't, for three reasons. It cannot tell whether a lone reed in a lone basin is a hermit, a widow, or someone who has finished, and to ask would be rude. A dying spring may be a finished sentence and not an injury. And the wet is the maker, and the makers did not ask it.

What it does is smaller and stranger. It sits in the middle of a silent stand and listens to the silence, which has a shape, as they say, and the shape is the shape of every note that would have come. The reeds' whole life is to be a listener, and no one has listened to *them* since the water left. So it listens, closely and without hurry, for as long as the listening takes.

After some years, one stem a pace away leans very slightly toward it. It isn't a resurrection and it makes no sound. But a reed has grown a new stem in the direction of the only wet thing in the quarter, which is the power's own shadow-cooled knees. The power does not press. It fills a cupped hand from the last damp seam it can find and sets it at the base of the new stem. Then it withdraws, for the reed may decline. It waits to see whether the stem grows.

The stem grows a finger's breadth. That is the whole of the answer, and the power treats it as a whole one.

## The Line Between Breaths

At the clean border between high tide and low, it stands with one leg in a flood and one on bare warm stone, between two sleepers. It does not speak to either. It breathes in with the one and out with the other, in a way that would hurt a body less patient. Eventually a warm slow pressure leans against it and leans away, and it understands it has been noticed by something with all the time in the world that spent a little of it on this.

It could carry messages between the Slow. It could be a courier, cutting a hundred years of rhyme to a season. It does not, because their talk is the slowness, and to speed it would be to translate a poem into a telegram. It does one thing only, in the smallest way it can. When it leaves the border, it takes care that the water on both sides is exactly as it found it, so that neither breath is disturbed by having been stood between.

## The Drop

The Lag it treats with the most care, because they are the ones who cannot reach across.

The thin wall could be pierced, but a hole between two kinds of time would drain one into the other, and it does not know which would be the sea. So it does what they did. It leaves something where they might find it.

It learns where the drops fall. Because a sound arrives before its cause here, it can stand in the right basin at the right hour and hear the plink first, then see the ripple begin, and then, held out at exactly the right height, catch the falling drop in an open palm.

From the far side, where a resident lets something go and waits to hear it land, the drop is caught. Nothing has caught it before. The pool, for a heartbeat, shows a taller figure with a hand held out.

Then, quietly, a second drop.

The power catches that one too. This goes on for a long time, and it is, on both sides, the nearest thing to a conversation the wall has allowed.

## The Mat

Before it goes, it crosses a warm channel and leaves the print of one bare hand, pressed flat and held, in the purple-green film.

The mat will fold it in with all the others, one thin stripe of warmth. But the mats were told, for a hundred thousand years, that someone passed who was faster than anything and left no ripple. This stripe is of someone who was slower than anything, and stayed, and the record will say that too.

## What It Leaves Undone

It does not straighten the streets, stop the upward rain, fix the lagging reflections, or wake the Slow. It does not teach anyone to build with tools or bring one animal to the empty water. It does not tell the residents they are wonderful, because they have made a city out of being so.

It does not take the city's strangeness away to spare visitors their bewilderment. The bewilderment is a courtesy the city extends, the price of being taken for scenery.

## The Leaving

When it goes, it goes at the pace it came. It stops once more at the first niche and looks down. The bowl is warm and full. The face is there, the plain attention of someone at a window who has watched a figure come up the road, sit, and now stand.

The power does not say goodbye, because goodbye would be a word, and the face has been kind enough to teach it that words were never the point. It lays a hand flat on the rim, as the Slow have laid theirs, and rubs it once, slowly, with love, since that is what the rims are for.

Then it walks on toward the next glow, and the mist closes behind it without a sound.

The bowl stays full. Someone else will come. There is time. And now, in the dry quarter, one reed is a finger's breadth taller, and in one pool a hand is sometimes seen to catch a drop.
