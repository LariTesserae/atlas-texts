# The Visitor

It came the way weather comes, so the cleft did not register it. The cloud roof did not part, and the tremor in the ground did not change its pitch. Something with more patience than the mountain settled on the north shoulder and looked down into the slot, and the first thing it felt was not pity. It was recognition. Here was a place that was entirely a process, and it had not asked to be saved.

## What it considered, and set down

It had the means to do a great many things, and it thought about each of them honestly.

It could lift the cloud and give the cleft light. But the lid is the heat trap. The green exists because the cloud holds the warmth in, and an open sky would frost the tree ferns to black posts in a week. The dark is not a deprivation here. It is the structure.

It could hold the fissure open, keep the vents pulsing and the ground warm for a thousand years, and make the shift permanent. But the whole economy runs on withdrawal as well as arrival. Peat is what a departed heat leaves behind, and the next flush is built on it. A cleft that never ended would never feed anything beyond itself. It would be a monument, and the plants have never been monuments.

It could fill the place with wings and small scurrying lives, since the emptiness looked at first like a lack. But nothing here is missing. There is no call or cry in the roar because the roar has no room for one. Any voice added would be the visitor's voice, not the cleft's.

It could wake the ferns into thought, give the moss a memory, let the silver columns know they had been standing in their own rain for years. It held this one longest, because it came closest to love. But a fern that knew it was dying when the heat drifted would suffer over something the fern currently meets with nothing but a slow reflex, and would be paying for a gift no one asked for. The cleft is not unhappy. It is busy.

It could lift the iron pipe out of the moss, or repair it, or erase it. It did none of these, and did not touch it, except once.

## What it did

It stayed. This was the first act and the largest, though nothing in the cleft could have known it. It stayed through the gulping, the shudder, and the detonation of the geyser, and through the next eleven or twelve minutes of clank and groan in the hidden conduits, and the next eruption, and the next. Hot grit came down on it and it let the grit fall. It listened until it could tell the ribs in the roar apart, the low stamping of the fumaroles from the metallic groan of the valves in the walls, and it understood that this was a language spoken by no one, and that it was well made anyway.

For as long as anyone had been near the cleft, the only visitors had wanted something from it. The engineers wanted the heat, and they left their pipe and never came back. Nobody had come simply to see. So the visitor saw. It saw the moss go from violent green to olive to gray along the gradient of the leaving heat, and it saw a new vent ringed with sulfur crystals like sugar, and beside it a seedling already taller than the vent would last. It looked at every tenant of the place until it knew them each once, from the black-fibered trunks to the crusts on the sulfur stones.

Then it went to the pipe and laid a hand on the flange, among the ferns growing from the bolts. The iron was warm and hummed like everything else. The whistle went on from its seam, one flute-note in the din, and it was the only tone the cleft had. The visitor did not tell the pipe that it had failed, or that it had not. It only let the pipe be touched by something that knew it was there. Then it took its hand away and left the moss to finish the work.

## The one gift

There was one thing it did that changed anything, and it was small, and it was in the cleft's own grammar.

The plants already move by spores on the steam wind, by seeds falling where a new vent has opened. They change address constantly, but only as far as the wind carries. The fissure will seal one day, and when it does the whole lineage of the cleft, the ferns that learned this noise and the silver felt and the knotted red shrubs, will stand or fail on one ridge, with no other warm ground within reach of any spore.

So the visitor gathered a little from each: a pinch of moss, fern spores from under the fronds, silver seed, red seed, a scrape of lichen, a smear of liverwort film. It carried them a long way, to another mountain where a fissure had opened only that spring. The rock there was black and raw, sharp as broken crockery, with a cloud lid just forming over it and no green at all. It did not plant them carefully or choose the best ground. It set them loose in the steam wind of the new place and let the wind sort them, because choosing winners was not its business. Some would not take. That was the cleft's own arithmetic, and it did not cheat it.

Then it went back, because it wanted to hear the geyser once more.

## What was left

Nothing the cleft would notice. The ferns shook in their own wet weather. The pipe whistled, a little more moss on it than before. A slab dropped from the wall with a crack and a long clatter, and the shrubs below sent up new stems through the gravel.

In a few years the fissure here will seal, the noise will thin to a drip, and a cold, quiet forest will stand on the ridge in the dark.
