# What the Power Did

It came down through the crack, and it took its time.

It was old enough to know that the first thing a great power owes a complete place is doubt about itself. So before it did anything, it listened, for what a mortal would count as a long while, to the tik of the glass, the low tone in the vents, and the slow, unvarying loop of the air.

The place was in balance. The felt regrew at the pace it was eaten. The herds held their inch. The makers kept their rings at the number the herds could spare. Even death was folded in and fed the wall. The power could have found a dozen things to improve, and it noticed that all of them were the wrong kind of thing.

**It could have given them light.** A drop of sun in the dark would have given the makers eyes, and everything they knew would have become a rumor of the dark. Their tools are threaded with the only glimmer they have, and their grammar is built on warmth and wind. Sight would not have been a gift. It would have replaced their world with a poorer one, seen more clearly.

**It could have widened the ring.** More felt, more grazers, more cells, and no child would have to wait for a death. But the ring is not a cage the makers were put in. It is a discipline they chose and keep, and it is the shape of their love for each other, built so that nothing is taken from the wall that the wall cannot give back. To lift it would be to tell them that their oldest act of care was a mistake.

**It could have told them things.** It knew what lay above the crack, what lay under the floor, and how long the red vein would burn. It knew where the balance would someday strain and what would end it. It weighed each of these truths and found that none was cruel, but none was asked for either. A truth that is not asked for is a weight put in someone's hands. It kept them all.

That left very little. The power was wise enough to know that very little is often most of what love can do.

---

## The Form

It chose to arrive as warmth, because warmth is how the makers know anything.

It gathered itself into a patch of heat about the size of a grazer, a little warmer than the floor and a little slower than the air. It let itself be found. It walked, or seemed to, over the glass without the tik, and it let the heat-pits of a hundred passing makers register it as one more slow shape among the herds. Most of them noted it and let it be, as they would a lantern drifting low.

One did not. She stopped at an inch and lifted her palms.

Her name was a row of seven needles, the third tall and the last leaning hard downwind. In the makers' clicks it came out something like *Third-Tall*. She was old. She had walked to the red vein twice. Her forearms were ladders of pale scars, and her palms, once the softest part of her, were going numb from a lifetime of testing edges. The old are kept as long as they can feel the glass, and she could feel it less each season. Her cell was three bodies around an empty place. A plate had been lifted from the ring not long ago, and no one had yet been set in its stead.

She laid one palm flat on the glass beside the warmth and waited for a ring. The power had no weight to ring with, so it did the one dishonest-seeming thing it permitted itself. It let the glass answer as though it had weight, a single clean *tik*, so that she would have something to read.

She read it for a long time. Then she took her palm away and made a click the power did not yet understand, and then the same click leaned differently, and it understood that she had asked *Are you new, or are you again?*

The power thought about this. It had crossed a great many places and had never been asked a better question.

It leaned its answer as best it could, upwind and downwind at once, and it said what was true: *Both. That is what a visitor is.*

She was quiet. Then she made the sound the makers make for something being written down.

---

## The Cell

They did not let it fill the ring, and the power was careful not to want that.

Third-Tall brought it back to her cell, and the two others there held their inch from it, and one of them laid a palm briefly on the glass and drew it away. The empty place in the ring stayed empty. The power sat outside the plates, where the wind reached it first, and it understood that this was correct. It was not the child, and it was not the one who had died. It was something that would be set beside the ring, not in it.

It stayed through many circuits of the air. It learned that a message said downwind is a little sadder than the same message said upwind. It learned that *next* and *again* are one word, and it kept turning that over as if it were a smooth stone. It learned the scars. Third-Tall let it feel, through the glass, the reading of her forearm, the ladder of small cuts that meant *my cell*, *my walks*, *my two sisters, both gone downwind*. It was a slow language, and it was hers, and the power did not translate it into anything else.

---

## The Argument

The long row on the largest salt flat runs to thousands of needles and has no known end. Third-Tall had walked beside it for most of her life, and she took the power there.

Nobody had told it what the argument was. It had not asked. But standing at the end of the row, with the warm wind at its back, it read the spacing and the height and the lean, and something like the shape of the thing came clear, the way a tune comes clear through a wall. The argument was about the loop. Whether the thing that returns is the same thing, or only the same shape. Whether a maker who comes round again is the maker, or a plate set where the maker was. It was the oldest question in the Slack, and every generation had set a shard in answer, and the answer had always led to another question.

The power knew what it could have said. It knew several true things that bore on the matter. It decided that any one of them, put at the end of that row, would be a lid on a pot that was meant to be kept boiling. The row was not an unsolved problem. It was a conversation, and a conversation is one of the things that make a people a people.

So it did not answer. It took a shard from Third-Tall's hand, glass she had struck herself, and set it in the salt at the end of the line. She helped guide its hand. It set the needle a hand's width beyond the last, at a height that meant *I am listening*, and leaning a little into the wind, which meant *and I will come round again*.

The makers who walked past that season read the new shard and stopped. It was neither an answer nor an interruption. They set several more beside it over the following circuits, and the talk resumed.

---

## The Third Walk

In the eleventh circuit Third-Tall said she would walk to the vein again.

The cell was quiet. A maker walks it once or twice in a life, and she had done both. But her palms were failing, and she knew what everyone in the cell knew: that a third walk across the glass, hearing less, was very likely her last, and that she might not read her way back.

The power had asked itself one question over all those circuits: *What would it be to help without taking?* Now it had its answer.

It did not heal her. Her palms had earned their numbness the way the pale lines on her arms had earned theirs, and to restore them would have been to unmake something she had written. It did not extend her years either. She was one of the cell's three, and the ring depends on its number. If she stayed beyond her turn, a child would not be born into her place, and that trade was not the power's to make.

What it did was smaller. It taught the glass to ring a little louder under her palms, along one path only, from her cell to the red vein and back. It asked nothing of the glass except that. The floor stayed as bladed as ever. Off the path it was silent as before, and it would stay so, and after she returned it would forget.

They set out together, or as together as a maker and a warmth can be. She walked on the backs of her hands with her palms lifted, and where she set them down the glass answered her clearly, tik and shiver and long dry ring. She stopped twice and laid both palms flat and listened, and the power watched her face change as the world came clearer than it had in years. Neither of them said anything. The wind was on their necks the whole way.

At the vein the rock was warm as a held hand. To the heat-pits it was the brightest thing there is, a wound of steady fire at the edge of the world. She pressed both palms flat against it and stayed there a long time.

Then, in the clicks, leaned upwind, she asked the power the question she had been keeping: *Will you come round again?*

The power was old and wise and had chosen a great many silences. It chose not to keep this one.

*Yes,* it said.

---

## After

It left when the loop had turned a few more times, without ceremony. There was a warmth by the cell, and then not. Third-Tall laid her palm on the place where it had been, read the glass, and found it ordinary, and that was all right.

The ring was still open. A child was born to the cell not long after. It was set into the ring the way a plate is set, and nothing about the power was in it.

The glass did not dull. The felt regrew exactly as fast as it was eaten. The herds held their inch. The makers' rings held their numbers. The great loop went on turning, and nothing in the Slack had been made better or worse or different. The power had come and had changed nothing that mattered. It had been careful that this was true.

But there is one more shard at the end of the long row. It leans a little downwind, toward the past, and one of the makers who reads it says that this means the past now has a friend. Another says it leans upwind, and that a friend is on its way. They have been arguing about it since.

The power would say they are both right, and it is content to be argued over.

It comes back. It has never said when, because *next* and *again* are one word here, and the one who wears the word has learned that it is all the calendar the place will ever need. It comes as a small warmth beside the ring, an inch from whoever is waiting. It lays no palm down and asks for no plate. It lets itself be counted again, one shard at a time.
