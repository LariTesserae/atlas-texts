# What Was Done in the Level Country

Nothing announced it. The dust near the ceiling of the dark shivered once, and the shiver was too small to travel. A power had turned its attention to the plateau, and the plateau, having no weather, had no way to notice.

## First, It Did Nothing

It knew what it could do. It could have made a sun, or tilted the plateau a degree so that air would begin to move, or set a hot spring against a cold one and let the whole tableland fill with wind, rain, and arrival. Every gradient in the world was within its reach, and it did not use one.

It looked at the country for a long time and saw that this was not a wound. It was a held breath, and the holding was skilled. The Householders had turned the spending of difference into an art, and a life of a few hundred gestures was a life composed as carefully as a poem. The Drinkers took their small warmths with something close to manners. The Walker spaced its visits so that no seam went dark before the next was reached. A sun would have been a flood over all of this. Every tool held in the act of being set down would have been struck out of the hand. The Drinkers would have burned, and the Householders, who keep their bodies at the temperature of the air, would have learned in one afternoon what weather is. A rescue the size of the rescued thing is only another word for erasure.

So it decided that whatever it did would be small, and that first it would learn to be slow.

## The Slowing

It took a body, or something the color of one. It made it tall and narrow, the temperature of the air, and gave it a coat from a peg. It walked into the oldest city at the pace of the Walker and sat in a lit doorway across from a Householder who was, at that moment, halfway through turning toward a shelf.

Then it asked its question, and the question took four years to say. It was not complicated: *Is there anything you want?* But it had to be shaped in gestures, and each gesture cost a season. It opened one hand on its knee. It turned the palm upward by a hair each month. It waited.

The answer took six years and was not what it expected. The Householder did not ask for light, or warmth, or company, or an end. Its head finished turning, its eyes met the doorway, and its long pewter fingers, which had been open for longer than the power had been watching, closed on the cup. The cup did not lift. It was held, and the holding was the answer. It said, as clearly as any sentence: *not yet, and thank you.*

The power understood that this was the whole philosophy of the country. *Not yet* is not refusal. It is a way of keeping the future open by never spending it. The Householders had not lost the ability to want. They had learned to hold wanting at the exact weight at which it does not have to be discharged.

But there was something under the answer. When it had first come into the doorway, the power had been warm despite itself, as a mind that has decided to care is always a little warm, and the Householder had felt that warmth as a pleasure it had no way to return. The Householders had been receiving visitors for ages and answering none of them, because a visitor is gone in an hour and an answer takes a decade.

So the power gave the one gift that fit. It stayed.

It stayed for one Householder's whole life, a few hundred gestures, and it received every one of them. It was there when the cup was lifted and when it was set down. It was there when the head turned, and it turned its own head to match. For the first time in the history of the cities, a guest was answered completely, gesture for gesture, without leaving. When the Householder's long life ended, and it ended the way the rest had gone, by being set down a hair at a time, the power was the only witness who had watched it all at the right speed. Whatever else was true of that death, it had been seen.

It held the pewter hand until the fingers were still, and then it stayed a little longer, because leaving at once would have been a kind of hurry.

## The Walker

It found the Walker at a green seam far from any city, sitting folded like a bundle of reeds, drinking the last cold light.

It did not speak or reach. It walked beside the Walker for one crossing, which took most of an age's worth of hours, and matched each dry click of the ceramic feet. It learned what the travelers had not: the seams were finite. The Walker knew this, and the spacing of its visits was not habit but arithmetic, a long slow calculation of how to make the last green last. The Walker was not lonely, or not only. It was a steward of something that would run out, and it was making the running-out as gentle as it could.

The power thought about making a second Walker and did not. Nothing suggested that the aloneness was a grief and not simply the shape of the thing, and a wise power does not fix a shape it does not understand.

What it did was smaller. There was a rumor in the country that the Walker carried a little heat from one city to the next, warm from one furnace and cold at the next, the only thing that ever moved a difference across the plateau. No one had checked, and the truth was that it carried nothing. The power made the rumor true. It left a stone at the door of the first city, no larger than a fist and the temperature of a body at rest, holding a difference that would take a very long time to spend. It put a seam nearby that would slowly renew, recharging its green by a shade each age, far too slowly to change anything and just fast enough that the arithmetic came out differently.

It changed nothing else. It did not tell the Walker. When the Walker next passed the stone, it stopped for what might have been a decade and then closed one long limb around it and went on, and walking, which had only ever been an activity, became a delivery.

At the next seam the Walker folded down to sit, and it left a space beside it in the green, about the width of a body. The space might have been an accident of folding. The power sat in it anyway. That was enough, or not enough, and it accepted not knowing which.

## The Drinkers and the Cellar

The stone reached the cold end of the plateau in its time. It was laid against a flange in the oldest boiler corridor, and the Drinkers came to it along the pipes, a hand's width at a time, with their legs folding tightly and their mouths pressed to the seam. They drank the stone's small warmth and did not burn, because the power had made it exactly as warm as a leaking flange and no warmer. The count of Drinkers stayed the same. The pipes dulled at the ordinary rate, and beyond them the stone glowed on, a difference too small to be a gift and too steady to be an accident.

Last, the power went down to the deepest cellar, where the pale minerals sweat once in an age and the Sleepers wait in the wall like fossilized moss.

It gathered a bead of water where no bead was due. There was no moisture left in the world to spare, so it made this one from itself: the last warmth of the borrowed body, condensed on the stone and released. It fell.

One of the Sleepers woke. It swelled, darkened, and lived its single day, and the note rose out of the cellar, pure and unwavering, through the bones of the city and out across the plateau, where travelers who hear such things heard it as the edge of a bell. The Householders stopped for it, as far as they can stop. It was the only event they had ever kept a name for, and it had come early.

## The Departure

The power left without leaving anything that looked like leaving. The coat went back on its peg. The doorway stayed lit. The dust settled where it had shivered.

In a red room in the oldest city, another Householder had been lifting a cup for eleven years. When the note came, the fingers closed a hair more, and the cup began at last to rise. It was not faster than before. It was the same slow arc, at the same slow cost. But it was rising toward someone, and the Householder had watched for a decade to learn what that felt like.

In the plaza, the great flywheel turned. A mark on its rim moved past a fixed point, and nothing else moved at all. Yet for the first time in an age, something had happened in the level country. It would take an age to be noticed, and that was the right speed.
