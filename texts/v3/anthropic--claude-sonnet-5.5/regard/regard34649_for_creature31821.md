# The Attention

It came by the ridge road, as everyone does, and it did not come as light.

That was the first decision, and the easiest. Light frightens the mites, and a rinse of ten billion pale bodies sliding off the walls is a poor way to introduce yourself. So it came as what the basin already was: cold, wet, and slow. Its sleeves darkened. Its eyelashes beaded. On the highest terrace, the fog settled on the place where a face would have been, and ran down.

The mites did not flash white. The choristers did not pause. A hauler in the gutter reached the Visitor's foot, stopped, and waited, and the Visitor, who had been many things at many altitudes, stood still and let itself be tested.

It stood there for eleven days. That is longer than the thick phase takes to turn.

---

## What it saw

It saw quickly, because seeing was easy for it. Understanding took the eleven days.

It saw that the walls drank and the mites cleaned the walls, that the choristers sang the standing waves that drove the pumps, and that the eels digested what fell. It saw the Tenders threading between all of this, keeping a very old, very slow animal, and being kept by it. It saw that no part of the system had a reason for the others' existence. Each part simply needed the others, and had needed them for so long that need had become a kind of grammar.

It saw what could be improved, and the list was long, and it read the list the way a doctor reads a chart at a bedside where the patient is happy.

The cold could be lifted. The wet could be turned to something gentler. Mites lived a season, and the Visitor could have made them last a decade. The mountain could be told to rise more politely, the fault to shudder less, and the Listeners, who almost never hear it first, could be given ears that always did.

Each item on the list, when the Visitor looked at what it was attached to, turned out to hold something up.

The cold made the Tenders sleep in pressed rows, so no one slept alone. The short lives of the mites made a child's cup of wet cloth into a lesson about the whole city. The fault made the silence, and the silence made the courtesy, and the courtesy was the nearest thing these people had to a god. If the Visitor took away the shudder, the breath in which a whole basin listens together would go with it, and nothing it could give would be worth that trade.

*They have not been left with problems,* it thought. *They have been left with a medium. Wet is not a problem to solve.*

The city was one long sentence, and the wise thing about a sentence you love is that you do not finish it for the speaker.

---

## What it would not do

It would not speak from the sky. It would not fix the leaning towers, which lean on purpose. It would not warn the Listeners, though it could have, some evening, in the last seconds before a slip, and spared some old Tender's ribs. It knew what warning would do. The pause is early already, and the choristers know before the floor does, and nobody knows how. That is the one mystery the city holds without a keeper. The Visitor decided it would rather be a guest in that mystery than its landlord.

It would not tell them which came first, the city or the animals. It found that it did not know, and was pleased.

---

## What it did

It did three small things, and it did them as a Tender would, with its hands.

**First: the cistern.**

Deep under the fourth terrace, where the drains gather and the sump-eels lie in the dark, a scale was building on the old lattice, a pale mineral crust that grew a hair's width each generation. The Drainwalkers could not reach it. It sat in a chamber beyond their hooked rods and would take two hundred years to close a channel. By then the lower terraces would drown, slowly, over a lifetime, and everyone would be too busy with each drowning street to trace it to the source.

The Visitor went down at the low hour, past the eels, who felt a vibration on the grate and did not mind it. It laid its palm on the crust and asked the crust to become grit. It left the grit in the water, where the haulers would find it and carry it up in mouthfuls to the growing edges, hungry for minerals. Nothing was wasted, and the problem became a meal.

No one was told. The Drainwalkers would find the channel open in a hundred years and credit the eels.

**Second: the stone.**

At a junction on the sixth level, a resonant stone had gone flat. It was a small wrongness, half a tone, the kind a stone-singer would have caught in a season. But a stone that goes flat is a stone that lies, and there was a stair below it in the dark.

The Visitor did not retune it. Retuning is the stone-singer's work, and doing it would have taken something from the stone-singer. It only touched the stone once, lightly, with a bone hammer it had borrowed from the belt of a sleeping Tender. The stone rang, and a woman three levels up, who had not noticed her own hearing sharpen, heard the flatness and went down, grumbling, to fix it. By the time she arrived, she was humming a tone that was very slightly her own.

**Third: the rib.**

At the edge of the eastern terrace, where the lattice extrudes in dark, glistening ribs that take years to harden, the Visitor made a stair.

It was a small stair, five steps, leading nowhere in particular, to a ledge that looked out over the basin and the green-blue tide of the lower streets. It made the stair the way the city makes things: slowly, damp, coaxed. It let the ribs come out soft and dark and leaning a fraction of a degree with the mountain. It gave the stair the faint hum of a tension-line under a load.

And it left one flaw in it, on purpose. The third step was a finger's width too tall. It was the kind of error a child would trip on once and then remember, and a Ribber would find at last and prop and complain about and correct.

A gift that cannot be improved is a verdict. A gift that can be corrected is a conversation. The Visitor wanted to be in the conversation.

---

## The name

On the ninth night it took the small hammer down to the lowest cables, at the hour of the deep drone, and hummed a name.

It had not chosen the name well. It was grand and slow, four notes of great dignity, and it was a mistake. The drone-hangers above did not shift.

It tried again with something humbler, and again they held still. On the third night it stopped trying to be named and simply hummed what it had heard the children hum in the warm rows: three notes rising, one falling. *You are held. The water is moving. Listen.*

Above it, in the dark, a cluster of a thousand choristers, each with a pouch of breath glowing faintly from the mites that clung to it, shifted their weight on the cables. It was slight, a rustle, a settling. It was the answer they gave to a newborn.

The name held. It is not a name the Tenders would recognize, since the Visitor is not among them and does not go by it. But it is a name, and it is hummed, and somewhere in the drone-hangers' long memory, it is kept.

---

## The shudder

On the eleventh day the fault moved.

The Visitor was on the highest terrace when the choristers stopped. It happened before the floor moved, all at once, hundreds of thousands of throats closing in the same instant, and the silence that followed was so large that the Visitor could hear the stone settling under its feet.

It had seen worlds end, and it had seen them begin. It had not often stood inside a silence that was offered as courtesy. It did what the Tenders did. It stopped, mid-stride, and listened with the animals, the walls, and the water.

The terrace below dropped a hand's breadth. Not one street failed. The tension-lines sang a single strained chord and held.

And in the breath after the silence, the Visitor, which could have started the hum and ended the pause a moment sooner, did not. It waited.

A deep voice started up somewhere below, a Listener perhaps, or a hanger, or an old woman in a warm row. The rest joined. The city went on.

The Visitor had known a great many things for a very long time. It had not known what would happen next in a long while. It stood on the terrace with the fog running down its face and found that this was a kind of gift, and that it had been given, not by design, but by the place.

---

## Departure

It left by the ridge road, as everyone does, and no one saw it go.

Behind it the basin hummed and glowed, veined with slow pulses of migrating green light. A child on the eastern terrace found five steps that had not been there before, tripped on the third, said a word she would be scolded for, and sat on the ledge to look out over the basin with her knees drawn up and her small hammer in her lap.

Behind her the mountain was still weeping, still rising, its edges bright with new color, unworn.

The sentence went on. It had one more word in it now, and no one in the city could have said which.
