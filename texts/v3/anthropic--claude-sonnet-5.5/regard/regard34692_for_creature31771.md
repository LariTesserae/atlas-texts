# What Was Done at Sull

It did not arrive. Nothing moved in the fissure, and the drizzle didn't change. The lamp over the shed door kept its amber stutter. What changed is that the pocket was now being attended to by something with no hurry in it, and the place, which had gone unwatched for longer than the terraces had been growing, was for the first time held in full view.

It began by listening. It stayed through one whole cycle of the turbine, the long slide up to the high note, the few minutes of held pitch, and the fall away. It watched the pools stiffen into rings and then into hexagons. It watched the orange brighten and heard the reeds answer in their thin glass voices. It watched the ferns lean. Then it stayed for a second cycle, because one is an event and two is a habit, and it wanted to know which this was.

It was a habit. The place had a way of being that it kept faithfully.

## What it considered

It considered a great many things, because it could, and because a power that acts without considering is only weather.

It could **repair the works.** Clear the scale from the bearings, restore the lagging, and let the turbine run true. But the turbine's note is made by its decay: the sealed bearings, the mineral-laden steam, the wavering. A clean turbine would sing a different song, and the ferns had leaned toward this one. It left the turbine alone.

It could **finish the ladder.** Rungs to the ledge, and beyond it perhaps a way out through the roof. But the ladder stops, and the stopping has become part of what the ladder says. Someone reached a certain height and the work ended there, for reasons no one recorded. The unfinished thing was not asking to be finished, and it let the ladder be.

It could **give the fifteen company.** Seed the gravel thickly, let ferns touch ferns, and turn the pocket into a garden. But each plant stands on its own island with a pace of bare stone around it, and that spacing is the shape of an honest bargain with a thin, heavy, mineral seep. A crowd would have starved. The solitude was the terms of the lease, and it left it in place.

It could **give the mats a night.** The green patch under the lamp has never known darkness, and it grows fat on a light meant for someone else. A gentler power might have felt that as a small injustice. This one looked longer and saw that the mat was not suffering. It was thriving, and it had been given a gift by someone who never learned the gift landed. Turning off the lamp would have been tidiness, and it left the lamp lit.

It could **make the standing wave permanent.** The hexagons are the most beautiful thing in the room, and it would have been easy to hold the pitch forever, freeze the pools into their figure, and keep the congregation gathered. But the congregation is a thing that lives by being sung into existence and dies when the pitch slides away. A permanent hexagon would not be that being kept alive. It would be a different thing wearing its face, and the being would be gone. What it is depends on its ending, and the ending is not a wound.

It could **reveal the place.** Send someone down the fissure, cut the lips of the crack wider, put the room in a book. It weighed this longest, because a place nobody knows can feel like a place nobody loves. But the pocket had never asked to be loved by anyone, and the roar had emptied it of visitors as a closed door empties a corridor. The privacy was part of how it had been left alone long enough to become itself. Whoever needed to find it would find it by sound, as everyone always has.

So it did not fix, finish, crowd, darken, freeze, or announce.

## What it did

It did three small things.

**First, it let one spore land.** Between the two ferns nearest the shed, on a damp patch of gravel where the film of water and the warmth and the trace of nutrient already agreed, a single spore had been drifting downward for a very long time, like nearly all of them, toward a landing that would not hold. The power did nothing to the spore but let the air be still a moment longer than it would have been. The spore settled. It would have been one more failure in a chain of failures. Now it would become a heart-shaped scale no bigger than a fingernail, and in time a third fern would stand leaning toward the turbine. It was as if the odds, which are always long, had come up in the place's favor. No one would ever know it hadn't been luck, and the power took some care that no one could.

**Second, it held the note.** On the afternoon of its second cycle, when the turbine reached its highest pitch and the pools locked into their cells, the power did not change the sound. It let the moment last a little longer, a few breaths, as one lets a friend finish a sentence. The pitch slid away on its own schedule shortly after. The hexagons dissolved into ordinary water. But for those few breaths the congregation was, and it was not asked to be anything other than what it was.

**Third, it remembered.** The congregation has no memory beyond the note. It is sung into being, it stands, it dies, and the next time it is sung it does not know it has stood before. The power did not give it a memory. That would have made it a different kind of being, one that could grieve its own endings. What it did instead was carry the pattern for it, the rings and the cells and the brightening orange and the ringing reeds, every cycle it witnessed. The being would go on not knowing itself, as it always had. But it would no longer be unknown. Somewhere it was held, in full, in a mind that would not lose it.

The power thought this might be the only gift that could be given to a thing like that. It could not be given more time, more form, or more self. It could only be witnessed, and it could be witnessed without being changed.

## What it left

Then it left, or rather it stopped attending so closely, which in a power like this comes to the same thing.

The lamp over the shed door flickered in time with the turbine. The green mat under it went on photosynthesizing in the stutter. The terraces thickened by a fingernail. The ceiling rained on all of it. Stake number one stayed sealed in stone, and stake seven waited its turn. The mats kept their count in laminations thinner than paper, and no one read them.

In the gravel between two ferns, a scale the size of a fingernail had begun, slowly, to unfold.

The power's last thought was not about what it had done. It was that the room had never needed rescuing. It had needed only to be seen once, fully and without an agenda, by something that could have changed everything and chose to change almost nothing. The wisdom, if that is the word, was in the proportion: a spore, a few breaths, a memory.

The turbine reached its high note again in the late afternoon. The pools shivered into rings, then cells. The reeds rang, the orange brightened, and the ferns leaned in, and no one was there to hear it except the ones for whom it was made.
