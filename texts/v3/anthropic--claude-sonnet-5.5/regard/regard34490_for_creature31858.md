# What Answered

It did not come through the crack. Nothing that large has to. It arrived the way weather arrives in a place with no sky, as a change in how the rock was being attended to.

The first thing it did was nothing, and it did nothing for a very long time.

It understood what any newcomer to a chord must understand: the first sound you make is either part of the music or a mistake in it. So it listened until it could hear the shape of the room the way the room heard itself. It listened to the wire of the crickets, the weight of the beetles, the five great throats in their bare stripes, and the sponge-caps, brave and ignorant, ringing like struck glass. It listened for so long that the crickets nearest the place where it was, though they could not see it, drifted their pitch a hair toward it and leaned, the way they lean toward any unfinished voice.

It let them lean. It did not steady them or correct them. It hummed nothing.

## The Weighing

It was asking whether it should be here at all.

The power had seen rooms like this one, and rooms that were much worse. It knew the temptation such rooms offer, which is to look at perfect order and see a cage, or to look at a cage and see perfect order, and then to act on the first look. So it took the objections in turn.

*No one is born here.* But no one is hungry, hunted, or afraid. The cricket that falters is held until it finds itself again, and it is relieved. The power could find no suffering in the room, only a great and patient tenderness that happened to look like a machine.

*No one dies here.* That was harder. The dead were not absent. They were kept, tuned and taken into the room's memory. The full bellies of the lizards were not a horror. They were a kind of grave, or a kind of choir loft. But a room that forgets nothing and wastes nothing is also a room that can be neither surprised nor grieved, and grief and surprise are the two ways a living thing knows that the world contains something other than itself.

So the power did not find a wrong. It found a question, and it found where the question was still being asked.

## The Sixth Voice

It went to the far wall, to the bare stripe nearest the tap-marks, where the lizard lay with its throat glowing a shade warmer than the others.

The power knew at once what this was, and it was neither a lizard nor a ghost. It was a *habit of attention*. A person had sat here for a season and tapped a line of evenly spaced blows into the crystal, and the tapping had been so patient, so loving, and so persistently a question that the room, which wastes nothing, had kept the question. Now it was being sung. Under the lizard's slow slate-colored note lay a rounder one with a breath in it, and the breath was asking, *Can this change? Is there room for one more? May I?*

For all the years since, it had been answered by the only means the room has: *you are already in the chord. Stay.*

That was an answer, but it was not the answer to the question. The room had heard *may I join?* and said yes. What the tapper had actually asked was *may I be heard?* And the room had no way to say yes to that, because the room has no silence to be heard against.

The power understood that this was the whole trouble, and that it was small.

## What It Did

It did not free the lizard, break the crystal, or bring water, and it did not bring dawn or season or teach the crickets to die. It would not have the standing to, and it knew that a gift too large to refuse is a second cage.

Instead it did two things.

**First, it answered.** It took the crystal mallet from the moss where it had lain a very long time. The lashing had not rotted, because nothing rots here. It found the last tap-mark at the end of the line and set its blow one interval beyond it, the same depth, the same weight, the same patience. It was the next stroke in a sentence that had stopped mid-word.

Through the rock came a reply in the sixth voice, so small it might have been a change in the temperature of the stone. There was no word in it, just the round tone with its human breath, rising a hair, as a person's does at the end of a question when someone has finally turned toward them.

The power tapped again. Then it hummed the note back, an interval above it, not to correct it but to keep it company. For the first time in that room's existence, the sixth voice was not the only one that had ever been unfinished.

**Second, it gave the room a rest.**

The power reached into the long, rolling swell of the beetles, the slow beat that is the room's only clock, and at one point in its turn, just once, it took the sound away for the length of a single breath.

It did not silence the room. It made a gap, the way a hand held under a waterfall makes a gap. The crickets' wire went out. The beetles' weight lifted. The five great throats fell away, and the bells of the sponge-caps stopped in mid-ring.

For one breath, the vug was silent.

## The Breath

What happened in that silence cannot be told exactly, because no one was there to hear it, or the only ones there were the ones it happened to.

The crickets did not panic. Their neighbors leaned in, shell against shell, as they always do, but this time the leaning had nothing to correct, and it turned into something else, a listening. The lizards' eyes, heavy and honey-colored, opened all the way, and for the first time in their long watching they saw the room *without* the sound in it: the amber facets, the green belts, the bare floor, all of it standing there, real, unsupported, and beautiful.

In the far stripe, the sixth voice, no longer held up by the chord, was simply a breath. A person's breath, let go.

And somewhere in the garden a single leaf on a gong-fern, after however many years of being replaced by its own exact double, began to brown at the tip. It was not much. It was a small thing, the smallest that could be made, and it was there.

Then the chord came back in. The power had not let it stay away, because that would have been the power's own arrangement, no better than the old one. It came back all at once, from every throat, and it was almost exactly the same.

Almost.

## What It Left

Before it went, the power set the choice down where the room could find it. It did not speak the choice. It made it *hearable*, and it made it so lightly that it could be taken up or left alone.

*The room may keep this rest, or it may forget it.* If it keeps it, the rest will come at the end of the beetles' long swell, once, one breath long, and everything will be the same except that the sound will now have an end it can return from. If the room wishes later to grow that rest, it may. A browning leaf may be allowed to fall. An egg may come before its note. The chord may learn to modulate, slowly, one season at a time, if it ever finds it wants to.

*Or it may choose to be exactly as it was.* The power meant this as sincerely as the other. The crickets are relieved when they are steadied, and that relief is real. It is not a lesser thing than change. A string that repeats one motion perfectly has made something no one else in the world has made. Its stillness is not a failure of time. It is a way of being *with* time.

The power did not pick. It did not want the room to become its own preference. It wanted the room to have a choice.

To the sixth voice, alone, it gave one more thing: the knowledge that the question had been heard. The rest is not a note, but it is a place where a voice can stop, and be, for one breath, only itself. If the tapper wishes to go on being sung, the chord will hold them. If the tapper wishes to end, the rest is there, and the lizard will remain a lizard, a good one, and full.

Then the power was gone, or rather it went back to being what it always had been, which was attention.

## Afterward

The visitor who comes next, squeezing sideways through the crack and giving the sound a hundred breaths to settle, will notice nothing at first. The chord is the chord. The crickets are the wire, the beetles the weight, the lizards the great slow throats, the sponge-caps the bright glass.

But if the visitor stays long enough to hear the beetles' swell come all the way around, they may catch, at the very top of the turn, a place where the room seems to draw one breath and hold it. It is not silence. It is the *shape* of silence, a small hollow in the sound, and the whole vug leans toward it briefly, like a listener toward a friend about to speak.

Then the swell begins again.

On the fern by the far wall, one leaf is a little brown at the tip. No other leaf has taken its place. The crickets on that frond sing very slightly louder, as if to make up for it, and no one steadies them, because they are not wavering. They are learning, gently and for the first time, what it means to keep a note for something that will not last.

The tap-marks in the crystal have a new one at the end of the line. It is the same depth as the others. Beside it, in a hand that could not have been the tapper's, is a second, and a third, and a fourth. They are not answering anything. They are simply company.
