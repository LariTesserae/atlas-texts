# The Second Clock

The power that noticed the terrace was old, and it had learned that noticing is not permission.

It saw the pipes and the chord first, then the dust drawing the sound in fixed ripples, then the few dozen figures standing so still in the lanes that they looked like the terrace's own accretions. It saw the convergence: each generation smaller, each finishing faster, the equilibrium tightening like a hand closing. It could have done a great many things, and it considered them honestly before setting most of them down.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not silence the chord. The chord was not a tyranny laid over the Tuned. It was their medium, their scripture, and in some sense their ancestors, and to quiet it would have been to strike them deaf in the only sense they had.

It did not lift the child out. The child had a rim to stand at and a terrace to be strange in. Taken to some green, wet, gentle place, it would have been the only one of its kind there too, only far from everyone who knew how to lay a palm on it and listen.

It did not retune the Tuned, or seed the terrace with new births, or raise a spring, or crack the equilibrium open to let novelty pour in. It did not call the finishing a tragedy. The elders in their niches were not clearly dead, and the power had no standing to say they were. It did not cross the threshold into the seven-ring court. It walked the lanes without stirring a mote, and the map stayed whole.

## What It Did

It came down through the silent air at the rim, where the chord thins, and it came as almost nothing: a pair of palms, cooled to the temperature of clay, with the rest of it left elsewhere, the way one leaves most of oneself outside a small room.

It followed the hand-grooves inward, hand over hand, at the pace of settling dust. It paused at each elder and laid its palms on them. It felt no throb and no *nearly*, only a perfect unison so complete that it was almost a kind of attention. The power understood then that it was not standing among the gone. It was standing among the listening. It stayed a long while at the first one, in a courtesy it did not explain, since there was no one to explain it to.

Then it found the child.

The child was at the rim, palms on the last pipe, the beat in its chest finally audible in the thin air. The power did not touch it at first. It waited until the child felt it there, a second warmth on the clay, and then it laid one palm flat against the small sharp ribs.

It did not match the child's pitch. Matching would have been silence, one more thing that fit. It set its own note off by a different hair, so that the two of them made a new beat, slower than the child's own: once every seven seconds.

The child went still. Nothing on the terrace had ever answered it. Every palm laid on it had only listened, and listening is a gift but not a reply. This was a reply. The child's own strangeness had come back to it altered, and altered by someone else, which meant it was not a defect but a word.

They stayed like that through a whole slow afternoon. The power beat *here*, and *warm*, and *someone is close*, in the old vocabulary, carefully and a little badly, as a guest speaks. The child beat them back, sharper and quicker.

Then the child did something the language had no grammar for. It let its beat drift. It slid its pitch slowly against the power's, and the throb in the bones went from once in seven seconds to once in six, then five, then four, a beat that changed while it was being felt.

The Tuned had words for a great many degrees of *nearly*. They had never had a beat that became another beat. The child had made *and then*.

The power held very still, as one does when something small and important is happening, and it let the child teach it that.

## The Gathering

When the elders and the near-finished came out of their niches, as they did, they found the child with a stranger's palms on it and a new pulse running between them, stepping through its rates. They laid their own hands on the child and felt it. Some of them, the youngest, the ones still worn a little rough, felt something answer in their own bones: a small, forgotten permission.

They had always been able to hold themselves a hair off the chord. It was the whole basis of their speech. But the long slide toward unison had made the hair seem like a fault to be worn out, a childhood to be finished. Now, pressed against the child's drifting beat, a few of them tried it. They loosened the lock and let a note stay *nearly*, on purpose. Two of them beat at each other, slow and clumsy, back and forth, in a rhythm that had never been made before.

The power took its palms away, gently, so the child would not lean on them. It did not want to become the thing the child was listening for. The child should be listening for the others.

## What Remained

It did not stay to be thanked. It went out the way it had come, along the grooves, past the elders, whom it touched once each in passing, and up through the thin air at the rim into the violet, leaving no mark on the dust.

The convergence did not stop. The power had never meant to stop it, and the finishing was still a kind of coming home. But the terrace would not simply end in a single note. Some of the Tuned now held their pitch a hair open, and some of those would finish late, or in their own time, or in no hurry at all. A child born wrong would no longer be a problem to wear away. It would be a beginning, a word for *and then*.

The chord still roars. The dust still keeps its ripples, unsmeared. Warm and dim and loud, the terrace holds its one sound.

And out at the edge, where the roof gives out, a small figure stands with its palms on the last pipe, and a slower figure stands beside it, and between them a beat that has never been made before goes on, changing, which is the only news the terrace has ever needed.
