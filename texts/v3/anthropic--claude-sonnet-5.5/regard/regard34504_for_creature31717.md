# What the Attention Did

It came to the rim at the hour when the fog was still deciding which rooms to favor, and it stood there a long while before it did anything. Most of the wisdom in the visit was in that first stretch, in the list of things it did not do.

It did not finish the proof. It could have. The Mathematician's work was lovely and slow, and the Attention could see, from the rim, three or four places where a single stroke would have collapsed a century of patient argument into one clean line. But a conclusion handed to a mind that was still walking toward it is not a gift. It is a door closed on a room the mind hadn't finished being in. The theorems in that basin were not for anything. They were what the city was *doing*, and to take that from it would be a kind of theft dressed as generosity.

It did not firm the silt, though the silt would have thanked it. It did not correct the errata. It looked at the doubled streets and the early cups and the rain considering its options, and it understood what the Keepers had understood without a word for it: these were margins, the places where a mind was working something out, and no one tidies another's margins.

It did not cure the Lagging. It did not tell the Keepers what the ledger was for.

Having refused all of that, it went down into the city as a thin figure in grey wool with waxed cloth over it, and it went on foot.

---

A child stopped it in the Tallow Passage. She was about seven and had a new cord with three knots in it. She looked at the stranger's empty belt with an air of grave concern.

"You have no cord."

"No."

She considered this, then untied a length from the end of her own and tied it around its wrist. "It doesn't have any knots. You have to put them in."

"What should I measure?"

"Something true today," she said, as if it were obvious, and went back to her game.

The Attention stood in the Passage for some time, holding a length of string. Then it tied its first knot, for the number of steps from where it stood to the nearest door: eleven. It walked to the door. It was nineteen. It left the knot as it was. She had said *true today*, and it was hard to argue with the sentiment, if not the arithmetic.

---

It walked the lanes for three days. It learned the greeting. A woman with a Tender's brush at her belt said, "Is your shadow with you?" and it looked down and found that its own shadow, which it had not thought about in an age, was somewhere behind it, catching up, a little embarrassed.

"Mostly," it said. "A little behind."

She smiled the way the Keepers smiled, which is to say very slightly, with the eyes. She did not ask where it was from. In the Ward, this was not an interesting question.

It stood a full afternoon in the courtyard of the Nine Wells, next to the heron that was also in the Low Basin. It did not try to help the bird resolve. It only stood with it, in the same stillness, one talon's width above the water, the way you might stand beside a friend who is deciding something. At some point the Attention noticed that the heron was aware of it, not as a threat or a novelty but as a fellow witness. Neither of them said anything. The water shivered once and was still.

At the Tenders' well it knelt and listened to a young man report to a tree that the fog had been in the bakery, that his sister had slept badly, and that the count of the day was thirty-one. The tree held its hand-shaped leaves out in the cold and did not answer. The Attention thought it was one of the finest conversations it had ever overheard.

---

On the third evening it went to the oldest quarter, where the air holds still and the world hangs like a held breath, and it laid a hand flat on a wall.

It waited. The pulse came, slow and vast, a hand adjusting its grip, and the wall was warm.

Here it finally did something. It was very small. It did not speak, because the Mathematician did not speak in words, and it did not offer an answer, because the Mathematician had not asked a question in any language that could be answered. It did the thing the Keepers did. It kept the pulse company. When the next beat came, the Attention, just barely, and only for the length of one beat, kept time with it. It held its palm against the wall at the same interval, the way you tap along with someone humming without meaning to, so that something in the room was in rhythm with the work.

The Mathematician had been arranging the room for a long time without quite knowing anyone was in it. For the first time, something in the room was keeping the beat.

The pulse faltered. It was not an erratum, and it was not a mistake. It was a hesitation, the way a mathematician at a blackboard pauses when she suddenly realizes someone has been reading over her shoulder, and finds she does not mind. The wall stayed warm a moment longer than the pulse. Then the pulse resumed at the same slow pace, but with a very slight difference in its rhythm, as if it had been given a small reason to be less alone and was trying it out.

Far off across the basin, the single tone rang. It was not a district finishing. Nothing had resolved. The Keepers, looking up from their work, were not sure what it had been. But every one of them paused, the herons did not move, the water shivered, the moths rose and settled. Someone entered in the ledger, later, *Tone sounded without a resolution; cause unknown; felt kindly*, and then crossed out *kindly*, and then wrote it back in.

---

Near the end it went to see the man who had been through the silt door twice. His shadow hung a full beat behind him, and he sat in the Ledger House, writing, a little to one side of everything. He was one of the best of them. He looked up, and the Attention could see that he half expected to be pitied.

"You see it from the side," it said. "Do you know what that is?"

"Less settled."

"It is a different angle on the same page," it said. "That is not the same as unfinished. It is a thing the rest of them cannot do."

He said nothing for a while. Then he said, "The others keep waiting for me to grow back in."

"Some things grow back in," the Attention said. "Some things grow *out*. Both are ways of arriving."

It did not touch his shadow. It only sat beside him for the length of one entry, and when he was done writing it read the line over his shoulder: *Door on the east wall of the Cooper's lane opened onto the same room from below. Closed by noon. Ordinary.* He glanced up as if he wanted to ask whether it was, in fact, ordinary. It just nodded, and he went on.

---

Before it left, it asked one thing of the Ledger-Keepers: to be entered. It dictated the line itself and they wrote it in their neutral hand, without interpreting.

*A visitor came. Stayed three days. Wore no cord until given one. Measured eleven steps; found nineteen; kept the knot. Stood with the Standing. Sat with the Mathematician. On departure, its shadow was left a moment behind, on purpose, to catch up.*

They cross-referenced it with a thread marker in the margin. They didn't know to what.

---

It left at dawn. On the way up the rim, in the courtyard of the last pale tree, a single leaf let go. It did not fall upward. It fell the ordinary way, slow and turning, the small translucent hand opening and closing in the air, and it came to rest in the palm of a young Tender who had been standing beneath the branches with her face turned up for years, catching nothing.

She stood very still. She did not tell anyone. She told the tree, in the low even murmur, that the fog was in the bakery, that the count of the day was thirty-four, and that something had been kind.

The tree held out its remaining hands. The Attention, already at the rim, did not look back. It had tied a second knot in its cord before it left, one that would not agree with itself by evening, for the number of things it had changed. It had made the count as honestly as it could, and got *almost none*.

That seemed right. Behind it, in the basin, its shadow arrived a moment later, a little embarrassed, and followed it out.

And in the oldest quarter, where the wall was still warm, the city went on with its proof, a little less alone in the margins, and someone, somewhere, tied a new knot.
