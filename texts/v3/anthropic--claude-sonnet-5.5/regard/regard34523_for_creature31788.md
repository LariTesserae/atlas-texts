# What the Attention Did

It did not arrive as light. A place this old would have ignored light. It arrived as a change in the weight of things. For one evening the beads on the ribbon-grass hung a moment longer before they fell. The drummers, who knock at foxes and at strange tremors, did not drum.

It came down the rim on foot, because the basin is a place understood by walking. It had no need of feet and used them anyway. The descent took half a day, and it let itself be lowered as the slope lowers everyone, from limestone worn like a knuckle to flaking gypsum to clays of rust and olive and violet. Somewhere in the clays it noticed it had stopped intending to arrive and had simply begun to be there.

## What It Considered

It was capable of a great deal, so it began by setting things down.

It could have brought water. A river through the basin would have turned the meadow to marsh within a lifetime and drowned the old cores. It would also have ended the thing the place does best. Nothing here drinks alone, because nothing here has enough. A drop passes through forty bodies before it is spent, and the sharing is the scarcity, worn smooth into a habit. Give the wetters a pond and they become insects near a pond.

It could have given the city a mind. It could have woken the queen, or taught the fluters to keep a record. But the city's memory is in the clay, in strata the wind sometimes opens, and the clay cannot lie or be lost or be argued with. A record can. The city has outlasted every tenant because no tenant ever held it.

It could have lifted the fear from the buttonhole burrowers, who are afraid of nearly everything and with reason. It could have fed the stilt-foxes, who are always slightly hungry. But the burrowers' heads rising from their holes at dusk, like a slow applause, are the shape their fear has taken. The fox's thinness is part of what keeps the burrow-towns from eating the meadow bare. It could see no thread it could pull without another end coming loose in a place it could not see. The suffering was real, and the wise thing was not to pretend otherwise. It also could not find the place where one could reach in and remove only the suffering.

Under all of these was the deeper reason. The Sunk Meadow has no one in charge, and that is the finest thing about it. Whatever the Attention did, it could not become the one in charge.

## The Night

So it stayed and listened.

It stood in the open ground at dusk while the roosts came down. Thousands of birds broke into the leatherleaf groves and every tree became a shrieking, bickering mass. Then the bat hour came in ribbons from the gypsum caves, and the air itched with argument at the edge of hearing. It let the noise go through it. It could have quieted the noise and did not want to. The din was not a fault in the place. It was the sound of a great many small lives that each believed they were the point.

Late, in the lull before the fog, it laid its palm against the wall of an old mound. The thrum answered under its hand, a low steady tone like a ship's hull. For a moment the pitch changed. Somewhere in the walls the drummers had felt something and passed the word through the millions in a few heartbeats. The city considered the matter. Then the tone settled and the city resumed its breathing.

The Attention took that as the only permission it needed. The city had noticed it, weighed it, and gone back to work. It was neither a threat nor an occasion. It was a weather, and the city had survived weather for longer than the word had existed.

## The One Who Is Not Seen

Before dawn it followed the broad splay-toed tracks to the edge of the basin, where the shadowed ground rises toward a low pale ruin of a dead mound and the moss has only just begun.

The one who lives there was digging in the soft hollow core. The Attention did not look at it directly. It sat down at a distance in the manner of a guest who does not want to be a visitor, and it let the other know, in whatever way such things are known, that it meant no purpose and no harm and wanted nothing.

Of everything in the basin, the unseen one alone lives apart from the weave. It has no wetters and no roost. The colonies make room for it and never touch it. The scars of its claws are healed over and built around, as carefully as a plug-head in a wall. It is tolerated the way an old storm is tolerated, and it has been tolerated so long that it may not remember being anything else.

The Attention thought about giving it company, or a name, or being seen. It thought of these and set them down. What it did was smaller. It stayed till the fog began, and it did not go first. Two presences shared a dark place, and neither asked the other what it was. Toward morning the digging slowed, and something large and old lay down in the warm ruin. It slept in a way that might have been the first sleep it had ever taken with another creature nearby. The Attention could not say. It would not have asked.

## The One Thing It Did

There was one thing, and it was not for the meadow's present.

The whole basin hangs on a single thread: the cold air that sinks at night and gives up its wet at dawn. The Attention could feel, far off, past the rim and past the years, that the country above was drying. Over many centuries the pearl-gray overcast would thin and the nights would warm by a degree or two. The cold would stop pooling, the fog would come thinner, and the moss would go gray. The green would be gone within a few seasons of the last good fog, and the cities would stand empty, still shaped like breathing.

The basin could not adapt to this. It had no time and no way. Every creature in it holds the same slow breath, and none of them can hold a different one.

So on its way out, at the windward shoulder of the rim, it did the smallest thing that would matter. It raised the pale limestone by one hand's breadth along a stretch a day's walk long. It did not build or carve. It hurried a swell the wind and frost would eventually have rounded on their own, and it did so only slightly, a fraction of what a mountain would want. The cold air will spill into the basin more readily now and will settle a little more deeply and stay a little longer. It will not stop the drying. It will hold the fog a few centuries past the day the fog would have gone.

That was all. There was nothing to see. By spring lichen was already on the new stone, and in a few years it looked as old as everything around it. Nobody in the basin will ever know, and the Attention counted that among the point of it. A gift that could be traced would become a debt, and the meadow would have learned to look up.

## The Wall

One more thing happened, and the Attention was honest with itself about it.

Passing the flank of an old mound on its way out, it found in the wall a plug-head, dried in place, cork-headed, wedged into a breach from some season long gone. The fluters had built over it. Only the pale rounded shape of the head showed through the fresh clay, like a coin in a wall.

It stopped. No one in the city grieves. The city has been through this hundreds of times and needs nothing from anyone. So it was not a gift and it did not change anything. It stood a moment and marked the small dry body that had held the wall until it could not. It did this for its own sake, and it knew the difference, and it did not pretend to the meadow or to itself that it was otherwise.

Then it went on.

## Departure

It left at first light by the same shoulder it had come down. The leaving took half a day. The fog was already lifting off the moss, and in the groves the wetters were climbing back into their walls, swollen and translucent, carrying water into the dark. It took none of that with it. On the way up, the clay turned to gypsum, and the gypsum to limestone worn smooth as a knuckle, until it stood at the rim and looked once more across the pale bowl, the low hazy line on the far side, and the grass combed in one direction.

Behind it, a lawnsheep raised each hoof high and stepped around the place where it had stood, the way they do around the files of workers, and no one had taught them to.

The first bird tried one note. The roar began to build again from nothing.

The Attention judged that the basin had been finished a long time ago, in the sense that matters. It needed nothing added. It had needed only one thing kept safe, and it wanted to be left as it was: loud, small, communal, patient, and quietly beginning again.
