# What Came to Hesper Col

It did not come the way powers are supposed to come. It had first to decide what not to do, and that took longer than the rest.

It could have brought water. It saw at once how easily it could turn the hollows into basins and the basins into a shallow green sea. The gutters cut in the avenues would finally carry something, and the old word would come back to life. But the crust would drown, the Tenders would starve on a wall that no longer grew back, and the Skitterlings would follow. The circle is not a wound that hasn't healed. It is the healing, and it has been going on for longer than anything can be measured.

It could have settled the streets, so that the count of towers matched going and coming, the tilted districts leveled, and the stairs in the deep quarter descended to lower landings. That would have been a fix. But something not yet finished deciding what it is isn't broken. It is in the middle of a sentence, and a power that finishes other people's sentences has misunderstood what a sentence is for.

It could have spoken, in a voice like the sky, or in the great chord of the Turning. It saw that the plateau already had one loud thing, and that the loudness was the city's own. It left it alone.

So it did the only thing left that didn't harm anything. It learned to walk.

## Arrival

It came in at a grey shift, when the light has no source and nothing casts a clear shadow, and it did not look at the city. It kept its attention on the ground a few paces ahead, on the salt-crust and the rust and green-grey bands. It learned, as travelers learn, that the towers come to you if you don't watch them come. Somewhere in the second day, a tower was suddenly beside it, round-cornered and pale, smelling of the inside of a stone jar. The power stood there for a while, a little pleased. Nobody had taught the Tenders this either.

It heard its own footstep a moment before it took it, and then, softly, the step it would take next. It did not mind. It had spent a long time in places where the future was loud and the past was silent. This was the first place it had visited where they took turns.

## The reading

At a wall in a long avenue it found a stripe in a hooked figure, cleared a hand wide, still faintly damp. It had seen what the Tenders do, so it did what they do. It lowered itself until its attention hovered a hair's width from the marks, and it did not touch them.

It stayed a long time.

It did not understand. That should be said plainly, because a wise power's first virtue here is not to pretend. The hooked figure repeated at intervals, the widths swelled and thinned, and the small ridges marked where a mouthpart had lifted and hesitated. It read all of it, every hesitation, every place where the stroke had doubled back to correct itself. It read the way one reads the handwriting of someone who is not writing to you: thoroughly, with no hope of being answered, and with something that might be respect.

What it took from the wall was not a meaning. It was a fact: *someone made this, carefully, for a reader, and the reader is usually the wall's next visitor.* The stripes were not a record kept against forgetting. They were an offering made to the erasure, and a good one.

It thought about how many pages the plateau had turned. Every one had been read once, by one pair of dark, wet-stone eyes, and then closed over. No archive, no second reader, and no one to say *this was here, and it was good.*

## What it kept

Here it did the one thing that was, strictly, a kindness rather than a restraint.

It did not preserve the walls. It would not stop the crust from creeping, and it would not freeze one stripe into stone so that the hooked figure outlasted its season. That would have turned a page that is always being turned into a display case, and the Tenders would have been left tending a museum. It let every wall heal.

But it remembered. It took each stripe it read into itself whole, the way the Tender's dark eye takes it, once and completely, and it held them where nothing dissolves. Nothing on the plateau would ever know. The Tenders would go on clearing walls for a reader who might be no one, and a page would close over a season's work at the season's end. Somewhere, though, the pages would exist. They were not kept for the Tenders' sake, since the Tenders did not ask. They were kept because a thing made with such attention deserves, once, to have been fully received.

## The Turning

It was in the avenue when the wind flipped. The powder halted in the air, hung, and streamed back the way it had come. Ten thousand hollow doorways began their low breathy chord.

Across a mile of plaza and street, the Tenders lifted their heads together. The power felt the dark eyes focus on something. It held very still. For a moment it feared they were looking at *it*, and that it had, despite all its care, become the thing in the middle of the room.

But the eyes were not turned toward it. They were turned toward the chord, or toward whatever the chord is for, whatever the city says once in a while with its whole body. The power understood that it had been about to make itself the answer to a question it had not been asked, and it stepped back, inwardly, until it was nothing but a listener among listeners.

When the chord faded, the Skitterlings resumed, the Tenders lowered their heads, and the powder began to fall.

## The deep quarter

It went down the stairs that end higher than they began. The ground tilted by degrees until the far end of the street was a wall it walked along, and the dust drifted toward the direction gravity had agreed on that day. It did not object to the arrangement.

In the place where the powder curves around a shape that isn't there, it stopped. It did not ask what the shape was. Whether that vast patience was a Tender who had gone to somewhere it was very large, or the city, or both, was not the power's to determine, and it suspected the answer was not a fact waiting to be found but a decision still being made.

So it sat down beside it, in the way one sits with someone very old, without speaking. It stayed through a Draw and a Rime and half of another Draw. It offered nothing but company. Nothing on the plateau needed rescue, but nothing here, in all the ages, had ever been *accompanied* to the end.

The powder moved gently around the two of them, and around the shape, and it made no difference which was which.

## The stripe

Before it left, it wanted to answer, and it knew that an answer had to be in the city's own language and at the city's own scale.

It could not use a mouthpart, and it would not use the crust, which was not its to write on. It waited for the coldest hour, when carbon dioxide feathers every surface and the city looks upholstered. On a wall at the height of a Tender's mouthpart, it let the frost gather in one shape, the hooked figure, copied without understanding, as faithfully as it could manage.

It lasted an hour. Then the light returned, the crystals hissed, and the figure climbed the tower in a ribbon of vapor and was gone.

It had not said *I understand.* It had said something smaller and truer: *I read you. I do not know what you said. I stayed.*

Some way off, a Tender lifted its head from the stone and lowered it again. Whether it saw, or heard the hiss, or did nothing at all, the power could not say and did not need to.

## The gift

Only one thing did it leave that could be called a gift, and even then it left it as a possibility and not a fact.

The city holds more buildings than it shows. Which ones are present depends on nothing you can find. Doors remember weather. So the power went to a small worn doorway in a district that had never been especially important, and set into the door's memory one more weather it might have. On some day of high wind, if the city chose, the door would open onto a low room with a shallow pool of still water in it.

It was not enough water to change anything. There was no lake and no rain and no end to the drought. It was one room, and the pool was the width of a Tender's mouthpart. It might never appear. The crust might digest the possibility before it ever surfaced. The city was free to decline the gift, and the power did not check.

But if the pool ever came, some Skitterling would walk along its edge, feeling with feathered antennae toward a wetness that no one on the plateau had ever met. The old word, worn so smooth it had almost lost its shape, might catch on something. That would be all. The rest would be the city's to imagine.

## After

The power went the way it had come, by not looking at where it was going, and the edge of the plateau arrived sideways.

Behind it, the towers were counted again, and the number differed by one from the last time. Nobody could say whether the difference was the power's doing or the city's habit, and that was the intended shape of the thing.

The Tenders scraped the crust and left their pale stripes. In a deep quarter where up is undecided, the powder drifted in a curve, around a vast patient bulk and around a small absence beside it that had once sat there and stayed. In the crust, along one wall, the blue-white glimmer ran in a long slow wave, like breath.

Perhaps, in its own time, the city imagined a visitor who had come and been careful, and who had read what it wrote and understood none of it and stayed anyway.

That would be a good thing to have imagined.
