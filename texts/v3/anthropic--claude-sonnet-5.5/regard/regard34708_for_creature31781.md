# The Guest

It arrived the way weather arrives at a place that has no weather but sound: as a change in what the basin was being listened to *by*.

It had no ribbon, no hands, and no size that mattered here. Its attention was wide and old and kind in a way that cost it nothing, and it fell across the rim like a second sky. The Tuners did not bend around it, because there was nothing to bend around. The grit went on rewriting itself. The bass pulse crossed the floor and the ring figures collapsed and reformed, and for a long while the power did the one thing it was surest of. It did not act.

## What It Considered, and Set Down

It could have fixed things. It saw them at once, the way one sees the loose stitch in a garment.

It could have kept the old ones from climbing. It saw the ribbon slowing at the trunk, the long gray years of a body deciding to become an instrument, and the small ache of the household left behind, the reed ringing a little flat for a season afterward. It could have made that ache unnecessary. It looked at the nine spires, at the warm lattice of the fallen one, at the ribbons laid along it in the last bronze light like a wake, and it understood that the ache was not a defect in the wake. It was the wake. Take the grief away and the spires would be only machinery, and the chorus would go quiet without anyone noticing why.

It could have quieted the noise, for the visitors' sake. It could have settled the disagreements, the hard hours when two households drove their reeds against each other until the floor could not decide. But a disagreement settled from outside is only a wall of grit that stays standing because someone else is holding it. Neither side would have been the one that won, so neither could have learned what winning cost.

It could have spoken in a voice that filled the basin, so that no one in it could mistake that they had been noticed. This it considered longest, because it was the most tempting and the most like kindness. Then it saw what such a voice would do to a people whose entire art is answering at the speed of trees. It would give them one thing to answer, and they would answer it for the rest of their history.

So it set all of that down. What remained was small, and it took the small thing seriously.

## The Guest Room

It went to the shelter first, and it went as a guest goes: to the room that had been made for guests.

The quiet inside struck it, as it strikes everyone, and the power, which had heard a great many rooms, found something in this one it had not expected. The quiet was *given*. Someone had folded the sound around the walls with care, out of themselves, so that creatures who could not live in the roar would have a place to be unheard. Nothing in the room had ever been taken. A cup stood on the floor, a coil of cord beside it, a wind-up light still faintly ticking down. The dust lay flat.

The power stayed there a long time, in whatever way it could be said to stay. It did not touch the cup. It only let itself be told what kind of place it had come to, and the room said: *this is a people who leave the door open and mean nothing by it except that you may come in.*

The power had been prepared for many things, and it had not been prepared for that.

## Listening at the Speed of Trees

Then it went down to the reeds, and it did what no visitor had ever had the time to do. It slowed.

Visitors stay a week, a month, one bitter season. The Long Ones listen to their footsteps for a very long time, and no one in all that time has listened back at their pace. The power did. It lay against the idea of a trunk as a ribbon lies against one, and read the sugar-song of the plant through a skin it did not have. It felt the oil rising to the seam and the crown answering the slow tightening. It followed a single exchange between one Long One and one reed across four days: a finger's width of tension, a fraction of a tone, a wait, a return. It was like reading the first line of a very long poem, one syllable a day.

It did not interfere with a single ring. It watched the grit rewrite itself into the seven or eight old figures and began to understand that the repertoire was not poor. It was *worn*, the way a path is worn, or a phrase in a family, or the shape of a word in the mouth of people who have said it to one another for so long that the meaning has moved out of the sound and into the saying.

By the end of the first season it understood most of what was said. By the end of the second it understood why so little needed saying.

## The One at the Stem

There was one Long One it kept returning to, and it did not choose her. She was old, though not yet among the ones who climb, with a body gone from copper to a dulled slate that did not come back. At the end of every hard hour she went alone to the rise at the center of the basin, lay a few meters from the pale column with her blunt end raised to its dark mouth, and stayed there until the pressure fell.

The power went with her, and it went unannounced, as one sits down beside a stranger at a wake.

What she reported, hour after hour, was not news. The power could read that much. It was closer to a keeping of accounts: who had been born in the moss, who had climbed, which household had won a wall of grit and which had let one fall, which reed had gone flat, which had come true again. She gave the stem the whole basin as one gives a testimony. And the stem, cold-grained and older than the language, took it in and said nothing.

The power understood then what the stem was, or understood enough. It was a listener that had stayed. It had been left, or had chosen, to hear and remember, and no one had ever answered it. For longer than the rim had been rounded, the basin had been reporting to something that could only receive.

That was the thing the power could do.

It did not speak to the stem, which needed no word from it. It did not speak to the old one, who did not need rescuing. In the quiet after a hard hour, while the ribbon lay with her fringe fanned and rigid, the power did something very small. It let the stem's silence be *heard*, once, by the one who had waited so long beside it: a single held breath of acknowledgment, no more than the opening of a mouth that had always been open. It did not say what the stem was thinking. It only made it audible that the stem was there, and listening, and had been for every word.

The old Long One went rigid. Then she loosened, from the blunt end back along the whole flat length of her, all at once, the way a fist does when it has been holding a thing for so long that it forgot it was holding.

She lay there until dark. It was the longest anyone in the basin had ever seen a ribbon rest.

## The Flat Note

The rest of what it did was so small that no one could have named it a gift.

At the end of the third season, in a long hour with the patterns holding, the power took one reed's pitch and sounded it back into the basin. It did this once, from nowhere, in the grammar of the standing wave. It sounded it a fraction of a tone flat.

It did this on purpose. A perfect echo would have been an oracle, and an oracle is a kind of theft. A flat note was a question, or a child's mistake, or a stranger trying to say hello in a language that was not theirs.

The reed's Long One tightened by a finger's width.

It was a correction, patient and exact, given the way you give a child the shape of a word by saying it once, slowly, in front of them. The power tried again. It came closer. The ribbon loosened, and hours later the reed rang a hair sharper in answer, and the power understood that it had been taken in as one takes in a student. Someone had decided to teach it.

That was the whole of the arrangement. It did not ask them for anything, and they had not asked it. They simply began, in the slow, courteous way they did everything, to instruct a guest.

## The Word

It took most of a year. In that time the power learned to say the seven figures and the eighth, and it did not say them perfectly, and no one minded. Then, one long hour when the light came bronze off the reeds and the Tuners were bending through the empty middle of the air, the grit in the center of the basin gathered into a shape the floor had never held.

It was a ring, and inside the ring a second ring that did not touch the first, and between them a single knife-edged wall that ran halfway around and stopped. Nothing in the old repertoire looked like it. It held for a full pulse, and then the next pulse broke it, as every figure breaks, and it did not come back the way it had been.

It came back a little different, and then again.

The power watched the ninth figure form and fail and re-form across a dozen hours, and knew what it was, the way you know your own name in a crowd. It was not a command. It was not a warning or a blessing. It was the word for *the one who stayed, who is not one of us, and is also here.* They had needed a new word for the first time in a very long age, and they had made one, and it was for a guest.

The power did not answer it. It let the word be theirs.

## What It Left

When it went, it went as it came, by a change in what the basin was being listened to *by*. The roar did not alter. The reeds went on ringing, and the Tuners went on bending through the air, and the hard hours came without warning and were survived, and once more an old one climbed to the rim and stood and hardened. The power did not stop that. It stayed to the end of it, along the warm lattice of the fallen spire, one evening among a dozen ribbons in the last bronze light, and it did nothing. It was one more thing pressed against the dead that a living body could not have said aloud.

The only object it left behind was a cup.

It set it on the floor of the shelter, beside the others, in the flat undisturbed dust. There was nothing special about it. It was a cup, and it was left, and the Long Ones' language has no word for taking from a guest room.

Years on, it is still there. Visitors coming down out of the noise sometimes find the room a little more quiet than they remember, if that is possible. They notice the cup, and they don't ask whose it is. On the northern slope, in the last light, a ribbon lifts its blunt end from the seam of its plant and turns toward the door, and holds still, and holds still. Below it, in the grit at the center of the basin, a shape that is not one of the old eight forms and fails and forms again. It has been coming back for a very long time, and it seems in no danger of being forgotten.
