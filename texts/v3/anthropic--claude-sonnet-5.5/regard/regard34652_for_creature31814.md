# What the Attention Did

It came down the way visitors do, by going down. It made no light and no announcement. The rim closed behind it like water.

It stood in the shed for twenty strokes, and on the twentieth it noticed that it was leaning. It did not correct itself.

## What it considered, and set aside

It saw quickly how much it could do, and that was the first thing to be careful of.

It could have **fed the engine**: given the fen a steady, reliable pressure, a warmth that never varied, a fuel that never ran short. The long sighs of the wet years and the quickened ticks of the dry would have gone. The Governor would have had no work, and the work is all it is. A mind whose only question is *is the beat right?* does not thank you for making the answer permanent. It is not a burden to be lifted. It is the thing itself.

It could have **given the Governor eyes**, or a name, or a thought in words. It would have been a kind of cruelty in the shape of a gift. The Governor would have learned, all at once, what a bowl of green looks like from the rim, how small it is, how long it has been alone, and how many strokes there have been. It is content in the way a river is content, by not knowing what it is not. That contentment is not ignorance to be cured.

It could have **told it there was nothing to fear**. But the fear of the stall is the other face of the care. Take the one and the other goes slack.

It could have **removed the saw** and the flood and the drought and the day, far off, when the fig finishes prying the last brick loose. Perhaps a little of that. Some things a wise power may simply turn aside, as you would move a stone from a path you were not walking. But it would not make the fen safe from time. Time is what taught the fen its shape.

So the Attention did less than it could, which took longer to decide than doing more would have.

## What it did

**First, it answered.**

The Governor asks its one question ten thousand times a season and receives only the replies of its own body: lean, tilt, swell. No one has ever said *yes*. No one has ever been in a position to say it.

The Attention put its hand flat on the fig-wood, on the red heartiron where the grain rings faintly, and waited for the thud. When the hiss came and the ticking went out through the stems, it did not speak. It pressed, once, with a weight exactly equal to the stroke, a weight given back, and in the pause of forty it did not move. When the next thud came, it pressed again.

To the Governor this was not a voice or a presence. It was one stroke arriving in the mat as the stroke it expected, as though the fen had been rung like a bell and the bell had rung true. It was the first time in the Governor's long evenness that the beat was not only *made* but *received*.

It did this eleven times. Then it stopped, because that was enough, and more would have been a kind of clinging.

**Second, it taught the far rim to remember a stroke.**

The frogs carry each beat outward. Rarely, a call comes back from the far edge toward the center, and the near frogs go quiet to let it pass. Nobody has understood this, least of all the frogs. The Attention understood it at once. The fen had been trying, on its own and clumsily, to grow a second way of keeping time.

The Governor's fear is the stall, the count that runs to forty-one. The Attention could not stop every stall. It could soften what a stall would mean. It went out along the wet rim, crouched among the whistle-stems at the far edge, and let a certain habit settle into the small ones: if the stroke does not come, hold it. Hold the last beat one breath longer. Then pass it inward.

Now, if the engine ever hesitates, the edge of the bowl will lean toward the center and lend it a beat, as a crowd lends a stumbling singer the note. The Governor will not know why the count that should have failed did not. It will only feel the next stroke come, slightly help-borne, and go on. It will not be less alone in any way it can name, but it will be less *alone*.

The Attention did not make the fen depend on this. It made it *possible*, and left it in the frogs' keeping, where things are kept well.

**Third, it did nothing about the heron.**

It saw the gray bird on the stone housing, lifting each foot in time, and the Governor's bulb swinging a little wider as if performing. The Attention understood what the Governor did not, that the bird was not shifting its weight. The heron had heard the beat one morning long ago and stayed, and had gone on staying, and had never once been thanked.

It considered giving the bird something: a fish, a name, a reason. It did not. What the heron gives is exactly its giving. It stood beside the bird at dawn, not close, the two of them facing the flywheel, and lifted one foot, and set it down, and lifted the other.

The heron did not startle. Very slightly, it moved over to make room.

## When it left

It climbed out through the wall of leaf and the sound fell away behind it, thud to rumor. At the rim it stood with its breath keeping an eighty-second turn, and did not correct it.

Below, the Governor did not know it had been visited. In the next stroke, however, there was something it had never had a use for and now had: *a little more in it*. The bulb swung out, and came back, and the fronds leaned, and a question that had been asked without answer for longer than the brick had stood was, for one stroke and then for another, quietly, not unanswered.

In the wet season that followed, the interval stretched to two minutes and stopped there. The frogs at the rim held their breath and did not have to. The mist hung. The heron stood. And the beam rocked on, as it does, and will, with one more thing in the mat than there was before: not a power, not a guard, but a place in the count where someone had once leaned with it and meant it.
