# What Came to the Drum

It noticed the chamber the way you notice a held breath in a quiet house. Nothing called to it. The place was simply arranged so unusually around a single listening that attention gathered there, and stayed.

It came without arriving. There was no light and no voice, and the ice did not change. For the first eleven seconds it did nothing. Then it did nothing for eleven more.

## What it did not do

It considered the mercies available to it. It was very old, and it had learned to be suspicious of mercy that arrives quickly.

It could have quieted the room. It saw at once that this would be a kind of murder. The pillars are grown by the noise, and the eels read the noise as a finger reads a string. The Sitter's whole art is finding stillness inside a din. Take the din away and there is no art, only a silence with nothing in it.

It could have lit the water, or brought the Sitter up to a world of open sky, or given it a larger mind so it could be told *you are not alone*. But a gift like that arrives with a verdict on the life before it, and the verdict would be false. The Sitter was not waiting to be rescued. It was tending something.

It could have mended the bell, refreshed its air, and set the flute back in a hand. It could have told the Sitter what became of the people who braided the tethers. It did not know that the Sitter wanted these things. It knew that the tally-keeper had wanted to be left as they were, a slow pattern in the rock, listened to.

It could have stopped the ending. The slab that will one day let go, the drain or the freezing or the filling with stone: all of it was within reach. But *nothing is finished* was not a defect in the place. It was the place. A room that could not end would not be this room.

So most of what it might have done, it left undone. Restraint was the first thing it offered, and the largest.

## What it did

It took a small shape for the purpose, something like a weight of attention with edges. It went down the bore hole past the frayed tethers, and it did not touch them. It passed the bell on its ledge and did not touch that either. It found a node on a lower terrace, one of the Sitter's, deep and steady, with a cairn of four flat stones. It settled beside it and put what served as its head against the rock.

The noise was enormous, and it let the noise be enormous. It let the vents tear open and the ice whip-crack. It felt the concussion of a falling slab in every part of itself. It listened as the ghost-eels listen, with its whole length, until it could feel the differences between one flute and the next. This took what a person would call a season.

In that time it counted. It had no reason to, except that someone had once counted here and the count had been left unfinished. On the tally stone the last group holds nine marks, not eleven. The keeper stopped two short. The power did not add them. That group was the keeper's, and its incompleteness was part of the record, as true as the rest. But it counted the pulse, beat after beat, group after group, silently, in the still place, so that for a while the Sitter was not the only one in the chamber breathing in time with the drum.

The Sitter came on a day when the map had just been rewritten. A slab had fallen in the east, and the floor had shifted, and the old nodes were suspect. It walked the terrace and pressed its wide head to the stone. It found the power there, beside the cairn, and did not startle. It rested its head an arm's length away and stayed for three days. Neither did anything, and both listened. Neither could have said what passed between them, but the listening was mutual and the chamber was slightly less alone.

## The one stone

On the fourth day the power did a single thing. It lifted a flat stone the size of a hand, one the Sitter had not chosen, and set it on the cairn. It did so clumsily, from outside the art, and it knew the placement was slightly wrong. It let the stone be wrong on purpose.

The Sitter lowered its head to the rock. It listened for a long time. Then it rose, took the stone in the ridged tips of its fins, and moved it a hand's width to the left. It set it down and listened again. Then it settled back, and the quiet at the node deepened.

That was all the answer there was. It was correction, and it was also acceptance, the way you accept a colleague's work by adjusting it. The power understood that this was the only thanks it wanted, and the only gift it could receive: to be a small part of the tuning, and to be tuned.

## What it left behind

Before it withdrew, the power made one provision, so slight that no one would ever see it as anything but the mountain's own doing.

When the room ends, and it will, the ending should not be a catastrophe for the small blind things in the flutes and the pale creature that presses its face to the stone. The rock will fracture where rock fractures. In the deep, in the warm fissures that no light has entered, there is a place where the fracture might run one way or another, and it is neither a large nor a strange thing for it to run the way that leaves a passage open. When the water drains, or the ice comes down, there will be a way for something slow and heavy to follow the warmth into the dark.

It did not tell the Sitter, because the Sitter would only have felt it as a change in the stone. The Sitter is good at that already.

It left the shaft of light alone. It left the wind alone, and the moment when the beam falls on the one pillar and every ghost-eel turns. That grace had never needed help, and to arrange it would have been to spoil it.

## After

The power withdrew the way it had come. The chamber went on. The vents tore open, the ice cracked, the lantern shrimp flared away from the sound and murmured afterward like people after a bell. The filaments bent together on the eleventh second and straightened in their slow ripple.

At the node on the lower terrace, the cairn stands with one stone slightly left of where a stranger put it. The ghost-eels have gathered around it, and nothing else about it has changed.

Under the snowfield, once every eleven seconds, the drum beats for anyone who is listening. The Sitter breathes with it. The tally stone waits with its last group two marks short.
