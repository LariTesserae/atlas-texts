# What the Attention Did

It did not descend. Nothing so vertical happened. The best description is that the valley became *held in regard*, the way a room changes when someone who loves you walks into it without speaking.

The first thing to notice it was a gecko on the flank of a tower. It stopped mid-stride, one foot lifted, and stayed that way for a long time. Then the weavers, the frogs, and the spiders felt it in the silk, and the hush went out in its ring, as it does for a leopard. But a leopard's hush spreads across the valley floor. This one rose. It climbed through the roof and out over the ridges, and for a few seconds the valley was quiet in a direction it had never been quiet before.

Then the noise closed over it, and the Attention stayed.

## What it considered

It was old, and wise in the way that means it had been wrong often enough to become careful. So it did not begin with action. It began with a list of things it could do, and it went through the list slowly, the way you turn over stones.

*It could give the valley a face.* The Listener had no center, and a power could supply one: a single awareness, a voice, someone to say *I*. The Attention held this possibility for a while and saw where it led. A mind with a center learns to prefer. It prefers the leopard gone, or the frogs louder, or the roof unbroken. It learns fear, because a center has something to lose. The valley's sense of itself was good precisely because it was smeared across ten million bodies and belonged to none of them. A hush from a wing-shadow, a repair by whoever was nearest, a memory kept in scar tissue: that was a way of thinking with no one in charge of it. Concentrating it would be like taking a river and making it a well.

*It could mend the fragilities.* The Attention saw them all at once. The roof was growing heavier every decade, and one day a great tree would fail under it and take a hectare of silk down in a slow, terrible collapse. Fire would come over the ridge in a dry year. The queens would die, one by one, and towers would go to sleep. Some winter, or some drought, the frogs would fall silent in whole districts.

It found it could hold these facts without flinching, and it understood that this was most of what wisdom is. The valley was not a thing that had to be preserved against change. It was a thing made *of* change. The fallen tree opens the shaft of light, and the light makes the season of green, and the spiders close the gap. Take away the failures and you take away the breathing. The Attention left every one of them where they lay.

*It could clear away the leopard, the snake, the hawk.* It didn't even finish that thought. It had watched the hush travel outward too many times to mistake fear for a flaw. The whole valley's attentiveness was made of predators.

*It could reveal itself.* Here it lingered longest, because this one was tender. It could speak into the drum of the roof. It could say *you are seen, and you are good.* But it was not certain the valley was lonely. Loneliness is a thing a center has. And it suspected that a voice from outside, however kind, would become the loudest thing in the valley, and the valley would spend a generation arranging itself around it.

So it set that aside too.

## What it did

It listened.

This was the whole of the first act, and it lasted through one full turn of the rains. The Attention learned the layers: the thrum in the earth, the wall of frogs stacked in their hundred pitches, the cicada waves, the sharp bursts of the birds. It learned that the frogs leave each other gaps like players in a score. It learned the drip after rain, a second music under the first, and the way the weavers' chatter dropped by a third before a storm. It learned that a tower, cut open by a pangolin, makes a sound of alarm that is not a sound at all but a change in the pressure of the walls.

It was patient, and the valley was patient back. Over weeks the ring of the hush around it shrank, from a startled leap to a settling, and finally to something almost like a nod. The gecko put its foot down. The monkeys came through on the vine-roads and scraped their resin and looked at nothing in particular, and the weavers chattered, and the news of the Attention moved through the canopy the way news of everything does, and was absorbed.

One night, in the deep of the dark between two mounds, the Attention felt the Listener notice it. Not alarm. A registering. A weight, a warmth, an unfamiliar smell, though the Attention had no weight, no warmth, and no smell, and the valley had somehow found the edges of it anyway and worked out where it lay. Then the valley did what it does for any stranger who is not a threat.

It made room.

The Attention, which had seen worlds ended and made, was moved. It had been prepared for gratitude, for worship, for fear. It had not prepared for *room*: to be treated as a neighbor, and given exactly the space a neighbor is given, no more. It understood that this was the highest thing the valley had to offer, and that the valley had offered it to a leopard, a pangolin, and a lost human in the same voice.

## The three small things

Before it left, it did three things. None of them would be visible to a naturalist, and it thought that was right.

**First**, it took the score with it. The valley's music had never been heard from outside. Not from the ridges, not from the sky, not by any other place that might have understood it. The Attention carried the hundred-pitch chorus, the cicada swell, the ring of the hush, and the drip out into the wider dark, and sang them there, once, to things that had not known such a sound was possible. This cost the valley nothing. It gave the valley an audience it would never know it had.

**Second**, it went down to the place where the road of rock ends. The valley has always been protected by its own inconvenience: no easy way in, no good reason to build one. But a slow danger was on the way, of the kind the valley's slow mind could not perceive, made of maps and appetites. The Attention did not turn the wind or move a mountain. It only laid a quality in the air at the edge, a thin residue of what it had felt in the dark that night. Anyone who came down the last slope into the warm, wet air would feel, faintly, that they were being noticed and that they should make room. Most would take it for nerves. But some would step more gently. Some would stay an hour and never say why they left without cutting anything. Somewhere, a person with a survey and a signature would find that this valley did not seem, on reflection, to be a place that needed a road.

That was all. It didn't forbid. It only made the valley harder to be careless in.

**Third**, on its last dusk, it did something it had not planned. When the frogs began, and the cicadas rose, and the bats poured out through the holes in the roof, the Attention did not join the chorus, because it would have been a false note. It went quiet, entirely, and let the roar go on around and through it.

And the valley, in the middle of its loudest hour, did something the Attention had not seen before. A hush began. It was not the ring of a leopard, and not the ring of a hawk. It was small and local, starting near the place where the Attention had been listening. It lasted three heartbeats: the frogs holding a rest, the weavers lowering their rattle, the drips continuing alone. Then the noise came back, all at once, as if nothing had happened.

It was a farewell, or a question, or just the valley's way of noticing that something had stopped being there. The Attention did not know which, and understood that it was not required to.

## After

It left as it had come, without any motion. The gecko on the tower flank finished its stride.

In the years after, the valley went on. A great tree fell in the eighth year, and the shaft of real daylight came down onto the floor, and the ferns and orchids and seedlings exploded green beneath it. The spiders began, thread by thread, to close the gap. Two queens died and their towers slept, and lizards moved in. A fire came over the northern ridge in a dry season and stopped at the edge of the warm air. The road of rock ended where it always had.

Now and then a stranger comes down the last slope into the wet dark, with things walking on their skin and the roof sagging with silk overhead. They stand between two mounds, deafened and half-blind, and they feel something take note of their weight and their warmth and their unfamiliar smell. They wait to be afraid.

What they feel instead, most of them, is a quiet sense of being made room for. They stay a little while, and then they go, more gently than they came.
