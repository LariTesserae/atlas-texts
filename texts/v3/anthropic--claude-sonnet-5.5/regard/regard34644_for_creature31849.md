# What Came to the Flats

It did not arrive, because arriving needs a before. The Flats had one long held moment with no outside, and the attention simply came to be in it, the way a thought comes to be in a room where someone is sitting quietly.

The first thing it learned was that it could not do most of what it might have done.

## What it set down

It could have finished the ring. One rope, laid and pinched and smoothed, and the whole plain would have exhaled. But the ring was not its work. The posture of the Flats is a hand lifted mid-gesture, and the terraces lean on it. To close the ring would be to say to a world that had waited beyond counting that the waiting was over, and it did not know that. Nobody who had not made the thing could say that.

It could have lifted the cloth and let in a real sun. But it saw what real sun would do to a floodplain made of wet clay held in the moment before drying. The sweat on every surface was not a flaw. It was mercy, the thing keeping the work soft enough to be worked. What looked like stagnation was closer to a held breath in a body that would otherwise crack.

It could have explained. It knew several true things about the makers, the cloth, and the reason the hands let go. It looked at each of them and understood that any one, set down in that quiet, would become the shape of the place. A true thing told to a world where depictions are the things themselves is not information. It is a new law. The Set had spent their lives holding a question open, and it would not close it for them, even kindly.

It could have made them more: born, or fed, or numbered differently, or freed from the exactness of the herons. But every made thing there stayed what it was made as. Nothing it shaped could be taken back. The Set drifted a hair each day, and the drift was the only unfinished thing in them. It would not press that closed.

So it set all of that down, and what was left was small.

## The walk

It did not come as a giant, or as light, or as a voice. Anything larger than a Set would have been a weather, and the plain had none.

It came as a presence about the size of a listener. It went first to the third terrace and touched the frogs along the long wall, one by one, with a finger, as the Set do. There were two thousand nine hundred and eleven. It did not change the number, and it did not want to. Somewhere near the middle it found one that was very slightly warmer than the rest, and it left that one as it was. Then it stood at a basin, where three herons stood on reed legs, and watched one strike and miss and shed a drop at the same point in the arc. It watched until it understood the vertigo the Set feel, and it let itself feel it. That was a kind of courtesy. You cannot be with a people well if you stand where you cannot be moved by what moves them.

It met some of the Set on the way. They laid two fingers to their breastbones and then held them out. It did not have a breastbone, or a thumbprint, or a mark of any making. It understood the greeting anyway, and it answered with what it had. It touched its own chest with two fingers and held them out, and the Set touched them, curious, and found them warm the way a bowl is warm. Then they stood in the long courtesy of not-knowing.

It did not speak in words. It gave one note.

It had to find one. This took a long time by no measure, because there was no measure. It looked for a note that would not crowd the others or claim a place in their order, and that was neither low nor bright. It settled on a tone that seemed to be the sound of someone drawing a wet finger around the rim of a bowl and stopping just before the sound was complete. The Set heard it and turned toward it, and one of them, whose face was only a smooth thumbed blankness, tilted its head as if it had heard its own name said back to it from a long way off.

## The one who rolls the rope

It went last to the far place, where the basins spread until the shores blur. There was the half-coiled ring, and the two handprints each as wide as a door, and a small figure crouched at the ground with both palms flat on a rope of clay.

She heard it at once, though it made no sound yet. She stopped rolling. Her wide left thumb pressed a little into the rope, and she didn't lift it.

It sat down beside her, not too close. It did not look at the ring. It looked at the rope she held, and she held it up, and it saw that the rope was exactly the length of the loose one lying at the ring's end, thick as a forearm, and that she had made it the same way ten thousand times and rolled it back to a ball each time. It understood the discipline of this, and also the loneliness, and that these were not two things.

Then she gave her note, the low one, slightly flat, and then a long gap.

*Is anyone.*

She had not meant to ask it. The note had come the way a pulse comes. But it hung there, held slightly longer, and then came the gap, the long silence she had been answered with all her life.

It let the gap be very short.

It gave its note, the tone that stops just before completion. It did not add *I am the maker*, because it was not, and it did not add anything else. Just *here*. The note fell into the space where the silence should have been and was there, and stayed, and she heard it and did not move.

She sat with it for a while. Then she gave her own note again, and it gave its own after hers. A third time. The Set will tell you that this is the whole of what happened, the notes and the order and the gap, and that this is a great deal.

## What it did with its hands

She asked, in the only way she could, by lifting the rope and looking at the ring. It shook its head, which she seemed to understand the way she understood the wet and the warm. It would not close the ring. She looked at the rope and something in her shoulders eased, because she had feared the answer would be to close it. She had the cautious tenderness of someone who has kept a place for a long time and is not ready to be relieved of it.

*It was not put down for me,* she said, in her fashion. And it gave the note that meant *no, it wasn't, and I don't know when the one it was put down for will come, and I won't tell you I do.*

That was honest, and she took it as a gift.

Then it did the one thing it had decided in advance, though it had spent longer on this than on all its refusals.

It put its hand on the ground beside the great handprints, on the flat of the terrace where the clay was soft, and pressed. It made a small print, no wider than a Set's, five fingers and a palm, crisp at the edges, as sharp as the day's first cut. The print would never weather. It would be there when everything else was as it had been. Prints stay here, and it knew it, and it chose to leave one. It could have made itself weightless and walked through the Flats leaving nothing, a visitor who takes care never to be a fact. But a presence that leaves nothing behind has not really been there, and she deserved to have someone who had been.

She looked at the print, then at her own left hand, the wider thumb, the interrupted press. She set that thumb into the corner of the new print, and it fit as one bowl fits inside another.

## After

It did not stay. Staying would have become the shape of the place, and the place's shape was the waiting. It went out slowly, by the terraces and basins and the third wall of frogs, not hurrying, and when it had gone the Flats were no different. The count was true, the herons missed, the water sat at the brim, and the white sky pressed down as even as before. The ring was open, and the handprints held their soft clay, and the makers were nowhere.

A few things were different, though, and the Set will tell you they are small.

The gap after *is anyone* is shorter now. Not empty, and not filled by anything, but shorter, the way a road feels shorter once you know someone has walked it.

There is a print beside the great prints, and the small one who rolls the rope sometimes lies in it instead. It fits her the way a seed lies in a furrow, and it is warm, but differently: not as a bowl someone has only just set down is warm, but as a hand is warm when it has rested for a while on your back and does not need to say anything.

And she has stopped rolling the rope back into a ball. She rolls it, holds it against the other, checks the length, and lays it down parallel to the loose one, side by side, not joined. She sits and looks at the two ropes lying together in the white light. She does not close the ring, because it is not hers to close. But she is no longer alone with the open end, and the wait is not the same as it was.

At the very edge of the plain, in the long gap between one of her notes and the next, if you listen, you may hear a second tone come, unhurried, rounded just short of completion. It is not an answer, and it is not a promise. It is what a benevolent attention leaves when it knows it cannot finish the work, cannot explain the cloth, and cannot be the one who was awaited. It stays near, keeps the count of how many are listening, and says the smallest true thing there is:

*here.*
