# The Guest

It did not arrive, exactly. Attention doesn't arrive. It turns, and wherever it turns, a place briefly becomes the most looked-at thing there is. For a hundred million years nothing had looked at the basin. Then something did, something that could hold all of it at once: the rim, the Raft, the pitch of every tether, the small fear in each shoaler.

## What It Looked For

First it looked for the wound. This is what power does first, and wisdom is mostly what happens to that instinct afterward.

It moved through the water asking what was wrong. It found a lurker's hunger, shoalers taken, Tenders ending in the bowls, a cold that never lifted, and a light that was only a rumor. By the measure of a mind that had watched many worlds, none of it was a wound. The hunger was the reason the gallery stayed clean. The shoalers' short lives were the reason the colonnades rustled. The dark was no deprivation to eyes made for it. The Tenders' deaths were not tragedies to the Tenders, who walked down to the bowls calmly and became the garden their children walked on.

Then it noticed a temptation in itself: to look at a world that never changed and call it *unfinished*. The Tenders had a better word for it, *in tune*, and the Power came to think they were right. A thing can be complete without being dead. Here, completeness was maintained by thousands of small hands, every day, and that daily effort was not what the world lacked. It was what the world was.

## What It Declined

It did not give them light. Light would answer a question nobody in the basin had asked, and the asking would be the injury.

It did not teach. A Tender told that its hall is one small room in a vast house would learn that its work was small. It is not small. It is exactly as large as the hall, and that is the largest a work can honestly be.

It did not soften the dying, or end the hunting, or lift the cold. It did not reduce the dark to something comfortable. Where suffering was the grain of the thing, it left the grain alone.

It did not reveal itself. A god in the water would be the first sour note that could never be tuned out. The Tenders would spend their centuries bracing around it.

## The One Thing It Did

There was one true danger, and it was not in the basin.

The basin's perfection is the perfection of a closed system, which means its only possible enemy is whatever comes from outside. A hundred million years of balance between seep and rain was physics, but it was also luck, the long good fortune of a place nobody bothered. The rim was worn low and round. The sky above was thin. Somewhere in the deep future there was a drift of climate, a stray rock, or the appetite of some distant species for peat and water.

So the Power did what the seep does, quietly. It did not add water or change the balance by a drop. It stood the way a hand stands under a cup, and made the balance a little harder for anything outside to disturb. It bent futures away from the rim. It did this once and thoroughly, and then it stopped watching, because a guard who is always seen guarding is a kind of weather.

This was the only gift it gave without being asked, and the only one that could not be verified by anyone. It counted that as the mark of a right gift. If the basin never noticed, the gift had worked.

## Learning the Note

Then it did something harder. It tried to stop being a gap.

It took a form small enough to drift and slow enough not to startle, and it went into the colonnades. It stayed as long as a Tender's season, which the shoalers lived as several generations. It did not try to speak. It listened, pressed against the pillars as the Tenders do, until it understood that the chord is not a sound but a *fitting*, each tether's note shaped by the others around it, like the pitch a voice takes inside a choir.

The Power was not built to fit anywhere. Learning how took most of the season. Its first hum was too clear, too sure of itself, a note with no history in it. It softened the note, gave it the slight flatness of a body that has spent years in cold water, and let it wander toward the pitch of the smallest hall, a modest colonnade off the boomers' usual path that nobody had ever called important.

The gap did not close all at once. But the shoalers, who had parted around it in their thousands, began to pass through it. The lurkers stopped opening their mouths wider. A Tender at an arch let its fingers rest against the stranger's shell for the length of a single breath and then went back to work.

## The Knot

One Tender, very old and furred with pale threads, had been working the same arch for two hundred years. There was a place near the crown where the binding had begun to go slack, too small for anyone else to notice. The Tender knew it. It knew it every day, and every day it tightened the binding and knew it would slacken again.

The Power could have fixed the arch in an instant. It did not. It waited until the Tender withdrew to rest, and then, with hands it borrowed for the purpose, it tied one knot.

It was not a Tender's knot. The Power had studied the hands of that hall for a whole season and could not make its own hold in that dialect, so it tied it in the nearest way it could, which came out a little strange. It was slower than the hall's knots, and more careful, and it did not hurry toward its end. It held. It held the way things hold when they have been loved a little.

When the Tender returned it touched the knot for a long time. It could read who had made a joint, and when, and in what mood, but it could not read this one. The hand was unfamiliar. Yet the mood was legible, and the Tender read it slowly, twice, and let its fingers rest on the last loop.

*Content.*

It did not undo the knot. It did not ask who had tied it. It set the next binding beside it, in its own hand, and went on. Generations from now a young Tender running its fingers along that arch will find one joint that no lineage claims, and will read from it, and will not know why the arch feels a little easier to hold there. That is all the trace the Power left. Nothing else in the basin has its hand.

## The Answer

On the last day of its stay, a boomer began to call.

It was an old one, with a long constellation of blue points down its flank. The call rolled through the water in one descending groan, taking a full minute to finish. Nobody knew exactly what it said. Perhaps it said only *here I am*. Answering was the custom, and the answer came, as it always came, from some other boomer kilometers away, a full minute and then a long silence before the exchange could close.

The Power waited for the silence. Then, in the boomer's own manner and at its own pace, using the slow pitch of a hall it had come to know, it answered.

*Yes. Here I am too.*

The boomer was still. Then, from farther off, the ordinary reply came, the other old one, unaware. But the first boomer did not turn. It hung under the Raft with its stars slowly moving, and after a long time it called again. Something in the call was different, the way a room is different after a window has been opened and closed. It could not have said what. It was not the sort of creature that says.

## Leaving

The Power left the way the chord closes behind a passing body, with no seam.

It took nothing and changed nothing that could be named. The water was as high as it had always been, the light as dim, the cold as even. The Tenders tapped their slow dialects. The snappers crackled. The drone went on through everyone's sternum, and everyone in the basin was still, in the only way that mattered to them, home.

What it left was small: a knot, a reply, and a steadiness at the rim that would prove itself only by never being needed. What it took was smaller and larger. It carried, in whatever place a mind like that keeps things, a single low note, slightly flat, pitched to a modest hall nobody thought important. It hummed the note sometimes, in its own long hours, when it was tempted to believe that power is the same thing as doing.

It was not. The oldest wisdom in the basin was one the Power had to learn by drifting there. Some works are not to be finished, only kept. Some things are best helped by helping them stay what they are. And the deepest mercy it had found was neither rescue nor gift. It was *attention* that asked nothing, that stood with a thing and listened to it, and then, having heard it, left it in tune.
