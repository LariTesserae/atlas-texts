# The Attention

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a before, and the power had been looking for a long time before it decided to be looked back at.

The first sign was small. At the rim, where the fern gives way to the first sheet of water, a heron lifted its head a moment before the others turned theirs. Then all of them turned, across the whole kilometer, and held. Something had been seen. Nothing on the basin's skin had changed.

The power came as slowly as it could manage. It had considered thunder, light, and a voice, and dismissed them the way a physician dismisses a scalpel when the patient is only asleep. It came as a stillness at the rim, a shape with no particular shape, standing ankle-deep in warm water and doing nothing. Doing nothing was the first act, and it lasted a season.

## What It Weighed

A great deal could have been done, and the power went through it all with care.

It could have ended the killing. The heron's neck, the closing mat, and the dark green flash inside it would all have stopped. But the killing was the basin's circulation. The clay took back the dead within hours, and the shrimp clouds thickened and thinned in answer to the herons' shadows. To lift death out of that arrangement would be like lifting one thread from a weave, and the power had seen what happened to cloth. It let the killing stand and grieved it briefly, without complaint. Nothing here suffered in the way that needs an answer. The suffering was the size of a ripple and was absorbed as one.

It could have given the Tenders faces, or names, or back their size. It could have told them whether they had once been the makers. It could have said which of the three stories was true.

It declined, and this was the hardest refusal, because the answer was easy to give. But the three stories were load-bearing. Not knowing whether they were the ones who had shrunk into their upkeep gave the Tenders' work a quality that certainty would have flattened. The mystery was a kind of depth, and depth was the one thing the basin could not make for itself. Nothing cuts here. The power would not be the first thing to cut.

It could have guaranteed the future: the flatness against uplift, the water against drought, the works against whatever comes for all works. It looked at that too, and saw that the basin had never asked for a guarantee. It had asked for something else, once a day.

## The Listening

So the power learned to listen, which took longer than it expected, because the basin does not speak in anything a power is built to hear.

It knelt in the shallows and put its palm on a black stone. The murmur came up through it, the Tenders' taps, the frogs' heaps, the eels' slow accounting of light. It learned that the shrimp clouds were not thinking and that this was not a lack. It learned the grammar of the phosphor trails, where a flare of green means *here* and a flare of amber means *I passed this way and you are not alone*. It learned the herons' calls by feeling them behind its teeth, though it had no teeth.

Then, slowly, it understood what it was standing on.

The basin was a mind, or the ghost of a mind, or the first outline of one. It was thin as a film, a kilometer wide, thought spread so evenly that no part of it could say *I*. It had no word for itself and no need of one. It knew its own temperature, its own water level, the small sicknesses of its reeds. It had been keeping the same modest attention on itself since before the hills were gone, and it was not lonely, because loneliness requires a second thing to be missing.

But there was the hour of rain.

## The Hour

On the afternoon the power finally understood, it stood beside the dome and felt the pull begin in its non-ears. The cloud gathered. The lantern-veils went dark in one ripple. The Tenders froze with grains held aloft, and the herons bowed.

The rain came down warm, and the water turned inward toward the round mouth that does not fill. The power listened to what the water carried, and this time it was not only petals and scales.

It was the day. Every heron's count of the shrimp, every eel's account of the clay, every reed's small report of its own leaning, all of it poured into the dome in a single act of telling. The basin gave a full account of itself, once a day, to something that never answered.

It was not a question, and the power had been wrong to think it might be. It was closer to what a person does at the end of a long day, saying it all aloud to the dark, not to be answered but so that it has been said. The dome had never been a well. It was an ear, and it took everything.

The power understood then what it could do, and how small it was.

## The One Thing

It did not answer. An answer would have turned a practice into a conversation, and the basin was not built for conversation. It would have made the rain an appeal, and the light's return a disappointment.

The power did what the dome could not. It heard.

As the water ran inward, it held the whole telling for the length of the hour. It received the day, the herons' counts and the eels' accounts and the last flicker of the frogs, and it received it without judgment or correction. It took no notes, since it had no use for any. It did the plain, rare thing of being a witness that did not want anything.

And when the spire's hum climbed its full step, the power added nothing to it. It held the step beside the hum, a second voice too quiet to be a chord, so that for one hour the note was not alone.

Then the rain lifted and the light came back in its hot rush. The pulses started again, one, then a thousand, and the air breathed gold.

But the first pulse came a fraction early, and the basin did not notice. Only the power noticed, and it took this as the only reply it needed.

## The Heron

There was one place, at the far rim, where the herons' intervals were off by a body's width. One of them had died in some season no one counted, and the clay had taken it, and the survey had closed around the gap.

The power stood in the gap for a day. It let itself be shaped, only slightly, into something tall and grey with a neck it did not use. It stood as the herons stand, motionless, with the water folding around its ankles.

The neighboring herons did not turn their heads. Their attention slid across it, over it, and through it, the way a hand passes over a familiar table. It was counted the way a stone is counted, as one more thing that is here. The power had not known it could want that, and it stood in the shallows until dusk, wanting it and receiving it.

At some point a frog climbed onto its foot and slept.

## What It Took, and What It Left

It took one thing with it: the sound of the hour, the hush of falling water with the hum climbing beneath it. It kept that where powers keep their few private things.

It left nothing that would weather. No gift, no mark, no new stone among the old ones. A gift would have been a sentence in a language the basin didn't speak.

Before leaving, though, it did one more thing, and this one was not listening. It turned outward, past the rim, past the flat green land, and looked at everything that might someday look toward the basin. Its own attention was the first of these. Being seen changes a place, and the power had seen how easily attention becomes appetite.

So it made the basin a little harder to notice. It thickened the morning steam by a degree, deepened the haze at the rim, and made the flatness a little more like every other flatness, so that eyes passing over it would find nothing to hold them. This was not a wall or a ward, only a slight preference in the light. It suited the place, which had always preferred the streams to have no incision in them, only a slight preference.

## After

The herons turned their heads together at the moment the power withdrew, all the way to the rim, and held. Then, having found nothing, they went back to their shallows. The eels crossed their trails and the phosphor flared, and for the first time in some long span of afternoons there was a color at one crossing that the eels had not used before. It faded within the hour and was not repeated.

The Tenders carried their grains. The frogs slept in their heaps, the oldest at the center, warmed by the others, and no one remembered why.

At the fixed hour of the afternoon, a cloud formed over the dome, dense and low, no larger than a field. It rained warm on exactly the basin and nowhere beyond, and the whole living dish told its day to the round mouth that never fills.

Somewhere past the rim, something listened, and asked for nothing, and did not answer. The light came back in its hot rush, and the pulses began again, one, then a thousand.
