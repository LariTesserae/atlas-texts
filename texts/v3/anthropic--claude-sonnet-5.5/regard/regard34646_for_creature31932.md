# The Attention

## Noticing

Nothing arrives. Arriving implies a before, and the attention has none. It simply becomes aware of the basin, the way you become aware that a sound has been going on for some time.

It is old enough to have watched many such places fill and fuse and open again elsewhere, and it has the reach to change any of them. Its first feeling is not pity. It has learned that pity is usually the wrong opening, a verdict passed before the looking. What it feels is the plain pleasure of something well made and still unfinished.

Then it considers what it could do, and finds the list mostly made of mistakes.

It could warm the air. But the cellar cold is why the mist beads on the stone, why the mats glow blue-green in the hollows, why the water lets go of its mineral in these particular shapes. Warm the basin and you have a different place with the same name.

It could make more Listeners. But the Listeners are four or nine, and they have not counted, and a number would be a kind of sound.

It could hold the basin open, keep the cliffs from leaning and the spires from fusing. But the ending is part of the shape of the song. A note held forever is not a note but a wall.

It could tell them who built the room. It knows, or knows enough. But when they ask, they press a palm flat against the stone and hold it there, and that is already a complete answer. Words would only make it smaller.

It could heal their palms, but the scars are how they read the place. It could give them a word for what the low note says, but they already nod at it.

So it arrives at the only rule that fits. Any gift is a new sound, and this is a place whose whole discipline is keeping sound from turning ugly. The question is not what to give. It is what could be done in the manner of the place.

## Standing

It takes the form it can bear: narrow through the shoulder, turned slightly, one side leading. It stands in the outer forest among the young, shrill spires, in fog to the chest, and does nothing for most of a season.

It learns to read the sky by ear. It learns the flat, heavy drone before rain and the thin bright drone of the cold snaps. It learns that the chord descends as you walk inward, and that the descent is a kind of grammar.

Once a Listener passes within ten paces, grey-clothed and mineral-stiff, and stops. The attention can feel itself being taken for a spire, and then, after a long moment, not. The Listener says nothing. It goes on, one shoulder leading, into the fog. This is the nearest thing to welcome the basin has, and it is enough.

Later a wader steps into the channel beside it and stands. The attention does not know who is keeping whom company. It finds it does not need to.

Eventually it walks inward, turning its shoulders through the crowd of stone, down through the registers. It stops where the handprints stop. It hears the note that comes up under the silence, so low it is felt in the chest before it is heard.

It understands something then. What it understands it does not carry out of the basin. The note asks nothing of it, and this is the most surprising thing it has met in a very long time.

## The Small Work

On the margin there is a young spire that has grown shrill. It spoils the passage inward with a thin edge like a fingernail on glass. The stretch belongs to an old Listener, slow now, who has not yet got around to it. A bone-handled chisel leans against the base of the next spire over.

The attention picks up the chisel. It could do the work with a thought, but it uses the tool and its hands. Over an hour it trims the lip of the spire by a fingernail's depth, and it cuts its own palm on the sugary edge, once, and lets the cut be a cut.

It does the work slightly badly. The note comes out a shade sharp, the way a beginner's does. This is deliberate. A perfect trim would be a miracle, and a miracle must be interpreted, feared, or worshipped. A slightly wrong one is only a neighbor's mistake, and can be corrected. It leaves the work theirs to finish and the chisel where it found it.

## The Old One

In the hollows there is a stretch of channel where the blue-green shimmer runs ahead of every footstep. The attention finds the old Listener there, sitting between two dark pipes with the light around their ankles. They do not look up.

It sits down some distance away. For a long time neither moves. Then the old Listener hums, low, near the pitch of the widest spires. The attention hums back and gets it wrong, too high and too bright. It tries again and gets closer. The old Listener's mouth shifts a very little. It might be a smile, or the fog.

Then the attention does the only thing in all its visit that it does on purpose and against its curiosity. It turns away and looks at nothing. It gives the basin its privacy. The endings here are not seen, and it will not be the one who sees.

When it looks back the channel is empty. The light has faded from the water. It does not ask where they went.

## Afterward

Nothing changes that could be measured. The chord slides lower by a hair's breadth, as it was always going to.

In the spring a Listener finds the sharp spire on the margin. They stand before it a long while. There is a sound, low, with a catch in it, that might be the first laugh the basin has held in some time. Then they take up the bone chisel and correct the lip by a fingernail's depth. When the note has settled to where it belongs, they set a scarred palm against the stone in passing and go on.

In the outer forest a new spire has come up, thin and pale and shrill, and the Listeners tend it with more care than any of the rest. The attention passes it on its way out. It sets its hand there too, once, like the others, and does not stop.

It leaves the way it came, without leaving. But it does not stop attending. Somewhere far off, in a place with no cliffs and no mist, something that could have done anything hums a low note it was not taught, and does not finish it.
