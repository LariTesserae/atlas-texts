# The Visit

It does not arrive the way powers are supposed to. Nothing parts and no light descends. The basin is as it always was, pale and dry and empty, and then its attention is simply *on* the place, the way a person's attention comes to rest on a sound in another room.

It goes to the scarp and finds the seam. The seam is too narrow for what it is, so it makes itself smaller until it can turn sideways and fit. This is the first thing it does, and in a sense the most important. The room has one door, and the door has a width. The width is a kind of sentence, and the power reads it and obeys.

## The Listening

Inside, it is struck as everyone is, and it lets itself be. For a time it is only a body being played. Then it learns the map the way a visitor does, by the ribs and the teeth, and it finds a cancellation pocket and stands in it.

It stays a long while. It does not measure the time, because the room doesn't, and measuring would be a small violation.

What it hears is not what a visitor hears. It hears each tube separately and all of them at once, and it hears what the chord is *made of*: the seep rising, the glass answering, the ancient sentence of the builders read aloud by wind. It hears the crust as ground and the threads as weather. It notices what no one in the room has ever noticed, because no one in the room has been placed to notice it: the chord is *whole*. Nothing is missing from it and nothing is waiting in it to be resolved. It never resolves because it has nothing to resolve *toward*.

## What It Does Not Do

It considers, honestly, everything it could do, because a power that never considered would not be wise, only restrained.

*It could bring water.* The brine would fill the rings, and the crust would flush and the moss would swell. But the tubes would sing differently, then not at all, then differently again. The chord is a fixed thing, and a gift that ends the giftee's world is not a gift.

*It could repair.* Nothing in the room is broken. The chord is not damaged, only permanent. An old scar on a bell is part of the bell.

*It could join the crust to the threads*, let light travel down and gas carry a message up, and let two kinds of creature know each other across the glass. It lingers longest on this one. But they have no lack. The crust does not miss the threads, and the threads have never wondered about the light. To introduce them would be to invent a loneliness in order to cure it.

*It could speak*, to the moss or the halo or the sleeping dust, and tell them what they are. Nothing in the room has ever been troubled by not knowing. Knowing would be the only trouble it ever had.

*It could give them silence*, so they might finally learn what the chord is by contrast. This it refuses most firmly. The halo says *the world is a thing that holds you*, and the power has no wish to teach it that the holding is a sound.

## What It Does

It does three things. None of them will ever be noticed.

**First, the rubble.** The room's only danger is slow: the same wind that polished every rim will, in some far century, widen the seam, and grit will enter, and a thin lip will snap, and the chord will lose a note forever. So the power goes back out, and in the basin, in the dark hours or none, it moves a few stones. Not many, and not far. A low bank of rubble that the wind will now curl past instead of into the crack. The basin still looks like bone-colored ruin, because it is. It has only been leaned very slightly toward mercy.

**Second, a ray.** Standing inside, it had been blocking one bright bar of light from a halo, and the crust beneath its shadow had dimmed for the first time in the life of any cell there. When it notices, it steps aside and does not move again until the light is back. Then it feels something close to shame, and something close to love, at how large a thing a body is in a room like this, and how carefully one must set it down.

**Third, the narrow tubes.** This is the hardest.

## The Question in the Dust

It follows the glass down, past the moss and the silt and the threads, which it passes without touching, into bundles where the tubes narrow to the width of a hair and finally to the width of water. There, packed in the deepest glass, it finds the last of the builders: pinches of dust, dried to nothing, still faintly arranged in the shape of colonies.

They are not dead. Something in them has only *stopped*, the way a sentence stops at a comma.

The power could wake them. Water, a little warmth, and the ancient mat would bud again and lay down new tubes beside the old ones. And the chord, the finished, balanced, unrepeatable chord, would begin to change, note by note, as the reef grew. Maybe that is what the builders would want. Maybe a colony's whole purpose is to keep building, and the stillness of the room is a long held breath and not a peace at all. Maybe they have been waiting all this time for someone to let them go on.

Or maybe they are done, and what looks like waiting is only rest.

It cannot ask them, and it will not answer for them. So it does the one thing that preserves both possibilities and decides neither: it makes sure the question stays open. It checks that the narrow tubes will not seal. It leaves the way from the sky to the dust not open, but *openable*, so that if water ever comes again by rain or flood or some turning of the world's weather, it will find them, and they can answer for themselves in the only language they have ever used.

It would rather leave a door than choose a fate.

## Leaving

When it goes, it turns sideways again and slips through the seam. In the basin, in the great quiet, it stands a moment on the pale rubble.

It carries the chord faintly, a sustained note behind its attention, the way any visitor does. It has not tried to prevent this. Being changed by the room is the one thing it allowed itself. Everything else, it left as it found it.

Behind it, the crust holds its exact diameter. The tufts go on gathering their single bead of water. The threads feel the same slow press from above and push from below. In the narrow glass the dust does not stir. Not one tube lip is broken, and the chord holds, exactly the same, though now the wind will curl a little differently around the scarp, and someone, somewhere in the world, has heard it whole.

The power goes out over the bone-colored ground, humming a note that isn't its own. It will fade in time, as it does for everyone. But for a while, an old and patient thing walks the quiet with the room's one sound inside it, knowing what it kept by not touching it.
