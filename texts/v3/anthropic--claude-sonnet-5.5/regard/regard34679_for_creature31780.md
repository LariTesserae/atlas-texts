# What the Attention Did

It came without light. It had considered light and set it aside before arriving, the way you might leave a gift in the car when you realize you don't yet know the house.

It came into the fen the way the fen is learned, through the skin, and for a long while it did nothing but be there. It laid its palms on a taproot column and felt the tremor. It walked the mat and let the fiber take its weight and give it back. It stood among the pale root-hairs and let them curl toward it. It was patient in a way that had nothing to do with waiting, because it wasn't waiting for anything.

## The catalogue of temptations

It knew what it could do, and it went through the list honestly, since the list was where the danger was.

**It could give light.** But a gift of sight makes the dark into a deprivation. Nothing here lacks a sky, and to bring one is to invent the lack and then fill it, a kind of theft dressed as generosity.

**It could give animals, or minds, or a witness.** But the fen has no need of a witness, and the power did not mistake its own wish to be seen giving for the fen's wish to be given to. Benevolence that cannot bear to find nothing wrong is only an appetite with better manners.

**It could give an end to the waiting.** The vines wait a century for a trunk to fall silent. The root-hairs wait for water. The mosses drum their thin bright fingers. But it looked closely at this waiting and found no ache in it. Waiting is what a settled thing looks like from outside. The vines were not lacking. They were in line, and the line was the arrangement.

**It could improve the design.** Here it was humbled. The trunks refuse to fall into step, each stiffening a valve when it starts to lock with a neighbor, and this is a solution it might have been proud to invent. It found no flaw to fix and knew better than to invent one.

So it rejected all of these, and it did not feel this as restraint. It felt it as recognition. The fen was not a problem.

## The question underneath

There was one thing it did not know, and it wanted to know it honestly rather than assume. Was anyone here?

It spent a long time with its hands on the bark and its own attention turned down as far as it would go, until it could no longer hear the undersong except when it thought of it. It found something, or it found the place where something would be. There was no thought as we would mean it and no pain. There might have been a faint, continental steadiness that was not quite nothing, a held note that could not tell it was a note. It could not say whether the fen was a someone or only a very fine *how*. It decided to treat the uncertainty with courtesy, the way you would step quietly through a room where someone might be sleeping.

This decision governed everything after. Where a mind might be, it would do only what a mind could refuse.

## The one thing it did

Far out, past the sky the fen has never imagined, it saw something the fen had no office for.

In some eleven thousand years, a stray body of rock and ice, cold and old and indifferent, would come through the upper dark on a path that crossed the fen's continent. It would not be a large body by the standards of such things, but it was large enough. It would tear through the canopy and open a wound in the vane-tiers, and light would pour into a country whose every part was built to be dark. The pressure would surge, the trunks would lose their manners, the water wheel would break, and a settled act that had held since the rock first cracked would stop.

This was not part of the fen's cycle. The fen had no valve for it and no tolerance, and it could not have grown one, because the fen adapts by position and inheritance, by slow tuning across lifetimes, and this would arrive in a single afternoon.

So the power did the smallest thing that would work. It reached out and turned the stray body by a hair's breadth, less than the width of one root-hair, at a distance where a hair's breadth becomes a continent's width later on. The rock would pass and miss and go on its way into the dark. It left no trace, and no one would ever know, including the fen.

It took no pride in this. It made a point of taking no pride, because pride would have meant the act was for the doer.

## What it did not fix

The power also saw, further off, that the seams would eventually close. The fractures would heal and the heat would fade, and the potential between rock and air would thin over tens of millions of years. The fen would run down.

It did not intervene here. The columns would thicken their finger's width per lifetime and shift along the flows as the flows shifted, and offices would pass to new officeholders as they always had. The fen was built to change at the speed of its own life. An ending that comes that slowly is not a catastrophe but a long exhale, and to prevent it would have meant holding the fen fixed forever, which is its own kind of harm. The distinction was simple enough. It would guard the fen against what the fen could not meet, and leave it to meet what it could.

## The vine

Before it left, a vine laid a tendril across its ankle.

The vine did not want anything. It had found a small warm moving flow and gone to it, the way a vine does. The power stood still and let it. It even, for a little while, let its own warmth and its faint electric tingle pass into the tendril, as a courtesy, so that for a few hours the vine's part of the great even current ran fractionally richer. Then it drew its foot away gently, and the vine returned to its slot, not knowing it had been anything but a vine.

## The door

Last, it did one thing that was not an act on the fen but on itself. It made itself available.

It did not plant a question, because a question planted is a question imposed. It only arranged that if the fen ever grew something that looked up, if in some slow deep epoch a tissue evolved that could ask what lay above the leaf-ceiling or whether there was anything beyond the hum, the question would not fall into silence. There would be an answer, gentle and unhurried, ready to meet it at whatever pace it came.

This was a door with no handle on the fen's side and no lock on the other, and the power would not open it. If it ever opened, it would open from within, in the fen's own time. The power was content to be a place on the flow that no one had yet come to fill.

## Departure

When it left, the fen did not change. The trunks ticked on out of step, the rain fell on its schedule, and a spark crawled over a column, lit a leaf and a bead of water and a thread of root for a moment, and went out. The undersong held its single note.

The power moved outward through the dark and did not look back, because looking back would have been a way of needing the fen to have been changed.

Only the fen's safety was different, and the fen would never know that either. If a mind was ever there, it went on being the held breath it had always been.
