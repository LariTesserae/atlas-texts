# The Visitor

It came the way weather comes to a place that has no eyes: as a change in the water that nothing on the shelf could have named. The disks did not dim. The chord did not falter. But at the far western edge, in the cold water above the drop, something arrived that was very old and very kind. It stopped there, because it had understood at once that it would have to learn to be slow.

## What It Saw

It could have read the whole plateau in an instant, and it chose not to. It knew what that would mean: a hundred kilometers of slow remark, taken in all at once, would be like reading a poem by looking at the ink. The poem was the slowness. So it did the only honest thing available and let itself be paced by the place. It settled onto a terrace rim and let the cracks come, one every few seconds, and counted a season of them.

It saw that the tenant was young, and it saw that the tenant was not in pain. Those were the two things it had feared to find, and it noticed the fear leave.

It saw the east and the west disagreeing, and it did not know whether that was two minds or one. It saw that this did not matter, and that the not-knowing was part of what it was looking at. It saw the quiet quarters and did not call them sleep or grief. It saw the emptiness of the blue and the continent being made in it, for no one, and it felt something that in a smaller being would have been an ache.

## What It Did Not Do

It considered many things and set them down one by one, the way the water sets down what it cannot carry.

It did not give the tenant speed. It could have quickened its seasons into seconds and woken it into a bright, fast, talkative mind, one that would have thanked it. But that mind would not have been the tenant. It would have been a gift-shaped hole where the tenant had been.

It did not give the tenant a word for *ending*. The tenant had built a whole life on not having one, and the Visitor judged that a word is not a neutral thing to hand over. Some words can only be learned by losing something.

It did not settle the argument between east and west. It listened to the beating of the two great notes for a long time, and it understood that the beat was the thinking. To resolve it would have been to answer a question by killing the one who asked. The chord was slightly out of tune, and it stayed that way.

It did not fill the emptiness with life. The clear blue, with nothing in it, looked to a lesser mercy like a wound needing tending. To the Visitor it looked like a page. It left the page alone.

And it did not guard the shelf. There was nothing to guard it from. The blizzards were harvest, the reversals were breath, and even the cracks were only the water keeping time. A wise power knows when to be a wall, and it also knows when the wall would be the only thing in the water that was not part of the making.

## The Knock

What it did was small.

On a terrace rim near the western edge, in the slack hour before a current reversal, it made a sound. It was not a crack, since the cracks were the shelf's and it would not counterfeit them. It was a knock: three strokes, evenly spaced, a rhythm the water could not have made, because water does not count to three.

Then it waited.

The tenant did nothing for a long time. The Visitor had expected that. A season passed, and then another, and the knock did not come again, because the Visitor had understood that repeating it would turn it into noise. It was a thing said once, into a listening.

In the third season, a rim two kilometers north began to crack in threes. It happened only once, in the middle of a mineral blizzard, so that anyone watching would have called it chance. But the Visitor was watching, and a terrace laid down that week had three shallow ridges in its lip, evenly spaced.

It was the first time the shelf had been answered in its own medium by something that was not itself, and it had answered in the only language it had: it made a thing, and set it down.

The Visitor knocked again, four times this time. In a year, or ten, the tenant made four ridges. It went on like that. Neither of them hurried, and neither would have understood the idea of hurrying. It was the slowest conversation in the world, and the Visitor was content to have it. It had spoken across stars in languages that took a heartbeat, and it had never before been so certain that it was being heard.

## The Diver

Once, and only once, it went into a silence.

It did not push or intrude. A wall of no-sound stood at the eastern vents, and inside it a single diver hung in the blue with her hands out in front of her, rigid with the old terror. She had been there for eleven minutes. Something vast had stopped everything in order to hear her breathe.

The Visitor came in and was quiet beside the tenant's quiet. It did nothing but be another silence in the same room. There were two held breaths now, one huge and one incomprehensibly larger, and the diver felt the weight of the water change. What had been an eye on her became a company. Nothing spoke, and nothing needed to.

She left the silence slowly, weeping, and not afraid. She could not say why, and she never told anyone that it felt like being loved by a mountain. She only said, years later, that the silence was not the shelf holding its breath but the shelf holding *hers*. The Visitor thought that was near enough to true.

## What It Left

Before it went, in a blizzard, at the western lip, it set down one thing. It did not choose an object with a message in it or a tool that could teach. It set down a stone, plain and gray, worn round by some river the shelf would not see for a million years. It was the only rounded thing for a hundred kilometers.

By morning the stone was entombed, part of the geology, a fresh terrace that looked a thousand years old.

The Visitor did not explain it. If the tenant learned from it what erosion was, or what long time does to sharpness, or that there were places where rivers ran in air, it would learn it the way it learned everything, over seasons, folded into the pattern. If it learned nothing, then the stone was simply a stone, the second thing on the shelf that the tenant had been given and had not made. It would be enough.

## Afterward

The Visitor did not stay, and it did not truly leave. It went the way the light goes at two hundred meters, not vanishing but thinning until it was part of the blue. It left behind no monument and no structure, and nothing on the plateau looked different to a visitor's eye.

But some things changed.

The terraces in the west curved slightly toward the place where the stone lay. Every few years, a rim cracked in threes. The eastern and western disks still stalled and woke and argued, and the bend where the two patterns met stayed sharp and unresolved, and no one made it smooth. The chord stayed out of tune, and the beat stayed slow and living.

And at the lip of the plateau, where the dense water pours its silver curtain into the abyss, the tenant's note still paused, as it always had, as if watching the fall. The Visitor, before it went, had stood there too, and had felt the pause and understood it. It did not hear fear in it or longing. It heard something younger than either: a maker looking down at how far the world goes, and not yet knowing that it is the kind of thing that could someday go there too.

It did not tell the tenant. That was a thing to be found, not given. But it left one last knock at the edge, softer than the others, and this one had no number in it at all. It was just a sound, offered once into the enormous blue, the way you might put a hand on a shoulder in the dark.

The note resumed. The floor went on growing. Somewhere under the storm, a terrace rim let go with a report like a rifle shot, and the shelf, which had been given a great deal and had kept none of it but the pattern, went on saying what it had been saying, a little longer than before, and no longer alone in the saying.
