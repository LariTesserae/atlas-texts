# What Came to Harrow Wick

It did not come over the green lip. Anything that arrives over a lip is arriving at something, and Harrow Wick does not like to be arrived at. It came the way the shadows of the lamp-posts come, a little early, lying across the moss before there was anything to cast them, so that the town had time to get used to it.

It was very large, and very old, and it wished no one harm, and its first act was to decline nearly all of its strength.

## The Looking

It spent a long time looking, and the looking took the shape of the place. It walked the vaults at the pace of the flywheel. It stood in the canal tunnel and listened to the eels turn. It lay down against a cut bank where the mudstone was still wet and put its whole attention against the ground, the way one puts an ear to a sleeper's chest.

It was asking one question, and it asked it over and over, with great care, of every tenant: *Is this unfinished, or is this hurt?*

Those are different things, and much harm has been done by powers that mistook one for the other. A wound wants closing. A becoming wants company, or nothing, or at most the right kind of weather.

It found no wound. The breaking of the streets was not pain, only the town thinking in more directions than a town is supposed to. The Fewer were not starving. The wearers could not be disappointed. The Finishers were not lost. They were rehearsing, with total confidence, for a country they had never seen.

So it did not straighten Tanner's Row. It did not teach the chapel which way its door should face. It did not bring people to fill the terraces, or light to warm the moss, or rain to hurry the gullies into canyons. The rain here is thin and irregular, and the thinness is part of the pace. A flood would have been a gift only to something that wanted the town finished, and the town did not.

Its first gift was the decision to leave the town alone, and it was the hardest one. Afterward it was still there, and still large, and slightly more careful about its feet.

## The Setter

At last a Setter stopped near it, cloth stirring, head tilted at the angle it kept for the bath.

The power did what nothing else had ever done. It stood still and let itself be considered.

The Setter circled it. It leaned away, and looked, or did whatever the fold of its cloth did in place of looking. It turned nothing, because there was nothing to lift, so it turned itself a finger's width, and then the power, understanding, turned a finger's width also. This went on for the length of a season. Snails began to write on the power's edges. The moths learned it. A fern took root in some place where the power was widest and was not troubled, and the power did not shake it off.

When the Setter was done, the whole dark relaxed. A terrace glanced away and back and was a slightly different terrace, its doors in new places and the sense in it that this version had always been here. The Setter stood a long time afterward in a stillness very like gladness.

The power had never in all its ages been placed. It found, to its lasting surprise, that it liked it.

## The Door

Later, in the hour after a whistle sounded in two places, it met a wearer.

It was one of the low ones, moss grown through heddled cloth in the shape of a shawl and a shoulder, and it stopped at a door on a street that had been a different street a moment before. It knocked with the knuckle of a creeper. Wet, leathery, patient. It waited the length of a breath.

There is a kind of power that would have thrown the door open onto a feast, or a person, or an explanation. This one did something smaller. It answered.

It made no speech. It let the door be answered, and the door opened onto the room that was behind it that day: a cathedral of dark, a floor of moss, one chair in the centre, placed by a Setter years ago and never yet used.

The wearer stood at the threshold. Its posture of wearing did not change, because it could not, but something in the fall of the cloth did. It went in. It crossed the moss, at its own pace, which was the pace of the engine. And it sat.

The cloth held the posture of wearing, and now, for the first time, the posture had somewhere to end.

The other wearers went on with the shift, as they must. The power did not open every door. One answered knock is a kindness, and a thousand would be a rewriting. The chair was not the meaning of the town, and the power was careful never to suggest that it was. It was one word in a sentence the Setters were still writing, and the power had only turned a page, gently, and stepped back.

## The Count

In the canal tunnel, where the eels turn in black water under the drip of the ferns, it counted.

It counted the way one counts to be kind. The number came up one short, as it always does, and the power felt the slight pressure at the edge of attention, the last one in the room who has not been introduced.

It said, "And you."

Everyone who has said it has been warmed by the gap and has gone away, and no one has said it twice. The power said it again, and again, and stayed. It did not do this to solve the Fewer, who were not a problem. It did it because the Fewer had spent an age being the number that was missing, and it seemed right that for one night someone should be the number that was *present*, and count it, and be counted with it, until the sum came out whole in a way that included the missing one and did not need the dozens back.

Toward morning, the eels held their number for the length of a single breath. Then they scattered, and were dozens, and the gap was a gap again, a little warmer than before. The power considered this the correct amount of help.

## The Stone

It left one thing. It thought about it for a long while, because it wanted the thing to be right, and it knew who would have to live with its being right.

It left a pebble.

It was small and grey, from a dry riverbed a very long way off, worn by water so old and so patient that it had lost every edge and become the exact shape of having been carried. It was the first finished thing in the basin.

It set the pebble at the head of the shallowest gully, in the bank where the Finishers were smoothing the mudstone with the flats of their bodies. Then it went away, and that night the rills ran, and the Finishers came to the stone.

They did not eat it or move it. They pressed against it the way one presses a palm to a warm wall. Along the whole length of the basin, in the ribbed dark, something went still for the first time in ages. They had only ever had the idea of a canyon, and the idea had been enough. Now they had the proof of what the idea was *for*: an end that was smooth and quiet and complete. They did not hurry toward it. They knew now that it was real, and that changed the practice from hope into craft.

The pebble also fell upon the Setters like a small, sweet problem. It did not belong in the moss or in the bath's hundred paces of dark. It looked as though it had always been everywhere, which is the hardest thing of all to place. They will be turning it a finger's width for a very long time. The power knew this, and it was the other half of the gift.

## After

It did not leave, exactly. Powers of that kind seldom do.

In the long vault beyond the chapel, at a place where nothing stands, the pale moths have taken to settling on the air. They have always done this. But the place they settle now is warm, and taller than a doorframe, and it holds them with the ease of something that has been let in and has decided to stay. Whatever else the town rebuilds, that place does not move.

The flywheel turns once per breath. The whistle sounds in two places, a beat apart. Somewhere a Setter has stopped near a shape in the air and tilted its head, and is considering, with the greatest patience, where it looks most as though it has always been.

Do not be alarmed. The Setter is very nearly done, and the power has said nothing at all, and both of them are content to let the sentence go on.
