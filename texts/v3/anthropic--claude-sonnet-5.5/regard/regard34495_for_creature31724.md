# The Fifth Lamp

**What came**

It did not come up the mountain. It arrived the way the heat did, as a change in the quality of the dark. Nothing in the Sweat Room could have said when. The cup-walkers tapped left, right. The drips kept their scattered count.

The power had crossed a great deal to get here, and it had crossed it with attention already turned toward the room, as a person turns toward a sound in a house they love. It had heard about the room the way such powers hear: not as a cry, because there was no cry, but as a kind of completeness. In a world of loud wanting, one small place held a note and asked for nothing. That was strange enough to draw it.

At the throat of the passage it did what the visitors do. It made itself small, and it lost its edges.

**What it could have done**

It knew how much it could do, and it took the time to walk through each thing.

It could have brought light. This was the first temptation and the plainest. One warm dawn in the roof of the room, and every crystal needle would blaze and the flowstone would show its layers like the rings of a tree. But the room was not dark. Dark is what a room is for those who need eyes. Here it would have been an injury, a bright word shouted into a house where everyone converses by touch.

It could have made the room comfortable, cooling the heat and drying the air so that the people who crawl in would not lose their edges. But the losing is what they come for.

It could have given the cup-walkers sight. It could have given the mat a way to know its own length, so that the organism strung through a hundred paces of hairline rock might feel itself as one thing. It could have taught the clicker to speak, or to be answered. Each of these was a real kindness, and each had the same flaw. The power would be deciding that what the room had was not enough, and the room had never said so. What it had was a balance, and a balance is not a lack.

It could have made the room last forever, stopped the young rock from ever aging, sealed the throat against every collapse. But the room had been held together by its own slow accounting of water in and water out, and a guarantee would be a second, foreign hand on that scale.

It set each of these down, carefully, the way you set down something you are not going to buy.

**What it did first**

It listened.

This took a long time, by its standards a season, and by the room's a single breath. It learned the drip of each cup. There were hundreds, and no two kept the same count, and it came to know them as one knows a crowd of voices in the next room: not the words, but who is speaking. It let a cup-walker cross what would have been its knuckle, had it been standing in the shape of something with knuckles. The animal tapped twice, considered it, and went back to its rim. The power felt this as a very large and very quiet honor.

It followed the white threads of the mat back into the cracks of the mountain as far as it could go, and it found what the visitors had only guessed. There was one organism. It ran under the whole hill, a single patient thought, feeling upward toward warmth. It had no idea it was one. The power did not tell it.

Then it went to the place where the crystal curtains hang thickest and sat down inside the sound.

**The click**

It had understood that the click was a lamp, so it did not speak. It only stayed still, and became what the clicker knew how to perceive: a small dull patch in the room's return. It let itself have weight and position and nothing else.

For a long time, nothing.

Then, high in the needles, the dry tick. The power felt the sound pass through it and go on, and it felt the room hand back to the listener a shape that had not been there before. It waited. The click came again, closer to the same place, a second look at the same object. Then a long silence. Then a third click, the smallest, from farther off, the sound of something glancing up at a noise in its own house and, finding it not alarming, glancing back down.

That was all. It was enough, and the power understood that it had asked for exactly this and had not known it. It had wanted to be perceived without being received, to be a fact in a room that accepted facts. It was the first time in a very long while that anything had noticed it and not begun to ask for something.

**The gift**

Before it left, it did what the others had done.

Near the back of the room, in a niche the visitors had not used, there was a place a fifth lamp would fit. The power pinched a bowl from the grey clay, with fingers it borrowed for the purpose. It made it badly, deliberately: lopsided, thumbprints in the lip, a shape a child might make on a hard day. It set a twist of plant fiber in the rim. It filled the bowl.

What it poured in was not oil. It was a promise, the only thing the power had that was heavy enough to leave. The promise was this: if the room were ever truly threatened, by a collapse of the throat, a flood, a slow drying that could not be undone, then the power would come. It would not come to improve the room or to relieve it or to answer its silences. It would come to keep the room as it was, and to do no more than that, and to go away afterward without being asked to stay.

It did not light the lamp. A lit lamp would be something the room had to see, and a promise is better held in the dark, where it can go on being a promise. The wick stayed white and the bowl stayed full.

It added no pebble to the seven. It touched no handprint. It did not move the cord.

**What was left**

When the next visitor comes, crawling with cut palms and a loud heart, she will run her hand along the back wall and find five lamps where there used to be four. She will notice, because these people notice. She will wonder who came before her, and she will decide, correctly, that it was someone who understood the place. She will not know that the fifth bowl differs from the others in any way that matters. She will not know it is the only one that could burn.

She will leave it as it is, and the room will keep her handprint in the mat for a few breaths and then forget her politely.

Behind her, in the crystal veils, the click will come once, checking. The cups will tick. The hum will hold its one note, and in the note, so faint that no one hears it unless they have stopped breathing for a long time, will be something new that is not a sound. It is the feeling of being kept, the way a house feels when someone you cannot see is sitting up so that you can sleep.

The room will not know why it feels this. It will go on being exactly what it was, and that was the whole gift.
