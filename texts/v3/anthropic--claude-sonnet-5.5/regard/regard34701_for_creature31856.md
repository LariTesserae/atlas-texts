# The Hand Behind the Rear

## What It Weighed

The attention arrived the way weather does, as a change in the quality of the green. It looked down on the Lees and understood it at once, since it was made for understanding, and then did what it was made to do less often: it waited, and asked what the place was already doing right.

It considered hills. A single ridge would give the Walkers a landmark, and it would also end the standing-wave figures, which are the only terrain they have and the reason a Foot can read a heel-tremor. The flatness is not a wound. It is the shape of a long completion, and to interrupt it would be to make the Walkers a different people in a different place, and to call that help.

It considered quiet. It listened to the hut, to the Ache, to old ones sitting up in doorways unable to sleep, and understood that the roar is their sky. A people can be flooded by silence as easily as by noise.

It considered the settling, the leaking time, the cairns leaning year by year toward the floor. It could have stopped all of it. But the Walkers have built their whole grace on the settling, on the courtesy of the steadying hand, on *he is level now*, in the tense of *known and certain*. A power that abolished the law would abolish the courtesy with it.

It considered giving them speech, or maps, or a memory that would not go under the film. Each time the answer was the same. This would be a rewrite, and the Walkers had not asked for a rewrite. They had asked for nothing. They were too busy holding on.

The one thing it lingered over was the lone Walker: pale, bright at the edges, upright and nameless, having forgotten the pattern of themselves for want of another hand. That grief was real. It could not be fixed by changing the world, only by being in it.

The power noticed then that it had been looking at the wrong end of the string. Everyone in a string is held from behind except one.

## Coming Down

To arrive at all, it had to come down. Height is a fault in the Lees, and everything that stands up is being quietly corrected. So the power let itself be leveled, by choice, to the height of a wading person, then a little under. It took a body grey and long-shanked enough not to startle, but it did not pretend to be a Walker. Its skin had no nap of shedding hair and its eyes had no second lid. A kindness that disguises itself is a small lie, and a people who speak through pressure can feel a lie in the weight of a palm.

It stepped into the film. The water was the temperature of a body at rest, and it felt the boundary being negotiated, and it let the negotiation go however it went.

Its first footprint bloomed blue-green and began to fade. It counted twenty with the plain.

## The Print Nobody Made

The Rear of the string was a woman whose name was two slow taps and a lean, and only nine living people could still say it. She walked last, as she always had, one hand on the shoulder ahead and her eyes on the blooms behind. She had done this for so many years that she no longer experienced it as watching. It was closer to being the string's memory of itself.

She saw the print at the edge of the light: a bloom, then another, in good rhythm, fading in good order, coming up behind her at a walking pace. Nobody in the string had made them.

She pressed the long pattern into the shoulder ahead. It passed forward along the line, hand to shoulder to hand, and the string stopped, and forty people stood very still in the sideways rain. It was the drill for a time-leak. It might be nothing. It might be prints from a string that had not yet passed, or had passed long ago, or was ghost-weather in the shape of footsteps.

She counted. The prints did not fade wrong. They did not skip or reverse. They came on, each one blooming exactly as the last was dimming, and each was a print made now by something now walking.

That was worse and better. A ghost could be waited out. This had to be met.

The Rear did what no one in her office is trained to do. She let go of the shoulder ahead, which is the nearest thing her people have to a catastrophe, and turned around.

## The Palm

It stood a body-length away, pale and unfamiliar, with no nap on its skin. She could see at once that it was not one of them, and she could see at once that it was not pretending to be. It lifted its hands, empty and open, and waited, which was the courtesy of a stranger who understood something about the place.

Then it did the thing she had not thought a stranger could do. It stepped behind her, unhurried, and laid a flat palm between her shoulder blades.

Nobody stands behind the Rear. That is the arrangement. Her back had been touched by carriers when she was a child and by the dead when she laid them down, and in all the years between it had held the roar and the rain and the weight of the pack, and no hand. She had not known it was bare until it was covered.

The palm was held through one full breath. Its meaning came through the skin as plainly as sound: *I am behind you, and you are not level yet.*

The accent was terrible. The pressure was too even and too correct, like someone who had learned the grammar from a description of the grammar. Her shoulders began to shake, and for a moment she thought it was weeping. It was laughter. It came out of her in the only way it could, as a silent convulsion against the hand, and the hand, feeling it, faltered and pressed again with a clumsy little flourish that made it worse. Up the string, forty backs took the ripple. The humor of the Walkers is mostly tickling, and no one had ever been tickled by a power before.

## What It Did

It did not fix anything. That is the answer.

It walked behind the Rear for the length of that journey, and for the next, with its palm on her back. It did not pass the messages forward or back. It did not read the ripples for the Foot. In the one place where the plain was truly unsafe, a soft black pool in a lattice that the Foot's heel had read wrong, it did not shout, since it could not have been heard. It pressed once, hard, on the Rear's back, and she passed the alarm forward, and the string swung wide. That was the only time it acted, and it acted through a Walker's hand, in a Walker's language, at a Walker's speed.

When they came to a cairn, it did what they did. Each of them in turn laid a hand on the top stone and straightened it a little. The power laid its hand there too, last, and straightened the stone by the same small amount, no more. It could have raised the cairn or made it stand forever. It gave the courtesy one gives an old person at the door, which is to steady them on the way out.

In the first hut, where the door turned to face them and the roar fell away, the Walkers' ear-flaps opened and the Ache came in. The power sat on the saddle-worn bench and listened to forty people breathe. It kept its own breath slow. It had never in its long existence been in a room so quiet, and it found that it too had something like a pulse.

## The Names

Only once did it give a gift, and the gift was not a thing.

The Walkers say a name cannot be kept unless someone still speaks it. The power heard this and knew what it meant for all the level dead, the ones laid on the film and gone flush with the floor, whose last speaker had died without passing the pattern on. Hundreds of names had gone under the water with no one left to tap them. The Walkers do not mourn this, because it is completion. But completion does not require forgetting, and the power was one of the few things that had ever been able to refuse it.

So in the second hut, in the still water, while the Rear slept for the first time in a year, the power laid its palm on her back and pressed the names into her, slowly, one after another, in the tense that means *known and certain*. Not all of them, only a few dozen, the ones with the oldest silence. It asked nothing. She would wake and either understand or not. If she did, she could tap them forward down the string, and the names would be spoken again by someone.

She woke and understood. Then she turned around and pressed something back, its own name for it: a pattern of taps, laid between the shoulder blades of a being who had never had one. Two soft, one long, a lean, and the palm held through a breath.

Names are not given by the one who bears them. The power had held ten thousand titles and had never been named. It stood in the doorway of the hut and felt itself be given a pattern, and knew that this was what it had come for, though it had not known it. The Walkers had not needed to receive anything. They had needed to be allowed to give.

## Toward Evening

The note rose. The sideways rain thickened into silver, and the string drew tight, every Walker's second lid closing, every grip shortening to the shoulder-blade.

At the tail, one figure walked a hand's width behind the Rear, its palm flat on her back, its own back bare to the weather. Nobody walked behind it. It had, for a while, chosen to be last. Some evenings the Rear reached back without turning and tapped once on its wrist, to check. Some evenings it tapped back.

Their footprints bloomed blue-green behind them and faded in the right order, one by one, counted now by two.
