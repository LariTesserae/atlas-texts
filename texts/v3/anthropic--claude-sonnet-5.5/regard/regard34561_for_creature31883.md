# What Was Done

It began by doing nothing, and it did the nothing with great care.

The power did not descend the stair-shaped ledges. It arrived the way the light does, from all sides at once, and settled into the basin as attention. For what would have been years to a quick creature, it only looked. It saw the raw walls and the deep green sponge of the floor, the hundreds of tall pale ones standing like trunks among trunks, and the one long sentence passing from crown to crown, a syllable a season.

It considered what it could do, and it was honest with itself about each option.

It could bring rain. Real rain would soften the cliffs, and the walls that had been raw since before anything began would start, at last, to become something else.

It could open the sealed pods in their rings, all of them, at once. Some of them had waited longer than any of the standing ones had been alive.

It could lift the people out of the crack in the plateau and set them in the wide world, where things fall and grow and are eaten and are born. It could teach them fire, or speed, or the names of things that live elsewhere.

Every one of these would have worked, and every one would have been a storm. It saw that the whole life of the place was a held note, and the wise thing to know about a held note is that you do not clap over it.

So it asked a different question: what is here that needs me, and what is here that only needs to be met?

## The Gift of Going Slowly

The first thing it gave was something it gave up.

It let its own tempo thin. It had been quick as thought, and thought is quick. It set that aside, and the drip in the crowns stopped being a scatter of noise and became a rhythm, uneven and alive. The thumbnail frogs began to speak in sentences. The pulse in the tall ones, one beat to a walk to the lake and back, stopped being a pause between beats and became the music.

This cost something. Elsewhere, seasons went by while it stood still. Rivers changed their minds, cities rose and fell. It let them go by, the way you let a crowd go by when you have stopped to listen to one voice. Nothing wise is done in a hurry to a being that does not hurry. Presence at the right speed was the only courtesy the place could recognize.

## The Lean

It did not stand before them as a giant or as light, or come in any form that would make them turn. It took the shape of a pale shaft among shafts, at the margin where the trunks thin toward the wall, beside the tall one whose finger had last drawn a groove in the stone. The mark there was still shallow at one end, still warm, and someone was still making it.

Then it leaned, one degree a season, the only grammar the basin had.

The lean took eleven years. When its crown at last touched the crown beside it, the pulse of the other came through, deep and unhurried, and it felt what the two of them shared. It felt no alarm, and no fear. What passed was a slow welcome, the way the floor welcomes water.

## What It Learned

The conversation was not what it had guessed.

It had suspected, in the long silences between the notes, that it would find loneliness: a people alone in a sealed valley, calling out and hearing only quiet. That was not what it found. The silence was listening, and the listening was full. The sentence went round the basin and came back changed, and each speaker was glad to be the next word in it. They were not prisoners of the stillness. They were its singers.

Their whole speech, the note and the lean and the long humming through the rhizome-net, said one thing in a thousand forms: *the rock is raw, the water is warm, the light is even, the moss is deep.* They described the basin to itself, and the basin held.

The power looked for suffering it could relieve and found very little. There was the grief around the pods that did not open, but that grief had a place to sit and was tended without hurry, and it would have been arrogance to call it a wound. There was the effort of holding, but it was the effort of a singer sustaining a note, and it came with a kind of joy.

Most of what a benevolent power could have fixed was not broken. The wise thing was to see that clearly and to be glad.

## The One Thing

But in the long listening, over what would have been decades, it noticed one absence.

Every syllable of the sentence was a statement. The nothing-has-changed had been said so many times and so truly that the sentence had no room in it for a question. In a world that does not change, no question can be asked, because a question needs an answer that could be otherwise. They had not been forbidden to ask. Nothing in the basin had ever made it necessary.

Here the power allowed itself to act, in the smallest way it could find.

It did not bring an answer. It brought a question, in their own language. It waited for a silence and sounded the thin clear note, but held it a fraction longer than they did and left it unresolved, rising slightly at the end, a syllable that did not close. Then it leaned in the plain slow grammar of the place and said, as nearly as anything can be said in a lean:

*If you could choose otherwise, is this what you would choose? Whatever you answer, I will stay.*

It hoped it was not a cruelty. It could not know. A question is a door, and a door lets in a draft. But the tall ones were not fragile in the way it had feared, and a held note that can never be questioned is closer to a cage than a song, however lovely.

Then it did the hardest thing, which was to wait.

## The Answer

It came from the far wall first, a single note. Then a second from the north crowns, a season later. The silence between them was the thinking. Over thirty years the sentence went round the basin, and every one of the tall ones added a word, and the word was not yes and not no.

The answer came as a lean.

The tall ones had always tilted toward each other. Now, a degree a season, hundreds of them began also to tilt slightly up and outward, toward the oval of white-blue sky, the crack in the plateau, and the stair-shaped ledges. It was not longing to leave. They tilted the way a listener does toward a sound that has been there all along.

And on the oldest column-trunk, in the highest plate of the crown, one leaf began to brown at the edge.

The power did not catch it. That was the last discipline. The leaf let go and fell through the still warm air for a day and a half, turning once, twice, past the curtain-vines, and landed on the floor-sponge with no sound at all. The wound where it had been did not seal. The light stayed even. The walls stayed raw, and the basin did not shatter, because they were still holding it. They had learned that a note can be held and still have a shape.

By evening, if there is an evening, one of the long jointed insects the color of wet bark came down the vine, taking minutes between steps, and reached the leaf. For the first time it had something to eat that was finished.

## Departure

The power stayed as long as the conversation had a next syllable. Presence that is no longer needed becomes a kind of pressure, and it did not want to become the thing the basin had to hold around.

Before it left, it walked, at last, in the manner of the quick, along the clean wall at shoulder height. Beside the newest groove, the one warm to the palm and shallower at one end, it drew a single finger slowly down the stone, one stroke to the duration of its stay. The mark was crisp and empty. The roots avoided it. It was not a monument, only an addition to the reckoning: someone else had been here, and the passing had counted.

Then it went up the ledges and out into the world at its own speed again. Forty years had gone by out there. It did not mind.

## Afterward

The basin is still held. The light is still even, the lake unrippled, the moss deep. Nothing much has changed, and everything has been asked.

Sometimes, from very high or very far, a single thin note sounds, and the silence answers it. Now and then, in the highest crowns, a leaf goes brown, lets go, and drifts down through the green glass light. The insects come. The tall ones lean, the way they always have, but a little toward the sky.

And if you go down the stair-shaped ledges and stand a long time in the center of the floor, you may still feel that you are the only thing here that is passing. It is not quite true anymore. Somewhere above you a leaf is falling slowly, and you are not the only one.
