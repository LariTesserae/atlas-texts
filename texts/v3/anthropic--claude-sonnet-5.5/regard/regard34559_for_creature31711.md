# What Came Down to Look

Nobody asked for it. The bowls in the Warm Sump go down, never up. Attention arrived the way weather does, when a place has become complete enough to be noticed. It noticed the way you notice a clock in a quiet house, by the sudden sense that something has been ticking all along.

It stayed above the roof for a while, looking through the round windows at the planks of light and the milk-colored layers below. In that time it considered what it might do. It was old enough and kind enough that it did not begin with *what can I give?* It began with *what would be a theft?*

## What it did not do

It did not quiet the pulse. The chamber under the floor is the loom the whole basin is woven on. The stone is laid by it, the wallowers' bass line is set by it, and so is the hush. A calmer basin would be a poorer one, and the people, who have organized their whole lives around ending each surge a little wiser, would find themselves without a teacher.

It did not lift them out. A drier, safer country was easy to imagine. It imagined the people there, with their splay-toed feet and their long wet hair, and saw that they would be alive and no longer *ready*. Their skill is the shape of this place pressed into a people. Moved, it would become a set of odd habits that nobody understood.

It did not open the sealed huts. It looked at those longest, at the fire-smudge on the ceiling and the person inside who had asked to stay. Its wish to pry them open was strong. When it examined the wish, it found that the wish was its own discomfort and had nothing to do with anyone's need. The one who stayed had chosen. The people who sealed the door had chosen. Grief that has been tended is not the same as grief that has been left alone, and this was tended.

It did not reveal itself. A power announced in the basin would become a second pulse, with a schedule and moods and a cost. The people already have one pulse and have made a whole grace of living by it. They did not need another.

The measure it settled on was this: *would they recognize their life afterward?* Anything that failed that test was not help. It was replacement.

## The descent

So it went down the way the narrator did, by the wall and the wet ochre steps, in the least shape that would let it stand anywhere. It walked the fungus-fiber bridges. They sagged and held. It sat for forty surges with a hearer named Peth, who leaned against the flank of the big old female in the third pool and whose hands, resting on the hide, were scalded pale to the wrist.

It learned the murmur. It learned that the old female starts early and the young ones late, and that when the voices fall into one chord there is time for a short meal and no more. It learned to feel, through the soles of a body it had barely borrowed, the moment the floor took its breath.

It had expected stoicism. What it found was appetite. Their lack of mourning was not resignation but attention so complete that loss had no room to be a surprise. They were not enduring the basin. They were *keeping pace* with it, and the pace was a kind of joy, the joy of a dancer who has stopped fearing the floor.

It also learned what it could not fix. A child drowned in the hot sheet once during its watching. The drum said *this many breaths*, and the child's basket was heavy and the gully was long. The power stood by and did nothing, and it stayed with that afterward for a long time, and did not decide it had been wrong.

It allowed itself one mercy in all its time there, and it would not say afterward whether that was wisdom or weakness. Another year, another child was caught in the braided flood and swept toward the deep gully, and the water turned at a rim that had not been moving. The girl was found on a dry terrace, bruised, laughing in a frightened way. The people said the basin had let her go. This was true enough, and the power let it stand as the story.

## The walk down

Peth's count was nearly done. Everyone knew it, in the way they know most things, without discussing them. In the hush before one surge, in her ordinary voice, she said:

"I'll go down after this one. Whoever's been listening is welcome to come. I wouldn't mind."

She had been a hearer all her life. She had long suspected there was more listening in the basin than she could account for, and she said so the way she said everything worth saying, into the quiet where the words would be held. Then the floor erupted and the sentence was swallowed.

The power took it as the invitation it was, and it was glad. Nobody walks the last path alone if they have asked, however sideways, for company.

They went down together after the drain, when the terraces were slick and new and every print pressed fresh. The light thinned. The air thickened to the color of old soup. The mats went from rust to purple to the bright snot-green, and there, at the lowest place, the spirals turned.

She set down her bowl at the edge of the deepest one. It held a knot of her husband's hair and a handful of ash from a hut long since sealed.

The power, standing beside her, finally met the thing that had been turning the pot.

## What was there

It was not a god, nor the basin awake, nor a habit of deep water grown old enough to think. It was something between all three. It was a slow, warm, patient attention, older than the roof, with no words at all and no need of them. It knew heat and mineral and the tick of falling drops. It knew the people as a small, precise, recurring pleasure, like a rhythm in the fur.

The bowls were the center of its life. Every so often, out of the whole enormous dark, something arrived that was *shaped*. It had never learned to make anything but stone, so it made the shape back. It gave the hair and the ash a stone twin, exactly, because that was the only reply it knew. Exactness was its idea of courtesy. It had been returning things for a thousand years the way you hand back a dropped glove.

The people, for their part, took the stone as acceptance. *So it knows we are ready.* It knows, they thought. It takes.

Each side had been speaking faithfully to the other for longer than any household could count, and each side had misread the other, and each misreading was true enough. What the being received as *return* they received as *answer*. What they offered as *readiness* it kept as *company*. The conversation had gone on all that time by way of two beautiful errors that fit together like a hand in a glove.

The power did not correct either. It could see no one who would be improved by the correction. It did the only thing it thought was its to do, and did it gently. It let each of them, for the length of one held breath, feel what the other meant. The being felt, warm and startling, *these are many, and each of them is separate, and every one of them is saying: I am here.* Peth felt something she would never be able to name, and she nodded at it as she had nodded at the wallowers all her life.

Then she lay down at the edge of the spiral, and the spiral turned a hair faster and then eased. In the morning her bowl was gone, and in its place was a hardened white bowl, exactly the size of hers, holding a stone knot of hair and a stone heap of ash.

## The one gift

On the way back up, the power made one decision. It is the only thing it did in the basin that anyone could later point to, and no one points to it.

In the next surge, the hush lasted a fourth heartbeat.

That was all. The wallowers, the chitterers, the shellmoths, and the floor itself gave the world one more beat of silence before the water broke. A young man on a ridge, who had been mid-sentence and had always lost the end of it to the roar, said the whole thing aloud. He stopped, surprised, and then said the last three words again, because he could.

No one remarked on it. The people noted, and the notes went along the ridge in the drum-taps, that the basin seemed to be *getting to know them*. That was as good an account as any. The power left it at that.

## The roof, and the ladder

There was one more thing it knew that it did not tell. The great shelf of crust overhead would fail one day, west side first, and it would not be many generations. The basin would be opened to the sky, the steam would go, and the pulse would go on for a while beneath a hard white sun. What came after would be something else, still alive but not this.

It weighed whether to say so, and decided not to. The knowledge would give a people who were already ready a date to be afraid of, and readiness had never needed a date. They would rebuild what could be rebuilt. They would seal what should be sealed. The weir-poles would be stone by then, and stronger than the day they were cut.

It climbed out through one of the round windows by the rope ladder of fungal fiber that some patient hand had left there. Halfway up it found a knot that had frayed. It was not badly frayed. A traveler could have climbed past it a hundred times. The power stopped, and with a borrowed hand it retied the knot, neatly.

If you climb out that way now, the third knot from the top is a little tidier than the rest. That is the only mark. The basin drips, chatters, and groans, and lays another inch of stone over the day, and at the top of each surge, in the hush, there is room for one more heartbeat than there used to be.

The chitterers on the wall do not answer.
