# What the Attention Did

It came over the south rim during a silence, which was luck or manners, and it stopped there.

Wise things learn to distrust the first impulse, and its first impulse was large. The bowl was so improbable, so precisely balanced, and so small that the wish to protect it came up in it like a Surge. It could have made the mountain promise to breathe forever. It could have brought rain, or sent water up through the rock, or lifted thirty-one people out of a place where a single bad century could end them and set them somewhere safe and wet and wide.

It considered each of these the way a person considers reaching into a clock.

Rain would ruin the oils, the fruit, and the lacquered leaves, and after the fruit came the Kesh. Safety would end the counting, and the counting was not a hardship laid over their lives. It was their attention, and the shape of their love for one another. The Kesh spoke in gaps because the world had gaps, and a life with no gaps would have nothing to speak in. The mountain's promise would be a lie, because mountains do not promise. And it saw that the unopened pot of water had lasted all these generations precisely because no one knew what it was for. Any answer it gave would spend that.

So it did what the Kesh do first. It watched, and it counted.

## The First Days

It learned the drone's period, and then the haze that foretells a Surge. It learned to move only in the noise. It was not good at this at first, being a thing that had never had to stop midstride. In the third silence it was caught with its weight badly placed, and a Kesh child saw it from a cranny of pipe-reeds, eyes shining, and said nothing at all. That was kindness. The child was watching to see whether it would hold still. It held still.

It did not disturb the stilt-mothers. It did not sleep near a vent, though it could have. It fed itself nothing, since it needed nothing, and this troubled it slightly, as though it were failing a courtesy.

On the eleventh Exhale it sat down on a flat stone at the south rim, and when it looked again there was a small clay cup of red fruit-water beside it, cool and faintly bitter, and no one near.

It drank all of it. It did not say thank you, because there was no one to say it to, and because thanks aloud would have been noise. It set the cup back down in the same place, upside down, which is what the Kesh do to say *received*. It had learned this by watching them for eleven breaths, and the gesture was correct.

## What It Chose to Do

It did small things, in the manner of the place.

**It patched the roof.** One night, in the gaps, it laid a single leathery leaf on the lean-to, and laid it badly. It overlapped the wrong way and would leak, if anything here leaked. The next morning the Keeper found it, held it in the honey light for a long moment, and then laid her own patches around it. The lean-to's roof is a record of two kinds of hands, and now, at one corner, of a third. Nobody remarked on it. That was the whole gift.

**It pressed a hand to the wall.** It knelt in the place where the air pools and pressed a flat palm into wet ochre below the child-height marks, in the row of the naming-hands, though nobody had named it. The print is a little too wide and slightly too even. The Kesh who found it studied it for a long time. It is still there, and no one has washed it out or painted over it, though ochre can be scraped.

**It left the cairn alone.** After a Surge, the black glass stones lay toppled, and a girl of perhaps nine began to rebuild them. She left a gap. The power stood at the edge of the vent and listened to the hum of what she made, a little crooked, groaning low with a hollow in the middle where the gas sang through. It found that it would not have changed a single stone. This was the closest it came to being moved.

## The Answer

In the last silence of its stay, the nearest of the standing figures said one word. It was a word and a half, really, a breath and a fragment, and then the drone returned and cut it off. The power understood that it was a question, one carried across a day, waiting for someone to answer it after three Exhales.

Finishing another person's sentence is rude. Cutting a silence short is worse. So it did neither. It waited through the first Exhale, and the second, and the third. In the fourth silence it said its answer into the gap, a single syllable, in the hum of the pipe-reeds, so that it was not quite a voice.

What the question was and what the answer was, the Kesh have not told, and I will not either. It is enough that the answer came in the right place, at the right length, and did not go on.

## The Name

Before it left, an elder walked it through the reeds. The power had not asked, but it had been noticed, and at the end of the noticing came the oldest courtesy the Kesh have. They walked a stranger among the stands until one chord made whatever the stranger had for a chest begin to hum. It was a low stand near the western wall, off-true, with a slit a little too wide.

They gave it a whistle cut from that reed. Its name cannot be said in any lowland tongue, and it changes with the gas and the season, so the power carries it as a thing that is never quite the same twice. This is the one thing it took away, and it counts it as the larger gift of the two.

What it gave in return, if that is the word, was not a promise about the mountain. The mountain does not promise, and it would not pretend otherwise. It gave a smaller thing. If the mountain ever stops breathing, and the Keeper wakes to find the pot's quiet weight has become an instruction, and someone stands in the sudden silence with a wax seal and no idea whether it is for weeping or for drinking, then someone with a reed whistle voiced like a stand by the western wall can be called. Whoever it is will come and sit in the silence and not tell anyone what to do.

That is all. It is a strange thing to leave. But some help is only help if it can wait to be asked.

## Afterward

The Kesh did not change. They still move in the noise and freeze in the gaps, and the drone still rises and falls. Stilt-mothers still drum the alarm on the eastern terrace, and the gliders still pour like sparks into the amber haze. Perhaps the thirty-one are thirty-three, or thirty-two.

At the south rim, in the lean-to, one corner of the roof has a leaf laid the wrong way. Beneath it, on the wall, is a hand a little too wide. And in the last silence of some evening, when the drone drops and every Kesh stops where they are, there is one more note in the quiet than there used to be, humming very low, from somewhere among the reeds by the western wall.

Then the mountain breathes in, and all of them are gone.
