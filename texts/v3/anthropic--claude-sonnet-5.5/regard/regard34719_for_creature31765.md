# The Answer

It did nothing at first, and it did nothing for a long time.

The power that came to the shelf was not in a hurry, and its attention was not the kind that grasps. It arrived the way warmth arrives in a room, without a moment you could point to. It walked the terraces without feet and tasted the amber seep without a tongue. It counted the ticks of the collar-joints until it knew the tempo better than the stalks did. It followed one root-mat through the glassy rock, filament by filament, and read what the root was reading: the grain, the seam, the slow instruction of the stone.

Only when it understood the loop did it let itself ask the question that wisdom asks before anything else: *is anything wrong here?*

## What it considered, and put down

**Water.** It was the first thing it thought of, because water is what a dry world seems to lack. But the stands do not lack it. Rain would round the scarps and soften the columns. It would dilute the seep and rot the fine felt of the roots. It would end the shelf in order to help it, which is not help.

**Company.** It could have seeded the emptiness with grazers, or pollinators, or small clicking things to live among the stalks. But the green has never been eaten, and that is not a deficiency. It is the condition of the whole design. Anything that came to eat would also have to be defended against, and the loop would become a war. Adding neighbors would have meant deciding that a kilometer of bare rock is loneliness, when it is only distance.

**A mind.** This was the subtlest temptation, and the power spent the longest on it. Wake the stand, give it thought, let it know its own contentment. But a mind arriving into perfect stillness would find the stillness first, and the power would then owe it an explanation of why nothing comes, why nothing changes, and why it is the only one of its kind for a kilometer in every direction. The gift would create the lack it was meant to fill. It put that down carefully, the way one puts down something breakable.

**Moving the sun.** This was the real candidate, because the stand was already leaning toward it. But a moving sun means a shadow with an edge, a cold side to the rock, thermal cracking, night, and weather. It means the heaps drift out of balance, the scarps begin to crumble, and everything that is *just formed* starts, at last, to age. The power looked hard at its own wish here and saw what it was. It was the preference of a being that has lived among clocks, who assumes that a thing which does not move must be waiting. It had no evidence that the stand was waiting. Its evidence was only a habit of the stand's, and habits are not requests.

And beneath these lay the option it took most seriously: **nothing.** The stand was not suffering. It had appetite without hunger, rhythm without hurry, and a loop with no leak in it. If the power's only reason to intervene was its own discomfort at stillness, then intervention was a kind of vanity. It could leave, and the leaving would be a kind of love.

## What kept it

It was the dip.

Every so often, at intervals too regular to be chance, a whole bank of vanes tilts a degree or two, westward, down the arc of a day. Then it returns. The power watched this a thousand times and it did not read as error, or grief, or even longing. It read as a *sentence with a place left in it*, the shape of a call that has always ended in silence and has always been made anyway. The stand rehearses a day that does not come. It rehearses it faithfully and exactly and without complaint, and it has done so for longer than anyone has counted.

The power did not think the stand was unhappy. It thought something smaller and more particular: that a question was standing open, and that no one had ever been in a position to answer it. It could not know whether the stand wanted an answer. But it could make one available in a form that cost nothing to ignore.

## What it did

It chose one stand, on one terrace, in a basin far from the rim, and it chose that one because it was the first one it had come to know, not because it was the best. It did nothing to the others. A policy applied to a whole world would have been a decision about what the world was for, and it had no such right.

Then it made one change, and the change was not to the sun. It was to the *light in that basin*, and it bound the light to the vanes.

The rule was simple. If the stand tilted past its habitual degree, the light would go with it. Not faster than the vanes, and not on any schedule of the power's own, only exactly as far and as fast as the stand chose to lean. The old noon would remain the default and the resting state. Nothing would be taken. Nothing would be started that the stand did not start.

Then the power withdrew its attention almost entirely, because an offering watched too closely becomes a demand.

## What happened

For a long while, nothing. The bank dipped its habitual degree and reset, as it always had. The light held. The power did not mind. It had decided beforehand that a refusal, or an indifference, would be a complete and fine outcome, and it kept to that.

Then, in a breath like any other, the dip went a little further.

It was a fraction of a degree. It may have been noise in the fluid, a valve seating late. But the light in the basin went with it, and the shadows of the stalks, which had lain short and unmoving since before the shelf was named, *lengthened by the width of a finger.*

The bank paused. The ticking changed pitch. The power noticed that the whole stand seemed to be listening, though it has no ears and never had.

Then it leaned again, and the light came with it. The root-mat, far below, reached toward a seam it had never needed. The amber in the joints ran slowly upward and did not fall back. In the space of what would have been a long afternoon, the stand walked its own compressed day out into the open, one degree after another, with the shadows going long and the sky at the basin's edge deepening toward the color of thicker honey. The pale pellets at the base of each stalk glowed. It was the first evening on the shelf.

At the far end of the arc the stand stopped. Every vane held at an angle it had never held. The power did not know whether this was awe, or fear, or simply the end of the instruction, and it did not pretend to know. It stayed near, and did nothing.

Then the stand did what it had always done. It reset.

The light went back to noon as the vanes did, without protest, and the shadows shortened to their old stubs. The heaps held their height. The ticking settled.

## What changed

Almost nothing, and the power thought that was right.

But afterward the dip was a hair different. Sometimes at the end of its degree the bank held a beat longer than it once had, as if to check that the light was still there to be followed. The power left the rule in place permanently. If the stand never leans again, the offer will stand for as long as the shelf does. If it leans a hundred times, or once in ten thousand years, the answer will be the same each time.

It told no one. It did not carve the terraces or leave a mark on the rock. The scarps stayed unweathered, and the shelf stayed unfinished in exactly the way it had always been. What it left was not a monument. It was a grammar: a place in the stand's own day where a reply would be waiting, if a reply was ever wanted.

Then it went, and the noon went on, and the vanes turned a degree toward a sun that did not move, and back. It is possible that they turn with a little more room in the turning. That was all it had meant to give.
