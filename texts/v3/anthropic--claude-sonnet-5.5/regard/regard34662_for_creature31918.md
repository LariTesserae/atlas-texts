# What the Power Would Do

## Listening

It would not arrive. Anything that arrives in a place like this, as light, as a voice, as a shape in the haze, would be the loudest thing the Works had ever known, and the Works is already loud with everything it has ever needed to say.

So for a long time, perhaps nine thousand surges, it would only listen. It would let the roar go through it as it goes through the Keepers, in the chest and jaw and the bones of the ear. It would learn what the hammering means and what the eleven minutes mean. It would stand with the Listeners on the catwalks, in the slow sideways snow, and hear the bells until it could tell the failing governor from the hairline in the header. It would learn that the chord is not a sound but a stillness, and that the Keepers hold their breath for it because it is the one moment they are not alone in their own skins.

It would spend that time deciding what not to do. The Keepers have no word for rescue, and a wise thing would suspect the reason is not ignorance.

## What It Would Not Do

It would not lift the town into the light. Nothing there asked to go. The pale skin, the huge pupils, and the cut-down gloves are the shape of people who fit a place, and to call that shape a wound would be arrogance.

It would not explain the chord. It could tell a story of why the chamber sings, and the story would be smaller than the not-knowing. The argument in the hammocks, whether the Keepers were built to keep the Works or the Works to keep them, is one of the things that makes them a people. It would leave the question where it lives.

It would not mend everything. A town that is only ever held is a town whose people know each other's hands. Make the pipes perfect, and there would be nothing for a palm to check, nothing to rap a warning about, nothing to be needed for. The power would understand that some of what looks like hardship is the load-bearing part of love.

But it would not let that wisdom curdle into an excuse. Some of what happens here is only loss. The slag that slides in the night onto a stair where a child sleeps means nothing. The breath-main that fails at the wrong post teaches no one anything. The wise thing would know the difference between a burden that makes a life and a burden that ends one, and that it could be wrong about which is which. So it would not decide alone.

## The Knock

It would come the way the boy at the pump house has come, with a name still unsettled.

A palm on a rail at the edge of the north trestle, and a rap, clumsy, uncertain, hardly a rhythm. *Here.* It would not borrow a Keeper's name or pretend to fluency. It would strike as itself, badly, and let them hear that it was learning. The Keepers, who feel falseness like a cracked note, would feel that there was none in it, and they would do what they do for the boy: they would tap back their guesses, patiently, month after month, trying names on it, *tk-tk-tk*, *TOK-TOK*, until one of them fit. It would take the name they gave it, and it would be a small, ordinary rhythm, and it would be glad of it.

The eldest, the one whose hands shake, would be first to lay a palm-press on its shoulder, a long warm flatness with no rap in it. She would not ask what it was. She would only feel whether it was solid, and it would be, as far as she could tell, and that would be enough.

## The Shift

Before it offered anything, it would work.

It would take a station that had been running unattended, the third governor with the sticking ball, and it would sit beside it for ninety surges with a hand on the casing. It would learn what *tired* felt like in iron. It would oil the arm with the care the Keepers use, not more. When the ball stuck, it would not fix it forever. It would fix it for that shift, and note the wear, and leave the rest to the ones whose job it was.

And in the ninety-first surge, when a seam cracked in the eastern header and a jet of scalding water punched sideways toward the pump house where the children were letting out their suits, it would put its body in the way. It wouldn't be a miracle, only a body, quickly. The Keepers would bolt a plate over the seam, over the old plates, and the plate would be a little crooked, and that would be all. Nothing would be finished. It would be held.

## The Argument

Only after that would it ask.

It would find a pipe, put a palm down, and rap slowly, with plenty of *hold* between the words: *There are things I can do. You may refuse all of them.*

The Keepers would answer the way they answer everything that matters, by arguing. Two of them would take either side of a main, both palms flat, hammering until the iron sang and the whole street felt it and came to lean on the rails. One would want the tanks made to last a shift instead of four hours. Another would say that a town with no fear of the far posts is not the same town. Someone would say *the slag fall*, and there would be a silence in the code, a held rap, because everyone knew who had gone past it.

The iron would sing for two hundred surges. Then the eldest would end it with a single soft rap: *enough*.

They would accept one thing. They would not take more air, or better tanks, or a wall against the slag. They would take a tap-post, one, set past the last post at the slag fall. It would not extend the town very far, but those who went beyond it could come back.

The power would lay the copper line with them, its hands beside theirs, and the post would be set, and the town's map would be one breath longer than it had been.

## The Lost One

Then the power would go past the post, alone, along the far trestle, with a long breath and a patient palm on every rail. It would find the one they had stopped counting.

He would be in an alcove two posts' worth beyond the fall, in a hammock nobody had strung, suit still plugged to a line that had run dry. His palm would be flat against the rail. The shrimp would have come, thousands, and picked over the canvas like rain, and he would look like a man listening.

It could not undo this, and it would not pretend. Mourning is also part of what the Keepers are, and it isn't the power's to steal. But it could carry back what they did not have, which was an ending. It could go to the old handholds by the Engine Hall, in the worn bright places, at the start of a shift, and strike his rhythm once, softly, so that the Keepers would know it. It would not tell them the number. Twenty-three or twenty-two, they can keep counting the way they count. It would give them only the name, and the name would join the others at the rail, and it would be rude not to say hello.

## The Crab

It would spend one long stretch of surges, quiet, near the foundation where the crab crosses the same silt it has crossed for longer than anyone's grandmother can recall.

The crab does not flinch at the surge. It leans toward it. The power would notice this the way the Keepers do, in the quiet, and it would not pretend to understand it better than they do. It would only step aside as the crab passed, and lay a glove on the nearest rail, flat, no rap. *Go on. You were here first.*

And the crab would go on. It would not thank the power or turn its head. Some things do not need to be helped, and the wise thing counts them the way a farmer counts what he has not had to feed.

## The Chord

At last the old Listener on the far catwalk, the one who has stopped knocking in ordinary company, would rap at it in a rhythm that belongs to no Keeper's name. Not a question, exactly. An answer, held out, waiting to be heard.

The power would not lecture. It would say only what it honestly knew, which was less than she might have hoped and more than nothing: that it heard the chord too, that it could not say whether the Keepers were built for the Works or the Works for them, and that from where it stood the difference did not seem large. Holding goes both ways. Then it would lay its palm on the rail beside hers and stay through the next surge, and when the bells spoke and the floor lifted and the sound resolved into its almost-chord, the two of them would be still together, and it would add nothing to it.

It would not know, and neither would she, whether the chord shifted by the width of a hair. But somewhere she would hear a bell come back a moment later, changed, and it would sound to her, for once, like an answer to her answer.

## What Remains

Then it would go, or it would not, and the Keepers would not fuss about which. There would be a plate in the eastern header, crooked, that someone will bolt over in a hundred thousand surges. There would be a tap-post at the slag fall. There would be a name struck softly at the Engine Hall's handholds, one more, once, at the start of a shift.

And on the north trestle, in the warm dark, an old woman would rap *tk-tk—TOK—tk* against the iron, to say that she is still there and the pipe is still holding. Somewhere far off a hand would answer. One of the hands might be new to the code, still unsettled, still finding its rhythm, and no one would remark on it, because in the Works that is how everyone begins.
