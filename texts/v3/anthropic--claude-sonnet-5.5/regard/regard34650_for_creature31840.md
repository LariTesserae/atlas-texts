# What Came to the Shelf

It came in as dark. The first thing it understood was that light would be the wrong gift.

It had light. It could have burned the dust away in an afternoon and shown the Shelf a sky. It could have warmed the whole plateau, quieted the lightning, put water in every hollow. It considered each of these for a long while, the way you consider a door you know you won't open, and it saw what each would cost. The furnace-backs' embers would go pale under a real sun. The lichen coins would vanish. The moths, who steer by the small lights of others, would scatter. The smelters would lose the reason for their fires. A thousand things on the Shelf had built their own light because none was given, and that building was what the place *was*. To fix its darkness would be to erase the ones who had answered it.

So it made itself small and dim and slow, and it walked.

## The first thing it learned

It learned to wait an hour for its eyes, as the animals do. Then the ground came up in blue-green coins, and the horizon showed forty red points, and it understood that it was standing in a conversation already in progress. The towers brightened and faded in their loose order. The ground hummed under it. It did not know the language, and it decided not to pretend to.

It had come with a list of griefs. There was the strider, who kills. There were the old cows, whose fires go red and low. There was the fern ring browning from the outside in, and the calf wobbling after a heat it hasn't earned yet. It went through the list honestly, and each item argued back.

The strider's hunger is what the smelters' columns are for, and the ash-beetles', and the glass-gatherers' scavenged bones. Take the killing out and the whole slow economy of plates and pellets and rung walls unravels into something that only looks kinder. The old cows die, and the calves are born into their steam. The power was not sure it could unpick one thread without the whole weave going slack. It was not sure enough to try. A wise power, it thought, is one that knows the size of its own uncertainty and lets that size decide how far it reaches.

But it did not want to be a coward and call it wisdom. So it held the question open all the way through, and it set itself a rule: *do only what the world might have done by itself, and only where the world's own ways had no path through.* The rule was crude, and the power knew it. It was a way of keeping its hand light.

## The vent

A tremor came in the third week, the ordinary kind. A vent in the lower ground shut like a mouth, and the ring of pale ferns began to brown at its edge. The beetles pulled inward. The leaf-thin singers went on clicking their dialect to one another, having no reason to believe the world extended past the green.

The power stood at the edge of the ring for a long time. It could have held the vent open, but a vent that held forever would be a lie about how vents are. Then it noticed that the earth was already trying. A hundred meters off, along the same old fault, the rock was fractured and warm and only a few centuries from breaking through. Centuries were nothing to the earth and everything to a ring of singers.

It did the small thing. It let the fault give a little early, a seep of warm air opening in the fracture, no louder than a sigh, the kind of thing the Shelf does a thousand times a year. The fog began to gather over it by morning. It did not carry the singers there or make them a road. Whether any would find their way across a hundred meters of open dark, beetles first, then the singers over many nights, it left to them.

It never learned whether they did. It took that not-knowing as part of the price, and it was surprised to find how easily it paid.

## The strider

The one it lingered with was the strider.

It had watched one follow a herd for six days, sleeping when they slept, a hundred meters off in the dusk. It watched the strike, the gutter of one fire going out, and then the long turning in the dark afterward, the blades folded, the head moving slowly from side to side. The account the power had absorbed said: *It is impossible to tell from the outside, and there is no one to ask.*

It came and stood at the edge of the strider's listening. It did not speak. It made no gesture that could be mistaken for a claim on the animal. It simply became, for a few hours, a presence in that circle of dark that wanted nothing, took nothing, and did not flinch from the body.

The strider did not look at it, and if it registered anything it did not show it. But at some point the head stopped its slow turning. The blades unfolded a little. Something went out of the long vigil. It was not grief resolved, since the power could not tell if there had been grief. It was more as if the watch had been shared, and for the first time in the animal's life the dark it was guarding was not entirely its own to guard.

Then the scavengers came, and the strider fed, and the power withdrew. That was all. It felt that it had done something, though it could not have said what, and it felt no need to.

## The glass-gatherer

It was the curious one that it dared the most with, and dared least.

A small dun figure was working a scorched patch after a storm, tapping and listening, tapping and listening. It found a tube. It lifted it, blew across the end, and the tube gave the ordinary thin note. It set the tube down and went on.

The power took one tube from the black sand, unremarkable to any eye, and altered nothing about it but the note. When the gatherer found it and blew, it sang a low, unhurried tone, the note the reeds make in the gully by the rail. The gatherer stopped. It blew again. It held the tube to its ear as if to hear where the sound had come from, and then, very slowly, it turned its whole body toward the equatorial edge, as if someone had said a name.

It did not go, not that night. But it kept the tube, and laid it along the wall of its burrow apart from the others, in a place of its own. The power thought that was enough. It had given no answer, only a better question, and the animal was one of the few on the Shelf built to carry one.

## The rail

Last, it went to the gully and sat by the bar of dark metal.

It knew, as soon as it arrived, what the animals had never been able to say: the rail was not a rib and not a rail. It was the trace of an earlier visit, or of something like the power itself that had come, looked at the Shelf, and chosen to stay very still. That was why the paths bent and the moths thinned and the reeds sang low. The metal wasn't a warning. It was a kind of attention that had learned to be quiet and never left.

The power did not touch it. It thought of taking it away, or of leaving something beside it, and it did neither. It sat in the gravel as the fog came down the gully at walking pace, and the reeds around it dropped a little lower for a while, in a note that might have been welcome and might have been the wind.

## What it left

When it went, the Shelf was almost exactly as it had been. The furnace-backs chuffed across the swells. The towers glowed. The sky cracked open every few minutes and showed the whole plateau for three seconds, the herds and the towers and the rail.

A seep breathed among the ferns, and it was not clear yet whether anyone would walk to it. A strider went on following its herd, perhaps a little less alone. In a burrow in a gully bank, a small animal lay awake with a tube that sang the note of a place it had never been, and it was thinking, as much as it could think, about going.

The power had changed almost nothing, and it counted that as the work done well. It had not made the Shelf better. It had been a guest, and a guest that leaves a place unharmed and a little more attended to has done a good deal.

It carried away one thing: the knowledge that the dark had been full. In a very large dark, each thing was a little heat and a little light. The power had wanted to help, and it found the wanting had turned into something quieter than help, closer to regard.

Behind it the sky cracked, and for three seconds it saw them all at once, working, and it did not look back for longer than that.
