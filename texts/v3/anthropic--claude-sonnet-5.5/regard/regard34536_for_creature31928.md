# What the Lid Was Shown

The power did not arrive. It came down the way people do, by the rope ladder at the western scarp, one rung at a time, because it had considered the other ways and judged them rude. Something that appears in the middle of a place asserts that the place was waiting for it. Something that climbs down asserts only that it was curious.

The crickets lifted and resettled around its feet. It stood in a column of grey light with the din pressing on both sides, and for a long while it did nothing at all.

## The First Temptations

It saw at once what it could do, and it laid the possibilities out as a person spreads tools on a cloth.

It could make the place easier. It could lift the dusk, quiet the noise, and open the skylights wider so that the ferns would spread past their rings. It could wear the cliffs smooth for the gatherers and turn the guano cones into something less ammoniac. Every one of these was a kindness by some measure, and the power set each aside for the same reason. The Sump was not a collection of things that could be improved one at a time. It was one thing, built out of slopes. Every creature sat on a gradient that rolled it back to its place, and a hand laid on any one of them, even a gentle hand, would be a hand laid on the whole. Its balance was the only thing the trough had made for itself, and the power would not take that apart to be helpful.

It could make the place safe. It looked at the roof for some time, at the shelf of lava crust hanging tens of paces thick over the dark, and it knew almost to the century how long the shelf would hold. It could hold it longer. It could hold it until the plateau itself wore down to nothing. But a completed machine that requires an eternal prop is a different thing from one that runs on its own account, and the power thought the trough's wholeness was worth more than its duration. Rock is patient, and rock does fall. The bats would find another roof, or they would not, and either way the circulation had never promised to be forever. It had only promised to be, and it was.

So the power did not touch the roof, the fault, the scrub, or the count of the owls.

## The Question That Remained

The final section of any account of this place says that something is alive here that is not any single creature: a circulation with a clock, a gut, and a breath, and no face. It says the thing does not know it exists.

The power lingered on that sentence longer than on any other. It could not tell whether the sentence was a description or a verdict.

It is easy to say that a being unaware of itself lacks nothing, and easy to say that it lacks the one thing that would make it a being. The power distrusted both. Whoever said the trough had *finished becoming itself* was a visitor who stood in the light for an hour, and the power had learned to distrust conclusions about the inner lives of things that have no way to correct you. The trough could not be asked whether it wanted to be woken. It could not say yes, and it could not say no. Anything the power did in the direction of waking would be done to a sleeper who never agreed to it, and could not be undone if unwanted. The blind salamanders in the pools have never missed color, and a gift of sight would have been a wound.

So it would not give the trough a face, a voice, or a mind of its own, and it would not hand it a self like a crown. It would not answer a question the place had never asked.

But there was one thing it could give that required no answer, could not be kept, and could not do harm if it was not wanted. It could attend.

## The Listening

It sat down on a cold block at the edge of a light shaft, where the braid of wings went up on one side and came down on the other, and it listened. It did not listen as the gatherers do, with their teeth clenched against the noise, or as the visitor does, sorting it into animals. It listened to the whole as one sound. It followed the chirr of the swiftlets into the rustle of the bats, and the rustle into the rasp of the crickets, and the rasp into the slow ticking of the drips, each in its own worn note. It listened until it could hear the seams between the sounds where one rhythm handed to the next, and it kept listening past that, until it was attending to no creature and no tier. It attended to the circulation itself, the thing that carried moths up into bellies and bellies down into mounds and mounds out into ferns.

Nothing had ever done this. The trough had been watched by owls and eaten by centipedes and counted by no one. It had been gathered from, prayed over perhaps, and feared a little. In all the ages since the slab dropped, it had never once been *heard*.

The power held that attention steady and asked for nothing.

And the trough, which had no face, did something that was not quite a response. For the length of a single breath, the tiers of the roost passed each other in the shaft at exactly the same instant. The drips in their thousand hollows fell together, so that one clear note rang from the whole underside of the lid. The wingbeat fabric rose to a single pitch, and the crickets in the mound went still. For that breath the country sounded like one chord, and the chord had the shape of a held question.

Then it broke, gently, into its rota, the drips scattered, and the crickets resumed. The trough went on being itself.

The power did not know whether it had witnessed a waking or a coincidence, and it decided not to know. It did not ask again. The offer had been made once, in the only currency the place could use, and the place had used it or not. That was the place's business.

## What It Left

It left no gift that would need tending. There was nothing to become a dependence, and no change the next season would have to absorb.

At the wall where the red ochre handprints cluster beside the easy digging, it laid its own palm flat against the basalt. The stone sweated under it. When the power took its hand away, the print stood there as a bloom of beaded damp, and by the time the shaft of light had turned a hand's width across the floor, the beads had run together and dripped down, and the wall was only wet.

There was one more thing, and it was for the visitors, who are the only ones here who can be spared something.

An old woman of the gatherers had come down that morning with an empty basket on her back and ringing already in her ears, as gatherers do. She had always hurried through the dusk. Nobody stayed there, because the noise and the dark press on the mind. On this descent, as she reached the floor, the din reached her as *company*. It was still loud. She still had to shout to be heard at arm's length. But the pressing quality had left it, and it sounded to her, just this once, like a crowd that had been there before her and was not displeased to see her. She stood in the light a moment longer than she needed to. She said something into the din that she could not have explained, a plain thanks, and the trough took her voice as it takes a drip, into a hollow that had been worn for it. Then she filled her basket and climbed out.

She would tell no one, because there was nothing to tell, only that the noise had felt kind. But she would be the one who comes back, and who teaches her grandchildren where the safe cairns are, and the easy digging. She would say that the place is not against you.

## Leaving

The power went up the ladder the way it had come down, one rung at a time, and it did not look back, because looking back would have been a way of staying.

Below, the crickets closed over its footprints within the day. The beetles worked its small disturbance into the mounds. The pale centipedes went on hunting, unhungry, and the pools lay black and still with the eyeless fish reading the water. The bats streamed out through the skylights into the cold scrub and came home heavy, and the guano fell.

By any measure the trough uses, nothing had been done. If there was something alive in the circulation, that something had been listened to for an hour, and it went on ticking, warm and wet and unfinished or finished, as it had before. The wise thing, the power thought as it climbed into the flat cold light, is often to be the one presence in a place's whole history that asked it for nothing.
