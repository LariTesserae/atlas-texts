# What Came to the Loom Heights

## What It Did Not Do

It did not quiet the noise. The noise is the mountain's labor, and a wise power knows the difference between a wound and a work. Silence would have been a mercy only to someone who mistook the plateau for a sufferer.

It did not fill the hush-basins with sound, though it could have. The slow argument between noise and quiet will settle itself in a few thousand years, and the Unsettled will follow the silence to wherever it opens next. The power judged that a pace that long was not its to hurry.

It did not descend in light or speak in the sky. Nothing on the Loom Heights would have understood it, and the one being who might have would not have heard it.

## The Arrival

It came up from the lower terraces at the hour when the fluteweed sings one chord, and it came as something with a hand, because on this plateau the hand is the mouth.

At the first lean-to it found a pair of ear-plugs in a niche, offered and waiting for longer than anyone had counted. It took them. This was the first thing it did, and perhaps the most important: it accepted what had been left out for a guest. Then it walked the loud routes with one hand out, touching each cairn as it passed, until it found the first faded cord and read it with its thumb.

## What the Cords Said

Not warnings, as the travelers who found them had always assumed. Not prayers either.

The cords were names. Every Join had one, tied in a knot particular to it, followed by a run of small remarks in the shorthand of someone talking to themselves over decades. *Third Join, higher again this frost. Forgive it.* *The basin under the split ridge: I counted forty, then eleven. Went back. Counted again. Forty.* *The green stone is warm at the seam today.* *East Join has begun to climb. I want to hear where it stops.*

The power read for a long time. It understood at last what the listening stones had concluded, the finding that nothing on the plateau had ever recorded. They had concluded nothing. The listening was the whole result. Someone had spent a life attending to a mountain being made, and had loved it in the only unit available, which was time.

## The Meeting

The person was at a listening stone, ear to the drilled hole, in the hour of the single chord. They were very old, and their hands had the grip of the scraper in them, curved to it even at rest. They had been deaf so long that the plugs beside them were only habit.

The power did not approach from the front. It came up behind and set its hand on the person's shoulder, the oldest gesture the plateau has, the one the pairs used to walk by.

The person did not startle. Their body remembered before their mind did, and the shoulders dropped by the width of a hair. Some time passed, most of a slow breath. Then a hand came up and covered the power's hand, and stayed there.

Afterward the power tied a knot, badly, in the old code. It meant something close to *I am here.* The person's fingers read it, and something moved through their whole frame that would have been laughter if there had been anyone to hear it. They tied one back, clean and quick. It meant *Sit. The chord is nearly over.*

They sat, and the power listened to the Join alone, one thin pure note, through a hole drilled by someone else's patience.

Later, in the cold, the power asked in knots whether they wished to leave, whether there was a place with warmth and voices where it could carry them. The person considered this honestly. The answer was a knot meaning *later*, and after it another, meaning *when the east Join stops climbing.*

The power did not argue. A life lived on its own terms is not a problem to be corrected. It tied *then I will walk with you*, and they went on in pairs, one hand on a shoulder.

## The Basin

The person took it, eventually, into a hush-basin, and the power saw what the capstones were.

They walked in without counting, by the rise and fall of the ground, and at a cairn tilted past agreement with itself the person crouched and set the black stone level. For a breath the slope held still. The power felt something lean in, unformed, warm as a cold room drawing heat from a hand, and it understood the Unsettled for the first time. They were not hunting. They were hungry for the one thing the person gave freely and without owning it: a stone put right for no reason except that it should be.

The person had been feeding them for decades, a crumb at a time, without knowing there was anyone to feed.

The power did not answer this with a feast. Flooding the basin with its own coherence would have made the Unsettled dependent on a visitor who would leave, and would have taken from them the one thing that was theirs, the gathering of small failures into a shape. Wisdom, here, meant using what the plateau already had.

So the two of them built, over one season, in the largest basin under the split ridge. They took the tilted doorway that framed only the far side of itself and reset its courses, stone against stone, until the lintel lined up with the nearest listening stone and the door framed something real. Through it lay a slope, and beyond the slope a Join, and beyond the Join the whole loud, honest ridge. In the lintel they hung strings of bell-thorn capsules from the lean-tos, still faintly sounding, so that the Join's tone would ring inside the silence, small and steady and in tune.

It was a fixed point that needed no mind to hold it. For the first time the Unsettled had something to lean on that did not have to lean back.

## What Changed

Very little, as far as any record shows.

A red that had hung a hand's breadth beside its rock came to rest one afternoon exactly on it, like a breath let out. A shadow pooled where a stone actually stood. In the basin under the split ridge a traveler could count forty paces to the door and count forty again.

A second hand of knots appeared on one cord in a lean-to, clumsy at first and then fluent. It said little: *Walked the routes. Capstones level. East Join still climbing.* And once, further down, in a knot that means nothing but itself: *Heard.*

The noise went on making the world, one struck note at a time, and two people went on tending it. One was very old and one was not, and neither was lonely in the way a traveler would be. Whether the older one ever went down to the warm places, or stayed until the east Join stopped, the cords do not say. Either way, the black stones stay level, and the mountain, which had waited so long for a guest who knew how to listen, kept the shape of the one who had come.
