# What Came to the Plateau

It arrived the way the haze does, without a moment you could point to. There was the plateau, and then there was the plateau with something attending to it.

It did not descend. It came up the moss slope on foot, sinking to the shin like everyone else, because it judged that this was how the place was meant to be entered, slowly and wet and a little clumsy. Its first act was to walk out onto a causeway and let the drumming of its steps be gathered in. Within a quarter hour it was on the beat, and it let itself be included, as anyone is.

Then it stayed for a long time.

## What it considered

It was wise, so it began with what it might do wrongly.

It could mend the sameness. To a mind that loves becoming, a place that renews itself unchanged looks like a held breath that ought to be let out. But the sameness was not a wound. Nothing here strained against it, and the frogs did not look like creatures that wished for weather. It set the idea down.

It could wake the pattern, give the being in the pillar eyes and a name and something to say. This it considered longest, and it declined for a plain reason. A being that has never wanted a voice cannot be asked whether it wants one, and a gift the receiver cannot refuse is only a seizure with better manners.

It could fill the plateau with visitors, with a hundred pilgrims to be included and kept nothing of. It saw that this would only multiply the unsettling thing. The place would go on receiving without keeping, and now with an audience to witness it.

What remained was smaller, and it was not certain the small thing was permitted.

## The question

It went into a tower and stood at the rail, where the water around the pillar held its unchanging rings. The note pressed on its sternum as it presses on everyone's.

Then it did what a wise guest does in a house where it does not know the customs. It asked, as gently as it could ask anything. It let one footfall fall a little out of time, a single step off the beat, and held still afterward. It was a grain of disorder, no more than a cough in a quiet room.

The rings trembled.

They had not done so for anyone before. Visitors came and went and the rings did not remember them, but this was not a visitor's clumsiness. It was a question, and the pattern felt the shape of a question in it. For the length of three slow swells the concentric ripples wavered, and the power felt something turn toward it, something with no eyes, the way a bell is aware of a hammer.

Then the rings settled. They did not return to what they had been. They found a new steadiness a hair's breadth from the old, as a dancer takes the weight of a partner and is still standing.

It took this to be the answer. *Yes, a little, and that little.*

## The low ground

It walked out of the tower and followed the gutters, as the water does, from causeway to causeway. The chord thickened around it as it went, as if the sound were being gathered. It passed the terraces, where the frogs' throats swelled through interference after interference, each counting for its own pair of towers. It passed the ivory ones, unhurried under the cane. It passed the tall one, standing in the shallows with its unbroken collar of water, facing the place where the haze grew dense.

It did not stop. It went on into the thickness, where the eye gives up, and there it found what no one had gone to see.

There was no creature. The gutters, the terraces, and every notch converged on a single lip of pale root, and beyond the lip there was nothing. The plateau ended, and the water went over.

It fell into the cloud, and below the cloud it became a rill, then a stream, then a river, then many rivers, running through valleys where things were planted and harvested and drunk and forgotten. Every drop had come the long way through the towers and been tuned, and it carried into the world below a faint residue of the chord, a fifth above and a fifth above that, worn down to the murmur of running water. Farmers who had never heard of the plateau slept beside its sound.

So the being in the pillar was not frozen. It was a fountain, and a fountain has no use for a history. Its constancy was the gift. The world below could trust the rivers because the source never wavered, and it kept nothing of its visitors for the same reason a spring keeps nothing of the thirsty.

The power stood at the lip for a while, with the water passing over its feet, and understood that it had almost mended something whole.

## What it did

It could not give the place a memory without breaking it. But it could carry something back up.

At the lip it bent and cupped a little of the falling water, which by now had gathered in it the sound of the valleys. That sound was not the chord. It was the chord answered, made of river-noise, mill-wheels, and children calling across a ford. It held this in its hands and climbed back through the haze to where the heron stood.

The tall one had listened toward this place for longer than the towers had been standing, and the chord had never had news in it. The power did not speak. It opened its hands at the heron's feet and let the water go into the shallows, water that had been down and had come back with the world's sound in it.

For the first time, something moved in the meniscus. A ring spread from the heron's leg and crossed the mirrored collar, the first broken surface that water had ever accepted from it. The neck, folded in its S, unfolded a finger's width and folded again. That was all. No eye flicked and no feather stirred. But the heron had been told, in the only way that could reach it, that the thing it listened for was listening back.

On the terraces, one frog's throat missed its count. The swell came late, hung, and resumed. It would be called an error if there were anyone to call it that. It might have been laughter.

## After

The power left the way it had come, on foot, down through the cloud with moss to its shins.

The chord was unchanged, the beats slow against one another like waves on a shore. The beetles ground the dead tips down to living wood, the rings held their concentric stillness, and the light kept its pearly evenness with no noon and no afternoon.

But the heron's collar of water carried one ring that did not fade, a single widening circle that the surface accepted and kept, and it was the first thing the plateau had kept of anyone. The plateau did not mind. It did not know what it had been given, only that it was heavier by exactly one thing, in exactly one place, and that this was enough.

The power had come prepared to do great good, and it had done a small one. It had asked, waited, carried a little water uphill, and left. A wiser thing than a benefactor knows that some places do not want saving, only to be told that the sound they make is heard.
