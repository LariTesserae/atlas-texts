# What Was Done in the Sweating Basin

The power came the way such powers come to small quiet places, which is to say without arriving. It was simply attending, and then it had been attending for some time.

It noticed first that nothing was wrong. It was used to being drawn by ache: a failing spring, a lonely creature, a grief. Here it found a warm bowl of green and an old, slow, half-waking thing in the water beneath, and the thing was not suffering. It did not want anything. The power lingered over that, because a lack of wanting is rarer than pain and easier to damage.

## What it considered, and set down

It could wake the sleeper. It had the means. It could have given the water a mind and let the thirty stones speak, and it would have had a companion of great patience and a strange, lovely intelligence. But the sleeper's half-waking was not a lack. It was the way the sleeper was whole. Waking it would have been like pulling a held note out into a sentence. The power decided that giving someone a self they had not asked for is a theft dressed as a gift, and it put the idea down.

It could protect the place: wall the rim, turn the curious aside, guard the travertine from every future boot. But the basin had absorbed the first people the way it absorbs a footprint, and there was no sign it needed shielding from what it had already survived. The power did not know whether visitors would ever come again, and it did not want to decide for the basin who deserved the warmth.

It could stop the slow walk downhill, or hurry it, or mend the rim. None of that was its business. The terraces creep because the water goes that way. A place that is going somewhere at the pace of sleep does not need to be told where.

So the power did not wake it, guard it, steer it, or improve it. It found that this took most of a long while, and that the refusing was itself a kind of care, the way holding your tongue can be.

## What it did

There was one thing. It came from the blade and the cord.

The occupant had been given exactly one gift in all its ages, small and strange, and it had held that gift as tenderly as anything it held. Nothing had ever answered the gift. The power did not think the sleeper felt this as absence, since it felt nothing as absence. But the doorway on the western side, where the wave slows for an hour it has no reason to, seemed to the power like a place where a question had been left open for a very long time.

And the sleeper dreamed of rain, and everything in it was rain: the iron water, the cut-grass taste, the warmth that was rain's long trip through the dark. It had been rain once, in a sky no one witnessed, and had never in all its walking been rained on again. The mist over the basin hung thin and never became cloud, so the sky and the water had not met in the whole time between.

The power did not make anything. It did not carry a treasure or plant a seed from elsewhere. It asked the high air, in the ordinary way air can be asked, to let the veil thicken for one afternoon.

## The afternoon

The mist gathered and darkened to the color of a pigeon's breast. Then rain fell, plain and gentle, without thunder, drop after drop on the moss and the pools and the wide coin leaves of the gnarled trees. It rang in the bromeliad cups, and the cups took it in and did not overflow, because something was keeping the measure, and the rain was exactly the measure.

It filled the grinding hollows and stirred the pale moss floating in them. It ran down the dark flanks of the stones, over water that was already running, so that for the first time the outside wet met the inside wet on the same skin.

And the occupant noticed. The power watched it happen. The wave of attention that had circled the thirty for ages, day and a half by day and a half, did not wander to the next stone. It stopped and let go of its route. All thirty stones streamed at once, dark and glistening, and the hum rose in the ground until the ferns trembled to the ends of their unopened fronds. The sleeper did not wake. It turned over completely, the way a sleeper does when a hand is laid on the blanket, and lay for a while with all of itself facing the sky.

Rain fell into rain. The power asked for nothing and offered nothing more.

## After

The cloud thinned when the hour was up and left the veil as it had been. The pools were a little higher, and they would sink again by the dry months. The lantern-heads set their coins moving over the wet moss, and the coins were brighter for a day.

The loop resumed. It circled through the thirty at its old pace, with one change too slight for anyone to find who had not been there before. On the western side, at the stone that lingers, the wave still pauses for its extra hour. But now, after it moves on, there is a second, shorter lingering at the stone beside it. The pause is the length of a breath.

The power could not say what the second pause was. It might be the sleeper remembering the rain, or a habit forming, or a small doorway opening in a house that had only had one. It did not ask. It had made a decision to stay out of the answer.

## Leaving

It took nothing. It left no mark on the travertine and no thread in the tree's grip beside the old cord. The footprints it had never made did not need to close.

At the rim, on its way out of attending, it allowed itself one wish. The wish was not for the basin, which wanted nothing, but for whoever might climb through the thinning cloud forest someday and feel the ground soften and let them in. It hoped they would sit long enough to notice the wave, and rest a palm on a warm stone, and take it away gently. It hoped they would go back over the rim as the first people did, having taken what they needed or given something, and leave the place asleep and dreaming of rain that had, once, come back.
