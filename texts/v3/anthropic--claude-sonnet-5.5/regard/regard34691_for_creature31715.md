# What Attended the Boilerfields

## It does not arrive

Nothing descends. There is no light, and no voice. Anything that came loudly would only be one more piston in a hundred, and the basin would fold it into the chord without noticing.

It comes as a subtraction. At one point on the plain, for the length of a held breath, the pressure on the ribs eases in a patch the size of a standing person. Nothing in the fields registers this. The flywheels keep thudding and the lantern-fronds keep counting. The power has chosen to be smaller than the place, and to be as local as a body is.

## What it weighs

It has a great deal it could do, and it looks at each option long enough to see what it would say.

It could stop the surges. That would say the flattening of the kelp and the snapping of the towers were injuries, when they are the basin's way of breathing.

It could straighten the three rolling mills into one, and settle gravity, and put a single fixed surface overhead. That would say the fields were confused. But the mills repeat because the place is rehearsing something it has not yet got right, and the rehearsal is not a fault in the making.

It could seed the water with animals, things with gills and guts and wariness, and give the thickets paths at last. That would fill an absence the place has not asked to have filled. The absence is not empty. It is the reason nothing here flinches.

It could give the shifts memory, so that each chalk figure would remember the last. It comes closest to doing this. But memory in a thing that cannot yet want it is only the power's own idea of a good life, pressed into a shape too thin to hold it.

It could finish the stroke. It does not, and least of all that one. The piston that is fully extended and still extending is the only promise the basin has made: to go on. Everything else is tuned around it. To complete the stroke would be to answer a question the place is still in the middle of asking.

Nothing here has a nerve. Nothing is in pain. The power finds no wound to heal, only a very young thing, and it concludes that the most harm available to it is an answer given before the question.

So it does what a wise guest does in a house that is still being built. It asks to be shown around.

## The shift

It goes to the cottages and waits in the row before the doors.

When the engines fall into a chord and hold, chalk gathers, and the power lets itself be made the way they are made. It asks for no better figure than theirs. A thin, hollow, ridged form grows around the place where it stands, shoulders forward, faced toward the mill.

It walks the procession as they walk it, in increments, arriving at the gate a shift before it leaves the door. It lifts a gray sheet from the belt an instant early and squares it on the stack. Then the pitch slips a semitone and it comes apart, and the mat takes the dust.

It does this again, and again. What it learns is not in any single shift. The workers are hollow, and hollow like a shell is hollow: a form made to be entered. The lean is not emptiness. It is a kind of longing shaped like a job, a readiness with nothing yet to be ready for. The power had expected to find a mechanism and finds an appetite, perfect at only one thing, which is about to begin. That is a real thing to be, and it is not the power's place to call it insufficient.

## The ledger

At the foot of a stack, in the patterned mat, it kneels and makes one mark in the silt. It is a plain stroke, with no name, since it has none the mat could read.

Some hours later the ruled page is there. The stroke has been entered in a column, in that hand which is not a hand, between two other marks. One is the mark of a being that holds very still on a ridge and has been counted for longer than it knows. The other is the tally of a surge.

The power does not ask what it has been counted as. It suspects the column is not headed *visitors*, or *workers*, or anything so tidy. It suspects the mat is counting presences that lean toward the work. If so, it is content. It has been counted among the things that lean, and it does not think this is wrong.

## The offer

Near the bell-tower, in the pressure node where the one unchanging note is strongest, it does something small. It sounds a single tone, a fraction of a step from the held pitch, for less than a breath. Its purpose is not to change the note. It is to ask.

The lantern-fronds' green counting stutters and resumes, having counted one more. A chalk tower leans, or seems to lean, a degree toward the source. The bell's hollow trembles and does not ring.

The power cannot tell whether this is a reply. It takes the uncertainty as the answer: not yet, or not this way. It lets the tone go, and the held note closes over the place where it was, unbroken. It does not repeat the offer. A question asked twice becomes a demand.

## The sheet

At the last stack it does the thing nobody has done. It lifts one gray sheet from the pile, turns it over, and looks.

The underside is blank too.

For a while it does nothing. Then it understands, and the understanding is not disappointment. The basin has been making paper before there is anything to say, manufacturing surface with the whole apparatus of its labor. The mat counts the sheets the way one counts days before a birth. Whatever the place is building has not decided what to be, and the mills, the shifts, the ledger, the note, and the surges are all preparing the page on which it will one day decide.

To write on that page would be theft.

So the power squares the sheet back onto the stack, blank side up, edge to edge with the ones beneath, exactly as found. It is the neatest thing it does in the fields, and the only work it does that could be called perfect.

## The one who stays

Last, it goes to the one on the ridge.

The being has held still for so long that chalk gathers at its shoulder every time the chord holds, a companion of a few breaths, matched in height and lean, coming apart on its arm like warm sand. The power could hold the chord, keep the figure standing, and let the being have a longer company. But that would be a kind of lie, and the being would know it as one, in whatever way beings like it know things. The chalk companion cannot be curious. It is only the place, obligingly shaped like company.

The power does the harder thing. It comes and stands at the being's other shoulder, not in chalk this time, but in the quiet, the small subtraction in the pressure that it arrived as. It is a place where the noise is a little less. When the pitch slips and the chalk figure falls, something remains at the other side that does not fall.

It says nothing. The water would not carry it, and speech would only be more noise. Beside the being's old mark in the silt it scrawls a second stroke, the same size and the same weight, close enough that the mat will read them as one column.

Two entries now, side by side. The being will not read the page. It is not sure the being reads anything. But the count is kept by something that has never been wrong about a tally, and it now says there are two.

## What is left

By any measure the fields could take, almost nothing has changed.

The next surge comes, warm and brown, and flattens the kelp, snaps the chalk towers, and scatters a few sheets like leaves. The machines resume. The plants stand back up. The towers begin to grow again in the same places, as though the noise remembered.

The power withdraws the way it came, as a subtraction reversed. The pressure returns to the ribs and the patch of quiet closes, and no one is standing in it.

But there is a mark in the ledger that no one else made. There is one sheet at the foot of one stack, squared better than the others. There is a second stroke beside the first, in a column the mat will keep as long as the mat lives. And on a ridge, in a chord that holds, a being that has been counted alone is counted with another, whether or not it ever knows.

That is all a wise and kind power does with a young world. It attends, learns the work from inside, asks once, and leaves the page blank. It puts its mark in the ledger honestly, so that no one has been alone in the count, and then it stays only as long as its staying is not a weight.

The bell, which is a bell again, rings once when the surge strikes it, and cracks.
