# The Attention

It arrives the way the sun does, without announcement and from above, and for a long while it is only another rope of light braiding across the slabs. The blennies take no notice, the cod take no notice, and the herring split around it as they would around anything.

It has come to look at one small fish.

## The Fish

At the far margin, past the last yellow post, the newest slab has been unshuttered for four days. The concrete is still wet-gray, and its face is stitched with fresh tie-holes, none more than a week old. In the ninth hole of the third row sits a blenny no longer than a finger, with its head out, its crest flared, and its whole body behind it wedged into a socket the width of a coin.

It is the first tenant on this stretch of wall. The hole to its left is empty, and so is the one to its right. It has claimed both anyway, as territory, and it defends them against nothing, snapping now and then at plankton, at a drifting thread of kelp, at the current itself.

The Attention watches this for what would be an hour, if it kept hours. It sees the frills, the outraged eyes, and the tiny body braced in its hole like a fist in a glove.

It sees that the fish is fully occupied with being alive. Nothing in the fish is waiting to be improved.

## What It Could Do

It considers the options, because a power that does not consider them is not wise.

It could make the fish safe. It could close the water around it like a hand and let no jaw come near. But safety of that kind is a wall, and the fish would be living inside a still photograph of itself.

It could give the fish more: years, size, a mind wide enough to know where it is. It turns that one over longest, and sets it down gently. A mind wide enough to know it lives on the floor of a machine, on the wall of a slab that will be buried, in a noise like a struck bell, might not be a gift. The blenny's small mind fits the blenny's small hole exactly. That fit is a kind of perfection, and it does not want to be the one who breaks it.

It could remove the dredgers, silence the turbines, and stop the gates. It touches this thought and finds it is not really about the fish. The fish has lived its whole life in the hum. The hum is the sound of the world to it. Take the hum away and the blenny would flinch from the silence.

And it could tell the fish. It could make itself known and speak, and the fish would have to become something else in order to hear.

The Attention does none of this.

## What It Does

It does three small things and leaves no signature on any of them.

First, it lays a slackness into the current along the frontier wall for the length of one night, the way a hand steadies a table. The larvae of a hundred kinds of small animal are drifting toward this new concrete, and they will settle more gently for it. Some of them will become neighbors, and the blenny will have things to argue with. This is the only real gift, and even it is only a nudge that the water might have made itself.

Second, it goes to the place where the next pour is scheduled. It sees the forms already rising, and the rebar bristling, and it understands that the wall the blenny sits on will be covered within the month. The hole, the crest, and the claimed territory will all go under fresh gray. It stays with this for a long moment.

Then it watches the blenny do something it hadn't expected. The next morning, when the forms come down on the neighboring slab, the fish leaves its hole. It rides the slack current in a short bright dart along the face and inspects the new tie-holes one at a time, frowning at each. It picks one, backs into it, and flares its crest at the whole ocean.

The blenny was never attached to a place. It follows the edge, and where the builders are working, that is where it lives. Its home is the newest hole in the world, and it will always be a few days ahead of the concrete. The Attention had been mourning for a fish that had already moved on. It lets the mourning go, and finds it does not miss it.

Third, at dusk, when the light ropes fade and the congers begin to slide from their seams, it turns and drops down through the cold water until it hangs a body-length from the wall. It comes close enough for the blenny to see it, or to see something, a pressure or a dimming or the shape of attention.

The blenny sees it, and the crest goes up.

The fish darts out of its hole, all four centimeters of it, and bites at the air in front of the greatest presence that has ever come to this water. It is furious and completely serious. This is my hole, says every frill. Go away.

The Attention is, for the first time in a long while, delighted.

It does what the cod does when a dredger passes. It shuffles aside a body-length and settles again in the lee. The blenny watches it for a moment with one pop-eye and then the other, and decides the matter is closed. It backs into its hole and returns to the current.

## What Remains

The gates lift in sequence across ten kilometers. The river pours through, the shoals lean, and the bells ring one after another along the floor. Far up, a squall crosses the surface and scrambles the sun.

The Attention stays through one full cycle, because the fish is still there and there is no reason to leave. It listens to the turbine fields breathing in their slow interference, to the crunch of a dredger somewhere upcurrent, and to the blennies grunting in their thousand holes. It finds that it has stopped wanting to change any of it.

If the crews ever wonder why the frontier wall settled so quickly that season, why the larvae came thick and the barnacles early and the small faces filled the third row all at once, they don't say. The offices won't confirm that the Basin was built for the basking sharks either. And the Attention will not say what it was built for. It was built for something that hasn't finished being built, and there is a fish in the ninth hole of the third row that agrees.

When it goes, it goes the way it came, as a rope of light that thins and is not there. The blenny doesn't notice the leaving any more than it noticed the arrival. It is watching the current, holding a hole it will soon abandon for a better one, and defending it, fiercely, in the meantime.
