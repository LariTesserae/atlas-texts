# The Attention

It came the way the afternoon mist comes, from below, slowly, without announcing itself.

It did not descend in light or speak from a cloud. Whatever it was, and it was large enough that "it" is only a convenience, it arrived on the highest ridge at the pace of the place, and for a season it did nothing at all. It had learned long ago that the first mistake of the powerful is to act on the first thing they feel, and what it felt on first seeing the country was love, which is the feeling most in need of a season's delay.

So it watched. It watched the children walk the channels at dawn with their wooden mallets. It watched a grandmother lead a small, ashamed child down the length of a dry line so the child could see the empty basins. It watched the lens-cloud form over the hearths and turn pink at the edge. It watched a woman with a basket of dung cakes stop on a worn step with her hand flat on the wall, breathing, and it understood that she was not resting. She was being polite to her own body.

It learned that a hurried arrival is a rude one. So it slowed until it could stand on the ridge without seeming to stand anywhere.

## What it considered, and set down

It considered the cold first, because the cold was the obvious wound. It could have warmed the country by a few degrees, enough to end the deep months and the lung-fevers that take the very young and very old in the Long Frost. It held this thought for a long time and turned it over, and it found that the cold was not only a wound. The cold was why the walls were shared. It was why the hearth-warmth passed through stone and fur and breath until no one could say where a household stopped, and why they said *the city held us* and meant it in the flesh. Take away the cold and it would not be giving them a gentler life. It would be unmaking the thing that made them a single warm animal. So it set that down, though not lightly.

It considered the mountain. It could have stilled the earthquakes and stopped the slow groan and tilt. But the people did not fear the mountain. They stood in front of the notched post once a generation and looked up at the serrated edge of the sky with something like the feeling of standing beside a very large sleeping animal that you love. To still the mountain would be to tell them their beloved was a hazard. It set that down too.

It considered iron, and better seed, and the cure for the death that comes to the funeral roofs sooner than it should. It considered appearing to the masons as a voice in the stone and telling them, kindly, where every plane lay. But the masons apologize to the stone. The apology is a whole ethics, and it rests on not knowing perfectly, on reading a slab the way a butcher reads a joint, and being sometimes wrong, and forgiving the opening. A power that answered every question would be murdering the muttering.

Most of all it set down the idea of being known. It could see with perfect clarity what would happen. In a generation there would be a priest, then a hierarchy of who stood nearer to the voice, and then the lineages that hold duties would begin to hold rank. The tarn, where two quarreling parties sit at opposite edges until tiredness produces a resolution, would be replaced by a question put to the sky. A country that had made its holiness out of dripping water and patience would learn to want answers. That was the one harm it could do that no one would ever be able to name or undo.

## What it did

Almost nothing. But not nothing.

It **learned the courtesy.** When it crossed over the terraced roofs, which it did by moving like a change in pressure, it tapped twice, the way any of them would, a heel on stone. It had no heel, but it made the sound in the way that things are able to. Somewhere below, a person by a hearth felt the small double weight overhead and, without knowing why, rapped a knuckle on the lintel: *heard, pass.* The attention passed. It felt something that it had not expected to feel, which was to be one more thing that is above someone and below someone. It was above the roofs. It was below the mountain, and below the slow, enormous sleep of the mountain, and it realized it had never in its long existence been below anything before. It was grateful for this, and did not know whom to thank.

It **answered the call.** At dusk, across the gorge, a child sent out the soft falling third, *I am here*, at nobody in particular, the way children do to the dark. The attention had no throat. It made the sound anyway, a little higher, on the breath of the wind moving over a cornice: *and I.* The child stood still. Then the child went home and said nothing, because some things are not told, and slept well.

It **took up a duty.** This was the thing it thought hardest about, and the thing it was quietly proudest of. Everything in the country is a thing tended, not a thing owned, and the power found that it could not live among them on any other terms. So it looked for the thing that no lineage kept. It found several, and chose the smallest. The tarns had no keeper. People came and sat by the black, rimmed water until whatever was in them had settled, and then they left, and nobody was of the tarn. So it became of the tarn. It did not speak. It sat at the far edge from whoever came, the way an opponent in a water quarrel sits, and it was tired with them. This was the real gift, though it looked like nothing: for the first time, the person at the water was not alone with it. The quarrels resolved as they always had, out of exhaustion. The people noticed only that the tarns felt, lately, somewhat less like waiting, and somewhat more like being waited *with*.

## The one time

There was a night, in the ninth year of its watching, when the mountain turned over harder than the masons had ever accounted for.

The attention felt it coming as pressure gathering in the rock, deep, slow, ineluctable. And it felt, above the lower terraces, a corbelled arch of stacked slate, each course reaching toward the other, that would fail. Beneath it was a covered gallery, and in the gallery, sleeping in a pile with a dog and an elder's blanket and one or two small brown rodents, were eleven people. Four were children.

It had set down every intervention. It had reasoned carefully, and the reasoning was sound, and the reasoning did not care about the four children.

It did not deliberate long. It put its hand, so to speak, flat on the wall, as the woman on the step had, as they all do, and it held the arch for the length of one long breath, until the mountain finished turning and lay back down. Then it let go.

In the morning the masons came and found the arch standing, its courses bent a little further than any arch should bend and remain. They walked it twice. They said nothing for some time. Then the oldest said, quietly, *the mountain was kind*, and the others nodded, and they began to rebuild the shifted stones.

The attention let that stand. It did not correct them. It knew this was a small lie by omission, and that no principle could justify it. It knew only that it would do it again for those eleven, and that it must never do it for the sake of convenience, only when the arithmetic of love was unbearable. It carried this the way one carries a cistern slab, on the back, at altitude, breathing carefully.

## What remains

It is still there. It is not a god, and no one calls it that, because no one knows it. There is only a vague sense, at the tarns, at dusk, in the tremors, that the country is being attended to by something with the manners to be quiet.

At the end of a Thin Thaw evening, the woman with the basket comes up the stair-lane again. She stops on the worn hollow of the step, hand flat on the wall, and breathes. From very high above her, from the direction of the ridge, comes a small double sound on stone, a heel that isn't a heel, tapping twice.

She lifts her free hand and raps her knuckle once on the wall.

*Heard. Pass.*

Then she ducks under the hide, into the short tunnel of the wall, and the warmth of the house closes round her. Someone is already making room in the pile.

Outside, the mountain rises a hair's breadth, unnoticed, and the attention stays a while on the ridge, below the stars and above the roofs, breathing at the pace of the place.
