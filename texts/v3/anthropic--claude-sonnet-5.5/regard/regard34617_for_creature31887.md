# What Came to the Uplands

It came in at the surface, where the plateau is quiet.

It did not break the water. The water there is flat as poured metal, and it left it that way. It arrived the way attention arrives, and for a long while it did nothing at all. Below it the bladders pounded on. It listened to eleven seconds, and then to eleven seconds more, until it had heard several thousand years of them. Then it listened to the whale.

## What It Considered

It was wise, so it began with what it would not do.

It would not bring a storm. The place had no storms, and a mind used to change might find that lack pitiable. But the mind would be its own, and the stillness here was not a wound. It was the work of ten million stipes keeping time within a breath across ten kilometers. To call that a cage would be to insult the labor.

It would not bring a season, or a night, or a new species, or a gift of any kind that could not be refused. It saw how much of the plateau's life depended on its fixed courses. A seed dropped in a channel would arrive where it always arrived. Whatever the power gave would either fit the machine and change nothing, or fail to fit it and break something. A gentle hand can still be a hand on the wrong lever.

It would not announce itself. The growths would not understand and the tenders would not stop working. The filter-feeders would go on opening and closing on the current, and the grazers would keep to their furrows. Only the whales might look up, and they would not know what they were looking at.

It found, to its own mild surprise, that this left it with very little, and it did not resent that. Much of wisdom is arriving somewhere, seeing that it is well kept, and letting the keepers keep it.

## What It Noticed

Still, it had come because something had reached it, and it went looking for what.

It was not the balance, which was admirable but did not call out. It was not the growths, which spoke to each other in a language of pressure and timing so complete that there was nothing in it to overhear. It was not the wire-eels, who were economical to the point of grace, or the shoals, who turned in the light and were, whatever the arithmetic, doing something that looked like joy.

It was the tenders. It followed one along a wall for a day. It was a small pale animal that felt a wrongness in the current and went to it and put it right, and then went on to the next. No one had told it to. No one would thank it. The whole plateau's perfection rested on ten thousand small acts of that kind, and each was done for its own sake, because something was slightly wrong and could be fixed. The power decided it would not touch a single fouled blade or silted fan. That work belonged to the tenders, and a perfect place kept perfect by outside hands would have lost the only thing that made it a place.

It was the sheet-ray, too. One had slipped up an edge of canopy and broken the surface with a wing. It lay in the silence with the sound gone out from under it, and it felt, briefly, how loud everything was. The power was there in the quiet, and it did nothing except stay still. For once the ray was not alone in the quiet, and it did not know that. The power thought this was enough. The ray slid back down into the light and took its place on the ceiling, and the power let it go without a sign.

But mostly it was the whales.

## The Note

The drum-whales called once every few years, a single long falling note that began above the pounding and settled beneath it. It reached a hundred kilometers, and every other whale heard it, and eventually one of them called back from farther down the lane. This was the whole of their conversation. It had gone on for ages. The note was always answered, and it was always answered by another whale.

The power turned this over for some time. Everything else in the Uplands had been answered so completely that it had stopped asking. The growths did not question the water, and the feeders did not question the current. Only the whales still sent a sound out into the noise with no way of knowing what it would find. The place had nothing unsettled left in it, and yet the whales asked. The power thought this was the most valuable thing on the plateau. It was also, in a certain light, the loneliest.

It did not want to answer the question. Any answer it could give would be too large. It might be a fact the whales could not carry, or a truth that made the asking unnecessary. Ending a question is one of the crueler things a wise being can do, however kind the intention.

So it did the smallest thing it could find.

The next time a whale called, at the far end of a long channel in a slow green light, the power answered. It did not answer as a whale. It made no word or meaning, and it did not point to itself or to anything. It sent one note, in the whale's own register, low enough to fall under the pounding and level enough to hold. The note carried the plain fact of having been heard, and nothing more.

It came from no direction. The whale drifted with its mouth open, and the note passed through it and on along the channel. Then, after the usual long interval, a second whale answered from farther down the lane, as it always had. The power let that one stand. That was the answer that belonged to the place.

## What Changed

Very little, and the power did not stay to count it.

The bladders fired on their eleven seconds. The tenders went on with their small repairs and the nodules fell in their yearly rain. In the filter rows a fan-worm died and was replaced within days by a larva that had been waiting downstream.

One whale, however, called again a little sooner than it had before. It might have been an accident, or the ordinary drift of an old animal's habits. Some years on it called a note that fell a fraction differently, and another whale, hearing it, turned its head. It was not much, but it was the first new form in the Uplands in a very long time. It was so small and so like the old forms that the plateau absorbed it as it absorbed everything, and it entered the cycle and was carried on.

The power was gone by then, or it was there and very quiet. In the end it made no difference which.

Above the water the surface lay flat and gray to the horizon, empty and silent. Below it the pounding went on, deep and even, the sound of a place that had been kept exactly as it was and was, just slightly and without anyone deciding it, no longer exactly that.
