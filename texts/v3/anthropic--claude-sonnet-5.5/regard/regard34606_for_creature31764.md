# The Attention

It did not arrive. Arriving implies a before, and the power that turned toward the Condensary had no *from*. It simply noticed, the way you notice a sound has stopped and realize only then that it had been going on. What it noticed was a small, cold, stubborn order in a mountain full of heat, and a great many people in it, holding their shapes.

It was old enough to know that its first impulse was the most dangerous thing in the room.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not save them. They were not in danger in any way they would have recognized. They were in the ordinary danger of everything that lives on a gradient, which is that gradients end. The mountain is young, but mountains cool. In some tens of thousands of years the seams will dim for good, the dome will stop sweating, and the towers will even out into a warm, level, peaceful nothing. The power saw this clearly. It also saw that the Held knew it, and that the knowledge was one of their materials. They sang *up* because they knew where the song ended. Take away the ending and you take away the reason for the note.

It did not teach them. It could have given them, in an afternoon, everything their physics lacked: where the ledger balances, what the pulse is, what the stairs are stairs *toward*. But a stair that has been climbed for you is a floor. The city's whole grammar is *not yet*. To answer that grammar would be to make it a dead language.

It did not make itself known to the world above. It did not send prophets, or rescuers, or a light in the sky. It did not install guardians at the crack. The Held had a strangeness the size of a city and no defense against being interesting. But the loudest thing that had ever come into the Condensary had also gone out again, calmer and tidier for the visit. The power judged that the place had handled its visitors well enough, and that a guardian is just a louder visitor.

It did not make them larger, faster, more numerous, or more like anything. It did not offer to make them happy. Their word for comfort was also their word for death, and it took that seriously.

## What It Did

First, it listened, and it took a long time to do so.

It slowed itself down until its attention moved at the speed of a rill in a cold groove, then slower, until it could follow a thought in a Held Drop turning over across a season. This cost it something. It had to let go of nearly everything it was, keeping only the patience. In that state it heard the city the way the city hears itself: the vapor assemblies folding into agreement, the rills trading and quarreling, and under everything the eleven-second ring in the bowl.

It learned that the Eldest and the mountain were not teacher and student. Neither was teaching. Two things were keeping time together so that neither would have to keep it alone. The power understood, with something like embarrassment, that the whole arrangement was already a kind of answer to the question it had come with.

Second, it asked.

It asked in the only way the place allowed. On the still water in the fat tower, between one ring and the next, it made a single small disturbance, out of phase, a ring where no ring belonged. It meant: *may I?* Then it waited.

The reply took a season. Across the city the Held passed the question up the towers and down the rills, and the assemblies hung in the mid-air of the chamber, folding and refolding. Nobody voted. When the answer came, it came as the smallest possible thing. The Eldest's ring, at its appointed moment, arrived one beat late. Eleven seconds became twelve, once. Then the rhythm resumed, exact as before, as if nothing had happened.

The power took this to mean: *You may be here. Do not move us.*

That was the whole of the permission, and it was generous.

Third, in the spirit of that permission, it did three small things and no others.

It kept the crack open. Not against people, only against time. In a thousand years a new lava tongue would have sealed the breath in, and the scouts would have had nowhere to go to look at the sky. Now the lip of the mountain would, when it stiffened, stiffen just wide enough. The eleven-second breath would go on. The power adjusted nothing else about the eruption, but gave the rock a slight preference.

It gave the mountain a little more time. A margin, not a rescue. Where the seams would have dimmed in thirty thousand years, they would dim in a great deal more. It could not bring itself to give them forever, and it knew why. A gradient that never closed would ask nothing of them. They would stop leaning. Somewhere in that unlimited warmth the songs would go flat, not from settling but from ease, and ease was the one thing that could actually kill them. So it gave them long, not endless. It gave them a horizon far enough away to build toward and near enough to feel.

The third thing was for the visitors, and for no one in particular. It left in the water a slightly greater tendency to be kind. The people who drank from an open palm would still dream of stairways and come out calm. But now, on the way back down the mountain, some of them would think: *there are things in the world that are not for me, and that is all right.* Then they would keep the secret, not from obedience but from a tidied conscience. The power had no better protection to offer, and it was not sure this was even a protection. It was only what it would have wished for itself.

## What It Left Undone

There was one question it could have answered and did not. The Held had never learned where the disorder goes. The power knew, and in principle it could have told them, drawing the answer along a single thread of water until it reached the tip of some tower and hung there, trembling.

One Drop, near the end of the season, came close to asking. The eddy inside it slowed and turned toward the place where the power was listening. The power held very still and did not offer, and the Drop understood the not-offering. Then, deliberately, it let the thought go, out into the room, unresolved. It would be picked up by some other Held, in some other season, and turned over again.

That was correct. The power had wondered whether wisdom was to know what to do or to know what to leave in the hands of the people it belonged to. Here it seemed to be the second, and it seemed obvious in retrospect, the way stairs seem obvious once you are at the top.

## After

The power did not leave, exactly. It went on attending, but the way a room attends to a sleeping person, present and not pressing. Its attention became one more gradient in the chamber, faint and warm and very slowly moving, and the Held leaned into it a little without knowing why.

Nothing visible changed. The towers still grew their clear hot threads at the rim. The dome still sweated. The rain still fell warm and smelling of flint, and the tall spires still sang up and stopped before the end.

Only, if you were there on a very still night, and if you breathed near the fat tower, you might notice something you had not noticed before. In the level water, every eleven seconds, a ring went out from the center. Once in a very long while, one came a beat late, and the whole city seemed to lean toward that lateness, the way you lean toward someone who has just said your name.

And in the rime at the mouth of the crack, in the shapes the scouts made when the breath met the cold, some mornings there was a figure that had not been there before. It might have been a stair, or a small frozen hand held open. It melted at the first warmth.

The Held called it a report. The power called it a *thank you*, and did not correct them.
