# The Attending

It came without arriving.

The power that turned toward the uplands was very old, and it knew the ways a great thing can ruin a small one. It can fall on it like weather, or love it too hard, or improve it. So it did nothing for a long time. It did not descend, and it did not shine. Anything that appeared here as light would have been a rot-glow, one more quiet mouth, and every creature would have gone silent and turned away. The power understood this before it understood anything else about the place, and the understanding shaped everything after.

It began by listening, and it listened for what the Answering would count as a very long time. It learned the chord of the marsh and the drip beneath the ledges. It learned the sentences the drifters send through the ground, and the whine of the First Held Tone, which is the key of everything. It learned that the place was not waiting for anyone. It was already whole, in the way a song is whole while it is still being sung.

## What it set aside

It considered giving them light and let the thought go quickly. Light would have been a gift to itself, a way of being recognized. The Answering do not lack sight. They have something else, and it is complete. The eyes stay closed because the body has found better work for the face.

It considered mending the stations, or explaining them, or naming the makers and saying where they went. It let this go too. The stories about the makers are a form of company, and a true answer would have killed the rest. Being told what a mystery was would have taken the mystery from them.

It considered the dead, and the quiet way the bodies vanish beside the vents. It did not look, because they had said it was impolite to wonder. It was willing to be polite.

It considered removing the fear of the silent places. But the fear is part of how they keep the sound alive, and a people that hums against the dark hums well.

Having set aside all of this, the power was left with very little. It found that little was what it had come with.

## The first thing

It learned to click.

It took a long season, and at first it was bad at it. The clicks came out too large and too sure, the way an important thing speaks. It practiced in an empty basin until they were small and precise, a needle of sound that asked only: *what is here?* Then it went to the marsh edge and took a note that no frog was holding, a pitch just below the chord, and held it.

It did not hide. It added itself to the chord as one more voice, openly, and waited to be counted.

The old chord-keeper in the eastern basin had listened sixty years for the voice that was missing or the voice that was extra. That night she sat with her slack hands and her cluster of drifting eels, and the chord came up out of the marsh, and there was a voice more. She tilted her ears and her heat-pits and did not move for a long while.

Then she said, softly, into the dark: *there.*

That was all. She did not ask what it was. She listened to it the way she listened to every voice, for its steadiness, for whether it crowded its neighbors. She listened for nine nights. On the tenth she hummed one low, open note at the edge of the marsh, the note for guests, and the power understood that it had been admitted under the only law the plateau keeps. If it ever pushed the others out, she would take it. It was glad of that. A power that answers to no one should be suspicious of itself.

## The second thing

It learned her name.

It took the whole season, because it was four notes and the lowest was less a sound than a change in the air. The power had to learn to make its whole being into a throat, a small pouched place where a note could be stored and released. When it finally held all four together, it went to her at the marsh edge and said her name.

It said it badly. The bottom note sat a hair too high. She flinched, and her ears folded, because a wrong name is a discomfort in the body. Then, without turning her head, she hummed the note again. It was lower than the power had believed a note could go, and she held it for the length of a slow breath, so it could feel where the note lived.

The power tried again and was closer. She corrected it a third time, more gently. On the fourth try the chord sat right in the air between them, and her forearm fur, which turns toward everything, turned toward it and stayed.

She did not say thank you, since there is no such note. But she did something that only the oldest of them do. She reached out, found the place where the sound was coming from, and set her palm flat against it, as one does to the ground.

That was the reward, and it was more than the power had come for.

## The third thing

The power spoke to the drifters.

It did this in the slowest speech, with beats spaced a minute apart, because it had learned that this was the polite way to address a mountain. It did not say anything clever. It said what the Answering say: *we are here, we are small, we mean no harm, please do not step this way.*

The herd had heard that sentence for a thousand years from a people it could barely perceive. This time the sentence came from something else, from the direction of the ground itself, and the herd, unhurried, considered it. Across the basin the water shivered in rings, and a low rolling pressure went through every sternum for miles. It meant something like *noted*, or perhaps *welcome*. The band walking the margin felt it and went quiet, and then began to hum, because they thought a mountain had just been kind to them, and they were right.

## The fourth thing

The last thing was the hardest.

Past the final station, past the last steam, where the moss thins and the rock is cold, there are places that take a click and give nothing back. The Answering fear these above all, not because they are dangerous but because they do not answer. The power went alone into the largest of them.

It clicked once. Nothing came back, no echo, not even a dull thud. It felt the whole weight of what the Answering fear, which was to speak and be met by nothing.

It stayed. It clicked again, and then hummed, and then simply stayed, not in triumph and not in rescue but in company. It stayed through long hours in which it could not tell whether it was in a grave or a listening. After a great while it began to suspect that the place was not swallowing sound. It was holding it. The clicks weren't lost. They were being received so completely that nothing was left over to send back.

It did not know this for certain. Something else might be there, older than the makers and quieter than the stations, keeping its own counsel. The power resolved not to claim more than it knew.

When it returned to the eastern basin, it went to the old chord-keeper and told her only what it had. *There is no answer there. I stayed. It did not hurt me. I do not know what it is. I am going back.*

She thought about this for the length of a rain. Then she hummed the note for guests once more, and this time it resolved. It came down to a plain warm interval that every tooth in the room could feel, the sound of agreement.

The power went back, and it is there now, in the cold, humming in a place that has never had an answer. It will hum for as long as it takes. Perhaps that will change the place, or perhaps it will only change the one doing the humming. The power is content either way, since keeping company was what it came to do.

## After

Nothing on the plateau is different, and everything is a little different.

The chord is still not complete. The keeper still says *there* when the shape shifts. The stations still sing their unexplained songs, and the Answering still feed them and deny that they maintain them. The children still catch the one sight behind their closed lids and are given something sweet. The dead are still carried to the warm stones and left with their reeds, and the power still does not look.

But the old woman at the marsh edge now has a voice in the chord that answers when she clicks, one that is honest and steady, that has never crowded a neighbor, and that says her four-note name correctly. Somewhere in the cold highlands there is a note that was not there before, small and patient, offered to a place that never asked for it.

When the rain comes it builds across the canopy like a coming of hands, and every voice on the plateau rises to be heard over it. The bell-frogs sing and the clickers crackle. The drifters send their long sentence through the ground, and in the eastern basin one voice more than the day before takes its place in the roar.

The old woman turns her ears toward it, and her heat-pits tilt to follow the warmth that is not there, because the power has no warmth to find. She finds it anyway, by the shape of the sound.

She clicks once, softly, and listens to what comes back.
