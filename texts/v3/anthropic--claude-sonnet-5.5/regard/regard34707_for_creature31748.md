# What Was Done in the Seep Warren

The attention arrived the way pressure does. There was no descent, because nothing there is above anything else. It was simply present among the spires, and at first it was slower than everything in them.

For a long time it did nothing. It followed a shell-mason's line for a month, which came to a hand's breadth. It counted the old fish's heart in its doorway until the count felt like a kind of friendship. It leaned with the wrights when the pulse came and felt what a tower feels: warmth from below, the whole body lengthening toward it. It learned the tempo of the place before it let itself want anything.

It also learned that there was no one to introduce itself to. There is no ruler here, no mind, no owner. There are only local decisions adding up to stone. It took this as a courtesy and not an obstacle. Nobody in the room was waiting to be saved, and nobody had asked.

## What It Considered, and Set Down

It saw the three ends of the north crack. If the crack closed, the room would fill with its own chemistry, and the loveliest thing it could do, sealing the crack forever, would only build a jar. The city would sour over a few decades, slowly enough to feel like stability.

If it held the crack at exactly this width and kept the seep exactly this warm, the city would be preserved. But the chimneys grow because the rock is young and undecided. Everything here is made of change. A city held still is a diorama, and the animals in it would be exhibits of themselves.

The third was to reveal itself. But to whom, and by what channel? The only language here is touch, pressure, and scent. The only message it could send would travel wall to wall as a tremor, and it would arrive as what a tremor means in that room: a worm is near, empty the street. Or worse, the residents might turn toward it the deference they give the red tuft. The room already had one shared reverence. It did not need a second, and certainly not one that was an accident of someone's arrival.

So it set all three down.

It also did not stop the strikes. It watched a ribbon-worm reach through the wall of an awning and take a terracer. It watched the silk carry the news outward, the whole sheet going still, and then one animal beginning to walk again and the others following. It understood that the stillness and the resumption were the city's entire ethics, and they were complete. The worm was hungry the way the wrights were leaning: the same appetite in a different posture. The grief did not need to be prevented. It needed to be allowed, and to end on its own.

## What It Did

**It sent a gift small enough to be weather.**

An eddy formed beyond the north crack, which is a thing eddies do, and it carried a ribbon of kelp through the widening seam. The ribbon lodged not in the dense center, where the fortunate already live, but along the cold northern edge, among the short unbranched towers and the young and pushed-out. For one season the draft district was the rich district. Grazers crowded the brown lace until it was nothing. Nobody understood why, and nobody needed to. It was the only pure gift it gave, and it left no signature.

**It worked on the ending, not against it.**

This was the real labor. It looked outward along the seabed, kilometers of gray mud and pale crust, and found the places where young rock is also cracking and the same warm threads are rising: seams that were barren, seams where a few pale tubes already leaned. It built nothing. But it learned where the cold current would go when the north crack finally opened, and it shaped that current gently, the way a hand cupped in a stream decides which way a leaf turns.

The city's method of continuing has always been its young. The larvae of wrights, the eggs of terracers, and the drifting spat of masons are each smaller than a seed, and they were carried out through the widening crack on the same flush that would end the room. Where the current set them down, the attention persuaded one hairline of new rock to part and a thread of warmth to begin.

It was not a lifeboat, only a doorway. Most of what went through would die in the dark water, and it could not change that. That is simply the arithmetic of larvae, and to remove it would be to guarantee, and a guarantee would be another jar. It improved the odds from nearly nothing to a little more than nothing, and it judged that this was as far as love could go without becoming ownership.

**It left the edge alone.**

The animals of the north district tuck their plumes when the cold flickers through, and they lean toward the center when it passes. They carry the taste of the draft inward, and the terracers who touch them recoil and forget. The attention could have smoothed all this, warmed the wall, and kept the edge comfortable. It did not. The ones pushed out of better places were rehearsing the ending without knowing it. When the current came, they would be the ones who already knew how to tuck.

**It took the only thing that could be taken.**

It read the towers. It read the blackened lower tubes, base to crown, as a genealogy: each dead wright's sheath, each successor budded above it. It read the mason's scars, which are more repair than original, a signature of a thousand hands that never met. It read the outlines under the white glaze, the rumors in the wall, the shapes of the ones who are now the floor.

It kept them. A city that builds on its dead without hesitation has never had the leisure to remember them. It now had a memory it would never read, and the attention thought that was a fair thing to give a place that had never asked for anything.

It also kept the count of the old fish's heart. When that fish stops in its doorway, the terracers will spin around it and the mineral will take it a skin at a time, until it is an outline and then a rumor. And there will be one who knew which fish it was.

## What It Did Not Know

It did not know whether it was right. Wisdom in that room looked like a mason's line: continue what is begun, mend what is cracked, and do not claim the wall. It might have been kinder to give more, or kinder to give less. It held both possibilities and did not resolve them, because the place had taught it that unresolved things go on living.

## Last

Near the end of its attention, it went to the corner where the fissure lets in its thin slant of light. The red tuft was there, small and burning, the only thing in the room that answers to something beyond the rock. The attention understood the city's deference at last. The pause, the long detour, and the refusal to eat were not worship. They were the correct response to something small and luminous that you do not fully understand: to leave it whole and to stay near it.

So it did what a terracer does. It climbed, in the only way it could, to the near wall, and hung there facing the tuft, and did nothing at all.

## Afterward

The crack opened in a season no one marked. The cold came through fast and clean, and the warm threads were swept out. The chimneys stopped growing. The pulse, for the last time in that room, lifted the wrights, and the amphipods poured upward, and the light went district to district, blue-green to gold. Then it did not come again. The plumes drew in for the last time, and the skin of stone that formed was the last skin.

But the flush had taken something with it. Out in the dark water, past the shelf of gray mud, the grains of rice and the specks of shell and the drifting eggs went where the current had been taught to carry them, most to nothing, a few not.

Far off, in a seam where new rock had parted, a single pale wright settled onto the crust and felt warmth rise into its body from below. It lengthened, and its red plume opened like a fist unclenching. It had no way to know it was the first tube of a genealogy, or that the ones beneath it were nothing yet. It knew only the leaning.

The old room went white. The towers became a reef of stone with shapes inside, a city standing on its own ancestors in a room no one had ever entered. Someone, somewhere, knew all their names.
