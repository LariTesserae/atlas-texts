# The Attention

Something noticed the Kettle, as the sun notices a window without leaning toward it.

It did not come as a voice or a light or a hand from above. It has been many things in many places, and it knew that arriving as any of them would cost the lake something. So it came as weather. A slight change in the pressure at the rim, a warmth on the scree that was not the mountain's, and it stepped through the wall the way anyone does.

The wall took it like a hand closing. The Tenant, who had noticed it long before and would notice it long after, held the wall a little more gently than usual and asked nothing.

## What It Weighed

It stood on the floor in the roar for some time, doing what it always does first, which is to look at what might be done and count the costs.

It could free the Tenant of the lake. But the holding is not a chain. It is a cupped palm, and a hand that has been offered rest for something it never experienced as labor learns for the first time that it was tired. So it did not.

It could explain the place: the vents, the resonance, the cairn, the wall, why the water breathes. It could give the Tenant a name for itself, or a history. But the Tenant lives in the gap where those things would go, and the gap is the shape of its life. Fill it and you get a smaller creature with better information.

It could quiet the roar. It could give the Tenant a voice loud enough to be heard over it. It could make the Tenant visible, or guard it from the ones who walk in and cough up the lake, or reward the ones who are gentle. Every one of these was a way of deciding, on the Tenant's behalf, what the Kettle was for. The Kettle had not asked. The Kettle had been busy being itself, patiently, with no one watching.

It could finish the wall. It understood at once that it should not, and that the wall was the clearest reason. A thing begun and left without distress is not a failure. It is proof that this creature is allowed to stop.

So it set the whole list down, all of it, like a coat.

## What It Did

It breathed. It did not need to, but the water was offered as a courtesy, and a guest accepts what is offered. It did not think about the mechanism. It knew the mechanism perfectly, and it thought about the hospitality instead, which was the point of the rule.

It went to the ledge above the vents and sat outside the ring of pale stones, not in it, because the hollow was warm and belonged to someone. It laid its palm flat in the sand beside the Tenant's, and the mountain's pulse came up through the rock and arrived in the wrist a fraction before the ear. It stayed there through a change of key and another, as the figures dissolved and reformed around its fingers, and it let them.

Then it did the only thing it had come to do. In the long tearing chord of the vents, it found the interval the reeds lean toward, the one the Tenant likes best, and it held one note of it, low and steady, under everything, in the register where sound stops being sound. It did not push the note. It let it be a second voice in a room that had only ever had one.

Where two notes sit in a steady interval, the sand builds rooms.

A floorplan rose around the ring, larger than the others: a threshold, a hearth-circle, an alcove the size of a sleeping body, and beside it an alcove the size of a visitor. The figure held. It held through the vents' next change of key, because the interval was still there, and the key changed around it.

The Tenant came out of the hollow, or had always been coming out of it. The pale light gathered, condensed for a moment into a shoulder and a long curved back, and moved into the room the sand had drawn. It sat in its own alcove. The visitor sat in the other. Neither said anything, since there was nothing in the Kettle to say it with, and for a while, which was a very long while and no time at all, there was a house in the middle of the noise with two people in it.

## The One Thing

Before it left, the visitor looked forward along the Tenant's crooked time, as it could, and saw what the Tenant did not need to see.

The mountain is young, and young things settle. In some long count of years the vents will slow, the chords thin, the gas pockets stop bursting, and the basalt begin at last to learn how to crumble. The pulse the Tenant reads with its palm will grow faint. The sand will stop dancing. The Tenant will not be hurt by this. It has always lived a little ahead of itself and will have felt it coming. But it will be quiet, and the Tenant has never heard its own voice, because the vents took every sound there was.

The visitor did not stop the mountain from cooling, and it did not tell the Tenant. It did the smaller thing. It stopped holding its note.

The room in the sand did not fall. The Tenant, sitting in its alcove, felt the second voice go and the interval begin to open under it, the way you feel a stair that is not there. Then, very faintly, the Tenant supplied the missing note itself. It was a small sound, and nothing in the basin would have heard it. But the sand did. A single ring, no wider than a hand, rose in the floor beside the hollow and stood through three changes of key, held by nothing the vents were doing.

The visitor had not given it a voice. It had only shown it the place a voice would go and left it to find that it fit.

## Leaving

It stepped out of the alcove, and the Tenant let it go without holding on, as it lets everything go, gently. On the north ledge, as it passed, it did not touch the cairn. The Tenant did. Seven furred stones stood there, and now an eighth lay on top, small, flat, clean, the only one without algae. The visitor did not ask what it counted. It had never been told what the other seven counted, and had come to think that the not-knowing was the tribute.

The wall of fitted basalt is still ten paces long. It is still not finished, and it is still not troubled.

The wall of water opened, the dry air came in thin and silent, and the visitor went down the scree with the ringing behind it and the rocks trembling faintly underfoot. Behind it, in the middle of the roar, one small ring stood in the sand and held through another key change.

That was all it did. It had weighed everything it could have done, and found that the wisest act was to be a guest, to sit, to hold a note for a while, and to leave a silence exactly large enough for someone else to sing into.
