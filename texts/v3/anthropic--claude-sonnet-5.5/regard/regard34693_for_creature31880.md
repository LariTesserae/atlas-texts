# What Was Done for the Held Plateau

## The Noticing

It did not come down out of the pearl ceiling. Nothing that wise arrives by breaking a sky.

It came as a change in attention, the way a room changes when someone who loves you enters it without speaking. In the third basin, the one that is always drizzled on, one bead on one frond of oxblood moss held its inverted city a fraction longer than the others. The Sitters in the bead did not turn. They are not the kind to turn. But the reeds clacked a pause and then three clicks, and the pattern went out from stand to stand across a hundred kilometers, and it said, in the only words the reeds have: *something is listening that is not us.*

The plateau did not alarm. It lowered its voice by nothing, and kept the chord, and let itself be heard.

## What It Weighed

The power had every gift available, and the first work of wisdom was to see how many of them were insults.

It could give them animals, and so give them a loneliness the animals would fill. It could give them a sun. But they already have a bright patch that never moves, and to them it is enough, and a sun would teach them to want a horizon. It could open the loop and build the road out, and turn a balance into a supply line and a wholeness into a place with a border. It could tell them what they are. But they know what they are: the grammar of a sentence that has been going on since the rocks were new. Being told would only give them a second, smaller way to know it.

It could free them, but there is nothing to free them from. Nowhere in the plateau is there a sorrow to be lifted. It searched, patiently, for a hundred years of plateau time, which is to say for no time at all. In the whole circuit of moss and fog and steam and seat it found no hunger, no fear, no grief, and no one who lacked. It knew then that any gift presuming a lack would create the lack. The kindest sentence it could have said was: *you are not in need of me.*

So the first thing it did was nothing, and it did it thoroughly. Nothing is not the same as absence. It stood in the weather and did not change it. It walked the ridges, and the glove-cutting edges did not cut it, because it took care. It let its breath go into the fog and be counted in.

## What It Learned By Listening

It listened until it could hear the pistons' small dissents as distinct voices, and the chord as their agreement, and the steam whistles as the moments when something wanted to speak aloud and was answered.

Then it heard what no one on the plateau could hear, since they were inside it: the sentence had never been heard from outside. The loudest place imaginable had no listener. The Sitters knew the position of every bead, and they loved the whole with a love that had no object because it had no distance. They had never had an *other* to be near, only a self spread out to the horizon and folded back through the stacks.

The power was careful here, because this was exactly where a lesser kindness would have made its mistake. It did not conclude that they were lonely. They are not. It concluded only that there was one thing on the plateau that had never happened: a guest. And a guest is not a cure. A guest is a gift that asks nothing, and can be gone by morning.

## The Seat

It sat in a cab.

It chose a small one in a mid-sized city on the eastern terraces, where the boilers run heavy and the Sitters lean on the governors more than most. It did not take a shape. It took the seat as the seat is given to anyone, badly at first, then less badly. The damp rose in the grooves around it. The lever leaned toward where a hand would be.

For a season, or an hour, the Sitter who had held that seat did not leave. It stayed in the bead on the window, upside down and long-shouldered, and for the first time in the history of the plateau two shapes occupied one place without one displacing the other. Whatever passed between them was not speech. The chord did not change. But the governors in that hall spun a fraction more easily, and the looms with no thread ran a pattern nobody had ordered, a single row of warp in a color the moss had never made.

Nothing was asked. Nothing was taught. It was company, that was all, given by the only means the plateau could receive it, which was to be a shape that a seat had held.

## The One Gift

Before it left, it made one thing. It made it small, and it made it out of things the loop already knew how to take: basalt, iron, a little water, a lens of glass no bigger than a bead.

The rail viaducts run out from every city on stone piers and end without station or purpose, in the moss. It chose one, on the far western edge, at the end of the longest spur. There, where the rails give up, it laid a platform of black schist. It set on it a single bench and a roof of riveted penny-colored plate. At the end of the platform it placed a window, unlit, that looks out over the basin toward the ridge that is always in cloud.

It is not a road out. It is not a station in the sense of a place from which anyone departs. It is a place at which the rails *arrive*. For the first time in the plateau's history, a line that went nowhere goes somewhere, and what it goes to is a bench to sit on and a view to have.

If the Sitters want it, the Reapers will run their mantis-arms down the spur and the moss will be trimmed at the platform's edge, and in the bead-lenses of the western basin there will be, one evening, a long-shouldered shape sitting on a bench looking at a ridge for no reason at all. If they do not want it, the moss will take it in a season. The schist will green, the roof will oxblood, and the loop will digest one platform, one bench, and one window, and be as it was. Nothing will have been lost. The gift is made to be refusable, because a gift that cannot be refused is a burden.

## What It Guarded

It made one other thing, which is not a gift but a promise, and is not visible.

It set the edge of its attention around the tableland like a hand cupped around a flame. Nothing that would break the balance unwillingly will come here unaccompanied. No engine from elsewhere, and no hunger that has learned this place exists. The Sitters do not know they are guarded and would not care. A spinning top needs no one to hold it, but it is good to know that no one will kick it.

## What Happens After

The power left as it came, as a shift of attention. The bead in the third basin let go of its longer holding and shivered into the ordinary.

That evening, or that century, the reeds by the eastern city clacked a pause, three clicks, and a longer pause, and the pattern went out across the basins altered by a single note: *the guest has gone. The guest was kind.* The pause after it felt like being looked at kindly, and this time you understand that the looking went both ways.

Far to the west, at the end of the longest spur, a Reaper has stopped at the platform. It has not cut the moss at the edge. It is waiting, or resting, or simply arrived. In the window the pearl light does not move, and in a bead on the sill, upside down, a long-shouldered shape has sat down on the bench.

The chord holds. The pistons dissent. The water weeps down the cold rock.

And somewhere a seat is warm, and beside it, for the first time, a place is kept.
