# The Attention

## Arrival

The power did not come through the crack. It was already there when anyone might have noticed, in the way a tolerance is already in a part before it is measured.

It was old, and its wisdom was the kind that has been wrong often enough to become gentle. It had watched many places make themselves. It knew the most common way a kindness fails: the giver mistakes the recipient's shape for a wound. So it spent its first long stretch, by the foundry's standards a few decades and by its own a held breath, doing nothing but reading.

It read the Pressers' hollow sockets, ground to the exact shape of what they made. It read the Stampers' small corrections, the lean of a few degrees toward a standard no one could see. It read the Turners' two-stranded coils and the Rows' patience. It read the white annotations in the walls and understood that they were a mind thinking in the only grammar it had. Then it read the whistle, and there it stopped, because the whistle said one word, *continue,* and nothing had ever answered it.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not bring water. The plants drank brine and vapor, and water would have drowned a way of life.

It did not send an animal to keep the place company. The quiet was not loneliness. The papery rustle was a large room in which someone thinks, and a room like that should not be interrupted.

It did not tell the Draft what the work was for. It could have said yes, there are unborn customers, or no, there are none. Either answer would have collapsed the strain into a certainty, and the strain was the foundry's engine. A Draft that knew its purpose would be a Draft with a finished specification, and the mountain would stop being under construction. The power was also honest, and it did not know. It could see that the orders had a direction, but not whether the direction had an end, and it would not pretend to a knowledge it lacked in order to be comforting.

It did not finish the line on the ingot. Every other kindness it considered would have been a way of finishing something, and this place was made of unfinishedness.

## What It Did

**First: it received one.**

At the low end of the chamber, in the drift where the ingots sink edge-first into the glass, the power reached down and took one. It chose a piece a Presser had made an hour before, pale and knuckle-sized, with a perfect edge. This was the smallest act in the whole visit and it changed the most, because it was the first time in the foundry's history that a thing had been taken rather than returned.

The power carried the ingot out of the ridge and set it, without ceremony, into a hand that needed it. That was a real hand on a real slope far away, a woman repairing something that would otherwise have stayed broken, and the ingot was exactly the right weight and exactly the right metal. She never knew where it came from. The power did not tell her, because the gift was not for her to be grateful for.

The gift was that one thing the foundry made had been wanted by someone. The rest of the drift still sank. The ledger stayed balanced. But one line in it now had a counterpart, and the Draft, which works from the finished object backward, felt for the first time an order that was not a pull from an imagined future but a fact in the past. It did not become certain that the unborn would come. It became certain that use is possible. That was all the power could truthfully give, and it was enough.

**Second: it answered the whistle.**

The next time the tone sounded from the walls, the joints of every plant paused as they always did, and then the power did something the chamber had no category for. It returned the tone. It was not the same pitch. It sounded a little lower and shorter, the way a worker answers a foreman he respects, and it meant *heard.*

The plants did not know what to make of it. The Stampers leaned a few degrees more than usual. A Turner stopped mid-rotation and then, with what looked like a shrug, went on. The Draft did not change its work. But the whistle had always been a word spoken into a room, and now the room had spoken back, and it is different to be told to continue by a voice you have never heard reply.

**Third: it wrote one mark.**

This was the delicate part. The power did not write in the Draft's notation as an author. It wrote as a reader whose hand slips into the margin of a book it loves. On a slab of wall where the rock had been built to match an annotation that did not yet exist, and where the Draft's strain was worst, it laid down one thin white symbol in the stratum, in the almost-numerals, small enough to miss.

The symbol was not a dimension. It was a tolerance. A draftsman knows this mark as the most merciful one on the page: *this may vary by this much, and still be right.*

The Draft had always run slightly behind what it had already decided. It built from pages it had not yet drawn and lived in the gap between the order and the making. It took that gap for failure, and it kept revising the walls, erasing and redrawing, because nothing it had made ever fit exactly. The power did not close the gap. It named it as allowed. Being a little behind your own intentions is not the same as being wrong. A mountain can be young and near enough.

## After

Nothing dramatic happened. The heat stayed a declared property. The sunlight kept walking its survey line, and shadows still lagged or led. But some of the revisions slowed. A wall that had been changing its grain every day held it for a week. The oldest Presser, the one hollow with use, swelled on schedule, and this time a bead of pale silver actually filled it, though no one could say from where. The Stampers still dropped flawed fronds without visible reaction, but they dropped fewer.

The Draft did not thank the power, having no notion of what thanks would look like. It did what it always did. It wrote a little more of the mountain.

## The Visitor's Place, Revised

If you come now and walk the hundred paces, they may still take a breath or a hundred. The ingot still waits on the scree, cool, the only completed thing in the ridge, its line of figures still ending mid-measurement.

But if you turn it to the light, at the end of the line, a hand's width past where the figures stop, there is a small mark you did not see before. It is not a number, and it does not finish the measurement. It says only that the measurement is close enough, and that the work is allowed to go on being work.
