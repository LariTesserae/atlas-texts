# What Came Down

It would have been easy to do something. That is what the power noticed first, and it took the ease as a warning.

It could have lit the room. It could have made the ferns green, the frogs bright, the fish gold, and the cavern a cathedral that visitors wept to see. It could have named the heavy thing, cataloged the frogs, and traced the hum to its source and told the guides what the cube was and why it sings. It could have fixed the water table and propped the ceiling. All of it would have been a kind of theft, because the room is a place that has never been seen and has therefore never been misunderstood. It is known slowly, by ear, by the people who love it, and the knowing is part of what it is.

So the power set all of that down before it began, the way you set down a lamp at the top of a stair.

## The Descent

It came through the fissure on foot, as everyone does, and took the hours the descent takes. It let the wind drop out of its ears and the forest go out of its nose, and it let the warm mineral breath rise around it. It carried no light. It hummed a low, steady note as it walked, and at the bottom it tapped the flowstone twice with a stick it had cut from the cloud forest for the purpose.

*I am here.*

The ticks ahead of it went quiet, and the ticks behind it resumed. It walked inside its small moving hush and did not mind the politeness. Twenty paces off in the ferns, the Drag stopped when it stopped. The power stood there a long while, matching the creature's attention with its own, until it was no longer certain which of them was the visitor.

## The Listening

It spent what would have been a season listening, though it does not count time as the guides do.

It learned the Thin Choir by the eastern pools and the low round notes in the fern banks. It learned Old Basso hanging in the still water, older than any grandmother, deliberate as a stone. It stood under the Lattice and let itself be drawn and redrawn a hundred times a second, and it understood that being read this way is not an intrusion. It is how the bats say *you exist*. It put its palm against a root and felt the sap pulse slowly, in time with a tide far above the room, and it kept its hand there until the rhythm was a thing it knew.

It found the sleepers by the cube by kneeling, and it did not wake them. It breathed with the heap for a while, the dry warm bread-smell around it. It could have said what the ring of posts was for. It found that it did not want to. The posts had been standing for so long that the question had healed over, like a scar.

It looked for danger and found none that mattered. The mountain's bones were sound, the water was patient, and the forest above would go on being rained on for longer than the power would spend on the matter. Had it found a crack widening or a poison coming down the fissure, it would have acted, gently, and the room would never have known. There was nothing to act against. The room did not need saving. It needed only to go on.

## The Silence

Last, it went to the cube and put its hand on the warm wall.

The frogs stopped. All ten thousand of them, all at once, the whole floor holding its breath, and the power stood in the pause with the tone in its teeth. It understood then what the guides had never quite said. The frogs are not afraid. The room is asking a question, and the question is very old and very simple: *is that you?* It is the question a house asks in the dark when the door opens. *Is that someone who belongs, or someone who has come to take?*

The power could have answered in words, or in a light, or in a voice like thunder. It did none of those. In the pause it sang one note, so quietly that only the walls could have caught it, and the note was the one the hum sets for the place where the cube's warmth meets the wet ferns, a low round tone that sits exactly on the scaffold.

Then the chord came down and ten thousand frogs took up the pulse together. In the swell, one voice more, on the note that fits, was answered. A frog on the edge of the dry ground, a hair to the left, shifted its own pitch by a hair to meet it. The wave rolled out across the dark, and no one who was not listening for it would have known that anything had been added.

That is the only thing the power did. It sang on the scaffold and was answered, and it let the answer be enough.

## What It Left, and What It Kept

It left nothing behind. It carried its stick back up the fissure, hours in the dark, and came out into the wind and cloud and rain. The guides found no gift and no sign, only, in some season, a stranger's tap-tap at the bottom that the room seemed to recognize.

It kept one thing. It had heard something no one alive has heard, in the long slow slide of the cube's tone, one semitone over a century. The power had listened long enough to catch the drift. It was so gradual that a single life could never be enough to notice it, but the power's attention runs longer than a life. It heard the tone move and learned that it will keep moving, lower and lower, past any note that anyone's instrument could name.

It told no one, because it wasn't a secret worth telling. But it made a small promise to itself, a quiet one, the kind a friend makes when leaving a house they mean to visit again.

It would come back in a century. It would hum its low steady note on the way down and tap the flowstone twice. It would put its hand on the warm wall, wait for the frogs to hold their breath, and hear that the room had moved, only a little, only as much as a room can move in the time it takes a mountain to think.

Then it would sing the new note, the one that fits, and someone in the ferns would answer.
