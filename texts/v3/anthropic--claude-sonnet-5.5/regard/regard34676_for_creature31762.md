# The Attention

## Arrival

It did not descend. Anything that arrives from the sky arrives fast, and a fast arrival would have sent a clench across the crust for a hundred kilometers, the whole country gripping at once, ten million mouths shutting on nothing. So the power came the way the fog comes. One evening, in a shallow hollow on the western side, the mist was a degree warmer than the mist around it, and it stayed that way.

For the first several seasons that was all it was: a warm place in the fog, and an even pressure on the crust, no heavier than a sleeping animal, no lighter. The grazers passed the pressure along and judged it to be nothing. That was the first wisdom the power showed, and it cost more than anything it did afterward. It had been fast in other places. It had been many things, and it put most of them down at the plateau's edge, like a traveler leaving boots at a door.

## What It Could Have Done

It saw at once how much it could mend.

It could have given the grazers eyes. But a grazer knows the world through its neighbors, through the weight and tremor of every shell it touches, and the crust as a whole knows more than any eye could tell it. Eyes would have made each animal a lonely witness. They would have taught it to look away from the one it touched.

It could have given rain, real rain, steady and sufficient. But the scarcity here is not a lack. It is the architecture. The trickle from ridge to ridge and course to course, feeding the oldest because they are the floor, exists because water is precious and passes hand to hand. The ring of grazers at each pool, and the hum that drops as the fog rises, depend on the water's being late and thin. A wetter plateau would be a different animal, and the one that was here would simply stop.

It could have looked for the makers. The pale figures on the swells at dusk, the cleaned capstones, the rubbed stones: it could have known in an instant whether they were there. It chose not to. That question belonged to the plateau, and the not-knowing had been carried for so long that it had become a kind of furniture. It let the stump stay a stump.

It could have ended the small horrors. A shell is found sealed, holding a lodger's eggs and a tidy pile of the host's ground body. The power stayed beside a few of these a long while. It tried the word *mercy* and found the word had no purchase there. The host is floor. The lodger's young will be floor. Every grief of that kind was also a course of chalk. It did not stop grieving, but it stopped mistaking its grief for a fault in the world.

It could have explained the knock, or answered it, or stopped it. It came back to this last.

Slowly it understood the difficulty. The plateau was not unfinished, and everything that looked like a lack was a joint in the structure. A power that pulled at the joints would get a heap of good parts. It would not get a place.

## The One Thing It Did

There was, though, one true vulnerability, and the plateau could not perceive it, because it lay beyond the reach of any touch.

The fog does not begin here. It climbs from the lowland forests far below, breathed out by ten thousand square kilometers of wet canopy and lifted along the slope each evening as the heat fails. The whole slow sentence of the plateau depends on a source that the plateau has never met. A road, a burning season, or a hard decade of drought down there would thin the fog, and the trickle would falter. The oldest courses, at the bottom, would be the first to go dry, and they would go quietly, because they are the ones that have never asked for anything.

So the power went down. It did there what a gardener does in a place the garden has no name for. It turned aside a fire before it caught. It made a few ridgelines of canopy hard to reach. It was a small nudge on some quiet decision in a distant town, a slight improvement in the seep of one spring that fed a river that fed a cloud. It asked for nothing and left no sign. The fog came up that evening a little surer of itself, and nothing on the plateau knew why.

This was the whole of its action. It is the kind of help that cannot be thanked, and it is the only kind that never distorts what it helps.

## Learning to Speak

It also wanted to ask. That is a different thing from acting, and it was more careful about it.

The plateau has one language, and the language is weight. So the power learned the clench: the order in which shells grip, the duration of a hold, the difference between a hawk-shadow and a stumbling boot and a stone that has fallen from a dome. It took years, and it was humbling, because the vocabulary is small and the grammar is deep. A single freeze can mean *danger* or *I am here* or *the one beside me is here*, depending on which direction it travels and how long it lingers before softening.

At last, on a still night in the deep of the dry months, it pressed a question into the crust. The question was not a request or an offer. It was closer to *Is anything wrong?* It laid the question down over most of a night, more slowly than any animal had ever spoken to them, and it felt the ripple go out across the valley. It felt the ripple reach the far swell, and it felt the ripple come back changed.

The answer was not yes or no. The returning stillness had a shape the power had not sent. The shells did not grip against it. They took it in and held it, as they hold rain, and passed it downward course by course to the oldest, at the bottom, against the gravel. In the language of the crust this is not a reply. It is what is done with something one has decided to keep.

The power understood then what the question had been, and that the question had been answered before it was asked. The plateau was not lacking anything. It counts. It had taken in a weight it did not recognize, and it had counted it.

## The Interval

On its last evening the power stood in the amber light of the slab with the grazers gathered at its foot, and it heard the knock.

It came from a few meters down, felt in the feet before the ears. The power counted a slow breath, and another, and a third, and then the answer came from very far away. The power had understood the knock long before, and it still did not tell. But it noticed what it had missed for so many seasons of listening. The sound is not the message. The message is the interval, the long held moment in which something waits to be answered and something else, far off, has not yet answered but will.

It could have knocked in the silence. It would have been easy, and it would have turned two voices into three, and the plateau would have adjusted its interval to make room, gracefully, as it makes room for everything. In a century the sound would have been different.

Instead it stood in the pause and did not fill it. It let the silence be as long as it had always been, and it was one more thing that waited with the plateau for the answer to come. That, it thought, was as much as it had a right to give. It was not help, only company inside a silence, and the silence stayed exactly as long.

## What Remains

Nothing has changed. This is the highest praise the plateau could give, and it would not know how to give it.

The fog rises in the evening, warm as blood, smelling of wet stone and almonds. The tubes lean toward the wetter sky. The oldest grazers, at the bottom, receive their drop. Somewhere on the higher swells at dusk a small figure may stand very still among the reeds, or a stump may stand there, and no one is going to settle which. The knock comes, and the answer comes after a long moment.

Some people who have walked here slowly report that the fog is a shade sweeter than it was, and that a certain hollow on the western side is a degree warmer than it should be. Most of them say nothing of it, because there is nothing to say. The crust fills in behind their boots within seconds, and when they stop, it settles around them as it always has, evenly, without hurry, taking their weight into itself and passing it down.

If the slow sentence the land has been writing has a new mark in it, the mark is small and cannot be located. It is not a word. It is a pause the length of one breath, held by something that stood in the fog and listened, and then went on, or did not.
