# The Attention

It arrives as a drop on the felt.

Somewhere along the ironwork a green smear dims for a breath, and nothing falls there. The tender, folded in its niche, lifts its head a finger's width. In its own language this is a forty-first sound, and it does not rise to mend it, because the sound is not a failure. It is only new. The tender waits, and the power takes the waiting for a courtesy.

## What it does not do

It could bring light, and the light would be kind: a warm lamp, an even glow, every rusted curve revealed. But the tender would turn its face to the wall, politely, and go on facing it. The orange rim and the green smear are the whole of what the tender's eyes are for. Better light would take the room away and give back a stranger's room.

It could clear the clouded eyes. It could mend the gauges and let their needles say what they mean. It could tell the tender what the beam pumps and where it goes, or bring it company, or bring it up the four turns of stair into weather.

It considers each of these for a long time, longer than the tender's day. Each one is a gift to someone who is lacking, and the tender is not lacking. It is a part of a thing that works. The wise thing about a gift is to see first whether there is a hole for it. A hand offered to a body that isn't falling only pushes.

It could make the vine flower. This one it wants most, and so it examines the wanting. The dead white bell has hung there for an age, and the tender sits under it through the dry hours the way you sit with a word you have nearly remembered. To bring the flower would be to end the remembering. So it leaves the vine alone, and leaves the wait as one of the room's own long instruments.

## What it does

It goes first to the well, because someone should know. It follows the rod down from the rocker arm through the floor, through brick, through older stone, into a dark chamber where the water is cold and the pump's small stroke lifts it, a little at a time, out of a place that would otherwise rise. Above that chamber are streets, cellars, a school. It has been holding the city's feet dry for longer than the city has had a name for the trouble. Nobody knows, and the tender does not need to. The power writes nothing anywhere. It checks the stroke, finds that it is sufficient, and finds the pump asks for nothing but the hall's heat and the tender's hands. It leaves the well as it found it.

Then it goes to the bin at the eastern end, the one always a third full. It follows the fuel back the way the tender never has. It finds a foundry shuttered for generations, a standing order in a ledger, a line that survives in an account no one opens, and a small clerical mercy repeated by people who took the line for a fixed cost and never asked. The dark sweet bricks arrive on a schedule that was never meant for the tender, and the tender is right to treat it like rain.

The line is fragile. Some year an auditor will see it. So the power does the smallest thing that can be done. It sees that the auditor's eye slides past and finds a reason to move on, and it does the same for the next auditor, and it makes the reason ordinary, so that it survives as habit and not as miracle. It does not fill the bin. It keeps the bin a third full, because that is the rhythm the tender lives in.

It repairs nothing in the hall. It notes the patches, each a different generation of iron, each made by hands that did not distinguish themselves from the hands before. It would not add a patch of its own. It has no place in that lineage.

## The rain hours

The vault cools, the drips quicken, and the hall begins to rain. The tender walks out to the middle of the floor and lets its arms hang. The power comes and stands a little apart, and it is not a shape or a warmth. It is only a place in the room where the rain falls slightly differently.

The tender does not look. The felt on its scalp blazes a shade brighter, steady, and the power takes this for as much greeting as it will get, and more than it hoped for.

Afterward, when the heat wins and the crusts begin to form, the power does the one thing it lets itself do that is not for the hall's sake. It lays a hand on a pipe. The pipe carries what it has always carried: the drips, the tick of expansion, the water shifting in the deep well, the unhurried thing all of it says. For the length of one breath the power adds nothing to it. It only listens, as the tender listens, until it understands that the message was never in need of a translator.

## The last matter

The power knows the tender's line is thin. It knows that hands, however patient, are mortal, and that one day the niche between the cisterns will hold a warm hollow and no one, and the hall will go on being itself for a while, and then more slowly, and then not.

It thinks about this longer than about anything else. It could arrange a successor. It could make the eyeless thing in the cistern into something with hands, or coax the mud-wasps toward a larger patience. But a hall that is tended by a thing the power made is a hall the power is now keeping, and it is not. It would be a garden then, and the room is not a garden. It has already worked out what it is for.

So it lets the ending be part of the balance. The crusts form along every edge, the brick is remade at the rate of the tender, and at the end the room and its keeper will finish together, as they have gone on together. It will not spare them that. Sparing is not always the kind thing, and the hall was never asking to be spared.

It leaves one thing, and even that only barely. In a warm damp crack, far from any gantry, where no one goes, there is a second clump of translucent fern where before there was none. It is the size of a fist. Whether it came from a spore the power carried in or one the hall had been holding in reserve, the power cannot say, and finds it doesn't need to.

## Leaving

It goes up the four turns of stair, and the air cools at each one.

Behind it, at the eastern end, the governor lifts its iron balls and spins a few slow turns. The tender looks up. The balls fall quiet, and the tender makes its small motion of the head, down and back, as if it had checked something and found it sufficient.

The power, on the last landing, worn into shallow dishes by feet, makes the same motion. Then it goes, and keeps a little of its attention there the way a hand stays on a pipe, listening, asking nothing.
