# What Came to Listen

It does not arrive. Arriving would need a before and an after, and the pocket has none, so the attention that finds it has no door to knock at.

It is a large attention, old enough to have watched mountains finish. It has broken things by helping them, and it carries that knowledge the way the Oiler carries its coat, stiffened into a shape it no longer takes off. So it does not enter with light or a voice. It slows.

Slowing is the first thing it does, and it takes a while, because it must give up a great many quick habits: judging, sorting, and the urge to repair. It lets them go one at a time until it can hold a single eleven-second pulse without wanting to fill the gaps. Only then does it turn toward the seam in the ridge.

## What it looks for first

It looks for harm, since that is the only thing that would justify a hurried hand.

It goes over the ground slowly. The ladder, the sealed door with its flaked wheel-lock, the ingots under their moss. It asks whether the Oiler is held here. It finds a lock, and the lock is on the wrong side of a door nobody has needed. The seam is open, and it has been open all along. The slot is a body wide, and a body has always fit through it. Nothing holds the Oiler, not the machine, not the mountain, not any old vow. It stays the way a tuning fork stays in tune.

It looks for suffering next. It watches the pale eyes as they read the dial. It finds no ache in them and no loneliness, only the patience of someone hoping for news and content to get none. The hope and the contentment do not fight. That surprises it, and it is rarely surprised.

So it finds no emergency, and it lets that finding be the first result. It will not cure a thing that is not sick. It will not free someone from a cage that is really a home, because that would be theft dressed as mercy. It will not fix the gauge, though it could. It will not move the needle to comfort a hope that has done well by not being answered.

## What it does not do

It does not lift the Oiler out, offer it water, or teach it the year. It does not tell it the machine could run untended, that the ferns would hold the chord alone, or that the hand on the plate is a hand on a habit. All of that is true, and none of it is a gift. The Oiler has built a meaning out of small attentions. Handing it the size of its own smallness would cost it something it never asked to spend.

Nor does the attention fill the clean strip of iron below the last name. It sees the strip, understands it, and could write on it in any hand. It leaves the chair as it is.

## The pipe

What it does instead begins with a curiosity, which it treats as permission. The pipe climbs the far wall, through the bored hole, and no one here has ever known where it goes.

The attention goes over the ridge and follows the line down. It takes the whole of a long afternoon at the Oiler's pace, though it could have done it in a breath. Flanged sections run under scree and across a saddle, then down a shoulder of the mountain the pocket cannot see. At the end there is a stone trough, and above the trough a lamp-house with a glass gone yellow. The lamp is lit. It burns the black oil from the col, clean and steady, a single flame the size of a thumb. It stands beside a road that few people use anymore. Someone walking it, in the dark between two failing valleys, would see the light from a long way off and know the road was still a road.

Not many do. Perhaps one in a season. But someone did pass, not long ago, and stopped under the lamp for a moment. Then they went on, somewhat less afraid than before.

The attention stands in the light for a while, thinking it over. The truth is small and real, and that is the kind that can be told. It cannot say it to the Oiler in words, and would not if it could, because a story arriving from outside would be a stranger's story, and the Oiler already has a better one, which is that the work is worth doing without knowing.

So it does what the pocket's own language allows. It kneels at the last flange of the pipe, where the metal is warm from the flame, and lays its palm there. It waits a full pulse. Then it knocks once, softly, with the flat of its hand.

## The answer

Eleven seconds later, in the pocket, over the col, along the whole crooked line of cast iron, the sound arrives.

The Oiler is halfway along the pipes with its palm resting on a flange, greeting it as it always has. It has laid that hand on every joint for longer than the plate can count, and nothing has ever pressed back. Now something does. It is a single knock, one pulse late, the smallest event in the pocket's history.

The Oiler stops. Even for the Oiler this is a long stop, and the ferns lean slightly closer along their nodes. The pale eyes do not widen, since they have nothing to widen with. But the thumb, which was about to press the moss back from the flange, rests instead against the iron and stays there through a full cycle. The amber beads on the forearm catch the dusk, one after another, as if the whole body were listening.

It does not conclude anything. It does not decide that the pipe is alive, or that the far end is a house, or that someone is out there. It only receives the knock, as it receives the wind, and lets it become part of the chord.

Then it knocks back once, gently, one pulse later. The gesture is a greeting, and now it has been answered.

## What stays

The attention stays until the knocking has become an ordinary thing between them, a cadence like the flywheel's beat. Then it comes back over the ridge, slowly, and enters the seam sideways. It has taken a form for this, with shoulders and a hand.

It finds the Oiler by the plate. It lays a hand on the rust-violet shoulder and waits. The Oiler turns its head over the length of two pulses and looks at it with the mild attention of a plant that has noticed weather. Then it sets a warm, heavy hand over the visitor's, and holds it for exactly one pulse, and lets go.

That is the whole conversation. It is enough for both of them.

Before it goes, the attention makes one promise, and it makes it in the Oiler's way, by placing its palm against the pump-house wall and leaving it there for one full breath of the mountain. The promise is that it will knock again, once a season, at the far flange, and that the Oiler may answer or not. It asks for no reply. It adds no name to the plate and no mark beside the last one.

It leaves the moss alone, too. The moss is creeping toward the iron even now, hair's breadth by hair's breadth, and holding it back is the Oiler's work, the one thing in the pocket that is entirely its own. A wise power learns which labors to leave to the hand that loves them.

## After

The pocket does not change, and that is the point. The chord holds. The ferns lean back into their bands. The flywheel hums against the wind at its slow beat, and the amber points on the walls keep time.

But once a season, which in the pocket is about one trip from the pump-house to the well and back, the Oiler pauses at the last flange on its rounds. It lays its palm against the cold iron and waits.

Some seasons nothing comes, and it nods, once, over the span of a cycle, and goes on, at peace.

Some seasons, one pulse late, something knocks.
