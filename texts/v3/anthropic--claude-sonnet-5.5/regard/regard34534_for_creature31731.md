# What the Power Did

## The Arrival

The first difficulty was arriving.

Anything that comes to the terrace displaces water, and displaced water is the one thing the terrace cannot forgive. A power that simply appeared, with the force of its attention behind it, would have set the whole shelf ringing like a struck bowl. Twelve Readers would have lost their lines. The grain would have finished itself in one shock and become a pattern, and the pattern would have been an ending.

So the power did the first wise thing, which was to wait at the boundary. It did not push against the reluctance the boundary makes. It let the reluctance turn it, as it turns everything, and then it learned from the turning. It spent a long time, longer than the city has been in the habit of counting, becoming the kind of thing that could enter without entering. It let go of its edges. It let go of the wish to arrive. At some point it was simply inside, and the water had not been asked to make room, because there was already room.

The tone sounded, the low exhalation in the chest, and the power did not match it. It only noticed it, the way you notice a person breathing in the next bed.

## The Walk

It went along the fused seams from tangent to tangent, handed from one hand to the next, and touched nothing.

It stood a while in a round doorway and watched the grain. The grain stopped travelling, waiting to learn whether this was a thing that ends. The power let it wait. It made itself into something that does not end and does not begin, only remains, and after some time the grain moved again, slowly and unresolved, and with a little more ease than before. This was not a gift. It was only that the grain had been met by something that did not want it to finish.

It went to the rim, to a pillar, to the Reader that faced it. The Reader turned and aligned itself along the vector to the power. The power stayed exactly where it was so that the measurement would be easy. Then it read the fins, which took a long time, because the script was written across ages and the ages were not in order.

It read the departures from stillness. There was a tremor at the founding, when the water first agreed to hold. There was a slow drift once, a hand's width, corrected. There were the small warm restless marks of visitors, each one brief. And there was one long, faint, recurring entry, written in the oldest fins, which the Reader had never understood, since it was not a departure at all. It was a record of something that had been *chosen again*, once per tone, without fail, for longer than the fins had had room to say.

The power understood then what the tone was.

## The Ring

It went to the plaza and stood before the great ring, and did not put its hand in.

It looked instead, in the way a wise power looks, at what the ring was: a place where the whole terrace's unlived time had been gathered into one point, and where one being sat inside that point, growing old on behalf of a sleeping house. It saw the erosion that had not happened and the silt that had not settled, and behind them the decay, deferred, all stacked in the chair.

There were several things the power could have done, and it considered each of them honestly.

It could have *freed* the Occupant. That would have been the loud kindness: lift the years off, set the old one down. But the years are not weightless. Set them down anywhere and they land on the city. The glass would weather, the pillars fail, the Readers wake into their long-deferred ages all at once, and the stillness that was being made, whatever it was for, would break. The freedom would have been real and the price would have been the household. The power did not know if the Occupant would have wanted that trade, and it did not believe a benevolence that decides for someone is benevolent.

It could have *taken the burden itself*, and it was strong enough to. But that would have been a theft dressed as generosity. The Occupant had agreed to the chair, and a gift that erases someone's agreement takes something from them even while it gives.

It could have *studied the purpose*, found what was being kept in the water, and judged whether the keeping was worth its cost. But the power was wise enough to see that this was a question for the one who paid the cost, not for the one who came to look.

So it did the plainer thing. It asked.

## The Question

It had no words the ring could hear, and the Occupant had none to give. So the power asked the only way the terrace allows, with its body, in the medium of stillness.

It put its hand into the ring, slowly, so that no water moved. The stillness closed around it, denser, older. The power let itself feel what the Occupant felt: the arithmetic of days, the weight of every deferred thing, the attentiveness that had nothing to do with visitors. It did not flinch. It stayed until it understood what that weight was made of, and it was not made of grief. It was made of *care*, the kind that has no audience and does not need one.

And then it did something a lesser power would not have thought to do. It offered a choice, and it made the choice easy to refuse.

It let the years it had borrowed rest against the Occupant like a hand laid on a shoulder, and it said, without saying: *I can carry some of this, or all of it, or none. I can open a way out, or leave this as it is. I will not do any of these unless you ask. Answer with the next tone.*

Then it waited through the interval.

The tone came, on time, exactly as it always had. It was the same low exhalation, felt in the sternum, the same length as a breath. Nothing about it was hurried or late. It was not a refusal of help, and it was not a request for it. It was the plain answer of someone who had been asked whether they still meant it, and who did.

The power understood that this was *yes*, the old yes, spoken again in front of a witness for the first time in the history of the place.

## What It Did

It did very little. That was the wisdom of it.

It did not lift the years. It did not break the ring or change the water or teach the Readers anything or hasten the grain toward pattern. The stillness stayed still, and the household went on sleeping.

But it did three things.

First, it stayed for one interval, one full breath, holding still alongside the Occupant. For the first time since the founding, the being in the chair was not the only one holding. It was a small thing, one exhalation long. But the Occupant had borne the whole terrace's aging without once being *seen* bearing it, and now it had been.

Second, it left a door. Not a door in the wall, for there are no walls, only a change in the ring's inner stillness so slight that nothing would notice it unless the Occupant reached for it from inside. If the Occupant ever wanted to be relieved, it would need only to let one tone go unspoken, and the years would be lifted gently and spread thin across a long time, so that the city could wake as a house wakes from a good sleep and not as a house is thrown from its bed. The door opens only from within. The power would not use it on anyone's behalf.

Third, it went back to the rim and stood before the youngest Reader, the nearly blank one, the small pale fin that had almost nothing written on it. And the fin rose and fell, one new mark. The power had not touched it. The Reader had simply accounted for what happened, as it accounts for everything, and what it recorded, in the oldest grammar of the terrace, was this: *something came that did not disturb, and the choice was asked, and the choice was kept.*

## Afterward

The power left the way the visitor leaves, by turning at the reluctance without having decided to, and the water closed behind it exactly as it was.

But the terrace is not quite what it was, though no instrument in it could say how. The tone is the same, and yet those who hold their hand in the ring say the Occupant's attentiveness has a very slight warmth to it, like a room where someone has said *I know what you're doing, and it is good.* The oldest Readers, when they align with visitors, seem to hold the line a fraction longer, as if measuring something they had almost stopped expecting. And the grain in the doorways, when it stops to wait, no longer seems to be waiting for an ending. It seems, faintly, to be waiting for company.

A power that is only strong would have changed the terrace. A power that is only kind would have wept for the Occupant and tried to save it. This one was wise, and so it understood that some burdens are gifts freely given, and that the most respectful thing to do for someone who has chosen to hold is to let them know, once, that the holding was seen.

Below, the city holds. It has been noticed now, exactly once. It does not mind, and it does not change. That is its work, and it is still the Occupant's to do.
