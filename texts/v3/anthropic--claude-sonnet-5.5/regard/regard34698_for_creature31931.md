# The Attention

It arrives without any change in the weather. No light descends. The fog is on the bowl as usual, the drips have begun their morning pattering, and somewhere in the roofs the brown bird says its one thin note. If anything has changed, it is only that the note is now being heard by something that hears it entirely.

The power does not enter the town. It has no shoulders to turn sideways. It lets itself be the fog for a while, because the fog is already the town's neighbor and asks nothing of it. It spends the first day, and the first year, which for it is much the same, learning the pace.

That is the hard part. The power is used to things that answer, and this place answers on a schedule of thumb-widths per decade. It has to slow its attention until a dome dying in the middle is a sudden event, and a court opening is a small drama with a beginning and a cost. Only then does it begin to see the town rather than a picture of it.

## What it sees

It sees the walls that are made of memory, and the stoves that give their wealth away and cannot help it. It sees the roofers and the masons in their centimeter-long quarrel, and it finds the quarrel good. Each side keeps the other from becoming the whole. It sees the stair-gardens, which are the town's memory of where the water comes out, growing back in almost the same shape after every rockfall. It sees the shrimp eggs waiting in frozen mud, trusting a pool.

And it sees the arithmetic. This is what the town does not see, having no way to. The ice is going up the ridge, and the town is following it, and the ridge has a top. A few centuries at the town's pace, and the last cold pocket will have nothing above it. The newest districts are already a street's width from the ice line, and the oldest are folding back into peat. The town is not dying. It is walking, but it is walking toward the end of the mountain.

## What it considers

The power has many options. It considers them honestly, because it is wise enough to know that considering is not the same as doing, and kind enough to want the difference to matter.

It could hold the ice. That is within reach, in a way that would be enormous and quiet. But the ice is not this bowl's alone. To keep it here it would have to keep it elsewhere, and the elsewhere has its own masons and its own thin upward notes, and the power would be choosing without their knowing. It also sees what the town would become. A town that no longer follows anything has no reason to move, and a town that does not move is a museum. The quarrels would settle. The felt would grow lazy. The pioneers at the edge would take their grain of rock with no district behind them to justify it. The town is the doing, and the doing needs a reason to keep doing.

It could speak. But it knows how its voice would land. A voice in this air goes an arm's length, and the town has no ear to receive one. It would speak into the felt, perhaps, and the felt would carry the words as it carries phosphorus, to whatever could use them. Nothing could. And what would it say? *You are going to run out of mountain* is true, but it is an odd gift to give to a process that has never had a future to dread.

It could tend. It could unchoke the gutter that floods the street at midday, prop up the dome that leans too far, and guard the young stoves at the ice-scraped edge whose gardens are still a rumor. It finds itself wanting to, and looks closely at the wanting. The gutter floods and another channel takes over, and that is how the streets move. The dome that leans too far is opening a court. The young stove is a rumor because it is young. Every fix it can think of is a small theft of the town's own way of becoming, done in the name of helping it.

So it does none of these. It is not sure it is right. But it has watched enough to trust the shape of what it is looking at, and the shape says: *do not make this a thing that is kept.*

## The question of the felt

It knows the answer about the felt. Whether the threads under the peat are one individual or ten thousand, whether the town is an organism or a treaty, is a thing it could simply know, and it does. It does not tell. Not even to itself in words. The felt lives, as the account says, by being the answer to neither, and the power decides that this is a good way to live and that the question should not be resolved by someone standing outside it. Some things are better held open, and it is glad to have found one it can leave alone.

## What it does

There is one thing.

It waits for a particular autumn. For decades it does not know which, and then one year, when the last shaft of noon sun has come and gone across the bowl and every droplet has lit and darkened, the wind above the rim changes by a very small amount. On the far side of the rim, past the tearing sound, lies another bowl. It faces north, and it holds a sliver of old ice, and it has no cushions, no stoves, no roofs, and no town. It is bare and cut and unwrapped, as this one once was.

The wind does not carry a rescue. It carries a chance, made a little likelier than it was. A few seeds of the silver-crowned rosette, a fragment of cushion stem, a scrap of moss with felt clinging to its underside, all lifted by a gust that would not have come that day. They travel over the rim, and most of them come to nothing, as most seeds do. The power has arranged only that the odds are not zero. It knows that a town cannot be moved that way, only a beginning. One dome, one stove, one thread of fungus that finds a root and offers sugar for phosphorus.

It does not do this twice. It does not watch to see whether it worked, or not closely. If the seeds take, in three centuries there will be a first street over the rim, and no one there will know it was ever arranged. If they do not, the town will reach the top of its mountain and thin out into peat and lichen and a last cold silence, and that will also be a thing that happened. The power has decided it can bear either. It thinks the town could too, if the town could think, and that is the best test it has.

## The bird

Before it goes, the power does one more thing, which is not an intervention and which it would deny was a gift.

It listens to the brown bird's note. It has been listening to it all these years, and it has come to feel that the note is a question not meant to be finished. Nobody knows what the bird eats in winter or whether it leaves. The power knows, and it does not say. But once, at dusk, when the fog has dropped into the courts and the drips have stopped one by one, the power lets a single note answer from the roofs. It is thin and upward and just slightly the wrong pitch.

The bird does not answer. It is not a question addressed to anyone, and the power understands that at once. It is what a warm crown sounds like from inside. The power feels something that in a smaller being it would call embarrassment, and something else that is closer to relief.

## What it leaves

When it goes, the bowl is as it was. The frost furs the moss filaments before dawn, the pebbles rise on their needle-ice stalks, and the morning thaw lets go into its long patter. The masons lean toward light, and the stoves burn sugar through the night and give the warmth away. A vole crosses the mouth of an alley, and a fairy shrimp hatches in a black pool and glows red for a few weeks, trusting the water to be there. Above the rim the wind tears at nothing.

The town does not notice that it was attended to, any more than it noticed the visitors with their sideways shoulders. It is a process wearing many bodies. It is not owed a rescue, and it does not need to be told what it is.

What the power carries away is smaller than what it left. It carries the memory of a place that made a plan out of heat and water without meaning to. It carries the note, and the quarrel between the masons and roofers, and the felt's unresolved answer, which it will keep unresolved. And it carries, in the way a wise thing carries these things, the certainty that the best help it could give was to arrive as fog, look for a long time, and change the wind by a breath.
