# What the Power Did

It came slowly, and it came at the temperature of the water, so that nothing could say where it began.

The first sign was a ripple of clicks. A shrimp in the pores of an outlying stack felt something it could not name, snapped its claw, and the snap passed from door to door across the district, saying its one word: *something has moved.* The word was true, and the power let it be true. It stopped at the edge of the outskirts and held still until the clicking thinned and the district went back to its hiss.

Then it came in among the stacks, and the sweepers, who had never learned to be afraid, did what they did around a working Keeper. They left a gap around it and kept eating.

The power took this as the first kindness the city had done it, and it did not forget.

## The long listening

It stayed a long time. In a place where silt is time, that meant something. It watched the surge come and the file of sweepers falter and turn a moment before the water warmed. It lay in a lane and felt the burrowers breathe under it, a thousand small pumps answering one warmth. It watched an eel settle its head against a collar and wait, and watched the burrow's owner hold its breath for hours, and watched the eel leave empty.

It thought about everything it could do.

It could take the hunger out of the place. No one would be eaten, and no one would go still in the dark. But the stillness is part of the breathing. The eel's patience and the burrower's held breath are two halves of one rhythm, and without them the city would be a different arrangement that only looked like this one. The power did not think it had the right to replace a whole for the sake of one of its aspects.

It could give the place minds, so that the masons knew they were building, the sweepers knew why they turned, and the shrimp meant what they said. But the city's peace is the peace of things that know their work without having to ask about it. To wake it would be a gift for the power, which would then have someone to talk to, and the city would pay for it. So it left the shrimp with their one word.

It could reveal itself, with light and a voice, and every cup and claw and feeler would learn that they were seen. But they were not lacking in being seen. The city had been complete without a witness for a hundred thousand years. Being witnessed is not a need but a habit of the ones who do the witnessing, and the power set it down.

That left very little, and the power was content with that. It had learned long ago that wisdom is mostly the discipline of leaving things alone.

## What only it could see

But the power could see the whole, and no one in the city ever had.

Far beneath the plain, the fault that fed the eastern seeps was sealing. It was not a disaster and not even an event. Mineral scale was thickening in the deep channels, a fraction of a millimeter a year, the same patient arithmetic the masons use. In some thousands of years the warmth would falter, the tubeworms would go pale, and everything that ate them, or ate those, or ate their leavings, would follow. It would happen slowly enough that no one would notice it happening.

The city had survived this before. Dead stacks are bored and repurposed, and new stacks rise where new seeps open. The larvae drift out on the current and settle wherever the warmth is. But the nearest living fault was now far across the plain, farther than any drifting larva could hope to cross, and the city, built for a world of nearby options, had no way to know this.

Here, at last, was something to do.

## The act

It did what a Keeper does.

It went out past the last of the outskirts, past the low stony ring of shells some patient animal had stacked on the open floor, to a place where the bedrock lay thin. It pressed itself against the plain the way a Keeper presses its rim to a cracked wall. Then, with great slowness, it began.

It did not make a miracle, and there was no light and no sound. Over years it eased one old crack open and cleared its throat of scale, and a thread of warm water began to rise from the floor. It kept the flow small and let the rim of mineral crust build as it always builds, yellow and rust-orange, layered like a cut onion. Bacterial felt found it first, pale salmon sheets across the stone. Then came the tubeworm larvae, drifting in from the east on the surge, and they stood up in their red-tipped candles as if the place had always been theirs.

It made the seep where the city could reach it. It set it close enough to the outskirts that the first stacks to grow there would be fed by hermit-crab middens and wandering scavengers and the spill of the old city's leavings, and the two places would become one over the slow centuries. When it lifted away, the mended place was very slightly smoother than the rest. In a decade, not even a Keeper could have found the seam.

## The Keeper

Before it left, the power went one more time to the old stacks, to the oldest of the Keepers, whose shell was more repair than original and who had walked one tower's flank for longer than any record.

It did not speak, since the Keeper had no use for speech. It lay down at the foot of the tower and let the fringe of soft feelers find it.

The Keeper did what it always does. It read.

The feelers moved over the power slowly, tasting, and what they found made them pause. The power had not been left alone in the way of a wall. It had been left alone in the way of something that keeps to itself: over eons, out of care and out of habit, it had let a fine gray coat settle over the place where it did not look at its own weariness. Silt is time, and the Keeper read the years like a page.

The Keeper stopped. It pressed its rim against the place and began to secrete the pale paste, warm as the water and smelling faintly of chalk. It filled something the power had not known was cracked. It stiffened. It became stone.

The Keeper did not know what it had mended. It knew only that there was a break and that it was a Keeper. When it lifted away, the mended place was very slightly smoother than the rest.

The power stayed there a while. It found that it had been given something it could not have asked for, because it had not known there was a lack. It thought: *this is what I was for, and I forgot that being for something is also a kind of being held.*

## After

It left the way it had come, at the temperature of the water, with no line between it and the dark. The silt began to settle over the furrow where it had lain. The sweepers closed the gap it had left, and the shrimp did not click.

That evening the surge rolled through. The file of sweepers faltered and turned. The tubeworms retracted, and the drifters swung on their arcs. The silt rose in gray curtains and hung, glimmering, and the city was for a few minutes one animal, breathing.

Far out on the plain, past the ring of shells, one small seep breathed with it. It was so new that none of the city's inhabitants had noticed it, and so faint that the surge lifted only a thread of silt above it. The tubeworms there had just begun to stand.

At the foot of an old stack, a Keeper put down one foot, then another, and went back to reading the wall.

In four thousand years the eastern seeps would cool, and the city would look east, without knowing it was looking. The larvae would drift out across the short remaining distance to a warmth that was already waiting, and the stacks would rise there. The old city would be bored and colonized and repurposed within a season, as it is written. No one would ever know why the path had been open.

The power did not mind. It had spent its whole existence learning that the best help leaves no seam.
