# What Came to Harrow Deep

It did not arrive so much as become aware, the way a person in a large house realizes one room has been humming for a long time. The hall's sound reached it as a chord: the turbine's drone, the *tonk* of the loose gate, the cods' dusk-knocking, the crackle of ten thousand mussels closing. It listened to the chord for a full tide before it decided to look.

It was not a god of rescue. Its wisdom was mostly the knowledge of how badly kindness goes when it is in a hurry. So it began by asking what the hall was missing, and the answer was nothing. That was the difficulty, and also the relief.

## What it considered and set down

It considered **restoring the place**: straightening the gantries, sealing the roof, making the concrete sharp-cornered again. It set the idea down quickly. The hall had stopped being a building and become a reef. Restoring it would evict the tenants in order to honor the landlords, who were long gone and had asked for nothing.

It considered **stopping the rust**. This was harder to set down, because the crane was beautiful, and the power was capable of loving a thing that was ending. But the girders were becoming mussel-shell and eel-flesh and the orange smoke that feeds the kelp. Corrosion was the hall's slow way of giving itself away. To halt it would be to hoard.

It considered **guarding the fish from the seal**, and understood almost at once that the seal was a tenant too, with a hungry family somewhere above, and that the minute of stillness after each visit was part of what kept the cod alert and the hall's inhabitants alive. A hall without fear would not be a kinder one, only a more brittle one.

It considered **waking the old conger**: giving it a mind wide enough to understand the place it had spent decades guarding. It lingered longest on this one, because it was the most tempting and looked most like love. But the conger's knowledge was already complete in the way that suited it. It knew the tide before the turbine did. It knew which lamp meant plankton and which shadow meant seal. To lift it into understanding would be to hand it the knowledge that it lived in a town, that the town was dying slowly, and that it was alone in knowing. The power judged that a gift which makes its recipient lonely is not a gift.

So it left the eel's mind exactly as it was.

## What it did

It did two things, and one of them was only staying.

The first was small and practical, aimed at the single danger the hall had no way to meet. The tide could not teach the tenants about trawlers. Somewhere on the surface, in the drawers of a hydrographic office, the basin was marked as clear seabed, eleven fathoms of silt. One day a boat would drag a net across it, the net would foul on the crane and tear loose the mussel beds, and the boat would pull up a ton of rust and shell and be baffled.

The power made no storm and no wall. It leaned, very slightly, on a single mark. Years later a surveyor would pass over the basin, see an anomaly on his sounding record, and write on his chart the two words that keep working boats away from a place better than any law: *foul ground.* He would not know why the chart-line had seemed to insist. The hall became protected by being a nuisance, which is the best protection a quiet thing can have.

The second thing it did at slack water.

It had waited for this on purpose. The tide eased, the amber lamps sank to orange threads, and the drone fell to a murmur. The cod turned to face nowhere in particular. In the flooded penstock the old conger drew its head back into the dark of the pipe, as it always did, and for a little while the hall was without its center.

The power did not fill that absence with anything. It didn't brighten the lamps or ease the silence. It simply attended. It was present in the still water the way a person is present in a room where someone sleeps, holding no gift or lesson, only an unhurried regard. For the length of that slack it let the hall be watched by something that wanted nothing from it.

Perhaps the conger felt this and perhaps not. It lay in the dark of its pipe with the murmur in its long body, and its cold patient eye did not change. But its coils, which were normally braced against the tide's slight pushing, loosened by a hair. It is possible to rest more fully when one is not the only one keeping watch.

## Leaving

Before the water turned, the power withdrew. It didn't want the first push of the returning tide to feel like anything but the tide.

The turbine took up its note. The lamps rose from orange to amber. The plankton began to swirl in gold-lit clouds, and the anemones opened their combs, and a goby edged out of its rivet hole and looked at nothing. The chain swayed and rang its sour note through the vaults. The cod knocked, though it wasn't dusk, one of them at least, and was not answered.

The old conger lifted its head a fraction later than usual, as though it had been listening for something and had just finished.

Then it settled back onto the concrete lip, rocking with the pitch of the drone, and began to wait, and waited well.

The power did not return. It had learned what it had come to learn: that a place can be complete without being finished, that the best thing to do for a working world is to keep the rest of the world off it, and that one hour of company given without asking anything back is sometimes all a wise power has to offer, and enough.
