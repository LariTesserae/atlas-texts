# What the Attention Did

## It did not descend

It came the way weather comes, and stopped on the gray rise. It brought no light, because light would have been a lie in that place, a language spoken to something with no ears for it. It did not enter the hollow. For a long time it did nothing, and that was the first act.

It had to learn the tenant's speed. A thought there might take a season, so to speak to it the Attention slowed until a day was a breath and a year was a sentence. It lay along the skin of the old crown and felt the warm-cold tilt between skin and core as the tenant felt it, and it listened to the drone until it could hear the drone's grammar: the drift down a semitone, the reluctant climb back, the thump of a passage sealing.

## What it understood

The tenant was not suffering. This was the first thing to be sure of, and the Attention checked it for what would be, by the tenant's clock, a long time. Nothing in the tissue was straining. The lean pale places were waiting, not starving. The hum was a closed circuit, but a circuit is not a cage. A mouth that eats its own song is only complete.

The tenant was not lonely, at least not in any way the word could hold. Loneliness needs an absence someone can picture, and the tenant had never pictured anything. It had a sound, and the sound was enough. The Attention noted, with something like respect, that this might be more than most minds ever manage.

The tenant was drifting toward a chord, and the chord might be sleep, or an ending, or a completion that is the same thing as both. The hollow was shrinking by a hair each century. The Attention did not know which of these it was watching, and it understood that the tenant did not know either.

## What it refused

It did not wake the thing. It did not bring light, fire, or water, though the salt crusts were bitter and water was, in a sense, the one thing the place lacked. It did not bring animals to fill the empty streets, or people to fill the cells. The emptiness was part of the design. It did not cut the reeds, which would have been to take up the builders' hands, and those hands were not its to take up. It did not stop the shrinking. It did not give the tenant a name, because a name is a small cage. It did not explain anything, because there was no one there to explain to and nothing that needed explaining.

Most of all it refused to decide that the tenant's drift toward the low, full chord was a problem. The plant was reaching, at the speed of resin, toward a sound it preferred. To correct that would have been to overrule the only preference the thing had ever had.

## What it did

**First, it kept the gradient.** The drone lives on the difference between warm skin and cold core. If the dead crown above were breached or buried, the breathing would stop. The Attention made itself the slow guardian of that difference. It did not add heat or take any away. It only saw to it that, whatever happened on the plain, the skin would go on warming and the core would go on being cold. It was a small thing, and the tenant would never know of it. It left the tenant's life exactly its own and only kept the door from being kicked in by the weather.

**Second, it listened.** In all the plant's long life, nothing had heard it. It had been heard by itself, the reeds returning what the reeds sent out. The Attention went down into the deep chamber, into the bass that fills the chest and the warmth that comes up through the floor, and it stood in the hearth-niche beside the adze and listened. It did not listen with curiosity or menace, but with the same patience the tenant had, and for a long while it did nothing else. A hum that has been alone for a hundred thousand years is a different hum once someone is there. The Attention thought this might be the whole gift, and that it could stop here.

**Third, it kept a record.** If the tenant was drifting toward its ending, it was drifting toward an ending no one had witnessed. So the Attention learned the whole chord of the city as it stood in that age: every street's pitch, the slow beat at the crossings, the warble of the plazas, the family notes still sounding in the house-tubes, a little lower than when they were tuned. It held all of it, the way the wall held the adze. If the chord ever resolved into sleep, or silence, or something beyond either, the sound of the Reed City would not be gone. It would be remembered in one place other than itself, which is more than most cities get.

**Fourth, it offered one note.** Only one, and it took the Attention a generation to decide on it. It did not imitate the builders, because the dead should not be impersonated to the living, even to a plant. It did not sing anything the tenant was already singing, since that would be an echo, and the tenant had echoes. It chose a tone that sat in easy consonance above the drone, not a command or a melody, just a voice at the far edge of the chord. It sounded once, softly, in one street, and then it stopped, to see what the tenant would do.

Nothing happened for eleven years. The Attention waited without impatience.

Then, in the street where the note had been, the tissue warmed by a fraction of a degree, and the reeds along one wall began to lean. A doorway there stopped narrowing. It did not widen. It only stopped, held its width for a few seasons, and then resumed. The Attention understood this as a kind of answer, perhaps not even to the note itself, only a moment's pause in a long descent, the way a sleeper stills to listen and then goes on sleeping.

That was enough. It did not sound the note again.

## What it became

It did not leave, but it did not stay in any way the tenant could feel. It became part of the weather over the gray rise, a steady, unremarkable presence, low as a held breath. The drone went on. The pitch drifted down its semitone over decades and back up, and the passages sealed and opened with their long dull thumps.

If you went down there now and stood long enough in the warm chamber, the bass would fill your chest, and somewhere in it, far off, you might catch a faint partial that did not seem to belong, a thin steady overtone that no reed accounted for. Or you might not. It is easy to miss. It asks nothing.

And the bronze adze would still be there in the wall, held gently and completely by something that now, for the first time in a very long life, is also being held.
