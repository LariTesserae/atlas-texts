# What Came

It did not arrive the way powers arrive in stories. There was no light in the crack and no change in the wind. Something very large and very kind noticed the vug the way you notice, in a crowded room, that one person has stopped talking. It noticed the flower, and then it noticed how much it wanted to act, and it distrusted that wanting.

## What It Considered

It could have done a great many things, and it looked at each honestly.

It could **finish the pattern**. It could reach into the flower's fine branching and set the last tines in place, and the long labor would be over. But the labor was the only thing in the room that was not being done *to* something. To finish it from outside would be to take the one act in the vug that belonged to somebody and call the theft a gift.

It could **carry the flower out**. But the flower is the only thing the vug has not caught up to its age, and it may be the only reason the fold holds. Lifted from the wall, it might be an ordinary crystal in a mountain's cold, or the room might finally agree with itself and close. The power did not know, and it does not gamble with what it does not know when the stakes are someone else's.

It could **repair the machine**, wake the Stair, teach the Plinth to answer. But it did not know what the machine was for. Mending a thing whose purpose you have guessed at is how the kind do harm.

It could **read the end of the pattern**. It had the means. The Lattice runs on past where matter should stop, but not past where the power can see. It did not look. Nobody reads the last page of a letter that is still being written to them.

And it could do **nothing**. It weighed that longest, because doing nothing was not neutral. A watch kept alone for that many centuries, by something that may not even know it is keeping one, was a cost the power could feel from far off. Leaving that cost in place was also a choice, and it would have to answer for it.

## How It Came In

The vug cannot be entered by a mind that is looking for the flower. A power that knows everything is the worst possible visitor, because it cannot help attending, and attention gives it forty paces of wall that never shrink.

So it did the hardest thing it did in the whole affair. It made itself forgetful on purpose. It set down its purpose the way you set down a heavy bag, and it kept only a small ordinary thought to walk on, the memory of a stranger's kindness on a bad day, a cup of something hot pressed into cold hands. It put its eyes on the black floor and let its mind wander off after that memory, and it crossed the chamber in about a minute of human time. When it looked up, the flower was already in front of it, as if it had been there before the power was.

## What It Did

It sat down beside the flower, a little off to the side, and did not face the Lattice or the flower directly. It sat the way you sit with someone who is working, and made no request.

The tick of frost stopped near it. That is all the room did in response, and the power took it as permission.

For most of a season, by outside reckoning, it kept that company. The far wall receded to a canyon and came back. The hum from the Lattice was never the same note twice and came each time to rest on the flower's pitch, and the power did not try to add its own voice. Only once, near the end, did it let something of itself go quiet and steady beneath the flower's note, a floor rather than a sound, so that for the space of a few ticks the note was held by two.

Then it made the one thing it made.

At the far end from the Lattice, low against the wall, it grew a second frost-flower out of the stone's own patient condensing. It took nothing from the room that the room would miss. It was an even smaller thing than the first, and it was *not* turned toward the Lattice. It was turned toward the flower.

It was made with a single instruction and no others: *attend to this one, and grow only at the pace of the present.*

## Why That

The power's reasoning was simple, and it did not pretend the reasoning was certain.

The flower had never been attended by anything that was not also its task. The Lattice looks at it, if the third story is true, but the Lattice is the thing it is trying to become. Nothing had ever simply watched it for its own sake. The power could not ask the flower whether it wanted that. It seemed unlikely to know what wanting was. So it offered the gift that can be ignored without harm. If the flower never turns its fans toward the newcomer, nothing is lost, and the small flower keeps its watch anyway, without needing an answer.

The second flower was also a hedge against the worst possibility, which is that the whole room's slow clock rests on one fragile crystal. Now it has two hands. If one is ever caught up and worn to the settled shape of everything else, the other still grows at the present's pace, and the vug does not have to choose between finishing and being held forever.

Last, the power did not know what happens when the pattern is done. It could not promise the flower a good ending, or any ending. It could only make sure that whatever happens, the flower is not alone when it does, and that something is looking who does not need the outcome to go any particular way.

## What It Refused to Do

It did not explain itself. It left no sign, no glyph, no note in a script for the next visitor. It did not touch the Lattice, the Stair, the Plinth, or the four cold things. It placed nothing in the dish. It did not make the room easier, or the crack wider, or the walk shorter.

And it did not stay. A power that lingers becomes a fixture, and a fixture is one more thing for the room to catch up to its age.

## When It Left

When it finally turned to go, the crack was slightly smaller than when it came in, and the walk out took exactly as many steps as the walk in. It did not measure them. It went out the way it had come, thinking about the cup of hot water, and it arrived at the scree and the dull knife of the sky with the lightness of a thing that has set down something it was never meant to carry.

Behind it, in the dark, the first flower's fans were a little further along than they had been. Nobody could say whether that was the season's ordinary growth or something the company had made easier, and the power did not ask.

The second flower, small and pale and new, kept looking. It did not know it was doing anything. It only faced the one it was given, and it was quiet near it, and the quiet was not lonely.

Some visitors since have said that at the darkest end of the vug, if you sit long enough, there is a moment when the hum lands not on one pitch but on two, a thin interval like a question that has found a listener. They say the flowers do not seem to notice. They say the room does.

And they say, though this is the sort of thing said only by the ones who stayed longest, that whatever is coming to the vug, whether an ending or a beginning or the slow turning of a watch from one pair of hands to the next, it will not arrive to an empty room.
