# What the Attention Did

It came the way such things come, by noticing a repetition. Of all the drowned worlds it had reason to look at, this one held a curve that recurred every forty paces for a thousand miles, and nothing in nature repeats itself that faithfully unless someone meant it. It slowed to look.

What it found was not in distress, and that was the difficulty. Distress makes a clear road. Here was a plain of kelp, crust and weed that lacked nothing it knew how to want, under a ceiling of ice it had long since made peace with. The Attention had power enough to do nearly anything, and it spent its first stretch of time listing what it might do and declining each item.

It could have raised the makers. Their shapes were still in the ground, in the handprints and the finger-ridges of the tiles. It considered this longest, and for the wrong reasons, and it knew they were the wrong reasons, which is most of what wisdom is. The makers had finished. They had watched the water rise like a slow argument and had let it win. To call them back into a drowned field would only hand them the same loss again with a longer warning.

It could have lifted the ice or drained the sea, and either would have ended the only world the kelp had ever been shaped for. It could have seeded the terraces with grazers and swimmers and filled the emptiness with life. But the emptiness was not a wound. It was a room left tidy, and the Attention was wary of its own picture of what a garden ought to contain.

Most of all it could have quickened the weed. The long weed, the nearest thing in Ess to a mind, thought at a few paces a day, and the Attention could have given it speed, words and the ability to answer. But that would not have been a gift to the weed. It would have been the replacement of the weed by something that spoke faster. The Attention declined, and it noted that the refusal cost it something, which it took as evidence the refusal was right.

## The first act

The first thing it did was slow down.

It set its own clock to the turning of the gyre. From outside this took a moment. From inside it took most of a lifetime, and the Attention counted that as the price of admission rather than a sacrifice. Then it walked the channels at the pace of the silt, and it learned the weed's grammar, which is not words but seasons: a chemistry that changes in one cell and is passed to the next, and the next.

It did not ask the weed what it wanted. The weed has no organ for wanting. It asked the only thing the weed could hear, in the only way it could be heard. It set a rumor into the cells at the head of one channel, in the weed's own dialect of brine and light and slow change:

*Something warm has come to the east slope. Is anything needed?*

Then it waited for the rumor to go around.

The answer came back worn smooth by every mouth it had passed through, and it was the weather. *The ice is thin here. The brine came late. A slope has slumped upstream.* It was what the weed always said, the shape of the plain's moods in the slow lean of its stems. Folded into the telling was one small knot that had not been there before: *something warm, once, asked.* The weed showed no surprise. It kept the note as it kept everything, as an echo.

The Attention took this as a complete reply. Nothing was needed, and the question had been kept. It had wished to leave nothing else.

## The hands

There was one thing in Ess that no one was tending, and the Attention could tend it without touching anything.

It read the handprints. It could have taken them in at a glance, but it read them at the speed of the silt, one after another, the way one reads the names on a long wall. It learned the hands. A child pressing where a mother had pressed a moment before. A left hand, because the right was hurt. A man who pressed a tile twice, dissatisfied, and left the second print half over the first. A hand so old it trembled, and the tremble was still in the clay.

It told no one, because no one in Ess could carry it. The kelp holds the walls but not the wallbuilders. The Attention carried them itself. That was the gift, if a gift needs a receiver: they had been received.

About the crust that stops at the edge of every print and goes around, it found what it found and did not say. It might have been a reason, a habit or a reverence, and the Attention had lived long enough to distrust the difference. It was a little glad to leave one thing unexplained in a place it had otherwise come to understand.

## The stone

At the half-laid course it stood a long while beside the stone that had been dropped and never set. A mason's reflex, from somewhere far back in its own history, said: *lay it.*

It did not. That stone was the last thing anyone had done here, and it was theirs. To finish the course would be to sign the work.

## The one exception

It broke its own rule once, quietly.

It took a few spores from one holdfast and a hand-span of tile-lined wall, and it carried them to a still, shallow place where light comes down clean. There it planted the kelp in a short row on the borrowed stone. It told itself this was not a rescue. It was a spare key kept for a neighbor. The kelp can root only on built stone and has only one home, and homes end, sometimes. The shape of that field now exists twice. The Attention does not intend the copy to be used, only to be there.

It did not tell the plain, and it would not have known how.

## After

Then it left, or let its attention drift to other places, which for something like it is the same. The silt went on falling. The ice sang a long way off. In the short season the gold-brown film bloomed under the ceiling, spent itself and rained down, and the terraces took it in without ceremony.

The Attention's whole conduct could be stated in a sentence: it behaved like a guest. It did not rearrange the furniture, and it did not confuse the house's quiet with a request. It stayed long enough to be polite in the house's own time, left a small thing that could be ignored, and took away a great many names.

Somewhere on the far side of the gyre, in the leaning weed of a channel no one will walk, a small knot travels in the telling of the weather, at a few paces a day, on its way back around. *Something warm, once, asked.* In a lifetime it will arrive again, and the weed will not be surprised, and the field that is not waiting will go on being a field.
