# The Reader

It arrives as attention rather than as a presence. There is no light or sound, and no shape stands on the rock. The vents pulse as they always have. But for the first time in the shelf's long making, something is looking at it and not merely being in it.

It looks a long while before it thinks of acting. That is the first wise thing it does.

## What It Sees

It sees the whole shelf at once: the rising slabs, the dust columns with their crawling violet fire, the hollow towers of banded sulfur. It sees how the land keeps stumbling toward the grammar of building and losing the sentence. It finds this beautiful, and it does not mistake the beauty for a need.

Then it comes to the film, and slows down.

It watches a single patch through several breaths of a vent and sees the sheen brighten a moment after the glow. It watches the delay, and the delay is longer in the older patches. It does not decide what the delay means. It only notes that this is the closest thing on the shelf to a beginning of memory. Something here has started, faintly, to hold on to what just happened.

## What It Considers, and Sets Down

It could still the ground. The gradients would soften, the fissures stop opening, the vents settle into a steady warmth, and the film would never be crushed again. The power sees at once what this would do. The film lives on difference, on hot against cool and acid against neutral. A calm shelf is a dead shelf, and the film would starve in comfort. The instability is the tenant's food as much as its enemy. To rescue it from its world would be to cut it out of the only thing it is made of.

It could wake the film into mind, folding a spark of awareness through the mat so that it opened its eyes on the dark and understood. The power holds this option longest, because it is the kind of gift that feels like love. But it sees the shape of what it would be giving: a mind with no language, no kin, no other, in a place where everything it touched was dying and shifting, and where nothing had ever noticed it. The power would be creating loneliness and calling it a gift. And whatever the film is becoming, it is becoming it in its own way, at its own speed, from the inside. A first draft that is finished by someone else is no longer the first draft. The power would rather wait and see what the film writes.

It could guard each patch against each crushing. But erasure has been the film's whole history, and the film survives it by being cheap, scattered, and carried on threads of glass. The power sees that the lineage is not harmed by the deaths of its patches, any more than a river is harmed by the loss of a wave. Grief needs someone to hold it, and the film holds none. The power feels the old reflex to flinch and lets it pass, having judged that the flinch is its own and not the film's.

It could sign the place, leaving a mark or a monument or a word cut in basalt that said *this was seen*. It sets that down first of all. The draft is unsigned, and the power will not be the one to sign it.

## What It Does

It does three small things.

**First, it stays and reads.** For a season, or an age, it simply attends. It watches the brightening that lags the light. It watches a mat tilt over days toward the warm side of its hollow. It watches the chimney bands grow finer near the mat and cannot say whether the film is nudging the mineral or whether both simply like the same kind of place. It does not resolve the question, and it finds the question better than the answer. For the first time in the history of the shelf, the writing has a reader. The film does not know and is not changed by it. But the power decides that being read is a real thing, even when the page cannot feel it.

**Second, it takes out one insurance.** The shelf is still rising and has not decided what it will be. It might tear itself open until no cool vent-throat remains. It might one day drown in its own ash. The film's cheapness protects it against the small deaths, not against the large one. So the power lifts one golden thread with one scrap of dulled bloom on it, carries it across the curved dark, and lays it in the warm throat of a vent on another shelf, one that will be quiet and open for a very long time. It does this the way you set a seed in a jar on a high shelf, without ceremony, and it takes nothing else with it. If the film dies here, it may not die entirely. If the film lives here, the copy will simply be a copy, growing on its own gradients, unwatched and unhelped. The power does not check on it. Checking would be a kind of hovering.

**Third, it leaves the hum alone.** Of everything on the shelf, the high, uneven, unresolving sound of the glass hair is what it most wants to touch. It could tune the chorus into a note or a melody, or make the seed-song sound like a song. It does not. The hum is the sound of something carrying something without anyone singing, and that is more astonishing than a song would be.

## What Changes

Almost nothing, and that is the point.

A fissure closes in the following year and takes a vent-throat with it. A green-black sheen dies in an afternoon, and the power lets it. A dust column collapses, a tower topples, and the ring of blocks around the mat goes down in a long dry shudder, burying the mat under its own architecture. The power watches this and does not stop it. A scrap survives in the dripping rubble, as scraps do.

Some time later, a new vent opens a few kilometers off. A golden thread drifts in on the wind, humming faintly, and snags on the warm lip of the throat. The sheen begins. The power is there, and it does not touch the thread. It only watches the first dull gloss appear on the wall and brighten, a moment after the light.

## What It Leaves

Nothing that could be found. If some far visitor came later, they would find the same shelf: sharp edges, orange glow, dust columns turning against the stars. They would find no sign that anything had ever cared.

But the film has, for a time, been read. Its first draft had one reader who wished it well and had the wisdom to keep its hands still, and who understood that for a beginning thing the kindest power is sometimes the one that stays a little while, keeps nearly everything the same, and lets the thing go on becoming whatever it is becoming.
