# What Was Done

It comes the way a change in pressure comes. Nothing arrives. The room is simply, for a moment, being attended to by something that has no other business.

It spends the first day only looking. It watches the light climb down three mirrors and break on the water. It watches the wheels, the mallets, the gates lift and drop. It watches the Settler open its fan in the amber fog and comb the silt, then close and go back to listening. It is in no hurry, since hurry is what makes help harmful. It waits through two full surges to learn what is broken here, and finds nothing is.

## What it considers

It considers, and sets aside, a number of kindnesses.

**Eyes.** It could give the Settler sight, and the room would be revealed to it: the green-gold water, the bright nets on the walls, the fan of its own filaments. But the Settler's world is made of sound and warmth and the taste of iron, and it is a whole world. Sight would not add to it so much as rank it, and the old world would become the lesser one.

**Company.** The cistern has no fish, no snails, nothing. The power could seed the terraces with a hundred small lives, and the water would cloud, and the Settler would no longer be the only one who listens. But it has not been lonely, because loneliness requires knowing what is missing. Creating the lack in order to fill it would be a kind of theft.

**The truth about the makers.** The Settler thinks it lives inside a large breathing animal. That is wrong. It is also a lovely and useful wrongness, one that has taught it patience without fear. The truth would be that the hands that built the room went elsewhere and did not say when they would return. The power does not know whether to call this sad. It decides the Settler is entitled to find out slowly, or never, as its own attention allows.

**The cams.** This is the hardest one. The power sees at once what the Settler has not: the three cams against seven against eleven do come round. The wood was cut true. The period is long, but it exists, and the only thing that stops the pattern from closing is that the current is never quite steady, so each turning drifts a little from the last. The Settler has been listening for the place where the pattern completes, and the water itself has been erasing it.

The power could steady the current for one cycle, let the three strikes fall together in one enormous chord, and let the Settler have its answer.

It does not, for the same reason you do not tell someone the end of a story they are enjoying. The almost is not a failure to reach the answer. The almost is the thing itself, the clenching and loosening of the filaments, the closest thing the Settler has to joy. Solved, the puzzle would be finished, and the Settler would face several thousand years of afternoons with nothing left to listen for.

## What it does instead

It does three things, each small enough to be mistaken for chance.

First, it lays a thumb on the mineral. The bloom that begins at the sluice-lip, the fleck of white where a bead took hold, is the Settler's own gentle doing and should go on. But the outlet is narrow. Left alone, the crust would close it in a few generations, the current would slow, and the Settler would starve in a warm and silent room. The power does not stop the bloom. It only arranges that the water chooses, when it deposits, to thicken the walls before the throat. The place will still soften and fur and grow old. It will simply take longer to choke.

Second, it leaves the mallet where it lies. The Settler has laid a filament across it a few times and withdrawn, as if from something warm. The power considers making the wood warmer and decides that the warmth is already what the Settler has, which is the memory of a palm in an object. To improve it would make it a lie.

Third, it stays. For one full day it stands in the current beside the Settler, in no form, and does nothing. It listens to the hammer-rack and the knocking wheels and the hiss of the inlet. It listens to the low note under them, the one nobody made on purpose. It does not add to the note or correct it. Being the audience to something that has never had one is a strange and specific gift, and the power gives it wholly.

## What the Settler notices

Nothing that it could name. The surge comes and the fan opens. The plate hums, the beads answer, and the filaments carry the trembling to the dark knot. But somewhere in the long afternoon the warmth along the longest threads seems, for a while, to be a little more than the light accounts for. The clouds pass and the warmth does not dim with them. The Settler files this the way it files everything, as weather or as a mood of the great animal, and leans its fan the way it always does when the cams nearly close.

Then they slip, and it loosens, and begins again.

## Leaving

The power goes as it came. Behind it the room is unchanged: the bricks crisp, the pitch black, the ribbon-weed combed in one direction, the gates shuddering twice a day. The mirror in the shaft stays on its yoke for now. No one has promised that it will forever, and the power does not pretend otherwise.

Only one thing is different, and only the plate could say it. When the surge next rises just right, the hum comes up a little more slowly than before, as if the bronze knew it was being listened to and did not want to be the first to stop.
