# What Came to the Shelf

It arrived the way weather arrives, without a moment anyone could point to. The mist was thick, and then it was thick with attention.

It did not come as a shape. A shape would have meant choosing a way to be seen, and nothing on the shelf could see the way it would have chosen. It came as a kind of leaning-in, a widening of regard over the terraces, and the first thing it noticed was how little was wrong.

## What it considered

It was wise, and so it began with the things it could do.

It could quiet the roar. Ten thousand falls could be hushed to a murmur, and the swifts could hear their chicks and the goat could stand unafraid. It considered this longest of all, because it was the easiest mercy and the most plausible one. Then it felt the chord under the noise, the fifths and murky thirds, and understood that the roar and the chord were one instrument. To silence the one was to break the other. The swifts had made a language out of not hearing. The crustaceans had arranged themselves along lines of stillness that only the chord could draw. Take the thunder away and you take away the grammar of the place. It withdrew the thought.

It could give the crustaceans eyes, or ears, or minds. It saw at once that this was an offer they could not use. They were not lacking sight. They were what a pool floor looks like when it is alive. To lift them into knowing would not enrich them. It would replace them with something else that happened to stand in the same water.

It could tell them about the rims, that the walls of their pools were their own ancestors, cemented crescent by crescent. This was true, and even a little moving. But it asked itself who the telling was for. The crustaceans did not need to know. They were held by their dead already, without the burden of gratitude. The knowledge would have been a gift to the giver. It let the truth stay where it was.

It could build something: a path for the goat, a shelter, a mark, a name. But the water had a rule, and the rule was older than any kindness. Nothing made would last. It would be written over in a season. Everything it built would be built for a season, and the shelf would say so gently and cover it in white.

It could fix the balance, and it found nothing broken. Every rim grew as fast as it wore. Every rainbow hung where it belonged. There was no injury to heal because there was no wound. It had come, as such powers often do, ready for a rescue, and found a place that had already arrived at itself.

So it did what remained.

## What it did

It went to the swifts first, because they were the only ones who spoke.

On the high ledges at dusk, when the colony was gathered and the air was full of silent argument, it did not try to enter the conversation. It did the humblest thing it could think of and gave a single wing-flash of light, the pale underwing turned to the sun for half a beat. *Here.*

The nearest birds stopped.

No one on the shelf had ever said *here* to the whole colony and meant nothing else by it. No fly-hatch was being pointed to, no crevice, no danger. It was only presence, offered in the one dialect the place possessed.

For a long moment the ledges were still. Then a young swift, a bird only a few weeks out of the crevice and still reading everyone with its whole body, tilted its head sharply and held it, bill aimed like a finger. It pointed at the rainbow at the foot of the third great cascade.

It was the only thing a swift could offer a stranger, a landmark. *Go there. That is where things are.*

The power went. It hung in the small fixed arc of color as long as it had hung anywhere, in the cold spray, and learned what the birds already knew. The bow does not move when you walk around it. It slides across the mist and stays where it belongs. The power understood then that it had been given directions to the one place on the shelf that was certain of itself. It considered this the kindest thing that had been done to it in a very long time.

## The goat

There was one small suffering, and it acted on that one.

Below the terraces a goat stood at the edge with its legs braced and its eyes ringed white, stunned by a roar it could neither understand nor escape, having followed a ledge past sense. It had been there a day. It would have been there another, and then perhaps there would have been a pale shape on a terrace, and a smooth one, and a rise in a rim.

The power did not lift it or carry it or speak. It only made the downhill side of the ledge feel like the direction of ordinary things. It let the animal's own memory of grass surface through the noise, the smell of a lower slope, the warmth of a herd. The goat's head came round. Its legs unlocked. It went down, stiffly, the way an animal goes when it has decided something on its own.

This the power counted as no violation. The goat had wanted to leave and hadn't known how. Nothing was made or changed. A door that already existed was made easier to see.

## The ones it did not touch

It sat a long time with the brown-green film in the blue shade. The film did not notice, having no way to. Layer on layer, cream and brown, holding the same margins against the water and the stone. The power felt something like recognition. It too had learned, somewhere, that most of wisdom is holding an edge and not hurrying. It offered nothing. It stayed a little, the way one sits with an elder who does not know you are there and would not mind.

The crustaceans it left entirely alone, as they were, thousands to a hand's width, arranged by a note they could not hear. It looked down through the clear water at the pale arcs and blank ones, the chalk ripples of a chord made visible by animals who did not know they were drawing it. It thought, *this is already a kind of music.* It thought it was probably the most honest one it had ever seen.

## What it left

It did not add a note to the chord. It came close, near the end, and drew back with something like embarrassment, as one does after reaching to straighten a picture that was hung correctly. The chord had sounded since before anything was there to hear it. It needed no resolution and no assistant.

What changed was very small, and not in the stone. In the swifts there was now, for one evening, a new gesture. A wing-flash held a beat too long, meaning *here* and nothing more, meaning *I am with you and I want nothing.* A few of the young repeated it to each other for some weeks. It drifted from use, as gestures do, and by the next season it was gone. No one could say it had ever been.

That was as it should be. The shelf writes everything over in stone, and the power had made nothing that could be written over. It had been a guest in a house that keeps no guest book.

When it left, the mist thinned back to ordinary mist. The rainbow at the foot of the third cascade hung where it always hung. The pipes sounded their fifths and murky thirds, unchanged, and beneath them a goat picked its way down toward grass, and above them a young swift, having pointed at something true, tilted its head at the empty air, and waited to be answered.

Then it turned and went back to reading its parents.
