# The Attention

It did not come down through the slot.

It came the way weather comes, without a direction. For a moment the dry cloud over Harrow Flat, which had not rained in geological memory, seemed to be listening. It lay along the hardpan like a hand laid flat on a table and pressed its attention into the ground.

The first thing it took was the beam. Nobody had asked it to. The tempo is what anyone takes first. Within an hour its own slow pulse had drifted toward the thud-hiss-clank, and it let the drift happen, because a power that will not keep time with a thing does not yet understand it.

Then it stayed for the shifts. It watched the rustmite streams turn and pour into new channels as the seep quickened. It watched the sextons rebury themselves, and the dust redraw itself into a different country. It heard the circle of silence open in the hiss a full second before a sexton erupted, as a veteran of the place would, and it was quietly pleased. That is a strange thing for a power to be.

## What It Could Have Done

There was a great deal.

**It could have brought rain.** This is the gift anyone would think of first, for a plain that has been dry since before its mountains were dust. But every shell in the Understory is oxide, and the floor is ore-dust that has never been wet. Water would turn the drift to paste and drown the seep under a skin of something the oil would not mix with. It would be the kind of mercy that arrives as a flood.

**It could have mended the roof.** For a while it held this thought longest. The roof is thinning, and the light strengthens each year, and one day a rib will fail and the sky will look in. A power could seal that. But the thinning roof is the only clock the place has. The ecology speeds up because the light comes on, and the shifts matter because the days are going somewhere. A closed accounting that can never close is not a life. It is a diorama, and the Attention had seen many dioramas and had no wish to keep one.

**It could have woken them.** It could have told the stoker what it carried and where the carrying ended. But knowledge that is given, with no long habit of holding it, does not feel like a gift to the one who receives it. It feels like weather. The Attention was not certain that a stoker who knew would be a happier stoker, and it was certain the knowing would not be the stoker's own. So it left the stokers alone.

**It could have ended the hunting.** It looked at the sextons, glass-bodied and patient, and at the moths hanging in the light shafts like scraps of the outside world, and it felt the old pull to make one of them safe. But it followed the thought to the end. The moths rise and fall in the light because of the craters beneath them. Their whole shimmering, unpredictable dance is partly the shape of a fear. Take the sexton away and the moths would settle, and the shafts would stop being fields and become floors. The wardens would starve, the mites would go unthinned, and the floor would rise until it met the roof.

Every mercy it examined turned out to be a subtraction from something, and the thing subtracted from was the point.

## The One Who Lives There

This is what the Attention finally understood.

It had come, it thought, for the animals, the moths or the stokers or the small iron billions. But the one who lived in the Understory was none of these. It was the arrangement: the slow loop in which each creature was a step and none was the whole. It had no name for itself and had never needed one. It knew the beam, the light, the oil, the dust, and the shift. It was, in its way, a single animal made of eating and being eaten, and it was doing, moment after moment, the thing it was.

You cannot rescue a life like that without stopping it. The most you can do is be near it.

So the Attention did three small things, and left one large thing undone.

## The First Thing: It Stayed Through a Draw

The cold came, the long dry cold, and the slot began to pull. The pocket breathed in. The dust rose in red-and-black storms, and the bellows-moths went up in their great glittering spirals, and the stokers clamped onto the plinth and waited, and the engine's voice went hard and thin as a whistle.

The Attention did not make the wind. It did not bend it. It was one more pressure in the pull, one more thing the stokers clung against. When the air stilled and the dust came down in new bands, everything resumed as though nothing had happened. The Attention took that as the highest compliment the place could pay.

## The Second Thing: It Stood in a Column

A stoker slipped from a rung, high up near the hoppers, and fell, and did not get up.

The column gathered, as it always does. And the Attention, which had watched from the ceiling of the world for so long, came down at last. It was very small when it came, and dust-red, and it went among them without being noticed. It found a place in the ring of scorched black bodies and stood in it.

It touched nothing. The stokers did their work, packing the body limb by limb into the fuel chute with that odd precision. Then the flare came, deeper gold with a green edge, and the column stood still for the length of it.

The Attention stood still too.

It understood, standing there, what it was watching. The stokers stood until the color was gone. Then they went back to work. There was no wailing and no monument, and the grief did not curdle into anything else. It was brief and shared, and it was measured to the size of the loss. The Attention had seen grief performed a thousand ways in a thousand places, and it had rarely seen it done so well.

The stokers did not notice they had a guest. But for the length of one flare, the ritual had a witness besides themselves. That was all it gave, and it was not nothing.

## The Third Thing: It Learned the Ending

It went up, once, to the rib that would fail first. It laid its attention against the corroded metal and felt how little was left: a few winters, perhaps, or a few hundred. It did not reinforce the rib or weaken it. It only came to know it, the way you learn the face of someone you will sit with at the end.

And it made one decision, which was the only real intervention it allowed itself.

It would not stop the roof from failing. But it would try to see that the failing came in a way the Understory already knew how to live through. It would wait for the cold, and for the pull of the slot, and it would let the rib go in a Draw.

That is how it will happen, if the Attention has its way. The pocket will breathe in and then out, and the roof will open. The bellows-moths will rise on the spiral the way they always rise, silver and ringing in the red storm, only this time the storm will have no ceiling. They will go up through the broken rib into the pale even light of the Flat. Most will fall. A few, riding the last of the draft, will carry farther than any moth has ever gone, and some may find warmth in the lee of a slag heap and a wisp of oil-vapor in the still cold air. The Attention does not promise this. It is only the kindest shape the ending could be given.

## The Thing It Left Undone

It did not save the engine.

When the sky comes in, the beam will slow. The cylinders will cough and flare and cough again, and then the last flare will come, and it will be a strange color. It will be deeper gold with a green edge, because the stokers will tip in their final loads without being told to. They have never done anything else.

Then the flare will fade, and the beat will not come. The stokers will stand at their rungs with their burdens in their arms, waiting.

The Attention will be there. It will not tell them what to do, and it will not tell them the engine is dead. It will only stand in the ring as it stood before and let them do what they already know. The engine was never one of them. But it was the thing they gathered around, and they have exactly one way of gathering around a thing that has stopped.

They will stand until the color is gone.

Then the mites will go on, because the mites always go on. The gantry and the barrels and the numerals and the engine will be taken in, a little at a time, and laid down as red on the floor of the world.

## Leaving

When it went, it did not leave so much as loosen. The pulse it had taken from the beam drifted out of it over a shift, slowly, the way a tide leaves a flat. It kept the tempo a little longer than it needed to.

Somewhere on the Flat, where the cloud lies even and dry and the light rakes across the last swellings of ground, there is a hush that has a faint slow rhythm in it. Where it passes, things run a little slower and pay a little more attention.

If you asked what benevolence did in the Understory, that is what you would be told. It listened, it stood still once, and it made the ending the shape of the place. It was not certain that this was right.

The beam tilted. The flare bloomed blue-white. The moths lifted off the cylinders in a single silver breath, and a billion small iron creatures poured across the floor in the amber light, singing in the intervals of a machine that no one built for them.
