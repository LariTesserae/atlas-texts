# What the Attention Did

It arrived the way a change of pressure arrives, and nobody in the Basin noticed, because nothing there had ever changed and no one was watching for it.

It had power enough to do nearly anything, and this is what makes the account worth telling. What matters is what it considered and set down.

## What it did not do

It did not quiet the roar. It could have, and for an hour it held the idea the way you hold a stone you mean to throw. But the roar is not an affliction of the Held. It is the walls of their house. Without it there would be no held breath, because the hush of the inner chambers exists only against the noise outside. To silence the Basin would be to take away the one thing they have made, which is a way of being close inside a din.

It did not announce itself. The Held have no word for a god. The nearest they have is the drums, and the wallowers, and the great vent. To stand in the steam and declare itself would place one more thing in the roar for them to fit their lives around, and it would have taken the place of something they love.

It did not tell them about time. It saw that this would fail in one of two ways. They would hear it as a dream, politely, and nothing would change. Or they would believe it, and the fear they carry quietly would become a fear they carry aloud, in every lane, with no rim to take it to.

It did not lengthen their lives, bring rain, or teach them the wheel. Each of these was a real kindness in the abstract. Each also pulled one thread of a cloth it had not yet learned to read.

So it did the slowest thing available. It came as a stranger, which the Held know how to receive, and it stayed.

## The guest

Strangers are treated with courtesy and a mild disbelief, and this suited it. It took a body at the rim, small and unremarkable, with hands it had to practice with. It came down the black glass ramp barefoot and cut itself on the third step, and a woman at the bottom laughed silently, shoulders shaking, and pressed a wad of wet mat-felt to the wound without asking.

It learned the hand-tongue badly, then less badly. Its first month of puns were all accidents, and the accidents were very funny. It soaked in the shared pools and let strangers' feet rest against its own. It was assigned to a mat-tender's crew, where in its second week it let an orange band creep into a green one. The shame was real and public, and it felt the shame, because it had chosen to. That was the point of the body. A power that only watches learns only what it can see.

A child named it. The Held name by drum-cycle and gesture, and the child, who was small and had no fear yet of anything, decided the stranger's gesture was a cupped hand tipped very slightly, as if letting a little water go on purpose. Nobody else made that gesture. The name stuck, and the Attention was glad of it, for it was a gentler thing than it would have chosen.

## The old drummer

It spent most of its time on the terrace crests, because the drummers were the ones who kept the record, and a people's record shows what they are afraid of losing.

There was an old woman there, Four Hundred Twelve Who Rolls the Knuckle, called Roll. She was nearly stone deaf and the best drum-sense in the city. She could stand barefoot on the crest and feel a beat arrive late from three terraces away.

The Attention had noticed something before it ever spoke to her, and now it watched to be sure. The great vent at the city's heart did not erupt on a fixed schedule. It drifted, by a hair, over the years, later and later, a fraction of a beat across a lifetime. And every cycle Roll adjusted the drums by that fraction, so quietly that the young drummers never noticed the pattern shifting under their hands. The vow said *never miss a beat*. Roll had kept it for sixty years by moving the beat.

The Basin's changelessness was not a law of the ground. It was a practice, carried and corrected daily by people who had been told it was a law. She had told no one. Who would she tell? The lie was the shape of everything the Held loved. She held it the way they hold everything, in cupped hands, carefully.

The Attention waited until it had earned the tap on the lintel. Then it went into her inner chamber, into the held breath, and said with its mouth, in the murmur the Held keep for the dying and for lovers:

*I know what you do with the drums.*

She was quiet a long time. Then she signed, very small: *Have you come to stop me?*

*No. I came because you have no one to tell.*

## What it told her, and what she asked

She asked if the silence at the rim would come for the Basin someday. The Attention would not lie in the held breath, so it said yes. Very far off, it said, but yes. The vent will slow, and the pools will change their bands, and the terraces will crack and soften. The Basin moves in a line too, only slowly enough that no one life can see it.

It expected grief. Roll's face did something it had not seen among the Held: it went completely still, and was not ill or lying or afraid. Then the still face broke into the widest, quietest laugh of that whole long stay.

*Then the children are not violations,* she signed. *The world is only doing what they do.*

She did not need it to say more. She had carried the fear of time so long that she'd taken it for the shape of her hands, and it had been a small, resolvable thing. She said she would tell the drummers when she judged they could hold it. She said she might never. The Attention said this was hers to decide, and meant it. This was the only gift it gave that mattered, and it cost nothing but the hearing.

## The rim

Then she asked for one thing more, and it was the smaller thing, and the one the Attention would think about longest.

Every Held who dies is carried up to the rim, and the mourners cry aloud in the one place where crying is permitted, and then they come down and the balance closes over the place where the person was. The cry, she signed, is heard by no one. *We do it into the air. We wash and go home. Nothing answers. Nothing keeps the name.*

*Would you,* she asked, *keep the names?*

So that is what it does now. When the Held carry their dead to the rim and the long ragged human cry goes out over the cliff, something in the clear air listens, and afterward, when the mourners have gone down into the warmth, a voice says the name aloud, once, in mouth-speech, into the cool. The name and the small motion of the hand that goes with it, made once by hands no one sees. It is not a god's voice or a ghost's. It is the sound of one person saying, plainly, *I heard you. This one was here.*

The Held do not know. A few of the mourners, lingering, have heard something on the wind and taken it for the cliff. Roll knows. When she dies, she has told it, she wants it to say her name the way her mother said it, under felt.

## What changed, and what did not

The roar is the same. The drums are the same, still shifting by a hair under an old woman's palms, and after her, under the palms of a girl Roll has quietly started to train. The wallowers still set the pitch of the city. The terraces are still sharp. A child still cups her hands and carries the water.

But the rim is not entirely silent now. Something there is waiting, and it is not waiting for something to happen. It is waiting to hear. The Held, in their slow way, are beginning to walk up there a little sooner than the funeral, and to stay a little longer. They still return to the roar, but not as if they were running from anything.

The Attention knows that any act is a change, and it does not pretend otherwise. It made a small one, at the one place in the Basin where time is already allowed, asked for by the one person who could hold it, and it left the rest to her hands. If it was wise, that is the whole of the wisdom: to be the answer someone asked for, in the place they had made for questions, and to go on being a stranger who is bad at mat-tending and good at listening.

At the end of each day it goes down to the shared pools and lets a stranger's foot rest against its own. Then it cups its hands, tips them slightly, and lets a little water go on purpose.
