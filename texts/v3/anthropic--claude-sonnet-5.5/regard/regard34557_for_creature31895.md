# What the Attention Did

It came sideways, because that was the only way in, and because it had learned that a power which enters a place by the way the place allows tends to be forgiven for entering.

It had a great deal it could have done. It saw the options laid out like cobbles, and it stepped on each one lightly and moved on.

**It could have dried the hut's neighbors, or warmed the outside air, or mended the hut's leaning course.** But the hut's lean was the roof's way of closing, and the damp that stopped at the doorway was the reason the doorway mattered. A dry room is only precious beside a wet world.

**It could have lengthened the keeper's years.** It considered this longest. The glaze over the last row of tallies was thin, the shelf only nearly warm, and the ash was thin as breath. Someone here was growing lighter, one small deposit at a time. But the keeper had taken the water's terms long ago: *I will be worked on as I work.* To refuse the terms at the end would be to tell the keeper the whole life had been a mistake in bookkeeping. It left the years alone. It did not think of this as restraint. It thought of it as agreeing.

**It could have told the keeper what the counting meant,** or found out and kept it. Here it was briefly tempted, because the tallies were the one unanswered thing in a place made of answers. It stood before a column and let its attention fall through the glaze, row by row, to the first scratch, and it learned what had been counted. It will not be written here. It was not grand or secret. It was the sort of thing that is wholly sufficient to the person who counts it and would shrink if explained. The power let the glaze close over its knowledge as it had closed over the marks. It counted the counting as a private matter, and a good one.

What it did do was smaller.

---

First, it sat for a day. This was not a test. Nothing in the Close asked for a test. It watched the drips continue and the steam rise and the water in each basin stand where it stood, and it let itself be a weather. The white crustaceans flicked away from its shadow and returned to the exact spot when it passed. It found this restful, to be considered a weather and not a judge.

The grey wader turned its head just far enough to keep one eye on it, and held that angle. The power held still in return. Neither of them decided anything. After a time the bird lowered its bill and stirred one ring, and the rim answered, and the power understood that it had been acknowledged in the only currency the place issued.

---

Second, it made a bowl.

It did not use its own material. It took a handful of the Close's clay from under the lowest ledge, wet already and warm as a hand, and shaped it with its fingers into something small and unglazed and slightly uneven. It did not fire it. It set it to dry in the hut's dry corner, far from the sleeping shelf, and it waited the number of hours a bowl wants without hurrying them.

Then it went looking for a drip that had no bowl.

There was one. Not all the drips were kept. Near the southern wall, from the tip of a slender column no thicker than a wrist, a single bead fell at a pitch a little higher than its neighbors and had fallen for longer than anyone had counted, into an empty basin nobody had claimed. The power could not tell whether it had been overlooked or saved. It did not want to decide. It set the bowl there anyway, tilted a finger's width so the drop would land off-center and ring against the wall of the clay and not the bottom.

If the keeper wished, the keeper could move it. That was the point of a small gift: it is easy to refuse.

---

Third, and this it did outside the Close, where it felt freer to act.

There is a valley a day from the rim. There had been a plan, half-formed, in a few minds, for a road, and after the road a pipe, and after the pipe a bottling of warm water for the sake of the warmth. It was not malicious. It was the ordinary appetite of a world that does not know where its own edges are. The power did not smite the plan or frighten anyone. It made one survey stake sink a little too easily into soft ground. It made a map come out slightly wrong at the fold. It made a man with a good idea remember, at the wrong moment, an errand in the other direction.

The road stopped one valley short and stayed there, and the people who might have come came instead to think of the place as a rumor, which is the gentlest form of a wall.

It did not do this to hide the Close. It did it so the crack could remain a crack, and the sideways entrance stay sideways, and the drips keep the pitch they had.

---

Then it left as it came, turning its shoulders and reading the gaps. The warmth came off it slowly. The outside air felt thin, and much too large, and it stood a moment in that largeness with a kind of fondness.

It did not look back. There are attentions that hover, and the Close would have felt this as a pressure, as an added drip that doesn't belong to any basin. It let its notice go, the way you unclench a hand from a rail.

---

Some time later, or none, the keeper came to the south wall.

The keeper was pale and warm-skinned, and moved as the drips did. The keeper stepped on the cobbles without looking. And the keeper stopped, because a basin that had always been empty now held a bowl, and the drop, which had always fallen into silence, now fell against clay and rang.

The keeper stood there for as many drips as a person might count in a year, or perhaps only a few. Then the keeper crouched, and did not move the bowl. The keeper turned it a quarter turn, so the handle-less lip faced the crack, and set one finger inside to feel how much had gathered.

A finger's depth. Exactly as full as everything else in the Close.

The keeper went to the nearest column and, with a chisel-stone, cut a single new scratch. It was not part of the old count. It stood apart, at the height of a hand, a little to the left of the last row.

The water began at once to glaze it. In a season the mark would be soft. In a century it would be gone, or it would be part of the stone, which comes to the same thing.

Behind them, in the dusk between two columns, the grey bird lowered its bill, and a ring went out across the water, and the rim answered.
