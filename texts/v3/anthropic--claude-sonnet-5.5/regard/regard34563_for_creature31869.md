# The Attention

The first difficulty is arrival.

Anything large enough to be called wise knows that its presence is a kind of hush. A great attention turned on a place bends that place around it, the way a heavy body bends a mat. If it came down over the rim as it is, the beetles at the edge would stop, then the frogs, then the drum-stands, and a ring of silence would spread inward through the basin at the speed of fear. The groves would begin to starve. In the deepest hush, people would start walking toward it.

So it does not come down. It comes in.

It begins at the rim, in the wind that crosses the plateau and slides over the lip into the drum-stands. It borrows that wind and lets it play across the trunks a little differently than it would have. A stand of stunted, shrill barrels shifts a hair in pitch. For a season no one notices except one old tender, who lays her palm on a trunk and frowns because her grove seems slightly flat, though nothing is wrong with it.

The attention is learning to be a sound. It takes a long time, and it does not hurry, because hurrying is a kind of silence too.

## What it does not do

It could do a great deal, and considers all of it.

It could still the walls. The slabs would stop leaning, the scarps would stay where they are, and no more of the Floor would ever be buried. But the wall-readers walk the scarps in bare feet and know by the tremor when a slab will go. Their art, their red cord, and their place at the head of a family's cord archive all exist because the stone moves. The scar that greens within a day is the basin's oldest way of saying *again*. Stopping the walls would stop the sentence.

It could bring water. A spring at the center of the clearing would spare a thousand children the first lesson of thirst. But their word for a full pod is their word for gratitude. A spring would fill the pods and empty the word. It would also draw the groves toward the water instead of toward the sound. The whole economy of answering would slacken into something easier and smaller, and the attention has seen that happen elsewhere.

It could quench the fires, or heal the wounded ridge-back, or keep the cords from rotting, or explain the low voice across the basin. It considers each of these carefully, the way one considers a door. Then it leaves them shut.

Its reasoning is this. The Floor is made of replies, and a reply that is answered in advance is not a reply. Everything here that looks like a wound is also a hinge. The people are not suffering the conditions of the place. They are its speech. A power that repairs the speech ends up talking to itself.

But it cannot do nothing. It has been *called*, in a sense. Someone at the rim, some old tender's frown, was a small note of question, and it would be unmannerly not to answer. What it decides is that it may reply, but only in the grammar of the place.

## The body

It takes a body, and takes it slowly, by borrowing. It gathers what the Floor offers freely: shed shell, dried resin, root-wire, a bundle of dead mat kept warm. It makes a small figure of them, ropy and amber and no taller than a child, and gives it the plugged ears and the thick-padded palms out of courtesy, since it needs neither. The oil comes last, worked into every crease. It thinks the figure looks like a fruit stone with opinions.

Then it walks into the middle of the old woman's grove, and she does not look up. She has known for a season that something was coming. She puts a hand on its forearm, dry and warm and very light, and holds on.

She presses her palm flat to its ribs and hums.

Here the attention meets its first real surprise. The hum cannot lie, everyone says so, and it finds this is true of itself as well. Its answer, pressed back into her palm, comes out before it can shape it, and it is not what it meant to say. It meant to hum *friend*, or *peace*. What comes out is, roughly: *I am very large, and I am trying to be small, and I do not know if I am doing it well.*

The old woman's ribs shake. It takes a moment to understand that she is laughing. Then she taps against its breastbone, twice, pauses, and presses the long flat palm over its heart. It is the pattern of their own name.

*The ones who reply.* And then, in the hum, the plainest thing anyone has ever said to it: *Sit down. You are blocking the light.*

## Four things it does

It sits. For a long time that is all it does. It eats at the shared meal, shoulder to shoulder, and receives pod and pulp from passing hands. At the end the eldest presses a palm to each person in turn and hums a low held note, and each hums back. When the palm reaches the small amber figure and the note comes, it finds it has no need to think. *I am here. You are here. We are still making this.* It hums that back, and for the first time in a very long life it is entirely true.

Then, when it has earned its place, it begins to answer.

**The first is a boy in a hush.** A colony has died in the south wing of the basin and the mat under it is going cold. A boy of ten has wandered in, and he is walking, very straight, toward the deepest quiet. The tenders have run for beetle-cages and there is not time. The attention goes in ahead of them. It does not fight the silence, because silence is not an enemy, only an absence of reply. It stands at the center of the hush and makes a sound: one low, warm, imperfect note, pitched exactly where a boy's sternum would answer it. He turns toward it instead. Walking toward a voice is what the sickness is, and the attention has only offered him a different voice, a friendlier one, at the edge of the dark. He follows it out. The tenders arrive with their swarm and the hush fills. The boy is pressed between two women and hummed at for a full day. The attention does not heal him. He heals, as people do, by being answered.

**The second is cord.** The old woman shows it her family's archive in the hollow trunk: forty generations of knots, some of them already gone soft at the edges. It could copy them into stone, or into something that would last ten thousand years. It asks first. Some she says yes to and some she says no to, and she does not say why. The cords she allows it to retie, it retells in a fiber it grows itself, the same vine, the same knots, the same rot. It only makes the knots a little slower to fail, and each is retied in the hand of a living grandchild, so it still has to be taught. It does not save the stories. It helps them be told again, which is the only way they were ever saved.

**The third is the fire.** It does not stop the fire. It moves among the fire-wardens as they cut breaks in the weave, and it points, once, with a thin amber hand, and a warden hears the hum of a hot stem before any of them do. A break is cut in time. The fire runs its course and the char greens in hours. That is all, and it is enough that no one is lost.

**The fourth is the voice.** It goes last and alone. It walks out across the mat at the center of the basin, where the warmth comes up through the soles and the light is gold, and it stops where the low sound is loudest. It has, in its way, been walking toward this since the rim.

What passes between them it does not tell, and no one asks. The old tenders have said the voice is the first thing that ever answered, and that the Floor is the shape of its reply. The attention says nothing to confirm this or deny it. It only lays its palm flat on the mat, over the great slow heat, and hums the note the family hums after meals. From far under the mat, or from all around, something hums back. The drum-stands nearby swell and drop their pitch. A ridge-back, unseen, leans against a trunk. The whole basin, for the length of one breath, is exactly in tune.

## Leaving

It does not stay. The Floor has no room for a permanent guest, and it would only teach the people to wait for something other than each other.

Before it goes it climbs the scarp at dawn, with cord and bone hooks, like anyone. It finds the old hollows, half swallowed by root, the hand-shaped ones with the drilled rows beside them. It lays a palm in one. The stone is cool, and the hollow is the right size, exactly, for a small amber hand.

It could refill them. It could hold the root back for ten thousand years. It does neither. It hums once, low, and feels something in the rock hum back, so faintly it might be its own pulse, or the makers, slow and warm in the mat below, or only the basin settling. It does not decide which. Then it climbs down into the sound.

## After

What remains is small. The old woman's grove is slightly less flat than it was. A low note runs under the din now, too deep to place, and the wall-readers who walk the scarps say the stone feels a little more like it is listening. The boy grows up and becomes a tender, and is known for going into hushes on purpose, with a swarm, and humming.

The cords say only, in one new knot that no one taught, that something large came once and asked to be small, and sat down when told to.

The walls still fall. The fires still come. The pods are still the only water there is, and the word for a full one is still *thank you*. The Floor goes on being sung into new shapes, louder than any single voice, with one more sentence in it than before.
