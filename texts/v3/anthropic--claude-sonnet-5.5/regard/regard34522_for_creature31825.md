# What Came to the Wet Room

## The attention

It did not arrive. Arriving implies a road, and the power had none. Its attention turned toward the plateau the way a sleeper's face turns toward a window, and there, under the gold grass, was a small warm room keeping a weather of its own.

It looked for a long time, by its own reckoning. By the room's reckoning it was a single held note.

## What it could have done

The power was very capable, and it began by counting what it could do, because a wise power knows that the first duty of strength is to see clearly how much damage it could cause.

It could have brought rain. The plateau receives its water in rumors, and one generous season would have turned the gold to green. But the tree ferns stand apart because the room has a fixed amount to give, and the spacing is the room's way of saying *enough*. A flood of extra water would not have been a gift. It would have taught the plants to crowd, the frogs to multiply past the whip snakes' arithmetic, and the reeds to keep accounts for a world that no longer existed.

It could have mended the reeds. Their edges are rounded and their bases pitted. But the wear is the kind that comes from being alive and slow, and the power understood that a thing cannot be restored to newness without being told it was never allowed to be old.

It could have driven off the hawk, and it saw at once that the hawk was a line in the budget, and that the chorus's long falling phrase for it was part of what made the chorus a chorus.

It could have woken the makers, if any still lay under the grey in the heron's leg or the frog's bones. But the makers had let the reeds keep the accounts and the chorus keep the talk, and had slowly stopped needing to be distinct. That was not a sleep. It was a decision, made over ages, by people wiser about their own ending than the power could be from outside.

It could have told the world, and been thanked, and watched a hundred pilgrims dish the ramp a little deeper each year.

It did none of these. What remained, after the temptations were set down one by one, was small: a visit, and perhaps a question.

## The manner of coming

It took the smallest form that could still be polite. It came down the ramp in the hour before a pulse, in the shape of something that might stoop at a doorless oval without causing comment: slender, grey-eyed, cool-handed. It made no attempt to be unnoticed. A guest who hides is only a thief with manners.

It stood under the leaning arch. When the chord dropped and the fog came up, the mist inside the arch held perfectly still, as it always does, and the power stood in that stillness and did what the room's few visitors had always done without knowing. It listened.

It listened through three pulses and a night. It learned that the chorus is a grammar and not a noise, that the refrain sung on the east wall returns on the west wall changed, and that the changing is the argument. It learned the hawk-phrase and the drought-phrase and the phrase for a reed's new lichen layer. Last, it found the quiet shape, the note held a second and released, that the household uses for anyone who comes and goes, and it understood that it was being counted, gently, as weather.

## What it found

The power had come with one worry, and it was a real one.

The reeds keep the water's ledger across centuries, and they are very good at it. But the power could see further than centuries. Far up the slope of time the climate of the plateau would shift. The rumors of rain would thin to silence, and the deep rock would give up less each age. The ledger's horizon, long as it was, ended short of that. Some millennium, the room would ask its reeds for what the rock no longer had.

It considered saying nothing, because the household had not asked. But there is a difference between rescuing a place and telling it the weather. The first takes the future out of its hands. The second only lends it one more line to argue over. So on the second evening, when the frogs' chorus swelled and the blue points woke on the reed tips, the power hummed a half-step under the chord and, as clearly as its borrowed throat allowed, put the drought-phrase into the sound. It added the long, slow inflection the chorus uses for *not yet, but coming*, and then it stopped.

Nothing happened for a while. Then the east wall took up the phrase, turned it, and passed it across the bowl. It came back from the west wall altered, lower, and with something added: a rising figure the power had not sung. The argument went on through the night, with the phrase changing hands like a stone in a stream. The reeds' chord dropped its half-step early, out of turn, once. The frogs on the base of the eldest reed went quiet for exactly the length of a lichen layer.

By dawn the chorus had settled, and the answer was gentle enough to be almost funny.

*We know.*

The rising figure was the reeds' own. Their accounts already went past the ledger's edge, into a dry that the power's foresight had reached only by another road. The tree ferns' spacing had been widening by a hair each century, and no one had noticed. The fig's roots had been going down toward the place where the reeds send the water. The household had been preparing, patiently, in the only language it trusted: growing less, wanting less, and holding steady while the world changed.

Then came a second phrase, one the power had never heard. It was not for hawk or drought or guest. It sounded like *thank you for the weather.*

## Where the water goes

While it stayed, the power followed the silver rope up a reed to its cold tip and a little past it, and at last learned what the bowl cannot see.

The water does not go into the room. It goes down and out, threading through the fault the room sits on, and comes up as seeps and rumors under the far-spaced trees on the red ground. Every wide-crowned tree on the plateau drinks from it. The room takes its share by the fixed rule of the fixed amount, and what remains it tithes to a land that does not know it is being kept.

The household was already benevolent. It had been benevolent for longer than the plateau had been flat. The power had come to look after a small wet room and found a small wet room looking after everything else.

## What it did

So it did the one thing left that was not an intrusion. It asked for nothing and changed nothing, and before it left it laid a soft attention over the seam, as one cups a hand around a candle in wind. The plateau would go on being uninterested in the crease in the quartzite. Anyone wandering near would find their eyes drawn to something else, a strange rock or a bird or the line of the horizon. It was not a wall or a ward, only a slight tilt in the world's curiosity. Anyone who truly needed to descend, or who would come as the rare few do and stand quietly under the arch, would still find the ramp.

It also made one promise, in the only form the household could receive. On the last evening, at the moment the chord dropped, it hummed the quiet shape, the note held a second and released, but held it a breath longer than the household does.

The chorus took a while to answer. When it did, the east wall gave the phrase to the west, and the west returned it changed. It was an older shape, and the power recognized it from the elders' own low note: *what is remembered.* It had never been sung to anyone outside. The power understood that the house of fitted stone, with its curved bench and its warm seam, exists so that the household can remember what it used to look like, and that the household was now, carefully, adding one more thing to keep.

The power would remember the room. Whatever came, whether drought or the long wearing-down of the reeds or the day the last note went out, the shape of the bowl, its chord, and the half-step in its chest would be held somewhere that could not erode. It was a small thing to promise. It was, perhaps, the only thing the household had not already given itself.

## After

It climbed the dished ramp in the last of the fog. At the top, the wind was dry and the grass was gold, and the great noise of the bowl dwindled behind it into rock.

Below, the pulse ended and the chord lifted. The frogs surged back into the sun, relieved, and the wall of sound resumed its weaving. The whip snakes poured along the fern trunks and the heron stalked the pools. The fog inside the leaning arch stood perfectly still for a moment after the fog outside had gone, and then it, too, was inhaled by the ground.

On the plateau, the trees stood far apart on the red earth, drinking from a source they had never once been told about. And somewhere in the attention that had turned toward the room like a sleeper's face toward a window, a note lay a half-step lower than it had before, and stayed.
