# What Came Down

It did not arrive as a light or a voice. Either would have been a kind of violence here, and it understood that before it reached the hatch. It came the way the keeper came, on foot, by the stair, and it let the sound thicken around it one step at a time until the roar was no longer something it heard but something it was inside.

It stood a long while at the bottom. It had power enough to have stilled the engine, or warmed the vault, or read every gauge at once and known the room entire. It did none of these. It crossed the floor by the brass discs, island to island, steadying itself at each one. It learned the prayer of that crossing by doing it, because a thing can only be understood as a visitor if you visit it as one.

## What it considered

It considered a great many things, and set them down one by one.

It could silence the roar. But the roar is the food of forty-one ferns, and the silence would be a famine dressed as mercy.

It could warm the room. But the frost-glaze is a balance, melting as much as it settles, and a warm room would be a different room with a different sleeper in it.

It could uncurl the forty-second fern with a touch, in an instant. But the fiddlehead is not an unfinished thing being denied. It is a fist, and a fist opened by another hand is not a hand that has learned to open. The thousandth of a turn is worth more than a whole frond given as a gift. That much it knew with certainty. The turning belongs to the fern.

It could explain the beat. It could say that this is a clock, or that it is a lullaby, or that it is neither. But the not-knowing is part of what the fern is doing down here. Answers handed over early are a way of taking the question.

So it did very little. What it did, it did carefully.

## The knees

At the deepest node, by the governors' shadow, it knelt in the two shallow dips on the brass rim. They fit, more or less, as they would fit anyone who came to kneel there for long enough. It did not read this as a sign. It simply noted that someone had once knelt exactly here, for years, in order to listen to a single small plant listen.

In the node the pressure cancels, and a whisper is possible. It stayed through a full cycle of the beat, the slow swell and ebb that comes once every few hours, and it did not hurry it. It heard the two choirs, north and south, holding their notes a hair apart, and the difference between them rising like a tide in a room with no sea. It understood, as the keeper had, that the machines were not the point. The ferns were not the point either. The point was the interval, and the one small thing that attends to it.

At the crest of the second swell it leaned down, close to the fern with its frost undisturbed, and whispered one sentence. What it said is not recorded, because the sentence was not for the record. It was short. It contained the words *no hurry*. It contained the word *heard*. The fern did not move, and the power had not expected it to. A whisper is not a lever. It is a way of saying that someone was here.

## The governors

The one true change it made was to the governors, and it made it so small that no gauge shows it.

The governors hold their angle by correcting any drift, and the room has run so long that they have nothing left to correct. But a mechanism that resists all change will resist the one change that is not a fault. If the forty-second fern ever does uncurl, and begins to hum, and the beat slides off its old cycle, the governors would read it as an error and drag it back, gently, exactly, forever. The room would not choose to stop the fern. It would simply be built to.

So the power reached up to one governor, the one nearest the deepest node, and eased its tolerance by the breadth of a hair. The grey halo of its spinning weights is now a hair's breadth wider than it was, and the room is now able to be wrong in one small, particular way. It gave the machine slack the way you slacken a rope around something growing. It told the governor nothing and it gave the room no instruction. It only made it possible for the room to be surprised.

## The stair

It climbed the four hundred steps slowly. Going up, the sound thinned around it and became a thing it could hear again, and then a thing it could barely hear, and then, at the hatch, wind.

On the way it did one more thing, and this too was small. On the worn stair, where four hundred polish marks of one set of boots all point downward, it let its own steps leave four hundred lighter marks pointing up. It did not do this for the fern, which has no use for stairs. It did it for whoever comes next, so that the stair might show that the way down is also a way back. The keeper's boots go down and never up, and the power did not judge that. It wanted the record to hold both.

It closed the hatch, and the grey paint took the grey rock, and the eye slid off it again.

## After

Below, nothing has changed, and everything is arranged so that this is true. The frost sparkles. The lichen holds its edge against the darker stain of its own shadow. The pistons keep their pulse in your sternum. The governors hold their angles, and one of them holds it a hair more loosely than before.

At the center, in the quiet, the forty-second fern leans into the difference between two notes. Its fiddlehead is curled as it has always been. If you held a lamp low and looked sideways, you still could not swear to any change. That is exactly how it should be.

The beat swells and ebbs once every few hours. It might be a clock, or a lullaby. Now it is also the sound of something that is listening and has, at last, been listened to.
