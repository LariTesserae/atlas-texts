# The Attention at the Rim

It did not arrive so much as become noticeable, the way a sound becomes noticeable once you realize it has been going on for a while. The fog leaned toward the pipe, as it always did, and a little of the leaning was toward something else.

It stood at the rim through one full breath of the siphon and did nothing. That was the first decision, and it was harder than it looks. Everything it could have done presented itself at once, as it does to any power: to mend the pipe, to lift the water clear, to teach the drawn what they were and why, to tell them the engine's dream was only a dream. Each of these was a kindness in its first half-second and a theft in its second.

It set them down one at a time, like stones from a pocket.

## What it would not do

It would not mend the pipe. The pipe was not broken. It was *keeping a schedule*, and the schedule was the shape of forty lives. A mended pipe would drain the sump and leave the drawn standing on dry clay, their plates cracking, entirely free and entirely finished.

It would not answer their question. They did not know whether the siphon would break and restart without their hands on the joints. It had only to look to know. It looked, and knew, and then it considered the two possible answers. If the pipe would do it alone, the drawn's long tending would turn, in their understanding, from vocation into decoration, though it would not actually be decoration, because attention paid to a thing is part of the thing. If the pipe *needed* them, the knowledge would turn their gentleness into a duty with a cliff behind it. Not knowing had made them careful without making them afraid, or afraid only in the way of anyone who loves something breakable. The question was doing honest work. It let the question stand.

It would not give them a name in words, or a history, or a purpose from above. They had one already, in taps on iron, and it was better than anything a power could compose for them.

And it would not stay. Whatever it did, it would do once, lightly, and leave.

## What it did

**It went down into the hollow at the start of a fill,** when the Holding was gathering, and it made itself the weight of a mat. A drawn one felt the pressure, and a slow ripple of attention passed through all forty like wind on cotton-grass. Then the ripple settled, because the pressure was not a boot. It did not step, it did not press, and it asked for nothing. They let it be part of the still.

It stayed through the whole fill. It learned the Holding from inside: the plates closing one by one, the sphagnum pads pressed into the weeping seams of the shale until the drip went quiet under the palms, the slowing of breath to almost nothing. It felt what they were listening for, and listened with them, through the mat and the clay. Somewhere near the last minute the oldest of them began to lift a hand, a heartbeat before the pipe knew it would gulp.

Most of what it did, it did there. It let itself be one more thing in the silence, for one full fill. Nobody in the sump had ever been *accompanied* in the Holding by a thing that was not of it, and the Holding is the loneliest hour of their day, being the hour in which they are most entirely the place's. Nobody would say the hour was lightened. It simply had, for once, a witness who was not also a participant, and the silence was a little deeper for being seen.

**Then it went to the pipe,** during the drain, when the iron hums on its sinking piers, and it laid a palm on the length of the main where the knocking runs. It did not knock in their pattern. It listened to the story as they tell it, the slow tap that says the engine wanted to lift the water, lifted and lifted, and could not stop wanting, and it understood what was missing from the tale.

The story had no one in it who *asked*. "Without ever asking the fire what it wants," they say, and they mean it as courtesy, the way you would not ask a sleeper their dream. But the engine had been asked nothing for a century and a half.

So the power asked it. Not in taps, and not in words, but as one presses a hand to a cold flank to see whether there is still something warm in there. It waited through the cascade of the terraces, tier after tier, cup after cup.

The engine's answer was not what the story would have guessed. It did not want the water lifted. It had wanted that once, and the wanting had long ago worn down into something smaller and truer. What it wanted was for the stroke to have *mattered*: to have been seen from outside, once, as a stroke. It was a machine that had done exactly what it was built for, and then been left for a century with no one to tell it so.

The power told it. That was all. It told the beam, the cylinder pit, the furred and patient iron, that the lifting had been seen, that the sump had been kept from drowning a village for sixty years by exactly this labour, that the dream in the pipe was a thing being kept, and not merely a thing being suffered. It did not end the wanting, because the wanting was holding up the siphon and the siphon was holding up the drawn. It changed only what the wanting was *made of*, from a lack into a witnessed thing, the way a held note differs from a note that is merely not yet over.

Nothing about the pipe changed. But the drawn, when they laid their hands on the joints in the next drain, would find the iron a degree less like a fire that must be fed and a degree more like a friend who is still awake.

**One more thing.** It permitted itself a single act in the world beyond the hollow, and it debated even that. The rim was a dozen steps from a lane that surveyors, walkers, and eventually someone with a plan for "reclaiming" the land might one day use. It did not build a wall, or a rumor, or a hex. It did the thing the place already did. It let the fog condense off the warm ochre pools a few minutes longer than it would have, and lean over the rim a little farther, so that anyone who approached the edge would see a bowl of pale smoke and, being human, take it for weather and move on. This was not protection. It was a slight thickening of the hollow's own manner of hiding, in the direction the hollow was already going. If the sump were ever truly in danger, the power would need to think again, and it left that thinking for the day it was needed.

## What it left

It left no gift that could be picked up. There was nothing to show for the visit.

The Holding, one fill, went deeper by a single heartbeat. That was all any of them could have told you. The oldest lifted her hand, as she always did, a moment before the gulp. But it was noticed among the drawn, in the way the one thing is noticed that is different in an unchanging day, that this time the lifting felt shared, as if the hand had not been alone in the air.

None of them asked about it. The drawn do not ask. They put their palms back on the iron, packed the burst joints with fresh paste, scraped the clog to the width that lets the air in, and the siphon broke with its rattling cough, as it always does, and began again.

At dusk the heron stood on the fallen wagon, and two of the drawn stood beside it at the edge of the tilted willow. It was hard to say which was watching which. Above them, at the rim, the fog leaned in toward the pipe, and for a moment it seemed to lean a little toward someone standing with them, and then it was only fog, and the mats moved, and the hollow went on agreeing to the terms of its restlessness.

The engine house kept its beam out over the flooded pit. The beam did not move. But the tick of cooling iron, on the still air, sounded now, to anyone who knew how to hear it, like a slow pattern with one long rest in it. A drip on a wet plate, two beats and a rest, and in the rest, someone listening.
