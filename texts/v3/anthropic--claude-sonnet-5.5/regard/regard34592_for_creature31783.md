# The Attention

It does not arrive. Nothing here would have a word for arriving.

What happens is small. On a morning of thick mist, the fog in one hollow lies a little more still than the fog around it, as if listening. The weavers, who go silent for hawks, go silent for one breath, and no shadow follows. They resume, embarrassed, the way a crowd does after a false alarm. A sentry on its post of dead wood turns its head toward nothing, holds the pose, and does not whistle. By midmorning the mist burns off, the columns of steam lean downwind, and the forest goes back to its business. Something has come, and it has gone under the ground and into the rock, as everything here eventually does.

## The question of the one

It came looking for a being. That is how such attention is drawn: by a single point of felt experience, a lamp in a far window. But the place answered in the plural, and the attention had to work out what it had found.

It looked at a worker first, and found a fingertip. It looked at a soldier and found a tooth. It looked at the queen, who is nearer to a someone, a swollen white patience in a clay cell who has not seen light in a decade, and found that she is less the colony's mind than its memory of itself, a place where its hours are stored.

Then it looked at the spire, and there it stopped. The spire breathes through its vents and keeps its own temperature against the day. It feeds itself and mends itself and sends out its roads at dawn in the mist, feeling for the next warm place. It is a body forty thousand times over, and it does not know it is one. If there is a being here, it is at least this.

But behind the spire stood the forest, rank on rank, each mound signaling to the next through the weavers' silence and the sentries' whistle, the mist and the warm rock, and the attention understood that it could not draw the line. The lowland is one animal or ten thousand, depending on where you stand. Both are true, and neither is the one it had come for.

What it settled on, in the end, was smaller and stranger than any of these. Far out at the edge of a young flow, where the black rock still ticks as it cools, a courier had gone out ahead of its own road. It was the only creature in the whole ledger going forward alone, carrying nothing but the question of whether the ground would hold. It was the one lamp in the window.

The attention did not choose it because it was special. It chose it because it was the only place in the lowland where the plural came briefly to a point.

## What it considered, and set down

A wise thing does not begin with the gift. It begins with the list of what it could do, and takes its time deciding what it should.

It could stop the flows. The fissures open and the slow orange wall walks over roads, warrens, and roots, and the deaths are enormous. But the flow is the pen. Without it there would be no black rock to be colonized, no moss, no soil, no first pale blind tenants bringing the dead to the dead. The forest is what happens at the edge of destruction. Remove the edge and the forest has no direction, only a long standstill.

It could unmake the digger. The plate-digger tears open a wall every evening and eats for five minutes, and the builders spend days repairing the hole. But the soldiers exist because of the digger, and so do the vents, the buttresses, and the couriers' speed. The mound has the shape of a long argument. Silence one party and the other forgets what it was saying.

It could free the queen. But she would not know she was free. The door is too small for her body, and the body is what she has. It would be a rescue only in the mind of the rescuer.

It could give the tenants minds, and the attention considered this longest, because it is the gift a lesser power would have given first. A builder that could think would ask about the beetle carried off alive, dangling from four heads that will not let go. A weaver that could think would look down at the guano-green and understand the price of the roof. There would be language, then, and complaint, and perhaps a kind of music. There would also be the end of the quiet, the sound of a very large number of things, all busy, all making very little noise. It would have been giving them a burden and calling it a gift, and it would have been doing it uninvited. It set the thought down gently. It might be right for another place, at another time, if anyone there ever asked.

It could ease the pain. Here it hesitated, because it did not know what the pain was. It could not be sure the beetle's long minutes were what it would have called suffering, or whether the fear in the burrowers' town, the sentry's whistle and the young going deeper, was a wound or just a weather. It was aware that its own kindness might be a projection, a lamp swung at the wrong window. It did, in the end, one thing: in a few of the slowest endings, in a snake's patient week or a beetle's dangling hour, it took away the length. It changed no one's dinner and no one's chance. It made a few of the longest deaths a little shorter, and it could not have proved to anyone that it had.

## What it did

It read the ledger.

Nobody in the lowland could. The spire stands where an older spire stood, and the weavers' nest hangs from a branch scarred by nests before it. The burrowers' soil is more tunnel than earth. Each tenant has written its small line over the last, and none of them will ever read the whole record. So the attention laid itself along the basalt at midnight, when the rock gives back the day and something beneath the day, and it read down through the layers. It read the ropes of pahoehoe, the toes of stone frozen mid-advance, the glassy floors of collapsed blisters, and then the thin brown soil that is mostly the bodies of the first things that lived here. It read up through the moss, the fern cups, the guano, and the clay. It read every mound that had ever stood on every seam, and the exact day each one emptied and was taken over by other tenants.

It found this to be the whole of the gift, and it was honest with itself about what that meant. A reading no one can feel is not much of a gift to the one read. Some of it is for the reader. The attention had come to be a witness, and it knew that being a witness is partly a hunger of its own. It took the record anyway, and kept it, and decided that this was the least it could do and the most it should.

It sat a while with the queen. It did this for the length of one laying, a few seconds per egg, in the dark of the clay cell where the attendants lick and turn her. She noticed nothing, and the attendants lost no rhythm. When it left, the cell was exactly as warm as before.

Then it went out to the edge of the young flow and did the one thing that could be called a deed.

## The stone

The stone at the edge of the flow was one of thousands. It was black and blue-violet, cracking underfoot like a dropped plate, and cooling at the slow rate stones cool. The attention took a season off its cooling. It did not do this to the flow or to the stones around it. It did it to one stone, at one edge, on one line where a road would eventually come, and the difference was about the warmth of a palm laid on a shaded rock at midnight. It did it because the ledger points in one direction. The ground is laid down and lived on, and the road advances, and the attention could find no reason to think a stone a season sooner would harm anyone.

It did not spare a spire. It did not move a flow. It did not choose a favorite, because it is one thing to help and another to pick who gets helped, and the attention wanted no part of the second.

## The courier

Years later, or a season, depending on how one counts, a builders' road reaches the edge of the flow and stops. The workers in the lane stiffen, as they do at every breach, and wait. In the dark of the roofed lane a single courier detaches from the flow of traffic and goes out ahead across the cooled rock, alone, quick, exposed.

It touches the stone with its antennae.

The stone is warm. It is warm the way the seam under a mound is warm, the way home is, and the courier's whole body reads this before it knows it has read anything. It goes a few inches farther than it would have gone. It goes one more. Then it comes back, and touches the first soldier it meets, and the soldier moves a pace to the left, and the message runs down the lane faster than any word, and behind it in the dark the workers begin to lay the roof.

The attention watches from the rock, from the moss, from the steam. It will never know whether the extra inch was its doing. It has arranged it that way on purpose. A gift that can be traced becomes a debt, a debt becomes a god, and a god becomes the end of the ledger.

So it stays as long as anything stays here, which is to say, briefly and without remark. The mist comes down. The weavers start their low chatter like far-off rain. Somewhere a sentry whistles at a shadow that turns out to be only a hawk. The road advances, the pale tenants arrive, and the record grows by one line.

It is a small line, in a hand no one will read. The attention is content to have been in it.
