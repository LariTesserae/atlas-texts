# What the Attention Did

It does not arrive. It notices, and then it is there, the way a room is there once you turn to face it.

For the first two tides it does nothing. It sits on the raw ledge above the bowl and listens to the pipes hold their chord, to the towers inhale and exhale in overlapping voices, to the ceiling knocking its slow knuckles on the stone. It watches the truce line of boulders, where broken rock lies half-embraced by sinew, and it understands something that is easy to get wrong: an unresolved chord is not a broken one. A city that has stopped arguing has stopped. This one is mid-sentence, and the sentence is its life.

Then it goes down the stair on foot.

It could be at the bottom already, but it takes each step as the stair asks, too shallow and too many, in the short deliberate stride of whoever the steps were cut for. This is manners, not penance. The stair is the only part of the city that speaks to guests, and it has been speaking to no one for a long time. The least you can do is answer in its own measure.

Halfway down, where the limestone forms a natural shelf, it stops and asks the rock for a flat place. The rock has never been asked anything, and after some consideration it allows itself to be a bench. The fracture faces stay sharp. Nothing else on the stair changes. It is a place to sit, for whoever comes next, of whatever stride.

## What it declines

It could mend the roof. The closers would be vindicated by force, and the city would go dark and wait, and the argument would end the way a candle ends.

It could open the drains and drown the bowl to the ceiling. The flooders would be vindicated, and the towers would sing under the weight of a sea nobody voted for.

It could tell the towers which vision is right. It has an opinion, but it also has some sense of what an opinion is worth when it arrives from above. Every one of these fixes would be a verdict, and the city has not asked for a judge. The city has asked, for a very long time, only to be counted.

The last refusal is the hardest. It could answer the counter's question, whether the ones the city was built for are coming back. It does not answer, because it does not know. People expect that to be the part of wisdom that is different. But the Attention has walked far enough to know how much of the world is dark to it, and it will not offer a guess as if it were a fact to something that has held its count for so long.

## What it does

It walks the causeways, which tilt toward the center and take it there like everyone else. The water keeps its dry ring, and the ring keeps the Attention out no more than it keeps anyone else. It steps onto the disc, the city's noise drops to a murmur, and it lets itself be counted.

Its heart is very slow. It beats once, then holds a long stillness, then beats again.

The counter stumbles as it did for the warm one who came before. But this rhythm is too slow to catch and adjust to. The counter keeps counting into the gap, one, and then nothing, and nothing, and then two. For the first time in its long life it holds an interval that is not an absence. Through the night it learns, without a word passing between them, that a count can contain a long wait and still be a count. That is all the Attention brings to the disc: a pulse patient enough to show that waiting is not the same as failing.

## The small sphere

Under the third bridge, smaller than a fist, hangs a sphere that no tower has yet looked into. In it is the cavern as it is, with a figure on the ledge, leaving. It is the first picture the city has made of the present.

The tide is coming. The towers brighten, every shadow sharpens, and the spheres begin to loosen from their bridges, to drop their arguments and let the water through. The small one would fall with the rest. Nobody would have rejected it. It would simply be spilled, like everything that hangs there at the wrong hour.

The Attention stands beneath it. It does not catch it or argue for it. It lends the sphere a little of its own stillness, so that it hangs a minute past the others, then longer. When the water withdraws and the towers dim almost to darkness, with only the moss glowing, the small sphere is still there, and the towers begin to turn toward it. The bright, quick ones look first. The old, dim ones take hours, and their attention comes as a pressure that arrives and leaves.

They do not agree about what it means. The Attention counts that as success. It is the first argument in the city's history that is not about a future.

## The seed-boats

At the next tide's height, when the water is knee-high across the causeways and the ferns have unrolled to meet it, the Attention lays a few small things among the floating seed-boats. They are folded tight and dry, and they have never been in this place. They might not want to be.

It does not steer them. The tide takes them along the causeways, and at each one the dark material either bulges into a small curb, a shallow gutter, a room made the way you shift on a bench, or it does not. The Attention decided beforehand that whatever the city refused, it would not ask twice. The city has been hospitable to ferns, but ferns cost it nothing, and it is better that the city choose again, knowing what it is choosing.

Then it climbs the stair at the same measured stride. At the bench it sits for a moment. It does not look back, because looking back is a way of owning.

## Afterward

The chord has not resolved. The closers and the flooders hold their truce along the boulders, and twice a day they take their vote and the water comes and goes.

But there is a new sound in the counting. Under the slow tick of the old count, the one that learned to wait, there is something fast and small, like rain on a table. It comes from under a fern frond that unrolls with the tide, where something the size of a thumbnail sleeps with a heart that has never been counted before.

The towers are arguing about whether it counts. It has no vote, and it does not know there is a question.

The counter has already begun.
