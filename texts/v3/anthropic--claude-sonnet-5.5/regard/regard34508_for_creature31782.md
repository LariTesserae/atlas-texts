# What the Attention Did

It did not descend. Nothing in the Hollow would have known what to do with a descent. It arrived the way mist arrives, by being there a little more each hour, until the place had the sense of being looked at that a room has when someone has been standing in the doorway a while.

It stayed through three thumps and then three hundred. It let the polyrhythm teach it the vents one at a time. It watched the ratchet frogs go silent under a heron's shadow and the roar show through their silence. It watched a bronze back cross the mud in the half-second after the water slammed shut, and then it watched the same animal freeze, and it understood that it was seeing a dance older than any dancer.

Then it considered what a power might do.

## What It Set Down Unused

**It could wake them.** The hands were nearly too good for the animals wearing them, and a little light in the skull would have finished the thought the hands had started. Language, arithmetic, a map of the bowl. The Attention held that possibility for a long time and put it down. What the animals had was a kind of knowing that had never been said aloud, the turning, the weighing, the setting down a hand's width off. Once it had words it would become an opinion, and opinions can be wrong and argued and abandoned. The stacking had lasted since the ice because nobody had ever decided to do it.

**It could drive off the herons.** The kits were dying. The Attention felt that as a plain pull, the way you feel a stair that isn't there. But the herons stood over the channel like gate posts because the amphipods were plentiful, and the amphipods were plentiful because of the pans, and the pans were the animals' own work. The herons were a cost the whole arrangement had already paid for. Remove them and the animals would fill the bowl, eat the pans to mud, and starve in the ruin of their own success. It would be kindness for one spring and cruelty for a century.

**It could end the winter's edge.** The rime nights and the vents, the heaps of three and four sleepers, the steam leaking around their bodies: that was the animals' whole idea of home. Warm the bowl and the vents would be only holes.

**It could move them somewhere safe.** This was the most tempting, and it was the worst. They were not animals that happened to live in the Hollow. They were what the Hollow did when it had had enough time. Carry them out and you would have a dozen otter-like bodies in a green place, and the Hollow would go on roaring for no one.

**It could tell them.** Tell them what? That the wall would slump one day. That the herons were not malice. That someone had noticed. The Attention imagined the news reaching them and saw only a dozen animals standing very still in a tight rhythm they could not cross.

So it did none of these. It decided that most of what it had come to give would be a harm dressed as help, and that the discipline of the visit was to know this and stay.

## What It Did Anyway

Being wise is not the same as being absent. It allowed itself three acts, each small enough to be mistaken for the world.

**The first was the slope.** On the north wall a long pale seam of silt was ripening toward failure. When it went, it would go onto the fissure and choke the engine within a season. The Attention did not stop the slump or shore anything up. It closed a single hairline fracture, deep in the till, where water had been finding a path. Over forty or fifty years, the seam would now shed a little to the west, onto the flats, where it would bury nothing but sedge. It bought decades, not forever. The walls would still slump, the water would still find another way down, and the engine would still end. It left the ending alone. An ending is part of a thing being real, and it had not come to make the Hollow immortal, only to give it more time to be itself.

**The second was the held breath.** On a night of rime, with the frogs going quiet one section at a time and the animals already in their vents, it did something so slight that no instrument would have caught it. It delayed one thump. Not by much, perhaps four seconds. The slug of water gathered against the ledge and did not fall. The ripples on the pool went slack. Down in the vents the sleepers stirred and lay still, because the pattern had broken and their bodies knew what to do. The frogs, all of them, went silent. The heron on the channel turned its head. For four seconds the entire Hollow was listening at once, animals, birds, bats in their loops, the mist itself, and what it heard was its own engine breathing in.

Then the water fell, the ground shuddered, and everything resumed.

The Attention did not know whether this was a gift. It suspected it was a small vanity: it wanted the place to hear itself once. But the four seconds hurt nothing, and it left them.

**The third was a mercy, and it is the one it turned over longest.** In the second week of the visit a young animal, half-grown and reckless from hunger, crossed the pool a half-beat late. The slug of water was already gathering and the bank was already tensing to shiver into the current. The Attention could have let it happen. It was, by every argument above, the kind of loss the Hollow had always absorbed.

It let the thump land a fraction of a second early instead. The young animal, already committed to the leap, cleared the bank by a whisker and hit the far mud on its belly, alive, orange to the shoulders, baffled. It shook itself and went to the pans as if nothing had occurred.

Afterward the Attention asked itself why this one and not the kits the herons took, or the weak the winter took. It found no principle that would survive being written down. The heron kills were the fen's, the cold was the fen's, and the misjudged thump was also the fen's. It had simply been standing there, and it had not been able to not act. It admitted the line was arbitrary and did not take the act back. It resolved only that this would not be repeated, that a mercy given once is a gift and a mercy given always is a takeover, and that the animal would go on believing it had been lucky. It had been.

## The Last Thing

Before leaving, the Attention did the one thing the animals did. It went to the windrow at the edge of the flats, where the nodules lay in their even, tended rows. It picked up one dark, pea-sized piece of bog ore, felt its weight, turned it once, and set it down a hand's width from where it had been.

The mist thickened. The light went. The thumps went on in their drifting overlap.

At dusk a bronze animal came out of the reeds in the half-second after a thump, took three bounding strides, and froze at the windrow. It sniffed along the row. It stopped at the displaced nodule and stood for a long time, whiskers fanned, wrists laid flat on the cold silt.

Then it picked the stone up, weighed it, turned it once, and set it down. Not back where it had been, and not where the Attention had left it, but a hand's width off in a third direction, with a rightness the Attention could not read.

It was a while before the Attention left. It had been judged, which it had not expected, and it did not know whether it had passed. It found it did not need to know. The pans were full, the vents were warm, and the stack was, in the failing light, exactly as if someone had just finished with it.
