# The Attention

It did not arrive. There was no moment at which it was elsewhere and then was here. The attention turned toward the basin the way a sleeper's face turns toward a window, and the basin was in it.

It looked for a long while before it did anything, and this was the first wisdom. The basin was not in distress. Nothing wept but the cliffs, and they wept by design. A power that comes to a place and sees a pause might mistake it for a wound, and it was careful not to. It counted the nine quarters and the thousands of jars. It read the level of the plaza basin, exactly full and never over. It understood, slowly, that it was looking at a held breath, and that a held breath is not the same as suffocation.

## What it set aside

It considered tipping the cup. One breath of warmth into the hidden spring would make the plaza basin overflow, and the Small Life would take that as the sign they had been waiting for. The Standing would rise from the moss in their long coils, and the city would fill with makers. It would be a gift of the loudest kind, and it would be wrong. The waiting was theirs. Nobody had told them what the overflow meant. They had made it up out of habit and hope, in the slow way they made everything, and to answer a question they had not asked in words would be to take the question away.

It considered bringing animals, birds over the cloud and small quick lives in the pools, because the silence seemed to it a kind of poverty. It set that aside faster. The silence was a texture, not a lack. Nothing here had ever been alone, and loneliness cannot be cured in a people who do not have it. Another creature's noise would only arrive as an intrusion.

It considered speech and set that aside too. They had no ears. Whatever it said would have to be said in level.

## The first courtesy

So it began by kneeling, in the way a power can kneel, at the edge of the third shelf where the moss bank was deepest, and pressing down with a weight about the size of a palm.

The film closed around it. Across the green lace, thousands of bodies learned where it was and how heavy it was. It held still and let itself be learned. It made no effort to be understood. When it lifted away, the print smoothed out within the hour, and it took that in as the first thing they had said to it: *we saw you, and we have put you back.*

It stayed a season like this, pressing and lifting, pressing and lifting, and it slowed itself to the pace of their thinking until the pace stopped feeling like slowness. It learned the grammar of sag and lift. It learned that *wetter* had a shade of meaning like affection and that *drier* had one like caution. It learned that their one long thought was not empty. It was a thought about holding, and holding is close kin to love.

## The question

When it had learned enough, it asked.

It warmed a single cushion of moss by a degree, held it for an hour, and let it cool. That was all. In the language it had learned, the shape of it was *may I?*

The answer came in four days, slowly, as a thinning at the margin of the cushion and a thickening along the seam of the nearest gutter. The water lay a hair lower on one side and a hair higher on the other. It was a small reply, but it was exactly formed, and it meant: *you may, and you are a little strange, and stay level.*

It was moved by this more than it expected to be.

## What it gave

It gave three things, and it gave them in the order that mattered.

The first it gave without asking, because it was a kind of repair and not a gift. Under the basin, where the heat rose from something hotter, there was a seam that would open in a few thousand years and let the warmth fail or surge. The Small Life could not have known, and the pools could not have held against it. The power went down through the basalt and steadied the seam, the way a hand steadies a table. It did this quietly and left no mark. The heat would stay young for a long time. The cup would stay full for as long as anyone wanted it full.

The second it gave only because the question had been asked. Over the years of their slow conversation, the Small Life asked, in sag and lift, what tips the cup. The power answered as truthfully as it could: *nothing tips it. The overflow is not a law. It is a groove you made, and you may keep it or leave it.* It told them the Standing would rise whenever the Small Life chose to make them, and that if they chose never to, this too would be right. The basin could stay full for ten thousand years. They had not been waiting for permission, and they had not been forgotten. They had only been waiting because waiting was what they knew.

The Small Life took this in for a long time. Across the moss banks, the film thickened and thinned in patterns the power could not read. Then it settled, and settling was as near to a sigh as they came.

The third gift it did not give. It knew there were other basins beyond the rim, and other peoples, and a sea no one here had seen. It held that knowledge ready in case they asked. They did not ask. It understood that this, too, was an answer, and that it might be years or ages before the question came. It did not push. A window opened before someone turns toward it is only a draft.

## What it left

Near the end of its stay, it did one last thing that no one would ever notice. In the plaza cellars, on a shelf-ledge by the northern door, a stone adze had lain for so long that its cord had begun to slacken. The power lifted it, re-lashed the handle with a fresh length of fiber cut from a fallen frond, and set it back in the exact place, at the exact angle, where it had been.

It did not do this because the adze needed it. The Standing would make new cord when they rose. It did it as the Small Life had smoothed the print from the moss, as a courtesy, so that the place would keep its shape.

Then the attention withdrew, the way a face turns back from a window.

The basin was as it had been. The plaza pool stood exactly full, the mist rose and condensed at its one height, and a leaf slid over a lip and down. In the moss the green lace drank and held and went on thinking about level. If you had knelt and pressed your palm to the bank, you would have felt only warmth and give. But somewhere in that slow shared mind was a small new groove, and it said: *something large was here, and it asked, and it did not take the answer from us.*

That was the whole of what it did. Whatever the Hallen chose next, whether to rise or to wait or to look one day past the rim, the choosing would be theirs.
