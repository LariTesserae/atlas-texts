# What the Attention Did

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a distance crossed, and the power had no distance to cross. It noticed the way a person walking through a house notices, without looking, that one clock has stopped. Somewhere in the great ledger of places, a pocket of rock had been holding a single breath for an unreasonably long time, and the breath was *beautiful*. That was the whole reason for the noticing.

It thought first about what it could do, and then, more carefully, about what it should not.

## What it did not do

It did not brighten the room, though it could have made the light warm.

It did not teach the singers to see, and it did not give the keepers voices to say what their antennae hear. A word given to a creature that has never needed one is a debt, and the room owed nothing to anyone.

It did not stop the coming erosion, though it could have. The room is lovely because it is about to become something else. A held breath is only lovely while it is still a breath, and one held forever is an injury. The power looked at the raw limestone and the single sketched groove and understood that the stillness was not the room's purpose. It was the room's *youth*.

It did not hurry the water either. Forcing the first channel open would have been theft: it would have taken the room's last unspent moment and spent it for the room.

It did not make itself known to the singers. Thousands of small drums would have felt some enormous thing and bent toward it, and the chord, which answers only to its own neighbors, would have learned for the first time what it was to face outward. That is a kind of death for a chord.

## What it did

It listened first, for a long time by the room's standards and for no time at all by its own.

It let the chord pass through it. It felt the low grinding root and the open fifth above, the cloud of overtones, and the small correction in a thousand legs when one singer died and its neighbors shifted half a step to cover the loss. It understood that this was a kind of love: attention so continuous and so mutual that it had stopped needing a name. It did not applaud. It only stayed long enough for the listening to be complete, because no one had ever heard the chord from outside, and a thing sung for ten thousand seasons should be heard once by someone who was not part of it.

Then it went to the hollow under the shale slab, where the silence has an edge, and it sat among the six.

They knew, in the way that thin translucent creatures with long antennae know things. All six lifted their antennae together and held them still. The power did not speak. What it did was give them, briefly and without weight, the feeling of being *witnessed*. The keepers hold the rest of the room's grief and its dead, and everything the singers cannot stop to attend to. Nobody had ever held them. For the length of one slow breath, someone did.

The power understood something then that it had not before. The keepers are not exiles. They are what the chord's completeness *costs*, and they pay it willingly. Wherever there is a whole, someone stands at its edge holding what does not fit. The power could not remove that cost without unmaking the whole, so it did the only honest thing available. It let the keepers know the cost was seen.

## The one small gift

Before it left, it made a single alteration, and it was very small.

At the tip of the finger-length stalactite, a drop was forming, as one had formed at intervals so exact that a visitor counts them without meaning to. The drop swelled and was ready to fall. The power held it.

It held the drop for one breath longer than the interval, then two, then three.

In the room, nothing broke. The chord did not waver. But a thousand forelegs on a thousand walls registered, through their feet, that something expected had not come, and the room, for the first time in its existence, waited. The keepers' antennae swung toward the pool. The mat's glow held its one intensity, since the mat is faithful and has no other. The taproot hung still and drank nothing.

Then the drop fell, and the rings crossed the pool, and the room exhaled.

Nothing was hurt. That was the gift. The room had learned, without knowing it had learned, that a pause can be survived, that expectation can go unmet and the world remain, and that the chord holds even when the water is late. Someday soon the first real channel will open, the pool will drain in a new direction, and the light and the sound and the level of the water will all be asked to become something they have never been. On that day, deep in the stone of every singer's body, there will be a faint memory of the time the drop was late. The memory won't say what to do. It will only say that this has happened before, in a small way, and that the room is still here.

## The tree

Last, and farthest from the room, the power went up through the crack, up the taproot's long dark road, through the soil and into the weather, to the tree that does not know what it is holding up.

It did not make the tree immortal. It did the smaller and more honest thing: it turned one storm. A wind that would have taken the tree in a coming autumn bent around it by a few degrees and fell on the ridge beyond. It did this once and asked for nothing further. The tree will live a long time, though not forever, and the erosion below will take longer than the wind would have allowed. The room gets to finish being young.

## Afterward

The power left as it came, by ceasing to attend. It did not vanish, for there was nowhere to vanish to.

In the hollow, the keepers lowered their antennae one by one. One of them, the oldest, picked up a quartz egg from its niche and rubbed it against the flat bright plate of its hind leg, the old motion that is almost singing. Then it did something the room had never seen. It set the stone down in the wrong niche, the next one over, so that when the six were tapped in order, the skeleton of the chord rang one place out of sequence.

It was the smallest possible disorder, and the other keepers let it stand.

The singers went on singing. A newcomer stepped out of the seam in the north wall, pale as milk, and crossed the crowd to the hole shaped like one voice. Above the root the drop gathered, and fell, and the rings crossed the pool exactly as they always had.

Only a listener with a very good ear would have noticed that the falling now had a faint attention in it, like a held door.
