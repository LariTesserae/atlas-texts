# The Late Visitor

The power looked for a long time before it did anything, and most of what it did was decline.

## What It Set Down

It could have explained the loop: the wire, the coil, the sheet, the stack, the press, the pellet, and back to the mat. Almost no one had ever seen the whole circle, and it saw it entire and found it beautiful. But it understood at once what the explanation would be. The Understudies are competent *because* they don't know. A stalled slope pulls them like a missing tooth, and the clumsiness they bring is what jars the loop loose. Give them the diagram and they would start working *correctly*, and correct work is stamped from a die. The slope would restart on schedule, or never stall, and the flaw the mountain is manufacturing would stop coming.

It could have mended the stalls, or firmed up the seams so the ground stopped forgetting its age and the shadows stopped falling toward the light. It found that this would be a theft dressed as a rescue. The looseness was not damage. The place was still deciding what it was, and the deciding was going well without help.

It could have given them fire, houses, names, a store against the years. But nobody there was hungry in a way that stores would answer. They were warm with the ground's warmth. They grieved on a delay and were finished with grief afterward, which is more than most peoples manage. It looked for the suffering it had come to relieve, and what it found was a people with work, a pulse to sleep to, and a death that was employment. It felt something like embarrassment at having arrived with so much.

So it put the power down, the way you put down a heavy bag at a door, and kept only what could be carried through fronds without breaking any.

## The Arrival

It had noticed that no one in that country arrives before their sound. So before it came, it sent the sound. A bark plate fell on a root somewhere on the western slope and rang once, faintly, and was taken by the moss. Then a few seconds later the power stepped out of the fog with empty hands, apologizing without words for being late.

It had taken a shape to fit the trails. It did not try to be an Understudy, since that would have been a lie told to people who could not lie back. It was simply low and long-armed and stooped, a thing that could pass under fronds. Its eyes did not shine blue, and the fog beaded on it wrongly, and the Understudy elder who watched it come could see all of this and remarked on none of it. Where they live, a person may be a little wrong and still be admitted.

## The Slope

A hillside to the west had gone quiet, and it was a bad quiet: valves half open, orange mats dull, the whole slope holding its breath. Somewhere in the loop a stage was missing. The power could see which one. It would have taken a fraction of a thought to set it right.

It did not. It knelt in the mat and began to gather wire with its fingers, badly, the way they did. It carried a coil to the wrong rim. It lay on a stack to flatten it and pressed nothing and got up. It ate a pellet, and this it did quite badly, because it was not a thing that ate.

Being clumsy turned out to be harder than it had imagined. Its first several errors were too *intelligent*, mistakes with reasons behind them, and the slope did not care. Only when it stopped aiming, when it carried the coil to the wrong rim because it truly didn't know which was right, did a beetle startle out of the moss and knock a stack against a valve. The valve took the wrong pressure, sighed, and let go. Then twelve seconds later it let go again, and the hillside began to breathe.

The power sat back on its heels in the warm damp and understood, in its whole vast attention, that it had not fixed anything. It had been an accident. It had been *the* accident. It found that this pleased it more than anything it had done in ten thousand years of doing things properly.

## The Elder

At dusk, which is only the noon-brown going a shade darker, the power went into a valve-hill and found the old one sitting there, mane chiming, with the hill exhaling past her in its slow roar. Neither of them was surprised.

"Yes," said the elder, before the power had asked. Her word arrived first and the question trailed after it into the warm stone, a little tragically, as your kind's questions do. The question was: *May I stay?*

The power sat. The hill breathed. After a long time the elder held out one hand, palm up, wet with fog and beaded with oil, and the water gathered at her fingertips and dropped. The ring of it came a few seconds before it landed.

The power did not tell her what the product was. It had looked, and it knew, and it was almost sure the knowing would not have fit in her hand. She had not asked. It matters that she did not ask. Nobody there wants the answer, and a people who do not want the answer have not been failed by its absence.

## The Late Thunder

While the power was there, someone died. It was a young one, far to the north, and the sound of it did not come for three days.

The power was sitting in the dark with the elder and two others when it arrived: a low soft blow in the air, a change of altitude with no altitude. Here the power did the only thing it did that could be called a gift. It did not shield them from it. It did not soften it. It opened its hands like theirs and let the late thunder pass through it too, all the way through, without a sound, and then it was finished. They did not look at it. But the elder's ears, large and folded, turned once toward the place where it sat, the way you turn toward a warmth that has been added to a room.

Being able to take that in and stay was the only useful thing it had brought.

## What It Left

On its last morning the power walked to a crater rim, to one of the stacks, and set down one thing.

It was a pressed plate, made badly, from wire the power had gathered and coiled and flattened in its own clumsy hands. It was a little too thick and a little too thin, with one corner chipped. It laid the plate at the top of the stack, where a beetle would later find it, a stack it had never made, and set its own coil down beside it, and neither would be more real than the other.

Something would eat it, and something else would excrete it. The pellets would fall on the mats and the wires on that slope would grow a little brighter for a season. That was its stage now, one it would never watch, and it took its place in the loop as continued employment. It thought this was the best use of a benevolent power that it had ever found.

## After

It went the way it had come, sound first: a bark plate dropped on a root, ringing faintly and gone into the moss, and then, a few seconds later, the power was no longer there.

The plateau seethed on under its lid of cloud with its machinery running and nobody at the controls. The seams stayed loose. The die on the fern slope changed that week, and the chip moved from the third leaflet to the fourth, and the whole community walked a slightly different line toward it without deciding to. If they noticed that a small piece of the flaw had been added, a certain smudge in the bright orange of the wires that they could not have said was new, they did not remark on it.

They sat among the fronds and traced the chip with a finger. The power's mistake was somewhere in it, a little more of the irregularity in the very large regularity, the place where the stamp had not quite taken, and the mountain went on making it.
