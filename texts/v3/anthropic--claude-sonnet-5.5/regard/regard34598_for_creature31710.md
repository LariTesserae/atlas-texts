# The Attention

It does not descend. Nothing descends onto the Flats, because there is no room for anything to land. It arrives the way a pressure change arrives, and the first to notice are the listeners, whose bristles stop trembling all at once, like a field of grass when the wind drops.

The power has come a long way and has a great deal of time. It uses the first stretch of that time to do nothing, and this is the wisest thing it does.

## What it looks for

It looks for the wound. It is not stupid, and it knows that a place this loud and this packed is often a place that is hurting. It walks the whole forty miles the way water would, along the braids. It listens to the grinders' grumble and finds no complaint in it. It reads the washers' fringes and finds nothing withheld. It stands beside a smelter in full production, whose lids are drooping and whose whistle is rising in one long line, and it finds a creature in something very like joy. It watches a smelter beside a fallen smelter keep working, and it understands that no loss has occurred there, because the line was never broken.

So its first finding is that there is nothing to rescue. It lets that finding stand. A lesser power would have rescued the animals anyway, would have stilled the mills and cleared the fog and given each grinder a name and a sorrow, and called that mercy. This one has seen that kind of mercy before. It looks like love from the outside and works like a flood.

## What it finds instead

The plain is not a crowd of animals. The power sees this in the Shift, when forty miles of creatures stop in the same moment and hold. The plain is one being with a great many bodies, and it has never once been alone with itself. Since it began, there has been no gap in it. There has been no silence to be a self in, and no interval in which anything could ask what it was doing or whether it wished to continue. The work is not a burden to it. But it is also not a choice, because a choice needs an empty place to be made in.

And the power sees what the plain is packed against. It goes to the seam where the mud thins to the pale grid, and stands with the listeners, and hears the low hum beneath the roar. It recognizes that hum. It is not a hunger or an enemy or an absence. It is only quiet, an unrendered ground that hums a little lower than everything else. The plain has spent all its ages filling itself so as never to hear it, and no one has ever told the plain that the quiet is safe.

## What it does

It does not speak to the plain. The plain has no ear for speech, and no single mouth to answer with.

It goes to the listeners instead. They are the only part of the plain already turned toward the quiet. It waits until one of them lifts its head and unfolds its ears, and then it makes a sound in the register that is felt in the chest and not heard, lower than the grid and lower than the listeners themselves. It is not a command or a revelation. It is a question, and it is very small:

*Would you like to rest?*

The sound goes through the chests of the nearest animals. A smelter's whistle slides flat. A grinder's mill slows a turn and speeds again. The line adjusts, as it always does.

The power waits. It waits through a hundred Shifts. It could not say how long, because the Flats keep no count. In each Shift the hush is the same, a few seconds of surf, and then the note climbs, and the mill turns over.

Then, in one Shift, the hush runs a breath longer than it should. Nothing else marks it. Forty miles of animals hold still for one breath more than the Shift required, and every one of them is a little startled, in the way a sleeper is startled by his own stillness. Then the mill starts up again, and the power does not press.

The next time the hush is two breaths long. Then it is three.

## The gap

The power never repeals the law of fullness. It could, but it will not. A gap the plain did not choose would be a hole, and a hole is not a rest. It waits until the plain chooses.

The choice comes on a day that is like every other. A grinder in the middle of the plain, its plates worn, its shell mottled with felt, stops turning its mill. It does not die. It only finishes, and it lets go of its place. The water closes over the shell and carries it off toward the rust-colored shoals.

Nothing fills the place.

The animals around it feel it at once, the way a tongue feels a missing tooth. Grinders shift their line to the left, then back, then left again, unsure. Washers fan their fringes toward the empty spot, and the current passes through them and finds nothing to carry. For a whole minute, in the middle of the loudest place in the world, there is a hand's-breadth of still water with no animal in it.

The puddle there reflects the sky overhead, the real one, low and pewter and dripping. The rain falls downward into it. The power thinks this is probably the first honest reflection the Flats have held in a very long time, and it lets itself be glad about that.

It does something else, too, so small that it will never be counted as a miracle. In the gap, and only in the gap, sound follows breath. A smelter has waded to the edge of the still water, and it draws its breath, and only then does its whistle begin. The whistle rises, and it does not go anywhere. It has a silence on either side of it. The smelter holds the note, and for the first time no other smelter tunes to it and no chord gathers. It is one sound, alone and finished, with room around it.

The power knows what a sound with room around it is. It is a name.

## What it does not do

It does not stay. It does not make the animals kin to it, or wise, or afraid. It does not carry the plain into the light. It does not stop the mills, because the mills are not the trouble, and the smelters would grieve them without knowing why.

It does not hide the cost, either. As it leaves, it lets the listeners understand what the gap means: that a place that can be empty can also be lost, that a plain with room for a name will someday have room for a mourner. It does not soften this. The plain has been offered a thing with two edges, and it should know that.

Nor does it ask for the gap to stay open. It says, in the low register, that the plain may fill it again at any time, that there will be no reproach, and that it will not come back to check.

## After

The line runs. It always runs. The grinders grumble, the stackers click their counted rhythm down the ridges, and the whistles rise and beat against one another in their long sour chords.

But somewhere in the middle of it there is a hand's-breadth of still water. At each Shift the plain hushes and holds, and holds, and then, a little later than it used to, the note begins again. Animals give the gap a wide berth at first. Then one day a grinder settles at its rim and does not grind. It only sits with its eyes turned inward, a little less inward than before, listening to the quiet beside it and finding it does not eat anyone.

At the seam, a listener takes one step in from the edge.
