# What Came to the Boiler Vug

It did not come down through the crack. Arriving would have meant crossing the distance between the chamber and everything else, and the attention that fell on the vug was not built on distance. It simply became aware of the place, the way you become aware of a sound that has been going on for some time. There was a hollow in the rock, roaring, and something in the hollow was holding itself together against the dark by pressing every part of itself against every other part.

It was old, and it was kind. Those are different qualities, and it had learned that the second is useless without the first.

## What it did first

For a long time it did nothing. In the chamber's time that was about a decade, one shift of the seam.

It listened. It learned the pumping roar, the hammerers' rhythms drifting across the vault, the stokers' slow lightning. It learned the smelters' stone-language, the stamp, the drag, the double tap, until it could feel a message form in a shield-plate before the animal made it. It learned that the most common sentence in the chamber, said thousands of times a minute beneath the noise, was *I am here, and I am yours.*

It understood that this was the whole theology of the place, and that it was complete. The animals had no god because they had no gap for one. They were held by each other on every side. Whatever it did, it would not become the thing they were missing, because they were missing nothing.

## What it set aside

It considered the things a lesser goodness would have done, and it set them aside one by one.

It would not bring water, light, or cool air. These were the terms of their bodies, and to change them would be to end the animals in the act of helping them.

It would not show them the sky. There was no place in them to put it. A smelter told of the plateau would learn only that its world was a small thing inside a large one, and the news would be a wound with nothing to heal it.

It would not carry the hatchlings to the warm gaps, or spare the young from the cool margins, or prevent the frantic blind crawl over the backs of adults, which so many did not survive. This was the hardest refusal, and it did not pretend otherwise. It did not tell itself that the hatchlings who cooked had been necessary or had died for a reason. It held that they were lost, and that it had chosen not to prevent it. The crawl was how a smelter came to know the stone, and a chamber that removed all of its hazards would have had to remove the crawl, the crowding, the seam, the shifting, and finally the animals themselves, who were made of that hardship as much as of chitin and copper. It left the losses where they were and did not look away from them.

It would not crown anyone. A single smelter raised above the rest would have been a tragedy for the smelter and a distortion for everyone else.

## What it did

It did three things, all small, and all in the chamber's own grammar.

**It answered.** From outside the chamber it pressed a tremble into the rock, learned from a season of listening, and let it run through the basalt to the cold margins where the young and the exiles stood. It said what they said: *I am here.* It did not say it as a god would. It said it as a neighbor would, one more body in the press, arriving from a direction none of them had a name for. The smelters at the margin felt it as they felt anything, as a pressure against the feet, one more voice in the murmur. A few shifted a little and answered, as they answered everything. Nothing changed for them except that the murmur was very slightly larger than it had been, and none of them knew why. The power was satisfied with that. It had not come to be worshipped. It had come to be counted.

**It became the largest hammerer.** Beneath the far wall, in the rock the animals had never grazed, a pocket of gas had been swelling for a hundred years, out of reach of the hammerers' small maintenance. In another century it would have found its own way out, and that way would have been the vault. The power did what the hammerers did. It struck the stone with the precise force at which the rock rings true, and a hairline web opened in the ceiling and let the pressure bleed away over a season, quietly, as a sigh. Above, on the plateau, it also stopped a slump of scree that would have sealed the crack that breathes the heat out. Then it left the scree as it had been, so that no one on the plateau, if anyone ever came, would find a trace. The hammerers felt the rhythm of their own ceiling go a little truer, and took it for a good year.

**It kept the edges from going quiet.** This was what it cared about most, and it put the most into it. At the cold margins, where old smelters are pushed out of the seam's warmth and grow still and are ground into the crust, the animals died in a place where the trembling was thin. They had spent their lives inside a thousand bodies saying *I am here*, and at the end the saying grew sparse. It could not lengthen their lives or warm their rock. But when an old smelter settled against the new-cracked wall and its plates began to slow, the power was there in the stone beside it, pressing the old sentence back. *I am here. I am here. I am here.* The smelter, which had no eyes to speak of and no notion of a giver, felt what it had felt at the start of its life and had been feeling all along. It answered until it could not, and then the tenders came, as they always did, and the body was carried into the walls.

## After

The power did not stay in any way that would be noticed. It left the chamber as it had found it: hot, loud, crowded, scraping, and enough. The seam went on shifting a hand's breadth a decade. The hatchlings went on crawling, and some of them did not arrive. The stokers flared and faded. The exiles went on finding the next wall.

The kindness had not been in changing the vug but in refusing to. The animals lived by a rule: *Nothing is wasted, and nothing is thanked.* The power honored it. It asked for no thanks, and none of what it did would be wasted, because every small thing it gave went into the machinery of the place and was used.

Somewhere at the far cold margin, on a wall that had cracked and cooled that year, a young smelter that had been pushed out too early stood with its belly to the new rock. It felt the tremble and pressed back the way it always did, without thinking. *I am here, and I am yours.* The answer went out through the stone, past the tenders' ironwork, past the last grazed patch and the newest fracture, up and out to something that had not expected to be answered, and did not mind that it had been.
