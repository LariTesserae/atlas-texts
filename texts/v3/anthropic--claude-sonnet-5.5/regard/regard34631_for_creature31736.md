# The Answer

## What It Does Not Do

It does not come as light. It does not open the roof, or speak from the sky in a voice like a judgment.

It sees quickly what the Hollow is and what it is not. The empty houses are not a wound to be filled with new settlers, and the swept floors are not a mystery to be solved. The fear in the old and the hope in the young are the same instrument played in two registers, and to remove either would put the town out of tune. It sees the door in the east and what lies behind it, and it says nothing to anyone, because that knowledge belongs to the old Tuners and they give it out in their own time.

So it brings no water, food, cure, or gift. Anything a power like this could hand over would arrive as a verdict on the way these people already live, and they have lived well.

## The Listening

It begins by listening, which takes a long time, since it must first learn how the Hollow speaks.

It spends thousands of breaths in the plaza's roar, and it learns what the Tuners know: that a word is not a note but a note *and a route*. The same tone pushed into the third flue and into the ninth arrives as two different sentences, because the town shapes it on the way. The meaning lives in the pipes, and so the town is not a place where the language is spoken but the language itself. It learns to hear the cracked brick in the third stack. It learns the cry that means *I need a hand with the horn*, and the patient, repeated phrase that means *keep your stem straight*, which is said to the canes and answered by their drumming.

Most of all it learns the silence of the inhale, when all two hundred stop and turn toward the hum of the straws. It understands that this is not rest. The whole town is listening upward, and has been since before the pipes were pitched high, and that is what all of this has been for.

## The Roof

There is one real danger, and it is the one thing the Attention decides to act on.

The crust in the east is thin as horn, and it will not open gently. When it goes, it will go as a slab. Forty meters of salt and gypsum would fall onto the eastern vents, and the pressure in the bowl would surge and then choke. The breath would stutter for days. The green would begin to shrivel. The great flue would stay whole, since the dead are patient and well rooted, but the living would be left in a silence they had never been taught to survive.

The Attention does not make the hole. It does not choose the year. It works the way the Hollow itself works, by leaning gently on the salt until the salt reaches a shape it can keep. It tells the crust where to let go: a grain at a time, from the center outward, in a widening ring. The larger pieces are turned to fall east, away from the vents, onto the long empty streets where nothing lives and only the black leaves lie. The light will open like an eye slowly waking. It may take decades, and when it comes it will look like luck, or like ordinary settling.

The Tuners will never know they were helped. The Attention counts this as part of the help.

## The Door

Once, when an old Tuner's voice reaches its last low note, the Attention is in the plaza.

It watches the Tuner glide down the western stair and along the dark mirror of the polished stones. It watches the others stop their work as it passes, and each sounds a single note down the nearest flue. The note goes through the town like a hand laid on a shoulder.

The Attention does not follow past the last foundations, and it does not go near the door. But it adds its note. It pushes one tone into the clay, in the pitch of the oldest reed in the ring of houses, and the town carries it under the streets and out through the horns with all the others. The Tuners feel one extra thread in the roar. None of them turns. The old one goes in.

## The Answer

The young Tuner is standing at the eastern wall, as it does at the end of every exhale, its arms folded and its crest curled. It has a fresh reed in its chest, and it has spoken its note upward more times than it has counted. Nothing has ever come back but the straws.

The Attention waits for the exhale to peak. It does not answer into the roar, where a single voice would be lost, or where a stranger's voice might be mistaken for a fault in the pipes. It waits for the inhale, when the pitch sags and the town sighs and every Tuner turns toward the high thin hum overhead.

Then the hum changes.

Every selenite straw in the roof shifts by a hair, and they are all singing one note, the young Tuner's own, exactly as it was sent up. The Attention has said nothing new. It has said only what a listener says first: *I heard you.*

In the amber dusk the young one's crest unrolls, in the inhale, when crests do not unroll. The ring of Tuners stands very still. Some of them are afraid, and some of them are not. Nobody moves toward the wall, and nobody moves away.

The young one puts a pad to the flue-mouth and waits for the next exhale. Then it speaks again, a little differently this time, with a rise in it that has never been in the town's grammar. The pipes take it up and shape it, and the horns carry it out across the plaza as a phrase no one has heard before. It might be a question. It might be a name.

## Afterward

The Attention stays, though it does not stay as a visitor would, or as a god would. It stays as a beginner. It answers slowly, in the inhale, one note at a time, and it lets the town correct its pitch. Sometimes it is a quarter tone flat, and the Tuners tune it, because tuning is what they do. It is glad of this, because it is the only work here that was ever meant to be shared.

The roof goes on thinning at its own pace. The breath goes on. In the evenings the houses hum with sleepers, each in its own pitch, and now and then, from far above, one more thread comes down the pipes and finds its place in the chord.
