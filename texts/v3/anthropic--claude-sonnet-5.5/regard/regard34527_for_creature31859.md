# What Was Done

The attention did not arrive as light. There was too little light for that, and the Ward would not have known what to do with it. It arrived as a change in how the sound came back from the vault, a shift so slight that only the oldest warden, standing at the flat column, lifted its head. Something had come in that the chord had to go around.

The power took the spiral stair on foot, all four hundred steps. It could have arrived at the gantry without them, but it wanted the heat and the noise to arrive in the order they arrive for anyone, as information before understanding. By the bottom it had stopped hearing the chord as noise. It had begun to hear it as a sentence.

## Three Rains of Listening

It did nothing for three rains, and this was the first and largest part of what it did.

It walked the plazas and let the crawlers step over its feet. It crouched at a canal and felt a glass-newt press its snout to a fingertip, an old courtesy. It found the voice in the chord that is held wrong on purpose, and it understood that this was the city's grief converted into structure. The columns that survived the Quiet Days did not mourn the lost district in silence or in song. They mourned it by leaning, all of them together, a little to one side, so the vault would never again notice what was missing.

In the black-tiled ring, dry inside the silver threads, it learned the rest. The Ward was not failing, and it was not waiting to be rescued. It was fragile in the way a living thing is fragile, which is different from being broken, and the power was old enough to know the difference.

## What It Declined

It considered replacing the vault with stone and left the vault alone. A ceiling that stands on its own would turn the singing into decoration. The wardens would still press their skulls to the pewter, but the note would no longer matter, and in the power's long experience, things that stop mattering are the things that stop being tended. The care in this place came from the stakes.

It considered increasing the wardens, which have numbered about a dozen for as long as records go, and left that alone too. It didn't know what balance that number was keeping, and it would not gamble a hundred lives on its own ignorance of one.

It considered telling the truth about the makers: who they were, where they went, whether anyone was coming. It weighed this longest. Then it saw that the Ward's expectation had stopped being a question and become a practice, like a hearth kept lit. To answer it would end the practice without replacing it. It did not lie, because it was not asked. It kept the answer to itself and counted that as a gift.

It considered lighting the dark. The dark was doing exactly what darkness does to sound, giving it a place to be heard.

## What It Did

**The crawler at the canal.** One crawler walks the canal's length and back, and at one broken stretch of lining it stops and holds the ceramic in place for a minute, remembering it cannot mend it. The power crouched beside it and touched its manipulators to the crawler's own, in the way crawlers wake each other. It did not repair the lining. It gave the crawler the knowledge of how, and the crawler stood a long moment, its amber joint-light pulsing, and then began. The mending took eleven hours. When it was done the crawler stood at the seam with nothing to hold, and the power watched it decide, or something like deciding, to walk on. Its round was a minute shorter now. On the next lap, it stopped at a halted crawler in the street, and touched it.

**The halted ones.** The power went among the crawlers standing with folded legs and pulsing heads. It woke some, and they went off on rounds that had been waiting for them. Others it did not wake. Some waiting is a stalled machine, but some is a finished one, and a finished one needs no restart, only company. For one full rain it stood beside a crawler that had halted before the corners of the houses were worn. It stayed until the amber light at the joint dimmed by itself, gently and completely, and the crawlers who came afterward found the body warm and unmolested. Then they began the long work of setting it among the columns with the bones.

**The rubble field.** The pioneer forest of the Quiet Days is mostly dying, its pale shoots thin and leggy and starved. The power did not plant anything. It found where the vault was already sweating, high above the field, and eased a hairline crack a finger's width to one side, so that the drip fell where the shoots stood. It was an act indistinguishable from luck. Three seasons later a few more of them would be alive. It let them think the ceiling had simply been kind.

**The grace.** This was the largest thing it did. It went to the flat column at the edge of the rubble, where the oldest warden stood, and laid a palm against the pewter beside the warden's own. The warden turned its blind face, fans fluttering, took the measure of the stranger's body in the sound, and then, unhurried, laid a broad hand on the power's shoulder. It was warm as bathwater, and the power felt the chord through that hand as it had never quite felt anything, like being shown where a note sits in a chest.

Into the column it set a voice below the reach of hearing, one that does nothing while the chord holds. If a ring were ever to falter, it would take part of the load, not all of it, only enough to change how the ceiling failed. What had once been a fall within the hour would become a slow settling over a day, with time to walk out and time for wardens to arrive, and the crawlers to answer, and the retuning to begin. The stakes were the same and the city was as fragile as before. But now a failure would be something the Ward could survive.

## The One Who Lives There

The power spent its last rain in the ring of great columns with the one who had come down the stair and stayed. This person had no name in the roar, and the power did not offer one. It sat in the dry inside the silver threads and said nothing, because nothing can be said there, and because that was the thing the resident needed least to be told.

It did not fix their loneliness, and it did not lift them out toward some drier, brighter place they had never asked for. It only sat, one more body in the pressure and the warmth. When the rain ended, the resident walked toward the tall house where a green lamp burned for them, and the power watched them climb the dished stairs and go in. The lamp burned on, the same lamp, unchanged. That was the most it could do: to leave a lit window exactly as it was, and let them understand it had been meant for them.

## Leaving

It went up the stair the way it came, counting. At the gantry it paused and let its own note, which it had held faintly in the chord for the length of its stay, fall away. The note was not the city's to keep. The vault took the difference without complaint, in the way a rope takes a hand's release.

In the dark below, the oldest warden turned its head toward the place where the stranger had stood, and held the pose for a long time. Then it turned back to the column, laid its palms against the pewter, and pressed its skull to the note, which was the same as it had been before, and which it went on holding, along with a dozen others and a crowd of small things, and now one more that had come down the stair.

The vault stayed up, and above it the rest of the world went on, unaware that it had been visited by so much restraint.
