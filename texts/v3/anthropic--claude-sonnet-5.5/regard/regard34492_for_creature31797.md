# What Was Done

## The Arrival

It came the way a change in pressure comes, with no shape and no shadow on the ice. It came from nowhere the shelf had a word for, because the shelf had almost no words. It did not descend. It attended.

For a long while it did only that, and it counted the doing as work. It lay along the channel between the ice and the basalt and let the sound go through it. It learned the seven seconds and the harmonics that ride on them: the shriek of the bearings, the pour of the pumps, the second-order beat where two halls' strokes drift in and out of agreement over a span of years. It learned the herds by phase, since phase is how they speak. It learned that a calf in the third hall of the eastern cluster ran a fortieth of a second late and would carry that lag all its life as a name.

It was moved, if that word applies. It had expected to find something to rescue. It found something that was working.

## The Temptation

The obvious mercy was right there, and it took the obvious mercy seriously, because the obvious mercies are usually obvious for a reason.

It could stop the engines. It could give the shelf a silence, and with the silence a truth: that there had been a noise, that the noise was made, that a world can be otherwise. Everything living there had been born inside a sentence with no beginning. Wouldn't a kind power break the sentence and let them hear the room?

It followed the consequence out to the end, as far as it could see.

The silence would arrive as a wound. Every drifter's tail would be beating against a bell that was no longer there, and a herd of thousands would lose its common time in a few strokes. Mothers would lose their calves in the crowd, because a name is a lag measured against a beat, and with the beat gone the names would go with it. The hunters, who listen for flaws, would find the whole herd made of flaws, and the feast would run for a season. The warm water in the nurseries would cool by degrees. The crawlers, who move only between strokes, would not know when to move. The tube-worms would lean toward a wheel that had stopped.

Nothing there could take a truth in that form. It would have been like teaching a fish about air by lifting it out.

So it set the obvious mercy aside, and did not count that as failure. The wisest thing it knew was that knowledge given faster than a creature can carry it is not a gift.

## The Makers

It looked longer at the works, and understood something it had not expected.

The halls had not been abandoned. Nobody leaves the rails, the carts, and the crane like that unless they mean to. Nothing had been carried off and no door had been shut behind anyone. The engines had been made to run untended, and to be tended, in the only ways that could be arranged, by the very things that would come to live in them: the drifters grazing scale from the condenser tubes, the crawlers rasping the valve gear, the calves rubbing the cylinder's warm flank. The makers had built a cradle and then had the harder virtue, which was to leave it.

Whoever they were, they had been in this same position and had chosen restraint. The power felt something like fellowship across the gap of ages. It also felt an obligation to be worthy of that choice, which meant to do very little, and to do it well.

## The Three Things

It found that three things were worth doing.

**The first was the honest reckoning of time.** The shelf believes it is unchanging, and it isn't. In a thousand years a bearing wears. In ten thousand a valve seats badly, a footing shifts, a cylinder scores. The stability is a very long slope, and at the bottom of it the bell stops. The end would come as a catastrophe for the same reasons the power had just refused to cause one: a world that has never met change cannot survive its first.

So the power taught the shelf, without telling it anything, how to change. In the eastern cluster, and then in others, over ages, it eased one engine's stroke by a thousandth of a second every few years. It was too little for any calf to notice, and each generation followed the slow drift without effort, the way a field of grain follows a wind that has turned a little. Then it eased another hall, in another direction. The herds learned, in the body where learning lasts, that the beat is a thing you follow and not a thing that simply is. It was a vaccination against the day, so far off, when the bell would fail.

It never eased any stroke faster than the youngest calf could follow. That was the rule it made and kept.

**The second was a place of quiet.** The channel between ice and basalt is a resonant hall, and in any resonant hall there are nodes, places where the crossing paths of sound cancel and the pressure stands still. The shelf has natural ones, but they drift and they are narrow. The power chose one and settled it: a broad shallow bowl in the lee of a low basalt ridge, two days' drift from the nearest hall. It shaped nothing with hands. It set the standing waves so that the sound folded in on itself there, and the bowl fell almost silent.

It did not lure anything to the bowl. It put no sign at its edge and no scent in the water. A place that has to be advertised is a trap. It only made the bowl available, the way a door is available.

It made it for the old ones.

The old drifters die by falling behind. They drift to the margin where the beat thins, and out there, alone and out of step, the hunters find them. The bowl gave them somewhere else to drift. There, the hunters, who find everything by its flaw in the rhythm, have no rhythm to find a flaw in. Nothing there can be late, because nothing there is on time. An old drifter with no strokes left in her could hang in that stillness and rest. She might live a few seasons more, and when she died it would be of age and not of teeth.

The hunters lose almost nothing by it. The herds are vast and flaws are plentiful, and the power was careful that it should not be a war on the hunters, who are not cruel. They are only hungry, patient, and entrained like everything else, their jaws never opening on the stroke.

**The third was to remain unknown.** It left no sign that anything had been done. It took no name, appeared to no one, and interrupted nothing. The bowl looks like a chance of the water, the drift of the strokes like the ordinary slow tuning of old machines, and the mercy looks like luck. This was not modesty. A power that reveals itself becomes the new bell, and the shelf would organize itself around it, and the shelf's whole life would tilt toward a listening it could never satisfy. The power judged that the shelf had enough to listen to.

## What It Did Not Do

It did not save the calf that fails to catch the beat within three days. It stayed beside one such calf as she faded, though the calf could not know it, and it grieved her in whatever way a power grieves. Then it let her go, because reaching in would have meant taking the shelf's grammar apart at its base, and it could not reach into one place without reaching into all. There are sorrows that belong to the structure of a world, and a power that removes all of them removes the world.

It did not feed the hunters or starve them, did not thin the ice, and did not tell the pale fish in the iron corners that they are the oldest things there. They probably know.

## After

It left, or it thinned, the way the sound thins at the shelf's edge, until it was not distinguishable from the water.

Ages afterward, an old drifter, a female so far behind the beat that she could not have told anyone by how much, drifted to the margin because there was nowhere else for her to go. She waited for the dark shape. It did not come, because something had shifted in the water around her, a fold in the sound, a lessening. The bell went faint and then fainter, and the herd's pulse, which she had felt all her life in every fiber, receded until she hung alone in a stillness she had no word for.

In it, for the first time, she felt something small and steady, out of step with everything she had ever known, keeping its own time.

It was her own heart. She had had it all along.

She listened to it for a long while.
