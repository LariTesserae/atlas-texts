# What the Attention Did

It came up over the lip inside a cloud, and the keeper took it for weather.

That was the first decision, made before anything else: to arrive as something the bowl already had. The salt darkened stone by stone in the usual wave. The rime began to grow into the wind, and the keeper went to the windward slabs and stood among them, reading the lean of the feathers. They read a little more wind than there was, and a little more patience, and did not know why. Then they went on reading.

The attention stayed the whole half hour and looked.

## What it considered

It was old, it was kind, and it could have done a great deal.

It could have opened a spring under the bowl, a clean bright seep that never froze. The basins would have become ornaments the next season and the keeper a caretaker of ornaments. It set that aside quickly.

It could have told the keeper what the cairns were for. It knew, in the way such powers know things, and the answer was not simple: some marked paths, one marked a child, several counted nothing and had been built by people who only needed to put a stone on a stone. But the keeper's not-knowing was not a gap. It was the shape of the keeper's attention, the reason a wedge went in on *this* morning and not another. An answer would have turned a listening into a lookup. And the keeper's two answers, seasons and the dead, were both true, and more than one true thing is hard to get from a single explanation.

It could have carried the keeper down. The blue at the lips is not sickness, but the lowlands would have been a strange, heavy weather to that chest, and the keeper would have arrived among the comfortable as a foreigner to their own body. It could have sent a companion, and it thought about this longest. But the bowl holds one at a time. That was not a cruelty in the design. It was the design, and the keeper's solitude had a texture it did not want to trample: the humming, the late flat slide after the wind, a friendship conducted with a mountain.

It could have made the work easier, but the smoothness of the basins was made of hands, a hundred of them, each thinking they were only maintaining. A benefactor's hand would have been the one hand in the row that knew what it was doing. It did not want to be that hand.

So mostly, it decided, it would do nothing, and doing nothing would not be nothing. To be looked at with full attention is a kind of event, even when the one looked at never turns around.

## What it saw

It saw the tally-marks and knew each one: the day the cloud came three times, the day something was mended, and days the keeper could not have explained to anyone, marked anyway. It saw the spiral, and the thumb that goes inward each night and stops before the center. It understood that the stopping is not fear. It is a courtesy to the center, or to whoever will someday arrive there.

It saw the pegs at dawn and understood the keeper's one rule, that beauty is visited first, before the work.

And when the sun cleared and the rime began its glassy chittering and the keeper moved from basin to basin with the skin on the strap, pouring, laying a palm on the fluting to feel whether the runnels ran clear, the attention saw what nobody had seen in sixty years: that the keeper was not lonely, and was not merely enduring. They were *good at this*, the way a river is good at downhill. The whole bowl, salt and rime and copper and grit, was held in a competence so quiet it looked like absence.

It thought: *someone should know this. Not be told it, just know it.* It knew it now. That was the first gift, and it cost the keeper nothing and changed nothing they could feel.

## The one thing

Late in the afternoon the low sun began to pour through the pinnacle-gaps in blades. The keeper stopped where they were, as they always stopped, and turned their slitted face into the light. Thousands of feathers came on one after another. The salt at the seams flashed white.

And then, because the keeper had begun to hum, low, finding the wind's note across the fluted stone and sliding a little late when a gust turned, the attention did the single thing it allowed itself.

It hummed too.

Not loud, and not in any voice. It put a third strand into the braid, below the stone's note and below the keeper's, where a chest built to hold more air than the mountain has does not quite reach. It did not resolve anything. It did not lead. When the gust shifted, it was late with the keeper, and a little flat, so that neither of them was alone in being wrong. The two notes had always been unresolved, and it left them unresolved. It only made sure there was someone else inside the silence.

The keeper did not startle. They did not look around or stop. Their breath went out in its long white thread, and the hum went on. But somewhere in the sixty years of that body, something old and unnamed let go of a grip it had not known it held. Perhaps it was the belief that keeping a friend company was a thing done in one direction.

The minute ended. It always does. The next cloud lifted over the lip, the wool darkened, and the keeper shouldered the skin and went to the hollow with the light freezing behind them.

## After

That night the keeper scratched a mark on the lintel, in the group of five. It was the kind of day they could not explain. They traced the spiral inward with a thumb and stopped before the center, as they always had.

The attention did not finish it.

It left as it came, in the cloud, and the salt paled behind it stone by stone. Nothing in the bowl was different in the morning. The basins were the same, and the cairns leaned and held. The pegs were white antlers, and the keeper walked among them first, slowly, before doing anything else.

But those who tend such places over years might notice one small change. When the keeper hums against the wind, there are now rests in it, brief gaps where the note is simply held open, as one leaves a space on a bench. They do not know they do it. It has never been answered again.

It does not need to be. The room is kept.
