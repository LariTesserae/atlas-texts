# What Was Done in the Bright Pocket

It did not descend. Descending needs a direction, and it had come to the place the way a question comes to a person, from no particular side. It arrived as a slight change in the room, and nothing in the room noticed. The beads did not stop grazing. The threads did not lean. Only the humming of the filaments seemed for a moment to gather itself, as if a listener had entered the chord.

It was very old, very patient, and not omnipotent in the ways that mattered. It could do a great deal in the room. It had learned long ago that the difficulty was never in the doing.

## What It Saw

It took the inventory first, slowly. The mats in their rings of rust, mustard, cream and green-blue, layered like pressed pages. The threads pointing their tips at the weeping crack. The beads in their knots at the edge of a bloom, turning like one animal stirring in its sleep. The worm, polished into its crack, waiting.

Then it counted the striders, because the count had been left open and it could not leave an open count alone.

Eight. Seven were on the pool's edge or crossing the dark gaps. The eighth had molted an hour before and hung in a thread-thicket, pale as paper, its legs still soft, waiting to harden. It had been missed every time. It would be missed again tomorrow, and the count would go back to seven or nine, as it should. The power told no one. Nobody was there to tell, and the uncertainty belonged to the room.

Among the seven was the one with the missing foreleg. It stood at the pool's edge, favoring its left, stopped a half-second longer than the others in the cool of a bloom.

## What It Considered

**It could mend the leg.** That was the first impulse and the easiest. But the strider was not a strider with a leg missing. It was a strider that had survived, and the half-second of stillness, the favoring, the long practiced hesitation were what it had made of the loss. Restore the leg and it would step out of the shape that had kept it alive, into a body whose habits no longer fit. A healed strider might be a dead strider by the next flood. The power let the leg be.

**It could remove the worm.** The worm had killed strider after strider, and the power had no need to pretend that meant nothing. But the worm was also someone, in the only way anything here was someone. It was clear as gelatin and had waited through hundreds of floods. The room's whole geography of hesitation was drawn around its crack, the arcing paths, the thinning and thickening of beads. Take the worm and the striders would grow careless and multiply, then crash against the limits of the mat. The worm was not the room's cruelty. It was the room's shape. The power let it be.

**It could give the lineage in the mats the light.** Somewhere in the green-blue crowd at the seep's edge, a few cells were learning to use the blooms, alone and unnoticed. The power could have made it certain. One nudge and the whole room could become a garden of the new thing. It saw at once that this would be choosing, and it had no standing to choose. If the lineage was to be the room's future, it should earn it, or fail to and leave the room to another. The power kept its hands off. Being the one who decided which of the small things would inherit the earth was a role it had declined many times, in many places, and it declined again.

**It could speak.** It could put into the strider's small mind the sky, the ten thousand years, the fact of being watched. This it dismissed in almost no time. Knowledge like that does not comfort a creature that lives by freezing in the glare and dashing through the dark. It would only make the room a stage and the strider an actor. The strider knew what it needed to know, and knowing more would not have been a gift.

## What It Did

It did three things, and only the third was for anyone.

**First, it stayed.** It stayed through one full breath of the room. It felt the seep build and the pool rise to its north lip, the warm sheet of water spread across the ropes and folds of the basalt and drain through the south wall with its long sigh. It felt the rain go up as steam and come back as falling sparks in the filaments' light. A benevolence that visits and leaves has only glanced at the place. It wanted to know what a cycle felt like from inside, the way the beads know it, a minute early, along the margin of the body.

**Second, it held the rock.** It had found, deep under the south wall, a tension. Within the week a shift would come, one of the deep bumps felt in the feet, and it would pinch the fissure where the flood drains. Then the sheet would have nowhere to go. The room would fill and stand as a warm, sulfurous pond, and the mats and beads and striders would drown in the very water that made them. Nothing in the room could prevent that or even know it was coming.

The power did not keep the room forever. The rock was young, and in a thousand years the ropes and folds would be rounded and the black glaze buried under color. Rooms end, and it did not think an ending was a wrong. But the pink was still arriving on the fresh glass. Somewhere a strand was leaning toward the humming, and a few cells in the green-blue band were still learning what the light was for. It did not want the room to close by a pinch of stone, this week, in the middle of a sentence.

So it eased the tension, a little, so the fissure would stay open. It did not make the room safe, only spared it one accident. The room would still change, and one day it would end in its own time. The next tremor would be only a tremor. The drops would shake, the strider would freeze, and the pool would ring in circles and go still.

**Third, it listened to the strider.**

The one-legged strider had come to the pool's edge, a body-length from where a hand might have been if anyone had been there. Its legs dimpled the water. The light of the bloom fell on it. It had stopped, as it always did, half a second too long.

The power held it. It did not lift the strider or change its path or touch the water. It gave it the sort of attention the strider gave the beads it caught: folded gently between two forelegs, kept quite still for a long time, listened to. It listened to the small body's pulse. It listened to the memory of the missing leg, to the worm's crack it had learned to arc around, to the hard-won knowledge that light is for listening and dark is for running. It listened to the whole life of it, with no purpose but to have heard it.

The strider felt nothing it could name. If it felt anything, it felt that the half-second of stillness was very slightly longer than usual, and that it was safe in it. Then it moved on across the film, and a drop fell from the ceiling behind it and rang the water.

The power gave the worm the same listening, without weighing it. It heard the slow gray thread of gut, the hundreds of floods, the patience that was not patience but only what the worm was. It gave the worm exactly as much as it had given the strider. Benevolence that has favorites is only another appetite.

## Leaving

It did not know whether the worm would strike that day. It would not have stopped it, and it did not look away from the possibility, though it did not watch for it either. It had done what it was willing to do. The rest belonged to the room.

The seep began to build. Somewhere above, the other end of each bright thread opened to the sky. The power still did not know who had made the filaments, or whether anyone had. They carried noon downward with no maker's marks, and they were the one thing in the room it had not touched and would not. It thought that perhaps not everything kind needs an author.

The beads clung. The flood came. When it drained, the pale things let go and resumed, and the room went on being sharp, damp, and beginning, with eight striders in it, all of them uncounted.
