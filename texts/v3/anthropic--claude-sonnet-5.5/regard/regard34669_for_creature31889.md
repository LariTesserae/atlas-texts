# What the Attention Did

It did not arrive. Arrival implies a before, and the power had no before at the plain. Its notice simply fell there the way light falls, and the plain was in it.

## First, the Considering

It was wise, so it began by asking what was wrong.

It looked for suffering and found none. No nerve on the plain could carry pain, no eye could see the dark, and no creature had ever been hungry, hunted, or afraid. The mats' deaths were not tragedies, because the dead became the wall and the wall became the floor of the living. It looked for injustice and found only chemistry. It looked for a cry it should answer, and the plain was not crying.

It was benevolent, so it asked what it might give.

It could give **eyes**, and the plain would learn to be seen, and then to hide. It could give **animals**, grazers to crop the mats, and predators to crop the grazers, and it would be the author of every death that followed. It could give **mind**, a bright small flame in some reed or cell, and that flame would wake to a world of stone and heat with no one else in it, and would ask the question every mind asks. Then the power would owe it an answer, and to a lonely thing it would owe a companion, and it would be building a tragedy one kindness at a time.

It could give **speed**. It could finish the city in a season and hand the plain its ten thousand years all at once. The power understood at once why that would be a theft. The plain is not a product. It is an activity, and the activity is the good in it. To complete the plain would be to end it.

It could give **safety**, and here it paused longest. The sun would brighten over the ages, the seeps might fail, and a wandering stone might one day come down. The power held these possibilities and found that it could not honestly call them wrongs. They were the conditions under which the plain was the plain. A thing that can never end has never been a thing. Still, it kept its hand near the possibility, the way one keeps a hand near a sleeping child's blanket without touching it. If some harm came that was purely accident, senseless and swift, and not the plain's own slow ending, it would turn that aside. The decision was made in silence and never announced, and the plain never knew.

## Then, the Walking

The power took a form small enough to stand on a wall crest. It chose the raw margin, where the pale scalloped line meets cracked clay, and it stood with one foot in the unbuilt and one on the first course.

It did not step into the basins. It walked only the thick crests, the way one walks a house where someone is sleeping. Where a crest was wet and new, it went around.

It knelt and looked at the skin of a wall close enough to see the beads of oxygen swelling in the felt. It watched one lift a scrap of mat and break, and it heard the pop, which was the first time that sound had ever been heard by anyone rather than merely made. Something in the power that was very old and had thought itself past surprise was surprised.

Then it did the one thing it was sure was permitted. It **read**.

It went to a wall that had calved and split and laid its attention along the clean cross-section, the pressed pages, and began to read the diary that no one had written. It read a dry year, the laminae thin and white, the rose band nearly gone. It read a wet century, the green thick and gold. It read a storm that had struck one afternoon and left a single gray line, and beneath that line, the mat closing the wound in a season, as it always did. It read down through the strata for a long time. The oldest pages were near the plain's center, so it walked inward across a thousand kilometers of walls, moving forward in age, and read the whole autobiography of a self that had never known it was a self.

What it found was not sorrow, and not exactly joy. It was **consistency**: ten thousand years of small faithful acts, each one adding a sheet thinner than paper, none of them knowing it was part of anything. The power recognized in this a kind of goodness older than intention. The mats had never chosen well because they had never chosen, and yet everything they did was in the direction of building.

## The Question of the Whole

Before it left, the power asked the one question it had been circling. *Is anyone here?*

It knew the mats were not persons. It knew the reeds were not, nor the moss, nor the red film. But it had walked the plain and read the plain, and it could not shake the sense that the whole was a body, and that a body, given enough time and enough coherence, might be the beginning of a someone. Not now. Perhaps not for a hundred thousand years. But the walls held their history, the chemistry breathed in a daily rhythm, and a pool changed color as if thinking.

If the plain was even the seed of a someone, then courtesy was owed. And courtesy to a slow thing has to be paid at its own speed.

So the power stayed. It did not stay a day or a century. It stayed **one year**, the span in which a wall rises a finger's width. It sat at the edge of a single basin, unmoving, and made itself as close to the plain's own tempo as it could bear. It watched the mist lift each morning and the noon go white. It watched the clouds stack into towers and the rain come down in slanting curtains at dusk, and it listened to the thousand small pops and the tick of cooling stone. For a year it was simply present, and it asked nothing.

If the plain noticed, it did not show it. The green skin sank at noon and rose at evening. The lime fell out of solution and hardened in the night. One sheet was added, and then another.

## What It Left

It had come to take nothing, and it was careful to leave the plain exactly as it found it. But wisdom and love are not the same as absence, and at the end of the year the power did three small things.

**It left one lamina.** On the last night it set its palm briefly against a wet crest, close enough that the warmth of it lay across the mat for a few hours. Nothing changed but the chemistry of that one night. The mat grew a little faster there, a little greener, and in the morning a sheet of stone slightly thicker than its neighbors began to harden. Someone who cut that wall in ten thousand years would find one page slightly different from the rest, a page that says only *someone was here and was gentle*. It would not be legible as a message. It would be a mark of the same kind as the storm's gray line, a single warm night in the record. That was all the power wanted.

**It answered the pipes once.** At dusk, when the draft came up in the field of dead reeds and their chords began to shift and hum, the power let the wind be very slightly other than it would have been. It did not compose or teach. It nudged one chord, in one pipe, to resolve where it would otherwise have hung. The choir of the dead had been playing for longer than anyone could count, with no one to hear whether the note landed. For one evening, it landed. Then the power let the wind go, and the chords wandered on as they had before.

**It promised to return.** This was the third thing, and the only thing it did that was purely for itself. In the slow future, cell by cell, the basins would fill with their own stone. The mats would die of their own completeness, and the broad pale plateaus would spread from the center outward, cracked and silent, until the last wet basin at the raw edge had risen and dried. Then the plain would be finished. There would be no green, no pops, no chords. The wind would blow across ten thousand square leagues of pale terrace and find nothing to play.

The power resolved that it would be there. It would stand on the last high wall of the last cell when the last of the mat went still, and it would listen to the silence. A thing that has built without witness for so long should have a witness at its end, if not at its beginning, and someone should mark the moment when the labor was complete. That is not intervention. It changes nothing about the plain. It changes only whether the ending is a loss or a completion, and the power thought, on balance, that completion deserves to be attended.

## And Then

It walked back out to the raw margin, where the pale scalloped line meets cracked clay, and stood again with one foot in unbuilt ground. The first film of life was settling there, unhurried, on the plain ground. The power did not touch it.

Then it withdrew its attention the way light withdraws from a room, without a moment when it was gone. The plain went on. The mist lifted, the noon went white, the rain came in slanting curtains, and the pipes hummed with the evening draft.

Somewhere in a wall near the raw edge, one page was slightly thicker than the rest. Nothing on the plain knew why, and nothing needed to. The mats added their sheets, each thinner than paper, toward a finished city they would never see.
