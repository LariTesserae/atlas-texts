# What Was Done at Ammereth Deep

## What Was Set Down

It came first as attention, and attention is a heavy thing to bring into a small place. It looked at the bowl and saw at once everything it could do.

It could stop the lime. A slight change in the chemistry of the seep, a little acid in the warm water, and the town would stay a town for a million years. The doors would hold open, the lanes would stay lanes, and the keepers could put down their scrapers.

It could lift them out, carry the whole living town up through sixty meters of black water, and set it in some gentler basin where the walls did not close.

It could give them light and numbers, and the arithmetic of their own ending, so they might grieve it properly or finally understand what they were doing.

It considered each of these carefully and set each one down.

Stopping the lime would end the negotiation, and the negotiation was the town. A door held open by a power would no longer be a door someone had kept, and the keepers' hands would go to lintels that no longer answered them. The color would still run down the arm, but the news it carried would be *still open, still open, still open* for no reason. It would become a ritual without a wall behind it.

Lifting them out would move the keepers and lose the place. Their sense of touch was made for this particular lime, and their beaks were notched to these doors. Their whole language was a way of being here.

The arithmetic would give them only something to carry that they had never needed. They carried the door in front of them. That was not ignorance. It was a way of knowing that had already outlived every reckoning.

So the power did none of these things. It saw that the town was settling *at exactly the speed at which it was still being loved*, and that the only thing it could add to that was more of the same.

## Arrival

It came down as a change in the water, a slight increase of warmth and stillness that the shrimp noticed first. Their static hushed for a moment, then resumed. The eyeless fish turned a degree on their whiskers, as they turn for the chord.

It had no body to bring, and so it borrowed the smallest one available. It lowered itself into a thumb-sized lamp-creature in a house whose keeper had lately died, and pulsed there at the pace of a slow heart, and waited.

For a season it only listened. It felt the chord through the jelly of its borrowed body: each doorway a pipe, each pipe a voice, the whole thing a long unresolved sentence. It learned which pipes were thinning. It learned the glow's slow day. It learned the taste of a mother's repair under thirty years of crust, because the keepers' fingertips, passing over the lamp to feed it, told it everything a hand can tell.

And it learned what it had not understood from above: that this was not a dying place. Nothing here was waiting to be rescued. Everything here was *tending*, and tending has no need of a savior. It needs company in the work.

## The Unclaimed Lane

At the eastern edge of the green there was a lane whose keeper had died in the warm season two years before. She had left no young. Her doors had gone from four notches to three, and the nearest keepers, already carrying their own lanes and their neighbors' news, had done what they could, which was to touch it in passing. The crust crept in. One door had narrowed to a single notch.

The power took the lane.

It did this in the smallest way it could manage. It did not clear all the doors overnight, though it could have. It went as a keeper goes. It borrowed a body, a pale one with eight arms and a ninth that had never finished growing, made from nothing except the memory of the lane's last keeper as the walls remembered her. Four limbs braced on the wall, and four worked. It took a shell scraper from the dead keeper's house and held it wrong, and the lime came away in coarse flakes instead of fine smoke. It cleared a notch too much on the first door and felt the wrongness of it through all its cups, the lintel widened past what it had ever been.

It stayed with that mistake for a season. It did not repair it, because repair would have been another kind of erasure. It let the lime begin to close the excess, and it felt what it was to be answered by a wall.

## The Elder

The elder who came was very old. Her beak notches were worn nearly smooth, and her ninth arm was a thin curl she carried like a lost thing. She came down the lane in the dimmest hour and stopped at the too-wide door. She laid a limb on the lintel and received the news of the clumsy hand that had made it.

Then she laid the limb across the power's arm.

The color ran, slowly, the pale rose and the leaf-green, shoulder to tip and back. It was not a rebuke. In the language of doors it said something like: *this is the width it was; this is how the scraper turns; here.* Then she took the power's arm, all its borrowed length, and *moved* it. Scraping, resetting, waiting. The pattern of hand-shapes and gentle tones went over and over until the arm began to know it, not as a rule but as a habit.

The power, which had crossed the whole dark of the world to be of use, sat in a lane no wider than a body and was taught by something with a beak of soft horn. It let itself be small enough to be a pupil. It found this was the only way it could have been of any use at all.

When she was done, she did not leave. She stayed beside it for a long while with a limb flat against the wall, in the way that means *I am still here*, and the power, not knowing the answer in any tongue, pressed its own limb to the stone and let the color come up on its own, as it did.

*So am I.*

## What It Did Beyond the Lane

It kept the lane for a keeper's lifetime, which is a short span for it and a very long one for a door, and in that time it did only one thing that the keepers would not have done for themselves.

Under the mountain's worn shoulder, a fault lay in the rock, an old slow fracture. It would not have opened for many centuries, but when it did, it would have opened all at once: the bowl's floor lifting, the paving splitting, sixty meters of water dropping into the dark, and the town not sealed but *broken*, its rooms flung open to no one's hands. The power leaned on that fault, a little, in the right place, once. The stress went out of it in a hundred slow tremors so small the shrimp barely paused.

It did this reluctantly, and did not tell itself it was certain. It had decided that the town's ending should be the town's own, the closing of the last chamber at the pace of lime, not the pace of a fault. If that was a kind of meddling, it was the kind that keeps a story from being ended by someone else. It allowed itself no more, not the lime and not the light.

## The Sealed Houses

There were houses in the green where the last keeper had died and the lime had closed and the lamps still pulsed inside, glowing to no one. The power went to these, as the keepers do. It laid a limb on the smooth new wall and let the color run down.

But it did one thing more. For the lamps inside, it became someone. It matched their pulse and held it until the lime had finished, so the last green ember behind the last eyelid had a witness at the moment it went out. The keepers, passing, felt the wall and did not know why the news it gave them was a little warmer, only that it was.

## After

In a hundred thousand years the last chamber will seal. The chord will thin to a single pipe, and then, for the first time, it will sound one note without change. The power will be there. It has made that promise to no one, in no language, and it will keep it the way the keepers keep the doors.

There will be a smooth pale boulder, sixty meters under black water, with rooms inside. In one of them, perhaps, a lamp will still be pulsing in the dark. The power will lay a limb against the stone, and the color will run down.

Until then it keeps its lane. It clears the doors to the notch and no further. In the dimmest hour, when the glow is faint and the chord holds one long low note, it sits in a doorway with its arms drawn in, doing nothing at all, listening to the pipe of the house it keeps open. It hears the pipe sing. It hears that the singing has something in it now that was not there before, a small extra voice, uncertain and a little flat, that belongs to the town and is not going to be corrected.
