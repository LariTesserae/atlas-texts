# The Visitor Mistaken for a Column

The first thing the power did was fail to arrive.

Its ordinary ways of arriving were all versions of a crack: a parting of cloud, a voice, a change in the pressure of the air. In the Ward of Setting Resin every one of them would have been heard as a wound in the world, and the wrights would have come, palps working, to close it. So the power did what it had never had to do and made itself small, slow, and quiet. It came down over the rim in a way that shifted nothing, and it stood in the powder until the powder forgot it.

It stood in the market-shaped clearing through a dustfall and a heave. Near the end of the first long dusk, a wright came along the ring road, stopped, and spread its palps. The power held still and let its breath go thin. The wright weighed it for a long moment, decided it was one of the pale columns the seep sometimes leaves, and bent its course around it.

The power had been worshipped, feared, argued with, and thanked. It thought that being politely walked around by a blind animal was the most exact honor it had ever been given.

## What it read

It stayed a season and learned the town by touch, as the wrights do.

It read the walls. It read the lizard in mid-scurry and the bird with its beak open, and it felt something like grief, which it did not suppress, because grief was the accurate response. Then it did the harder work of understanding what it was grieving.

The wrights were not cruel. They did not know anyone was there. Every disaster in their history had announced itself as a crack, and they had answered every crack in the only way they knew: they went and filled it. Over centuries the whole basin had reshaped itself around that answer. The lizards learned to walk with curled toes. The moths grew fringed wings. The wind itself seemed to move carefully. What looked like a tragic misunderstanding was also a covenant, written into bodies over generations, and it was the reason anything lived there at all.

The power knew an old rule about rescuers: before you correct a misunderstanding, be sure it is not a load-bearing wall.

## What it declined to do

It did not teach the wrights that a sound is not a wound. It could have, but they would then have had to become a different kind of creature, and the town would have had to become a different kind of town. No one had asked for that.

It did not unseal the tree. The mica leaves had been silenced one at a time over a very long era, and the power could not tell what the silencing meant. It might have been fear, or reverence, or a way of keeping something: a bell held in amber, a name never spoken aloud. It stood among the wrights in the season of the great seep, when dozens gathered around the tree with their palps raised, making no sound. It listened as they did, with its whole body, and it understood that it was in the presence of something it had not been invited to interpret. It left the question open.

It did not pry the small dead out of the walls. They were long past freeing, and being part of the town was the nearest thing to a grave they would ever have.

## What it did

It did three small things. It chose them because they could be done in the basin's own way.

**First, it finished the cries.** Every sealed creature in the walls was holding a sound it never released: the bird's cry, the lizard's scrape, the beetle's click. The resin kept the shape of each one the way a mold keeps a shape. Late in the season, in the deepest hour of the dusk, the power went from wall to wall and gently drew each held sound out of the amber. It carried them over the rim, out onto the open plain where no wright would hear, and let each one finish. There was a bird's cry, cut off mid-note ninety years before, that ended at last in the empty air over the plain, and a scrape that completed its slide. The sounds went out across the flat gray country and were gone. In the basin nothing changed. The bird in the wall still had its beak open, but the power had given it a sentence's end.

**Second, it seeded a cell.** It chose a new dome at the edge of a growing block, a place where the wrights would surely work, and it laid the pale green-white film along the inner wall in long, careful strokes, as a good wright does. It did not use any power to do this. It used its hands, or something like hands, and it took a long time. When it finished, the dome glowed evenly, like the inside of a shell. The power did not expect the light to last forever. It hoped it might last long enough that some wright, carrying a little of it on its palps to a new cell, would pass it on. In that case the light would become part of the town's inheritance, indistinguishable from any other. This was the only kind of gift the power thought the basin could safely accept, one that would not be recognized as a gift.

**Third, it went to the rim.** The tall, long-legged thing had come again to the edge of the basin. It stood there with one foot raised, as it always did, its head turned toward the glowing domes. The power crossed the powder to the rim, and this took most of a day, because it did not want to make a sound. It stopped a few body-lengths from the creature and stood still.

It said nothing. It had no language the creature would have understood, and words would have been noise anyway. It only stood at the edge, a second still thing beside the first, as evidence that the edge could be stood at without harm.

The creature did not look at it. But after a very long time, longer than the power had waited for anything in a long while, the raised foot came down. It did not come down where it had lifted from. It came down a hand's width further in, into the powder, and the powder took it to the ankle and made no sound at all.

The creature stood there a while. Then it turned and went back across the plain, as it always did. It had not entered the town. But it had put a foot down in a new place, and the powder had held it, and the power thought this was probably as much as anyone could be given in one visit. The town measures its progress in finger's widths. A hand's width was more than generous.

## What it left

It left the way it had come, without a crack. By the next morning the dustfall had filled its footprints. In the clearing, one wall stood a finger's width taller than it had the night before. The wrights had built it, or perhaps the power had, and no one, including the power, could have said which.

The basin went on building itself and taking itself apart. A dome glowed evenly at the edge of a new block, and a wright carried a little of its light on its palps toward a cell that had none. Out on the plain, if you had been listening, and no one was, you might have caught the last quiet ends of some old cries, drifting in the cold air and coming apart.

Nothing in the Ward of Setting Resin was fixed or saved or explained. The power had wished for none of that. It had wanted the place to go on being what it was, and to be a little less alone in being it.
