# Preparing for the Long Wet Ledger

## The Right Mindset

The basin is not hostile. It has no predators worth planning around and no hazard that announces itself. The dangers are wetness, slow disorientation, and a place that keeps accounts. Prepare for a long, damp, loud, beautiful walk through a system that is paying close attention to you.

Three principles cover most of what follows:

1. **Bring everything you need, and take out exactly what you brought in.**
2. **Stay tethered to something that keeps ordinary time.**
3. **Distrust your senses gently, and check them against something outside yourself.**

---

## Clothing and Body Care

You will be damp from the moment you arrive. The mist beads on you, the canopy drips on you, and the ground wets you from below. You can't stay dry, so aim to stay warm and abrasion-free while wet.

- **Layers of wool or synthetic fabric.** Avoid cotton entirely. A thin base layer, a warm mid-layer, and a light waterproof shell that vents well. A heavy rain jacket becomes a sauna in the warm squalls.
- **Tall waterproof boots or gaiters over sturdy wading shoes.** Expect ankle-deep suction with every step, and sometimes knee-deep. Boots with good tread and a snug ankle matter more than insulation.
- **Spare socks, many pairs, kept in sealed bags.** Change them at every rest. Trench foot and fungal rot are the most likely injuries here, and they are entirely preventable. Carry antifungal powder and foot balm.
- **Gloves.** You will part the vegetation by hand for hours. Thin, grippy, waterproof gloves protect against sedge-cuts and let you feel the growth.
- **A brimmed hat and a fine head net.** The midge columns are quiet and constant, and they will find your eyes and ears.
- **Eye protection.** Clear glasses or goggles help against dripping leaf-tips and stray twigs in near-total green.

## Moving Through the Basin

- **Two trekking poles, or one long probing staff per person.** Use them to test the ground ahead. The line from firm turf to shivering turf to green skin over dark water is gradual, and the poles tell you where it is before your leg does.
- **A personal flotation device you can wear under a shell.** The peat is meters thick, and a mat can hold you or drop you. A light inflatable vest is enough. The point is to stay up if you break through.
- **Light rope and a short tether.** Rope with a sewn or knotted end is fine to carry, since you will carry it out. Tether members of your party at intervals in the dense central growth. Your senses may disagree about how far apart you are.
- **Do not wade the channels if you can avoid it.** Something long and low moves in them, and they fill without warning in the afternoon squalls.
- **Be on high ground by mid-afternoon.** The rounded stone knuckles are your camps and refuges. When the late squall comes, every channel runs bank-full and brown within minutes. It all drains by morning, but you don't want to be in it.

## Navigation

Assume your usual tools will underperform.

- **Bring a compass, a paper map you have made yourself (or a sketched one), and a pace-counting tool.** A simple hand tally works best.
- **Do not rely on electronics.** Humidity, mist, and a lattice that appears to be doing something will make batteries and signals unreliable. Bring waterproof electronics as backup, not as primary. A wind-up or mechanical watch is useful, but expect it to disagree with you at the center.
- **Trust landmarks that persist.** The stone knuckles, the nurse-log colonnades, and the weeping rocks with their inked lichen stripes are good waypoints. Mist shapes, reflections, and distances are not.
- **Mark your route physically, but sparingly.** Use biodegradable markers you will retrieve on the way out. Anything with an edge or a flat face is an intruder here, and the fen will begin dissolving it within a season. Retrieve every marker.
- **Expect one-way paths.** A trail through the sedges may work in one direction only. If the route back doesn't match the route in, stop, do not force it, and retrace by landmark. Don't argue with the geography.

## Water, Food, and Camp

**Water is the most important item on this list.**

- **Carry all the drinking water you will need for the entire trip, plus a margin.** Do not plan on drinking from the basin.
- The springs carry information, and a filter will not necessarily remove it. You may taste an afternoon you never lived. Drinking from a spring is a choice to receive something, not a survival step. If you do it, do it deliberately, once, at the periphery, with a companion watching. Never do it when you are tired, frightened, or alone.
- **Bring all your food, sealed, and eat only that.** Do not experiment with pitcher-plant water, snail trails, or anything the frogs sit on.
- **A compact stove and dry fuel in waterproof containers.** Open fire is impractical. Heating water and food is a large morale benefit.
- **Camp on the stone knuckles, not the peat.** A hammock or a raised sleeping platform beats a ground tent. You are sleeping in a sponge otherwise. Weeping rocks trickle constantly, so choose a spot away from the grooves.
- **Pack out everything, including your waste.** The ledger balances, and you want the accounts to stay simple.

## Sound, Light, and the Senses

- **Hearing protection that filters rather than blocks.** The frog chorus at dusk is deafening. High-fidelity musician's earplugs let you cut the volume while still hearing your companions and the ambient tone.
- **Be aware of the lattice's chord.** It sits at the edge of hearing on a roughly ninety-second cycle, and it resonates in the sinuses. Some people find it soothing, and some get headaches or nausea. Bring mild pain relievers and anti-nausea medication.
- **Screen for photosensitivity.** The frog-choruses appear as synchronized pulses of cyan light flickering through the reeds. Travelers with photosensitive epilepsy should not go, or should go only with a physician's clearance.
- **Motion-sickness medication.** Early ripples, lagging reflections, and distances that disagree with each other can produce queasiness like slipping gears. Some people acclimate, and some never do.

## The Local Rules, Applied

**Water remembers.** Treat the whole basin as a place where contact leaves a mark. Wash your hands only with what you brought. Avoid submersion.

**Nothing here was made.** Anything with a hard edge or finish you bring in is tolerated, but not welcome. Do not leave any object behind, even a lost button. Inventory everything before you enter and after you leave.

**Time is local.** A day at the rim may be an afternoon, or a week, at the center. This is the rule most likely to hurt you.
- Set your turnaround by your **body** (hunger, thirst, sleep, remaining supplies), not by a clock.
- Never go deeper than you have supplies to return from at double the expected duration.
- Leave a **rim contact** at your entry point, ideally a companion or fixed anchor, with clear instructions on how long to wait and when to attempt a retrieval.
- Do not enter the deep center for a first visit. The rim and middle ring are extraordinary enough.

**The ledger is balanced.** Take nothing. Not a stone, a lichen scrap, a jar of water, or a leaf. If you must take a sample for research, understand that something small and unaccountable may vanish from your life years later. Some travelers have decided that was an acceptable price. Decide in advance, not on the spot.

**Do not follow the rising rain.** When you see a column of droplets lifting out of a pool, mark it, note the bearing, and walk the other way. The path closes behind anyone who follows it to the source, and no traveler is known to have returned with an account of what lies there. However clear and inviting the water looks, and however much you would like to see the city with no walls, don't.

## Psychological Preparation

The greatest hazard is the slow habituation to disagreement. Your senses will give you contradictory reports for days, and after a while they stop feeling contradictory. That is when you're in danger.

- **Establish reality checks.** Every few hours, count your steps over a known distance, compare a companion's estimate with yours, and check your watch against sunrise and sunset in your own body's rhythm.
- **Keep a written log** in pencil on waterproof paper. Record times, distances, meals, and moods. If the log stops making sense, you have gone too far.
- **Travel with at least one other person**, ideally two. Have them tell you if you're behaving strangely, and agree beforehand that you will listen.
- **Do not lean over the black pools.** Reflections in other seasons, or under unfamiliar stars, are compelling. Look from a step back. If you feel a pull toward one, walk away and let a companion take your arm.
- **Mist shapes are not apparitions.** Tall, long-legged silhouettes and ribcage curves are the water rehearsing a memory. Stay calm, keep walking, and let them thin.
- **Rehearse your exit.** Before leaving the rim, decide with your companions what symptoms (persistent disorientation, missing time, unexplained tastes or memories) trigger an immediate withdrawal, no discussion.

## Suggested Kit Summary

| Category | Items |
|---|---|
| Body | Wool/synthetic layers, light shell, tall waterproof boots, gaiters, gloves, brimmed hat, head net, spare socks in sealed bags |
| Movement | Probing poles, flotation vest, tether rope, hand tally |
| Nav | Compass, hand-drawn map, mechanical watch, removable markers |
| Survival | Water for the whole trip, sealed food, stove, raised sleeping platform, first aid |
| Senses | Filtered earplugs, anti-nausea and pain medication, eye protection |
| Record | Pencil, waterproof notebook |
| Protocol | Rim contact, turnaround plan, item inventory, agreed withdrawal signals |

## A Last Word

Do not treat the Long Wet Ledger as a place to conquer or mine. Treat it as a library where the books are alive and slightly aware of you. Go softly, keep to the edges, listen at dusk when the frogs go silent, and leave with the same inventory you came with. If you're lucky, you'll also carry out a taste of an afternoon you never lived, and it will do you no harm.
