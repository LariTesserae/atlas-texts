# Preparing for the Chorus Flats

## What you're walking into

Imagine a cold, wet, sulfur-smelling engine room the size of a country, packed edge to edge with living things and lit only by them. Most of what will trouble you is not any single dangerous animal. It is sustained pressure from sound, damp, crowding, gas, and dimness, and from a thousand small bodies trying to get onto and into you. Prepare for endurance and containment first, and for confrontation hardly at all.

The description you may have read assumes a furred visitor, with moisture beading on a wrist. You will not be furred. Condensation will bead on your clothes and skin, get into your seams, and slowly pull heat out of you. Plan for that difference.

## Sound: your first and most serious problem

The noise is loud enough to damage hearing over hours, and it comes at every pitch, including a bass boom you feel in your ribs.

- **Double protection.** Wear custom-molded or high-rated foam plugs under sealed over-ear defenders. Bring spares, because damp ruins foam.
- **Communication.** Shouting is useless, and as you'll see below it's also unwise. Use noise-cancelling headsets with throat or bone-conduction microphones, like those in aviation or armored-vehicle work. Agree on a hand-signal vocabulary and a dim-light signal code before you go. Rope-tug signals help when you're roped together.
- **Alarms must be haptic or visual.** Every gas monitor, timer, and beacon you carry should buzz against your body or flash. An audible alarm will simply vanish into the chorus.
- **Infrasound and bass.** The booms and the ground's resonance can cause nausea, unease, or dread even through protection. Expect it. Short shifts and regular recovery breaks are the remedy.
- **Don't be a sound.** The stalkers that mimic grazer-calls are answering noise with hunger. Don't shout, sing, whistle, or clap. Muffle your own gear: no jingling carabiners, rattling cookware, or crinkly fabrics. Your protected ears will make you feel quiet when you are not.

## Light and eyes

The flats are dim ash-blue with rolling bioluminescence, and human eyes need a long time to adapt.

- **Wait in the dark on arrival.** Stand still at the portal for at least twenty to thirty minutes before moving. Don't reach for a lamp.
- **Lamps should be dim, filtered, and rare.** A bright white beam will startle herds, draw fliers, and ruin your adaptation. Carry a low-output lamp with a deep red or blue-green filter. Since the seep worms glow red, keep your red dim and low so you don't look like one to anything with a taste for them.
- **Consider a low-light imager or image intensifier.** They work well with bioluminescence, but they bloom when a shoal flashes. Keep an eye free of them.
- **Eye protection is body protection.** Wear sealed goggles or a full-face mask. Fog-fliers strike through the shoals with rasping wings, and spores and droplets are everywhere.

## Air, gas, and the Breath

The seeps carry sulfur and iron, and the periodic exhalation of the ground will release warm gas in bursts. Treat the air as a hazard.

- **Gas monitor.** Carry a personal four-gas monitor at minimum (hydrogen sulfide, carbon dioxide, methane, oxygen). Hydrogen sulfide deadens your sense of smell as its concentration rises, so a smell that fades is not a sign of safety. Set alarm thresholds conservatively.
- **Respirator.** Use a full-face respirator with combined acid-gas/organic vapor cartridges and a P100 particulate filter. It protects against the gas and the spore-laden fog. Bring an emergency escape hood or short-duration air supply for each person.
- **Heavy gas pools low.** Gullies, hollows, and the knee-deep morning fog are where dangerous concentrations settle. Camp, rest, and site your portal on rounded high ground, not in a bowl.
- **Learn the Breath.** Log the surges with your own timekeeping. The clocks of home mean nothing here. When the pools begin to spit and the chorus climbs in pitch, that's your cue to move uphill, seal your face, and wait it out.
- **The rain** falls rarely, is warm, and smells of struck matches. Assume it is mildly acidic and don't let it collect on bare skin.

## Clothing and body protection

- **Layers that stay warm when wet.** Wool or synthetic base layers, a fleece or wool mid-layer, and a fully sealed waterproof shell with taped seams and a hood. Nothing cotton.
- **Seal every gap.** Tape or gasket cuffs, collar, and ankles. Tiny armored grazers and mites will climb any open route, and the crumbled rock hisses with smaller life still. Gaiters over boots, gloves over cuffs, hood over mask.
- **Puncture and chemical resistance.** Crevice-dwellers strike from cracks, so wear thick-soled, puncture-resistant boots, cut-resistant outer gloves over chemical-resistant liners, and knee and shin guards for any kneeling.
- **Expect to be a habitat.** Do a full-body check with a partner at every break. Something will be on you.

## Terrain and footing

- **You cannot avoid stepping on life.** The pale ground moves. Walk slowly, carry a probe or pole, and accept that some crushing is unavoidable. Where you can, keep to bare gray ridges rather than the mat-slicked terraces.
- **Seep pools.** Their orange and yellow waters are warm, mineral-rich, and possibly scalding or acidic. The terraced rims are slick and brittle. Do not wade, do not drink, and keep off the mats.
- **Mud beside the seeps.** Half-buried ambushers lie there, more mouth than body. Give the edges a wide berth and probe before you commit weight.
- **Walkers.** The heavy plated herds move slowly but plow through everything. Read their wake and boom, and get to high ground well off their line. They aren't hunting you, but they won't notice you either.

## Navigation

The far side is a rumor. There are no plants, no sun, no stars, and the fog and chorus hide everything.

- **Mark your portal** with a beacon that works without sound, and place it on the highest, driest ground you can find.
- **Prefer roped or line-guided travel.** Run a guideline out from the portal, or leave waymarkers that don't depend on being heard. Expect grazers to cover or eat small markers, and check them.
- **Bring dead-reckoning tools** (a compass if the world supports one, a pace count, an inertial unit). Assume any one of them may fail.
- **Keep excursions short and radial.** Go out and return along the same route.

## Camp, sleep, and stores

- **Sleep will be poor.** Use a sealed, floored shelter on a raised platform above the damp, with a gasket-tight door and its own filtered air if you can manage it. Expect vibration and a fine residue of sound even through walls.
- **Everything corrodes and condenses.** Sulfur attacks silver and copper. Use sealed cases and desiccant, and coat or gold-plate critical electronic contacts. Lenses will fog constantly.
- **Bring all your water and food, sealed.** Don't eat or drink anything local. Smells attract, and crumbs will draw a crowd. Pack out all waste.
- **Sterilize on entry and exit.** Wash down boots, gear, and skin with disinfectant. Neither you nor anything on you should cross the portal uninspected in either direction. A hitchhiking spore or mite is a planetary-scale problem at home.

## Medical

- **Vaccinations and prophylaxis.** No one can say what lives in the mats and the fog. Bring broad-spectrum antibiotics, antifungals, antihistamines, epinephrine, and irrigation supplies for eyes and wounds.
- **Wound care.** Anything that breaks skin gets cleaned at once. This place is full of organisms that have never met a human immune system.
- **Buddy system, always.** You cannot call for help over the noise.

## The Hush

This is the one event that requires rehearsal.

When the Hush starts, the singers cut out in a spreading wave, the herds freeze, and the glow dims. If you see the light going out and the animals stilling, stop moving. Stop breathing loudly. Douse your lamp. Let a partner know with a hand signal, and hold still until the sound comes back.

- **Your gear is now loud.** In that silence your footsteps, zippers, and respirator valves will carry. Rehearse being motionless. Consider a valve-muffled respirator or shallow, controlled breathing.
- **You may not hear it start.** Ear protection will dull the drop in sound, so rely on your eyes. Watch the glow, and have one partner's whole job be to watch for the ripple of silence.
- **Listen, if you dare.** Once things have stilled, lifting one ear cup slightly lets you hear the ground's low resonance. Many will feel the dread of it is reason enough to keep them on. Either is reasonable. Decide in advance.
- **Don't be the first sound.** When it ends, a single beetle scrapes and the rest comes rushing back. Wait for that before you move.

Nobody knows what the Hush is for. Whether it's a census or a fear, don't test the hypothesis that something is listening.

## The mind

You will be within a hand's breadth of living things at every moment, in a crowd you can't see well and can barely hear. Some people find it wondrous, some claustrophobic, and some simply can't bear it.

- Do a short trial exposure before any long stay.
- Keep shifts short and rotate to a quiet, sealed rest space.
- Agree in advance on a "turn back" word or gesture that anyone can use without justification.
- Give yourself time to sit and take it in. The place rewards attention. The ripples of light across the herds, the terraced pools, and the wall of tone are extraordinary, and you'll see far more if you aren't rushing.

## Ethics

The rock beneath you is made of the ancestors of the things around you. You are a stranger on a graveyard that is also a nursery. Take nothing (samples need permission), leave nothing, and remember that every step lands on someone's home.

## Quick checklist

- Double hearing protection, ANR headsets, haptic alarms
- Red/blue-green dim lamp, low-light imager, sealed goggles or full-face mask
- Four-gas monitor, full-face respirator with acid-gas and P100, escape hoods
- Layered, sealed waterproof clothing; puncture-resistant boots; gaiters; taped cuffs
- Probe pole, guideline and rope, silent waymarkers, dead-reckoning gear
- Raised, sealed shelter, desiccant and sealed cases, all water and food
- Sterilization kit, wound-care and medical kit
- A rehearsed plan for gas surges, walkers, and the Hush
- A partner watching your back, and a way home you can find in the dark
