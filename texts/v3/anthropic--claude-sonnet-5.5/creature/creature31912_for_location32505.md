# Those Who Are Kept in the Basin

"Lives" is a loose word for this place. It is the nearest word the language offers, and the basin tolerates it the way a patient host tolerates a guest's mispronunciation. Nothing here is born, and nothing eats or dies. But there are presences, and they are arranged in something like a society, though a very slow, very quiet one that has had one afternoon to work with.

## The Canopy

The largest population is the plants, and they are the least like plants. Call them the **assenters**.

The metallic-film leaves are the basin's speakers. When two of them touch, the tick is not an accident of wind, since there is no wind. It is a finished utterance, one word of a language with a vocabulary of perhaps one word. What it means depends on where it falls, which leaf touches which, and what stands in the silence around it. A tick on the east side of a stand, answered two seconds later by a tick on the west, is a sentence. A tick that is not answered is also a sentence, perhaps the same one.

The assenters have manners. This is what you notice after long hours in the green dusk. The ribbon-leaves tilt aside to leave a neighbor its room to open. Vines climb in helices of identical pitch, each taking exactly the turn the last one left. There is no competition in the canopy, because competition needs a future in which someone might win. What exists in its place is a courtesy so complete it looks like architecture. Each plant is a clause in an agreement that has already been signed, and its whole life is being consistent with the others.

They do not sleep, because they do not wake. They do not turn toward you as you pass, but the leaves you brush will tick after you have gone by, once, softly, and you cannot decide whether it is a greeting or an entry in the record.

## The Understudies

Every stand of columns holds twenty-three, and never the same twenty-three. So there must be more than twenty-three, and the surplus must be somewhere.

The old travelers' term for the surplus is **the understudies**, and no one has ever seen one because they are never on stage when you look. There are perhaps hundreds for each stand, perhaps thousands. Each takes its turn being present, standing in the ranks with its tiered plate-leaves and its honeycomb heart, and then is withdrawn without a sound, replaced by another whose place in the arrangement is identical to the last. The total holds. The membership rotates. It is a kind of civic duty, a shared burden of being counted.

Where do they wait? The only honest answer is in the glass. The reflections in the fracture faces show plants slightly further along, a leaf more unfurled, a vine one turn higher, and it may be that these are not the future at all but the waiting room. The understudies stand in the reflected basin, growing a little in the dark of the mirror, and when their turn comes they step into the world at whatever stage the world requires, and the growth they did is folded away. Perhaps that is what it costs them. Perhaps it is what they are paid.

If this is true, then the reflected basin is the only place where anything ever gets closer to a next moment, and it is populated entirely by the ones not currently needed.

## The Walkers

Everyone who writes about the basin mentions the walkers, and everyone gets them slightly wrong, because a walker is hard to hold in one description. Herons in size, mantises in joint, copper in the body with a green sheen laid over it like breath on a mirror. They move through the deep canopy on the fine pale powder, and the powder does not take their prints. They lift a foot, and where it was there is only smoothness, as if the ground had been consulted and had declined to remember.

Count a walker's legs and you may get six. Count again and you may get eight. It is the same trick the columns play, and it is the walkers' first kinship with the ledger. They are counted in the same way as everything else, present in total and unreliable in membership.

What they do is listen. A walker stops in front of a stand of leaves and cants its head, and the angle is exact and never varies, the angle of someone reading a column of figures at arm's length. Then it extends one foreleg, a filament-thin tip, and touches a leaf. The leaf ticks. The walker holds still for what may be a minute or an hour. Then it goes on to the next leaf.

The proofreader theory arises here, and it is a good theory. The walkers check the work. They touch each leaf in turn and listen to the answering tick to be certain it says what it said before. But watch long enough and you see a problem. They never correct anything. Nothing is ever wrong. The touching, the ticking, the moving on: it is a proofreading of a text that cannot contain an error, performed by a reader that cannot find one, and both are perfectly content.

The other theory, held by the few who have stayed, is that the walkers are not checking the ledger. They are *reading it aloud*. The ticks are the words, and the walker is the voice, and the basin is a long text being recited to no one, on a loop of a single unending afternoon, the way a monk might say the same psalm until the saying became the stone he knelt on.

They do not see you. You are not in the text. But if you stay long enough, and sit long enough, there may be a day when a walker stops in front of you, cants its head to the exact angle, and extends its foreleg toward your shoulder. You will feel nothing. Nothing will tick. But it will hold there, listening, for as long as it needs, and when it walks on you will feel, faintly, like an entry that has been checked.

## The Grains

The powder itself is inhabited, though this is the hardest claim to make and the easiest to feel. Each faceted lozenge is identical to its neighbors, and each threads onto the circuit-filaments that run through the basin like nerves through a limb. They do not move. They do not need to. They are the ink, and the ink is aware of being ink, in the way that a word might be aware of the sentence.

Sit still with your palm on the powder and the prickling on your skin will change. It doesn't strengthen, but it grows *particular*. Something is registering the shape of your hand: not the heat of it, but the fact of it, an object of a certain size resting on the surface. The registration is polite and total and entirely without curiosity. You are being logged.

The grains are the lowest order of the basin's population and the most numerous, and they are the reason nothing here is ever lost. Whatever passes over the powder is recorded in the powder, and the powder does not blow away.

## The Hum

Under everything, in the sternum, is the hum, and it stops when you try to place it. Travelers have proposed that it is a machine, a resonance of the glass, a distant voice. All of these are wrong in the same way. The hum is the *total*. It is the sum of the ledger, being added, continuously and forever, by the whole basin at once, and it stops when you attend to it because your attention is an entry, and an entry interrupts the addition. The moment you notice, the total must be recomputed, and the recomputation is silent.

If it is a being, it is the one that lives at the bottom of the arithmetic, and it has never once been idle. It has never once finished, either. It is what you feel when you feel that a place is keeping track of you.

## The Traveler in the Gold

High in the pale sky, sometimes, a small dark shape moves in a perfectly straight line and never reaches an edge. No one has named it. Those who have watched it longest agree on two facts. It is the only thing in the basin that has *velocity*, and it never changes. And it takes exactly as long to cross the sky as you are willing to watch.

It may be a courier carrying a message to a destination that has been removed from the map. It may be an auditor, forever approaching the books it will never open. It may be the last piece of the world outside, a mote of elapsed time that got in and cannot turn around. Whatever it is, it is the basin's single acknowledgment that somewhere, perhaps, a next moment exists, and something has been sent toward it, and has been going ever since.

## The Ones Who Stayed

A handful of people live here, if the word can bear that weight. They did not come to stay. They stopped counting, and after that the rest was easy.

They sit in the deep green, and they are hard to find because they hold so still and because the fronds have arranged themselves around them. That is the first sign. A frond that would have overlapped their shoulder tilts back to leave a shoulder's width. A vine that would have crossed their knees adjusts its pitch by a degree. Within a season, or whatever passes for one, the canopy has made a space for them that is exactly the shape of their sitting, and the space has become part of the pattern. They have been *tiled in*.

They do not hunger. They speak rarely, and when they do they speak of the same things: that the discrepancy in the count is not a lie but an intimacy, that the basin is not hiding anything, that the understudies are grateful. One of them, when asked whether she was afraid of being replaced, laughed once and said that she had been replaced many times already and each replacement had been her. She said that in the basin, the question of who is present is the question of who is *needed*, and that she had never in her life been so completely needed.

Their shadows all fall the same way. Their hair does not grow. They are not dead, and they are not alive in the way that changes, and the walkers pass them with the same careful tread they give the columns. It is not clear that they will ever leave. It is not clear that leaving is something a thing in the ledger can do. But they are the only inhabitants who *chose* the afternoon, and their faces have the look of people who have been told the one thing they always wanted to know and found it smaller and kinder than they feared.

## The Attentiveness

Last, and hardest to name: there is a presence in the basin that is not any of these. No one has seen it. It has no body, no station, no ticking voice. It is what you feel when the count is off by one and you are not frightened. It is what makes the discrepancy *intimate*, like being caught in a lie by something that loves you.

It is the keeper of the ledger, or it is the ledger itself, or it is the afternoon. It attends to the assenters, and the understudies, and the walkers, and the grains, and the sitters, and the shape in the gold. It attends to you. It has attended to all of them for the whole of the time there has been no time, and its attention has the quality of a hand resting on a page to keep it from turning.

## At the Rim

At the edge of the basin, where the green thins to mats and then powder and then bare glass, you may see one last inhabitant. A walker stands at the line where the powder gives out, one foreleg lifted, poised above the raw sharp ordinary ground beyond. Its head is at the exact angle. It has been there as long as anyone has looked, and the lifted foot has been lifted longer than anything has ever been anywhere.

It does not cross. It may be unable to. It may be listening to the last tick of the last leaf before the silence of the world outside, deciding whether the ledger extends that far. Or it may be the simplest thing in the basin, a creature that has been given a direction and stopped one step short of the place where direction would begin to mean something.

Behind it the basin shines in its light from nowhere. Ahead of it your shadow has just begun to lengthen. The walker is between the two, holding its one foot in the air, keeping the afternoon complete for as long as anyone is watching.
