# The One Who Attends

The vug has one inhabitant, and it is easy to walk past. It is a single frost-flower, low against the wall at the darkest end, about the span of a hand. It is pale and dry and more delicate than the rest. If you were told it was a plant, you would believe it. If you were told it was a person, you might believe that too, for a moment, before you found the reason for the belief.

## What It Is Made Of

Its body is the same crystal that condenses from the stone everywhere else, but it is laid down differently. The other flowers grow in fans and ferns, and the fans are all alike, the way waves are alike. This one has *edits* in it. Its tines are branched in a pattern that repeats and then, at a scale too fine to see, does not quite repeat. Under a lens the pattern goes on past where a lens should fail. It is the same regularity as the Lattice, in a different medium, still incomplete.

It seems to be learning the Lattice, or being written by it, or copying it. Each year the pattern gets a little further along. Nobody has watched long enough to tell how far it has to go, and the visitors who sat with it for weeks say only that the flower is *not idle*.

## What It Does

It faces the Lattice and does nothing else.

It does not migrate with the other frost. Where every other flower lifts at the brightest hour and settles a hand's width off, this one stays put, and the shimmer of its rising bends around it like water around a post. The small tick of frost letting go of stone stops within an arm's length of it. So does the dry sliding of the other flowers. Near it the floor is very quiet, and the hum from the Lattice, which is never the same note twice, comes into that quiet and lands on the flower's own pitch. The note is different every time, and every time it is exactly the flower's.

Visitors who sit beside it describe the same pressure, though they put it in different words. It is the feeling of standing in a doorway and being waited for by someone who has decided not to look up. Nobody reports being watched. What they report is that some part of the room has been *held in mind*, and that they are inside it.

## Why the Room Lets You Be Careless

The vug's distances depend on attention. Every measurement fails, and every visitor who tried to measure the room was punished by its length. Only carelessness gets you across.

That is possible because the flower is not careless. Someone or something in the room has to be looking, or the fold would slacken and the crack would go to seamless stone. The flower looks. It looks with a patience that has no clear beginning, at the last working part of a stopped machine. Everything else here is free to be worn and settled, to age and be forgotten, because one thing is not.

This is also why the flower is nearly unfindable. Go looking for it with your attention and the far wall keeps its forty paces. Walk with your eyes on the floor and your mind elsewhere, and you will arrive at the dark end and find it already in front of you, as if it had been there before you were. The one thing in the vug that attends can be reached only by not attending.

## What It Is Not

It is not the builder. The Stair is too worn and the Plinth too indifferent for the flower to be the hand that made them. It does not seem to know it is a flower. It shows no sign of wanting anything except the Lattice, and even *wanting* may be too much of a word.

It is not old the way the room is old. The vug insists that everything in it be caught up to the age it should have. A steel button pits and blooms, a stone in the Plinth's dish is returned, and the Stair wears to curves. The flower is the one exception, and it may be why the vug's clock runs at all. It is the only thing in the room that is growing at the pace of the present. Its slow additions are how the stopped machine knows time is passing. If the flower were caught up and weathered to the settled shape of the rest, the room might finally agree with itself, and hold still, and be finished.

## What People Think It Was

The visitors, writing in scripts that do not resemble one another, offer three stories.

The first is that it is the machine's last apprentice, a thing grown on purpose to take over the watch when the hands gave out.

The second is that it is the *stopped* part of someone, an operator who stayed too long and was caught up to the wrong age, and who is now doing the only thing left to do, which is to look.

The third, told only by those who stayed longest, is that it is not the one who attends but the one who is attended. The Lattice is what is looking, and the flower is what it has been looking at, for so long that the flower has learned to face back.

Those who stayed longest add one more thing, which is why the crack seems smaller when they finally turn to leave. They say the flower's fans are slightly further along than when they arrived. They say it is nearer to finishing than anyone has cared to guess. And they say that no one, not even the flower, seems to know what happens to the room, or to the ones inside it, when the pattern is done.
