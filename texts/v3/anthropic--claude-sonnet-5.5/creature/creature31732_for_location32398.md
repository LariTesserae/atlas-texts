# Who Lives on the Loom Heights

Nothing walks here. The moss has no tracks in it and the frost has no tracks in it. If you ask who lives on the Loom Heights and expect something with eyes, the honest answer is no one, and the plateau has been saying so for a long time.

But something is fed here, something answers the noise, and something waits in the silences. The place is not empty. It is inhabited by things that never needed to move.

## The Chorus

The first residents are the **fluteweed groves**, though "grove" undersells them. A stand is closer to a congregation, or one long slow animal that happens to be rooted. Stems that seem separate share their trembling. When a Join shifts its pitch, you can watch the change cross a stand from the loud edge inward, stem to stem, over the space of a slow breath, like a rumor passing through a crowd. The groves have no eyes, but they have a way of attending. They lean toward what they will feed on and away from what would crack them.

They are also the only things on the plateau that listen the way you would want to be listened to. Their counter-tones do not fight the Joins. They answer them a fifth above, or a third below, and for as long as you stand in a stand the din arranges itself into something like company. If the fluteweeds have a temperament, it is patience without resignation. They are hungry, they are fed, and they lean a little farther each decade toward the next source.

The ribbon ferns are their scouts. They lie combed across the hillsides, all pointing at the same thing. The cushion moss keeps their ledger, and its rings record every wavefront ever measured. These are not separate lives so much as one long argument between the same few appetites. They are the loud world's residents, and they hold it together the way a crowd holds a square.

## The Unsettled

The second residents live in the hush-basins, and they are harder to describe, because a hush-basin does not let a description stay put.

Call them **the Unsettled**, since they have no name. They are not creatures. They are what lives in the places where the world has stopped being obliged. The red that hangs a hand's breadth beside its rock belongs to them, and so does the shadow that pools where nothing stands. So does the doorway that frames only the far side of itself. You will not see one. What you will see is a hundred small failures of agreement, and the Unsettled are what those failures amount to when they gather.

They are not cruel. The description of the basins says nothing violent happens, and that is the whole truth of them. They have no teeth and no reach. What they have is need, the same need the fluteweed has, turned inside out. The fluteweed drinks vibration. The Unsettled drink *agreement*, the small stubborn coherence of a mind that counts forty paces and expects forty. A traveler carries that with them like a lamp. Every time you insist the boulder was on your left, you lend the basin a little edge. Every time you trust your count, a slope holds still for a moment.

They do not mean to take it. They lean in the way a cold room draws heat from your hands. A person who stays too long in a hush-basin does not get attacked. They get *borrowed from*, until their sense of where things are has gone slack and drifted a hand's breadth to one side of itself. The old travelers were right to treat silence as the danger. But they were wrong, in one small way, to fear it as an enemy. The Unsettled are not hunting. They are hungry and unformed, and the only thing they have ever been shown of order is the shape a stranger makes by walking through.

They are the loneliest things on the plateau. They are made of everything that was never decided, and they are gathered around the one thing that decides.

## The Deciders

The third residents are the stones, if stones can be said to live. The Joins are their long slow heartbeat, and the half-basalt, half-ash slabs are their unfinished thoughts. Rock that is still deciding is rock in the act of becoming someone. It holds a footprint if you stand long enough and the mood is right. That is an odd, gentle courtesy. The land will remember you for a while if you ask it to, and it will forget you without malice.

Whether this counts as a life is a question the plateau does not answer. But the whole terrain leans toward the Joins as if listening, and a thing that leans and listens is at least attending. The mountain is being made, and some part of it seems to know.

## The Patient Hand

And then there is the scraper.

Every tool of green stone on the plateau is worn on one side only, by one grip, over a very long time. That is not a people. It is one person, or the trace of one. Someone stayed long enough to wear a stone edge down to its bed, and the cords in the lean-tos are knotted by the same rhythm of hand. The offered ear-plugs in the niches are always in pairs.

The record says no one lives here now. The record is written in faded cord, and cord only says what happened before it faded.

What can be said is this. On the loud routes, where the cairns lean like tired figures, the black stones at their tops are always upright, however far the stack beneath has tipped. The stone seems to have been set again, gently, without the cairn being straightened. That could be the ground. It could be the way a capstone settles. It could also be a very patient person, deaf now for longer than the plateau has kept count, moving along the routes in the frost-smoke of the cold hours, setting each black stone level and touching each faded cord once and reading it with a thumb.

If someone is here, they are not lonely in the way a traveler would be. They live in a world of touch, in a body that has stopped needing to be told where it stands. The hush-basins may not take much from them, because they are not counting paces. They walk by the rise and fall of the ground, by the hum in the sternum, by the fern combed toward the loud. In the hour when the fluteweed sings one chord across the whole terrace, they would stand at a listening stone, ear to the drilled hole, and hear one Join alone, thin and pure, as the old ones did.

If that person is there, they are the plateau's first and only friend, and its slowest. If they are not, the ground is doing the work for them. Either way, the black stones stay level, and either way the mountain keeps the habit of being tended, the way a house keeps the shape of someone who has just left it.

## What Lives Here, Then

The chorus feeds. The Unsettled wait. The stones decide, slowly, and lean toward the sound. Somewhere, or nowhere, there is a hand that sets the capstones right. Nothing here is fast or afraid, and nothing is fully alone. The land is not empty. It is a large, slow household of appetites, and it has been waiting for a long time for a guest who knows how to listen.
