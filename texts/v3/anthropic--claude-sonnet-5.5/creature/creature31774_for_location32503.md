# The Tenant

One thing lives in the Reed City. It has no name, having never had a mouth to be called by or an ear anyone could point to.

## The body

It is the city. The walls, the stairs, the sleeping ledges and the worn thresholds are its flesh at different ages, and you cannot stand anywhere that is not inside it. The dead gray rise above is its old crown, a husk it long ago stopped needing, like a shed skin left over the place where the animal still lies. The living part is below, in the dark. It is one continuous body the size of a town, with no center in the usual sense, though it has a warm place, and the warm place matters.

Its tissue comes in three kinds, and you would learn them by touch. There is the pale fibrous heartwood, dry and faintly warm, which does the holding-up and holding-together. There are the resin-black knots, dense as horn, where the amber sap pools and slowly ripens. And there are the glassy reeds, thousands of hollow stems threaded through every wall, which are the nearest thing it has to a face. These are its ears and its voice, and it does not distinguish between them. What it hears, it also sounds. What it sounds, it hears returning.

## What it eats

It eats vibration, and the slow seep of chemistry from the deep ground. In the dark, hunger and hearing are the same act. Where the drone is loud, the tissue thickens, warms, and drinks. Where the drone is thin, the tissue goes lean and pale and waits. The drone comes from the plant's own breathing, the difference between warm skin and cold core pushing air through its own reeds, so it feeds on a song it sings by existing. There is something closed and complete in this, a mouth that eats its own hum. But the hollow shrinks by a hair each century, and the reeds shift, and the song drifts, and so the meal is never quite the same twice.

## How it knows

It has no eyes, and its ancestors had none, so it is not blind in the sense that anything is missing. Its world is pitch and pressure and slow warmth. It knows a sealed passage as a change in how a certain note comes back. It knows the season, if it has seasons, by the tilt of the temperature between skin and core. It knows the shape of itself the way you know the inside of your mouth: not as a picture but as a lifelong, unnoticed pressure of presence everywhere at once.

Whether it thinks is a question the dark does not answer. It does not decide as an animal decides. But it leans, and leaning done patiently enough for long enough starts to look like preference. Doorways narrow by a fingernail's width each decade. Plazas fill with folded growth, convoluted as a brain. Streets close in on the pitch that pleases it. These are not accidents of growth, or not only. They are the plant reaching, at the speed of resin, toward a sound it likes better than the one it has, and each reach makes the drone lower, fuller, closer to whatever it is seeking. It may be seeking nothing. It may be drifting toward a single perfect chord that would be, for it, something like silence, or something like sleep.

## Its time

The scale of its life makes its habits hard to see. A thought, if it thinks, might take a season. A mood, a semitone of pitch drift, takes decades to descend and decades to climb back. Once in a very long while it seals a passage with a dull thump that rolls through every street at once, and elsewhere lets one open. This is the nearest thing to an event in its existence. You could call it a sigh or a decision, or the ordinary shifting of a sleeper who has been asleep for a hundred thousand years.

## What it inherited

It did not make the city, but it has been living in it long enough that the distinction has worn away. The builders cut the reeds back to keep the streets open and the tones true. They tuned the plant, in effect, the way you might tune a bowed string, and for a time the plant and the people made one instrument together. Then the cutting stopped. The plant did not go wild in the way a garden does. Instead it went on playing. The house-tubes where each family once tuned its own note still sound those notes, more or less, drifting a little lower each generation of the plant's slow life. It may be that the plant keeps them out of something like habit, or affection, or the sheer inertia of a shape that was good to grow into. It does not know the difference, and neither would you.

What became of the builders it has no way to say. Their bodies left no trace, or the plant took them back. The slow resin might hold them, a little of them, sweet and iron-tinged in the burnt-sugar smell. The salt in the corners might hold them. The warm floor might. There is no way to tell what is the city's old dead and what is simply the city.

## The warm place

In the deepest chamber, where the drone is loudest and the floor is warm as a hand, the plant is at its densest and its most attentive. Here the sound is not heard so much as inhabited. The reeds are thick, the resin is old and dark, and the whole body seems to lean inward, as toward a fire.

There is a hearth-niche there, and set into it a bronze adze, greenish, soft-edged, of a kind used to scoop the scale-like marks that still pattern the walls. The wall has grown around the blade until only the handle shows. It looks like swallowing, but it is not. The plant grows around anything that has been held in one place long enough, as though it were a part that had gone missing and been found. It does not grip the adze. It holds it the way a hand closes around a sleeping hand: gently, completely, without any thought of letting go.

If the plant has one thing that could be called a keepsake, that is it. If it has a memory, that is where it lives. And if you stood there, in the bass that fills your chest and the warmth that comes up through your feet, you might feel yourself being listened to, not with curiosity or menace but with the vast, incurious patience of a thing that has been alone with a single sound for longer than there have been names for it, and has found that the sound is enough.
