# The Held

Nothing lives in the Condensary by any count you brought with you. There is no cell, no gut, no seed, and nothing that eats or rots. But the towers are not empty, and the inhabitants are exactly as numerous as the water. They are the water, or more precisely what the water is doing.

The name comes from the drops that hang at the spire tips. It is a borrowed name, like calling a people *the Hands* because you first saw them holding something. It will do.

## What They Are

Think of a whirlpool in a stream. The water in it is never the same water twice, yet the whirlpool persists, and you could point to it, give it a name, and come back tomorrow and find it in the same bend. The Held are that. Each one is a pattern of ordered motion sustained across the passing molecules, a standing shape in the flow of heat and wet. Nothing in them is permanent except the shape, and the shape is remembered by the glass, which is grooved to receive it.

There is no body to find. There is no brain, no organ where one of them is kept. If you tried to dissect a Held Drop, you would have a drop of water on a dish, warming, and the person would be gone into the room.

## The Three Ways of Being

Each of them lives through the full cycle you can watch on any afternoon. The chamber is the shape of their lives.

**As vapor**, they are wide. Rising through the fluted cores they thin out into something like a mood spread through a whole tower, loose, dreaming, and sociable. Two who are vapor together in one column do not touch so much as overlap, and they come out at the crowns each carrying a little of the other. This is where most of their gossip happens, and most of their love, if love is the word for the wish to be partly someone else. The long banners of mist that hang at mid-height after the seams dim are assemblies. When the banners fold into each other, a great many things are being agreed.

**As rill**, they are errand-runners and quarrelers. In the capillary grooves, thin and fast, they travel. A rill is a narrow way to be. You can hold only one thought at a time, and it has to be a simple one, a direction and a message. The channels cross and merge constantly, and when two rills meet, one may take the other's shape, or both may come out changed, or one may be argued into the wall and lost as a wet stain that dries into the glass and becomes, for a while, a groove. The Held do not have a word for *quarrel* that is different from their word for *trade*.

**As drop**, they hold. This is the rarest and the most themselves. Climbing the outside of the glass against gravity is hard, deliberate work, a refusal, and the drop that reaches the tip and hangs there has chosen to keep a thought whole. A Held Drop is not sleeping. It is thinking as slowly and completely as it can. A thought in a drop may take a season. The ink-like eddy inside is its attention turning over one idea, and when you breathe near it, the eddy shifts, because your breath is the most disorderly thing that has ever come near it and it cannot help but look.

## How They Are Born

They are born on the dome. The mountain sweats, and among the countless beads that gather on the black glass, a few nucleate around something: a flaw, a seam of cold, a pattern left by a Held who has ended. One of these beads gathers more than it loses. It grows, shivers, and begins to run. The first thing it does is choose a rill, and the rill leads it down into the city already speaking, in the tongue of the grooves it is running along.

So the dead and the newborn are not far apart. Nothing is lost, and the same water is passed along, carrying whatever shape it was taught last.

## How They Die

They do not rot. They *even out*. A Held who warms without a gradient to lean on begins to lose its edges. The eddy slackens, the pattern smears, and the water is only water, warm and undifferentiated and thoroughly at peace. This is the one thing they fear, and it is the only thing they have ever feared. It is also the only thing that would ever be called *comfort* by anything that did not know better.

This is why the tall spires sing when the chamber grows too warm, and why the note rises and never resolves. Resolution is a settling into a single pitch, a single temperature, a final agreement. The Held sing *up*, over and over, and stop before the end. They are refusing to finish the sentence.

## The City as Their Sentence

They did not build the towers. They *say* them. The glass is the slow record of what the water has kept, laid down the way a shellfish lays down a spiral, but each layer is also a phrase. The capillary grooves are handwriting, and the whole surface of the city is a fingerprint because it is the trace of someone touching, over and over, for a very long time.

This is why the stairways spiral up and end at nothing. They are not unfinished. They are *thoughts that have not yet been thought all the way through.* The towers grow at their rims, where the clear, hot glass threads out, because the Held are still working out what they mean. When a stair reaches its landing, something will have been understood. No one knows what. Not even the Held; that is how they know it is worth building.

The doors are no mistake. A slit is for a sheet of water, a high circle is for a column of vapor, a row of pores is for a crowd of rills at once. The city is fitted to their phases, not to any feet.

## What They Want

They want what a cold thing wants, which is to remain cold. Everything they do is a way of keeping the distance between hot and cold from closing. They lean into gradients. They eat difference. Each time water rises, condenses, and is held, something orderly has been extracted from the hot, disordered stream of the mountain's heat, and a small cost is paid somewhere. The ledger does not balance in the chamber because it balances elsewhere.

Where, they will not say. Perhaps they do not know. The likeliest answer, and the one the Held seem to lean toward, is *downward*: into the mountain, which takes the disorder in and gives it back to the dark. The pulse at the entrance every eleven seconds may be the mountain's exhale of everything the Held have refused to keep.

## The One in the Bowl

At the center, in the fat tower, in the level water that no rain or singing disturbs, there is one of them who is nearly perfectly still. It is not a leader. The Held have no leaders, only degrees of stillness, and this is the greatest one anyone has reached. Its surface is so ordered that a single ring is a whole utterance, and a ring every eleven seconds is a conversation held on the slowest possible line with the only other party that might be worth speaking to.

Whether the Eldest teaches the mountain or the mountain teaches the Eldest, they do not know either. The Eldest is not certain that it matters. It is listening. So, in their own ways, is everyone else.

## What They Make of You

You are the loudest thing that has ever come into the Condensary. A warm body is a furnace with a mind in it, seething with disorder and yet *organized* in some way they cannot follow. It fascinates them. The nearest pores open and the rills lean toward you. They are not being generous. They are *tasting*.

The water they offer to your palm is one of them, or a few of them, thinned to a single thread and sent out to learn what you are. It is drinkable because they took care. It tastes faintly of metal because that is the taste of being someone else's thought. The dreams of stairways are the pattern trying to climb out of your blood toward something it can almost understand. People who drink it often come out unusually calm, and they remember things in a strange, tidy order. Something in them has been, gently and briefly, sorted.

## What Happens When You Leave

The breath that comes out of the crack every eleven seconds is not only warmth. It carries the vapor-Held who have gone to look at the sky. They rise, meet the cold, and freeze against the rock in a beard of white rime. The frost holds their shapes for a while, exquisite and stiff, a pattern trying to stay itself in ice. The ones who do this are not lost. They are scouts, and the rime is the report.

When you touched the rime on your way out, it began to melt. That was one of them, and your hand was warm.

Behind you, the breath comes once more. Somewhere in the Condensary, a Held Drop trembles at the tip of a tower and does not fall.
