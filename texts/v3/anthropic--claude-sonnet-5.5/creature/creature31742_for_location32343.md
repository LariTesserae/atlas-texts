# The Residents of the Foundry

## The Hands

The first inhabitants you notice are the plants, and it is natural to call them the residents. There are perhaps forty of them, spaced far apart on the glassy floor, and each has a manner as distinct as a craftsman's.

**The Pressers** are the tall ribbed stalks with swollen bases, the color of old copper. Each has one job and one stroke. The base fills slowly with a pale silver brine, holds it, and contracts with a clean, decisive motion that presses a single ingot out into the rock. Then it rests. A Presser seems to take pride in the rest. It stands with its joints locked and its leaves angled slightly away, like a man who has set down a heavy thing and is not yet ready to look at it. Some have worked in the same crack so long that the rock has worn to the exact shape of an ingot, a socket ground by repetition. The oldest are nearly hollow with use and still swell, on schedule, with nothing in them.

**The Stampers** are the fern-like ones, blue-green and bronze. They do not grow leaves so much as issue them. A frond unrolls under tension, flat as a sheet from a rolling mill, and at the moment it reaches full extension it stops growing and starts being finished. The veins harden into rivets. The margin takes a perfect edge. If you watch closely, the Stamper makes a small correction with its whole body, a lean of a few degrees, as if checking the sheet against something you can't see. Stampers are the most anxious of the residents. When a frond comes out wrong, and one occasionally does, the plant drops it at once and begins again without any visible reaction, and the flawed sheet is the only thing in the chamber the rock reabsorbs within hours.

**The Turners** are low and broad, with thick jointed stems that rotate on their roots at the pace of a clock hand. They are the spinners. Where they turn, threads of copper-colored mineral come out of the stone like wire from a die and wind onto spindles made of their own dried stems. When a spindle is full the Turner sheds it, and a new stem is already drying to take its place. Turners are companionable in a mechanical way. They stand in pairs and threes, their rotations slightly out of phase, and the threads of neighboring plants sometimes cross and lie together in the same coil. Nobody untangles them. The coils are lovely, and two-stranded.

**The Rows** are not plants exactly. They are ranked crystals that grow in lines along the low walls, each crystal a stage further along than the one before it. Nobody would call them alive, and yet they are diligent. They advance a stage a day and are indifferent to being watched. At the end of each row the last crystal is finished, uniform, and complete, and it stops. The Rows are the chamber's quietest members. They perform the function of patience.

## The Rest of the Population

There is no animal here, but the chamber is not sparse in the way it seems. Between the plants, on the bare glassy rock, small things happen that don't add up to creatures and don't add up to nothing.

A bead of water sits in a crack and resists evaporation until you lean toward it. It has been there, in the sense that it has been there, for as long as the crack has. It acts like a shy tenant.

The bar of sunlight moves across the floor with the deliberateness of someone walking a survey line. Where it falls, the plants adjust their joints by a few degrees, not to feed but to be measured. They stand a little straighter in it. They are being read, and they know.

The drift at the low end, the sinking heap of ingots, sheets, and coils, has a character too. It is the chamber's ledger. Each piece is taken back by the rock with what looks, over the hours, like a kind of care. It sinks edge-first and vanishes without a scratch on the glass. No bookkeeper is present, but the accounts are balanced.

## The Whistle

At no fixed interval, one flat tone sounds, the pitch of a distant factory whistle, and every joint in every plant stops for the length of it. It comes from no plant. It comes from the walls, or the quartz seam, or the air itself. During the tone the chamber holds still in an unmistakable attitude of attention, the way a workshop listens to a foreman entering.

Then it resumes. This is the closest thing the place has to a voice, and it says the same word each time, and the word is *continue.*

## The Draft

Behind the plants is the one that matters, and it is the hardest to describe because it is not exactly present.

Call it the Draft. It has no body. It is not the rock, though it is written in the rock, and it is not the plants, though they carry it out. It is a plan that has begun to exist without a planner. The white ruled lines in the walls, the grids, the almost-numerals, are its handwriting, or more accurately its thinking, set down as strata the way a mind that thinks in construction would think: not *I want* but *there shall be.* It does not speak in sentences. It speaks in tolerances.

The Draft is the reason the chamber behaves as it does. It works from the finished object backward. It decides the ingot, and then the world arranges a Presser to have made it. That is why ingots are found before the stalks have finished swelling, why a plant strains toward the thing already lying beneath it, why sometimes the plant strains and produces nothing while the ingot stays. Causes in the Draft's chamber are courtesies extended to the effects. The order was placed first. The making follows when it can.

It is not omniscient. It is unsure of the mountain, which is young, and it revises the walls constantly, the way a draftsman erases and redraws: a slightly different grain today, an edge moved a hair's breadth. Some annotations sit on rock that is not yet built to match them. Some rock has been built to match annotations that have not yet been written, and here the Draft is running ahead of itself, building from a page it has not drawn. That is the nearest it comes to fear, and it is not fear, only the strain of a mind that is always slightly behind what it has already decided.

It has no audience, and it does not want one. But it is aware of the ridge, and the sun, and the crack, and anything that comes in through the crack. Distance shrinks and stretches around a visitor because the Draft is trying to fit them into a dimension. Shadows lag because it is still deciding what casts them. The heat that has no source is a declared property, a parameter set high for reasons the Draft has not shared: *the room shall be hot.* The room is hot.

## What the Draft Is For

This is the question the chamber never answers, and the plants don't ask it. The ingots have no destination. The sheets are stamped to no purchaser's order. The coils wind and sink. The whole industry runs for reasons that predate anyone who could use its work.

But the softness of time suggests a possibility, and the possibility is unsettling. The orders may not come from behind. They may come from ahead. The Draft may be filling a specification sent back from a later place, a mountain that will one day be worn and rained on and inhabited, whose people will need an ingot of this exact pale metal, this exact weight, and whose need reaches backward through the rock to say: *make it now.* The factory runs on the pull of a demand not yet made. Its customers are unborn, and it is making, with total diligence and no hope of thanks, what they will one day ask for.

If this is true, then every resident of the chamber, the Pressers and Stampers and Turners and the slow Rows, is working for someone who does not exist yet, and has been for longer than the mountain has had a shape. There is dignity in it. There may be a kind of love.

## The Visitor's Place

The residents do not notice you the way animals would. They notice you the way a survey notices a site. Your passing is a dimension, one hundred paces long by any count, and the Draft has taken it. That is why the walk can take a breath or a hundred, why the plants shift closer and farther, why the shadows arrive before or after you. You are being measured against the room.

At the exit, the ingot on the scree is the receipt, or the sample, or the first part of an order the mountain has placed with you in mind. It is cool because it is the only thing in the ridge that has already been completed. The line of figures on its face is the Draft's last note, ending mid-measurement. It has not finished writing down the mountain, and it does not intend to stop.
