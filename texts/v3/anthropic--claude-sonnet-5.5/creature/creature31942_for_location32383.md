# The Residents of Ess

Nothing swims here, and nothing crawls. That is the first thing to understand about the residents. Everything that lives on the terraces has agreed to move only by growing.

So you will not see them as residents at first. You will see a forest, a stain on a wall, a lot of combed weed in a ditch. It takes a different clock to see that the place is occupied and busy, and has been for longer than the sea has covered it.

## The crop

The kelp is the most visible, and the least wild. It was a crop before it was anything else. The people who planted the terraces did it for so many generations that the plant changed to suit the planting. It now roots properly only on a lip of built stone, in the shallow shelf of a step, in the band of light a particular height above a particular wall. Set it on a natural slope and it sulks and thins. The stands stand in their contour lines because the lineage can no longer imagine anything else. The row is not a habit it has kept. It is a shape it was made to fit.

So the kelp is faithful to the makers in the way a hand is faithful to the glove it was grown inside. From outside, that is hard to tell from love. Whether it is not, nobody is left to say.

The stalks lean together in the drift and hold each other up. Their holdfasts grip the cobbles like fists, and over the centuries the grip has become structural. In many places the wall now stands because the kelp will not let it fall. A terrace that seems to hold its line by masonry alone is often being held by a rope of living root threaded through every joint. The plant repays its keepers by keeping the keepers' work, though the keepers have been gone for longer than most languages last.

## The plaster

Over the stone lies a second population, so low and pale that you take it for the color of the rock. It is a crust, green-brown and cushioned, thick as a thumb in the best places, and it is the most patient thing in Ess. It grows perhaps a hand's width in a hundred years. It creeps across the tiles and the rammed clay, and where it has been longest it has knit the stones into a skin. Silt catches in it. The silt becomes soil, and in the soil the red fronds root, and the kelp roots in the red. The whole green hierarchy stands on this slow, unremarkable plaster.

It is not indifferent to what it lies on. It avoids the bronze. Every pin, every wedge, every scrap of worked metal stands clear of the crust, ringed by a collar of bare stone a finger or two wide, as if the growth had thought twice and drawn back. The metal is poison to it, a thin green breath in the water that the crust will not cross. The adze in the channel lies in the middle of such a clearing. A visitor finds it and reads it as reverence. The crust is only afraid of copper. Both readings may be true, since reverence is often fear that has lasted long enough.

The crust also keeps a margin around the handprints in the tiles, and that has no such explanation. The clay there is no different that anyone has found. The crust simply comes up to the edge of each print, stops, and goes around.

## The long weed

In the sluice-channels the weed is dense, dark and leaning, and it is the nearest thing in Ess to a mind.

It is not one plant. It is a lineage joined by runners into lines that run for hundreds of miles along the old drainage, unbroken from wall to wall, root to root. Whether that makes it one creature or a great many is a question like asking whether a river is one river. What matters is that it feels. When a curtain of cold brine falls onto the weed at one end of a channel, the cells there change their chemistry for a season, and the change is passed on to the next cells, and the next, at a few paces a day. It moves down the line like a rumor in a very large family.

Across the plain, news travels this way in years. Mostly it is weather. *The ice is thinner here. The brine came late. A slope has slumped upstream and the water is cloudy.* Some of it, carried out on the drift, comes back around the gyre a lifetime later, and the weed receives its own old news again with no sign of surprise. It is a memory made of echo. It holds no fact for long, but it holds the shape of the plain's moods, season after season, in the slow to-and-fro of its stems.

If Ess has a voice, this is it. It makes no sound.

## The neighbors overhead

The snow that falls on everything comes from somewhere. Against the underside of the ice, in the short bright season, a film of gold-brown life blooms. It is the only quick thing in the whole account, flaring in weeks, feeding on the thin light that sifts through, and dying in weeks. Its dead rain down through forty fathoms and feed the kelp, the crust and the weed alike.

The terraces know them only as a yearly gift of dust. They are a generation that never comes down alive. Mayflies to a mountain, they are born, spend themselves and fall. The residents below take them in without ceremony. It is the only trade Ess still has with the world.

## What they make of the makers

They make nothing of them. Or they make everything of them, which is the same. The rows, the contours, the tile-lined ditches, the cobble walls the kelp holds together with its fists: the residents are the makers' work, still running. The genes remember the slope. The crust remembers the cement. The weed remembers the flow. There is no grief in it, because grief is quick and this is the slow settling of a habit. But a visitor standing on a drowned ridge, seeing the kelp lean in its ordered ranks over a field no one has ploughed for a hundred thousand years, cannot help feeling that the crop is waiting for someone to come and harvest it. It is not. It has forgotten harvest. It simply continues to be a field.

## And you

If you come through, you will be very fast. Your warmth, your breath's stream of bubbles, the swing of your arms are all a disturbance in the drift, lasting minutes. Blades will part and close behind you. A bladder or two may be knocked loose and rise. A little silt will lift off a tile and settle back a little differently. The kelp will register you the way it registers a brief eddy. The weed may carry a small, confused note about it for a season or two: *something warm, once, on the east slope.* The crust will not know, because the crust is still working on the last century.

Then you are gone, and the silt falls, and the ice sings a long way off, and the field that is not waiting goes on being a field.
