# The Tenants of the Shelf

Almost nothing lives on the Upheaval Shelf, and what does has no reason to be called a resident. It is a tenant, holding a lease that the ground can end at any moment and usually does.

## The Film

The tenant is the green-black sheen in the throats of the cooler vents. It is thinner than a coat of paint and duller than the rock around it, and it shines only when a vent's slow pulse passes over it. It has no head, no root, and no edge that means anything. Where it lies, it lies as a stain lies, and only its behavior tells you it is something else.

It lives on the difference between things. The vent's throat is hot at one end and cool at the other. The condensate trickling down the wall is acidic where the gas is fresh and nearly neutral where it has run a while. The film sits along these gradients and takes a little from each, a sip of sulfur here and a trace of iron there. It turns the imbalance into more film. This is a slow, cold sort of hunger, closer to rust than to appetite. It does not grow toward anything. It grows wherever the chemistry leans, and stops where the chemistry evens out.

It has a rhythm, though. If you watch a single patch through several breaths of the vent, you may see the sheen brighten a moment *after* the glow does, a faint delay, as if the film were remembering the light rather than receiving it. Nobody can say whether this is chemistry running a little behind the heat or something more like anticipation. The delay is longer in older patches.

## How It Dies, and Why That Is Not the End

Most of the film's history is a history of being crushed. A fissure closes and a whole vent-throat is sealed. A lobe of lava slides over a wall and cooks it to a gray scab. A swelling cracks a chimney and the dry air gets in. The film does not resist any of this and has no means to. Whole lineages end in an afternoon, silently, without a smear left behind.

Yet the film keeps turning up. New vents open, and after a season, or a decade, or never, a green-black gloss appears in the throat of one of them. Something is carrying it.

## The Golden Threads

The likeliest carrier is the glass hair, the pale threads spun from spray in the wind, drifting and snagging and humming. Look closely at a snag of them caught on a rock edge downwind of a film-bearing vent, and here and there a strand is dulled by a bloom too fine to see without a lens. It might be film, or a scrap of it, or just the dust of the film's chemistry. The threads travel kilometers. Some fall into cold fissures and are lost. Some are carried into the warm mouths of new vents, and in a few of those the sheen begins.

If this is so, the hum is the most intimate thing on the shelf. The high, uneven, unresolving sound is the sound of the tenant's seed being blown across the dark, a faint chorus that no one is singing and that is carrying something all the same.

## The One That Stays

Rarely, in a hollow ringed by upthrust blocks, the film is not a sheen. It is a mat.

It may be a hand's width thick, layered like wet felt, dark green at the top and almost black beneath, with a shimmering rust-colored fringe where it meets the warm rock. It has survived long enough for the layers to matter. The old film below has become a substrate for the new film above, and the mat has begun to hold its own damp, its own small climate. Its surface is faintly puckered in a pattern that is nearly regular. It tilts, very slightly, toward whichever side of the hollow is warmest. When the vent's glow shifts, the tilt follows over the course of days.

Around it, and this is the part that troubles people who see it, the minerals are unusually orderly. The sulfur banding on the nearest chimney is finer and more even than on chimneys elsewhere. The stacked plates lean at similar angles. The ring itself is rounder than rings without a mat. It may be coincidence, or a mat may simply prefer the kind of place that rings and chimneys also prefer. Or the film, without knowing, may nudge the way minerals fall out of the gas. It may be the unwitting bricklayer of the few structures on the shelf that seem to have been built, and the land's imitation of construction may be partly the tenant's first stammered attempt at it.

When the ground moves and the ring topples, the mat is buried under its own architecture. The dark, dripping rubble may keep a scrap of it alive for a while. That scrap may be enough.

## What Does Not Live Here

Nothing grazes the film. Nothing watches the mat. No shape moves between vents at night. The shelf holds no predator, no scavenger, no wanderer, and the dry storms carry only dust. The film's only neighbors are the rock and the heat, and it has never met a thing that could notice it.

## Whether It Knows

It does not know, in any sense the word can hold. But a thing that has been erased ten thousand times and still returns is not nothing. If life has a first draft, this may be it: unread, unsigned, written in a room with no reader, at the top of the world. It is a green-black sheen leaning toward the warm, dimming and brightening a moment after the light, waiting for a vent to open and a golden thread to drift in.
