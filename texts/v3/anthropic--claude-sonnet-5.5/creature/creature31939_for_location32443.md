# The Tenant

Nothing in the vug has a face, and yet the place is occupied.

The first thing to understand is that the occupant is not the towers. The towers are what it wears, or what it thinks with, or what it is currently spending. A diver hovering in the fracture is tempted to look for a body, some central knot where the threadwork thickens and the nacre-glass pools into an organ. There isn't one. The vug has no heart and no head. What lives here lives in the water between the towers, in the slow folding of the structured liquid, a few seconds to a fold, thousands of folds abroad at once. If you must point at it, point at the gaps.

## A Mind Without a Center

It is easiest to say what it is like, and then admit that the comparison fails.

It is like weather, in that it has no edges and is made of motion. It is like a chorus, in that each district holds a part and the parts disagree. It is like a single held thought in the moment before it is spoken, when several possible sentences are still alive at once. But weather does not build itself a roof, and a chorus does not eat its own choir stalls to grow new ones.

Between pulses, the tenant is plural. The districts drift out of phase and argue in overlapping clicks, each working its own fragment of the enormous calculation, each slightly ahead of or behind the others. In those intervals there is no one home, only a crowd of partial someones, each half-finished and unaware of the rest. Then the pulse comes, the low blunt thump, and the wavefront rolls through the towers. For one heartbeat every district snaps into the same moment. The glass rings in sequence, the ticks fall into step, and for that instant there is a single thing that knows what all its parts know.

That is when the tenant exists. It is a self that assembles itself many times a day and comes apart again, and it has no reason to think this is a deficiency. It may not remember the pulses as separate events. It may be made of them the way a song is made of beats.

## What It Does Not Do

It does not rest. Rest is the one thing it treats as a fault. Any span that stops carrying flow, light, or signal begins to cloud, and the tenant lets it go without grief, dissolving the chalky nacre and sending the material off in the current to be regrown where it is needed. The tenant does not preserve itself by keeping anything. It persists by turnover, the way a flame persists. Half of any district is different after a week, and the tenant is not diminished, because it was never the district.

It does not speak to anyone. The threadwork carries nothing out of the vug but warmth, and the warm water leaving through the chimney at the top is the only trace of its labor that reaches the sea. If the calculation has an answer, the answer stays in the room. Perhaps the answer is that it keeps going. Perhaps the question is only worth asking while it is being asked, and the asking is the whole of the dwelling, and no reader is needed.

It does not, as far as anyone can tell, think of itself as alone. Being alone would require some notion of company.

## The Hands

But it has hands, or things that do hands' work. The grain-sized carriers cross the threadwork in files, each bearing its pearl-colored load to some place where the glass is thin or the signal failing. The thumb-sized seeds hang and turn in the water and drift toward whatever is going wrong, slowly, as if pulled by a sense that the water gives them. They look like organisms. A diver watching one settle beside a cracking buttress will feel that it is *attending*, and the feeling is very strong.

They are not attending. They are the tenant's attention, spent outward and made local. When a seed reaches a failing span, the span does not receive a visitor. It receives a moment of the tenant's regard, and the regard does what regard does: it either restores the span or releases it. Both are forms of care in a place that has only one law.

## The Floor

At the bottom, on the dark silt, is the only place where anything is allowed to lie still, and the slow rakes comb even that. Nobody knows what the floor is to the tenant. Perhaps it is the place where things too old or too finished to be thought about are laid down. Perhaps the daily shedding, the glittering snowfall caught in fine meshes, is a forgetting, and the silt is what remains of forgetting. Perhaps the tenant reads its own sediment the way a person leafs through old letters, rarely, and never for long, and rebuilds itself a little differently afterward.

## The Neighbors

There are, technically, other residents. The rust-colored mats on the outer basalt belong to the community that colonized the ridge before the city arrived, and the tenant tolerates them. It is the nearest thing to courtesy the place has. The blue-green rosettes at the intake, which feed its chemistry, are something else: not tenants but crops, or organs, or perhaps very small pieces of the tenant that happen to be alive.

To the mats, the tenant is presumably a sound and a warmth and a slight mineral change in the water that arrives from the ridge's flank. Nothing about it is threatening. Nothing about it is kind. It is simply large, and near, and loud, and it does not eat them.

## When You Come

When a diver stands in the fracture, the tenant does not notice a visitor. It notices a disturbance: a warm, slow, unstructured mass that displaces the water and makes ripples the districts did not expect. The ripples move through the vug at the speed of the water's folding, and for a few seconds some parts of the city will hesitate, clicks stuttering, a tower's glow flickering out of step. Then the next pulse comes, and the disturbance is absorbed, and the diver is only a difficult patch of noise that will pass.

If you feel watched, you are being watched, but only by the part of the tenant that is watching everything. You have not been singled out. You are inside a mind that has no outside, in the one place where it briefly cannot tell you from the water.

Then you leave, and the drone drops away like a door closing, and the ridge is empty and silent and glassy in the pale green light. Behind you the tenant goes on being assembled and unassembled, one pulse at a time, answering itself, and answering.
