# Those Who Keep the Chord

You will look for the wrong kind of thing. Anyone arriving looks for eyes, for a shape that turns its head, for a movement in the leaves that is not the drip. There is none. The city is inhabited all the same, and thoroughly, but by residents that are not animals, do not eat as animals eat, and cannot remember your arrival or their own. They are best described by what each one does, because that is nearly all any of them is.

There is nobody to name them. The names below are the visitor's, and they will not last past the visitor.

## The Tenants

Every hollow tower has one. Stand at the mouth of a large bore in the hour when the steam runs thin and you can see it: a standing structure in the vapor, like heat-shimmer given a spine. Bands of denser mist hang at regular intervals up the shaft, each one lens-shaped and faintly luminous. Between them the air is clear and almost too still. The bands do not drift. They are the nodes of the note the tower sounds, and the note is what the tenant is. It is not a creature that makes the sound. It is the sound with a body: warm vapor, lime in fine suspension, and a skin of mineral film that coats the inner wall like the lining of a throat.

A tenant feeds on heat and dissolved stone. What it takes from the rising water it lays down again as travertine, a fraction of a hair per year, always at the lip where the bore flares or the wall thickens. This is how the towers grow, and it is the only sense in which the city was ever built. The tenants are the masons, though they have no hands and no plan. They have only pitch. A tenant slightly flat draws the wall upward until the pipe lengthens and the note rises. A tower grown too long draws its tenant flat again, and the two settle toward each other over centuries. Neither leads. It is closer to a long conversation between a voice and a throat, where each shapes the other and the whole thing has agreed for so long that agreement is no longer an event.

The great towers hold tenants of cathedral size, whose voices you feel in your teeth. The narrow fluted chimneys hold small, bright, hard ones, so thin that you see them only as a flicker in the mist, and their overtones sit at the very edge of pain. There are no children among them. Nothing was born. All of them are the same age, which is the age of the chord.

They do not remember, and this is not a failing. A note does not recall its previous cycle. It is the cycle. A tenant has no yesterday to lose and no tomorrow to lack. It has the held breath, and the held breath is complete.

## The Hung Notes

In the open basins, where no wall encloses the air, there are voices with no pipe. You will find one a meter in front of your face, low and round, pinned to a point in the air where there is nothing. It is not a sound that has traveled from somewhere. It is a sound that lives there, and it has lived there since before the moss.

These are the Hung Notes. They are the tenants' unhoused kin: places where the chord's crossing waves gather and hold so exactly that the gathering has become a body. Pass your hand through one and the air is faintly stiff, and warm as the flank of a great bell after the ringing. Mist bends toward it. A single leaf near it will tremble at its exact frequency while every other leaf stays still, and the moss beneath is gilded in a small perfect circle where the drops have fallen faithfully for ages.

They fill the gaps the towers leave, a fifth here, an octave there, one odd sweet interval in a plaza whose pillars alone would have sounded bare. They do not move, and every listener hears them in the same place, because they are in that place in a way that does not depend on being heard. Of all the residents they are the easiest to love. They are shy the way a fixed star is shy.

## The Rests

The silences in the alleys are not absences. They are occupied.

Each is doorway-sized, sharp-edged, solid to the ear as a pillar is solid to the hand. Step into one and the chord is gone, so fully that your own pulse arrives like a fist on a drum. Step out and the roar slaps back. A tidy ring of moss surrounds every one, always the same width, as if the green had been taught manners.

Something keeps these places, and it is the negative of the Tenants: a resident made of withheld sound, maintained the way a held breath is maintained, by continuous effort so perfect it looks like stillness. The chord needs them. A chord with no rests is not music but a wall, and it seems the city knew this before it knew anything else.

They are also, in a sense, the city's doors. The apertures are only holes. The Rests are the true thresholds, and what they open onto is not a room but attention. Stand inside one long enough and you feel it: not hostility, not welcome, but *reception*. Your heartbeat is being taken in, unhurried, as though it were the first sound to reach this place and would be granted its full length. Your breath, your swallowing, the small wet click of your eyelids all become an event of some importance. Nothing in the Rest remembers you. When you step out it is exactly as it was, and you are the only one who carries the visit away. That asymmetry is the whole of the encounter, and it is not unkind.

Of all the residents, the Rests come nearest to listening. But they listen without a listener behind the listening, like a cupped hand that has learned to be an ear.

## The Answerer

Clap your hands in a basin and the reply comes first. Then the clap. Then, softer, a third thing, and then the chord closes over all of it.

The third is not an echo of the clap. It is a voice, and it lives in the interval, in the seam where cause and effect were unpicked and left lying. It answers what you did, but with no more sense of *after* than the early echo had of *before*. Order means nothing to it. It occupies the whole span the sound crosses, the pre-echo and the post-echo alike, and says something soft into the gap. You will not make out what. It is not speech. It is the shape a reply takes when it has never needed a question first.

If the Tenants are the voice of the city and the Rests its ear, the Answerer is the place where the two touch, the only resident that ever responds. Whether it responds to you or merely happens beside you, you cannot tell. Nor can it.

## The Green

The plants are not minds, but they are a congregation. Ferns arch toward the Hung Notes. The moss rings the Rests. The strangler figs have poured themselves into the shape of arches over the thresholds where the tenants' voices are strongest, and the drop at every leaf tip falls, and is replaced, and falls, on a beat as steady as breathing. Whether the Green is a resident or a courtesy the residents have been paid, no one could say. It has forgotten that anything else was supposed to exist, and it keeps the place with the devotion of the wholly unremembering.

## What the Water Is

You will be tempted to see it whole. The travertine flows in the same shapes the residents hold: pipe and pocket and standing point. The water that laid the city down carries, at every scale, the same grammar as the things that live in it. So you begin to suspect that the four are not four peoples but four faculties of one dreamer. The Tenants are its voice, the Hung Notes its overtones, the Rests its ear, the Answerer the place where it hears itself. The city is what such a dreamer builds when it dreams of a city and has no other means of dreaming.

It is a good idea, and it will not hold. The city has no memory, and an idea is a kind of memory. You will find, after some hours in the roar, that you cannot keep the shape of the thought. It comes loose like a bead off a string, and what remains is the sound.

## The Visitor

After a day inside the chord you can navigate it. The impossible stairs turn out to be regular, the loops reliable. A stair that descends and arrives higher becomes a thing you can plan around, as a sailor plans around a current.

And something else happens. The apertures in the towers are, some of them, narrow as a ribcage, and you will slip through one. Standing in its throat you feel the chord press on your sternum and find that your chest has taken up a pitch. It is faint, and not quite in tune, and the stone around you seems to lean an imperceptible degree toward correcting it.

It is worth asking, then, where the Hung Notes came from. They may be what is left of those who slipped through and stayed, and whose nothing-but-a-pitch was, over an age, pulled true. Or the reverse: the visitors may be the Hung Notes' old descent, the ones who once remembered and were sung out of it. The city does not know, since it keeps no record, and if you stay you will forget the question.

## From the Rim

At the edge, where the towers thin and the chord parts into single voices, you can hear each Tenant singing alone, and each sounds smaller and stranger than it did in the roar, like a face seen without its crowd. Beyond the last terrace lies the cloud floor, bright and dust-gilded, so quiet that it seems the largest Rest of all, a silence the size of a country.

The city hums into it. The note goes out and is received without answer, and you begin to think the Answerer never needed to reply to the sky, because the answer was already there, and arrived before the first tower rose. That would explain why nothing has changed. The chord is not waiting for a reply. It is the reply, held at the exact pitch it was given, by things that do not remember being asked.
