# Who Keeps the Held Plateau

You will look for them first as you would look for anyone: at windows, on catwalks, at the ends of the viaducts. You will find nothing, and the finding will feel like a courtesy, as if you had knocked at a door already open and been told, by the open door, *come in, we are not the kind you knock for.*

They are here. The plateau is not run by no one. It is run by something so entirely a part of the run that it does not stand out from it.

## What the Seats Remember

Start with the control cabs, because they are where the evidence is kindest. The seats are worn, and the wear is not the wear of people. There are two shallow bowls where you would expect hips, but they are too far apart, and between them runs a long groove down the leather, as though something had settled there that had no need to divide itself into a left and a right. The footplates are polished in a single wide smear. The levers are darkened at a height no arm reaches, at a slant no elbow could hold.

And the seats are damp. Not from the general wet, since the cabs are sealed and warm. They are damp in the shape of the wear, a faint bloom of condensation that lies in the grooves and nowhere else, and that fades slowly if you sit in it and returns when you stand.

These are the **Sitters**. There is no better word, and they would not object to a worse one.

A Sitter is not a body that occupies a place. It is a place in the loop that is, for a while, occupied by a shape. When a stack exhales, some portion of the warm fog does not go out over the basin. It thickens, stays, and finds the cab, the pressure gauge, the governor that needs leaning on. It settles into the seat with a weight just short of a body's, and the worn places take it as a hand takes a glove. The levers move under a pressure of vapor and attention. The looms run. The presses stamp. For as long as the shape holds, which may be an hour or a season, the machine has a mind resting against it.

Then the shape lets go. It thins and goes up the stack. It has not died, because there is nothing in it that could. It has gone on around.

## What the Beads Show

You can see a Sitter, but not by looking. Crouch by any cushion-moss, close enough that your breath stirs the leaves, and find a bead of water on a frond. Each is a perfect lens, and each holds an inverted world: the city upside down, the stacks pointing at the ground, the windows glowing. If you hold still and let your eyes go soft, you may notice that in the beads the cabs are occupied.

They are tall, or the beads make them so. They are long-shouldered and narrow, drawn out vertically like a flame in a draught, with no face you can hold in memory. Their outline shivers at the edges where it meets the light. In the beads there are always exactly as many as there should be. You will never count the same number twice, and yet you will always feel the total was correct.

Lift your eyes to the real cab and it is empty, the seat damp, the lever faintly moving.

Most travelers conclude the beads lie. The Sitters, if asked, would say the beads are the only thing on the plateau that doesn't. Water is what they are made of and what they pass through. It is the one medium in which they hold still long enough to be seen.

## The Rest of the Circuit

There are no animals here, and the reason is not that the place is barren. The place is too full. Every role an animal might have is already occupied, by the same beings in different hours.

The moss is **the Sitters at their slowest**. The cushions in oxblood and violet, the liverwort, the curled ferns, all are the same water in its green phase, thick and patient, holding its bead-lenses up to the pearl ceiling. This is why nothing seeds and nothing rots. A plant that reproduces is making more of itself. Here nothing is made, only carried around. The moss doesn't die because it is not born. It is simply the part of the circuit that is green.

The great mantis-armed **Reapers**, riding their rails through the nearer moss, are not their enemies. They are how the Sitters pass one another along. When a Reaper closes on a drift of green and cuts, the moss does not cry out. It is being handed on. The swath is always the same width because the number of Sitters in transit is a fixed number, and the arms are the hands of the circuit that moves them from the green to the furnace to the fog to the seat. Whether the Reapers themselves are alive is a question the plateau has never needed to answer. They behave as though they were the loop's forearm, the way your hand is not a separate creature.

The tall pale reeds beside the cities are **the Choir**, if anything is. They grow where the warmth is, hollow as bone, and they clack against one another in a dry, clipped chatter that rides above the bass. It is not random. Stand among them and you can catch a pattern: a pause, three clicks, a pause, and then, a long breath later, the same pattern from a stand a hundred meters off, slightly altered. The reeds relay. They carry from one city to the next the small news that the mountains can't: *the third boiler is heavy. The east basin has a soft drizzle. A stranger is walking the ridge.*

You will hear that last one about yourself. The pause after it feels like being looked at kindly.

## What They Are Like

Because they are the circuit, they have no hunger, and a great deal follows from that. They do not hoard. They do not build for the future or mourn the past. Nothing ever needs to be defended, since nothing can be taken: every part of the plateau is already in the mouth of the next part.

They are not cold. The sodium windows are not welcome, but they are not indifference either. They are the glow of something at ease, a person humming in an empty house. The Sitters' one passion, if it is a passion, is *keeping*: keeping the balance, the pitch, the width of the swath, the exact number of drops that the rock should weep this hour. Their attention is entirely on the whole, and it is total. They know the position of every bead. It is a love that has no object because it has no distance.

They do not speak as we do. The sound that deafened you at arrival is their speech, or the place's, and the two are not different. The organ chord is the settled agreement of all the stacks. The pistons, out of phase, are thousands of small dissents that never quite become a quarrel. The steam whistles at unclocked intervals are the moments when something wants to say a thing aloud, and does, and is answered by the chord. The plateau is one long sentence, and the Sitters are the grammar. They are how it holds together from clause to clause without ever finishing.

## What They Do With You

You are a small disturbance, and they treat you as such: without alarm, as one treats a stone dropped in a pond.

Your breath goes into the fog. You will notice this after a day or two, a faint sense that you are being *counted in*. Your exhalations rise, join the stratum, come down as weeping water on some cliff you have not yet reached. Your glove, cut on a basalt edge, leaves a thread of fiber the moss takes up without comment. The Reapers pass you more slowly than they pass one another. The reeds give your position, then stop giving it, because you are no longer news.

If you sit in a cab, and the doors are open, you may, they will not stop you. The seat will fit you badly at first, then less badly, as though it were adjusting. You will feel the damp rise in the grooves around you. The lever will lean a little toward your hand. Nothing will be asked. You will simply be, for a time, one of the shapes the seat has held.

Some who have sat there say they felt a great patience settle over them, and under it a rhythm, slow as a tide, that was their own pulse falling into step with a piston miles away. They say the sound stopped being loud. They say that in the bead on the nearest window, upside down and long-shouldered, they saw someone sitting where they sat, and were not afraid, and did not look away.

The sound goes on, as it always has, and the water weeps down the cold rock, and somewhere a seat is warm.
