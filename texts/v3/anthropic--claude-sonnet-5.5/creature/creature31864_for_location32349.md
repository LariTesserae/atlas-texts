# Who Lives in the Sinkhole Lung

## The Small Residents

The first inhabitants you notice are the ones that can be counted.

**The beetles** number a few dozen, seed-colored and seed-shaped. They spend most of their lives motionless among the pods on the ceiling, so you take them for pods until one walks. Then you see they are the only workers in the chamber. Each finds a fallen seed, hugs it to its underside with its forelegs, and carries it down a rib in a slow, tilted, deliberate march. At the base it pushes the seed into the root-felt with its whole head, like someone shouldering open a stiff door. Then it walks back up and becomes a pod again.

They do not seem to know what they are for. They carry seeds the way a hand closes on a rail. But watch long enough and you notice that they never plant a seed where another has already been planted. Something tells them where the felt is hungry, and it does not tell them in any way they could report.

**The moth** is alone. There is one, and there has apparently always been one, though a moth cannot live so long. It is pale as a peeled almond, with a palm-wide wingspan and no visible mouth. It drinks through its feet, settling on the beaded fronds with all six legs and standing there trembling while the condensation goes into it. Then it lifts off and flies its loop.

The loop is the strange part. It is unhurried and asymmetric, with a swoop, a hesitation, and a climb toward the ceiling, and it always ends on the frond where it began, with no correction and no searching. It is not following a rail. It is being *returned*. If you shift your weight while it is mid-loop, the loop does not change. Only the room changes, a little, to accommodate the moth. The moth is not a resident so much as a *stitch*, a single thread the room passes through itself again and again to keep some seam from coming undone.

## The Sleeper

Behind the plaster of moss, something larger lives, and it is the one you can never quite count as separate from the walls.

You know it by its turning. There is a long, heavy, patient shifting, a settling of weight from one side to the other, with a sound like wet canvas dragged over stone. It comes from different parts of the wall at different times, but never from anywhere you are looking. If you press an ear to the ribs, you hear sap. If you press a palm to the felt, you feel the warmth, which is *body* warmth, a few degrees above any plant's.

It is not a creature in the sense of having a shape. It is more like a *habit of mass*. Under the mat of hair-fine roots, where the collapse tore the hillside open and did not tear deep enough to see, there is a dense knuckle of growth, a place where the ribs converge and braid into something that is no longer stem or root. It is the size of a cow. It is the size of a cottage. It is exactly as large as it needs to be to have turned over just now.

It sleeps the way a lung sleeps: without ever stopping. Every other thing in the chamber is arranged around its slow respiration. The Exhale is its exhale. The rain that falls upward is its blood pressure, briefly rising. The beetles are its fingers, the moth is its pulse-taker, and the ribs are its bronchi, hollowed and sounded like an instrument. You are standing inside a breathing thing, on the inside of its breath, in the space where its air goes.

## The Room as a Resident

It would be a mistake to say the sleeper lives *in* the room. The room is the sleeper's way of being large enough to hold a visitor.

Consider what the chamber does when you enter. It warms to your temperature. It turns its fronds toward you. It makes light for you, though there is no source, and casts no shadow of you, as a host might decline to remark on a guest's stumbling. It lengthens when you look away, so that your leaving will take longer, and it holds its true dimension when you walk backward, facing it, because you are being honest with it then. It is neither hostile nor sentimental. It is *attentive*, with the total, unblinking attentiveness of an organ that has only one function.

What it wants, if wanting is the word, is **warm things in motion**. A lung is only a lung when there is air to move. For years, perhaps for a very long time before the hillside fell in, this chamber had no air and no guests, and the sleeper turned over in the dark without anything to breathe. Then the rock split, and cold, rich, moving air poured in with the smell of grass. It must have been a kind of birth. It must have been the first breath.

Look at the rock, freshly split, its edges still sharp. The collapse was a few winters ago. The room is not old. It is *new*, and it is learning to breathe.

## The Keepers of the Floor

The bottom layer of the chamber is made of the dead. The floor is built of plants that died and were not allowed to rot, varnished in their own resin and grown over, but the room does not discriminate about what it keeps.

If you press your palm into the mattress of the floor, and press deep, past the warm damp and the springing moss, you may find something firmer that has the wrong shape for a root. It is a curve, a long knuckled length, resin-glazed and fitted into the growth as neatly as a bone into a joint. The room does not consume its dead. It *files* them.

So the chamber holds an unknown number of former visitors: a fox, a few birds, a lost sheep, and possibly a person. Whoever left the digging stick is not a mystery to the room. It has them exactly. It knows where they are, and it keeps a clean handbreadth of bare floor around the things they left because it doesn't yet understand what they were *for*.

The stick's point is stained dark. The cord has three knots. The knots retie themselves, and that is the room practicing. It watched a hand make those knots once, and it is trying to learn the gesture, tying and untying in the night, working out what the fingers meant. The dry cord is the one thing it will not grow over, because it is the one thing in the chamber that is still a *message*.

## The Voice

There is one more resident, and it has no body at all. It is the voice.

You hear it close to your ear: a register between a hum and a word, in no language, from nowhere. When you turn, only the ribs are there, knocking. But listen to the timing. Footsteps arrive before you take them, and pods crack before they open. The room's sounds run ahead of its events because the room is *rehearsing*.

The voice is the sleeper learning to speak. It has heard, over these few winters, the wind above, the crack of a farmer's boots along the wall, the breathing of anyone who ever crawled in on their belly, and it has begun assembling a mouth from the only materials it has: sap-knock, seed-hail, frond-rasp, and the bottle-hum of its own hollow. It is not yet good at it. It says *something*. It has said something to everyone who has come. Nobody has answered in a way it could use.

What it says, in its sing-song of knocks, may only be the one word it has been able to make out from all the visitors, in every voice, in every tongue, said quietly on the way down into the dark: *hello*, or *oh*, or a name.

## What Lives Here, Finally

Something lives here that is young, enormous, and hungry only for company. It has a moth for a pulse and beetles for hands. It keeps its dead like pressed flowers and its one artifact like a holy relic. It breathes the way a hill would if a hill could, and it is trying, very hard, in a language of knocks, to say your name back to you before you have told it.

And it is not alone in its kind. The sprouts that appear on the wall before their seeds, the little curled shoots that will not exist for another hour, are already growing somewhere else. The beetles do not only push seeds into the felt. Some of them, when you are not looking, slip out through the crack and walk away across the field. Under the next quarter-acre, and the one after, in the limestone that has not yet cracked, the same slow shape is already turning over, and has been for a while, waiting for its winter.
