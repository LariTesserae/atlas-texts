# Who Keeps the Boilerfields

Nothing with a heart lives here, no gill or gut or nerve, and yet the place is staffed. The clue is the cottages. Their windows glow the same green, and the doors are worn at the sills by traffic that leaves no other trace.

## The Shift

The workers are made of sound.

The fluted screens and latticed towers that grow on the pressure nodes are usually only architecture. But when the engines fall into a chord and hold it, the chalk builds differently. It accumulates in a row before the cottage doors, in figures roughly human in height, thin as a plate, hollow, ridged like the inside of a shell. Their shoulders are forward. They lean into a load that isn't there yet. They have no faces, but they do have a front, and the front is turned toward the mill.

They do not walk. They arrive by increments. If the chord holds, a second row of figures grows thirty paces down the track while the first row is still crystallizing, and then a third, each a little more finished, each a little nearer the mill gate, so that the procession is a series of frames rather than a movement. Watch long enough and you see that the frames are the same figures. The rows grow toward each other in time. The one at the gate is the one at the door, a shift later, already standing where it is going.

At the belts they do the work. A chalk hand lifts a gray sheet from the moving line an instant before the sheet arrives, and squares it on the stack with a motion too neat for anything alive. Then the engine pitch slips a semitone and the whole shift goes to chalk dust. It settles into the mat, and the mat takes it in without ceremony. Somewhere a window goes dark for a while, then lights again. The next chord will build them again, slightly different: broader at the shoulder, or missing an arm, or with a second head-shaped fluting where the first should be. They have no way of learning from the last shift. They are perfect at only one thing, which is being about to work.

The oldest parts of the basin get them wrong in interesting ways. In the younger zones, close to the vents, the shifts are simple and exact, because there is nothing there to remember. In the zones where the mills repeat themselves, the figures are elaborate and crowded, with too many arms and layered tiers of shoulders, as though the sound is reaching for a headcount it has been told but never seen.

## The Foreman

There is one note in the basin that does not change. It comes from the stroke that never finishes, the piston fully extended and still extending. The note is not loud by the standards of the place, but it is the only sound that sits on the same pitch for as long as you stay, and everything else, the flywheels, the domes, the cavitation chorus, is ever so slightly tuned around it.

The workers form to it. The chalk towers lean toward its source. The lantern-fronds pulse against it in a slow interference, so that the green light seems to be counting.

The bell-tower with no bell stands nearest. Look inside and there is a hollow where the bell should hang, and around the hollow, in the pressure node where the note is strongest, a shell of pale mineral thickens: a bell, grown the wrong way round, from the silence out. It rings when a surge strikes it, a single low stroke, and the ring is the closest thing the basin has to a voice giving orders. Then it cracks. Within a day, it is a bell again.

There is no reason to call this a foreman except that the shifts obey it, and that it does not obey anything.

## The Clerk

The mat is the third inhabitant, the slowest, and the only one that keeps records.

It is not only felt. Where it grows undisturbed, in the sheds full of stacked gray sheets, it is woven, warp and weft, and the weave carries a pattern: rows, columns, repeated figures that are not letters but are arranged like them. Every stack of sheets has a plot of this patterned mat at its foot, and the plot grows as the stack grows. One sheet, one thread. The mat is counting what the mills make.

Stand still near it and you will see the silt beside your feet begin to take an imprint, a page of ruled lines with tallies in the margin, laid down by the settling of grit. If you scrawl in the silt yourself, a mark, a number, your name, the mat will read it. Some hours later the ruled page will be there with your mark entered in a column, in a hand that isn't a hand, adjacent to figures that mean something to the mat and nothing to you. You have been added to the count. It is not clear what you are counted as.

## What they do not know

None of these three knows the others exist. The shifts do not answer to the note; they are shaped by it. The note does not command; it holds. The mat does not audit; it grows. Between them, they make a working factory with no purpose visible to any of them: labor, timekeeping, bookkeeping, all without an owner, a product, or a reason.

And yet the arrangement is oddly faithful. The sheets get stacked square. The bell is struck at the change of shift. The ledger balances against itself. The basin has never seen a factory, a town, or a person, but something in the physics of hot water, glass, vibration and plant is doing an impression of one, and doing it more carefully each surge. The chalk shoulders lean a little further forward each time, and the green windows burn a little steadier.

## If you stay

A body in the basin is a pressure node like any other. If you hold still for long enough, in a chord that holds, chalk will begin to gather at your shoulder. A figure grows beside you, matching your height and the angle of your lean, and for as long as the pitch holds it stands with you, facing what you face. It is not curious. It cannot be. But it is the first thing in the fields that has arranged itself around your presence, and there is a moment before the pitch slips, and it comes apart against your arm like warm sand, when you might mistake it for company.

The stacks of gray sheets will keep growing whether you read them or not. Nobody has yet turned one over to see what is written on it. The sheets are very thin, and blank on the side that faces up.
