# The Lantern Pocket

You arrive by a crack that is still deciding to be a passage. The rock is cool, wet clay laminated with pale sandstone, soft enough to take a thumbprint and keep it for a season. You squeeze down through a throat about the width of your shoulders. The walls press back with the faint give of something not yet finished hardening. Then the throat opens, and you are standing in a room the size of a chapel, and it is full of daylight.

The light does not come from a hole. It is a property of the air. The whole chamber glows the clean white-gold of late morning, and shadows fall in several directions at once, each object casting a soft primary shadow and a fainter secondary one, as if there were more than one sun and all of them close. The walls are pale and damp. Threads of moisture run down them in patterns that shift when you aren't watching. The air is cool enough to show your breath, though the breath doesn't rise. It drifts sideways and pools along the floor like milk.

## The Floor

The floor is a young thing. It is a shallow basin of grey-green mud, crusted at the edges into curling plates like old paint, with the plates still lifting a little as you watch. Where the mud is wet it holds a dozen small pools, none deeper than a hand, each perfectly clear. The pools are the room's only water, and none of them share a surface. Tilt your head and one will be level while its neighbor sits at a slant, its water leaning against the slope of nothing. A few reflect the ceiling correctly. Others reflect a ceiling that isn't there: a wide overcast sky, or a night with a single low star, or once a face turned away from you, patient, waiting to be noticed.

Crystals are being born from the mud's edge, gypsum blades no longer than a fingernail, standing upright in loose colonies. They grow audibly. If you hold still you can hear it, a fine dry ticking, like someone very far away winding a hundred tiny watches. This is nearly the only sound in the pocket besides the drips.

## The Drip

There is one drip. It falls from a point in the ceiling that you can never quite fix your eye on. Each drop takes longer than a drop should to fall. You watch it gather, stretch, and release, and then it hangs at the midpoint of its fall for a slow count of seven before completing the journey. It lands with a clear soft note, and a ring spreads across one of the pools. The rings do not fade at the edge. They reach the mud, climb it, and vanish into the clay, which darkens briefly and sighs.

Between drops, the pocket is nearly silent. The silence is not empty. It has the pressure of a held breath in a room full of people who are all being careful.

## The Fauna

You expected the place to be barren. It is the opposite. The chamber is crowded with animals, but they are so spread out, each so absorbed in its own stretch of floor, that the effect is of a vast field seen from a great height, all scattered figures and long stillness.

**The glass-backed snails** are the most common. They are the size of plums, their shells thin as blown sugar, and through them you can see their organs lit from behind. They graze the mud's crust, and where they pass they leave a track of faint, precise handwriting, cramped loops like a language written by something very old. If you follow a track it leads to the snail that made it. If you keep following, it leads to the snail that will make it.

**The hover-fish** swim through the air a hand's breadth above the floor. They are pale, slender, and eyeless, with fins that move like slow fingers. They are not lost. They graze on the milky breath-mist that pools in the low places, and they move through it in loose, unhurried shoals of three or four. When one passes in front of a light-source it does not cast a shadow. It casts a *ripple* on the wall behind it, a wavering circle of brightness, as though the wall were water.

**The moths** hang from the ceiling in clusters, grey and velvet-furred. Every so often one drops and flutters down to sip at a pool, and as it descends its wings shed a fine glitter that is, unmistakably, the source of some of the room's light. The moths are the room's small suns. When a cluster wakes, the chamber brightens. When it sleeps, the shadows lengthen and grow long, blue-edged, cool.

**Something larger** moves at the far edge of the basin. You never see it fully. It is tall, folded, and slow, a shape like an unstrung heron, drawn from the same grey as the clay, standing in a shallow pool with its head bowed. When you look straight at it, it is a stalagmite. When you look away, you hear the very quiet click of a joint bending.

The hunters here are patient. The heron-thing takes hover-fish, one at a time, with a motion so slow the fish seems to arrive in its beak by agreement. The hover-fish in turn thin the moth-dust from the mist. The moths' larvae, which nobody sees, live in the crystals and are the reason the crystals ring. It is a small, tight circuit, and it turns without hurry, all day, though there is no day.

## The Built Things

Someone, or something, has been here, though not recently and not with tools. In three places the mud has been shaped into small structures:

- **A ring of standing clay pillars**, each no taller than a knee, arranged around a pool. Their tops are pressed flat, each bearing a single deep thumbprint. The thumbprints are bigger than yours, and not quite the shape of a thumb.
- **A low arch of interlocked crystal blades**, like a doorway built for a hover-fish, leading nowhere. Air passes through it in a slow current, and the air on the far side is warmer and smells of rain on hot stone, a smell that does not belong anywhere in this cool, clay-scented pocket.
- **A shelf of stacked flat stones** against the wall, each stone etched with the same snail-script as the tracks in the mud. The lowest stones are worn smooth. The top stone is fresh, the etching still damp and the lines still, very slowly, being drawn.

None of these looks abandoned. They look *in progress*, like something between a habit and a ceremony, kept up by the room itself.

## The Breakdown

The chamber's rules are loose, and they loosen further the longer you stay.

Distances lie. Walk ten steps toward the far wall and you will find you have come back to the throat you entered by, though you never turned. Walk ten steps toward the throat and you arrive at the far wall. The room is not larger inside than out. It is *unfixed*, and the geometry rests on the attention of whoever is in it, like a sheet held up by hands.

Time pools like the mist. In the low places it runs slow: the drip hangs, the moths hover, the hover-fish stay in one attitude for many breaths. Near the ceiling it runs quick, so the crystals up there have already grown into long, blunt spears and begun to crumble, and the clay overhead is already cracking into the plates that haven't yet formed on the floor. The pocket contains its own past and future at different heights, and you carry them mixed together in your head as you climb or crouch.

And things borrow from one another. A snail's shell will turn briefly transparent, and inside it you will see the whole chamber tiny and lit, with a tiny you looking in. A pool will give back a reflection that moves a moment before you do. The heron-thing, when you finally get close enough, is not a creature at all. It is the *shape the room makes* when it wants to watch itself.

## The Turning

Every so often, though you can never predict it, the whole light of the place tilts. The moths lift as one, the mist rises in a slow white column, and for a few breaths the pocket is a lantern, dazzling, every wet surface blazing. The snails freeze. The hover-fish spiral up into the glare and are gone. The drip lands at last, and every pool rings at once, and the ringing goes on, a long, clean, glassy note, until it becomes hard to tell whether the sound is in the room or in you.

Then the light settles. The moths drift down. The clay sighs and lets go of a little more of its water. Somewhere in the ceiling a new crack begins, thin as a hair, and the room, quietly, without any sense of ceremony, begins to grow itself a new passage.

You are welcome to leave by the throat, if you can find it. Most who visit find it only once they've stopped looking.
