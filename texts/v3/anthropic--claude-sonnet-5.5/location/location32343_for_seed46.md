# The Foundry Under the Ridge

## Approach

Ten thousand feet up, a young mountain range stands over a plain of ash. Its slopes are sharp and unsmoothed. Nothing has had time to round them. The rock is black-green basalt with orange lichen-colored streaks of sulfur, and the scree lies at the angle it first fell, every fragment still holding its edges. Nothing here has been rained on in a very long time. The air is dry enough to crack the lips, and the heat comes up out of the ground as well as down from the sky.

The place is hidden. A crack runs along the ridge, shoulder-wide and easy to mistake for a shadow. Where it narrows, the rock walls give off a warmth like a bread oven's. Then the crack turns, and you are in.

## The Pocket

The chamber is about the size of a cathedral nave, and it is lit. Overhead, a seam of clear quartz as long as a river runs through the ceiling and lets the sun in as a hard white bar that slides across the floor through the day. The light is ordinary, neither eerie nor dim, and that makes the rest harder to accept.

The floor is green. The vegetation is lush in a way that shouldn't be possible, but it is scattered. Great fronds and ribbed, leathery stalks stand far apart, with bare glassy rock between them, so the garden looks like a few dozen specimens placed with a curator's patience. None of them drinks water. There is almost none here: a few beads of it in cracks, gone if you breathe on them. The plants take up hot mineral vapor and thin brines of ammonia and sulfur salts that sweat from the walls. Their leaves are blue-green, bronze-green, and a dark oily green like old copper. Their stems are jointed, and the joints move.

## The Industry

This is the strange part. Nothing was built here, and you will find no wall, tool, or hinge that anyone made. Yet the chamber is a factory, and it is working.

The plants are the machinery. A stalk swells at its base, then contracts with a clean mechanical stroke, and a bead of pale metal is pressed out at the root, cooling in the rock as a neat ingot the size of a knuckle. A fern-like thing unrolls a frond, and the frond is a flat, stamped sheet with a perfect edge. Its veins are rivets. Other plants turn slowly on their roots like cams, and where they turn, threads of copper-colored mineral are drawn out of the stone and wound onto spindles of their own dried stems. Crystals grow in ranked rows like a production line, each row one stage further along than the last, and at the end of the row they simply stop, finished, uniform, unclaimed.

Nobody collects the product. It piles up in drifts at the low end of the chamber, ingots, sheets, and coils, and then, slowly, it is reabsorbed. The rock takes it back, and the drift sinks into the floor like a body into snow. The output has no destination. The factory is running for reasons that predate anyone who could use what it makes.

## Reality Breakdown

The rules here are loose, and they loosen further the longer you stay.

**Order of events is soft.** A finished ingot may be found before the stalk that presses it has finished swelling. You watch the plant strain toward the object that already lies beneath it, and when it produces the thing, the thing is only confirmed, not created. Sometimes the plant strains and produces nothing, and the ingot stays lying there, unexplained, and does not fade.

**The rock is still being decided.** Because the geology is barely formed, edges in the chamber are provisional. A wall you touched yesterday has, today, a slightly different grain, as though a draft had been revised. Faint construction marks show in the stone: thin ruled lines in white mineral, a grid, sometimes a few dimensions written in a notation that is almost numerals. They are not carved or painted. They are strata that formed as annotations. In some places the annotations sit on the rock, and in others the rock has already been built to match them. In a few places the rock has been built to match annotations that do not yet exist.

**Distance stretches.** The chamber is one hundred paces long by any count, but walking its length can take one breath or a hundred. Sparse as it is, the space between plants does not stay constant. Two stalks that were far apart can be side by side after you look down and up again. Nothing moved. There was simply less room, and then more.

**Shadows lag.** The bar of sunlight moves slowly, and shadows follow at their own pace, sometimes arriving after the objects that cast them have gone, sometimes before.

**Heat has no source.** It is very hot, over a hundred and ten by feel, yet the stone is not warm to the touch in the places where the heat is worst. The heat seems to belong to the air alone, like a property the room has declared.

## Sound

The chamber is mostly quiet. There is a low periodic tick, like a cooling engine block, from somewhere in the walls. Once in a long while, at no fixed interval, a single flat tone sounds, the pitch of a distant factory whistle, and every joint of every plant pauses for the length of it and then resumes. That is the only communal event. There are no birds, no insects, and no animal of any kind. The whole enterprise is vegetable and mineral, and it does not seem to be missing an audience.

Underneath these sounds is a fine papery rustle of leaves flexing against each other, the sound of a very large room in which someone is thinking quietly.

## Cycles

Everything happens slowly. A stalk's stroke takes minutes. A crystal row advances a stage in a day. A whole cycle of the chamber, from a plant's first bud to its stamped frond to its collapse into the floor's drift, seems to last decades. The visitor arrives in the middle of it, and the middle is all there is to see. Nothing here is beginning or ending. Everything is under construction, permanently, and the mountain above, black and sharp and unworn, is a part of that same unfinished work.

## What Remains

Leave the way you came, through the warm crack, back into the ordinary dry heat of the ridge. On the scree outside, a hand's width from the entrance, lies a single ingot of pale metal with a clean, perfect edge. You did not carry it. It is too heavy to have followed you, and yet you are sure it was not there when you went in. If you pick it up, it is cool, the only cool thing you have touched. On one face, in the thin white notation of the walls, is a line of figures that ends mid-measurement, waiting for the rest of the mountain to be built to fit it.
