# The Chorus Pocket

From above there is nothing to see. The plateau is a low, bleached pan of gypsum and salt-crust, cracked into plates the size of doors and pale enough to hurt the eyes. Heat shimmers off it. Wind moves across it in long dry sheets, and where it crosses one particular fissure, no wider than a man's shoulders, the wind changes pitch. Travelers who know this call the fissure the Throat. Those who don't take it for a trick of the heat and walk on.

Going down takes about an hour. The crack widens into a stair someone cut with stone picks and antler wedges, and every step is worn to a shallow bowl. The sound arrives before the light does. It begins as pressure in the ears and becomes a wall: a roar of ten thousand pipes, a low grinding drone with high whistling harmonics threaded through it. It is loud enough to blur your vision and to make your teeth feel loose. Then the stair turns, and the cavity opens below you.

## The Hollow

The Pocket is a blister in the rock, a gypsum geode-cavity perhaps four hundred paces across and a hundred deep, its ceiling a shallow dome pierced by dozens of natural light-wells. Sun falls through them in hard white columns that slide slowly across the floor through the day. Where a column strikes the crystal walls, selenite faces throw the light back in bars and scattered coins, so the whole hollow glows without a single shadow being dark. Nothing here is dim. The air is warm, dry, and faintly sweet, like baked clay and cut stems.

The floor is low, the lowest ground for a day's walk in any direction. It is covered in green.

## The Green

The vegetation is lush, and there is almost no water. Understanding this is the first rule of the place. The plants here do not drink. They feed on the sound.

They are tall, fleshy, ribbed things, like fluted columns of jade, some with broad membranous leaves stretched taut like drumheads, others bristling with fine hollow filaments that tremble constantly. Their tissue is full of a clear, viscous oil that beads at the tips, and that oil is the only liquid in the Pocket. It holds what little moisture the plants pull from the vent-fog that seeps up at dawn. The leaves turn toward the loudest sources the way sunflowers turn toward the sun. The drumhead-fronds vibrate visibly, their surfaces shivering into standing patterns, and the plants grow thickest where the roar is worst. The quiet corners are dead scrub, brittle and grey. The loud ones are jungle.

Somewhere far below, a hot vent breathes upward through a lattice of natural chimneys. It is the engine of everything. Its breath drives the roar, and the roar feeds the green.

## The City

The builders understood this, so they built a city of instruments.

There are hundreds of structures, terraced up the curved walls and clustered across the floor in tight spirals. Almost all of them are tuned. Towers of fired clay tubes are stacked like organ ranks, each open at the top to the rising vent-wind. Houses have walls of hollow reed bundles bound with sinew and sealed with gypsum plaster. Bridges are strung with wind-harps of gut and hammered copper wire. Streets are grooves between resonating walls, and standing in one, you feel the whole architecture vibrating through your soles.

The tools that made it are simple: stone adzes, bone awls, copper chisels beaten cold, kilns fed with dried stalks, and rope. There is no iron, no wheel that you can find, nothing that runs on anything but hands and patience. But the geometry is exact. Every pipe length was chosen. The city is a single enormous voice, and the plants in the terraces and courtyards, each tended in its planter of baked clay, are its audience and its livestock.

## Emptiness

The city could hold thousands. It holds perhaps thirty people.

They are the Tuners. They move slowly through the huge lit spaces, spaced far apart, each a small figure on a stair or a rooftop. They wear ear-wraps of felted fibre and thick wax. Many are deaf by age forty. They speak with their hands, with taps on the wall, with touch on the collarbone, and with signal-cloths waved across the light columns. They clip fronds, patch cracked pipes, scrape salt-bloom from the mouths of tubes, and carry the clear oil in small clay jars. Otherwise they are still, sitting in patches of sun with their palms flat against a warm wall, reading the city's mood through the skin.

Whole districts are unoccupied and perfectly maintained, or once were. Some pipes have silted and gone silent, and the plants around them have died in neat brown rings. The Tuners walk around these places without comment.

## Fauna

There are few animals. Pale, hard-shelled beetles graze the frond-edges, and their footfalls make a dry ticking you feel more than hear. Small, long-limbed lizards the color of jade sit motionless on the pipes, their throats fluttering, apparently drinking in the vibration. In the highest light-wells, translucent moth-things drift up and down the columns of sun. One large predator is spoken of: a pale, blind, many-legged creature that hunts by feeling footsteps through stone. It is rarely seen. In the Pocket, where everything shakes, it is thought to be nearly starved, or mad.

## Rhythms

The place is never still. Through the day the sun-columns crawl. The plants track them and the sound, so the whole green floor slowly rotates its leaves like a crowd turning its head. In the late morning the vent gusts hard and the roar climbs into a scream that sends the Tuners indoors to the padded inner rooms. Dust of fine gypsum lifts from the floor and hangs glittering in the light. At dusk the light-wells go orange, then dark, and the wind reverses. The pipes sing a different, lower chord as cool air drops back down the Throat, and the drone becomes almost, for an hour, something like a lullaby.

Erosion has settled here. The clay tubes are polished by centuries of wind and hand, their lips worn smooth as river stones. The steps are dished, the gypsum walls fluted and scalloped. Nothing new is being carved. The city has reached a balance with its own decay, and the Tuners repair only what they must.

## Strangeness

The oddest fact is this: the plants remember. Take a cutting to a quiet place and it will wilt in days. But play it a recording of the Pocket, or hum a certain chord, and it will lean toward you. The Tuners say the whole green mass is a single listening thing, and the city is the shape of its attention. They have never said what they think it is listening for.

Sometimes at the deepest point of the noon roar, when the gusts peak and every frond blurs, the sound drops out of hearing altogether. For a few heartbeats there is only pressure and light and the smell of hot stems, and the Tuners stop what they are doing and turn their faces to the dome, waiting for something that so far has not arrived.
