# The Boiler Vug

Two kilometers up in a volcanic plateau, under a slope of black scree that has never been walked on, there is a hollow the size of a barn's interior. It has no door. From the surface you would find only a crack the width of a forearm, breathing out heat that shimmers over the gravel, and if you laid your ear against the rock you would hear the place through the stone as a low, continuous grinding, like a mill heard through a wall.

## The shape of the hollow

The chamber is a gas bubble that was trapped when the lava cooled around it, then stretched by pressure from below. Its ceiling is a low, blistered vault, close enough that a tall animal would scrape its back on it. The floor slopes toward a seam in the far wall where the rock is still half-molten. There the stone doesn't glow so much as smolder, a dull cherry-brown like the underside of a stove plate. This is the only light, and it is barely light. It shows edges and the moving humps of bodies and almost no color.

The rock here is young and unfinished. The walls are raw: ropy basalt, frozen drips, ledges snapped clean where the ceiling cracked as it cooled. Nothing has weathered. There is no soil, no dust deeper than a fingernail, no rounded pebble. Sulfur has begun to bloom in yellow crusts around the vents, and clusters of needle crystals are growing in the gas pockets. Everything is at the beginning of becoming something, and the change is slow. A crystal might gain a thumb's width in a lifetime.

## Heat and air

The air is close to the temperature of a bread oven and thick with sulfur dioxide and hot metallic vapor. It is almost perfectly dry. What moisture exists is a few wet-looking films of molten salt that creep in the cracks and harden into glassy varnish. The animals have no use for water. Their blood is a heavy, syrupy fluid built on sulfur compounds, and it runs hot enough to sting a human hand.

## The crowd

The chamber is full. Every ledge, every hand's breadth of floor, and the ceiling itself are occupied by animals living shoulder to carapace in a press of bodies so dense that the floor is rarely visible. The dominant creatures are the **smelters**, low-slung, armored things the size of large dogs, with flat plated backs the color of old pennies. They look like beetles that were forged rather than born. Each has a bellows-like chest that pumps audibly, drawing hot air across internal chambers where mineral ore is roasted in their bodies. They graze on the sulfide crusts and scrape iron-bearing rock with wide rasping mouths. Then, in a process that is the closest thing this place has to industry, they cast their waste as small dense pellets of nearly pure metal, dull gray beads and short bars, dropped into the gaps between their legs.

This is the one made thing here. The pellets are not built; they are excreted. But the **tenders**, small many-legged scavengers like scuttling wrenches, gather them in their pincers and wedge them into the crevices in the walls. Over generations, this has made rough shelves and ribs of metal in the rock. They are not structures in any deliberate sense, only habit accreted into hardware, a slag ecology.

Above the smelters, the ceiling is roofed with **hammerers**, pale, wing-cased insects that cling upside down in thousands. They strike their hardened forelimbs against the basalt in overlapping rhythms. It is a display, a way of sorting mates, and a way of keeping the crust cracked so gas can escape.

At the hot seam, where the rock is closest to melting, live the **stokers**, thin, black, long-limbed creatures that thrust their snouts into the glowing cracks and drink the heat. They breathe in bursts, and each exhale throws a small spray of sparks. These are the nearest thing to light sources, and they flare and fade all through the dark.

## Sound

The noise is enormous and continuous. There is the wet pumping roar of thousands of bellows-lungs, an ocean-like rush with no gaps in it. Over that comes the metallic clatter of hammerers, ticking, ringing, and every so often locking into a shared rhythm that swells to a shuddering clang before scattering again. Pellets clink as they drop. Steam and sulfur gas whistle out of fissures in thin, shrill notes. Underneath everything is the deep groan of the rock itself, expanding as it heats. Standing inside, you would feel the sound in your ribs and teeth before you understood it. Conversation is impossible. The animals communicate through vibration, by pressing their bodies against the stone and each other.

## Rules of the place

- **Nothing is wasted.** Every waste product is someone's food or material. The smelters' slag feeds the tenders, the tenders' shelving shelters the hammerers' eggs, and the dead are ground into the sulfur crust.
- **Heat is the currency.** Proximity to the seam is status. The largest smelters crowd close to the glowing rock, and the young and old are pushed toward the cooler edges, where the air is merely deadly.
- **The place is growing slowly.** Each decade the seam shifts a little. The chamber is gently inflating, and fresh rock is exposed, cracking and cooling. The animals migrate along with it, slowly, over generations, like a city that follows its own furnace.

## Outside

Far above, the plateau is silent and empty under a thin, cold sky, and the only sign of the vug is that heat-shimmer over a crack in the scree. No one has ever heard the chamber. No one has stood in it. It exists entirely for itself, and it roars in the dark.
