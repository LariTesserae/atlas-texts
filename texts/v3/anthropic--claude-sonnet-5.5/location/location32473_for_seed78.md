# The Understory of the Held Sky

Above four thousand meters, the land has been roofed over.

Nothing else can be said first. Seen from the few places where anything can see it, the high country is a single green-black surface, unbroken to every horizon, rolling over ridges that were pushed up only a few thousand generations ago. The surface is the top of one kind of plant, *the Lid* as the people below call it, a felted, rubbery, cold-loving mat of interlocked stems and leaf-plates. It has spread across the fresh mountains the way frost spreads across a window. It is nowhere less than a body-length thick, and over the ridges it is thicker than a house.

Beneath it is the place worth describing.

## The Hollow

The mountains here are young and still rising. Their bones are raw: black basalt fins, scarps of shattered granite, fault-walls a thousand meters tall with edges that have not yet been rounded. The Lid grew across them faster than the rain could wear them down. It did not cover the valleys so much as span them, roping from crest to crest on its own woody cables, sagging between like a tent over a mountain range. Between the crests, under the sag, the air is open and enormous.

This is the hollow. From the valley floor the ceiling is two hundred meters up in the low places and more than a kilometer in the great basins. It is a dark, slowly heaving underside, ribbed and dripping. Almost nothing occupies the volume. You may walk for a day and see only the floor, the far walls, and the ceiling. Between them lies cold, wet, black air, and you can feel its size without seeing it.

The light is nearly none. What comes through the Lid is a gray-green rumor, a dimness like the inside of an eyelid, strongest at the thin seams where two sheets of the mat have not quite joined. There the light falls in long slanting blades that pass through fog and lie across the ground like fallen timbers. Elsewhere the dark is complete and lived in. The plants have made their own answer to it. Pale filament-lichens on the ridge walls glow a cold blue-white when touched, and the glow lingers along the path of a hand or a hoof for a slow count of thirty. The valley floors carry these trails, meandering lines of dying light where something walked.

## Water

It is damp rather than wet, and the dampness has a pattern. Rain seldom falls on the Lid, because the mountains are high and the air thin and dry above it. The water is made inside. Cold breath rises from the ground, from streams that seep out of the fresh fault-cracks, and it hits the underside of the Lid and condenses. The Lid drinks some of it and lets the rest gather in drip-tips, thousands of hanging spires, each a hand's length of stiff green cone. Water beads, swells, and falls.

So the hollow has weather without sky. Fog forms in the basins at night, if night means anything here, and burns off in the thin daylight seepage. Along the ceiling, in places where the underside has folded into long troughs, water gathers into ropes of runoff that finally fall as slender free columns, sometimes a hundred meters of open air before they hit the ground. Each column leaves a crater-pool. The valley floors are chains of these pools, linked by threadlike streams that run south, cold enough to ache the bones, clear as struck glass.

Some of these pools are the only open water for a day's travel. Around each is a ring of animals that keep an uneasy peace.

## The Ground

The floor is not soil. It is the Lid's own dropped matter, centuries of shed leaf-plates and cast filaments, packed into a springy, black, tea-smelling peat. Walking on it is quiet, and one sinks to the ankle. It is threaded with the white hair of the Lid's roots, which are not roots but *descenders*: ropes of living stem, thick as a thigh, that drop from the ceiling and plant themselves in the floor. Where a descender lands it thickens and becomes a pillar. In the older parts of the hollow these pillars stand in loose colonnades, hundreds of meters apart, each ribbed and dripping, each with a gathering of small life around its base. They are the nearest thing to trees. They hold up the sky, and they are how the sky feeds.

Between the pillars there are huge stretches of nothing. There is black peat, fog, a rare boulder fallen from a scarp, and the echo of your own step.

## Sound

The dark is loud. This is the strangeness people come to expect and never quite get used to.

The Lid is a drum. Every drip that hits its underside, every gust that moves across its top, every fault tremor that runs through the young mountains, all of it sets the great membrane ringing. The valleys hum with a low, never-ending note that changes with the wind, a bass chord you feel in the sternum before you hear it. Rain-columns hit the pools with a sound like torn cloth. The drip-tips tick and patter in a vast irregular rhythm, like applause heard from a mile off.

Over this bed of sound, the animals call. They call because they must, since sight is useless. The whole fauna of the hollow is a fauna of voices:

- **Bellcrakes**: tall, stilt-legged grazers, moss-gray and gaunt, that graze the peat in loose herds of thirty. Each carries a hollow crest of horn on its skull and sounds a single sustained tone through it, tuned to its own herd. In the fog you hear the herds pass like distant church bells moving across a field.
- **Ceiling-gliders**, pale membrane-winged things the size of a forearm, hundreds together, that live on the underside of the Lid and drop off it to glide from spire to spire. They click. A flock in flight is a rattling storm overhead, and you feel the small wind of it.
- **Drip-wolves**, long, low, cold-blooded-looking mammals with luminous eye-rings, hunting the bellcrake herds by listening for the one that has stopped calling. They move in silence and in that world silence is the hunting sign. A herd goes quiet all at once when they are near.
- **Peat-choristers**, small burrowers, frog-like, whose evening chorus rises from the ground in a great wave. It starts at one end of a valley and runs to the other in about a minute, so that the whole floor seems to breathe out.
- **The Lid-tenders**, slow multi-legged creatures, big as ponies, that walk the ceiling upside down, grazing the underside of the mat and trimming it. They are almost never seen, but their hooked feet leave rows of scars and their dung falls in wet, steaming rain. The pillars around them grow fatter.

The fauna is abundant, but the volume is so great that it appears sparse. A herd is a rumor in the fog. A glider flock is a shift in the dark. Numbers exist, and they are dispersed.

## Motion

Nothing stands still for long.

The mountains are young enough to move. Small quakes come every few hours, tremor-shivers that set the entire Lid ringing and shake dust and seed-flakes down in a slow, glittering fall. Larger quakes crack the ridge-faces and open new seeps, and the Lid, sensing the change, sends out pale green shoots to bridge each new gap within a season. A collapse can drop a hundred meters of ceiling. When it does, a shaft of true light stabs down, and for a few days the floor beneath it blooms in wild, savage color, red flowers and white ferns and ground-crawling vines that burn through their lives before the Lid closes over again. Animals come from days away to stand in that light.

Every day the fog rises and falls. Every season the drip-tips swell with a different chemistry, and the color of the underside shifts from bruise-purple to olive to a sick pale yellow. Every year something comes down: a torn-away piece of the Lid the size of a lake, tumbling slowly through the dark, or a cornice of snow from the ridge-tops above, which sifts through the seams and lands as a thin, brief white on the black peat.

The cold does not vary. It stays a few degrees above freezing in the hollow, and frost sits on the scarps and the tops of the pillars, growing in fern shapes.

## People

There are people here, few, and they leave few marks.

The builders are small bands, a dozen or so, who follow the bellcrake herds along the valleys. They have no metal. Their tools are chipped basalt, bone, and the Lid's own materials: cord twisted from descender-fiber, waterproof sheets peeled from leaf-plates, pitch boiled from the drip-tips. They build little. Where a band camps for a season, they raise a small round shelter of bent stems and leaf-plate, chinked with peat, with a smoke-hole that leads nowhere because they seldom burn anything. Fire in the hollow is a dangerous thing. The Lid is damp but it is fuel, and the old stories tell of a burning that ran across the roof for a year.

Instead they carry the blue lichen in clay pots and keep its cold light.

Other marks are stranger, and older. On the ridge-faces, out of reach, are lines of small **cairn-stacks** of black stone, each balanced on the next, set at intervals along the traverse routes. No living band remembers who made them. They are stacked with care, and they mark something, perhaps the safe way through the fault-country, perhaps a count of the dead. Elsewhere there is a single ruined hut, older than the band-lore, made of basalt slabs and roofed with the bones of something enormous. A rope bridge of ancient fiber crosses a narrow cleft. The band that uses it re-twists it every year and does not say why it is there.

They do not go up. The top of the Lid is the world of wind and thin air and killing cold, and nothing is known about it but stories: that it is a plain of endless soft green, that light falls on it in full, that it is where the plants keep their flowers.

## Character

The place is a cathedral built by something that does not know it is one. It is huge and empty, resonant, dripping, alive on every surface. The mountains are still raw beneath their green and could crack open again. The fog stands in the basins. A thin light drifts down through the seams, some pool is ringing with a falling thread of water, and far off in the dark a herd sounds its long slow chord and is answered from somewhere across the valley by another.

Between the calls, the silence is never quite silence. It is the slow enormous sound of a whole roof, held up in the dark, breathing.
