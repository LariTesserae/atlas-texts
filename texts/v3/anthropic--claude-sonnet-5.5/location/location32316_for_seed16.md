# The Chorus Hollow

From the salt flat above, there is nothing to see. The plain is white, flat, and cracked into hand-sized plates that curl at the edges in the heat. Somewhere in that whiteness is a crooked seam where the crust has sagged, and under the seam is a room the size of a small town. The room is one long, low bowl, sunk about forty meters below the flat and roofed over by the same crust that hides it.

## The Roof

The roof is a skin of gypsum and salt, thick in some places and thin as horn in others. Where it thins, light comes through as a dull amber, the color of a lamp behind tea. The light is dim, and it moves. When the sun crosses the thin places, pale coins slide slowly over the floor, and everything they touch flushes for a moment. Between the coins the Hollow sits in a brown, warm dusk.

Hanging from the roof are selenite stalactites, long and faintly furred, and thousands of thread-thin mineral straws. They are bone-dry. Nothing has dripped from them for a hundred thousand years, but they hum when the air moves.

## The Air

It is very hot, the heat of a closed kiln. The floor exhales it. Cracks in the bowl's bottom vent dry gas, dense and faintly sweet, smelling of struck flint and warm resin. Nothing here is damp. The air scours the nose and lips, and after an hour the tongue feels like felt.

The vents do not run steadily. The whole Hollow breathes on a cycle of roughly forty minutes. During the long exhale, hot gas pours up from the floor, the fronds stand rigid, and the air shimmers. Then the pressure drops. The inhale draws a cooler, gritty wind in from unseen fissures at the bowl's edge. It is only a little cooler, but after the exhale it feels like mercy. Every living and built thing in the place has been shaped by this pulse.

## The Green

The vegetation is lush and it does not need water. It drinks heat and gas, and it bleeds a black, fragrant, tarry sap from the rock's own seeps. Nothing in it is watery. Leaves are leathery, waxed, and glossy as boot-toes. Stems are hard and hollow. Sap runs thick and dark in them, and where a stem is broken it beads like pitch.

There are three main forms.

**Flue-ferns** grow in dense stands out of the vent-cracks. Their fronds are the size of doors, rolled at the tips like unlit torches. In the exhale they unroll, stiffen, and lean toward the heat. In the inhale they curl shut with a dry, ratcheting rattle, like thousands of fingers closing on paper.

**Drum-canes** are hollow, jointed, and twice the height of a person. They grow in ranks along the old streets. Each is capped with a thin membrane that booms softly when air is driven across it. A grove of them at full exhale produces a slow, rolling, sub-audible pulse that you feel in the breastbone.

**Lantern-mats** are low, dark, spreading carpets, black-green and slick, that cover the shaded flagstones. They do not glow, but in the coin-lights their surfaces flash a metallic bronze, so that patches of the floor seem to blink.

Pods of every size hang in the canopy: fists, jugs, great woody urns. Every few minutes one of them dries past its limit and bursts with a crack like a snapped board. Seeds and a fine dark dust, the plant's spores, spray outward. Because the pods ripen at different rates, the Hollow is full of random sharp reports, a slow ragged applause that never quite stops.

## The City

The town is old, and it is built to be heard.

It is made of stacked stone and fired clay. The stone is quarried salt-rock, ochre and grey, dressed with hand tools and set without mortar, and the clay is baked into big pale bricks. Walls rise two or three storeys, stepped back like terraces. There are no arches of any subtlety, only corbelled vaults, each course leaning a little further in until the gap closes. Wherever it was needed, thick sheets of beaten copper have been nailed on, now gone the green of old coins.

The plan is dense at the center and then stops. A ring of houses and workshops surrounds a large open floor, a plaza too wide for the few structures around it. Beyond the ring, the streets thin into single lines of doorways, then into foundations, then into ordinary rubble and green. Most of the bowl is empty. Long stretches of flagstone lie with nothing on them but a mat of black leaves and the moving coins of light.

Erosion has settled everything. The stair-treads have been worn into shallow dishes. Corners are rounded into thumbs. Sand-blast from the inhale winds has scoured the west faces of walls to a smooth polish and left the east faces sharp. Dry gullies have been cut across the plaza, fine-grained and branching like old veins, by centuries of gritty wind and heat-cracking, not by any flow. Lichen-like crusts, rust-orange and sulfur-yellow, feather the north sides of the stones. Nothing here is broken by violence. It has only been leaned on, gently and for a very long time, until it reached a shape it can keep.

## The Voices

The city's builders knew about the breath, and they made a great many instruments of it.

Clay flues are set into the wall-bases and run under the streets to the vents. Every house has a whistle-stack in its chimney, a set of graduated hollow bricks that sings when the exhale comes through. Copper horns as wide as a cart-wheel flare from the plaza's edge. Bundles of cane are lashed into organ-ranks on the roofs. Reed-tongues are riveted into slabs of bronze. When the exhale peaks, the whole town sounds at once, and the effect is more like weather than music. It is a roar of layered pitches, breathy, flat, and rasping, rising slowly over half a minute. The drum-canes thump under it, the pods crack over it, and the fronds rattle through it. Standing in the plaza, you hear it in your teeth.

Then the inhale comes. The organ-ranks sigh in reverse, a wheezing suck of air, the pitch sagging. The fronds click shut. For a moment the Hollow is nearly quiet, with only the seeds pattering down and the thin, high hum of the selenite straws.

Nobody seems to be playing the town. There are no animals, not a bird, an insect, or a lizard. No footprints mark the dust but the wind's. Whoever built the instruments is gone, or is not the kind of thing that walks. Yet the instruments are kept. The flues are clear. The reed-tongues are unbroken. Freshly cut cane has been lashed into the newest organ-rank, and its bindings are green.

## Rules of the Place

- **Nothing here is wet, and nothing needs to be.** The sap is oil, and the cycle of heat is the closest thing to a tide.
- **The breath sets the hour.** Everything (leaf-fold, pod-burst, pitch of the town) keys to the forty-minute pulse. If it ever stopped, the Hollow would go silent, and the green would shrivel within days.
- **The worn parts are the most used.** The brightest, smoothest stones lie along one path, from the western stair through the plaza to a low door in the eastern wall. Something has been taking that route for a very long time.
- **The roof is thinning.** In the east, a coin of light has grown larger over the years. At that spot the crust is almost transparent. When it opens, the Hollow will get its first sun in a hundred thousand years, and the chorus will change.

At the far end, past the last foundations, the floor rises to a bare shelf of rock and the low door stands open. Inside it is darker, hotter, and louder, and the breath from it smells of resin and flint.
