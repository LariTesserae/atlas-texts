# The Unfinished Shelf

The land stands at about four thousand meters, but nothing here feels like a mountain. It is a tableland, a raised floor of raw rock that runs out to the horizon in long, shallow swells. Its edges are still sharp. Fault scarps cut across it in clean lines, and the cliff faces they expose have never been rained on. The rock is dark, close-grained, and slightly glassy, like basalt that cooled a moment ago. Nothing has rounded it, and the crests of the ridges are still knife-thin. Talus has not formed. Below each scarp the ground is simply level again, as if the cliff had been sliced from a block and the offcut removed.

There is no water in any state, and there never has been. The air is dry enough to feel faintly mineral on the tongue, like a struck match or a warm coin. The sky is a pale, clean amber-blue, and the sun is ordinary in size and brightness. It is a warm day with no trace of cold in the shadows, roughly the temperature of skin. It is always this warm day.

## The Green

The plateau is lush anyway, and the lushness is the strangest thing about it. Thick, overlapping growth covers the shallow basins and climbs the gentle slopes in dense stands. It is a deep bottle green, shading to oxidized copper at the tips. Nothing in it needs water. The plants run on a heavy, amber, oily fluid the color of weak tea, which they draw up from seams in the rock. Where the rock is cut, you can see it seeping in beads. It is thick and slow, and it smells of warm resin and hot metal.

The plants have a structure I can only call mechanical, though nothing here was built. Each one has a **stalk** like a jointed tube, ringed with collars that flex and settle as the internal fluid moves. At the top is a **vane**, a broad, ribbed, fanlike leaf, slightly cupped, that tracks the sun in small, patient increments. Some stands have grown into **banks** of hundreds of vanes, all tilted to the same angle. From a ridge, the whole basin looks like a field of turned dishes.

This is the industrial quality of the place. The plants do not merely live. They **process**. They pull mineral out of the rock through root-mats, and inside their stalks they sort it, in steps you can see through translucent joints. Heavy particles sink through a chain of chambers, and pale crystalline grains settle out at the bottom. The plants then **extrude** the grains as fine, regular pellets, which drop and pile around the base in neat cones of gray-white salt-metal. Every stand has its own heap, and every heap is very much the same size. It is manufacture without a manufacturer: throughput, sorting, output, and a floor of accumulated product, all grown from seed.

## Sound

It is quiet, but not silent. Across the whole shelf runs a faint **ticking**, dry and irregular, like a cooling engine block. It comes from the collar-joints as fluid pushes through them. Standing among the stalks, you hear a soft, low **thrum**, a pressure-hum just under tone, and now and then a small **click-and-sigh** as a valve seats. Beyond the stand the sound thins to almost nothing. The wind is light and steady, and it whispers over the sharp rock edges with a thin, reedy note. Nothing calls, because nothing is there to call. There are no insects, no birds, and no grazers. The green has never been eaten. The only sounds are the plants' own workings.

## Ground

Between the stands, the bare rock is unsoftened. There is no soil, and instead a thin **grit** of pellet-dust and fine glass lies in the hollows. Underfoot it crunches lightly. The rock surfaces show flow-lines, frozen ripples, and gas vesicles as clean as the day they set. Cracks are narrow and crisp. In places the rock has fractured into **columns**, tall and hexagonal and unweathered, with faces bright enough to throw back the sun.

The plateau is divided into wide, shallow terraces, each a few meters higher than the last, stepping up toward a distant, slightly higher rim. The terraces are fault-made. The rock plainly moved and stopped. Each riser is a fresh face, and the green grows right up to its foot and then stops, as if it respected the edge.

## Rules of this place

**Nothing progresses.** This is the world's central rule, and the longer you stay the more certain it feels. Everything about the shelf says *just formed*: the sharp edges, the bare cliffs, the plants still filling their first basins. But it does not go on forming. The scarps do not crumble. The pellet heaps grow to a certain size and then stay that size, the extrusion balanced exactly against some slow reabsorption at the base. The fluid rises and falls in the joints at a constant rate. The vanes track the sun and reset. It is a place stuck in the instant of becoming, held in a perfect and tireless balance. If you came back in a thousand years, you would find the same cone at the same height and the same edge on the same rock.

**Emptiness holds.** The stands are separated by wide stretches of bare rock, sometimes a kilometer or more. The horizon is open. You can walk for an hour and see only a few dark-green islands of working plants and a great deal of clean, cut stone. The scale is regional. A person could cross a valley of it in a day and never feel the far edge close in.

**Nothing has been made.** There are no walls, roads, cairns, or tools. The industry is entirely botanical, and the only regularity is the regularity of growth. If you feel watched, it is only by the ranks of vanes, turning very slightly after you as you pass, following the sun and not you.

## Toward evening

The light does not change. This is a world of noon, or something so close to it that the difference does not register: a level, warm, amber brightness with shadows short and unmoving. The vanes hold their angle. The ticking goes on. Heaps of pale pellets glow a little at the base of each stalk, and the rim of the far terrace is a thin dark line under a sky the color of thin honey.

You could stay a long time and it would not become a different place. It has finished becoming, and it has settled, sharp-edged and green and humming, into the moment before anything wears away.
