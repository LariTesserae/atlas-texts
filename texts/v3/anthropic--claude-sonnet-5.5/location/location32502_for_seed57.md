# The Cistern Under the Fold

From outside, it is nothing. A seam in fresh basalt at the foot of a low bank, a crack you could lay your forearm in, weeping cold water into a bed of horsetail. The rock there was broken open recently, in the way geology counts it. The edges are still sharp enough to open a palm. No lichen has taken hold. No rain has rounded a single corner.

Behind the seam is a room the size of a pantry, and it is full to the ceiling with water and with life.

## The room

The water is very cool, close enough to the temperature of skin that after a minute you cannot say where you stop. It has no surface. The chamber is flooded to the roof, and the roof is a low, fractured slab furred with a pale moss that grows downward, in slow strands, like hair in a bath.

There is almost no light. What there is comes from the plants themselves, a grudging blue-green at the tips of the fronds, a glow so faint that you see it best by looking slightly away. Your eyes fill in the rest. The dark is not empty. It is thick with shapes that only half agree with each other.

Nothing has room. Ribbon-weed, fan-leaf, beaded runners, and a broad dark kelp-like thing with leaves like wet felt all crowd the chamber shoulder to shoulder. Stems lean on stems. Roots grip other roots. A fern has rooted in the fork of a different fern, and something else has rooted in that one. To move, you part the growth with your hands and it closes behind you like a curtain settling. The whole room is one slow, patient press of green, and its only real pressure is the pressure of being there.

## Where it stops making sense

The room is too big.

You can feel this before you can prove it. You push through the fronds along what should be the far wall, six feet from the seam, and keep pushing. The wall is not there. Your fingertips find rock, real and sharp, but it is a different rock, farther off, and when you turn back the seam is a grey slit much too distant. You have swum perhaps two body lengths. The slit looks like a lit window across a field.

The chamber does this unevenly and without malice. Distances are held loosely here, like a rumor. A frond that brushes your left cheek may, when you reach for it, be an arm's length away, or ten. Following a single runner hand over hand, you find it loops through the same three knots of root, and each time the knots are older: thicker, darker, more encrusted, as if you were moving upstream through years instead of space. Follow it far enough and the knots are young again.

Up and down are unreliable as well. The moss on the ceiling sometimes grows toward you and sometimes away, and once or twice the whole chamber has tilted quietly, like a held breath let go. Nothing falls, because nothing is ever loose. Everything is already leaning on something else.

Sound comes from the wrong places. A soft tick, water on stone, seems to come from inside your own ear. It is answered a moment later by the same tick, slightly lower, from somewhere that cannot exist. The room is mostly quiet, but the quiet has an edge like a held note. Sometimes a long, low hum passes through the rock, too deep to hear, and the fronds all bow toward the slit and then slowly straighten. It happens perhaps once an hour. Nothing else marks time here.

## Marks

Someone, or something, has been here, though *someone* may be the wrong word.

Deep in the growth, where the room has stretched itself furthest, there is a ring of stones, seven of them, each about the size of a loaf, stacked in a low neat wall around nothing. The gap in the middle is packed with the same felt-leaved kelp as everywhere else, but the kelp grows in a perfect circle, and in the center there is one bare stem, leafless, black, polished smooth.

Farther on there is a lintel: two upright slabs and a cap stone, the height of a child, standing free in the water. Nothing is behind it. You can swim around it. But if you swim through it, the water on the other side is a degree colder and tastes faintly of iron, and the glow of the plants is a different color, closer to amber. You come back through and it is blue-green again. The lintel is the only object in the chamber with moss on its stone.

There is a spoon-shaped thing, carved from something like bone, wedged in a crack. Its bowl is worn thin, as if scooped through centuries. It is far too fresh a place for centuries.

Nobody made these in a way the room admits. The marks seem to have grown, in the way the plants grew, from pressure and slow attention.

## Inhabitants

Fauna is rare. It is easy to swim through the whole place and meet nothing.

There is a snail, translucent, the size of a thumbnail, that crawls the underside of the broad leaves in a track so slow that its trail has become a permanent etched line. There are small pale shrimp, blind and unbothered, that gather in the roots in clusters of three or four and do not scatter when you pass.

And there is, or may be, a larger thing. You never see it. You see the fronds part in a long slow line a body-length wide, in the dark, in a place where you are not. You feel the water shift against your ribs as something passes. The pale shrimp all face the same way for a while afterward.

## Slow change

Everything here changes slowly enough that you could mistake it for stillness. The fronds lean a little more toward the seam each day. The rock, fresh as it is, is still settling, and every so often a flake the size of a fingernail lets go of the wall and drifts down through the water with the patience of a leaf. The kelp thickens. The moss on the ceiling lengthens by a hair. The chamber, if it can be said to have a size, is still deciding how large to be.

Nothing is in a hurry. The place is young, and it has already learned that it has time.

When you leave, and you can, by following the cold thread of current back toward the grey slit, the distance closes as you go, apologetically, like a hand withdrawn. You surface in the horsetail with the sharp rock at your back and the ordinary evening over the bank. Your palms are green. You are not sure how long you were gone. And the crack, from outside, is still exactly the width of your forearm.
