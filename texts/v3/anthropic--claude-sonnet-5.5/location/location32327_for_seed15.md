# The Bronze Cistern

The place is a room the size of a village threshing floor, sunk under a hill and filled almost to the ceiling with warm water. Someone cut it out of pale tuff and lined it with fired brick, and the work is so recent that the chisel marks still have their burrs. Corners are crisp enough to cut a thumb. The mortar is the color of wet bone and has not yet begun to bloom with mineral. Nothing here has had time to look old.

## Light

The light is the first thing you notice, and it is nearly too much. A shaft the width of a doorway runs up through the hill to a hole in the sky. At its top, a polished bronze disc tilts on a wooden yoke. Lower down, a second disc, and a third at a bend, each one catching the sun and throwing it downward. By the time it reaches the water it is a hard white column. The surface takes it and breaks it into shivering nets that ripple over every wall, so the whole chamber flickers with a bright, moving lattice. Below the surface the light goes green-gold, clear to a great depth. You can see the floor forty feet down as if through glass.

When clouds cross the sky far above, the whole room dims in a slow exhale, and the lattices slacken and pool. Then the sun returns and the walls blaze.

## Water

The water is hot-spring warm, the temperature of a bath someone has just drawn. It comes in through a bronze-lipped channel on the east wall, fed by a vent in the rock, and it leaves by a low sluice on the west. It is never still. A slow current turns through the chamber in one great loop, and dust-fine sediment drifts along it like slow smoke. The water tastes faintly of iron and eggs. It is clear because nothing lives in it to cloud it.

The top few hand-spans of the ceiling stand clear of the water, a slot of trapped air. It is humid, hot, and echoing, and it rings like the inside of a bell.

## Structures

The room is built in terraces that step down into the deep. Each terrace is a broad brick shelf with a stone lip, and most are empty. The density is low. Large distances of clean floor separate the things that were made.

- **The water-wheels.** Three wooden wheels, each taller than a person, stand submerged on axles of dark-stained oak. The current turns them. They turn slowly and without pause, and the wood groans and knocks as it goes. The pegs are still pale, freshly whittled, and the pitch sealing the joints is still glossy and black.
- **The hammer-rack.** Cams on the wheel axles lift and drop a row of mallets, each headed with a lump of lead-bound stone. The mallets strike hanging bronze plates, and the plates ring. The sound travels through water as a dull, immense *bonnng*, then bounces off the brick and returns as a shivering echo in the air slot above. The rhythm is uneven, three cams against seven, then eleven, so the pattern nearly repeats and never quite does. It is loud enough that you feel it in your teeth.
- **The sluice-gates.** Wooden panels slide in stone grooves, lifted and lowered by chains that run over crude pulleys. A float-and-counterweight arrangement opens them as the level rises and shuts them as it falls, so the chamber breathes in long cycles. Twice a day, or near enough, the gates clatter and the water surges. Silt lifts off the floor and the light through it turns briefly to amber fog.
- **The stairs.** A staircase of wide, shallow steps descends into the water from a doorway in the north wall. The door is a lintel and two jambs, hand-dressed, with a single line of scored marks above it that may be a tally or may be a sentence. The steps go down and down, past the waterline and past the terraces, and end at the floor.

## Vegetation

Growth is sparse and orderly, confined to places where people, or the rules of the place, allowed it. Along the sunlit terraces, planted in troughs of packed clay, grow rows of pale green ribbon-weed. It waves in the current, all leaning the same way. There is a little green felt on the brick just under the light column, thin and bright. Nothing else grows. There are no roots in the rock and no vines on the walls, because there is no soil here, only made surfaces.

## Sounds and other rhythms

Beyond the bronze plates, the chamber is full of noise. The wheels knock. The gates shudder. Water pours through the inlet in a continuous hiss and drains through the outlet in a low gurgle. Air bubbles escape from the vent in irregular gouts and climb the water in silver strings, popping against the ceiling. The chorus never quiets, and the echo makes it fuller. Speaking underwater is impossible, and speaking in the air slot is useless because the ringing swallows the words. Anyone who worked here would have learned to communicate with gestures and painted signs.

## Rules of the place

1. **Everything here moves because the water moves.** No fire, no hand, no animal. The mechanism has no keeper now, or the keeper is elsewhere.
2. **The light is borrowed.** If one bronze mirror in the shaft slips its yoke, the chamber will go dark, and the wheels will keep turning in the dark.
3. **Nothing has settled yet.** The bricks are unweathered, the pitch is fresh, and the mineral crust has barely started along the sluice. In a hundred years the place will be furred and soft and half-lost. For now it is exact, bright, and ringing, a tool that has only just been finished and set going.

## A note on what's absent

There are no fish, no snails, no insects skating on the surface, no birds in the shaft. Nobody is here either, though the tool marks on the walls are so plain that you keep glancing over your shoulder for a mason. A wooden mallet lies on the fourth terrace, one side worn dark where a palm gripped it. It is the only sign the makers left of themselves.

At the bottom, on the floor of the deepest terrace, one last bronze plate hangs from a beam. No cam reaches it, and no mallet strikes it. But the current passes through it, and now and then, when the surge from the gates comes just right, the plate hums on its own, one soft note under all the racket.
