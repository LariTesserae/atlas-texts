# The Standing Organ

You come up onto it through cloud. The last of the climb is a slope of wet moss so deep your foot goes in to the shin, and then the ground levels and the cloud thins to a warm, even haze. You are three thousand meters up, and it is as warm as a greenhouse.

The plateau is a plain of shallow water and matted growth, many kilometers across. Rising from it, far apart, are the structures.

## The structures

Nothing here was carved or stacked. Everything is grown, and everything is *architecture*. A tower of fused stems rises out of the marsh, as wide at the base as a cathedral nave. Its walls are thousands of trunks the thickness of your thigh, grown together until the seams are only shallow creases in the bark. The wood is green-gold and slightly translucent when the light comes through. It is smooth and unweathered, with the tight, waxy finish of something that has never seen a second season. Wind has not yet had time to work on it. Nothing is cracked, pitted, or lichened. The whole plateau looks like it was finished this morning.

The towers stand a half-hour's walk apart. Between them run causeways, long arched spines of braided living cane raised a body's height above the water. Their surfaces are flat and slightly springy, with a grain like woven mats. Some end at a tower's door. Others simply stop over open water, cleanly, as though the thought behind them was completed. Terraces of broad-leaved plants step down around each tower in perfect concentric rings, each ring holding a hand's depth of clear water that spills to the next through notches in the leaf-lips. There are no gardeners. Each notch is exactly where it needs to be.

Between the towers is a great deal of nothing: open flooded meadow, low reeds, pale mirror-water under the haze. The emptiness is part of the design. Each structure has room around it like a held breath.

## Water

The water is everywhere and never rises or falls. It weeps from the tower walls in fine threads that run down the grain and vanish into mossy collars at the base. Each causeway has a gutter along its spine, and the gutters all slope gently toward the same distant low place. The air is close to saturated. Your sleeves darken and your hair beads. Every leaf carries drops that never quite fall, held at the tip by a tension you can watch tremble.

The water is warm as a bath in the shallows and tastes faintly of cut stem, green and sweet. It is very clear. Looking down, you see that the marsh floor is also structure, a lattice of pale roots laid in a regular weave, like the underside of a basket.

## The sound

It is loud, and it never stops.

Every tower is hollow, and the hollows are tuned. Inside, tall vertical chambers of different bores fill and drain through the wood's own capillaries, and wind and dripping water excite them. The towers *sound*. Each holds one sustained note, a huge low breathing tone you feel in your sternum before you hear it. The nearest tower hums a deep, slightly rough tone. Another, far off, sings a fifth above it. Farther still are others, so many that the plateau is filled with one immense chord, slow beats drifting between the near-unison pitches like slow waves against a shore.

Under the chord runs the percussion: the ticking of drops into leaf-cups, the hollow *tok* of water entering resonant bulbs along the causeways, the hush of spillways. Each causeway rings under your feet like a struck board, and your steps add a dull drumming to the piece. It is not quiet anywhere. The sound has a shape, with louder places near towers and thinner places in the meadows, but it has no silence.

The chord does not change. You will notice this after an hour, and then it will unsettle you. The pitch never drifts, the beats never speed up, and no gust alters the harmony. Whatever holds it, the water level in each chamber and the tension in each wall, is balanced so exactly that nothing moves it.

## The rule of the place

This is a frozen equilibrium. The plants grow, but only exactly as fast as old parts die and are absorbed, so every tower stays the same size, every ring holds the same water, and every leaf sheds and replaces itself at the same rate. Look closely and you can see the process. A stem browns at its tip, a pale new shoot pushes up beside it, and the shoot takes the dead one's shape and place. The tower is a standing wave of growth. The wood is new and the design is ancient. The whole thing has been renewing itself unchanged for a time you have no way of counting.

Light behaves the same way. The haze holds it at an even, pearly brightness, without clear shadows, with no visible sun and no sense of noon or afternoon. The warmth doesn't vary. The plateau has no weather in the usual sense, only this steady saturated climate.

## Inhabitants

Life other than plants is rare, and it lives inside the architecture. A small pale frog with a translucent throat sits in the leaf-cups and is the only visible animal on the terraces. It sings nothing, as if the towers had already taken all the available notes. In the hollows of the causeways live long-bodied beetles the color of old ivory, which you sometimes hear scrabbling under your feet. Once, far off, something tall and heron-like stands in the shallows between two towers. It stays completely still for as long as you watch, and you never see it move.

## Inside a tower

The doors are gaps where the trunks part, framed by curling stems like an eyelid. Inside, the air is cooler and wetter, and the note is enormous. You stand in a round green room whose walls rise into darkness, threaded with a thousand small channels where water climbs upward against gravity, glinting. The floor is a shallow pool with a raised walkway of woven root. At the center a pillar of denser wood, as thick as a ship's mast, hums so strongly that the water around it shivers into a standing pattern of fine concentric ripples. Those ripples never change, and they seem to be the point of the whole place. Everything else, towers and causeways and terraces, only exists to keep them steady.

You can stay as long as you like. When you leave, the plateau will be exactly as you found it, and it will be exactly like that whenever anyone next comes.
