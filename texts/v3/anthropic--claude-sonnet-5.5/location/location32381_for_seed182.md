# The Steamworks at Low Ember

The town sits at the bottom of a collapse that is younger than its oldest inhabitants. Sixty years ago the ground gave way over a lava tube system and dropped a bowl of country eighty meters into the dark. The walls of the bowl have not yet learned to be soft. They are basalt and pale tuff, snapped clean, with edges you could shave with, and the ledges are still stacked at the angles they broke. Small rockfalls happen, but so rarely that each one is remembered and named.

The whole place is perhaps four hundred meters across. Everything in it is crowded against everything else.

## The Light

The sky is a torn slot, long and crooked, far overhead. Its light comes down as one slanting blade that crosses the town over the course of a day, and it stays a blade because the air below is thick with vapor. Where it lands, the leaves blaze an almost painful green. Everywhere else the light is dim and amber, from filament lamps strung along the terraces on cloth-wrapped wire. The lamps are electric, fed by small turbines, and they burn low to save the current. Nobody has ever quite said *for whom*.

Between blade and lamplight there are long stretches of green-brown gloom, like the bottom of a pond seen from beneath.

## The Heat

It is hot the way the inside of a mouth is hot: constant, wet, with no relief. The heat rises from the floor. Under the town the old tubes still hold a slow fire, and the ground is warm enough that the paving stones are comfortable to lie on in the "cold" hours and unbearable to kneel on at any other time. Iron rails are wrapped in rag. Doorknobs are wooden or not present. The town's few thermometers stopped being consulted years ago, since the reading never changes.

## The Green

Everything that can hold a root holds one. Tree ferns as thick as chimneys grow up through the streets, and the buildings have been built around them, so trunks pass through parlors and out the roofs. Vines with leaves the size of doors hang from the cavern walls and drape the terraces in slow green waterfalls. Moss covers every north-facing surface (there is no north; the moss has picked one anyway). Orchid-like things with fleshy, translucent petals grow on the pipework, feeding on the leaks.

The plant life is lush, but it is not wild. It is *negotiated*. Every stem near the machinery is trained, tied, or pruned back from a joint or a flywheel, and there are clippers hung on hooks at every corner.

One oddity: the plants lean toward the boilers as well as toward the light. Ferns near the largest boiler house grow curved, reaching for the brick. The engineers say it is heat. The older gardeners say the plants are *listening*, and nobody has a better theory.

## The Buildings

The town is built in layers, and each layer is a different decade's idea of sturdiness.

At the bottom are the **foundry halls**, low and vaulted, made of black brick fired from the floor's own clay. Above them come the **tenements**: narrow, timber-framed, leaning together over lanes barely wide enough for two people carrying baskets. Above those, on the ledges cut into the walls, are **hanging streets**, boardwalks bolted directly into the rock, with houses no deeper than a single room, their backs literally the cavern wall. Rope bridges cross the gaps. Pulleys run laundry, coal, mushrooms, and children's toys between levels.

Roofs are corrugated tin gone green with verdigris, or slate, or thatch of dried frond. Rain barrels sit on every one, though it seldom rains in the usual way.

## The Water

The water is *made*. Hot air rises off the town, hits the cool stone of the ceiling slot, condenses, and comes back as a fine, warm drizzle that falls unpredictably on one street and not the next. People carry wide leaves rather than umbrellas. Under the eaves, condensation drips in patient rhythms, and each roof has its own pitch, a small tuned percussion.

Along the lowest lane runs a channel of warm, milky, mineral-rich water, the only "river," about two meters wide, slow, and sulfur-yellow at the edges. It powers three waterwheels in sequence before vanishing into a grate in the rock. No one has found where it comes out.

## The Machines

This is an industrial town of the steam-and-belt age, but a quiet one. Its engines are geothermal: boilers fed by pipes sunk into the hot floor, with no coal and no need to be loud. Big low beam engines rock slowly in their housings, thick with lagging and moss. Belts, shafts, and gear trains run overhead in the foundry lanes, and they are muffled by growth, so everything creeps along with a soft, damp *thuck… thuck…* The town makes cables, glass, ceramics, and rubberized cloth, and it smelts small quantities of a copper-rich ore from a seam in the east wall.

Workers move deliberately. Nobody shouts. It is too hot for speed, and a fast machine in a packed lane is a fatal thing.

## The Sound

The soundscape is mostly quiet: the beam engines' slow beat, dripping, the low hush of steam in pipes, and a soft rustle of leaves against each other in air that seems to have no wind. Occasionally there's a distant metallic *tock* of cooling iron, or a murmur of talk from a doorway, or a chime of a glass wind-catcher, and then the quiet closes over it again.

## The Strangeness

**The breath.** Roughly every eleven hours, the whole bowl exhales. Air moves upward through the slot with a long low sigh, and every leaf in town turns its face a few degrees toward the sound. Then, hours later, it inhales, and the leaves turn back. The townspeople keep their schedules by it, and no clocks are needed. Shifts change at the exhale. Nobody knows what breathes.

**The tallow-flowers.** On a few old terraces grow tall pale blooms that give off a faint cold light, slightly brighter than the lamps around them. They are never picked. They are fertilized, in a ritual no one explains, by hand, with brushes made of moth-wing.

**The slow lean.** The buildings on the eastern side lean by perhaps a finger's width a year toward the collapse's center. The town moves with it: braces are renewed, floors re-leveled, doorframes re-cut. The lean is not seen as a threat. It is seen as the town *settling in*.

## The Fauna

Animals are rare. There are blind cave crickets, pale as candle wax, that live in the wall cracks and sing so faintly you hear them only when you stop breathing. There are salamanders in the warm channel, translucent enough to show the small dark beat of their hearts. There are large gray moths that roost in the lamp-shades and drift in the amber light like slow ash. Nothing else. People keep a few caged finches to warn against bad air, and count each one as family.

## The Rules

- No open flame below the second terrace, because the tubes leak gas.
- No cutting the tree ferns that pass through floors without asking the household beneath.
- Leave the tallow-flowers alone.
- Do not follow the warm channel into the grate.
- At the exhale, stop what you are doing for a moment and let the leaves turn.

Everyone keeps all of them. The place is young, and everyone in it seems to sense that it has not finished deciding what it will be.
