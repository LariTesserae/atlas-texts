# The Warm Dark Commons

The water here is the temperature of a body. You would not feel where you end and it begins, and that is the first thing the place does to a visitor. It is warm, slightly thick with dissolved minerals, and it tastes faintly of iron and eggs. It stands about two hundred meters over a broad drowned plain, and no light from the surface has reached it in a hundred thousand years. What light there is, is made here.

## The ground

The plain is low, a wide shallow bowl, so gentle that the slope can only be read by the way silt migrates. Long ago it was a river delta, and its old channels still braid across the floor as shallow sunken trenches, softened and rounded, their banks slumped into ramps. Sediment is a fine gray-tan flour that lifts at a touch and settles in slow curtains. Under it the bedrock shows in places: pale limestone, ribbed and pitted, worn into cobbled domes.

Warmth comes from below. Along the old fault lines the plain leaks: seeps of heated water shimmer up out of the floor, bending the dark like glass. Around each seep there is a rim of mineral crust, yellow and rust-orange, layered like a cut onion. The seeps are the reason anything is here.

## The city

Everything built here was built by things that are alive or were.

The largest structures are the **stacks**, calcified towers grown by colonial animals over tens of thousands of years. They rise thirty, fifty, ninety meters from the plain in clusters, and their surfaces are so perforated that they look like sponge, honeycomb, or pumice. Water moves through them constantly. Each has its own slow internal current, entering low and exiting high, so a stack behaves like a chimney turned into a lung. The old ones have been worn round by ten thousand years of current. Their corners are gone, their tops are smoothed into shoulders, and their flanks are gently fluted where water has run the same course for ages. They look less constructed than settled, like a town that has stopped arguing with its landscape.

Between the stacks are the **lanes**: cleared channels of hard-packed shell and mucus-cemented grit, kept open by armies of small grazers that scour them and by the tread and drag of larger animals. Lanes branch, cross, and dive under arches where two stacks have grown together. Some are a body-width across. A few are broad as avenues, lined with rows of upright tube-dwellers set shoulder to shoulder like a colonnade of fingers.

The lower city is burrow-work. The mud of the plain is honeycombed with warrens: mounds of cemented pellets, chimneys of pressed silt, ridged mud collars around burrow mouths, each collar shaped by its owner's particular gait. In the densest quarters, called nothing by anyone, the floor is a mosaic of these collars, tens per square meter, some breathing out threads of cloudy water in slow rhythm.

At the edges the city thins into **outskirts**: single stacks standing apart, isolated mounds, hermit-crab middens, a low stony hill of shells stacked by some patient animal into a ring. Beyond those, the open plain resumes, and the fauna thins to wanderers.

## The dark

There is no sun to sort day from night, but the dark is not empty. Living light punctuates it. Small blue-green sparks kindle on the flanks of stacks when a current changes. Rows of pale lamps run along the bodies of passing fish like the windows of a slow train. A drifting jelly makes a dim violet cloud, and when something bumps it the light shivers outward in a ring. Mostly the lights are brief and quiet. Between them the water is a black so complete that the eye starts to invent color.

Some animals hunt with light and some hide from it. A few give off a red glow that most others can't see, and wait beside their prey in perfect concealment.

## Vegetation, such as it is

Nothing green grows. What passes for plant life is chemical: mats of pale bacterial felt that coat the seep rims in white and salmon-colored sheets, and slow tufts of filament-weed rooted in warm cracks, trailing a meter or two and combing the current. Around the seeps, tubeworms stand in dense stands like rows of red-tipped candles. They are the base of the whole city. The animals that build it eat what grows on the seeps, or eat those animals, or eat their leavings.

## Fauna

Abundant is a mild word. Every surface is occupied.

- **The stack-builders**: colonial, translucent, each a soft cup the size of a rice grain, lined up by the millions. They are the city's masons, laying down calcium a fraction of a millimeter each week. Their surfaces glimmer faintly at night, and a whole stack can pulse with it when disturbed.
- **Lane-sweepers**: flat, many-legged grazers, pale as candle wax, that move in loose lines along the avenues, eating the film that would otherwise close them. They are the city's only traffic, and they never stop.
- **Ghost eels**: long, colorless, slow. They thread through the stacks and lanes in search of the sleeping.
- **Great drifters**: broad, leaf-shaped animals, something between ray and jellyfish, that hang above the city in slow circuits with dim lanterns along their edges. Their shadows aren't visible, but the lit surfaces below them go dark in a passing wave.
- **Clickers**: small shrimp in the thousands, living in the stack pores, whose snapping claws make the only regular sound.
- **The Keepers**: the strangest presence, and the reason the place is a little unusual. They are large, slow, armored animals, broad as a door, that tend certain stacks. They do not build. They patrol, and they *repair*. A Keeper will go to a cracked stack and stand pressed against the break, secreting a pale paste from its underside that hardens into fresh calcite. They are found only on the oldest stacks, and their shells carry the same pitted texture as the walls they mend. Some have been staying at a single tower for centuries.

Predators are constant but unhurried: a spined fish darts from an arch into a school of glassy prey, and the school pinches shut and reopens. Scavengers wait beneath. Nothing is ever wasted for long.

## Sounds

Mostly quiet. The dominant sound is a low, textured hiss, the water moving through ten thousand pores. Above it, at intervals, come tiny sharp clicks from the shrimp, sometimes in ripples across a whole district as one startled group sets off the next. Rarely there is a groan far off, deep and long, as a stack settles or as gas breaks loose from a seep. It reaches you through the chest before the ears.

## Rhythms and rules

Over the whole plain, the water breathes. Every few hours, a slow surge of warmth moves through, driven by pressure in the seep network. It lifts the silt, turns the currents in the stacks around, and sets the drifters swinging. The animals have adjusted to it so exactly that the surge is the nearest thing to a clock. Grazers reverse direction before it arrives. Tubeworms retract a moment beforehand.

The rules the city runs on:

1. **Warmth is the only wealth.** Everything crowds toward the seeps, and the closer, the older and denser.
2. **Nothing is abandoned.** A dead stack is bored, colonized, and repurposed within a season.
3. **Silt is time.** The rate at which mud settles on a surface tells you how long it has been left alone, and the Keepers read it.

Far out on the plain, where the outskirts fade, the water cools very slightly and the dark deepens. You can turn and look back at the city, a low scatter of faint blue-green sparks in the black, breathing.
