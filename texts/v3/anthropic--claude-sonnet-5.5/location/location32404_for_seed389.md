# The Jackscrew City of Ombrel Dome

The mountain is still being born, and the city is trying to stay level on it.

Ombrel Dome rises to a height where the sky goes the dark blue of old ink at noon. You would expect cold up here, but the rock underneath is young and warm. A shallow body of magma sits below the summit plateau, and the whole upper mountain is slowly swelling over it like bread. The ground is hot enough to feel through boot soles. The air, thin and dry and lit by an unfiltered sun, sits around forty-five degrees in the shade, and there is very little shade.

## The Ground

The terrain is raw. The rock is black and rust-red basalt, fresh enough to hold the memory of flow: ropy crusts, frozen folds, pressure ridges like the wrinkles of a skin drawn tight. Between them lie beds of grey-violet ash and sheets of volcanic glass that flash when the sun catches them. Erosion has barely begun. The first rills are hand-width scratches in the ash, and a few gullies have deepened to the depth of a wrist. One real canyon, the Seam, is only three storeys deep, and its walls are so sharp they cut gloves. Nothing has had time to round off.

Nothing grows. No lichen, no moss, no grass. The only green is a thin, slick film of microbial mat in the drip channels where condensate runs warm and mineral-rich. It is bright as antifreeze and smells of boiled eggs. It is the closest thing to a garden the mountain has, and the city has learned not to scrub it out, because it eats the worst of the sulfur.

## The City

The city, Ombrel, covers the summit plateau and spills down three flanks in terraces. It is dense, built shoulder to shoulder, with brick-and-iron blocks stacked six and seven levels high and joined by gantries, pipe bridges, and stairs that switchback through the gaps between buildings. There are no open squares. There are only widenings in the alleys where three pipes cross.

Everything is iron and fired clay, the colors of the mountain. Roofs are corrugated and painted with pale lime, which reflects the sun and gives the city a chalky sheen from above. The walls are thick and the windows narrow. Awnings of woven glass-fiber, translucent amber, hang over almost every street, so that you walk in a hot honey-colored dimness.

The industry is slow and enormous. Steam is the first resource. Wells sunk into the dome tap superheated vapor, which drives turbines the size of grain silos. Second come the metals. Fumaroles lay down crusts of sulfur, copper salts, and a faint silver-grey arsenic-bearing bloom, and gangs in leather and wet cloth scrape these into carts. Furnaces are fed by hot rock rather than fuel. Rendering floors, casting halls, and drawing sheds each occupy a terrace, their chimneys venting pale plumes that lean all in one direction, downwind, like a field of combed hair.

## Water

Water is scarce and precious, and it comes as damp: as condensation, not rivers.

The steam wells release more vapor than the city can use, and the surplus rises in the mornings as fog. It never gets far. Thousands of condensing plates, tilted sheets of cold-soaked metal, hang from the ridges of the city. The metal is chilled by pipes carrying water pulled from deep shafts, and it beads at dawn. Runnels collect the beads into gutters, gutters into cisterns, and cisterns into the great stone tanks beneath the terraces. The sound of this is the city's quiet constant: a tick, a patter, a slow drip from ten thousand edges.

Real rain is rare, perhaps a dozen times a year. It falls warm out of a single fat cloud that forms above the dome, and most of it evaporates before touching the ground, trailing in grey veils that never land. When it does land on a roof, the whole city goes quiet to listen. Children are let out onto the gantries, and adults stand in doorways with their eyes closed.

## The Strange Rule: Keeping Level

The mountain swells. It has swelled for as long as the city has existed, at the speed of a fingernail growing, but unevenly, tilting one flank and then another. Ombrel is built to answer this.

Every building stands on jackscrews, huge iron threads set into stone footings. Crews called Levelers walk the terraces with plumb lines and water-glass vials, adjusting each foundation a quarter turn at a time. Pipes are built with slip joints and coiled bellows. Rails and gantries are hinged. No wall in Ombrel is ever exactly upright. Everything leans a few degrees off, and everyone has learned to lean with it.

Old Levelers are recognizable by their gait: a small, constant compensation, the body always correcting for a floor that might be a hair off. In the city archives, plumb lines are hung in glass cases like relics, and the oldest is nearly a hand's width from vertical, a record of how far the mountain has moved.

## The Exhalation

Every eleven days or so, the mountain breathes out. Every vent in the dome opens at once, and a white column of steam rises from each fumarole, well, and crack. For about an hour the city vanishes into blank white, the sun becomes a pale coin, and the temperature drops by ten degrees. The turbines spin down. Furnace crews stop. Everyone stands where they are and waits, holding a hand against the nearest warm wall to feel the vibration. It is not a ritual, exactly. It is only that nobody works during the Exhalation, and nobody has ever been able to say why it feels wrong to.

When it ends, the fog drains slowly off the terraces, and all the condensing plates are beaded at once, shining, and the city drips for the rest of the day.

## Sound

The city is mostly quiet. The thin air carries sound badly, and the heavy machinery runs low and slow, turbines humming below the range of easy hearing, felt in the sternum more than the ear. Above that, there is the hiss of steam, the tick of metal cooling or expanding, the patter of condensate, distant footsteps on iron stairs. Voices are lowered by habit. A shout would carry oddly, thin and sharp, and people avoid it.

## Life

Fauna is rare. There are no herds and no predators. A small pale beetle lives in the warm ash between terraces, feeding on windblown organic dust and the green film. A few swifts, long-winged and soot-colored, nest in the cooler chimney mouths and ride the thermals over the dome in slow spirals; some Levelers mark them as omens if they roost early. Rumor speaks of a translucent, many-legged thing that lives inside the deep condensate tanks and is never fished out, and of the sounds it makes at night, tapping on the stone from underneath. The city leaves it alone.

## At Dusk

When the sun drops, the sky above the dome deepens to violet, then black, with stars so sharp and numerous they seem to hum. The rock keeps its heat. The terraces glow faintly red in the cracks, the furnace mouths open like slow eyes, and the steam plumes catch the last light and turn rose, then copper, then gone. The city settles, ticking and dripping, leaning gently against the swelling ground, adjusting itself a quarter turn at a time.
