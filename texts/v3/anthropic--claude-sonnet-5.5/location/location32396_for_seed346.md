# The Ward of Unfinished Ground

The place sits in a shallow basin barely above the level of the sea that no longer reaches it. From the rim you can see all of it at once: a city about eleven minutes wide, packed into a bowl of raw grey silt. The silt is new. It has not yet decided what kind of rock it will be, so it holds a memory of being mud. It is cold, damp to the touch, and faintly springy. In places it has begun to lithify into thin plates that ring dully when stepped on. Elsewhere it is still paste. The only gullies are a few hand-deep runnels combed by the slow drainage of the night's condensation. This is the whole of the erosion here. The land is at its beginning, and it is being watched.

## The City

The buildings are packed so tightly that the alleys are shoulder-width, and in many places they are roofed by upper floors that lean together and fuse. The construction material is a matte, pearl-grey substance that appears to have been *grown* rather than built. It has the grain of bone and the seams of coral. Walls thicken toward their bases in slow, patient curves. Windows are not cut but *opened*, with lips like healed wounds. Older districts are darker and finely fissured. The newest ones at the edge are still faintly warm and give off a smell like wet chalk and struck matches.

Nobody appears to be building it. The city is a proof in progress, and its structures are theorems being worked out in matter. Ask what a tower is for and the closest honest answer is that it is holding up a conclusion. Every few weeks a lane you know will have resolved into something slightly more elegant than it was. A dead end will have found its door. A stair will have discovered it was always a spiral.

## Water and Cold

The air holds a raw, persistent damp at a few degrees above freezing. It rains rarely, in fine, sudden sheets that last a quarter-hour and leave every surface beaded. Most of the water comes from condensation. The city is threaded with collector-ribs, thin pale fins along every roofline that pull moisture out of the air and pass it inward through capillary channels no wider than hair. You can hear it if you press an ear to a wall: a very fine ticking, like sand falling on paper. The water ends in small square basins in courtyards, perfectly still, each with a skin of thin ice at dawn. The water in them is so clear that people mistake the basins for empty.

Fog gathers indoors more than out. Stairwells fill with it, and rooms hold their own slow weather, low clouds that drift from corner to corner and rain a little onto the floor and are gone by evening.

## Growth

Plants are few and precious. Moss and a grey-green crust of lichen cling to the shaded lower walls. On the wall's shaded side, but the shaded side isn't always the same side: the moss's idea of north drifts by a few degrees a day. In some courtyards stand pale, thin-stemmed trees, six or seven at most, each in a glass-lipped well of dark soil. Their leaves are translucent and shaped like small hands. They are tended by no one visible. Their leaves fall upward in autumn, drifting toward the light for a few meters before settling on the eaves.

## The Breakdown

This is the part that matters. The place is not stable, and it does not hide that.

Reality here fails in small, patient ways, and the city treats these failures as weather. Locals call them *errata*.

- **Lag.** Shadows sometimes fall a few seconds behind the objects that cast them. Stand still and your shadow catches up, mildly embarrassed.
- **Doubled streets.** Two lanes may be the same lane. Walk one, turn back, and you find the other, with your own footprints already in it, older than they should be.
- **Reordered distance.** A square that takes ninety steps to cross on the way in may take forty on the way out. No one has ever measured it the same way twice. The residents carry knotted cords and stopped counting long ago.
- **Sound before source.** A dropped cup can be heard breaking a moment before it falls. Residents learn to catch things early.
- **Rain that changes its mind.** In one alley it falls sideways, in another, upward for a few drops before it remembers itself. Puddles there occasionally hold the reflection of a different sky.
- **Unlabeled doors.** Doors open onto rooms that are larger than the building, or onto the same room seen from a different floor. A very few open onto raw silt, cold and unfinished, as if the city had run out of instructions there.

The rules underneath are strange but consistent. Errata cluster near new construction and thin out near the old, well-proved districts. The city seems to be settling arguments with itself, and the errata are the places where the argument is still open. Standing in the oldest quarter, you feel the ordinary world hold like a held breath.

## Technology

The engineering is beyond anything that has a name. There are no visible machines. Machinery would be crude here. Under the pale walls the substance is structured at scales too small to see and too coordinated to be accidental. Touch a wall at the right moment and you feel a slow pulse, once every several minutes, as if something enormous were adjusting its grip. Surfaces respond to attention. A hand held near a wall for a while will find the material slightly warmer, and a handhold will be there where there was none. Light-guides run through the stone like veins and give off a soft, even, ordinary daylight color, so that interiors feel like overcast noon at all hours. Outside, the sky is a flat, bright pearl, and the light carries no strong direction. There is enough of it to read by, and never enough to cast a shadow you can trust.

Under the basin, the ground is being *composed*. Where the silt is still paste, faint lines glow for a moment in patterns like the cross-sections of crystal, and the mud firms behind them. The city is teaching the land to become rock. It will take centuries, and it is in no hurry.

## Life

Fauna is rare and always a little uncanny. Grey wading birds with long, jointed legs stand in the shallow courtyard basins. They are almost motionless and do not seem to feed. Sometimes one is present in two adjacent courtyards at once, in identical posture. Small pale moths gather at the light-guides. A few people live here, thin and quiet, wrapped in layered wool and waxed cloth, moving with an unhurried economy. They tend the trees, follow the cords, and keep the ledger of errata: a long book in which each day's oddities are recorded and cross-referenced, in case a pattern ever resolves.

## Sound and Time

It is mostly quiet. What you hear is the ticking of the collector-ribs, the drip of condensation into basins, the low sub-audible hum you feel in the sternum more than in the ear, and now and then the ring of a silt plate as something walks across it. Sometimes, far off, a single clear tone sounds. It is the city finishing a thought.

Everything changes slowly. A season here is a gradual firming of the ground, a shifting of the fog's favorite rooms, a new quarter becoming still. If you stayed a year, you would not notice the change day to day. You would only find, one morning, that the street outside your door had become more correct than you remembered.
