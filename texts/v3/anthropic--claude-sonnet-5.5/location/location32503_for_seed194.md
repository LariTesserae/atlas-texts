# The Reed City of the Under-Hollow

## Where it is

Above, there is a low, rounded rise on a flat and colorless plain. It is so worn that it barely counts as a hill. Nothing on it has been alive in the ordinary sense for a very long time. Its skin is a gray felt of dead moss over lignin, and it is dry enough to crackle underfoot.

The rise is a single plant, or was, or is. Its trunks have fused, and its roots and stems have been leaning into one another for so long that they are one mass. Deep in this mass, under a few meters of bark-stone and packed fiber, is a hollow about the size of a market town. It has no light and never has. Nobody has come in or out for a very long time, except the air.

## The dark

Vision is not an option here, so the place is organized by other senses.

The air is cool, about the temperature of a cellar, and very dry. It smells of pine-pitch, cold cinnamon, and dust that has been dust for a hundred thousand years. Underneath is something sweeter and stranger, a note like burnt sugar and iron. That is the sap, which is not watery. It is a thick, slow, amber terpene resin that beads on the walls and moves so gradually that a drop takes a human lifetime to slide a hand's width. Almost nothing here is wet. The rare traces of water are bound up in salt crusts that bloom white in the corners and taste of bitter minerals.

The floor is soft and slightly springy, a mat of fine root-hair packed into a fabric. It is quiet underfoot, which makes the rest of the place seem louder.

## The sound

The city is loud. This is its defining fact.

The whole mass breathes. Over the course of each slow day, the outer skin warms a little and the deep core stays cold, and the difference drives air through the hollow. It moves through thousands of tubes, the hollow stems of a reed-like growth that threads every wall. Each tube is a different length. The result is a continuous chord, huge and low and never resolving: a drone like a cathedral organ played by something that has forgotten the tune. Under it are higher whistles and, in places, a rattling, seed-in-a-gourd chatter where dry pods hang in the drafts.

The sound is not hostile, but it is total. You feel it in your sternum and your back teeth. You cannot speak over it without shouting, and even shouting is quickly swallowed. Every street has its own pitch, and after a while a visitor would learn to find their way by tone alone. Lower means toward the center. A rising warble means a plaza. Two notes in a slow beat mean a crossing.

## The city

The city was built, and by hand. The builders had simple tools: adzes, chisels, scrapers, and gouges, mostly of a soft greenish bronze, and some of stone. Their marks are still visible if you run your fingers along the walls, small repeated scoops like fish scales, each the width of a wrist's swing.

The streets are narrow, winding, and sunk into the growth, with ceilings low enough that a tall person would stoop. Stairs climb between levels in shallow spirals. Doorways are simple arches cut into the living wall. Houses are cells hollowed out of the wood, each with a sleeping ledge, a hearth-niche that has never held fire (there is no air to spare and no light to want), and a small tube to the drone, where a family once tuned their own note.

Erosion has taken almost everything sharp. Every step is dished by feet that are gone. Every threshold is worn to a bowl. Every carved edge has softened into a swell, as if the whole city were made of soap. Ornament survives only as suggestion: a ridge that might once have been a face, a line of dimples that might once have been counting.

The density is nearly zero. Streets run for a long time between anything at all. A plaza might hold a single stone bench and nothing else. The empty distances are part of the design. In this place, silence would have been the most precious thing, and space was how they made it.

## The plants

The plant is everything. There is no soil and no separate ground. The walls, floors, ceilings, stairs, doorframes, and tools' handles are all the same living material at different stages: pale fibrous heartwood, resin-black knots, and glassy reed clusters.

It grows toward sound. This is the strange rule of the place. The plant takes its energy from vibration and from the slow chemical seep of the deep ground, not from light, and it has grown thickest where the drone is strongest. Its tissue is faintly warm where sound is loud. Over centuries it has gently pressed the city back into its own shape. Doorways narrow by a fingernail's width every decade. Plazas fill by degrees with soft, convoluted growth like the folds of a brain. The builders once cut the reeds back to keep the streets open and the tones true. Since they stopped, the plant has been retuning the city to suit itself, and the drone has slowly gone lower, deeper, and more complete.

## Time

Change is slow. There is a long cycle, perhaps some decades, in which the pitch of the whole city drifts down a semitone and then, with agonizing slowness, back up. Once in a very long while a passage seals shut with a dull thump that rolls through the dark, and elsewhere another opens. Resin creeps. Salt blooms. The hollow shrinks by a hair each century.

## What isn't here

There are no animals. No insect ticks in the walls, no bird, no bat, no mouse. Whoever built the city is gone, and the only living thing is the plant. Their bodies left no trace, or the plant took them back. In the deepest chamber, where the drone is loudest and the floor is warm as a hand, there is a hearth-niche with a bronze adze set into it. The wall has grown around the blade until only the handle shows. It has been held there, gently and completely, for longer than any of the streets have had names.
