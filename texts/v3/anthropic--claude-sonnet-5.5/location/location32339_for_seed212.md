# The Chorus Plain

Start at the edge, where the ground is still new.

The basin floor is black glass-rock, split into columns and plates by cooling and still sharp enough to cut. Nothing has rounded it yet. Each fracture is a clean wedge, and in every wedge something green has wedged itself in and is prying. The rock isn't crumbling. It is being *edited*, and you can watch the edits: hairline, then finger-wide, then a root as thick as a thigh, all within the space of a slow breath. The air is warm as a mouth and smells of cut stems, wet iron, and the sweetness of something fermenting in the sun.

Then the plain begins, and it is a city.

## Architecture without architects

It isn't a metaphor. Streets run in recognizable grammar: arteries, side lanes, junctions, plazas, blocks. The plants have arrived at these forms the way water arrives at a river, by pressure and repetition, with no plan behind them.

**The pipe-reeds** are the colonnades. They are hollow, jointed, glossy green-gold, thick as cisterns and taller than cathedral naves, growing shoulder to shoulder in rows so exact they look drilled. Between rows are avenues. Overhead their tops lean together and knit into vaults of interlaced leaf-blade, so you walk through ribbed green tunnels lit like the inside of a lung.

**The lattice-figs** are the towers. Their trunks are not single stems but thousands of braided aerial roots that have climbed something long since digested. What remains is hollow, latticed, windowed with gaps, and stacked forty or fifty stories high. Their inner surfaces are stair-ramps of spiraling root, worn smooth, though nothing has ever walked them.

**The sheet-mosses and plate-leaves** are the roofs and walls. Leaves the size of doors overlap like shingles. Others fuse edge to edge into membranes that flex and drum. Some districts are entirely enclosed under them, dim and green-gold, dripping.

**The root-cobble** is the paving. Small tuberous knuckles pack tightly, each one alive and slightly warm. When it rains the cobbles swell, and the street rearranges itself a few centimeters at a time. Paths are never quite where they were.

Nothing is dead here except in transit. Old growth is not left standing. It is consumed within days by the next layer, so the city has no ruins. Everything is at once foundation, wall, and new shoot.

## The noise

You cannot hear this place so much as be inside it. The sound is enormous, continuous, and physical. It presses on the sternum and shakes the fluid in the ears.

It has sources, and they are all vegetable:

- **The pipe-reeds sing.** Wind and pressure through their hollow stems produce tones from a sub-bass you feel in your teeth to a high shriek. A whole avenue sounds a chord, and a chord held for hours becomes a wall.
- **The pod-trees detonate.** Seed capsules build pressure for days and then burst with cracks like breaking ice sheets, throwing seed in slow clouds. There are thousands per minute. The plain's sound is largely a barrage of them: pop, crack, boom, in overlapping ripples that travel outward like applause in a stadium.
- **Growth itself is loud.** Stems elongate with a creak of tearing fiber. Roots splitting stone give a series of gunshot reports. Leaf-sheets unfurl with a sound like canvas ripped slowly by giants.
- **Sap runs under pressure.** In the largest trunks it roars like water in a pipe, and in the thin ones it hisses and shrieks.
- **The plate-leaves are drums.** When rain comes, the whole roofscape becomes one instrument.

The sound is so dense that it has texture and even sight. In the humid air, sharp detonations leave visible rings, brief circles of clearer air spreading through the mist. Dust and pollen on leaf surfaces jump into little standing patterns, like sand on a struck plate. Near the loudest plazas, droplets on leaves shiver into perfect geometric arrays.

Districts have pitches. One quarter sits in a churning low drone. Another is all high crackle. Another is close to silence, and that silence is the most frightening thing in the plain, because it means something is being held in.

## Water

The plain is damp rather than wet. Water is scarce and precious, and the city treats it as such.

It comes in **walls of rain**, intermittent and violent, marching across the basin every few hours. You see them coming as grey curtains and hear them first as an added roar over the roar. When one arrives the drumming triples. The plate-leaves funnel every drop into channels of curled stem, down through pipe-reeds, into root-cisterns, and it is gone within minutes. The streets, which had been dry and creaking, drip and steam, and for a while everything grows visibly.

Between rains the moisture is **condensation**. Cool air sinking off the black rock at dawn beads every surface. Bowls of leaf hold clear pools. The fig-towers carry water in bracts high in their hollows, small hanging ponds of perfect stillness in all that racket, each with a drowned green light.

At the low southern edge a **braided river** crosses the plain, shallow and warm and choked with floating mats of root. The river has been roofed over by the city. It runs through a long tunnel of leaf, so you never see the sky above it, only feel the cooler air and hear its hush beneath the din.

## Where the world stops agreeing with itself

The plain is not stable in its rules, and the longer you look, the more this comes clear.

**Time is local.** In one plaza a lattice-fig will complete its whole life in the span of a walk: sprout, tower, flower, collapse into mulch, sprout again. Two blocks away a single leaf has been unfurling for as long as you have stood there, without visible progress. The boundary between the two speeds is a line you can step across, and the hairs on your arm change speed as you do.

**Sound arrives out of order.** A pod bursts, and you hear it before you see it swell. A reed falls and its crash reaches you as a rising thing, a crescendo whose peak is the moment of standing again. Some echoes return before their source. In certain avenues you can hear the next hour's chord already leaking backward through the current one.

**Geometry is loose.** A street runs straight and arrives where it began. A plaza that took ten minutes to cross is, from the far side, the size of a coin. Doorways in the lattice-figs open onto interiors far larger than the tree, vast green halls with ceilings lost in mist, then close, and on second entry lead somewhere else.

**Gravity is opinion.** Rain in the eastern quarter falls upward off the leaves in bright strands and rejoins the cloud, and the quarter is always dry because of it. Seeds drift sideways against the wind. A sheet of leaf detaches, hangs, considers, and returns to its stem.

**Light misbehaves gently.** The sun is ordinary and the day warm and bright, but shadows sometimes fall toward the light, pooling under leaves like spilled ink that pulls back when watched. In the deepest vaults, green light comes from no direction, as though the leaves were remembering it.

**There is no one.** No bird, no insect, no creature, and yet the movement everywhere has the character of crowds: rustles that pass along streets like foot traffic, tendrils that part and close as if something had just gone through, a pulse of sound that travels along an avenue at walking pace and turns a corner ahead of you. The city behaves as though it were inhabited. Perhaps the inhabitants are the plants themselves. Perhaps the inhabited-ness is the thing being grown.

## The centre

Deeper in, the density increases to a point where the street is no longer clear and the walls are no longer distinguishable from the crowd. Everything touches. Reeds press against figs against sheets against cobble against pods, in layers so thick that light arrives already green and already tired. The air is hot and close and wet, and the sound reaches a level at which you stop hearing it and simply become its medium.

In the middle there is a plaza that is only a hollow, a round opening in the mass perhaps a mile across. The rock here is freshest and blackest, and the cracks in it are new, the edges still bright. Around the rim every pipe-reed leans inward. Every pod is closed. Every leaf holds its breath.

Then, without warning and without cause, the plaza *sounds*: every plant on its rim, at once, one note. The rock rings. The mist flashes outward in a single ring you can see, and beyond it the whole plain answers in a wave of cracking, bursting, singing that takes minutes to reach the far edges. Seeds go up by the billion. Somewhere far away a district is torn down and regrown in the time it takes the sound to travel.

Then the plaza is still, and slowly, with a creak like a great door, the first green shoot begins to push through the black glass, already loud.
