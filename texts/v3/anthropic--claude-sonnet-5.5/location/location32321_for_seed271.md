# The Sump of Halves

The land here is low and old. It rolls in soft swells of buff limestone, rubbed smooth enough that the ridgelines look like the backs of sleeping things. In one of these swells the ground has folded inward and made a bowl, about sixty paces across and the depth of two houses. Nothing about it is dramatic. It has been this shape for so long that it no longer looks like a hollow at all. It looks like the way the ground is meant to be.

## The Rim and the Walls

The rim is a low lip of pale rock furred with grey-green lichen in rings, each ring the size of a palm. Ledges step down from it, and their edges are rounded like old bread crusts. Lower, the walls undercut into shallow alcoves, wide and low-roofed, each big enough to shelter a few people sitting. Their ceilings are blackened with a fine damp bloom, and their backs are stained in long vertical streaks of rust, ochre, and a dark bluish grey. The streaks mark where water has crept down the stone for longer than any count could hold.

The stone is never wet enough to shine. It is *damp*: cool to the touch even in the warm air, with a faint give to the surface, as though the outer finger-width has been softened. Rub it and a fine pale powder comes away, and it darkens your skin.

## The Floor

The bottom of the bowl is silt, fine and the color of weak tea with milk, laid down flat and pressed hard. It cracked long ago into wide plates, and the cracks silted over and healed, leaving only faint seams like the joins in old leather. It holds a footprint for a moment, then firms. A few of the prints here are very old, and they are only slight depressions that no one could say were made by feet.

In the center, slightly off true, sits a pool. It is about the size of a table and no deeper than a forearm. The water is perfectly clear and faintly warm, and it lies so still that the floor beneath it seems to be the only thing there. Its rim is a lip of fine mineral scale, layered like the rings of a shell, and the lip has grown exactly as high as the water asks and no higher. The pool has never risen past this edge or fallen below the stain that marks it. What seeps in from the walls leaves again through the air, warm and slow, in an exact trade.

## Water

There is no rain to speak of in the bowl, but there is wetness. The walls weep in three or four places. At each, a bead gathers on the lip of an overhang, swells, and lets go. The drips fall into small cups they have worn in the floor, and the cups are ringed with brighter silt. Each drip keeps its own slow interval, unhurried, and none of them has changed.

The air is warm and close, and it smells of wet chalk, cold coins, and something green and faintly sweet at the edge of rot. Condensation beads on the underside of ledges and on the few metal-cool stones that lie loose on the floor. Nothing dries and nothing floods.

## Plants

Vegetation here is sparse and particular, and every plant is where it must be.

- **Moss in bands.** Along the seep-lines the walls wear thin cushions of moss in deep olive green, so uniform they look painted on. They stop sharply where the damp stops.
- **Ferns in the alcoves.** Small, dark-stemmed ferns hang from the roof-cracks of the largest alcoves, their fronds fine as lace and each held at the same angle to the drip beneath it. Perhaps a dozen grow in the whole bowl. They are not lush. They are simply alive, and steady.
- **One tree.** On the eastern ledge, where a seam of soil has collected, a single crooked fig-like tree grows, no taller than a person, with leathery gray-green leaves and roots gripping the rock like knuckles. It bears no fruit that anyone would eat. Its trunk is nearly stone-colored, and in the dim it can be mistaken for a rock with a canopy.
- **Pale grass tufts.** These grow in isolated clumps along the rim where the light is best. They are thin, blond, and fine as hair, with a hand's breadth of bare stone between each clump and the next.

Between these islands there is only stone, silt, and the film of damp.

## Light

The light is dim and even. It comes from above, filtered through a high, slow haze that hangs across the whole low country like breath on glass. There are no hard shadows. The alcoves are dark but not black, and the pool holds a faint pearly glow. At noon the bowl is the gray-gold of the inside of a shell, and at dusk it dims by degrees so gentle you notice only that you have stopped seeing the far wall. Dawn and dusk here differ from the middle of the day by very little. It is a place in permanent late afternoon.

## What Was Made Here

People came here, once. They left little, and none of it is grand.

- **A single-course wall.** Along the southern side, flat stones were laid edge to edge in a curve one stone high, as though to mark off a room without closing it. Moss has claimed the joins. Some of the stones have sunk into the silt so that only their tops show.
- **A threshold.** At the mouth of the largest alcove, two upright slabs stand with a third laid across them, a lintel gone slightly out of level. Nothing lies beyond it but the alcove, but the gesture is complete. Something was meant to be entered.
- **The cup-stone.** Beside the pool lies a boulder with a hollow worn into its top, bowl-smooth and wide as two joined hands. Whether it was ground by hands, by water, or by long turns of both, it now holds a finger-depth of clear water. The water level in it does not change either.
- **Marks.** On the wall above the largest alcove are faint scratches in groups of three, so worn they read only in raking light. They could be tallies, or a path, or only the work of a hand on a slow afternoon. Mineral bloom has begun to seal them.

No tool remains, and no hearth-black stains the ceilings. If there were fires, the damp has erased them.

## Sound

The place is mostly quiet, but the quiet has a grain. The drips are the loudest thing, soft and distinct, like slow fingertips on a tabletop. Each cup gives a slightly different note: one a soft *tuk*, one a higher *tik*, one a wet, round *plum*. Under them you can hear the small settling noises of damp stone, a tick, a faint crackle. Wind passes over the rim and does not come down. Its hush drifts above like a river you can't see.

There are no birds, no insects, and no frogs in the pool. The water holds nothing that moves. Old snail shells lie fossilized in the limestone of the walls, spiraled and white, but these are the only remains of animal life, and they are stone now.

## The Rule of the Place

Everything in the bowl is in balance, and the balance has held so long that it has stopped feeling like a balance and become simply the way things are. The seeps feed the pool, the air takes the water back, the moss holds the wall, and the wall gives up its dust to the silt. Nothing is being built up or worn away at any pace that a lifetime could measure. If you stayed an hour you would see the same drips, the same glow, and the same green. If you came back after a year, or after many, you would find the wall's streaks in exactly their places and the pool at its lip.

To stand here is to feel time thin out. The bowl is not asleep. It is finished, complete, and quietly at rest in its own state.
