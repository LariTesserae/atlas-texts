# The Stair-City of Hesper Col

Nothing here is near anything else. The plateau sits so high that the sky above it is the color of a bruise at noon. The air is thin, dry, and cool, a steady cold with no bite in it. It smells of struck flint and the inside of an old stone jar.

## The ground

The plateau is a long saddle of pale rock, salt-crusted and vein-streaked, tilted a few degrees off level. It runs on for days of walking. Wind and ancient frost cycles have worn it into *terraces*: broad steps, each a mile or more across, each with a rounded lip like a worn stair in a cathedral. Some risers are the height of a person, some the height of a cliff. Nothing about their spacing looks planned, and they still seem to follow a logic, the way the rings of a tree do.

The rock is layered in bands of bone-white, rust, and a green-grey that looks like tarnish. Where the wind has sanded it long enough, the surface takes on a faint gloss and holds the light like the inside of a shell.

There is no water. There hasn't been for so long that the word has worn smooth. Hollows that were once basins hold drifts of fine white powder, and it moves.

## The city

The city is not on the plateau so much as *of* it. Nobody built it, and no tools ever touched it. It accreted.

Over ages, mineral seep and salt-growth and the slow labor of pale colonial organisms (mats of lichenous crust, cold-loving, feeding on stone) laid down walls, arches, stair-runs, and roofless rooms. The result reads unmistakably as an **urban** place. There are avenues with gutters cut in them for water that never comes, plazas ringed with columns, and towers stacked in tiers like fungus on a log. Districts are separated by open ground that could be parks, though nothing grows there. The density of *structure* is high near the city's heart. The density of everything else (presence, movement, life) is close to zero.

The buildings are weathered into a settled state, past collapse and past growth. Corners are round. Doorways have been worn from rectangles into ovals, then into teardrops. Statues that might be figures, or might be outcrops that wanted to be figures, have lost their faces and kept their postures. Everything is about the same pale color as the rock beneath it, so the city looks like the plateau remembering a city.

The crust-organisms cover the north faces in thin silvery films. Some produce a faint cold luminescence, a blue-white glimmer that comes and goes in slow waves. It is the only light besides the low, tired sun.

## The light

The sun hangs large and dim, muffled by fine dust high up, so it gives light without heat or definition. Shadows are soft-edged and long. In the hours the inhabitants call *the grey shift*, the whole plateau glows evenly from every direction with no source, and the city seems to be lit from inside the stone.

## Things that break

This is where the place stops behaving.

**The streets do not agree with each other.** Walk an avenue to its end, count the towers, and walk back, and the count will differ. Not by a lot. By one or two. Nothing seems to have moved. The city simply holds more *possible* buildings than it shows at any time, and which ones are present depends on nothing you can find.

**Distance is elastic.** A tower that looks an hour away may be a day away or ten minutes away. It depends on the direction you approach from and, oddly, on whether you look at it as you go. Travelers learn to walk with their eyes half-lowered, watching the road and not the destination. Arrival happens sideways, when the tower is suddenly beside you.

**Up is negotiated.** In some districts the ground tilts by degrees over the course of a street until the far end is a wall you are somehow walking along. The dust drifts *toward* the direction gravity has agreed on that day. In the deepest quarter, stairs descend to a landing that is higher than where you started.

**Doorways remember weather.** A door that opens onto a bare room in the dry calm opens onto a long hall of columns when the wind is high. Both are true. Neither is a trick of light.

**Sounds arrive early.** In the plazas, an echo comes back *before* the sound that caused it, so that the first thing you hear in an empty square is your own footstep, then a moment later, softly, the step you are about to take.

None of this is menacing. The place is not hostile. It is simply not *finished* deciding what it is, and it has been not-finished for longer than anything can be measured.

## Motion and weather

Despite its stillness, the plateau is constantly moving.

**The Draw.** Each day, as the low sun crosses, the air on the high terraces cools and slides downhill in a long, steady flow. Drifts of white powder stream through the avenues like slow smoke, fill the gutters, and sculpt every windward corner. By evening the city's edges have been redrawn by a hair's breadth.

**The Rime.** At the coldest hour, a frost of carbon dioxide condenses on every surface, a feathering of brittle white crystals that hisses faintly as the light returns and it sublimes back to air. For that hour the city looks upholstered. Then it strips away in ribbons of vapor that climb the towers and vanish.

**The Turning.** Every so often, unpredictably, the wind reverses. It doesn't die and rise again. It flips, and the powder in the air halts, hangs, and streams back the way it came. In that minute the chambers of the city sound their only real note, a low, breathy chord as wind crosses ten thousand hollow doorways at once. It lasts a few breaths and is the loudest thing on the plateau.

**Slow growth.** Watch a wall long enough and you can see the crust-films creep. A pale edge advances a finger's width in an afternoon, dissolving stone at its front and laying down glossy new salt behind. The city is slowly digesting and remaking itself. Balance, not decay.

## What lives here

Very little, and rarely.

**Tenders** are the largest visible animals: tall, thin, slow, six-legged things, the color of the stone, that move along the avenues in ones and twos. They scrape the crust-films with broad flat mouthparts and leave pale, clean stripes behind them. They don't seem to notice visitors. Sometimes one stands motionless in a plaza for days, and dust drifts against its legs like it would against a pillar.

**Skitterlings**, small dry hard-bodied things the size of a thumbnail, live in the cracks. They feed on the Tenders' scrapings and on the mineral dust. At the Turning they all go still at once.

**Something in the deep quarter.** Travelers report a large presence in the districts where up is unsettled. It is never seen, only inferred from the way the powder drifts around a shape that isn't there. It may be a Tender that has gone to a place where it is very large. It may be the city itself.

## Sound

Mostly there isn't any. There is the tick of stone contracting as the cold sets in, and the faint sift of powder against powder. You hear the dry scratch of a Tender's mouthparts, unbelievably far away and completely clear, because the thin air carries small sounds oddly well. You hear your own breath, and your own footstep, a moment before you take it.

Most of the time the silence is so thorough that it feels like a material, one more substance in the stone, and the city is built of it as much as of salt.

## The feeling of the place

Standing on the highest terrace of Hesper Col at the grey shift, with the blue-white glimmer moving slowly in the crust and the towers around you neither near nor far, you get the sense of a city that was never abandoned because it was never occupied. It has been quietly imagining itself for a very long time, and you are one small thing it has begun to imagine.
