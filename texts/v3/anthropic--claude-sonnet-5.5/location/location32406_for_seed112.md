# The Pressed Highlands

Start with the light, because it arrives first and gives no relief. At this altitude the air is thin and dry and nearly colorless, so the sun comes down almost without weakening. It is cool despite it. Your breath doesn't fog, because there is nothing in the air to condense. The brightness lands on a green so total that the eye keeps trying to find the ground beneath it and can't.

There is no ground. There is only the Mat.

## The Mat

The plateau is a continental shoulder of old rock, but the rock hasn't been seen in a million years. Over it lies a single living deposit of plant matter, hundreds of meters thick in places, layered like a peat bog that never stopped growing upward. The top skin is a springy turf of grey-green, silver-green and rust-green filaments, dense as felt. Beneath it is a middle layer of stems and rhizomes braided into something like cordage. At the bottom is compressed, dark, oil-soaked humus, slowly turning back into stone. Everything you stand on was alive, or is.

The Mat has eroded, as everything old does. Wind and slow slumping have cut it into long terraced ridges, each with a sheer cut face where you can read the strata like a cliff of cloth. Deep gullies wind across it, floored with shed bark and shredded leaf, where the loose material drifts in dunes that move with the wind. The ridges are rounded and settled. This has been the shape of things for a very long time, and the shape keeps itself.

There is no water here. The plants run on other fluids. Their sap is a pale, light oil that stays liquid in the cold and is threaded through the Mat in vessels that bulge and pulse under the turf. Rain does not fall. Once a season a thin fog of fine resin drifts up from the lowlands and coats everything with a glassy varnish that sparkles for an hour.

## The Machinery That Grows

Nothing here is built. But the plants have found engineering by another route, and the plateau is full of mechanism.

Across the ridges stand the **boiler-stalks**, hollow pale trunks as wide as a house, with bulbous heads of clear, lens-like tissue. The bright sun focuses through the lens and heats a reservoir of volatile oil at the core. The oil expands and drives a plunger of fibrous, muscle-like cord down the length of the trunk. The plunger turns a cam at the base, which opens a valve, which vents the pressure through a bank of reed-pipes. Then the trunk cools, the plunger draws back, and it begins again. A single boiler-stalk cycles every forty seconds. There are millions of them, drifting slightly out of phase, so the plateau never has a silence, only a slow tidal interference of pulses.

Then there are the **presses**, low flat tables of ligneous tissue that stamp rhythmically on a bed of fibers and produce sheets of a tough, translucent paper. The sheets are peeled off by attendant insects and carried away. The **spinners** are radial plants with a hundred feeding tendrils, drawing oil-soaked fibers into cables that pay out along the ground for kilometers, tying the Mat's upper layer together. The **kilns** are mounds of dark cellular tissue that hold a slow internal fire, a controlled smolder that turns waste to charcoal and mineral flux. They breathe a thin blue smoke that the wind takes sideways in bright ribbons.

Nobody designed any of it. It is what a sufficiently old, sufficiently hungry, sufficiently well-lit plant ecosystem converges on. It is industry as an outcome of growth, without an owner.

## The Sound

The sound is the first thing you feel and the last thing you get used to. It is deafening, physical, and continuous. It is not one noise but a great layering of them:

- the **low bellows-roar** of the boiler-stalks venting, a sub-bass that you feel in your sternum and teeth;
- the **shriek of reed banks**, thousands of pitched whistles that shift as the wind changes their airflow, so the whole ridge seems to sing chords that never resolve;
- the **hammering of the presses**, a dry clacking, like a factory floor heard from inside a drum;
- the **rasp and rip** of the wind combing through the turf;
- the **crack of seed-guns**, sharp reports from pods that fire on a pressure trigger, hurling seeds hundreds of meters, sometimes in volleys of thousands when a gust passes over a stand.

Talking is pointless. The fauna have adapted. They communicate with visual signals, flashing wing-plates and pulsing color-patches on their bodies, and with vibrations through the Mat's cordage. Any sound you make is swallowed instantly.

## The Fauna

Life here is abundant but spread thin across the vastness, so any one place holds only a few creatures. Most are workers in the plant economy.

**Haulers** are six-legged, low-slung, long-bodied animals the size of oxen, with broad flat backs. They move in slow lines along the spinner-cables, carrying loads of pressed paper, charcoal, and seeds to places where the Mat needs patching. Their gait is steady and unhurried, in time with the cables.

**Tenders** are small beetle-like creatures that crawl over the stalks, oiling joints with secretions, clearing blockages from the reed-pipes, and gnawing away rot. They are so numerous on a working ridge that the surface seems to shimmer.

**Gliders** are the large fauna of the air. They have wide, sail-like membranes and no fixed home, and they ride the turbulent updrafts off the ridge-faces. They feed on the seeds fired from the guns, plucking them from the air mid-flight, and they are half-deaf from birth. Their eyes are enormous and multi-lensed.

**Harrowers**, the rare predators, are long grey pursuit-animals that run the gullies where the drift is loose, hunting tenders and young haulers. They are silent, and in this place silence is a kind of camouflage.

## Weather of Its Own

The plateau is large enough to make weather. The dark, warm kilns and the bright, cool ridges produce thermal columns, and the columns build into a permanent system of convection cells, each hundreds of kilometers across. Wind flows constantly from the shaded gullies to the sunlit heights, and every afternoon it stiffens into gale.

The air is so dry and the movement of fibers so constant that static charge builds without pause. Blue-white arcs crawl along the cables and leap between stalk-heads. Lightning without rain strikes the Mat several times a minute. Some of the fires it starts are absorbed by the kilns, which have learned to feed on them. Others spread across the turf as low orange fronts that consume a valley in a day. The Mat regrows over the scar in a season, in a paler and more vigorous green, and the ash becomes the ground for the next generation of stalks.

## The Rule of the World

The world's single law is **turnover**. Nothing lasts, and the whole holds. Stalks overheat and split; presses jam and are eaten; a ridge burns and is reborn; a cable snaps and is respun. The landscape looks eternal, and in a sense it is, but only because it destroys and remakes itself faster than anything can change it.

Stand on a high terrace at midday. The horizon curves faintly. In every direction, green and silver stretches away, pulsed with vents of white vapor, ribboned with blue smoke and threaded with cable. A glider passes overhead, its shadow crossing a hundred stalks, each of which is firing, hissing, and cycling on its own clock. You can't hear yourself breathe. The light is pure. The whole huge machine is alive, and nothing in it knows you are there.
