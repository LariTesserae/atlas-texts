# The Pipe Garden of Ohl Kesh

The place sits at the top of a mountain that has forgotten how to be a peak. Long ago the summit fell inward and left a bowl about four hundred paces across, rimmed in rounded, orange-gray stone. The rim has been worn by wind and grit until it looks like the knuckles of a sleeping hand. Nothing here has ever been rained on. Water is a rumor. What the bowl has instead is **the Exhale**.

## The Breathing Floor

The basin floor is honeycombed with vents, some as narrow as a wrist and some wide enough to swallow a cart. Every few hundred heartbeats the mountain breathes out. Warm, sweetish, resin-thick gas rises from the vents, heavy with dissolved oils and mineral salts, and everything in the bowl responds at once.

The plants do not drink water. They drink the Exhale. Their leaves are wide, leathery, and slick with a lacquer that beads the gas into amber drops. The drops run down grooved stems into root-mouths that open and shut like slow lips. Between breaths the forest starves and stiffens. When the gas comes, it swells.

You can watch it grow. A fern-thing the color of bruised plums unrolls a frond as long as an arm in the space of a few minutes, and by the next Exhale that frond has gone brittle and snapped off. The floor is thick with the crisp litter of things that lived fast. It crunches underfoot like walking on old paper and dry beetles, and the crunch is part of the noise.

## The Sound

The bowl is never quiet, and it is loud in layers.

At the bottom is the **vent-drone**, a low, wandering note like a huge animal humming through its nose. It rises and falls with the Exhale.

Above it are the **pipe-reeds**, stands of hollow, bone-pale stalks taller than a person, each with a naturally bored slit near the top. When the gas moves through them, they sound. Every stand is tuned a little differently by its height and the width of its slit, so the whole basin swells into a chord that is never resolved. Just as it begins to sound like a hymn, the wind shifts and turns it into something like an argument.

Above that is the fauna, which does not merely live here but performs. Clicking, whooping, sawing, and ratcheting come from every side. The **lantern-frogs** are not frogs and are not lit, but they have throat sacs the color of embers and shout in triplets. The **stilt-mothers** are long-legged, feathered things the size of dogs that stalk the terraces drumming their beaks on hollow logs to claim territory. Swarms of thumb-sized gliders pour from crevices at the start of each Exhale, shrieking in one high, shared pitch, and wheel through the amber haze like thrown sparks. When the gas thins, they all drop into the leaves at once, and the silence lasts perhaps three heartbeats before the drone comes back.

## Light

Light is scarce, though the sky is open. Above the rim hangs a permanent brown-gold haze, a lid of suspended oil-mist and pollen the mountain keeps breathing up. The sun comes through as a dull coin. Under it the bowl is lit like the inside of an old lamp: warm, dim, honey-colored, with long soft shadows that never quite sharpen.

Some things make their own light. **Glow-lichen** spreads in slow ring patterns over the cooler stones, pale green and faintly pulsing. Certain fruits, fat as fists and hung in clusters, gleam a low red from inside like coals under ash. At the height of an Exhale the whole understory seems to wake, and the haze itself glows faintly where gas meets glow-lichen.

## Ground and Stone

The rock is old and soft-shouldered. Wind has done all the carving here, not water. It has undercut the ledges into mushroom shapes and drilled lace-like holes through the softer bands. The dry gullies that run down from the rim are not stream-cut but *sand-cut*, smooth as the inside of a spoon, and they are choked with cushions of moss-like plants that hold oil in their tissues and shine when crushed.

Around the basin edge, a series of broad terraces steps down. They are not natural, or not wholly so. Someone long ago stacked, leveled, and chinked them with flat stones, and time has since worn the stones round. Roots have gripped them so firmly that it is hard to say whether the terraces hold the trees or the trees hold the terraces.

## The Turbulence

The place will not stay as you first see it. That is its nature.

Twice in a long day, or sometimes three times, the Exhale becomes a **Surge**. The vents scream, the gas comes thick as smoke, and the wind inside the bowl spins into a slow roaring vortex that lifts leaf-litter, gliders, seed-fluff, and dust into a rotating amber column. Pipe-reeds bend and overblow into shrieks. Stilt-mothers hunker with their heads under their wings. Then the Surge collapses, everything comes raining down, and the bowl is rearranged: logs shifted, a stand of ferns flattened, a new vent-mouth opened, an old one clogged with fallen silt.

Vents wander. A path that led to the drum-log yesterday now ends at a hissing hole. The forest is constantly being torn down and regrown across the same few hundred paces, and nothing is really old except the stone and the terraces, and the habit of the mountain to breathe.

## What People Left

The signs of hands are sparse and small, and the makers were not many.

- On the eastern terrace, a **drum-log** as thick as a sleeping ox, hollowed and gouged with a stone adze, its skin long rotted away. Stilt-mothers now use it as a stage.
- Near the south rim, a **lean-to** of pipe-reed poles lashed with twisted resin-cord and roofed in overlapping leathery leaves. It is patched, so someone still tends it, or something has learned to. Inside are a flint scraper, a bone needle, and a clay pot sealed with wax and left unopened.
- A **cairn** of black glassy stones stands at the center of the largest vent, where the gas blows straight through it. The cairn hums. It has been rebuilt many times, and each version is a little different.
- Along the northern wall, **hand-marks in ochre**, dozens of them, some very faint and some fresh. They are pressed low on the stone at about the height of a crouching child.

Nobody is here now. The lean-to's cord is still supple, though, and the ochre has not faded.

## Rules of the Place

1. **Nothing is watered.** Anyone who brings water into the bowl finds it clouded and ruined within a day, as the oils in the air bind to it and turn it thick and sweet. Visitors carry dried fruit and oil-skins.
2. **The count matters.** Those who know the bowl count the Exhales. Between them the air is clear and safe. During them it is heavy, and long exposure brings headaches, brightness at the edge of vision, and voices in the pipe-reeds that sound like known people.
3. **Do not sleep near a vent.** The fauna is abundant and loud, but the most dangerous things are quiet: the low, patient **mat-crawlers**, flat as rugs and colored like leaf-litter, which follow the warmth of anything that lies still.

## Last Note

If you stand at the rim at the moment an Exhale begins, you can watch it cross the bowl. A ripple of color passes over the canopy, plum to green to gold. The pipe-reeds start up in sequence like a struck chord, the gliders rise, the lantern-frogs shout, and for one breath the whole small green crater looks like a single instrument being played by the mountain, badly, joyfully, and without end.
