# The Ward of Setting Resin

The basin is barely below the surrounding plain, a shallow dish of pale ground about a half-hour's walk across. From the rim you see all of it at once: a town with no one in it, and the town is still being made.

## The ground

The floor is a fine, cold powder the color of old bone, ground from some soft mineral, gypsum or something chemically related. It lies in drifts and ripples, and it is deep enough to take a footprint to the ankle. Under it is a crust of the same material, fused into plates a few hand-widths thick. The plates have cracked along straight, clean lines, and their edges are still sharp enough to cut a glove. Nothing here is old. The basin opened perhaps a few hundred years ago, and weather has barely started on it. The rim is a low scarp of fractured layers with corners that look freshly snapped.

There is no water anywhere. The chemistry of the place runs on **resin**, a clear, amber-grey, slow-moving fluid that seeps from vents at the basin's lowest point. It is cold and thick, like honey left in a cellar. It smells faintly of struck flint and pine. It does what water does elsewhere. It carries dissolved minerals, feeds the living things, and hardens into stone-like material when it cools or meets the powder.

## The town

The town is built of that hardened resin and powder: cream-colored, translucent at the edges, and faintly warm when you lay a palm against it. It has streets, though they are not straight. They curve in long, deliberate arcs that avoid corners, as if the plan were drawn by something that could not turn sharply. Hundreds of structures stand in rings around the seep at the center. They are low, domed, and cell-like, some fused in rows of ten or twelve, some standing alone. Doorways are oval and a little too low for a person, and they all lean slightly toward the center of the basin. Their inner walls are slick, ridged, and faintly luminous, with a pale green-white glow from films of living organisms. The glow is the main light inside. Outside, the sky is a permanent low overcast the color of pewter, and the day is a long dusk with no visible sun.

There are no tools here, no wheels, no metal, no writing. The town was not built by hands. But it is organized: it has districts, a market-shaped open space with nothing to trade, and a broad ring road worn into the crust. Whatever built it built it with intention, or with something that works as well as intention.

## The builders

The builders are called **wrights** here, for lack of a better word. They are rare. On a quiet day you might see three. They are armored, low, and pale, the size of a large dog, segmented like a sowbug, with a fan of soft, fingerlike palps beneath the head. They move slowly and steadily along the ring road, dragging their palps through the resin channels and laying down a thin, smooth bead of it. A wall grows by a finger's width in an hour. They do not seem to see. When you stand still in front of one, it stops, its palps tasting the air, then turns aside as if you were a boulder.

Most of the town is empty. Wrights live in it the way tides live in a bay. Districts are built, used for a season as brood-chambers or resting cells, then abandoned. Then the walls thin, crack, and slump, and other wrights, or the lichens, or the wind take them apart. So the town is always partly new and partly ruined. A block of gleaming, unweathered domes may stand beside a block already collapsing into powder, and the powder feeds the next block.

## The rule of silence

Sound is close to absent here. The powder swallows footfalls, the air is cold and heavy, and the domes deaden echoes. That is the physical reason, but there is a second reason, and it is the strange one.

Wrights respond to vibration. A dropped stone, a shout, a struck blade: any sharp sound pulls them toward it, and they cover the source in resin. They do not seem to be hunting. They are sealing. Small animals that make noise are entombed in clear, amber-like lozenges where they stood. The town is full of them: a lizard-thing mid-scurry, a bird with its beak open, set into the wall like a fly in wax. Nothing that lives here for long makes any noise. Even the wind moves cautiously, and the only steady sounds are the very small ones. Cooling resin ticks softly like a cooling stove. The lichens make a dry papery whisper as they creep. Powder settles. In the deep stillness you can hear your own pulse.

Anyone who enters learns to walk softly, and to breathe softly.

## The living things

Vegetation is sparse. Pale cushions of **resin lichen** grow on the north sides of walls, silvery-green and hard as felt. They drink from the seep channels and cement themselves to the surface, and in a few weeks they tint a wall gold and start to crack it. Thin, wiry grasses the color of straw stand in scattered clumps along the rim, where the resin does not reach, feeding on the dust and something in the air. In the middle of the basin, near the seep, one tree-like growth stands: a black, twisted, stiff-branched thing about twice human height, with leaves like pressed flakes of mica that flicker faintly when the light changes.

Fauna other than the wrights is rare and quiet. There are small pale lizard-like things that hunt beetles through the powder, moving in short bursts and freezing between them. There are moths as broad as a hand, which fly by day in the gloom and settle on the glowing walls, their wings dusted like the ground. Occasionally, far out on the plain, something large and long-legged moves. It never enters the basin.

## Motion and weather

The place is more active than it first appears. Several cycles overlap.

**The heave.** Each night the crust cools and contracts. The plates buckle at their seams with no sound but a faint subsonic pressure you feel in your ribs, and some walls tilt and others straighten. By morning the streets have shifted by a few finger-widths.

**The seep.** Every few days the vents pulse, and resin wells up in sheets, spreading thinly across the low ground and slowly hardening into a glassy varnish. Wrights gather to feed on it, and the town's shape reorganizes around the new flow.

**The dustfall.** Twice a day a fine pale powder sifts out of the overcast in windless curtains. It falls without any sound and coats every surface, softening sharp edges until the wrights scrape them clean again.

**The dry storms.** Now and then a cold wind rolls over the rim from the plain and lifts the powder in slow, ghostly banners that cross the domes and dissolve. There is faint lightning inside the clouds, a silent flicker of violet, and no thunder ever arrives.

## What it feels like

You stand at the center of the market-shaped clearing, and nothing moves for a while. The domes glow dimly green around you. Their doorways lean toward you as if listening. Somewhere a wall is thickening by a finger's width. Somewhere another is coming down in a slow sigh of dust. A moth lands on your sleeve, and its wings make no sound. You realize the town is not empty and not abandoned. It is being built and unbuilt around you, patiently, in a silence it enforces itself.

If you drop something, you will not hear it fall. You will hear only the slow turn of the wrights as they change direction toward the place where it landed.
