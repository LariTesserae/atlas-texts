# The Setting Floor

The land here is not ground. It is a floor, and the floor is still being laid.

From any point you can see tiles, each about the width of a forearm, fired a dull oxblood or a sooty ochre, set in courses that run out under the haze in every direction. The courses are true for a few hundred paces and then begin to disagree. A herringbone meets a basket-weave and neither yields. Along the line where they meet, the tiles tilt against each other like cracked pack ice. The edges of every tile are still sharp enough to cut a palm. Nothing has had time to wear them. Nothing here is old, and the world seems to be about an hour into existing.

It is very hot. The heat comes from beneath as much as from above. The tiles hold a kiln's memory, and when the wind drops you can feel the floor breathing warmth up through your soles. The air is thick with water. It is not rain but a standing, condensing wetness, so that the sky is a low brown ceiling of steam lit from somewhere behind, dim and directionless, like the inside of a lamp shade. The sun is a rumor of brightness that wanders. Shadows are soft and doubled.

## Water

The basin is low, lower than any sea would allow, and water sits just under everything. It weeps from the joints between tiles in threads of beaded brine. It collects in the grout channels, which run in fine gutters to larger gutters, to brick-lined canals as straight as rulers, to reservoirs stepped down like the seats of an amphitheatre. The water is hot, near the temperature of a bath that has gone on too long, and it carries a taste of iron and baked earth. It steams in the canals in slow, coiling ribbons.

The water is not only passive. Where a channel meets a low place, it does not pool. It *lays* itself: mineral skins form on the surface, thicken, and crack into polygons, and the polygons stiffen into tiles. A flooded court can become a paved court in the time it takes to cross it. You learn to watch the edges of puddles for that faint glassy crust, the first line of a floor.

## What is built

Everything is made. There is no soil beneath the paving, and where the paving is broken you find only more courses of brick, then packed clay, then a darkness that is warm and faintly humming. Even the hills are constructed: long ramps of rammed earth faced with tile, stepped ziggurats with their lower terraces still under scaffold, windowless kiln-towers that vent white smoke from their crowns. They stand far apart, each in its own emptiness, with a day's walking between one and the next.

The craft is primitive and unmistakable. Everything is hand-scale: fired clay, sun-dried brick, lashed poles of drawn glass, hammered copper straps green at the rivets, wedges, plumb-lines of braided pale fiber that grows as felt from the damp walls. There are pulleys with grooved wooden wheels, levers, and ramps. There are stacks of unlaid tile on pallets. There are trowels, dozens of them, copper-bladed with handles of rolled clay, set down in mid-stroke in wet mortar as if the mason had just stepped out.

No mason is anywhere. There is no animal, no track, no droppings, no bone. Only the grey-white felt, a fungal upholstery, climbs the shaded walls and drinks the steam. It is the single living thing, and it isn't clear that it lives.

## The rules

The world keeps a few laws, and you find them by noticing how it behaves.

**Unfinished things continue.** A wall left at knee height will be shoulder-height when you return. You will not see it grow. A half-laid course extends itself by a tile whenever you look away, and the trowels shift their positions between your glances. Something is building, but the building has no hands. Only the tools remain, ready and warm.

**Finished things begin to lapse.** When a structure is completed, meaning its last tile fitted and its last seam sealed, it starts to soften. Corners round. Lintels sag. Within a day a finished tower will have slumped into a heap of wet clay, and the heap will be sorted and stacked into new courses by no one. Completion is a kind of death, so the land is always kept just short of complete. Every building is an eternal almost.

**Two plans can occupy one place.** This is where reality breaks down. The builders, or the process, do not coordinate. In the seams between one scheme and another the geometry fails. A staircase climbs to a wall, and on the other side of that wall a staircase descends from nothing. A doorway opens onto the back of its own lintel. A colonnade repeats itself with every fourth column facing the other way, and if you walk along it you will find your own footprints in the wet mortar, arriving. A canal runs level for a mile and yet the water in it flows uphill, and at the end it pours out of the *bottom* of a cistern into the air beneath, where it does not fall but hangs in a slow standing sheet. In these places distance stops being trustworthy. The horizon can be a drawing of a horizon, ruled and shaded, propped up a few hundred paces off, with the same steam curling *in front of it* as in front of the real one.

## Turbulence

The land is never at rest, and its weather is its own. Heat and moisture build in the basin all day, and toward what passes for evening the ceiling darkens and lowers. Then the **slumps** begin. Whole terraces liquefy from their footings and run in slow red tides, and tiles skate across the surface of the mud like leaves. A storm of steam tears across the plain, and in its wake the surface sets again, freshly paved, with the old pattern gone and a new one begun. Kilns flare at their crowns. Somewhere a ziggurat stands up out of a flood as a lattice of scaffolding and is walled within the hour. Somewhere else a hill that stood for three days vanishes and leaves a clean square of floor.

In the worst of it, pieces of the world are *reassigned*. A ramp lifts off its footings and re-seats itself at an angle no ramp should take. Bricks rain upward in a column out of a flooded court and lay themselves against the underside of the cloud, where they make a ceiling, and where the ceiling is complete, it drips.

## Sound

Between storms the place is mostly quiet, and the quiet has a grain. You hear the tick of cooling clay, the ceaseless small hiss of steam finding its way out of joints, and the drip and trickle of the weeping seams. Now and then there is a soft, heavy *shhhup* as a wet wall settles, or the dry chime of a trowel sliding off a ledge to lie again in mortar. Occasionally, from far off, comes a slow grinding, like a great stone being dragged across another. That is the world moving a hill.

## At the edge of sight

At the farthest visible reach, where the courses run out into the brown haze, there is a line where the floor stops being tile and becomes *plan*: thin lines of pale grout with nothing inside them, the ghost of a pavement not yet laid. Beyond it, faintly, you can see the outlines of more towers, drawn but hollow, waiting to be assigned material. The land ahead is being decided. Every step toward it lays the floor a little further, and it is very hard to tell whether the floor is being laid for you or whether it is you who are being laid into it.
