# The Vug at Ninth Saddle

Above the last cairn, where the scree slope tilts against a sky the color of a dull knife, there is a crack you could put your arm into. It is the only door. Behind it, folded into the mountain like a coin in a coat lining, is a chamber about the size of a chapel nave. The chamber does not agree with its own size.

## The Floor

The floor is basalt-black and fine as flour where it has been ground, but it has been ground by nothing anyone can name. The air is still and very thin, the way it is at five thousand meters, and cold enough to sting the teeth without killing anyone quickly. Nothing has blown here, and no water has ever run. Still, the ridges in the floor are rounded and soft-shouldered, worn to the settled shapes of things that have long since finished changing. Every edge has been eased by some patient process that left no trace of itself.

Scattered across the black are **rime-flowers**, though the name is a courtesy. The frost is not water. It condenses out of the stone itself, a pale dry crystal that grows in fans and ferns on the shaded side of every rock and never on the lit side. At the brightest hour it does not melt. It lifts off the rock as a faint shimmer and settles again a hand's width away. In a slow cycle of many days the whole crop of frost migrates a few centimeters across the floor, like grazing that no animal is doing.

Nothing lives here. That is not the same as nothing being alive.

## The Light

The light is dim and comes from nowhere. Some of the walls glow the amber of a coal seen through a closed eyelid. The glow is not lamplight. The rock gives it off the way a struck bell gives off sound, and it is strongest where the rock is most worn. Shadows do not agree with it. A boulder near the entrance throws its shadow toward the crack, one behind it throws its shadow toward the far wall, and a third throws none. If you stand still for an hour, you may notice that each shadow is consistent with a different sun.

## The Wall That Isn't There

The far wall is about forty paces away. Walk toward it and it stays about forty paces away. Nothing is dramatic about this. Your legs do the work, the floor scuffs under your boots, you count your steps, and at step ninety you understand you have been walking a long time. Turn around and the crack is close behind you, a bright slot the width of a hand, no farther than when you began.

Distance in the vug is not a single number. It depends on which way you are facing and on how long you have been paying attention. Inattention is the only reliable way to cross the room. If you walk with your eyes on the ground and your mind elsewhere, you will reach the far wall in perhaps a minute, and the crack will be an ordinary forty paces at your back.

Visitors who have written of it (a handful, over a handful of centuries, in scripts that do not resemble one another) all describe the same mistake. They try to measure it.

## The Artifacts

Nothing was built here in a way that resembles building. There are seven objects that might be called structures. All are small, and all are set into the rock as though the rock had grown around them.

**The Lattice.** On the left wall, a mesh of dark filaments, thinner than hair, spreads across a surface the size of a door. It is regular to the limit of the eye. Under a lens the regularity does not stop. It continues past where a lens should be able to resolve, and the pattern keeps being a pattern at scales where matter should have run out. It is warm to the touch, one degree above the stone. Frost will not grow within a hand's breadth of it.

**The Plinth.** A cylinder of something like ceramic, knee-high, with a flat top worn into a shallow dish. Nothing sits in the dish. Yet if you place a small stone in it, the stone is returned over the next day, not to the dish but to the exact spot you picked it up from, with the frost undisturbed on its underside.

**The Stair.** Five steps rising out of the floor and ending in air. The stair is the most eroded thing in the vug: its treads are dished, its edges gone to curves. On the fifth step, gravity is slightly wrong. A dropped pebble falls sideways, toward the Lattice, at about a third of a gee. Whoever built the stair meant something by it. The wear says it was climbed for a very long time.

**Four smaller things**, which look like door hinges, a comb, a bowl, and a knot. None of them can be moved. All of them are cold in a way that is different from the cold of the air.

## The Rules

The place has habits, which are slow enough to be mistaken for laws.

- **Things settle toward their own history.** An object left in the vug will, over weeks, take on the weathering of an object that has been there for ages. A steel button left on the floor acquires a soft patina, then pits, then a rounded bloom of the same pale frost as the rest. It is not decay. It is being *caught up*, as if the vug were correcting your object to match the century it thinks it should be in.
- **The room breathes at the pace of geology.** Every so often (nobody agrees on the period, but somewhere near a season) the far wall recedes, and the room's second dimension lengthens until the vug is a canyon. It stays that way for days. Then it shrinks back. The frost-flowers ride the change without seeming to notice.
- **Sound is scarce and almost polite.** There is a tick, once every few minutes, as frost lets go of stone. Under it, at the edge of hearing, is a tone that is almost a hum and almost the sensation of a hum. It is loudest at the Lattice and is never quite the same note twice. The crack admits a thin wind-whistle that dies within three paces of the entrance, as if the air outside had forgotten how to enter.
- **No one is ever seen leaving.** This is not sinister. The lit crack is always there when you look back. Yet people who have stayed a long time report that when they finally faced it, it had become slightly smaller, and that the walk out took the *same* number of steps as the walk in, however long they had been sitting.

## What It Might Be

The visitors who theorize agree on little, but they lean toward one idea. This is a **stopped machine**, not a ruin. It is a mechanism built to hold something open, a fold or a pocket or a pause. The Lattice is the last working part. The plinth, the stair, and the four small things are its tools, its instruments, its hands. The erosion is not the vug aging. It is the vug being *held* at a single age while everything inside it slowly agrees to be old.

Or it is a room with a window, and what is being looked out at is not a place but a time.

## The Last Detail

At the darkest end of the vug, low against the wall, one frost-flower grows toward the Lattice instead of away from it. It is the only one that does. Its fans are more delicate than the others, and it does not migrate. Each visitor who has mentioned it says the same thing, in different words: it looks *attentive*.

Nothing else here is.
