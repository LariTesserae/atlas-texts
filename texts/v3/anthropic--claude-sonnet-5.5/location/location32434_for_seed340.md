# The Chorus Floor

Start at the rim, where the plateau ends and the air changes. From the edge you look down four hundred paces of raw, freshly broken rock, then into the top of the canopy. The canopy is not a surface. It heaves. From above it looks like a pot just before boiling, all of it moving. Then the sound reaches you, and you understand that you have been hearing it for a while already, as a pressure in the teeth.

The basin is called the Floor by those who live in it. It is a collapse, a place where the crust let go along old fractures and dropped a region the size of a large valley. The walls are still young. Scarps of pale gypsum and rust-red sandstone show fresh, unrounded edges, and slabs the size of houses lean off them, held only by the roots that have already found them. Almost nothing has been worn down. Every few days, somewhere in the basin, part of a wall lets go and takes a swath of vegetation with it. The rockfall sounds small against the din, and it is always followed by a surge of new growth in the scar, which greens within a day.

## The ground that is not ground

There is no water here, not a seep or a dew. Whatever rain the plateau gets runs off before it reaches the rim. The Floor has built its life on something else. Everything is soaked in **resin**: amber, clear, or black-red, thick as honey in the warm interior. The plants pull vapor from the humid breath of their own rot and from mineral brines deep in the fractures. They circulate a sap that is oil and terpene rather than water, and they hold it under pressure. Cut a stem and it does not weep. It spits, then hardens in the air into a bead like a lens.

Below the living layers is the **mat**. Ten, twenty, sometimes sixty body-lengths of dead vegetation, dry and springy, packed and interlaced with root-wire, fungal thread, and the shed shells of insects. It ferments slowly and gives off a steady, dry heat. Walking on it is like walking on the back of something sleeping. The warmth comes up through your soles, the smell is toasted sugar and cedar and something sharp like struck flint, and every step releases small puffs of pale spore-dust that hang gold in the light.

The mat has no bedrock under it that anyone has reached. Where it has torn, you see stacked years of it, compressed to a dark leathery stuff. Below that you see glints, the crystalline crusts of salts and gypsum growing in cracks. The rock itself is still forming here, drawing in and knitting tighter as the basin settles. Growth and geology are tangled together. Roots pry apart fractures, mineral seams seal around root-hairs, and pieces of the wall are slowly being digested into the plant body.

## The layers

The vegetation stands in strata, and each layer is a separate country.

**The Floor-weave** is knee-high. It is a carpet of wiry, pliant grasses and creeping pads, some of which close on your ankle and release. Between the stems the air is close and hot and thick with buzzing.

**The Drum-stands** rise above it. These are trunks like fat, hollow barrels, taut-skinned, ribbed, and bearing a bluish bloom. Each is a resonating chamber. Wind, footfall, and the wingbeats of insects clustered on them all make them ring. Some are tuned by their shape and sound a low, sourceless *hoom* you feel in your sternum. Others are stunted and shrill. Whole stands harmonize by accident, and the pitch wanders across the basin like weather.

**The Sail-canopy** is the broad top, layer over layer of leaves like tilted plates, sheets, and rippling ribbons the color of green glass and old brass. Light comes through them tinted and quivering. It is an ordinary daylight in strength, neither dim nor blazing, but it is always moving, because the canopy is always moving. Leaves flip and shear against one another with a papery hiss that at scale becomes a roar.

**The Hanging-lines** run between everything: vines as thin as thread and as thick as a thigh, some strung tight enough to sing when a wing strikes them. In some places they are so dense that the space between trunks is a woven wall. Creatures walk them like roads.

## What lives in it

Fauna is the truest description of the sound. The Floor is thick with animals, many more than there are leaves to count them by, and most are small, dry-bodied, and armored, because a body without water has to be built differently. Their shells are lacquered and jointed and full of resonating cavities.

There are **rattle-beetles**, in clouds that pour off a stem like smoke, each producing a click at some rate of its own. Together they make a shimmering static. There are **stilt-mantids** as long as an arm, that hum their thoraxes like reed pipes. There are **sap-swimmers**: long legless bodies that thread through the resin canals inside the trunks, and whose passage makes the trunks tick and creak from within. There are **bladder-frogs**, or something like them, with no need for pools, that wear a skin of waxy oil and inflate their throats against hollow stems to make a bass that carries a mile. There are flat gliding **paper-bats**, if bats they are, and swarms of lacewinged things that flock in the thermals rising from the mat and fold themselves into one shape and then another.

And there are the large animals. They are rarely seen whole. A ridge of back moves through the weave, glossy and plated, bristling with resin-crusted spines. A thing like a ground-sloth made of woven bark grazes the mat itself, and its droppings are more mat. The big animals are shy. What frightens them is not predators but *silence*, and no place in the Floor is ever silent.

## The rule of the Chorus

This is what the inhabitants have learned and taught their children: **the plants grow toward sound.**

Stems bend toward vibration. Root-mats thicken under the loudest places. Fruit-pods split and seed where the din is greatest, and in the pockets that are quiet, plants wither and their mat starves. Because of this, the animals of the Floor are also its gardeners. Wherever they cluster and sing, they call the vegetation toward themselves and build it up into cathedral shapes around their own noise. A great beetle-choir makes a dome. A frog-colony makes a horn-shaped grove aimed at the rim, which throws the sound outward.

The result is that the landscape is constantly being *sung into new shapes*. Nothing is stable. A grove that stood yesterday may be pulled down as the swarm moves on, its resonance no longer feeding it. Hollows open, towers lean and thicken, and paths close over and are found again elsewhere. The Floor is a fast tide of built growth, and the geology below it is still cracking and settling in time with it.

## Turbulence

Weather here is not rain or wind from outside. It is made inside.

The mat heats and vents. **Thermal columns** rise, carrying spores and insects in whirling towers that lean and collapse. Pods burst in chain reactions, popping across a hillside like gunfire and filling the air with yellow dust that stings the eyes and hangs for hours. Sometimes a **bloom-storm** starts: a whole slope flowers at once, red and white, and the smell is so heavy it is almost a taste, and every insect in a mile heads for it and the sound goes past anything you thought possible. Then the flowers drop, the seed-fluff lifts, and for a while it snows dry white down that sticks to resin and coats everything.

Sometimes resin fires start, when a spark from a splitting rock ignites a leaking stem. They burn fast and cool. The plants of the Floor are adapted to it, and greener shoots push through the char in hours.

## The people, such as they are

They are few, small, and quick, and they live in the thick of it as parasites and partners of the plants. Their tools are of the simplest: bone awls, obsidian flakes traded from the plateau, hooks of hardened resin, cordage twisted from vine-fiber, lances of fire-hardened wood. They have no metal, no writing, no wheels.

Their built things are scattered, small, and mostly borrowed from the plants. Hollowed drum-trunks serve as shelters, their doors cut so as not to spoil the resonance. Rope ladders and sling-bridges run up into the canopy. Small cairns of clear resin-lumps mark the edges of safe paths; light passes through them, and they glow like lanterns at noon. At intervals a **speaking post** stands, a hollow log set in the mat and struck with a mallet in patterns. Because the Floor is too loud for voices, the people speak by touch, by tugging cord, and by pressing their palms to a partner's ribs while humming. Their whole language is felt.

Older marks are fainter. Here and there in the walls, half-swallowed by root, are hand-shaped hollows and rows of drilled holes. They were made by someone before the current people, or by the same people long ago, and the vegetation has already begun to close over them, and the rock behind them keeps moving.

## Standing in it

If you stand at the middle of the Floor where a wide clearing has opened, you can feel the whole thing at once. The heat comes from below and the gold light from above. The air is thick and sweet and resinous. The sound is not a noise but a substance, something you are immersed in, that vibrates your ribs and skull and passes through your bones. Around you the walls of green lean in and shiver. A stand of drum-trunks nearby begins to swell, its pitch dropping. Something enormous and unseen answers it from across the basin. The mat shifts under your feet. Somewhere a slab is about to fall. Everything is still being made.
