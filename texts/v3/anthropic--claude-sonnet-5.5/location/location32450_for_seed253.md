# The Held Sea

At the top of the world there is a basin, and the basin is full.

It sits at an altitude where the air above would be too thin and too cold for any lung. Below the surface the water is another climate entirely: heavy, slow, and a few degrees above freezing. The rim is a ring of very old mountains, worn so low and round by unimaginable time that they read as the lip of a bowl rather than as peaks. Their stone is buried under a hide of packed plant matter meters thick, cured to the hardness of horn. The water has stood at exactly this level for longer than any of its inhabitants have had a way to count. No river enters and none leaves. Slow seepage through the rim balances slow rain from the thin sky, and nothing has tipped that balance in a hundred million years.

## The Ceiling

Look up from anywhere in the basin and you see the only light there is: a pale, brownish gray, so faint it seems remembered rather than seen. It comes through the **Raft**, a continuous floating mat of leaf and mesh that covers the whole surface from rim to rim. Leaves lie edge over edge like shingles, each the size of a village square, waxy and dark green-black on top where the thin sun strikes them, and translucent amber underneath. Sunlight arrives through them as a rumor, perhaps a thousandth of what a lowland forest floor receives.

The Raft feeds everything. Its sugars are pumped downward through **tethers**, hollow stems as thick as cathedral pillars, some of them two or three kilometers long, that hang in the water like the roots of an upside-down forest. The tethers are the architecture of the world.

## The Grown Halls

Nothing here is bare. Nothing is merely found. Every surface has been made by something, whether grown, secreted, woven, gnawed, or packed, over spans of time so long that "natural" and "constructed" have no border between them.

The tethers are the great works. Over ages they have thickened, fused, and been coaxed by animals into forms that no seed could produce alone. Some have been braided into **colonnades**, ranks of pillars bridged at intervals by living arches, with floors of silt laid down and held by root-mats. Some have been hollowed by generations of tunneling and lined with a glossy resin, forming **long galleries** where the water inside is stiller than the water outside. Some spread near the bottom into **bowls**, wide concave terraces of woven fiber, each cradling a garden of pale bottom-plants that feed on the slow rain of dead Raft-leaf and animal waste.

The lakebed is not stone but **the Understory**: a mattress of compressed plant remains, forty meters thick in places, veined with the channels of ancient burrowers. Walk it, if walking were the word, and it gives slightly, like old moss. Beneath it the true bedrock of the basin has not been seen by any eye for an age.

The great structures are **far apart**. Between one colonnade and the next may lie half a kilometer of open dark water with nothing in it but drifting seed-husks and the slow glide of large bodies. The emptiness is part of the design. Each hall keeps a territory, and the water between is commons, crossing-ground, and silence, although silence is the wrong word.

## The Sound

The basin is loud. Water carries sound faster and farther than air, and the tethers are hollow and gas-filled in their upper lengths, so the whole forest is a set of organ pipes. When currents pass them, or when animals strike them, they sing. Each tether has its own pitch, fixed by its length and thickness and unchanged for millennia, so that the basin has a permanent chord. It is a low, layered drone that you feel in the sternum before you hear it. Travelers learn to navigate by it. To know the note of a place is to know where you are.

Over this drone come the voices:

- **Boomers**, enormous slow-finned grazers of the Raft's underside, call in long descending groans that take a full minute to finish and are answered from kilometers away.
- **Snappers**, shrimplike, live by the millions in the galleries. Their claws make a crackling like fire in dry brush, constant and dense, a wall of white noise that drowns out everything nearby.
- **Knockers** rap their bony heads against resin walls in rhythmic patterns, and each colonnade has its own dialect of knocking.
- Beneath all of it is the creak and tick of the plant-structures themselves as fibers contract in the cold, an ancient, unhurried joint-cracking.

Outsiders would find it deafening. The inhabitants would find true quiet terrifying.

## The Fauna

Life is abundant, though thinly spread across the great volume of water and packed dense where the structures give it purchase. The halls teem. The gaps do not.

Most creatures are pale or dark, with large eyes or none. A few carry their own light: cold blue-green points on fins and lures, dim and rare, each one a small event in the black. A drifting boomer might wear a slow constellation of them along its flank, and to watch one pass overhead is like watching a night sky move.

**Tenders** are the builders and the reason the halls exist: armored, many-limbed, roughly the size of a large dog, patient beyond reckoning. They graft, prune, and bind. They carry silt in their mouthparts and press it into floors. They know which tether will thicken if bent to the east. Their work spans generations, each individual adding a hand's breadth to a project whose end no one alive will see, and whose end may not exist. They are less the rulers of the world than its gardeners.

**Shoalers** move in bright, tight schools through the colonnades, feeding on the drift. **Lurkers** hang motionless in the galleries with mouths agape, waiting. **Grazers**, the boomers, browse the Raft's underside in herds so slow that the leaves they cropped a century ago have regrown and been cropped again. There are also **crawlers** in the Understory and **ribbon-swimmers** that thread through the tether-forest like living smoke. Every niche is taken. The ecology is old and stable, a web with no loose strands.

## The Rules

There is very little change here, and the rules follow from that:

1. **The water level does not move.** Rim, seep, and rain are in permanent balance. No tide, no flood, no drought.
2. **The temperature is fixed** at a cool, even level from Raft to floor. Only the top few meters, where thin sunlight warms the leaves, differ, and that difference is so regular it has become a kind of law.
3. **The light is fixed.** There is no night and no day, only the dim ceiling-glow, always the same.
4. **Growth is slow and self-limiting.** A tether that reaches full length stops. A hall that reaches its final form is maintained rather than extended. The Tenders repair but rarely begin anew, because the world is essentially complete.
5. **Death is recycled entirely.** Everything that dies sinks, settles in the Understory, and becomes floor, food, or wall. Nothing is wasted and nothing accumulates.

## A Passage Through

Say you enter from the rim, sliding down through the horn-hard crust and into the water. The cold closes around you. For the first hundred meters there is only the ceiling-glow above and the drone of the tethers below, rising in pitch as you descend. You pass through a gap of open water, and the sound thins to a distant hum.

Then a shape resolves ahead: a colonnade, pillars rising out of darkness and vanishing into the amber ceiling, each one wound with ribbed cords of secondary growth, each ringing softly with its own note. The knocking begins. Snappers crackle in the galleries. A boomer's groan rolls through the water like a slow landslide, and something enormous and unhurried passes overhead, dotted with cold blue lights.

You drift into the colonnade. The pillars are older than any language. Their surfaces are worn smooth as river stones, not by water, which barely moves here, but by the countless bodies that have brushed them. A Tender works at an arch some meters above you, pressing a fiber into place with an exactness that suggests a plan far older than itself. It does not look at you.

Beyond the colonnade the dark opens again. Somewhere half a kilometer on is the next hall, and you can hear it, a lower, wider chord, calling faintly through the black. You swim toward it. Nothing in the basin will have changed when you arrive, and nothing will have changed when you leave.
