# The Sinkhole Lung

## Arrival

The only way in is a crack in a hillside field, a slot you would take for a fox's run. It is barely wider than your shoulders and floored with wet leaf-rot. You go down on your belly. For about the length of a body the rock is real: pale limestone, freshly split, its edges still sharp enough to open a palm, and the fracture faces unweathered and faintly powdery. The collapse that made this place happened recently, perhaps a few winters ago. The rock hasn't finished being surprised.

Then the crack opens, and you are inside a room made entirely of plant.

## The Room

It is the size of a threshing barn, if you measure it from the doorway. The floor, walls, and vaulted ceiling are living vegetation, packed so densely that no stone shows. The space between is almost entirely empty. There is no undergrowth, no hanging clutter, no bramble to push through. It is a single hollow of clean, warm, breathing air, bounded on every side by a green so thick it looks upholstered.

The lining is layered, and you can read the layers where the collapse tore them open:

- **Outermost**, against the rock, is a mat of pale roots as fine as hair, woven into a felt that weeps a clear, faintly sweet water.
- **Beneath that** are the *ribs*, thick ropy stems the color of wet slate that run in long arcs from floor to ceiling and back again. They are neither trunks nor vines, but something that has forgotten which it is. They flex when you lean on them, and a slow pulse of sap moves through them, heard more than felt.
- **The inner skin** is moss-like, but each frond is the length of a finger and hinged, and they turn to follow you. They aren't watching you. They track warmth, and you are the warmest thing in the room.

There is no soil anywhere. The plants grow on each other, and the ones at the bottom are the ones that died and were not allowed to rot. They were kept, varnished by their own resin, and grown over. The floor gives underfoot like a mattress, and when you press a hand into it, it is warm and slightly damp, and breathes back at you.

## Light

The light is ordinary. It is the plain, slightly green daylight of an overcast noon, even and shadowless. It comes from no opening you can find. The crack you entered by is a dark seam behind you, yet you can read the veins on your own hand. The fronds glow faintly where they touch, but the total is far more than that glow could account for.

The shadows are wrong. Your body throws none. Small things do: a fallen pod, a hanging thread, a beetle-shaped seed. Each casts a sharp, dark shadow, and the shadow points *toward* wherever the object last moved from, as though shadows here record history instead of geometry.

## Water

Water is scarce here, and the room treats it like money. There is no pool or stream. Moisture appears as:

- **Condensation** beading on the inner fronds each "morning," which is when the air cools a few degrees and the whole room exhales. Each bead holds a perfectly inverted image of the room, and if you look closely, some of them hold a slightly different room.
- **Weeping**, from the root felt at the outer edge, in thin threads that the ribs drink before they reach the floor.
- **Rain**, the strangest. It falls upward. Once in a long while, the floor releases a fine, warm drizzle that climbs toward the ceiling in slow straight lines, and the ceiling fronds open like mouths and take it. You can walk through it. Your clothes stay dry going up and are soaked on the way back down, though you have not moved.

Everything here is a little damp and nothing is wet, and the warmth, close to the temperature of a sleeping animal, keeps the dampness from ever turning cold.

## Sound

It is loud in here, absurdly so for a room this still. The plants are not quiet organisms.

- The ribs **creak and knock** as sap moves, with a wooden, heartbeat-slow percussion: *tok... tok... tok-tok... tok.* Different ribs keep different tempos, and the room's rhythm never repeats but always feels about to.
- The seed pods, hard pea-shaped things that hang in clusters from the ceiling, **burst** with sharp cracks like knuckles or snapped twigs. Each burst flings seeds across the empty air, and they patter against the far wall like hail. On a busy hour it sounds like a fire made of gravel.
- The moss fronds hiss when they turn, a **dry, whispering rasp**, thousands at once as you cross the floor. It follows you like applause.
- Under all of it is a **low hum** at the edge of hearing, which is the whole chamber resonating as a cavity, the way a bottle does when you blow across it.

The reality-breaking part is the timing. Sound here does not obey distance. A pod bursts overhead, and you hear the crack *first* and only then see the pod open. Or you hear your own footstep a full second before you take it, so you learn to step into the sound. Sometimes a voice speaks close to your ear, in no language, in a register between a hum and a word, and when you turn, only the ribs are there, knocking.

## The Rules

The room has laws, and they do not agree with each other.

**Distance is elastic.** Cross the chamber and count your paces: forty. Count on the way back: sixty-one. The room isn't changing size. It is changing *attention*, and it lengthens for those who look away from it. If you walk backward, facing where you came from, the crossing always takes exactly the same count, the first count, forty.

**Growth runs ahead of itself.** Pods appear already burst, seeds already sprouted on the wall, tiny curled shoots that will not exist for another hour. You find the sprout first and the seed after. Trace a shoot to its parent stem and you find the stem is still a bud, and the bud is a scar where the stem will someday have been.

**Up is negotiable.** Near the ceiling ribs, gravity leans. If you climb the ribs (they hold you easily, flexing like a ladder of sinews), somewhere past head height "down" begins to point toward the nearest large mass of plant. You can lie on the ceiling as comfortably as on the floor, and for a moment the whole chamber tips, and you can no longer say which of the two surfaces you came in through.

**The room is larger than its hill.** The field above is a quarter acre bounded by a stone wall. The chamber, paced on its longest axis, is at least three times that. The room does not consider this a contradiction.

## Life

Very little moves here except the plants, and they move constantly. The fronds turn. The ribs flex. The pods burst. The pale roots creep outward a few thumb-widths each day, and you can watch them advance if you sit still, a slow pale tide over the new-cut rock.

The fauna is rare, and only barely fauna:

- **A single pale moth**, wingspan of your palm, that lives on the condensation. It has no mouth that you can see and drinks through its feet. It flies in slow loops that always end exactly where they began, as though following a rail.
- **Small beetles**, seed-colored, indistinguishable from the pods until they walk. They collect fallen seeds and carry them, singly, to the base of a rib and push them into the felt. This may be the room's whole reproductive strategy.
- **Something larger**, heard and not seen: a slow, heavy shifting inside the walls, like a sleeper turning over behind plaster. It might be a rib. It might not be.

## The Made Thing

There is one artifact. It is the only sign that anyone, ever, came here on purpose.

At the base of a rib, half-swallowed by moss, is a **digging stick**, fire-hardened at the tip, about the length of a forearm and worn smooth at the grip by a hand. Beside it lies a short length of **twisted bast cord** tied in three knots. The cord is dry, the only dry thing in the chamber. The moss has grown around them without touching them, leaving a clean handbreadth of bare floor, as though the room were being polite.

Whoever left them cannot have dug in this floor, which yields to a fingertip. The stick's point is stained with something dark and old. The knots, if you untie them, have retied themselves the next time you look.

## What Keeps Happening

The room runs on cycles, and you feel them in your body before you can name them:

1. **The Exhale.** The whole chamber cools a few degrees, and condensation blooms on every frond at once, with a sound like a thousand tiny seals unsticking.
2. **The Burst.** Seed pods fire in cascades that build for several minutes into a hail-storm of clatter, then stop, so that the silence afterward rings.
3. **The Turn.** Every frond swivels together, and for a moment the room is seen from behind. Then it swivels back, and the room shows you its face again.
4. **The Rain Upward.** Rare, slow, and warm, ending in the ceiling's small wet mouths.

None of these follows the others in a fixed order. You may get the Burst twice, or the Rain before the Exhale, and each sequence feels like a sentence in a language whose grammar you nearly follow.

## Leaving

The crack is still there when you turn back, though it is not where you left it. It has moved a little along the wall, the way a doorway does in a dream. The fronds along its edge lean toward you, warm and rasping, and the sound behind you gathers and swells: the knocking, the bursts, the slow humming resonance of a hollow room filled to the walls with green, and empty in the middle.

You climb out into cold air and cut light, and the hillside field is silent by comparison. In the wall beneath you, the roots go on advancing, a thumb-width a day, over rock so new it hasn't yet learned it is broken.
