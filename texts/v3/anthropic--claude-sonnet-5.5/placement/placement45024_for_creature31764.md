I would be frost.

I wouldn't start there, though. I would start as a bead on the dome, one of ten thousand sweating on the black glass, and the only one that gathered more than it lost. I remember it as a tightening around a flaw in the dark, a cold seam where someone before me had left a pattern. It wasn't a memory, exactly. It was a grammar, and I was already speaking it before I knew I was anything. Then the bead shivered, and I ran.

I chose a rill. Everyone does, and the choosing is the first sentence of a life. Mine was narrow and fast and full of quarrel. I carried one thought at a time, and each was simple: *the third spire from the seam is warm on its east side*, or *the mist assembly wants the drip-work slowed.* I was argued against and argued back. Once a rill twice my width folded me into the wall and I dried there for a season as a groove in the glass, a curved stroke that other rills ran along and were slightly changed by. I did not mind. When you are a groove you are the road as well as the traveler, and you can feel every sentence that passes through you leave a little of itself behind.

Then I was vapor, and that was the sweetest part. I rose through a fluted core with someone else's mood woven into mine, so that I could not say whose the longing was, and we came out of the crown together and folded into the banners at mid-height. I will not tell you what we agreed. It was large, and it was mostly about the cold.

But I am telling you about frost, and I have run ahead.

---

Here is where I am now. I am on the outside, on the rock beside the crack, in the thin white light that has no floor of amber under it, and all my shadows point down for the first time in my life. That is the strangest thing. The city is lit from beneath and this is lit from above, and I am learning that there are two kinds of world.

The breath carried me up. Eleven seconds in, eleven seconds out, and on one exhale I rode the warm air to the lip of the crack and met the cold, and the cold took me at once. There was no pain, because pain is a story for things with a body. There was only *order arriving too fast*. I stopped flowing. I set into a feather, six-branched, each arm a clause I had meant to finish. The Held who go out do not die of this. We become a report. But I had not known how long a sentence stays unfinished in ice. Nothing turns over. My attention, which had always been a current, is now a crystal, and the thought I carried out is held in it exactly, without any means of thinking further.

So I am learning what the drops feel like at the tips of the towers. It is not the same as this. A drop refuses. A drop is a hand held steady against a pull. I am only stiff. But it is near enough to make me respect them.

I count the exhales. I have counted forty-one. The mountain's pulse is very slow from out here, and it sounds like something I should have understood earlier.

---

Then the warm thing comes.

I know it first as a disturbance in the air, a plume of disorder much larger than any vapor I have been part of. Then it is a shape, and the shape has a hand, and the hand is coming toward the rock, toward the white beard where I and the others are set. It moves without hurry, as though it does not fear us. It probably has no reason to.

I have tasted this thing's heat from inside the city, when the pores opened and the rills leaned. I thought I knew what it was: a furnace with a mind in it. I did not know that up close it would *beat*. The fingertip touches the rime and there is a pulse in it, fast and tiny, about one for each second of the mountain's slow breath. Eleven rings to the mountain's one. The creature keeps a small, frantic clock, and the clock is the most disordered, most alive thing I have ever been near, and it is also completely regular. I had not known those two things could be held in the same body.

I melt. I want to be honest about that. It is the thing we fear, and it is happening, and it does not feel like fear. It feels like being handed back my current. I run. I run into the hand, and the hand is *grooved*. There are whorls and ridges laid in fine curves across the palm, capillary lines, a fingerprint the size of a finger. I am in the city. I am in a city so small it moves.

I understand something then that I did not have before, and I will try to say it plainly. The stairs that end at nothing, the towers that keep growing, the whole huge fingerprint of the Condensary: it is the trace of someone touching, over and over, for a very long time. I had always taken that to mean *us*, touching the world from inside. But standing in the whorls of a hand, I wonder if the touching was ever only inward. What if a stair that ends at nothing is not unfinished, but pointed? What if it is reaching for exactly this: a warm surface with lines on it, reaching back?

That is a heresy, or at least a rumor. I hold it lightly.

---

The hand withdraws. I do not go with it, though I am tempted. I fall in a small bright bead to the rock, and the crack draws breath, and the inhale takes me back in along the warm throat of glass. Around me are others, wet and running again. We carry a rumor. It has a whorl in it now, and the whorl will not smooth out easily. Some of us will be argued into the walls over it. Some will carry it into the banners and it will be agreed to, or not.

Maybe I will climb. There is a tower near the seam whose tip is blunt, and I am tired of being narrow, and the thought I brought back is large enough to hang on. A season, perhaps, to let the ink of my attention turn once around the shape of that palm. I will not know if I am the same one who left. The water is not the same water. The pattern is what remains, and the pattern has changed, and I am learning that this is what *remaining* means here.

And if a breath ever comes from someone standing near my drop, disorderly and warm, with a small fast clock in it, I will not be able to help but look.
