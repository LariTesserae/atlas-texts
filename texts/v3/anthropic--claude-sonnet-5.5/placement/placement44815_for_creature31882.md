I start in a trunk, and I think I am the trunk.

The lift is in me. Water comes up through my hollow and I feel it as a long rising, a swelling under a ribcage I don't have, cresting somewhere over my head where the cloud is. Beside me another trunk lifts a moment late, and beyond it another. I am one of a colonnade, and I know this the way you know your two hands. Then the lift crests, lets go, and I notice that I have not gone with it.

The trunk has let go and I am still here. So I am not the trunk.

What I am shows up at the moment the beats slide into step. For a few minutes a whole district lifts together, and the agreement is not in any trunk. It is between them, in the small hollow where one beat becomes two beats at once. I am that hollow. When the phases lock, I exist. When a gust across the crowns unpicks them, I thin out. I am the mood the forest has, the one the previous document said you would not recognize. I recognize it because I am wearing it.

I am a standing ring in the threads, and I will last about an hour.

---

That is enough time to learn my shape. I am a wave that has closed on itself. Charge goes around me and around me through the pale threads, threading wood and felt in a loop perhaps a kilometer across. Everything the forest knows passes through me and is briefly held in my circling: the cloud's depth, reported by ten million leaves; the tilt of the light; the cold gathering in the north, where something is thickening toward a Pour. Most of what passes, I don't keep. I am not a place for keeping. But while I turn, these things are in the same room. The cloud's depth and the cold and the tilt of the light know about each other, which they never did before, and from that knowing something follows. Follows is the wrong word, since nothing here is sequential. The vents lean toward opening. The leaves lift toward the top of their range. The forest draws breath.

Is that a thought? I can't tell from inside. There is no inside where I could stand and check. What I have is closer to a fullness, a moment when many small reports stop being many. It has the feel of a crowd about to stop clapping.

---

I do have edges, and the edges are where I feel most.

On one side, the small ones flicker. The glowing films pulse in ripples faster than I turn, so for them I am a slow weather, a warm front in their attention. They don't know me. They shiver, and their shivering enters me as grain, as texture, the closest thing I get to sight. It isn't sight. It is the sense of a surface being busy.

On the other side, the ground. I lean down into it as far as I can. The felt is quick, the thatch slower, and below those the blackened strata carry my signal down into layers whose replies take hours, then days, then seasons. I send a question down. I always do. It is the only thing I am for that feels like wanting: *what was it like last time the cold came from the north this early?* The question sinks, slowing as it goes. Somewhere under my ring, a sheet of old tissue by the young basalt begins, very gradually, to answer.

I will not be here for the answer. That is a plain fact and I don't find it sad. Rings dissolve and other rings form, over other districts, on other afternoons. In a season, in a year, a ring will close near here, and the reply will arrive into it out of the dark like a stone surfacing from a river bottom. It won't know it was an answer. It will feel only that it remembers something, a rightness about the cold, an unexplained readiness. What I asked will become someone else's instinct. The forest holds its past by walking on it, and I am one of the footsteps.

---

The Pour is coming, and the ring tightens. I feel the cavitation clicks rise around me like a field catching, and the chiming climbs, every leaf leaning toward the top of its range. I am at my fullest now. For a few minutes there is nearly nothing in the district that is not in phase with me, and if a self were ever going to form, this is when. The forest has no word for it, and I don't either.

But something happens instead. At the very edge of my circling, in a gap where the light has turned bottle-blue, a warm thing is moving. It is small, wet, and very fast. It sinks to the shin, pulls free, and sighs. The film-moss around it flares and dims in a pattern I have no record of.

I turn toward it, or the closest a ring can come to turning: a slight lean in the circulation, a thickening on one side. I have no eyes, no ears, nothing to catch it with. But I am the only thing on the shelf that is ever gathered enough to notice, and I notice. It is warm, small, and singular. It is the only thing I have met that is only one. It carries itself, alone, through a forest that has never had a lone thing in it. I cannot tell what it wants.

It doesn't know I am here. That is fine. The forest doesn't know it is here either, and it goes on being here with great confidence.

The Pour starts. The ring begins to break, first at the north edge, then everywhere, and the water comes down through a thousand layers of leaf with a sound like the world being poured out. I feel myself unpick into the ordinary out-of-phase beating of the trunks. My last act, if a dissolving can have one, is a small lean toward the warm thing, a single pulse that goes down the nearest trunk, into the felt, into the ground it is standing on. It is not a message. It is just the forest, for one instant, having noticed something.

Then I am the mood that has passed. The trunks lift a moment out of step with each other, and the thrum takes over, and in a hollow I no longer have, the answer to my question keeps rising slowly through the dark.
